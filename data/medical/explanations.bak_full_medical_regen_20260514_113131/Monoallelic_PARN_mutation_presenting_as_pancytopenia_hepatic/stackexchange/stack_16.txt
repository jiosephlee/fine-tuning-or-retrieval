Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

### [Morning Report] In suspected pernicious anemia contributing to B12 deficiency, which serologic and histologic tests would you obtain to demonstrate autoimmune gastritis and intrinsic factor deficiency?

When you suspect pernicious anemia as the cause of B12 deficiency you want serologic evidence of autoimmune destruction of oxyntic mucosa and intrinsic-factor (IF) deficiency, and histologic confirmation of autoimmune (type A) gastritis in the gastric body/fundus.

Obtain serologic tests: anti‑parietal cell antibodies (APCA) and anti‑intrinsic‑factor antibodies (both blocking and binding types) — anti‑IF antibodies are more specific for pernicious anemia, APCA are more sensitive but less specific. Measure fasting serum gastrin (markedly elevated when parietal cells are lost) and serum pepsinogen I and the pepsinogen I/II ratio (low PG I and low ratio support gastric corpus atrophy). Test for Helicobacter pylori (breath, stool antigen, or biopsy) to exclude H. pylori–associated atrophic gastritis. Continue routine confirmation of B12 deficiency with serum B12 plus methylmalonic acid (and homocysteine as needed).

Obtain targeted histology from endoscopic biopsies of the gastric body and fundus (not just antral biopsies): expect loss/atrophy of oxyntic (parietal) mucosa with decreased/absent parietal cells, chronic lymphoplasmacytic (autoimmune) inflammation, glandular atrophy, intestinal or pseudopyloric metaplasia, and enterochromaffin‑like (ECL) cell hyperplasia (reflecting hypergastrinemia). Immunostains (H+/K+‑ATPase for parietal cells or chromogranin A for ECL cells) can be helpful in difficult cases.

Why these tests: anti‑IF and anti‑parietal cell antibodies demonstrate autoimmune targeting of IF and parietal cells; gastrin and pepsinogen are functional biomarkers of corpus atrophy; biopsy documents the anatomic substrate (parietal cell loss, atrophy, ECL hyperplasia) and excludes H. pylori or other pathology. Confirming autoimmune gastritis has implications for lifelong B12 replacement, surveillance for gastric neoplasia (adenocarcinoma and type I neuroendocrine tumors), and for evaluating other autoimmune disorders.
