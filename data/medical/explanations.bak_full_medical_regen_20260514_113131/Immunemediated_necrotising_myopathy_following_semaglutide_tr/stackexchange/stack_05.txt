Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?

### [Pathophysiology] How does complement-mediated membrane attack complex (MAC) deposition contribute to myofiber injury in IMNM?

Complement-mediated injury in IMNM is best understood as an antibody‑driven, non‑cell‑mediated attack on the myofibre membrane that culminates in necrosis rather than classic lymphocyte‑directed cytotoxicity. In patients with IMNM the sequence is typically: circulating autoantibodies (for example anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP when present) or other immune complexes bind antigens at or near the myofibre sarcolemma, this immune complex formation activates the classical complement pathway, and terminal complement components assemble as C5b‑9—the membrane attack complex (MAC)—on the sarcolemmal surface. Insertion of MAC pores increases sarcolemmal permeability, allowing uncontrolled calcium influx, disruption of excitation–contraction coupling, mitochondrial injury, activation of calcium‑dependent proteases and phospholipases, and finally irreversible myofibre necrosis.

Pathologically this explains several hallmark features of IMNM: necrotic and regenerating fibres with prominent macrophage myophagocytosis but only sparse T‑cell infiltrates, and positive C5b‑9 staining on necrotic (and sometimes adjacent non‑necrotic) fibres. MAC deposition therefore acts as an initiator of myofibre death and a stimulus for secondary innate inflammation (macrophage recruitment and phagocytosis), rather than a T‑cell–mediated process. Clinically, this mechanism accounts for the brisk CK elevations and the responsiveness of many cases to therapies that reduce antibody production or complement activity (corticosteroids, IVIG, complement inhibitors in experimental settings), because lowering antibody titers or interfering with complement assembly prevents further MAC formation and ongoing muscle injury.
