Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?

### [Management] Which steroid-sparing agents are commonly used in IMNM, and in what clinical scenarios would you escalate to rituximab or other advanced therapies?

In immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) you almost always start with high‑dose corticosteroids for rapid control, but long‑term steroid monotherapy is undesirable because of toxicity and because many IMNM patients need additional immunomodulation to achieve steroid‑sparing and durable remission. The common “next‑line” steroid‑sparing agents are methotrexate, azathioprine, and mycophenolate mofetil — chosen because they are familiar, generally well tolerated, and have the best evidence across inflammatory myopathies for permitting prednisone taper. Calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus or ciclosporin) are used when faster or stronger T‑cell suppression is needed or when mycophenolate/azathioprine are not tolerated. IVIG occupies a special role: for IMNM (particularly anti‑HMGCR or when there is severe weakness, dysphagia, or respiratory/cardiac involvement) IVIG is often used early and may be continued as maintenance; in many series IVIG produces rapid clinical and CK responses and is considered first‑line in combination with steroids for severe presentations.

Escalation to rituximab or other advanced therapies is driven by disease severity, autoantibody profile, and response to initial therapy. You should consider rituximab when a patient has refractory disease despite an adequate trial of steroids plus a conventional steroid‑sparing agent (or steroid + IVIG), or when the phenotype is severe from the outset (progressive profound weakness, persistent very high CK, bulbar involvement/dysphagia, respiratory failure, or cardiac involvement). Anti‑SRP–positive IMNM and some seronegative cases are more often refractory and are frequent scenarios in which teams escalate to rituximab. Cyclophosphamide is reserved for life‑threatening or organ‑threatening disease (severe myocarditis, rapidly progressive respiratory failure) or when rapid, broad immunosuppression is required. Plasma exchange and other biologics (e.g., abatacept, tocilizumab) have been used in small series or case reports for refractory disease but lack high‑quality evidence and are generally tertiary options after rituximab or cyclophosphamide fail or are contraindicated.

Clinically, apply these principles: if a patient improves promptly with steroids ± IVIG and can taper off steroids, no escalation is needed; if weakness or CK plateau or worsen after an adequate trial (weeks to a few months depending on severity) of combined therapy, escalate. Bulbar, respiratory, or cardiac involvement should prompt early aggressive therapy (steroids + IVIG and early addition of a steroid‑sparing agent, with low threshold for rituximab or cyclophosphamide). When choosing agents, account for comorbidities (liver disease, infection risk, fertility), screen appropriately (TB, hepatitis, TPMT for azathioprine), vaccinate before B‑cell depletion, and monitor CK, strength, and adverse effects closely.
