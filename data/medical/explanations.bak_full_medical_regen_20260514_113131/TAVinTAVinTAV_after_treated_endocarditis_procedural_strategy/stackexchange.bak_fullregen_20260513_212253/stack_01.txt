Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

### [Differential Diagnosis] In an older man with prior TAV-in-TAV who re-presents with dyspnoea, a mean gradient of ~50 mm Hg and moderate-to-severe intraprosthetic regurgitation 18 months after Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis, what are the leading causes of transcatheter aortic prosthesis dysfunction you would include in the differential (at least four), and which features in this case help you prioritize among them?

Leading causes to consider include structural (degenerative) leaflet dysfunction, prosthesis thrombosis, recurrent or residual infective endocarditis with leaflet destruction, pannus or fibrotic overgrowth/adhesion, and mechanical causes related to frame underexpansion/prosthesis‑in‑prosthesis (patient–prosthesis mismatch or leaflet impingement). Paravalvular dehiscence/annular disruption is another cause but is less consistent with isolated intraprosthetic regurgitation.

How to prioritize in this patient:

1) Structural degeneration / leaflet fibrosis with mechanical restriction — highest on the list. The clinical course, with prior bioprosthetic degeneration, a documented episode of PVE (which can accelerate leaflet damage), and MSCT/TTE showing two overlapping stented valves with a severely reduced effective orifice (minimal lumen 13.7 mm, AVA 0.5 cm2) all point to fixed leaflet immobility and tissue fibrosis as the dominant mechanism causing combined high gradients and intraprosthetic regurgitation.

2) Frame underexpansion / prosthesis‑in‑prosthesis haemodynamic compromise (PPM) — also very likely and often coexists with leaflet dysfunction. The evolution after TAV‑in‑TAV frequently narrows the effective orifice; the small measured lumen and very high gradients are compatible with reduced annular area from overlapping frames and incomplete expansion of the Evolut inside the original device.

3) Recurrent or residual PVE with leaflet destruction or perforation — possible but less likely here. The patient had treated Enterococcus faecalis endocarditis 18 months earlier; however, preprocedural TEE and MSCT reported no vegetations or paravalvular complications and blood cultures were negative, arguing against active infection as the cause of the current dysfunction. Nonetheless prior infection could have caused irreversible leaflet damage that manifests now.

4) Prosthetic valve thrombosis — should be considered because it causes sudden rises in gradient and can produce restricted leaflet motion; it is more common in the months after implantation but can occur later. It is typically diagnosed by hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) and reduced leaflet motion on gated CT or by response to anticoagulation. In this case thrombosis is possible but less favored because CT and TEE done during workup did not report HALT or suggest thrombus, and the time course plus prior degeneration/PVE make structural causes more likely.

5) Pannus / fibrotic overgrowth or leaflet tear/prolapse — pannus is a slower, progressive process producing stenosis and sometimes regurgitation; leaflet tear/prolapse (mechanical failure) can cause isolated regurgitation. Either could contribute, but the imaging and clinical history (progressive stenosis with secondary regurgitation, multiple layered frames) point primarily to restricted leaflets and frame-related narrowing rather than an acute leaflet tear.

Finally, paravalvular dehiscence is unlikely because the regurgitation is described as intraprosthetic and no paravalvular leak or annular complications were seen on TEE/MSCT.

Practical next steps driven by this differential are: review gated contrast CT for HALT/thrombus and leaflet motion, correlate with TEE for perforation or vegetations, and consider a therapeutic trial of anticoagulation only if imaging suggests thrombosis. If imaging supports fixed structural restriction and frame underexpansion/PPM, reintervention planning (as was done) is appropriate, with attention to coronary protection and strategies to optimise effective orifice.
