Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A

### [Management] Describe the typical sensitivity of HNF1A-MODY to low-dose sulfonylureas and the main adverse effect to monitor when prescribing them.

Patients with HNF1A‑MODY are classically highly sensitive to low‑dose sulfonylureas. Because the defect is primarily a progressive beta‑cell secretory failure rather than insulin resistance, sulfonylureas—by closing KATP channels and amplifying residual insulin secretion—often produce a rapid and marked fall in glucose and HbA1c even at doses lower than used for typical type 2 diabetes; this is why many patients can be moved off insulin onto a single low‑dose sulfonylurea.

The principal adverse effect to monitor is hypoglycaemia. Given the exaggerated secretory response, even standard or modest doses can precipitate symptomatic or severe hypoglycaemia (and weight gain). Clinically, start very low and titrate slowly, educate patients about hypoglycaemia recognition and treatment, ensure frequent self‑monitoring of glucose during dose changes, and be particularly cautious in those with renal impairment, irregular oral intake, or concomitant medications that increase hypoglycaemia risk.
