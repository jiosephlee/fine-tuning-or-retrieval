Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A

### [Clinical Pearls] What oral glucose tolerance test pattern is commonly seen in HNF1A-MODY, and why might fasting glucose be less abnormal early in the disease?

In HNF1A‑MODY the classic OGTT pattern is relatively preserved fasting glucose with marked post‑load hyperglycaemia — a large incremental rise after the 75‑g load and a very high 2‑hour glucose. Mechanistically this reflects an early, selective defect in glucose‑stimulated insulin secretion: first‑phase insulin (and early C‑peptide) responses are blunted, so the pancreas cannot contain the postprandial excursion, whereas basal (tonic) insulin secretion that maintains overnight/fasting glucose is often relatively preserved early in the disease. In addition, HNF1A mutations impair glucose sensing (reduced GLUT2 expression and downstream glycolytic flux), so β‑cells fail when challenged by a glucose load even though fasting glycaemia may remain near normal. Clinically, therefore, an OGTT or postprandial glucose measurement is more sensitive than a single fasting glucose to detect HNF1A‑MODY in early stages.
