Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Management] In a patient with suspected TMA and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia while awaiting ADAMTS13 testing, what immediate management steps should be taken, and which blood product is generally avoided unless there is life-threatening bleeding?

Immediate steps when you suspect a thrombotic microangiopathy with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) while ADAMTS13 is pending

1) Rapid diagnostic triage
- Send urgent tests: ADAMTS13 activity (stat), peripheral blood smear for schistocytes, CBC, reticulocyte count, LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test, coagulation panel (PT/aPTT, fibrinogen, D‑dimer) and STEC/shiga toxin testing as indicated. These help distinguish TTP, DIC and STEC‑HUS.  
- In the reported case the peripheral smear (15–20% schistocytes) and rising LDH/haptoglobin nadir pointed to MAHA; ADAMTS13 was sent immediately.

2) Don’t wait for ADAMTS13 to treat suspected TTP
- If clinical suspicion for TTP is moderate–high (eg, neurologic signs, severe thrombocytopenia, MAHA, high PLASMIC score) start plasma exchange (PEX) urgently—ideally within hours—because severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10%) is a medical emergency. PEX is lifesaving in TTP.  
- In the case PEX was started empirically as a bridging therapy while ADAMTS13 results were pending.

3) Concurrent empiric therapies
- Give high‑dose corticosteroids when immune TTP is suspected. Consider caplacizumab if available and TTP is likely while awaiting confirmatory testing.  
- Supportive care: transfuse packed red blood cells for symptomatic anemia or hemodynamic instability; treat the underlying trigger (eg, antibiotics for sepsis); provide renal support/dialysis for severe AKI as needed (this patient required continuous renal replacement therapy).

4) Use PEX as a bridge in uncertain cases of TMA
- PEX may transiently help non‑TTP TMAs and is frequently used while the diagnosis is clarified. That is what was done here prior to starting eculizumab for suspected complement‑mediated HUS.

5) If complement‑mediated HUS (CM‑HUS) is diagnosed or strongly suspected
- Arrange for eculizumab early after balancing infectious risk. In active, uncontrolled bacteraemia delay complement blockade if possible until cultures are controlled and meningococcal vaccination / antimicrobial prophylaxis are in place. In this case eculizumab was started after blood cultures became negative and after initial vaccination and prophylaxis planning.

Which blood product is generally avoided
- Platelet transfusion. Platelet transfusions are generally avoided in TMA/TTP because they can exacerbate microvascular thrombosis and worsen ischemic organ injury. Give platelets only for life‑threatening bleeding, before an urgent invasive procedure, or rarely when bleeding cannot be controlled—ideally coordinated with PEX and hematology.  
- In the case report platelets were managed cautiously; earlier thrombocyte/fibrinogen support had been given during overt DIC, but routine platelet transfusion in the setting of evolving TMA was avoided unless bleeding dictated otherwise.

Bottom line: while awaiting ADAMTS13, urgently send diagnostic tests, initiate empiric PEX and steroids if TTP cannot be excluded, provide supportive care (RBC transfusion, dialysis, treat infection), and avoid platelet transfusion except for life‑threatening bleeding.
