Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Management] Before starting eculizumab, which meningococcal vaccines are recommended, what is the ideal timing, and what antibiotic prophylaxis strategy should be used if complement blockade cannot be delayed?

Before you give terminal complement blockade you must both vaccinate against meningococcus and, if you cannot wait for protective immunity, provide immediate antibiotic cover. The key points are these.

Which vaccines
Use both a quadrivalent capsular conjugate meningococcal vaccine covering serogroups A, C, W and Y (eg, MenACWY — Menveo, Nimenrix) and a serogroup B vaccine (eg, Bexsero or Trumenba). Eculizumab blocks terminal complement and markedly increases risk of invasive disease from encapsulated organisms, particularly Neisseria meningitidis of any serogroup; protection therefore requires both ACWY and B vaccines.

Ideal timing
Ideally give the ACWY and B vaccines at least 2 weeks before the first dose of eculizumab to allow development of protective antibody titres. If you can delay therapy that 14‑day window, do so. If the patient has already received prior appropriate meningococcal immunization according to local adult schedules, proceed based on those records and timing.

If complement blockade cannot be delayed
If eculizumab must be started immediately (active disease, life‑threatening TMA), give the ACWY and B vaccines immediately (do not withhold vaccination) and begin antibacterial prophylaxis. Continue antibiotics until at least 2 weeks after vaccination to cover the window before vaccine‑induced immunity develops. Many centres use oral penicillin (eg, phenoxymethylpenicillin 500 mg twice daily or 250–500 mg three times daily) or amoxicillin as first line; for penicillin allergy use an alternative such as ciprofloxacin (single daily dosing) guided by local susceptibility and infectious‑disease advice. In practice, some experts continue long‑term prophylactic penicillin for the duration of eculizumab therapy because vaccination reduces but does not eliminate risk. Provide written patient education (signs/symptoms of meningococcal sepsis), ensure rapid access to urgent medical review and consider standby parenteral antibiotics for use if fever/meningitis symptoms develop.

Practical summary
Give MenACWY + MenB and wait ≥14 days if clinically safe. If you cannot wait, vaccinate immediately and start antibiotic prophylaxis (eg, oral penicillin/amoxicillin; ciprofloxacin if penicillin allergic) and continue antibiotics until 2 weeks after vaccination — with a low threshold to continue longer or indefinitely while on eculizumab, in consultation with infectious‑disease specialists. Monitor closely and educate the patient about prompt presentation for febrile illness.
