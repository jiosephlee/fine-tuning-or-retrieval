Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone

### [Management] According to contemporary guidelines, what is the recommended first-line therapy for acute parenchymal neuro-Behçet’s disease, and when should an additional immunosuppressive agent be introduced?

The preferred initial treatment for acute parenchymal neuro-Behçet’s disease is high‑dose systemic corticosteroids to rapidly suppress CNS inflammation — typically intravenous methylprednisolone pulses (eg, 1 g/day for 3–5 days) followed by a high‑dose oral prednisone taper (about 0.5–1 mg/kg/day) with gradual reduction guided by clinical and radiographic response.

Because parenchymal neuro‑BD carries a high risk of relapse and accrual of irreversible neurological injury, contemporary guideline practice is to introduce a steroid‑sparing immunosuppressive agent early rather than using steroids alone for prolonged periods. Azathioprine is the common first‑line steroid‑sparing drug used concurrently or started soon after the pulse steroids (ie, at diagnosis or within the first days to weeks) to allow timely steroid tapering and reduce relapses. For severe disease, major neurological deficit, brainstem/deep lesions, treatment failure, or frequent relapses, stronger agents (eg, cyclophosphamide or anti–TNF agents such as infliximab/adalimumab) are recommended.

In short: treat acute parenchymal neuro‑BD with high‑dose corticosteroids immediately and plan a steroid‑sparing immunosuppressant (usually azathioprine) early — essentially at or soon after diagnosis — reserving cyclophosphamide or anti‑TNF therapy for severe or refractory presentations. This approach balances rapid control of inflammation with prevention of relapse and steroid toxicity.
