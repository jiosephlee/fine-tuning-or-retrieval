Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone

### [Management] Which organ involvements in Behçet’s disease are associated with worse prognosis, and what long-term immunomodulatory strategies reduce relapse risk in neuro-ocular disease?

Major organ involvement determines prognosis in Behçet’s disease because it reflects both higher inflammatory burden and complications that are irreversible or life‑threatening. In practice, the organ manifestations that portend a worse long‑term outcome are central nervous system disease (parenchymal neuro‑Behçet’s and major venous sinus thrombosis), ocular disease with recurrent sight‑threatening uveitis, large‑vessel disease (arterial aneurysms—classically pulmonary artery—and large‑vessel thrombosis), and severe gastrointestinal involvement (deep ulceration with risk of perforation or bleeding). These sites are associated with increased morbidity and mortality and usually require more aggressive, sustained therapy than isolated mucocutaneous disease.

Prevention of relapse in neuro‑ and ocular Behçet’s rests on long‑term immunomodulation rather than intermittent corticosteroids alone. The evidence and clinical practice points are:

- Azathioprine is a well‑established steroid‑sparing agent for both ocular and parenchymal neuro‑Behçet’s: it reduces attack frequency and preserves function and is commonly used for maintenance after control of an acute episode. Its oral toxicity profile and cumulative data make it a first‑line maintenance choice.

- Anti–TNF agents (infliximab, adalimumab) are highly effective for refractory or severe ocular disease and for disabling neuro‑Behçet’s. They reduce relapse rates and rapidly control inflammation; they are used when azathioprine/other conventional agents fail or when vision/neurological function is threatened.

- Cyclosporine is effective for ocular disease and reduces eye attacks, but it can cause or worsen neurologic disease and therefore should be avoided in patients with known neuro‑Behçet’s.

- Interferon‑alpha has demonstrated benefit in ocular Behçet’s and can reduce relapses; it is an option in some centers for refractory ocular disease.

- Cyclophosphamide or other potent cytotoxic agents are reserved for life‑ or organ‑threatening disease (severe vascular, major CNS involvement) or when biologics are contraindicated; they are not routine long‑term maintenance drugs because of toxicity.

- Colchicine is useful for mucocutaneous and some articular manifestations but does not reliably prevent neuro‑ or severe ocular relapses and should not be relied on as sole maintenance therapy for neuro‑ocular disease.

Clinical reasoning: because neuro‑ and ocular involvement cause irreversible structural damage (brainstem lesions, optic nerve/retinal injury) and relapses commonly produce cumulative deficits, the therapeutic goal is durable suppression of pathogenic inflammation. That requires a glucocorticoid‑sparing maintenance agent started after induction control—azathioprine is a common first‑line choice, anti‑TNF agents for refractory or severe presentations, and avoidance of agents (e.g., cyclosporine) that may worsen other involved organs. Therapy must be individualized to severity, prior response, comorbidities and infection risk, and patients require long‑term monitoring for relapse, drug toxicity, and complications of chronic immunosuppression.
