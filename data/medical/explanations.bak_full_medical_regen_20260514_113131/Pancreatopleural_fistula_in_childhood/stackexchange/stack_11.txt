Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Clinical Pearls] A child has recurrent hemorrhagic pleural effusion, mildly elevated pleural fluid ADA, negative GeneXpert for TB, and very high pleural fluid amylase. How should you interpret the ADA result, and what diagnosis should be prioritized?

Think of ADA as a screening clue, not a diagnosis. Pleural ADA above the usual cut-off (commonly ~40 U/L) increases the probability of tuberculous pleuritis, but mild-to-moderate elevations are nonspecific and occur with many pleural processes — empyema/parapneumonic effusion, malignancy, haemorrhagic effusions, and any intense pleural inflammation can raise ADA. GeneXpert-negative testing and a haemorrhagic, recurrent effusion argue against straightforward tuberculous pleuritis here.

In this child the dominant abnormality is an extremely high pleural fluid amylase with imaging showing a collection tracking from the pancreas into the chest. That constellation — recurrent, massive blood‑stained effusion, very high pleural amylase, history of blunt abdominal trauma, and MRCP/CT evidence of a tract — points to a pancreatopleural fistula. Prioritize pancreatic etiology and urgent pancreatic imaging/management rather than empiric antitubercular therapy. In practice, stop chasing TB as the primary cause, obtain (or rely on) amylase/lipase and cross‑sectional imaging (MRCP/CT), and direct therapy toward pancreatic rest, drainage, somatostatin analogues ± endoscopic/surgical repair.
