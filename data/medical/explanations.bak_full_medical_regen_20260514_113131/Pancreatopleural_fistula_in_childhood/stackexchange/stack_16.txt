Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Management] List two surgical options for refractory pancreatopleural fistula and indicate the clinical scenarios in which each is preferred.

Two reasonable operative strategies for refractory pancreatopleural fistula are distal pancreatectomy (± splenectomy) and lateral pancreaticojejunostomy (Puestow) — choose by the ductal anatomy and underlying pancreatic disease.

If imaging/ERCP shows a discrete disruption or persistent leak arising from the distal gland or tail (often with a pseudocyst tracking into the chest) the preferred operation is distal pancreatectomy with drainage of the retroperitoneal collection. Resection removes the damaged segment that is the source of the fistula and is favored when the leak is localized, when there is a large infected or symptomatic pseudocyst in the tail, or when endoscopic stenting cannot bridge the site. In children attempt splenic preservation when feasible to reduce long‑term immunologic risk.

If the patient has chronic pancreatitis with a dilated main pancreatic duct and multiple strictures or diffuse ductal disruption (a “leaky pipe” without a single distal source), a lateral pancreaticojejunostomy (Puestow) is preferred. This decompressive drainage procedure lowers intraductal pressure, allows internal drainage of pancreatic secretions, and treats multiple downstream leaks or strictures when transpapillary stenting has failed or is not feasible.

Practical points: attempt endoscopic stent therapy first when anatomy permits. Reserve distal resection for a localized distal leak/pseudocyst and Puestow for diffuse ductal dilatation/strictures. In all children weigh endocrine/exocrine loss and try spleen‑preserving techniques and parenchymal‑sparing operations when oncologically and technically appropriate.
