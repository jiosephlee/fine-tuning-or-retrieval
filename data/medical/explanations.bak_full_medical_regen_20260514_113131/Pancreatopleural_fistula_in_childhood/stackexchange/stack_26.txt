Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Management] In a child with PPF and evidence of pancreatic ductal narrowing proximal to a leak on MRCP, what is the rationale for transpapillary stent placement across the papilla and, if possible, across the disruption?

When MRCP shows a ductal narrowing just proximal to a leak, the endoscopic strategy is driven by simple hydrodynamics and wound-healing physiology: you want to lower upstream intraductal pressure and provide a low‑resistance route for pancreatic juice into the gut so that the fistula no longer pours into the mediastinum/pleura and the disrupted duct can epithelialize and heal.

Placing a transpapillary plastic stent across the papilla (and, when feasible, across the site of disruption) achieves that by converting the pancreas from a high‑pressure, leaking ductal system into one that drains preferentially into the duodenum. Crossing a proximal stricture with the stent does two additional things: it dilates the narrowed segment (reducing the obstruction that is driving the leak), and it effectively “bridges” the disruption so that secretions bypass the extravasation site, limiting further enzymatic digestion of surrounding tissues and allowing the disruption to close. In short, stenting addresses the cause (ductal obstruction and pressure gradient) rather than just the symptom (pleural fluid).

Clinically, this translates into higher rates of fistula closure and faster resolution of pleural collections compared with continued conservative therapy alone. The technical aim is to place the largest-caliber stent the duct will accept, across the papilla and, if possible, with the distal end beyond the disruption; stent duration is usually weeks to months with serial reassessment. Be mindful of procedural risks (pancreatitis, stent occlusion/migration, infection) and of special considerations in children (smaller ducts, need for pediatric endoscopy expertise). In a child like ours—ductal narrowing proximal to the leak who failed or is unlikely to respond to conservative measures—transpapillary stenting is a physiologically rational, minimally invasive next step to decompress the system, bridge the leak, and promote healing while avoiding or postponing surgery.
