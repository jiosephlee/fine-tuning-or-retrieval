Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Morning Report] A school-aged child presents with recurrent right-sided pleural effusions that reaccumulate after thoracentesis and a recent history of blunt abdominal trauma. Which two imaging modalities, in order, best define the diagnosis and delineate ductal anatomy for potential therapy?

Think of this as a two‑step problem: first find the pancreatic source of the chest problem, then define the pancreatic duct anatomy if you are planning definitive (endoscopic) therapy.

A contrast‑enhanced CT (chest/abdomen) is the best initial study — it rapidly demonstrates the pleural collection, mediastinal/retroperitoneal tracking and any pancreatic pseudocyst or duct disruption that points to a pancreatopleural fistula. Once CT raises the diagnosis and you have biochemical support (very high pleural amylase), proceed to ERCP to map the duct precisely and allow simultaneous therapy. ERCP is the gold‑standard for delineating ductal anatomy and enables transpapillary stent placement or sphincter therapy to reduce ductal pressure and close the fistula.

MRCP is a useful noninvasive alternative to ERCP for duct visualization (helpful when ERCP is unavailable or high risk) and often confirms the fistula, but ERCP remains preferred when planning therapeutic intervention.
