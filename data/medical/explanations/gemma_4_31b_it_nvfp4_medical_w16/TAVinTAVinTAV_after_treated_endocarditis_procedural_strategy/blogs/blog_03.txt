Title: Blog about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

# The Coronary Maze: Preserving Access in a Triple-Stented Aorta

Imagine your patient's aortic root as a Russian nesting doll, but instead of wooden figures, you have three overlapping layers of cobalt-chromium and nitinol. Now imagine that this patient also has three-vessel coronary artery disease. This is the anatomical nightmare of the "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV" procedure.

When we perform a TAVI, we often talk about the "neo-skirt"—the excess tissue and displaced leaflets that bunch up at the base of the valve. In a single TAVI, the neo-skirt is a known risk for coronary obstruction. But in a triple-stented aorta, we aren't just dealing with one skirt; we are dealing with a cumulative wall of metal and displaced bioprosthetic tissue that can effectively "wall off" the coronary ostia, turning a future PCI into an impossible mission.

## The Case: A Decade of Valve Iterations

The patient was a man in his late 70s with a complex cardiac history: 3-vessel CAD, COPD, and a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%. His journey began in 2014 with a 27mm Lotus valve. By 2022, he developed severe bioprosthetic stenosis and received a TAV-in-TAV with a 23mm Evolut. Shortly after, he hit a major complication: *Enterococcus faecalis* prosthetic valve endocarditis.

While the infection was successfully treated with six weeks of targeted antibiotics, the aftermath was a disaster for his hemodynamics. By 2024, he presented with severe restenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. His aortic valve area (AVA) had plummeted to 0.5 $\text{cm}^2$. With surgical explantation essentially off the table due to the depth of stent integration into the aortic wall and his high comorbid burden, the team opted for a third transcatheter intervention.

## Navigating the Maze: The Strategy

The goal for the third valve (an Acurate neo2) wasn't just to restore flow, but to do so without obstructing the coronaries or permanently sealing them off from future access. The team relied on three critical pillars:

**1. The MSCT Roadmap**
Multislice CT (MSCT) is not optional here; it is the blueprint. The team used MSCT to map the landing zone, measuring the minimum lumen diameter (which was only 13.7mm in the Evolut prosthesis) and calculating the exact distance from the anticipated neo-skirt to the coronary ostia. This allowed them to plan the implantation depth with millimeter precision.

**2. High Implantation Strategy**
A common mistake in TAV-in-TAV is implanting too deep, which pushes the "skirt" of the new valve directly across the coronary ostia. In this case, the team utilized a high implantation strategy. By deploying the Acurate neo2 higher up, they pinned the previous Evolut leaflets against the stent frame. This served two purposes: it prevented the old leaflets from folding inward (which would further reduce the effective orifice area) and it kept the neo-skirt clear of the coronary openings.

**3. Commissural Alignment**
In a triple-stented aorta, the "mesh burden" is immense. If the commissures of the three valves aren't aligned, you create a chaotic web of metal struts that can make it impossible to engage a coronary artery with a guidewire. The team used CT modeling and fluoroscopic landmarks to align the Acurate neo2 commissures with the previous frames, consciously preserving a "window" for future PCI.

## The Result: Hemodynamic Recovery

The immediate post-procedure TTE showed a high mean gradient (67 mmHg) and an AVA of 0.9 $\text{cm}^2$, suggesting significant patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). However, as is often seen with self-expanding valves and LV remodeling, the hemodynamics improved. At one year, the patient remained asymptomatic with a mean gradient of 26 mmHg and an EF of 65%.

## Clinical Pearls and Take-Home Points

*   **The "Metal Burden" is Real:** Every subsequent TAV-in-TAV increases the risk of coronary obstruction and makes future coronary access exponentially harder.
*   **High Implantation = Coronary Safety:** In redo-TAVI, aiming for a higher deployment helps prevent the neo-skirt from covering the ostia and minimizes the inward folding of previous leaflets.
*   **CT is the Compass:** Precise measurements of the landing zone and coronary height via MSCT are mandatory for navigating triple-stented anatomy.
*   **Lifetime Management:** We are entering an era where we must plan the *third* valve at the time of the *first*. Commissural alignment isn't just a technical preference; it is a requirement for future viability in patients with known CAD.
*   **PPM may evolve:** Initial post-dilation and LV remodeling can improve the effective orifice area over the first few months following a complex TAV-in-TAV.
