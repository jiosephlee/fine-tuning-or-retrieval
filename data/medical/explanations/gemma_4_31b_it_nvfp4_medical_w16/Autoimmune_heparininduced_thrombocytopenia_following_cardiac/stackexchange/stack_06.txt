Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] High-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) was administered to this patient. What is the therapeutic rationale for using IVIg in aHIT, and how does the platelet response typically differ between aHIT and classic HIT?

In autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (aHIT), the therapeutic rationale for administering high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) is to block the platelet Fc$\gamma$ receptors. Unlike classic HIT, where antibodies typically require heparin to bind to platelet factor 4 (PF4) and activate platelets, aHIT is characterized by antibodies with heparin-independent platelet-activating properties. These antibodies continue to trigger platelet activation and consumption even after all heparin sources have been discontinued. IVIg interferes with this process by saturating the Fc receptors on the platelet surface, thereby preventing the aHIT antibodies from inducing platelet activation and subsequent thrombocytopenia.

The platelet response to IVIg differs significantly between aHIT and classic HIT:

*   **Classic HIT:** Platelet recovery is typically slow, occurring over one to two weeks as heparin is cleared from the system and antibody titers naturally decline. IVIg is not standard therapy for classic HIT as the primary driver (heparin) is removable.
*   **aHIT:** A hallmark of aHIT is a "brisk" or rapid increase in the platelet count following the administration of high-dose IVIg. This rapid recovery is often used as a diagnostic clue to distinguish aHIT from classic HIT.

In the presented case, the patient exhibited a particularly severe phenotype of aHIT. While he did receive high-dose IVIg (300g total), he showed only a modest increase in platelet count (from a nadir of 13 $\times 10^9$/L to an interim peak of 54 $\times 10^9$/L). This blunted response, coupled with a serotonin-release assay (SRA) showing only partial inhibition by Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibodies, underscores the unusually reactive nature of this patient's antibodies and the severity of his autoimmune process.
