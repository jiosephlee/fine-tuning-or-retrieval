Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] Dermatomyositis is characterized by several pathognomonic cutaneous findings. Beyond the classic heliotrope rash and Gottron's papules, what is the 'Holster sign,' and what is the significance of nailfold capillaroscopy in these patients?

The **holster sign** is a cutaneous manifestation of dermatomyositis characterized by macular erythema located on the lateral aspect of the thighs, mirroring the position where a handgun holster would typically rest. In this case, although the patient lacked the classic heliotrope rash and Gottron’s papules, the discovery of a rash on the right lateral thigh was a critical clue that pointed toward a diagnosis of dermatomyositis.

**Nailfold capillaroscopy** is a non-invasive diagnostic technique used to visualize the capillaries at the nail fold. In patients with dermatomyositis, this tool is significant because it reveals the underlying systemic microangiopathy that characterizes the disease. Key findings include:
*   **Periungual telangiectasias:** Dilated, enlarged capillaries.
*   **Microhemorrhages:** Small areas of bleeding within the nail fold.
*   **Avascular areas:** Regions where capillaries have dropped out, leaving "ghost" vessels or empty spaces.

In the presented case, the finding of proximal nailfold microhemorrhages and avascular areas on capillaroscopy provided early objective evidence of microvascular damage, which—alongside the elevated creatine kinase and the holster sign—allowed the clinical team to look beyond the initial presentation of "pseudoangioedema" and correctly diagnose SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis.
