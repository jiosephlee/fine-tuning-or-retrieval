Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] A middle-aged patient presents with progressive, non-tender neck swelling and facial erythema. After excluding deep neck space infections and typical bradykinin- or histamine-mediated angioedema, what autoimmune inflammatory condition should be considered, and what clinical 'clues' in this case suggested a systemic rather than a localized process?

In the clinical scenario of progressive, non-tender neck swelling and facial erythema where infection and classic angioedema (histaminergic or bradykinin-mediated) have been excluded, clinicians should consider **dermatomyositis** as a critical differential diagnosis. While facial and neck edema—often termed "pseudoangioedema"—is an uncommon presentation of dermatomyositis, it can masquerade as a primary otolaryngologic disorder or an idiopathic angioedema.

In this case, several key clinical "clues" shifted the diagnostic focus from a localized neck process to a systemic autoimmune inflammatory condition:

*   **Neuromuscular Findings:** The patient presented with proximal muscle weakness (specifically in the hip flexors) and mild myalgias. This systemic distribution of weakness is a hallmark of inflammatory myopathies and is inconsistent with a localized neck infection or isolated angioedema.
*   **Cutaneous and Ungual Markers:** While the classic heliotrope rash and Gottron’s papules were absent, the patient exhibited generalized facial erythema (including the nasolabial folds), a rash on the lateral thigh (the "holster sign"), and periungual erythema. Notably, nailfold capillaroscopy revealed microhemorrhages and avascular areas, which are highly suggestive of a systemic connective tissue disease.
*   **Laboratory Evidence of Myonecrosis:** The discovery of a profoundly elevated creatine kinase (CK) level (16,184 U/L), along with elevated aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), provided objective evidence of widespread muscle inflammation and damage.
*   **Multisystem Involvement:** The presence of severe pharyngeal dysphagia and an extrathoracic restrictive pattern on pulmonary function tests pointed toward a systemic process affecting the striated muscles of the upper esophagus and the pulmonary parenchyma.

The pathophysiology of the neck swelling in this patient was not due to vasoactive mediators but rather reflected severe inflammatory edema arising from myositis of the strap muscles and surrounding soft tissues. This case underscores the importance of a comprehensive physical examination—specifically checking for proximal weakness and periungual changes—and the utility of a CK level when "angioedema" presents with atypical features.
