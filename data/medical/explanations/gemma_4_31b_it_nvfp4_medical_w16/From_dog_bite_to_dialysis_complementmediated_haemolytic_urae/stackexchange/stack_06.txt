Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] What is the mechanism of action of eculizumab, and why is it imperative to provide meningococcal vaccination and antimicrobial prophylaxis prior to initiating this therapy?

Eculizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to complement component C5, inhibiting its cleavage into C5a and C5b. By blocking this terminal step of the complement cascade, eculizumab prevents the formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC; C5b-9), which is the final effector mechanism responsible for cell lysis. In this patient, who developed complement-mediated hemolytic uremic syndrome (CM-HUS), this blockade was essential to halt the dysregulated activation of the alternative pathway that caused systemic endothelial damage and subsequent thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA).

The requirement for meningococcal vaccination and antimicrobial prophylaxis stems from the physiological role of the terminal complement pathway in host defense. The MAC is critical for the lysis of encapsulated bacteria, particularly *Neisseria meningitidis*. By inhibiting C5, eculizumab creates a state of functional complement deficiency, significantly increasing the patient's susceptibility to fulminant meningococcal disease.

This risk is particularly salient in this case, as the patient had recently suffered sepsis from *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*, another encapsulated gram-negative rod that relies on complement susceptibility for clearance. To mitigate this risk, the patient received vaccines covering all major serogroups (Menveo for A, C, Y, and W; Bexsero for B). Because the vaccine-induced immune response is not immediate, and this patient’s therapy was initiated before the recommended two-week post-vaccination window, prophylactic antimicrobial therapy with phenoxymethylpenicillin (Apocillin) was required to ensure patient safety during the transition to full immunization.
