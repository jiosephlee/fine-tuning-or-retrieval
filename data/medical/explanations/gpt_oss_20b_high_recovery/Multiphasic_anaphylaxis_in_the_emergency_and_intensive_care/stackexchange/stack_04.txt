Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] According to current adult anaphylaxis guidelines, what is the recommended epinephrine dose based on body weight, and how does this compare to the dose delivered by a 0.3‑mg auto‑injector commonly used in the UK?

**Epinephrine dosing in adult anaphylaxis**

**Guideline recommendation**  
Current adult anaphylaxis guidelines (e.g., EAACI 2022, AAAAI 2023) prescribe *0.01 mg /kg body weight* of intramuscular epinephrine, with an upper limit of **0.5 mg per dose**. In practice, this is often simplified to a flat 0.5 mg shot for any adult who weighs >50 kg.  

**What the UK 0.3 mg auto‑injector delivers**  
The standard UK auto‑injector contains 0.3 mg (300 µg) of epinephrine, which is the dose used in the early episodes of the case reported by Schneider et al.  
- For the 104‑kg patient in the report, the weight‑based dose would be 1.04 mg (0.01 mg × 104 kg), but the guideline cap limits the dose to 0.5 mg.  
- The 0.3‑mg auto‑injector therefore provides **60 % of the guideline‑maximum dose** and **~30 % of the weight‑based target**.  

**Clinical implications**  
The patient required four total injections – the first two from the 0.3‑mg auto‑injectors, followed by two 0.5‑mg intramuscular doses – illustrating that the UK auto‑injector under‑dosed the initial treatment and led to repeated rescue doses. A 2025 retrospective analysis showed higher rates of escalation of care among patients who received the 0.3‑mg dose first, underscoring the importance of delivering the guideline‑recommended 0.5‑mg dose for adults >50 kg.

**Bottom line**  
For adults who weigh >50 kg, aim for 0.5 mg intramuscular epinephrine (0.01 mg/kg up to the 0.5‑mg ceiling). The 0.3‑mg auto‑injector used in the UK is lower than recommended and may necessitate additional doses, as demonstrated in the presented case.
