Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Morning Report] Based on the UK anaphylaxis guidelines, which clinical features confirm anaphylaxis in this patient, and why does the pattern of symptom recurrence after initial resolution qualify as multiphasic rather than a sustained reaction?

**Clinical Pearls – UK Anaphylaxis Guidelines**

**1. Features that confirm anaphylaxis in this patient**

The 2021 Resuscitation Council UK guidance defines anaphylaxis as a sudden‑onset, potentially life‑threatening reaction involving two or more organ systems **or** a single system so severe as to require emergency treatment. In this case:

| Organ system | Clinical feature (UK 2021 criteria) | Evidence from the case |
|--------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------|
| **Skin / mucosa** | Urticaria, flushing, pruritus, generalized erythema, facial swelling | “Generalised erythema and urticaria over her entire body; pruritus and flushing” |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension | Non‑invasive BP 74/50 mm Hg (initial ED vitals) |
| **Respiratory** | Hypoxaemia / respiratory distress | SpO₂ 88 % on room air; RR 22 /min; dyspnoea and increased transpiration |
| **Airway / oropharynx** | (Not required – airway was spared) | No tongue or throat swelling |

The patient demonstrated three simultaneously involved systems (cutaneous, cardiovascular, respiratory) with *severe* hypotension, satisfying the UK definition of anaphylaxis. The rapid response to intramuscular epinephrine within minutes also aligns with the recommended therapeutic approach.

**2. Why the recurrence pattern is multiphasic, not a sustained reaction**

A *sustained* reaction would entail continuous, unresolved symptoms after initial treatment. The UK guidance distinguishes this from a *biphasic* (or *multiphasic*) reaction, defined as:

> **“A recurrence of anaphylaxis‑related symptoms after an apparent clinical resolution, occurring within 24 h of the first episode and without re‑exposure to the allergen.”**

A *multiphasic* reaction is simply a biphasic reaction that has recurred more than once. In this patient:

- **Initial episode** – severe hypotension, hypoxaemia, urticaria; resolved after one 0.3 mg IM epinephrine and supportive care.
- **First recurrence** (within ~6 h) – required a second epinephrine dose; fully resolved again.
- **Second recurrence** (within ~8 h) – third epinephrine dose; resolved.
- **Third recurrence** (within ~10 h) – fourth epinephrine dose; resolved.

Between each episode, the patient experienced full clinical remission (normal BP, resolution of rash and breathing difficulty) and received no further clarithromycin exposure. The pattern therefore meets the UK definition of *multiphasic* anaphylaxis (at least three distinct relapses after resolution).

**Key take‑away**

- **Confirmation of anaphylaxis** was achieved by simultaneous cutaneous, cardiovascular, and respiratory involvement, per UK guidelines.
- **Multiphasic pattern** is evident because symptoms recurred multiple times after complete resolution, rather than continuing without interruption, distinguishing it from a sustained reaction.
