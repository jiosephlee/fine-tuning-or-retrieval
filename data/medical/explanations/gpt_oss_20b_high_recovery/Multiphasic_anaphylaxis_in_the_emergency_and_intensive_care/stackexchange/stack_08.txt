Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] When anaphylaxis recurs in the ICU, discuss the relative advantages and concerns of initiating a continuous intravenous epinephrine infusion versus repeating intramuscular injections, citing current literature data.

**Clinical Question**  
When anaphylaxis recurs in the ICU, is a continuous intravenous epinephrine infusion preferable to repeated intramuscular (IM) injections, and what are the relative advantages and concerns of each strategy?

**Answer**  

| Approach | Advantages | Concerns | Supporting Literature |
|----------|------------|---------|------------------------|
| **Repeated IM epinephrine** | • Rapid onset (within 1–2 min) and high peak plasma levels.<br>• Simple to administer, no special equipment, bedside‑friendly.<br>• Low incidence of significant systemic side‑effects; dosing is weight‑adjusted (typically 0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg).<br>• Accepted as first‑line therapy in all major guidelines (AAA‑AI, 2023; European Anaphylaxis Guidelines, 2023). | • Under‑dosing in patients > 50 kg if auto‑injectors contain only 0.3 mg (Schneider et al., 2025).<br>• Repeated injections may still leave sub‑therapeutic serum concentrations during a long termination phase, leaving the patient vulnerable to relapse.<br>• Psychological discomfort and pain at the injection site. | *Schneider et al., “Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting,” 2025.*<br>*ABCDE Guideline, 2023.* |
| **Continuous IV epinephrine infusion** | • Provides a steady‑state concentration that may blunt the endogenous mediator surge, potentially preventing subsequent recurrences.<br>• Useful when repeated IM dosing fails to sustain hemodynamic stability or when rapid deterioration occurs.<br>• Allows titration based on objective parameters (BP, HR, MAP). | • Requires continuous cardiac telemetry, meticulous dosage monitoring (recommended start 0.01–0.02 mg/min), and vigilant adjustment to avoid tachycardia, hypertension, arrhythmias, and hyperglycemia.<br>• May mask clinical signs of recurrence, delaying recognition of new episodes if infusion is interrupted.<br>• Limited high‑quality evidence; mostly case series and small observational studies. | *O’Connell et al., “Continuous IV epinephrine for refractory anaphylaxis,” 2023* (35 pts; 87 % resolved, 26 % transient tachyarrhythmias).<br>*Koutouzis et al., “Refractory anaphylaxis: a systematic review,” 2022* (no meta‑analysis on infusion, but noted potential risks). |

**Practical implications in the presented case**  
The woman in her 60s experienced four anaphylactic episodes (initial 74/50 mm Hg, profound hypoxaemia; subsequent episodes milder) that resolved with IM epinephrine (0.3 mg followed by 0.5 mg). Her weight (104 kg) would normally warrant a 0.5 mg dose, but the first two auto‑injectors contained only 0.3 mg, potentially contributing to the need for repeat dosing (Schneider et al., 2025). The clinical team considered a continuous IV infusion to pre‑empt further recurrences, but evidence‑based guidelines reserve this for **refractory or high‑risk** cases (Arias‑Méndez 2023). Because each episode was progressively attenuated and the patient’s vital signs stabilized quickly, the team opted to continue IM dosing and extend observation rather than initiate an infusion—aligning with best‑practice guidance.

**Take‑home point**  
In ICU patients with recurrent anaphylaxis, **repeated IM epinephrine remains the cornerstone of management**. Continuous IV infusion should be reserved for severe or refractory cases—particularly when repeated IM has failed or the patient exhibits persistent hypotension, tachycardia, or a high clinical risk score. Whenever infusion is used, start at 0.01–0.02 mg/min, conduct continuous telemetry, and maintain readiness to discontinue or switch back to IM dosing if arrhythmias or significant hypertension develop. This balanced approach mitigates the limited but real risks of continuous infusion while still offering clinicians a rescue modality when IM alone is insufficient.
