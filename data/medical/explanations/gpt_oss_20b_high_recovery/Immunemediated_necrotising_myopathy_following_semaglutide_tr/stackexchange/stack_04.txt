Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What findings drive clinicians toward diagnosing drug-induced immune mediated necrotising myopathy instead of statin toxicity related rhabdomyolysis,
inclusion body myositis or viral myocarditis?

**Clinical Pearls – Immunologic vs Non‑immunologic Myopathies**

**What points tip a clinician toward drug‑induced immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM)**  

| Feature | Typical Presentation | Why it favors **IMNM** |
|---------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Onset & Course** | Gradual proximal flaccid weakness over weeks‐months even after stopping pill | **Drug‑induced IMNM** often lags behind exposure because an autoimmune cascade takes time to evolve (“immune priming”). In the reported case symptoms grew unmistakably **four months** after the single semaglutide dose – far too slow for pure toxic injury that appears acutely (usually <1 week). |
| **Creatine kinase trajectory** | Marked rise (>10× ULN), plateauing at several thousand IU/L persisting beyond 6 weeks | Steroid‐independent stability is characteristic of an antibody‐mediated process—the patient’s peak ‑≈ 4 000 IU/L remained high despite semaglutide withdrawal until immunosuppression was begun. Rhabdomyolysis peaks are astronomically higher (>20 000 IU/L) initially and auto‑resorb within days once the inciting agent stops. |
| **Autoantibody panel** | Negative anti‑HMGCR/srp despite being seronegative | Up to one third of IMNM patients lack known antibodies yet still meet all other triage criteria—seronegativity does *not* exclude diagnosis when pathology is conclusive (see below). |
| **Muscle Biopsy** | Extensive fibre necrosis + macrophage phagocytosis + complement C5b9 deposition; *few* CD3⁺ T cells | “Necrotising” pathology is archetypal for IMNM versus constructive polymyositis (dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate). Rimmed vacuoles/cytoplasmic inclusions required for inclusion‑body myositis were absent. |
| **Cardiac Findings** | Mild myocardial oedema without late gadolinium enhancement; troponins modestly raised | Viral myocarditis usually shows pronounced troponinemia ≥ 200 pg/mL plus classic LGE patterns on CMR—absent here—in favour of skeletal‐muscle pathology alone. |
| **Response to Therapy** | Rapid improvement after pulse steroids ± IVIG while maintaining near‑normal renal/hepatic function thereafter | Autoimmune disease recovers promptly once immunomodulation begins whereas pure toxic rhabdomyolysis resolves with hydration alone without steroids/IVIG efficacy expected beyond supportive care. |

---

### How These Findings Exclude Other Conditions

1. **Statin Toxicity / Rhabdomyolysis**  
   *Typical*: Acute onset within days/weeks → extremely high peak CK (>50 000 IU/L).  
   *Decline*: Rapid reduction post‑withdrawal & aggressive rehydration within days → no persistent neuro deficits once first peak passes through kidneys’ light bulb energy drain pathway deck presented-- whereas this patient’s weakness continued relentlessly until immunosuppression began.*

2. **Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)**  
   *Typical*: Chronic fatigue & wasting spanning years → distal arm/finger flexor loss early ‑ rarely bulbar strength deterioration alone.*  
   *Biopsy*: Rimmed vacuoles + Congo red amyloid inclusions → profoundly different from macrophage ‘smooth’ phagocytosis seen in our patient.*

3. **Viral Myocarditis**  
   *Typical*: Chest pain/arrhythmias ± shortness→ basketball driven.<br> • Troponin usually rises sharply (>200 pg/mL).<br> • Cardiac MRI demonstrates patchy late gadolinium enhancement indicating myocardial fibrosis.\n\n*This patient’s CMR only showed transient oedema without fibrosis,* clarifying that primary injury remained confined to skeletal musculature.*

### Summary

In lamellate terms:

* Progressive proximal tetany over weeks (not an “acute crash”)
* Sustainedly high creatine kinase (>10× ULN beyond 6 weeks)
* Seronegative yet imaging/biopsy consistent “necrotic” phenotype
* Mild cardiac stray indicators without molecular hallmark lesions

When these elements align—as illustrated by Lam et al.’s semaglutide cohort—they jointly compel a clinician toward enzymatically active yet **drug induced**, *immune‐mediated*, *necrotising* lifelong favouring approach rather than transient toxicity or classic dystrophic processes like IBM or solely cardiac myocarditic change.*
