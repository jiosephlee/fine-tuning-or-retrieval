Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the cellular cascade—from antibody binding through complement activation—to precipitate macrophage-mediated ‘myofiber phagocytosis’ characteristic of seronegative immune mediated necrotising myopathy.

**Cell‑level cascade underlying macrophage‑mediated myofiber phagocytosis in seronegative IMNM**

1. **Muscle‑cell exposure of cryptic neo‑epitopes** – In the setting of semaglutide exposure (or another inciting trigger) muscle sarcolemmal proteins become aberrantly expressed or modified so that previously hidden epitopes are exposed or are presented in a pro‑inflammatory context (e.g., up‑regulated MHC class I).

2. **Binding of pathogenic IgG** – Autoantibodies—undetectable by routine panels (the patient was anti‑HMGCR‑negative and anti‑SRP‐negative)—recognise these altered epitopes and bind their Fc regions on damaged fibers. The intense local IgG deposition provides a scaffold for downstream effector pathways even if circulating levels fall below assay sensitivity.

3. **Classical complement activation** – IgG–Fc binds C1q → recruitment/activation of C1r/C1s → cleavage of C4 and C2 → formation of the classical convertase (C4b2a). This complex cleaves circulating C3 into C3a + C3b; surface‐adsorbed C3b attaches back onto myofibers giving an opsonic “coat” that is then amplified by alternative pathway components.

4. **Complement amplification & MAC generation** – Further cleavage yields additional convertases (C4b2a → C4b2a2b). This generates terminal factors that assemble into the membrane attack complex (C5b‐9), which deposits along damaged sarcolemmal membranes—explaining the positive MAC staining seen in this biopsy.

5. **Inflammatory mediator release** – Anaphylatoxins C3a/C5a recruit and activate innate cells, particularly MAC‑positive neutrophils and resident monocytes migrating into muscle interstitium via chemokine gradients such as MCP‑1/CCL2.

6. **Macrophage recruitment and activation** – Recruited CD68+ monocyte‐derived macrophages sense opsonized tissue fragments via CR3/CD11b×CD18 binding to iC3b/C3bi, while Fcγ receptors engage immune complexes directly released from injured fibers.

7. **Myophagocytosis** – Activated macrophages engulf entire necrotic or partially intact fibers (“myophagocytosis”), a phenomenon highlighted by acid phosphatase positivity surrounding affected units on histology—a hallmark signature identified in the patient’s muscle core section with concentric lysosomal activity around torn fibers.

8. **Secondary inflammatory amplification** – Phagocytosed material further stimulates interferon pathways within macrophages, perpetuating local tissue injury despite absence of visible lymphocytic infiltrates—a pattern that distinguishes immuno‑mediated necrotising myopathy from polymyositis or inclusion body myositis.

In summary, although routine serum autoantibody assays can be negative in seronegative IMNM—as occurred here—the underlying mechanism involves IgG-directed classical complement initiation culminating in MAC deposition, which both directly injures fibers and flags them for clearance by phagocytic macrophages—an effector loop perpetuating necrosis and regeneration observed clinically throughout this case.’
