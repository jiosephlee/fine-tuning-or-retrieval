Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] In patients who develop proximal limb-girdle weakness several weeks after initiating a GLP-1 receptor agonist yet lack detectable anti-HMGCR or anti-SRP antibodies how should empiric treatment typically be started?

**Empiric management of seronegative immune‑mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) triggered by a GLP‑1 receptor agonist**

When a patient presents with progressive proximal limb‑girdle weakness that emerges several weeks after starting a GLP‑1 RA and tests negative for anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP antibodies—as in the semaglutide case reported by Lam et al.—treat as drug‑induced IMNM with high‑dose corticosteroids and consider early addition of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG).

1. **Discontinue the GLP‑1 RA** immediately upon suspicion of myopathy.  
2. **Initiate high‑dose glucocorticoids**:
   * **Intravenous methylprednisolone 1 g once daily** for 3–5 days (pulse therapy), *or*  
   * **Oral prednisone at ≥ 1 mg/kg/day** if IV access is not feasible.  
3. **Add IVIG if**  
   * Creatine kinase remains > 5–10× upper limit of normal, **or**  
   * Dysphagia/dysphonia or respiratory compromise are present—clinical hallmarks of severe IMNM—as occurred in the Lam et al case where IVIG (2 g/kg over four days) was required following pulse steroids.  
4. **Taper steroids over several weeks**, monitoring CK and strength weekly until normalization; maintain low dose prednisolone (> 30 mg/day) during transition off therapy if rapid improvement is seen, mirroring the successful tapering described in the report.

This algorithm—pulse steroids followed by IVIG in selected severe cases—mirrors the empiric regimen that led to full functional recovery in the referenced patient, demonstrating efficacy even when classic myositis autoantibodies are absent.
