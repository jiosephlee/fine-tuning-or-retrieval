Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Which laboratory result most effectively indicates that an elevated creatine kinase reflects muscular injury rather than liver damage?

**Key laboratory cue**  
A **markedly elevated serum creatine kinase (CK)**—often several thousand units per liter—coupled with **only mild or no elevation of the transaminases** (AST/ALT) signals skeletal‑muscle injury rather than hepatocellular damage.

**Why this works**  
- **CK** is essentially restricted to striated muscle and rises dramatically (> 10–15 × upper limit normal) when myocytes are damaged.  
- The **transaminases** are present in both liver and skeletal‑muscle tissue, but in a hepatic insult they rise proportionally more (often ≥ 5–10 × ULN), whereas in isolated myopathy they are usually only modestly increased or stay near normal.
- In our patient the CK peaked at **4 000 U/L** while **AST was 191 U/L and ALT 165 U/L**—only ~twofold over upper limits—an excellent example of a “CK‑dominant” pattern characteristic of immune‐mediated necrotising myopathy.

Thus, when faced with hyper‑CKemia, evaluate its ratio to serum transaminases; a large disconnect (high CK with mildly raised/aspectively unchanged AST/ALT) most reliably points to muscular origin rather than liver damage.
