Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Morning Report] When proximal weakness evolves roughly four months after a single dose of semaglutide—with serial creatine kinase rising modestly initially then exceeding four thousand IU/L—what does this time course argue favor an autoimmune process rather than an acute toxic injury?

**Answer**

The 4‑month lag between the single semaglutide injection and the dramatic rise in creatine kinase (CK) together with insidious proximal weakness strongly favors an autoimmune necrotising myopathy rather than an acute drug‑toxicity picture.  

1. **Temporal pattern** – Acute toxic myopathies (e.g., statin‑induced rhabdomyolysis or tirzepatide‑associated rhabdomyolysis) typically present with a sudden spike in CK (often >10 000 IU/L) within hours or days of exposure, and CK falls rapidly once the offending agent is removed. In this case, CK was only mildly elevated (337 U/L) at the time of first semaglutide dose, and climbed slowly over 4 months to >4 000 U/L—consistent with a gradual immune response rather than immediate cell lysis.

2. **Clinical course** – The patient developed progressive proximal weakness and dysphagia over several weeks, not an abrupt loss of strength typical of toxin‑induced myonecrosis. The delayed nature of symptom onset mirrors that seen in drug‑triggered immune‑mediated necrotising myopathies where an initial trigger precipitates autoreactive T‑cell/B‑cell activation that takes days to weeks to produce muscle destruction.

3. **Biopsy findings** – Muscle histology revealed necrotic/regenerating fibres with macrophage infiltrates and rare T‑cells—a classic picture of IMNM rather than pure toxic myonecrosis, which would show diffuse fibre necrosis without organized inflammatory infiltrates.

4. **Response to immunosuppression** – Rapid clinical and biochemical recovery after high‑dose steroids plus intravenous immunoglobulin is characteristic of an immune mediated process; toxic injuries generally improve simply by stopping the drug or providing supportive care without such intensive therapy.

5. **Contrast with reported tirzepatide rhabdomyolysis** – In that case, CK spiked immediately to >57 000 U/L and normalized within days of withdrawal, highlighting that classic drug toxicity has a different cadence than the chronic rise seen here.

Thus, the protracted progression from modest CK elevation to severe disease over months argues for an autoimmune mechanism—an immune response triggered (but not solely caused) by semaglutide—rather than a straightforward acute toxic injury.
