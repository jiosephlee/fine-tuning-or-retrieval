Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] In patients undergoing cardiac surgery, the classic onset of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) is usually between postoperative days 5 and 10. What factors or exposures can precipitate an earlier onset (<5 days) and why might preoperative heparin use be a risk factor?

**Key Point**

Early‑onset (< 5 days) HIT after cardiac surgery is most frequently precipitated by prior heparin exposure—especially pre‑operative heparin given for procedures such as coronary angiography or catheterisation. Pre‑operative heparin primes the immune system; circulating anti‑PF4/heparin IgG (or heparin‑independent “autoimmune” antibodies) are already present and react immediately when heparin is re‑administered during cardiopulmonary bypass. This bypasses the usual 5‑10‑day latency and produces a rapid platelet fall, as seen in the case described.

**Why pre‑operative heparin is a risk factor**

1. **Immunologic priming** – Even a single exposure (2000 U UFH at coronary angiography in the index patient) can generate class‑switching IgG against PF4–heparin complexes.  
2. **Rapid re‑exposure** – Cannulation and CPB inoculate large amounts of UFH (45 000 U in the case) within hours of the pre‑operative dose, allowing the pre‑existing antibodies to form immune complexes and trigger platelet activation at the first postoperative day.  
3. **Autoimmune HIT** – The patient’s serotonin‑release assay showed heparin‑independent platelet activation, characteristic of autoimmune HIT, which can cause platelet activation immediately after exposure without the usual lag time.

**Other exposures that can prompt early onset**

- High‑dose or prolonged intra‑operative heparin (e.g., >10 000 U total).  
- Re‑use of heparin on successive procedures within a short interval.  
- Early postoperative LMWH prophylaxis (although less common, it can precipitate HIT if antibodies are already present).

**Bottom line**

In cardiac surgery, a history of pre‑operative heparin exposure is a red flag for very early HIT. Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for HIT when thrombocytopenia develops within the first 5 days of surgery, and the screening and confirmatory platelet‑activation assays should be obtained promptly.
