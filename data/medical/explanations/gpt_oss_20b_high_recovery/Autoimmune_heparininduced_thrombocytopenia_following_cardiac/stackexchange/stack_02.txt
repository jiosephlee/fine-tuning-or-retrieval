Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Differential Diagnosis] List the differential diagnoses for a sudden drop in platelet count after cardiac surgery that can also present with thrombosis or stroke, and describe key laboratory findings that help to distinguish them from HIT.

**Differential for a sudden, peri‑operative drop in platelets that can also cause thrombosis or stroke**

| Condition | Typical clinical picture | Key laboratory clues that separate it from HIT (classic or autoimmune) |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Classic heparin‑induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** | ↓ platelets 4–10 days after heparin exposure, often coupled with venous or arterial thrombosis | • PF4‑polyanion ELISA **positive**. <br>• Serotonin‑release assay (SRA) **activated** at 0.1–0.3 U/mL UFH but not at high heparin. <br>• Coagulation studies (PT/PTT, fibrinogen) **normal**. <br>• No schistocytes; no hemolysis. |
| **Autoimmune HIT (aHIT)** | Very early fall in platelets (≤ POD 5) after *prior* heparin exposure; can be profoundly thrombocytopenic and hypercoagulable | • PF4 ELISA **strongly positive** (often high OD). <br>• SRA shows **serotonin release even at 0 U/mL UFH** (heparin‑independent) and partial inhibition by Fc‑receptor blockade. <br>• Coagulation studies **normal**. <br>• Absence of schistocytes. |
| **Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)** (acquired) | Severe thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, neurologic deficits, renal dysfunction, fever | • **Schistocytes** on smear (≥ 5 / 10 WBC). <br>• **↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin, ↑ reticulocytes**. <br>• **Normal PT/PTT, normal fibrinogen.** <br>• **ADAMTS13 activity <10 %** (rapid bedside assay if available). <br>• PF4 ELISA **negative**; SRA negative. |
| **Complement‑mediated TMA (atypical HUS)** | Similar to TTP but often with prominent renal failure; may be triggered by surgery, drugs, or underlying dysfunction | • **Schistocytes, hemolysis** as above. <br>• **Low complement C3/C4** (or evidence of complement activation). <br>• **Normal coagulation times, normal fibrinogen.** <br>• PF4 ELISA **negative**. |
| **Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)** | Platelet consumption with microvascular thrombosis; can present with stroke or bleed | • **Prolonged PT, aPTT.** <br>• **Low fibrinogen**, **↑ D‑dimer**. <br>• Presence of **schistocytes** may be variable. <br>• **PF4 ELISA negative**; SRA negative. |
| **Catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS)** | Multiple organ thromboses, including stroke, and sudden drop in platelets | • **Positive antiphospholipid antibodies** (aCL IgG/IgM, anti‑β2GP1, lupus anticoagulant). <br>• Coagulation times **often normal**; fibrinogen may be normal or mildly low. <br>• No specific PF4 antibody; SRA negative. |
| **Drug‑induced immune thrombocytopenia (e.g., post‑transfusion purpura, antibiotic‑associated)** | Acute thrombocytopenia; rarely associated with thrombosis | • **Serum drug–platelet antibody** (may be detected by granulocyte agglutination tests). <br>• Coagulation studies **normal**; no hemolysis. <br>• PF4 ELISA **negative**; SRA negative. |
| **Mechanical platelet consumption (e.g., by CPB, prosthetic valve)** | Mild to moderate thrombocytopenia; usually no systemic thrombosis | • No schistocytes; normal hemolysis labs. <br>• Coagulation studies **normal**. <br>• PF4 ELISA **negative**; SRA negative. |

---

### Highlight: How the case supported a diagnosis of aHIT over other entities

1. **No schistocytes** and **normal coagulation times** made TTP, atypical HUS, or DIC unlikely.  
2. **PF4/polyanion ELISA OD 2.2** was strongly positive, but the **SRA showed strong serotonin release even with 0 U/mL heparin**, while at higher heparin concentrations no activation occurred—classical for aHIT.  
3. **Partial inhibition by the Fc‑receptor–blocking antibody** (IV.3) reflected an unusual, heparin‑independent, Fc‑receptor–mediated pathway typical of an autoimmune variant.  
4. The **lesion of the left parietal lobe** was consistent with a thrombotic stroke, a hallmark complication of HIT/aHIT.  

Collectively, the laboratory pattern—normal PT/PTT and fibrinogen, absence of schistocytes, high‑OD PF4 ELISA, and an SRA that is heparin‑dependent *and* shows serotonin release at zero heparin—confirms autoimmune HIT and excludes the other thrombotic microangiopathies and consumptive coagulopathies that also present with sudden thrombocytopenia and thrombosis after cardiac surgery.
