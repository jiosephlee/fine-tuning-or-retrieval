Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Management] Describe the fluid restriction regimen used to manage SIADH in this patient, including target intake and monitoring parameters.

**Fluid restriction for SIADH**

In this case the patient’s SIADH was treated with a **strict fluid restriction of less than 800 mL per day**. The goal was a gradual, safe rise in serum sodium—typically 4–6 mmol/L over 48–72 h (no more than 8–12 mmol/L over the first 24 h) to avoid osmotic demyelination syndrome.  

**Monitoring protocol used**

1. **Daily serum sodium** – measured each morning to track the rate of correction.  
2. **Serum and urine osmolality** – checked when sodium rose >145 mmol/L or if clinical symptoms (e.g., nausea, confusion) appeared, to assess ongoing water restriction efficacy.  
3. **Clinical weight and volume status** – daily checks for euvolemia, ensuring no signs of dehydration or over‑restriction.  

The patient’s sodium rose from 124 mmol/L to 136 mmol/L by day 16, with ADH falling to normal levels and no need for hypertonic saline or desmopressin. This conservative approach, guided by the aforementioned monitoring, safely cured the hyponatremia associated with his acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease.
