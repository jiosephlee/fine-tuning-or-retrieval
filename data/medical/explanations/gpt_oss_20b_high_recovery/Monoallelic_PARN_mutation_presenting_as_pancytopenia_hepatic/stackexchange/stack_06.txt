Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] "Why do patients with monoallelic pathogenic variants in PARN more commonly present with isolated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis compared to patients with biallelic variants who usually have dyskeratosis congenita?"

**Clinical Pearls – Pathophysiology**  
**PARN heterozygosity → isolated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis; homozygosity → dyskeratosis congenita**

**Key point:**  
PARN encodes a 3′–exoribonuclease essential for maturation of the telomerase RNA component (TERC). Heterozygous loss causes a modest reduction in TERC availability (“haploinsufficiency”) that preferentially affects organs with extremely high cellular turnover—particularly type II alveolar epithelial cells—so patients first develop idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Complete loss (biallelic pathogenic variants) drives severe early‑onset telomere attrition that overwhelms multiple tissue systems simultaneously, yielding classic dyskeratosis congenita (DC) with its mucocutaneous triad and bone‑marrow failure syndrome.

---

### Why heterozygous PARN mutations manifest mainly as IPF

| Feature | Mechanism | Typical tissue impact |
|---------|-----------|-----------------------|
| **Allele dosage** | One functional allele → ~50 % activity | Partial telomerase insufficiency sufficient only for highly replicative compartments |
| **TEC degradation** | Decreased removal of post‑transcriptional oligo(A) tails from TERC → reduced steady‑state TERC levels | Less efficient telomerase assembly → impaired elongation during rapid DNA synthesis cycles |
| **Compensatory pathways** | Other nucleases and RNA‑processing enzymes partially compensate | Lungs & bone marrow can tolerate lower residual activity longer than skin/mucosa |

In our index patient (c.613 T>C; p.Cys205Arg), a monoallelic missense change predicted to damage the R3H domain caused:

* **Pulmonary fibrosis** – diffuse ground‑glass opacity and subpleural scarring on CT (figures 1 & 2).  
* **Bone‐marrow pressure sensitivity** – pancytopenia that worsened after stressors such as severe B12 deficiency—but remained largely reversible when nutritional deficiencies were corrected, implying an underlying telomere limitation only exposed under duress.  
* **Subclinical mucocutaneous findings** – premature greying and nail changes were subtle at presentation, showing incomplete penetrance common in heterozygotes.

Because alveolar epithelial repair demands rapid cell division and telomerase function during injury response, even mild telomerase impairment precipitates abnormal fibroproliferation leading to IPF while skin/hair/mucosa, which divide more slowly under basal conditions, remain less affected until later stages or additional hits occur.

---

### Why homozygous PARN mutations produce dyskeratosis congenita

Biallelic pathogenic variants eliminate nearly all functional PARN protein:

* Drastic depletion of mature TERC -> profound telomerase shutdown.
* Telomeres reach critically short lengths during early development.
* Multiple rapidly replicating tissues—including keratinocytes (skin), melanocytes/nail matrix cells (pigmentation/nail dystrophy), oral mucosa (leukoplakia), hematopoietic stem cells—fail catastrophically.
* This explains the classic triad of *“oral leukoplakia–nail dystrophy–reticular skin pigmentation”* plus bone‑marrow failure seen in most DC patients described with DKCB6 inheritance.

In contrast, heterozygotes typically manifest appreciable phenotypes only after cumulative stressors or at tissues especially vulnerable to limited telomerase reserve—hence isolated pulmonary or hepatic fibrosis without overt mucocutaneous disease initially.

---

### Clinical Take‑away

When encountering a young adult with unexplained interstitial lung disease and subtle systemic features but no classic DC triad:
1. **Consider a germline TBD**, especially if bone‑marrow or hepatic involvement is present even if mild.
2. **Order next‑generation sequencing focused on telomere‑related genes** (TERC/TERT/RTEL1/PARN).
3. If a *heterozygous* pathogenic PARN variant is found:
   * Counsel about risk of progressive IPF and monitor lung function closely.
   * Watch for late‐onset bone‑marrow failure or liver dysfunction—telemetry often guides surveillance frequency.
4. If a *biallelic* mutation is detected:
   * Expect and screen for discoid mucocutaneous lesions and earlier hematologic compromise.
   * Discuss possible stem‑cell transplant sooner given broader organ involvement distribution.

The divergent presentations underscore how allele dosage modulates sample tissue vulnerability within the same underlying molecular pathway—a key concept when interpreting genotype–phenotype relationships in rare inherited disorders.
