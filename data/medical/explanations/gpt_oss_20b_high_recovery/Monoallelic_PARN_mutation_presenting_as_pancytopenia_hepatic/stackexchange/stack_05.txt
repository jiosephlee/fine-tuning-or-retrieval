Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Management] "Describe the first‑line treatment strategy for severe vitamin B12 deficiency complicated by ineffective erythropoiesis characterized by basophilic stippling and Cabot rings."

**First‑line treatment for severe vitamin B12 deficiency with ineffective erythropoiesis (basophilic stippling + Cabot rings)**  

1. **High‑dose parenteral cyanocobalamin**  
   * **Initial loading** – 1 000 µg (1 mg) IM or IV once daily for **5 days**.  
   * **Intensive phase** – same dose **weekly for 4 weeks**.  
   * **Maintenance** – 1 000 µg monthly until serum B12 normalizes and dietary absorption is confirmed.  
   * Rationale: Parenteral therapy bypasses malabsorption (duodenal gastritis → reduced intrinsic factor) and quickly restores marrow reservoirs, reversing megaloblastic hemopoiesis while clearing abnormal cytoplasmic inclusions such as basophilic stippling and Cabot rings (as observed on the patient’s peripheral smear).

2. **Supportive transfusion**  
   * Transfuse packed RBCs if hemoglobin < 7–8 g/dL or symptomatic anemia (dyspnea, tachycardia).  
   * The described patient received whole‑blood transfusions during the acute phase while awaiting correction of the store deficit.

3. **Concurrent folate supplementation**  
   * Oral folic acid 1 mg daily started immediately with B12 therapy to curb residual megaloblastic changes and prevent red‑cell morphological abnormalities until folate stores are re‑established.

4. **Follow‑up laboratory monitoring**  
   * CBC with reticulocyte count at days 7–10 and again at one month to document response; a rising reticulocyte count confirms effective erythropoiesis restoration.  
   * Serum B12 should fall into the normal range (<350 pg/mL) within 2–4 weeks of therapy initiation.

5. **Address malabsorption if persistent**  
   * If intrinsic‑factor–mediated deficiency remains after initial correction, periodical IV/IM B12 may be required indefinitely; dietary counseling and management of gastric atrophy (as seen on duodenal biopsy) can further reduce relapse risk.

In summary, the cornerstone of management is a structured course of high‑dose parenteral cyanocobalamin—daily five days, weekly four weeks, then monthly—paired with supportive transfusions and oral folate while monitoring hematologic response. This regimen directly targets the defective nucleotide synthesis responsible for ineffective erythropoiesis marked by basophilic stippling and Cabot rings.</span>
