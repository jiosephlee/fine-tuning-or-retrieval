Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] "What are the classic mucocutaneous triad manifestations characteristic of dyskeratosis congenita?"

**Answer**

The mucocutaneous triad defining classic dyskeratosis congenita (DC) consists of:

| Manifestation | Typical appearance in DC | Relevance to the presented case |
|---------------|--------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Oral mucosal lesion** – most often a nondiffuse **leukoplakia** or atrophic “bald” tongue with loss of papillae | This is one of the earliest and most specific cutaneous clues | In this patient a bald tongue with atrophic papillae was noted (Figure 1), fulfilling the oral component even though classic whitish patches were not described |
| **Nail dystrophy** – brittle, ridged, spoon‑shaped or otherwise abnormal nails | Nail findings are present in >70 % of DC patients and present even when other features are subtle | No nail changes were documented in this report; however their absence does not exclude DC or telomere‑biology disorders |
| **Reticular skin pigmentation** – diffuse, mottled hyperpigmentation often accentuated on knuckles and back | The pigmentary pattern is highly characteristic and usually persists into adulthood | The patient displayed knuckle hyperpigmentation—a key member of the pigmentary component |

These three findings together produce what is traditionally termed “the DC mucocutaneous triad.” Recognition of any two components plus evidence of bone‑marrow failure suffices for diagnostic suspicion. In practice, many adults with telomere‑biology disorders—including those with monoallelic *PARN* mutations such as this patient—may lack all three classic signs yet still harbor significant systemic disease (bone‑marrow failure, hepatic fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis). Hence a high index of suspicion remains warranted when other hallmark features of impaired cellular replication are present.
