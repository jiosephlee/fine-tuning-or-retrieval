Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] After commencing eculizumab therapy for complement‑mediated HUS, why are meningococcal vaccination and antibiotic prophylaxis mandatory, and what is the recommended timing and schedule of immunization?

**Why meningococcal vaccination and antibiotic prophylaxis are mandatory after starting eculizumab**

| Reason | Clinical basis |
|--------|----------------|
| **Loss of terminal complement** | Eculizumab blocks C5, abolishing the membrane‑attack complex (MAC). Encapsulated bacteria such as *Neisseria meningitidis* (MenACWY and MenB) rely on MAC for bactericidal activity. The patient in the case report was placed on eculizumab while still at risk of a severe, often fatal meningococcal outbreak. |
| **Inadequate humoral response without complement** | Complement is needed for efficient opsonisation and antibody‑mediated clearance. Even a normal antibody level may be ineffective if MAC is absent. Vaccination primes the adaptive immune system before complement inhibition, ensuring that enough functional antibody is produced once eculizumab is given. |
| **Increased incidence of meningococcal disease** | The FDA and EMA label for eculizumab lists meningococcal infection as a serious, life‑threatening adverse event; incidence is 400–500 times higher than in the general population. |
| **Legal and ethical protection** | Failure to provide vaccine/prophylaxis can lead to medicolegal exposure and patient harm. |

**Recommended timing & schedule**

1. **Vaccination before complement blockade**  
   - Give a single dose of **MenACWY** (Menveo® or Menactra®) *and* a single dose of **MenB** (Bexsero®) **≥ 2 weeks** before initiating eculizumab.  
   - In our patient, the first doses of both vaccines were administered right after the third plasma‑exchange session, *just before* the first eculizumab infusion (900 mg IV weekly). Because the inoculations occurred within 2 weeks of starting eculizumab, antibiotic prophylaxis was started (Apocillin®) to cover the vulnerable period.

2. **Second‑dose schedule**  
   - **MenACWY**: second dose at **2 weeks after the first dose** (or 8 weeks after the initial dose if a two‑dose series is planned).  
   - **MenB**: second dose at **4 weeks after the first dose** (or 8 weeks after the initial dose if a two‑dose series is planned).  
   - The case report followed a pragmatic schedule: second doses were planned 8 weeks later on an outpatient basis – a widely accepted alternative that balances immune maturation with logistical feasibility.

3. **Antibiotic prophylaxis**  
   - If vaccination is given within 2 weeks of eculizumab initiation, administer oral penicillin G/ amoxicillin (or Apocillin®) as a daily regimen for **at least 2 weeks** of eculizumab therapy, or until a clear antibody titre is documented.  
   - Continue prophylaxis throughout the entire maintenance eculizumab course in patients who have not yet completed the full meningococcal vaccine series or who have low post‑vaccine antibody titres.

4. **Serologic monitoring**  
   - Measure anti‑MenACWY and anti‑MenB IgG titres 4–6 weeks after the final dose. A titre ≥ 1.0 µg/mL (MenACWY) or ≥ 0.5 µg/mL (MenB) is considered protective. Repeat titration at least once per year if eculizumab is continued > 12 months. 

**Key take‑away for the day‑to‑day practice**

- **Give meningococcal vaccines at least 2 weeks before eculizumab.** If urgent eculizumab is needed and vaccinations cannot be fully completed, start daily penicillin prophylaxis for the full duration of the eculizumab course.  
- **Use a two‑dose series for both MenACWY and MenB** (2‑week and 4‑week intervals respectively) when possible, but an 8‑week interval is acceptable for practical reasons.
- **Confirm protective antibody levels** before ceasing prophylaxis.  
- **Explain the rationale** to patients: eculizumab impairs the MAC; the vaccine teaches the immune system to produce antibodies that will function even in the absence of complement.

The case report illustrates this approach—vaccines were given just before eculizumab induction, antibiotic prophylaxis was administered, and the second vaccine doses were scheduled 8 weeks later. This regimen protects the patient from the life‑threatening risk of meningococcal infection that accompanies terminal complement blockade.
