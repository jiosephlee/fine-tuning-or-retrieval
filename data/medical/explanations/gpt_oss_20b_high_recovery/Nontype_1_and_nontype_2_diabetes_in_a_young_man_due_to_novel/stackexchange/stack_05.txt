Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] A HNF1A‑MODY patient has a fasting C‑peptide of 0.4 nmol/L, HbA1c of 10 %, and is initially started on basal insulin. After genetic confirmation, what therapeutic switch is most appropriate and why?

**Therapeutic switch:** Discontinue basal insulin and initiate a GLP‑1 receptor agonist (e.g., dulaglutide 1.5 mg weekly; liraglutide 1.8 mg daily).  

**Why:**  
- HNF1A‑MODY causes progressive β‑cell insufficiency; the patient’s fasting C‑peptide (0.4 nmol/L) reflects residual secretion that can be amplified by a GLP‑1 agonist.  
- GLP‑1 agents improve fasting and post‑prandial glucose, promote weight loss, and have a low risk of hypoglycemia compared with insulin or sulfonylureas.  
- In the reported case, dulaglutide lowered HbA1c from 9.1 % to 6.0 % in 6 months, allowing insulin withdrawal and subsequent maintenance on metformin + DPP‑4 inhibitor.  
- Current consensus for HNF1A‑MODY recommends sulfonylureas initially, but GLP‑1 agonists are preferred when insulin is used or when hypoglycemia risk or weight control is a concern.
