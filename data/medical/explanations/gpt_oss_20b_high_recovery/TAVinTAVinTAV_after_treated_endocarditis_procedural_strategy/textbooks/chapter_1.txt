Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation, Initial TAVI Implantation and Early Complications

The patient is a man in his late 70 s who had a history of multi‑vessel coronary artery disease treated with several percutaneous coronary interventions between 2009 and 2014. He also had a high‑risk comorbidity profile that included arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, and a permanent pacemaker implanted in 2014 for third‑degree atrioventricular block. This constellation of conditions placed him at prohibitive operative risk, a key factor that guided the decision to pursue a transcatheter rather than surgical approach for severe aortic stenosis.

Clinically, the patient reported progressive exertional dyspnoea, a spastic left‑sided chest sensation on walking, dizziness and fatigue, with a functional limitation of 200–300 m. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed severe aortic valve stenosis with a mean transvalvular gradient approaching the upper limits of severe stenosis and an ejection fraction (EF) of approximately 54 %. The descending gradient was attributable to an increase in afterload on a chronically hypertrophied left ventricle, a hallmark of the haemodynamic sequelae of fixed valve obstruction. The patient’s symptoms and imaging findings fulfilled the criteria for intervention documented in contemporary aortic stenosis guidelines and were consistent with a high surgical risk calculated on the EuroSCORE II, which in this case approached 27 %.

Given these factors, the first transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was performed in 2014 using a 27‑mm Lotus valve. The Lotus platform is a self‑expanding supra‑annular valve that offers a low‑profile delivery system and a flexible nitinol frame, which can accommodate annular undersizing and reduce torque on the annulus—attributes preferred in patients with small annuli or complex annular anatomy. The procedure was successful, with an immediate reduction in transvalvular gradient and a modest improvement in LV systolic function.

Despite this initial success, the patient’s early post‑implant course was marked by progressive symptoms and worsening imaging over the next few years. By late 2021, diameter‑echo and Doppler studies documented a mean gradient of 83 mm Hg and a decline in EF from the initial 54 % to 42 %, indicating significant structural valve deterioration of the Lotus prosthesis. Clinically, the patient reported return of dyspnoea and fatigue, corresponding to a New York Heart Association class III–IV functional status.

A second, valve‑in‑valve intervention was undertaken in May 2022, in which a 23‑mm Evolut (Medtronic) self‑expanding valve was implanted within the failing Lotus device. This procedure restored forward flow and relieved the positional obstruction, leading to an improvement in EF back to 54 %. However, a femoral access‑site complication ensued: a pulsatile haematoma and a pseudoaneurysm at the puncture site necessitated emergent open vascular surgery for repair. Femoral complications are a recognized risk of transfemoral TAVI, especially in elderly patients with arterial comorbidities and a history of anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy. The morbidity associated with this complication delayed further definitive management and underscored the delicate balance between procedural benefits and vascular risks in high‑risk patients.

The early trajectory of this case illustrates the typical pattern of initial symptom relief following TAVI, followed by gradual structural degeneration, and the importance of vigilant post‑procedural surveillance. The early haemodynamic recovery achieved after the Evolut implantation demonstrates the reversible component of LV dysfunction when the outflow obstruction is relieved, while the late‑appearance of structural failure highlights the finite durability of bioprosthetic transcatheter valves in the setting of long life expectancy. The femoral pseudoaneurysm complication emphasizes the necessity of meticulous access-site management and the readiness to manage vascular complications in this population.
