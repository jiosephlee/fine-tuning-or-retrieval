Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 5: ## Clinical Implications, Guideline Context and Future Directions

The presented case illustrates the growing reality of multi‑prosthetic transcatheter aortic valve therapy. While current evidence for single TAV‑in‑TAV procedures is robust, the feasibility of a third valve after endocarditis remains proof‑of‑concept. Clinical decision‑making therefore hinges on blending guideline recommendations with meticulous, patient‑specific planning and innovative procedural strategies.

### Guideline‑based management of prosthetic valve endocarditis
The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines recommend surgical replacement as the preferred treatment for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) when the infection is uncontrolled, when there are complications such as heart failure or refractory vegetations, or when prosthetic dysfunction is significant. Nonetheless, the guidelines acknowledge that in patients with prohibitive surgical risk and a favorable microbiological response, a prolonged medical regimen can be considered, particularly when the prosthesis is viable and the infection has been definitively eradicated.  In the patient described here, a six‑week course of ampicillin‑based therapy achieved sterile blood cultures and transesophageal echocardiographic remission.  His EuroSCORE II of 27.4 % and significant comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, gastrointestinal bleeding) shifted the risk–benefit calculus toward a transcatheter solution, aligning with the ESC’s emphasis on individualized care.

### Early multidisciplinary planning and coronary‑access preservation
The complexity of serial TAV‑in‑TAV procedures necessitates a proactive, heart‑team–led approach.  In this case, cardiac surgeons, interventionalists, imaging scientists and anesthesiologists convened before the first intervention to evaluate the patient’s anatomical constraints.  Three‑dimensional multislice computed tomography (MSCT) permitted precise measurement of the neo‑annulus, coronary ostial height, and stent‑frame geometry, enabling the implantation team to plan a high, supra‑annular third valve that would align with the Evolut frame and preserve coronary flow.  This pre‑procedural strategy is now a cornerstone of contemporary practice, particularly as the cohort of patients requiring repeat TAVI expands.

### Risk of patient‑prosthesis mismatch and mitigation by high implantation
Recurrent transcatheter interventions inherently compound the risk of patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM) because each new valve intrudes upon the existing lumen.  The case demonstrates that a targeted high‑implantation approach can ameliorate PPM: by positioning the third valve at the level of the Evolut leaflet commissures, the distal frames were effectively “trapped” together, preventing further encroachment on the effective orifice.  Hemodynamically, this strategy yielded an initial mean gradient of 67 mm Hg and an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² immediately post‑procedure, which improved dramatically to 27 mm Hg and 1.1 cm² at one year—well below the threshold for severe PPM.

### Emerging techniques: valve fracture, BASILICA, and leaflet laceration
New procedural adjuncts—bioprosthetic valve fracture, BASILICA (Bioprosthetic or Native Aortic Scallop Intentional Laceration to Prevent Iatrogenic Coronary Artery Obstruction), and intentional leaflet laceration—have been employed mainly in the setting of high‑risk coronary obstruction.  Their technical applicability in triple‑stented anatomies is still under investigation.  While a bioprosthetic coronary fracture may theoretically expand a stent‑loaded valve, its safety with trespassing stent frames and scaffold cannot yet be assured.  Similarly, BASILICA has not been reported in a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV setting; the presence of multiple rigid frames may constrain the ability to safely lacerate root tissue.  Consequently, these techniques remain investigational and should be reserved for select scenarios where postoperative coronary obstruction is imminent and medical or transcatheter options are exhausted.

### Limitations of surgical re‑explantation in heavily stented patients
Open surgical explantation of a third transcatheter valve entails a highly invasive procedure with considerable morbidity.  The patient’s deep root fixation, extensive stent integration, and high cardiovascular risk during cardiopulmonary bypass made the surgical risk prohibitive.  Moreover, re‑construction of the aortic root after two prior TAVI procedures would most likely require valve‑root replacement and ascending aorta patching, further amplifying operative complexity and recovery time.  Thus, non‑surgical alternatives, despite their own technical challenges, represent a more pragmatic approach in such patients.

### Research gaps and the need for dedicated imaging and registry data
The paucity of systematic data on TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV outcomes limits evidence‑based guidance.  Dedicated imaging algorithms that incorporate stent‑frame deformation, neo‑annulus expansion, and hemodynamic simulation are required to predict procedural feasibility and long‑term durability.  Emerging computational fluid dynamics (CFD) models can estimate transvalvular flow patterns and identify regions of high shear that predispose to accelerated tissue degeneration.  Prospective registries that capture pre‑procedural imaging, procedural details, and serial imaging outcomes would accelerate learning and standardisation of best practices.

### Future surveillance strategies: routine MSCT and virtual‑flow simulation
Routine MSCT at 6‑ to 12‑month intervals after each TAVI implantation can objectively track neo‑annular area, stent expansion, and the presence of late leaflet thickening or pannus.  Coupling serial CTs with virtual‑flow simulation enables early detection of hemodynamic deterioration before clinical decompensation, allowing for proactive planning of re‑intervention.  In patients with multiple stent layers, such surveillance may also refine predictions of coronary accessibility and inform the choice of future valve platforms or adjunctive procedures.

---

In summary, the case demonstrates that a third transcatheter valve can be safely and effectively implanted after endocarditis‑treated PVE when guided by meticulous imaging and multidisciplinary planning.  ESC 2025 recommendations for PVE are balanced by individualized risk–benefit analyses that consider surgical prohibitive risk.  High‑implantation, commissural alignment, and careful preservation of coronary access help mitigate PPM and procedural complications.  As the field advances, engineered imaging‑driven algorithms, CFD analysis, and robust registries will be essential to optimise clinical outcomes in this increasingly common patient population.
