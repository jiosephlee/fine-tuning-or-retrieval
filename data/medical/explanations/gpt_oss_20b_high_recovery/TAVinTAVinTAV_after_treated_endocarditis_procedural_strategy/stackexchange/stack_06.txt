Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] In planning the third TAVI with an Acurate neo2 valve into a prior Evolut frame, how did the team use CT‑derived measurements to decide the valve size, implantation depth, and post‑dilation strategy?

**Clinical Teaching Point – TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV Planning**

In this patient, the pre‑procedural multislice computed tomography (MSCT) provided three critical pieces of information that shaped every step of the third‑valve deployment:

| Clinical Decision | What the CT Showed | How it Guided the Field |
|-------------------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **Valve size** | Minimum lumen of the existing 23 mm Evolut cage measured 13.7 mm. The internal diameter of the Acurate neo2 S (nominal 22 mm) is 20 – 21 mm; a 20 mm frame can be accommodated when the Evolut’s lower stent is partially expanded by the new valve. | Because the Evolut frame already occupied a significant portion of the aortic root, the team chose the smallest Acurate neo2 (S) to avoid oversizing that would risk annular rupture or coronary obstruction. CT‑derived annulus dimensions confirmed that the new valve would fit snugly without excessive oversizing, a key ‘no‑risk‑zone’ for a serial TAV scenario. |
| **Implantation depth** | CT‑based 3‑D models showed the Evolut stent struts and leaflet commissures at a height that would allow the new valve to sit just above the Evolut frames. Coronary ostial height and sinus of Valsalva were measured, revealing a > 15 mm clearance from the neo‑skirt to the coronary oris when the neo‑valve was placed high. | The team elected a *high* implantation—aligning the Acurate neo2 commissures with the Evolut leaflets—to (1) use the Evolut frame as a scaffold for leaflets, (2) preserve the residual orifice area by preventing an additional “neo‑skirt” in the sinuses, and (3) maintain coronary clearance. The CT‐derived touchdown level was translated into fluoroscopic markers (co‑aligned waist, B‑plane) to replicate the 3‑D plan in‑the‑hybrid suite. |
| **Post‑dilation strategy** | CT simulation of valve expansion demonstrated that a fully expanded Acurate neo2 would nevertheless be partially compressed beneath the stiff Evolut stent, especially at the level of the remaining stent frames. | To achieve optimal radial force, a 20 / 40 mm high‑pressure balloon was selected for post‑dilation. The balloon size corresponds to the target diameter of the neo‑valve + an allowance for the Evolut’s radial stiffness, and rapid pacing underscored the need for a brief, controlled inflation. CT had shown that post‑dilation would not jeopardize coronary flow (no predicted encroachment), so protection was deemed unnecessary. |

**Bottom Line for Residents**

1. **Measure the “real” landing zone** (minimum lumen, stent strut height, coronary clearance) rather than relying on nominal valve sizes.
2. **Choose the smallest valve that will clear the residual root anatomy** and minimize oversizing—especially in a multi‑stent setting.
3. **Plan a high, commissure‑aligned deployment** to use the existing stent as a scaffold, preserve the effective orifice, and protect the coronaries.
4. **Use CT‑simulated expansion to size the post‑dilation balloon** (often slightly larger than the valve’s nominal diameter) and confirm that no new obstruction will be created.

The team’s CT‑derived roadmap enabled a safe, effective, and hemodynamically favorable third‑valve implantation—demonstrating how detailed imaging can bias every procedural choice in complex repeat TAVI.
