Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the pathophysiological mechanisms that can lead to structural valve deterioration in transcatheter bioprostheses, and explain how a prior episode of infective endocarditis may modify these mechanisms.

**Pathophysiology of Structural Valve Deterioration (SVD) in Transcatheter Bioprostheses – With a Note on Prior Infective Endocarditis**

1. **Mechanisms of SVD in Transcatheter Valves**

| Mechanism | How It Occurs in a TAVI Device | Clinical Consequence |
|-----------|------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Leaflet calcification & fibrosis** | Bioprosthetic tissue, once exposed to the high‑velocity jet at the annulus, undergoes calcium deposition and stiffening. In the case, the *Lotus* valve (2014) progressed to a mean gradient of 83 mm Hg by 2021, a classic hallmark of calcific stenosis. | Progressive transvalvular gradient, ↓ leaflet mobility, ↓ effective orifice area (EOA). |
| **Mechanical fatigue & shear stress** | Repeated valve‑to‑valve flexion, especially when a new prosthesis is implanted inside a prior one, compresses the underlying leaflets and the stent frame itself. The TAV‑in‑TAV (2022) created a “double‑layer” frame, and the subsequent **TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV** further amplified this fatigue. | Rapid deterioration of leaflet function, early increase in gradients. |
| **Pannus, tissue ingrowth, and hypo‑plastic fibrous tissue** | Chronic low‑grade inflammation can lead to neointimal proliferation over the valve‐frame junction, narrowing the lumen (as noted with only 13.7 mm of residual lumen in the Evolut frame on MSCT). | Subclinical narrowing leading to restenosis or regurgitation. |
| **Inflammatory‑mediated degradation** | Pro‑inflammatory cytokines released by the host or due to chronic infection accelerate collagen breakdown and enzymatic remodeling of the leaflet matrix. | Accelerated SVD, often presenting abruptly. |

2. **How Prior Infective Endocarditis (PVE) Alters These Processes**

* The patient developed *Enterococcus faecalis* PVE three months after the Evolut implantation (Sept 2022). Blood cultures, TEE and CT confirmed acute infection and subsequently resolved after 6‑week antibiotics.
* **Direct leaflet injury:** Vegetations and associated bacteriologic toxins compromise the structural integrity of the leaflets, causing micro‑tears and focal necrosis. This can manifest clinically as the abrupt rise in mean gradient to 35 mm Hg and intraprosthetic regurgitation seen after the PVE episode, despite a previously successful \(E\)= 42 mm Hg gradient after the Evolut implantation.
* **Inflammation‑induced calcification:** The inflammatory milieu initiates osteogenic differentiation of valvular interstitial cells, hastening calcium deposition in minutes-to-months rather than decades. Consequently, the *Evolut* valve exhibited severe restenosis (mean gradient 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm²) by Jan 2024—earlier than expected for a bioprosthetic valve of its age alone.
* **Adjunct mechanical burden:** Friction between the *Evolut* frame and a now‑infected leaflets can cause additional shear damage. When a *third* valve (Acurate neo2) was implanted, the high implantation strategy partially “un‑folded” the *Evolut* leaflets against the outer frame, improving central flow but also exposing more of the damaged leaflets to external force, further accelerating SVD if left untreated.

3. **Implications for Management**

* After complete antibiotic therapy, the patient’s infection risk had subsided; no long‑term suppressive therapy was required. Hence the surgical decision pivoted on **anatomical feasibility and cumulative mechanical burden** rather than persistent infection.
* CT‑guided planning verified adequate coronary clearance and predicted a residual lumen of ≈13 mm, informing the choice of a supra‑annular, low‑profile valve (Acurate neo2) and a high‑implantation strategy. The goal was to:
  - **Minimize added mechanical stress** on the already damaged leaflets;
  - **Preserve coronary access** (critical in a patient with triple stent frames);
  - **Enhance effective orifice area** post‑re‑deployment.

In the presented case, these strategies culminated in a sustained functional benefit at 1 year, with a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg and an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm² (moderately stenotic but clinically acceptable).

**Bottom line:** Structural valve deterioration in transcatheter bioprostheses is driven by calcific stiffening, mechanical fatigue, and chronic inflammation. Prior infective endocarditis superimposes acute leaflet injury and an inflammatory cascade that can precipitate premature and more aggressive SVD. Comprehensive imaging and a strategy that mitigates added mechanical load are essential when planning repeat TAVI in this setting.
