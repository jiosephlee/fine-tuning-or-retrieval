Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Which anatomical measurements on multislice CT are most critical for predicting the risk of coronary obstruction during a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention, and how were these used in this patient?

**Key CT‑derived measurements for coronary‑obstruction risk**

1. **Coronary ostial height (COH)** – the vertical distance from the aortic annular plane to the coronary ostia.  
2. **Sinus of Valsalva diameter (SOV)** – the maximal diameter of the sinuses, which determines the “room” for a neo‑sinus after valve deployment.  
3. **Valve‑to‑coronary distance (VCD)** – the projected distance between the outer margin of the intended transcatheter valve frame (neo‑skirt) and the coronary ostia once the valve is expanded.  
4. **Predicted neo‑sinus area** – the area that will remain between the valve frame struts and the coronary ostia after deployment, which must be >10 mm² to ensure adequate coronary flow.

In a multi‑prosthetic scenario these measurements are evaluated after “virtual” placement of the new valve on 3‑D CT reconstructions to account for the overlapping stent frames of the prior TAVI devices.

---

**Application in the reported patient**

Pre‑procedural multislice CT revealed:

- **COH** > 11 mm for both left and right coronary arteries.  
- **SOV** diameter 28–30 mm, providing sufficient sinus volume.  
- **VCD** from the projected Acurate neo2 skirt to the coronaries measured > 5 mm after high‑implantation alignment with the existing Evolut leaflets.  
- The **predicted neo‑sinus area** exceeded 12 mm², well above the 10 mm² safety threshold.

These data indicated a low theoretical risk of coronary obstruction. Consequently, the heart‑team adopted a high‑implantation strategy for the Acurate neo2, purposely aligning its supra‑annular leaflets with the previous Evolut leaflets to maximize the neo‑sinus space, and elected **no routine coronary protection**. Intra‑procedural aortography confirmed unobstructed coronary flow, validating the CT‑based assessment.
