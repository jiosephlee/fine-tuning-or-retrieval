Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Explain three design features of the Acurate neo2 that make it suitable for implantation within previously deployed transcatheter valves, and consider their impact on haemodynamics and future coronary access.

**Acurate neo2 – Why it is a good partner for a valve‑in‑valve‑in‑valve**

| Design feature | What it does | Hemodynamic / coronary‑access impact observed in the case |
|-----------------|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **Supra‑annular leaflet placement** | The leaflets sit *outside* the stent frame, resting on a supra‑annular ring. | In the 75‑year‑old with a 13.7‑mm lumen, the supra‑annular design allowed a 1.1 cm² effective orifice area at 1 year (vs 0.9 cm² immediately post‑implant). The higher‑level leaflets do not compress each other, giving the valve a lower trans‑valvular gradient (mean 26 mm Hg at 1 yr) even after three layers of stent frames. |
| **Low stent‑frame height and short skirt** | The frame is thin and the external “neo‑skirt’’ sits just below the coronary ostia. | Implantation at the level of the existing Evolut leaflets kept the new frame above most of the sinus. This minimized the risk of coronary occlusion (no need for protection) and left a wide channel for future coronary pacing/diagnostic intervention—critical in a patient with three coronary stents. |
| **Comm­is­ural‑alignment capability** | The Neo2 has a commissural latch‑design that can be rotated and fixed to match the orientation of existing valves. | By aligning its commissures with the Evolut leaflets (guided by CT modeling), the team maximized the central opening, reduced patient‑prosthesis mismatch, and maintained a clear pathway to the coronaries. In practice the valve’s commissural marks allowed the operator to keep the neo‑skirt away from the sinus of Valsalva, avoiding “chop‑over” of coronary ostia. |

**Bottom line**  
The Supra‑annular, low‑profile, alignment‑friendly architecture of the Acurate neo2 gives it the “space” and “flexibility” needed to sit comfortably inside two already‑implanted transcatheter valves. The design preserves a larger effective orifice area, keeps coronary clearance, and keeps a direct route to the coronaries open for future interventions. Thus, in a triple‑valve scenario, the Neo2’s features translate directly into the improved hemodynamics (mean gradient drop from 49 to 26 mm Hg) and the safety profile observed at 1‑year follow‑up.
