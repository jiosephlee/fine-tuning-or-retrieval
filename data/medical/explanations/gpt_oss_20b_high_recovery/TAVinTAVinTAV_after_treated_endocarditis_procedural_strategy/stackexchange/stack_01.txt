Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the recommended imaging modalities and key multislice CT parameters for assessing feasibility of a third TAVI in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV and a resolved episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis?

**Imaging roadmap for a third TAVI after TAV‑in‑TAV and treated endocarditis**

| Modality | Why it matters in this setting | Key parameters & thresholds |
|----------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE)** | Baseline valve performance (gradient, regurgitation, LV function) and titration of heart‑failure therapy before, during and after the re‑intervention. | • Mean transvalvular gradient; <30 mm Hg post‑procedure is target. <br>• Effective orifice area (AVA) >1.0 cm² (or indexed AVA > 0.8 cm²/m²) to avoid patient‑prosthesis mismatch. |
| **Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE)** | Confirmation of infection resolution (no vegetations, paravalvular leaks) and precise assessment of leaflet motion in a multi‑prosthesis context. | • Absence of vegetations or abscesses; <1 mm paravalvular leak. |
| **ECG‑gated multislice computed tomography (MSCT)** (gold standard for procedural planning) | 3‑D reconstruction of the aortic root, existing valve frames and coronary anatomy; prediction of coronary obstruction and effective landing zone. | • **Annulus**: perimeter, area, eccentricity, maximum/minimum diameters.  <br>• **Coronary ostia height**: ≥10 mm above the annulus (the higher the better, especially with multiple frames).  <br>• **Sinus of Valsalva diameter**: ≥28 mm to accommodate a third valve.  <br>• **Valve‑to‑coronary distance** at the level of predicted neo‑skirt: ≥4 mm (lower risk of obstruction).  <br>• **Minimum lumen diameter within the prior Evolut frame**: ≥13 mm (as in this case) to ensure adequate flow.  <br>• **Landing zone length** (distance from annulus to aortic wall) ≥12 mm to allow crimping of a third‑generation self‑expanding valve.  <br>• **Neo‑skirt height**: predicted from 3‑D model; keep it <2 mm above coronary ostia when possible.  <br>• **Root curvature & ascending aorta dimensions**: to anticipate delivery system tortuosity.  <br>• **Commissural position** (relative to coronary ostia) for future coronary access; target ~45–50° per commissure. |
| **Optional valve‑fracture / intentional laceration planning** | Not required in this particular case, but 3‑D CT can be used to model potential valve‑fracture scenarios if valve‑to‑vessel wall apposition is suboptimal. | • Simulated frame expansion and post‑dilation balloon sizing. |

**Practical pearls from the case**

- The 2024 MSCT revealed a **13.7 mm minimum lumen diameter** of the Evolut frame, confirming adequate space for a third valve and guiding optimal device sizing (Acurate neo2 S).  
- Detailed modeling of the **coronary ostial height** and **valve‑to‑coronary distance** allowed the team to plan a **high implantation** that preserved coronary flow and preserved future coronary access—critical in a patient with multiple coronary stents.  
- The imaging protocol used ECG gating and a high‑pitch, 64‑slice scanner with contrast bolus timing tailored to the aortic root, ensuring sub‑millimeter resolution of the complex frame geometry.

**Bottom line**

For a third TAVI after TAV‑in‑TAV with a history of resolved endocarditis, the imaging ladder is:

1. TTE → baseline valvular physiology.  
2. TEE → infection clearance and leaflet morphology.  
3. ECG‑gated MSCT → 3‑D root anatomy, coronary clearance, landing zone and valve‑to‑coronary metrics.  

The CT parameters above—annular sizing, coronary heights, sinus diameters, valve‑to‑coronary distance, and minimal lumen diameter within prior frames—are the decision points that determine whether a third transcatheter valve can be safely implanted, how it should be positioned, and whether coronary protection or high‑implantation techniques will be needed.
