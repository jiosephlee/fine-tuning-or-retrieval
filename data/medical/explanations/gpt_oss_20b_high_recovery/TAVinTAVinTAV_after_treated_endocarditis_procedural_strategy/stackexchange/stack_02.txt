Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] In the case of a patient who underwent TAV-in-TAV for a degenerated Lotus valve and subsequently developed Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis, what factors influenced the decision to treat with antibiotics alone versus surgical intervention?

**Clinical Pearls:** *Choosing Antibiotics Alone for Prosthetic‑Valve Endocarditis After TAV‑in‑TAV*

- **Infection profile** – Enterococcus faecalis vegetations were small, limited to the prosthetic leaflet surface, and did not produce an abscess or prosthetic‑valve perforation. Six weeks of ampicillin‑based therapy cleared the infection (negative blood cultures, no residual vegetation on TEE or MSCT).  
- **Hemodynamic impact** – The patient had only mild residual regurgitation and no heart‑failure decompensation. Post‑therapy echocardiography showed stable valve function and no prosthetic dysfunction that would have demanded urgent surgical replacement.  
- **Guideline framework** – ESC 2025 guidelines recommend surgery for prosthetic endocarditis only when there is uncontrolled infection, abscess, or severe valvular dysfunction. This patient met none of those criteria.  
- **Surgical risk and anatomy** – EuroSCORE II 27.4 % (very high operative mortality), COPD, diabetes, prior gastrointestinal bleeding, pacemaker, and two preceding TAVIs. Surgical removal would have required a root and ascending‑aorta replacement, a technically prohibitive operation in a heavily scarred, multi‑stented annulus.  
- **Multidisciplinary consensus** – The heart‑team weighed the minimally invasive nature of catheter‑based therapy against the prohibitive surgical morbidity. The decision to treat medically was reinforced by this consensus and the absence of any residual infection.

**Bottom line:** In this high‑risk TAV‑in‑TAV patient, the combination of a small, responsive Enterococcus infection, preserved valve function, and prohibitive surgical risk led the team to pursue targeted antibiotics alone, reserving a repeat transcatheter valve for the later, purely mechanical valve degeneration.
