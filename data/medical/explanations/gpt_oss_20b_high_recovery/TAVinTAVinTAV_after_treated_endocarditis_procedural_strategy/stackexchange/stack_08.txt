Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] During the third TAVI procedure, why is commissural alignment emphasized, and what fluoroscopic landmarks or CT models guide this alignment?

**Why commissural alignment matters in the third‐generation TAVI**

In a serial TAVI where a third valve is implanted within two previous transcatheter frames (Lotus → Evolut → Acurate Neo2), the overlapping stent lattices create a *triple‑stented root*.   
1. **Coronary perfusion and future access** – If the new valve’s commissures are not positioned over the “open spaces” of the prior leaflets, the neo‑skirt can sit higher than the coronary ostia or, worse, can bridge an existing stent post and block a coronary ostium.  
2. **Effective orifice area** – Misalignment can cause leaflet folding against the inner stent frame, increasing residual gradients and precipitating patient‑prosthesis mismatch.  
3. **Procedural simplicity** – Aligning the new valve’s commissures with those of the previous Evolut leaflets permits a predictable, high‑implantation trajectory, avoiding the need for aggressive post‑dilation or the risk of iatrogenic obstruction.

**How we guided alignment in this case**

| Step | Tool | What we measured / visualized |
|------|------|--------------------------------|
| **Pre‑procedural imaging** | Multislice CT (3‑D reconstruction) | • Orientation of the Evolut commissures relative to the annulus and coronary ostia.<br>• Spatial relationship of the Lotus and Evolut stent frames.<br>• Predicted “landing zone” for the Acurate Neo2 and its commissure alignment with the Evolut leaflets. |
| **Fluoroscopy (in‑procedure)** | Dual‑view orthogonal projections (LAO 30° / RAO 30°) | • The radial posts of the prior Evolut frame appear as “kissing” hooks on the fluoroscopic image.<br>• The new Acurate’s three commissural markers (on the outer aortic frame) are rotated until they overlay the Evolut posts identified on CT.<br>• Confirmation that the opening trajectory aligns with the sinus of Valsalva and that the neo‑skirt remains above the proximal coronary projected lines. |
| **Execution** | Coaxial deployment with a Safari guidewire, rapid pacing, and post‑dilation | • The valve was positioned so that its commissures were *1–2 mm* above the Evolut leaflet hinges (as predicted by the CT model).<br>• Post‑dilation further spaced the Evolut frame, improving central flow. |

**Clinical takeaway**

In a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV setting, commissural alignment is not an aesthetic preference—it is a safety and efficacy imperative. By anchoring the new valve’s commissures to the known orientation of the prior leaflets, we preserve coronary flow, maintain a larger effective orifice area, and set the stage for any future coronary interventions. Detailed CT modeling and real‑time fluoroscopic landmarks provide the roadmap for achieving this alignment in the third generation of transcatheter aortic valve replacement.
