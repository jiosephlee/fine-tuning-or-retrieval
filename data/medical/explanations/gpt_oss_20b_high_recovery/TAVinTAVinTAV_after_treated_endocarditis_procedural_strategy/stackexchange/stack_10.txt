Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] How can patient–prosthesis mismatch be minimized when performing a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, and what procedural steps in this case contributed to resolving the initial high gradients?

**Patient–prosthesis mismatch in a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV can be minimized with a disciplined, imaging‑driven strategy that focuses on valve selection, deployment depth, and post‑deployment optimization.**  

1. **Valve sizing and design** –  
   • Use a supra‑annular, self‑expanding prosthesis (e.g., Acurate Neo 2) when the annulus is small or when multiple frames are already in place; supra‑annular leaflets provide a larger effective orifice area (EOA).  
   • Avoid the cascade of valve framing by selecting a device whose external diameter matches the residual lumen; in this case, a 31‑mm Acurate Neo 2 (size S) was chosen because CT measured a 13.7‑mm minimum lumen within the Evolut frame, leaving adequate space for the Neo 2 without over‑crowding the annulus.

2. **High‑level implantation** –  
   • Deploy the third valve at the plane of the pre‑existing Evolut leaflets rather than at the native annulus. This “high‑implant” strategy anchors the new valve above the old stent, minimizing overlap of stent frames and preserving a more central flow channel.  
   • In the case, the team aligned the Acurate Neo 2 commissures with the Evolut leaflets based on CT‑derived 3‑D models, ensuring that the Neo 2 skirt did not encroach on the coronary ostia while also avoiding the “double‑layered” frame that would reduce EOA.

3. **Optimized pre‑ and post‑dilation** –  
   • Mechanical expansion of the underlying Evolut stent can be achieved by high‑pressure balloon dilation after valve deployment. The 20/40‑mm balloon used for post‑dilation in this patient likely coaxed the Evolut frame into a more open geometry, improving leaflet motion and reducing residual velocity.  
   • Balloon pre‑dilation (22/40‑mm) also facilitates deeper and more stable positioning of the Neo 2, which reduces paravalvular leak and limits over‑compression of the new leaflets.

4. **Commissural alignment and coronary protection** –  
   • Aligning commissures to preserve future coronary access and to avoid a “neo‑skirt” between the basal part of the valve and the coronary ostia is essential when multiple devices occupy the root. In this patient, care was taken not to pack the neo‑skirt below the coronary arteries, which helped maintain adequate coronary flow and eased the post‑deployment interrogation of gradients.

5. **Continuous haemodynamic reassessment** –  
   • Immediate post‑procedural transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and aortography flagged a high mean gradient (67 mm Hg) and a severely narrowed EOA (0.9 cm²). Early recognition allowed the operator to perform targeted post‑dilation, leading to a rapid clinical and haemodynamic improvement (gradient 27 mm Hg at 1 month).  
   • Re‑evaluation at 1 year confirmed sustained resolution of the initial mismatch (gradient 26 mm Hg, index AVA 0.4 cm²/m²).

**Key steps in this case that resolved the initially high gradients were therefore**  
- **Pre‑procedural CT modelling** that facilitated valve sizing and the choice of a supra‑annular self‑expanding prosthesis.  
- **High‑implant deployment** at the Evolut leaflet level, ensuring the new valve did not add a second, low‑rise frame at the annulus.  
- **Meticulous commissural alignment** to maximize clear central flow and preserve coronary distance.  
- **Aggressive, staged balloon dilation** (pre‑ and post‑deployment) that expanded both the Neo 2 frame and the underlying Evolut struts, thereby enlarging the central orifice.  
- **Immediate post‑procedure surveillance** with TEE and aortography, enabling rapid corrective measures that translated into a marked reduction in transvalvular gradients over the first months.

By adhering to these principles, clinicians can attenuate PPM even in the most complex triple‑prosthesis scenarios, achieving durable haemodynamic outcomes as demonstrated by the 1‑year stability seen in this patient.
