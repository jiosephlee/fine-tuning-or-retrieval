Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] For a patient presenting with a new marked increase in transvalvular gradient and a 0.5 cm² AVA after a second TAVI, list the differential diagnosis and describe how TEE versus imaging distinguishes each entity.

**Differential diagnosis for a sudden rise in transvalvular gradient (mean 49 mm Hg) and a 0.5 cm² aortic valve area (AVA) after a second TAVI**  

| Entity | Key imaging features | How TEE distinguishes it | What CT adds |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------------|--------------|
| **Structural valve deterioration (leaflet calcification, tear or fusion)** | Restricted leaflet excursion, thickened or fused leaflets. | TEE shows loss of leaflet opening, preserved‑to‑focal‑motion; color Doppler confirms elevated peak velocity. | CT demonstrates cusp thickening, calcium burden, and any intracavitary stenosis (e.g., Mahle‑type leaflet tear). It also accurately measures the minimal intra‑prosthetic lumen (as in this patient—min lumen diameter 13.7 mm). |
| **Prosthetic valve thrombosis** | Hypo‑attenuating filling defect, often mid‑cycle; may coexist with thrombus on radiopaque stent posts. | TEE can identify an echodense mass on the cusp or stent strut and delayed leaflet opening. | CT, especially with late‑phase contrast, shows a low‑density filling defect that does not calcify. |
| **Valve malexpansion or malposition** | Under‑expanded stent posts; high‑deployment angle; incomplete apposition to annulus. | TEE displays uniformly high gradients but provides no precise geometry; it may note an “abnormal” valve orientation. | CT is definitive: 3‑D reconstruction shows stent diameter at each level, depth relative to annulus, and any unfolding of a prior frame (as in this case, where the Evolut frame’s minimal lumen dictated the need for a third valve). |
| **Valve–patient mismatch (PPM)** | Small annulus relative to valve size, leading to a low effective orifice area despite proper expansion. | TTE/TEE calculates AVA via continuity equation; if AVA <1.0 cm² and gradient high, PPM is suspected. | CT measures annular diameter and area, permitting prediction of indexed AVA for the chosen valve size. |
| **LV outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction** | Papillary muscle or hypertrophic septum encroaching on the LVOT, causing a gradient that can mimic valve dysfunction. | TEE visualizes LVOT flow patterns; color Doppler shows high velocity jets just below the annulus. | CT demonstrates anatomic LVOT diameter, septal thickness, and any subvalvular obstruction. |
| **Coronary obstruction or ostial compromise** | Decreased coronary perfusion, absent flow beyond an occlusion. | TEE cannot reliably assess coronary flow; only indirect signs (e.g., unclear flow in the aortic root) may be noted. | CT precisely measures coronary ostial height and distance to prosthesis, allowing assessment of potential obstruction. |
| **Residual or new paravalvular leak (PVL)** | Regurgitant jet around the prosthesis; can coexist with high gradient if coupled with leaflet restriction. | TEE is the gold‑standard for detecting and grading PVL via color Doppler. | CT localizes the PVL aperture and quantifies its size relative to prosthesis geometry. |

**Integration in practice**

In this patient, routine TTE initially revealed the abrupt rise in gradient and AVA. Because TEE’s acoustic shadowing limits precise assessment of the complex three‑layered prosthetic architecture, we proceeded with multidetector‑CT imaging. The CT quantified the Evolut prosthesis lumen as only 13.7 mm, confirming severe structural limitation and guiding the decision to implant a self‑expanding Acurate neo2 at a high position to preserve central flow and coronary clearance. Post‑procedural TEE confirmed normal leaflet motion and a transient high gradient that resolved with post‑dilation—consistent with under‑expansion rather than new thrombosis or infection. Subsequent follow‑up TTE demonstrated a stable mean gradient of 27 mm Hg and AVA of 1.1 cm².

**Teaching point**  
For any patient with new severe prosthetic aortic stenosis after TAV‑in‑TAV, a dual‑modality approach is essential: TEE for dynamic valve function and identification of thrombus or valve‑related defects; CT for detailed anatomic assessment of valve expansion, frame interaction, LVOT size, and coronary artery safety. This strategy enables precise etiologic diagnosis and tailored re‑intervention, as illustrated by the successful TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV outcome in this case.
