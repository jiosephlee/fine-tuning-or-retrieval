Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Morning Report] How does the glucagon stimulation test aid in the assessment of β‑cell function in suspected MODY, and what does the patient’s result indicate about his disease stage?

**Answer – How the glucagon stimulation test fits into the work‑up of MODY and what the patient’s result tells us about his disease stage**

The glucagon stimulation test (GST) is a bedside‐level dynamic assessment of residual β‑cell mass. After an intravenous bolus of 1 mg glucagon, insulin (and therefore C‑peptide) secretion from pancreatic β‑cells is acutely amplified via the cAMP‑mediated pathway. The magnitude of the rise in C‑peptide therefore reflects how much functional β‑cell tissue remains and is useful when the differential diagnosis includes type 1 diabetes (autoimmune β‑cell loss), type 2 diabetes (relative insulin insufficiency with insulin resistance), or a β‑cell–specific genetic defect such as MODY.

| **Interpretation of GST (stimulated C‑peptide)** | **Implication** |
|---------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| **>0.60 nmol L⁻¹ (or >1.8 ng mL⁻¹)**               | Preserved endogenous secretion → argues against absolute β‑cell destruction (i.e., classic type 1). |
| **0.30–0.60 nmol L⁻¹**                           | Moderately reduced secretion → indicates β‑cell dysfunction but not complete loss; seen in early‑stage MODY or advanced type 2. |
| **<0.30 nmol L⁻¹**                               | Severely impaired → compatible with longstanding type 1 or end‑stage β‑cell failure. |

In the reported case the GST showed:

- **Fasting C‑peptide 0.39 nmol L⁻¹** (baseline, low but detectable)  
- **6‑minute stimulated C‑pep 0.83 nmol L⁻¹** (increase of ≈ 0.44 nmol L⁻¹, still below the 0.60 nmol L⁻¹ “preserved” threshold)

**What this means for the patient**

1. **β‑cell function is still present but markedly diminished.**  
   The rise after glucagon is real (≈ 110 % increase) yet the absolute value remains below the level that would be considered “preserved.” This pattern is classic for **HNF1A‑MODY in the “moderate” stage**, where the transcription‑factor defect has already produced a progressive loss of insulin secretory capacity but has not yet resulted in complete β‑cell exhaustion.

2. **Helps exclude classic type 1 diabetes.**  
   In autoimmune diabetes the C‑peptide response to glucagon is usually <0.30 nmol L⁻¹ even early after diagnosis because the autoimmune attack rapidly depletes β‑cell mass. The patient’s modest but measurable response, together with negative GADA/IA‑2A, makes type 1 unlikely.

3. **Distinguishes from pure insulin‑resistant type 2 diabetes.**  
   In early‑stage type 2 patients, fasting C‑peptide is often >0.6 nmol L⁻¹ and the glucagon‑stimulated value exceeds 1 nmol L⁻¹, reflecting a relatively robust β‑cell reserve that is “over‑working.” Our patient’s low baseline and blunted peak point toward an intrinsic secretory defect rather than simple insulin resistance.

4. **Provides a baseline for therapeutic planning and follow‑up.**  
   Because HNF1A‑MODY patients retain some β‑cell capacity, they respond well to agents that amplify insulin secretion (low‑dose sulfonylureas, GLP‑1 receptor agonists). The GST result reassured the team that withdrawing insulin was safe once glycaemia was controlled, and it gave a quantitative reference to monitor future decline (e.g., repeat GST after several years).

**Take‑home teaching point**

The glucagon stimulation test is not a diagnostic test for MODY per se, but it is an inexpensive, physiologically meaningful adjunct that:

- **Demonstrates preserved but impaired β‑cell reserve** (the hallmark of many MODY subtypes, especially HNF1A),  
- **Helps rule out autoimmune β‑cell loss**, and  
- **Informs choice and timing of β‑cell–targeted therapy**.

In this young man, a stimulated C‑peptide of 0.83 nmol L⁻¹ signals **moderate β‑cell dysfunction**, consistent with an intermediate disease stage of HNF1A‑MODY—enough residual function to allow safe transition off insulin to a GLP‑1 receptor agonist–based regimen, yet insufficient to rely on diet alone. Continual monitoring of C‑peptide (fasting or GST) can track disease progression and guide future therapeutic adjustments.
