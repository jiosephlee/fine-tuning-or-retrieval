Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why are SGLT‑2 inhibitors contraindicated or used with caution in patients with HNF1A‑MODY?

**Why SGLT‑2 inhibitors are contraindicated or must be used with great caution in HNF1A‑MODY**

HNF1A‑MODY is caused by loss‑of‑function mutations in the transcription factor **HNF‑1α**, which controls expression of several renal tubular proteins, most importantly the **sodium‑glucose co‑transporter‑2 (SGLT2)**. In normal kidney proximal tubules HNF‑1α drives synthesis of SGLT2, the carrier that reabsorbs ~90 % of filtered glucose. When HNF1A is defective, SGLT2 expression is intrinsically reduced, so the renal glucose‑threshold is already low and patients frequently present with **glycosuria despite modest plasma glucose levels** (the case report notes “glycosuria…attribut­ed to reduced expression of SGLT2”).

SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) act by *further* blocking the residual transport capacity. In HNF1A‑MODY this produces three inter‑related hazards:

1. **Exaggerated osmotic diuresis → volume depletion**  
   The kidney is already “leaking” glucose and water. Adding an SGLT2 blocker can precipitate marked dehydration, orthostatic hypotension, and in extreme cases vascular collapse. The case discussion explicitly states that “HNF1A‑MODY patients may experience severe dehydration and diabetic ketoacidosis due to high inhibition of residual SGLT‑2 activity.”

2. **Euglycaemic (or normoglycaemic) diabetic keto‑acidosis (DKA)**  
   With less insulin secretion (the patient’s stimulated C‑peptide was only 0.83 nmol/L), the counter‑regulatory milieu favours lipolysis. The additional glucosuria removes a substrate for glycolysis, shifts metabolism toward ketogenesis, and can trigger DKA even when blood glucose is not markedly elevated. This phenomenon is well‑described for SGLT2 inhibitors and is amplified when baseline SGLT2 activity is already low.

3. **Increased risk of genital and urinary infections**  
   Chronic glucosuria creates a nutrient‑rich environment for fungi and bacteria. The case report lists “genital infections” among the adverse events that may outweigh any potential cardiovascular benefit of the drug class in this population.

Because the therapeutic goal in HNF1A‑MODY is to **preserve residual β‑cell function and avoid hypoglycaemia**, the standard first‑line agents are low‑dose sulfonylureas or, as illustrated in this patient, GLP‑1 receptor agonists plus metformin. Both improve glycaemia without further impairing renal glucose reabsorption.

**Bottom line for the bedside:**  
In HNF1A‑MODY the kidney already has a reduced SGLT2‑mediated glucose‑reabsorption threshold, so pharmacologic SGLT2 inhibition adds little glycaemic benefit while markedly raising the risk of dehydration, euglycaemic DKA, and infections. Consequently, SGLT‑2 inhibitors are either contraindicated or should be used only after careful risk‑benefit assessment, with strict monitoring of hydration status, ketones, and renal function.
