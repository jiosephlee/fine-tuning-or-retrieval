Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe how the ACMG criteria were applied to classify the novel HNF1A p.N270S variant as pathogenic/likely pathogenic in this report.

**How the ACMG/AMP criteria were used to classify the novel HNF1A p.N270S variant as pathogenic/likely‑pathogenic**

The authors applied the 2015 ACMG/AMP framework (Richards et al., *Genet Med* 2015) by gathering **independent lines of evidence** that fall into the three strength categories (very strong, strong, moderate, supporting). For a single heterozygous miss‑missense change in an autosomal‑dominant disease such as HNF1A‑MODY, the combination **PVS1** (null‑variant) is not applicable; instead the classification rested on **PS**, **PM**, and **PP** criteria as follows:

| ACMG code | Requirement | Evidence supplied in the report | Strength |
|-----------|-------------|--------------------------------|----------|
| **PM2**   | Variant absent from population databases (gnomAD, 1000 Genomes, ExAC) | The authors state that p.N270S “was not reported in the literature, VarSome or ClinVar” and is not present in population frequency repositories. | Moderate |
| **PM5**   | Novel miss‑missense change at an amino‑acid residue where a different pathogenic miss‑missense change has been established | Asparagine 270 lies next to residues (e.g., N266K, N271S) that are already classified as pathogenic in HNF1A. The authors note that the variant alters an amino‑acid **adjacent to known pathogenic residues** in the DNA‑binding domain. | Moderate |
| **PM1**   | Located in a mutational hot‑spot or critical functional domain without benign variation | p.N270S resides in exon 4, within the well‑characterised DNA‑binding domain (aa 150‑274) where >70 % of HNF1A pathogenic variants cluster. No benign variants are reported in this sub‑region. | Moderate |
| **PP3**   | Multiple in‑silico tools predict a deleterious effect | The AlphaMissense deep‑learning score for p.N270S is 0.995 (near‑maximal), supporting a damaging impact. The authors cite this high score as computational evidence. | Supporting |
| **PP4**   | Patient’s phenotype is highly specific for a disease with a single gene etiology | The proband has classic HNF1A‑MODY features: early‑onset non‑ketoacytic hyperglycaemia, autosomal‑dominant family history, negative pancreatic auto‑antibodies, modestly preserved C‑peptide, and glycosuria (reflecting renal SGLT2 down‑regulation). This phenotype matches the known HNF1A‑MODY spectrum. | Supporting |
| **PP1 (co‑segregation)** | Variant segregates with disease in multiple affected family members | The report indicates that the same heterozygous p.N270S mutation is present in the mother and brother, both with early‑onset diabetes, establishing co‑segregation across ≥ 2 affected relatives. | Supporting |
| **PP5** (optional) | Previously reported as pathogenic in a reputable source | Not applicable because the variant is novel; the authors explicitly state it is absent from ClinVar/VarSome. | – |

**Summation of criteria**

- **Three moderate** criteria (PM2, PM5, PM1)  
- **Three supporting** criteria (PP3, PP4, PP1)

According to the ACMG combinatorial rules, **≥ 2 moderate + ≥ 1 supporting** is sufficient for a **“likely pathogenic”** classification. The presence of **three supporting** lines pushes the interpretation toward the upper end of the “likely pathogenic” range; some laboratories would upgrade to **“pathogenic”** when strong functional data were available, but in this case the authors conservatively reported the variant as **pathogenic/likely‑pathogenic (P/LP)**, reflecting the weight of the collective evidence.

**Key teaching points**

1. **Population absence (PM2)** is critical for novel variants; a low allele frequency (<0.001) in large reference cohorts argues against a benign polymorphism.  
2. **Domain‐specific clustering (PM1)** and **adjacent known pathogenic residues (PM5)** provide disease‑specific context that is especially powerful for transcription factors like HNF1A, where the DNA‑binding domain is a mutational hotspot.  
3. **Co‑segregation (PP1)**, even with only two additional affected relatives, adds strong phenotypic concordance, reinforcing the genetic link.  
4. **High‑confidence in‑silico prediction (PP3)** (e.g., AlphaMissense > 0.9) contributes supporting evidence but never substitutes for functional data.  
5. **Phenotype specificity (PP4)** is a reminder that molecular classification must be anchored to the clinical picture; the classic HNF1A‑MODY traits in this patient make the genetic finding highly plausible.

By systematically applying these ACMG criteria, the authors demonstrated that the novel HNF1A p.N270S variant fulfills the evidence thresholds required for a likely‑pathogenic classification, justifying its use in guiding precision therapy for the patient and his affected relatives.
