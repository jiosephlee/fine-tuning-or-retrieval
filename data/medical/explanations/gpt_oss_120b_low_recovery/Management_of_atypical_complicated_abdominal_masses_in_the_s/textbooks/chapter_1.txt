Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation and Initial Assessment  

The patient was a 42‑year‑old nulliparous woman in the early second trimester of pregnancy (21 weeks gestation). Her pre‑pregnancy body mass index was 33 kg/m², placing her in the obese category and adding an element of physiological stress that can amplify both obstetric and surgical considerations. She had no prior live births (G1 P0 A1) and her first‑trimester screening, including non‑invasive prenatal testing, had demonstrated a low risk for chromosomal aneuploidies and a normal fetal anatomy survey.  

### Chief complaints  

At presentation she reported **acute, severe pain** localized to the **upper left abdominal quadrant** as well as to the **pelvic region**. Both pain sites were rated 5 cm on a 10‑cm Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). Concomitantly she described **mild dyspnoea** that worsened when she attempted to lie supine, suggesting a restrictive effect of a large intra‑abdominal mass on diaphragmatic excursion.  

### Vital signs and general status  

On initial assessment in the emergency department her vital parameters were within physiologic limits for pregnancy:  

| Parameter | Value | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Oxygen saturation (SpO₂) | 97 % (room air) | Adequate oxygenation |
| Heart rate | 74 bpm | Normal sinus rhythm |
| Blood pressure | 100/60 mmHg | Normotensive for gestation |
| Temperature | Afebrile initially; later low‑grade fever (≈38 °C) | Evolving inflammatory response |
| Respiratory rate | 16 /min | Within normal range |

A 12‑lead electrocardiogram was unremarkable, and there were no signs of hemodynamic compromise.  

### Physical examination  

**Abdominal inspection** revealed a uterus larger than expected for 21 weeks, with a palpable, taut‑elastic mass occupying the entire left sub‑phrenic area. The mass was **approximately 18 cm** in greatest dimension, smooth, non‑fluctuant, and markedly tender on palpation; there were no stigmata of ascites or peritoneal irritation.  

**Pelvic (bimanual) examination** disclosed a **fixed, rounded, hard mass** in the lesser pelvis measuring roughly **9 cm**, bulging slightly into the posterior fornix. The uterus was palpable but displaced superiorly by the upper mass; cervical motion was within normal limits and no cervical discharge was noted.  

These findings raised immediate concern for **space‑occupying lesions** of uterine or adnexal origin that could compromise maternal respiration, uterine blood flow, or fetal growth.  

### Laboratory investigations  

Initial blood work reflected several pregnancy‑related and infection‑related abnormalities:  

* **Hemoglobin 9.9 g/dL** (reference 12.0–16.6 g/dL) and **serum ferritin 25 µg/L**, consistent with iron‑deficiency anemia, a frequent accompaniment of obesity and high‑parity pregnancies.  
* **White blood cell count 11.3 × 10³/µL** with a neutrophilic predominance (9.3 × 10³/µL), modestly elevated above the normal pregnancy range (4–10 × 10³/µL).  
* **C‑reactive protein 1.39 mg/dL** (reference ≤ 0.3 mg/dL), indicating an acute‑phase response.  
* **Urine culture** grew *Klebsiella pneumoniae* highly sensitive to cephalosporins, establishing a concurrent urinary tract infection that could contribute to the low‑grade fever.  
* **COVID‑19 nasopharyngeal PCR** was negative, excluding SARS‑CoV‑2 as a contributor to respiratory symptoms.  

Serum tumor markers—including CA 19‑9, CA 15‑3, CEA, β2‑microglobulin, and LDH—were within normal limits. **CA‑125** was modestly elevated at **41.8 U/mL** (reference < 35 U/mL), with a rising trend from 36 U/mL measured two weeks earlier. Although CA‑125 rises physiologically during early pregnancy, a value above the expected median for the second trimester warranted attention when combined with imaging findings.  

### Initial symptom control  

Analgesia with **intravenous paracetamol 1 g twice daily** and semi‑orthostatic positioning (head of bed elevated 30–45°) were instituted promptly. The positioning aimed to reduce diaphragmatic compression by the gravid uterus and the large abdominal masses. This regimen decreased the VAS pain scores from 5 to 1 cm, indicating effective short‑term relief without the need for opioid analgesics, which could jeopardize fetal well‑being. No supplemental oxygen or ventilatory support was required.  

### Multidisciplinary triage  

Given the complexity of a pregnant patient with large intra‑abdominal masses, a **multidisciplinary team (MDT)** was assembled immediately. The core members included:

* **Emergency medicine physicians** for initial stabilization and infection control.  
* **Obstetricians** to evaluate fetal viability, gestational age, and obstetric risk.  
* **Maternal‑fetal medicine specialists** to interpret ultrasound findings and counsel regarding pregnancy‑specific imaging constraints.  
* **Radiologists** experienced in obstetric ultrasound and non‑contrast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), essential for safe lesion characterization.  
* **Gynecologic oncologists/surgeons** prepared for potential operative intervention if oncologic suspicion or acute complications arose.  

The MDT conducted a rapid risk‑benefit analysis: the patient’s worsening pain and dyspnoea, the size and vascularity of the masses, evolving fever, and the minimal but rising CA‑125 suggested that a **definitive diagnosis** was needed promptly to avert maternal or fetal compromise. This collaborative assessment set the stage for targeted imaging (ultrasound followed by non‑contrast MRI) and, ultimately, the decision to proceed with surgical exploration when conservative measures proved insufficient.  

In summary, the initial presentation combined **obstetric, surgical, and infectious** elements that required a systematic, coordinated approach. Careful documentation of the patient’s demographics, symptomatology, physical findings, baseline vitals, and laboratory data guided the subsequent diagnostic pathway and informed the multidisciplinary decision‑making process.
