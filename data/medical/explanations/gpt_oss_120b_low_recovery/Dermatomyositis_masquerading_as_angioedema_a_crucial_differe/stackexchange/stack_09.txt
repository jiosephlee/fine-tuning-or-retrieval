Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] Why is creatine kinase a useful screening test in atypical cases of presumed angioedema, and what CK threshold should raise suspicion for an inflammatory myopathy?

**Answer**

Creatine kinase (CK) is a rapid, inexpensive laboratory test that measures skeletal‑muscle injury. In the setting of “angioedema‑like” neck swelling, an elevated CK points to an inflammatory myopathy rather than a true histamine‑ or bradykinin‑mediated edema.  

**Why CK is useful in atypical “angioedema”**

1. **Pathophysiologic clue** – True angioedema results from increased vascular permeability without muscle necrosis, so CK remains normal. Dermatomyositis (and other inflammatory myopathies) produces necrotic and regenerating muscle fibers, releasing large amounts of CK into the serum.  
2. **Diagnostic discriminator** – When a patient presents with unexplained deep neck or facial swelling, the differential includes infectious deep‑space cellulitis, Ludwig’s angina, hereditary/acquired angioedema, and myopathic edema. A markedly raised CK (>5 000 U/L) is not seen in infectious or bradykinin‑mediated processes, but is characteristic of myositis.  
3. **Prompt triage** – CK results are typically available within hours. An early abnormal value can shift the work‑up from airway‑focused imaging and antibiotics to rheumatologic serologies, MRI of the neck, and muscle biopsy, expediting definitive therapy.  

**Threshold that raises suspicion for inflammatory myopathy**

- **CK ≥ 5 000 U/L** (≈10‑15× the upper limit of normal) is strongly suggestive of an active inflammatory myopathy and should trigger a myositis work‑up (myositis‑specific antibody panel, MRI, EMG, or biopsy).  
- Values **> 10 000 U/L**, as in the presented case (16 184 U/L), make an inflammatory myopathy virtually certain and practically exclude isolated angioedema.  

In the reported patient, the neck edema was “pseudo‑angioedema” produced by diffuse muscle inflammation; the CK of 16 184 U/L prompted timely serologic testing (SAE‑1 antibodies), imaging, and biopsy, leading to the correct diagnosis of dermatomyositis and the initiation of high‑dose steroids, IVIG, mycophenolate and rituximab.  

**Take‑home point for the bedside clinician**

When confronted with unexplained neck or facial swelling that lacks the classic triggers of histaminergic or bradykinin‑mediated angioedema (no ACE‑I, no urticaria, normal complement, atypical chronic course), order a CK. A result **≥ 5 000 U/L** should raise immediate concern for an inflammatory myopathy such as dermatomyositis and redirect evaluation and treatment accordingly.
