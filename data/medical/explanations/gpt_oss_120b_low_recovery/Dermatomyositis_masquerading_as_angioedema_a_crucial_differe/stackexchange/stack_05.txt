Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] How does the presence of anti‑SAE1 antibodies influence the risk stratification for malignancy in dermatomyositis patients, and what cancer screening protocol is recommended for this high‑risk group?

**Anti‑SAE1 Antibodies and Malignancy Risk Stratification in Dermatomyositis**

In the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) cancer‑screening algorithm, dermatomyositis (DM) itself is a high‑risk factor for occult malignancy. Additional serologic and clinical variables are then used to up‑ or down‑grade that risk.  

| Variable | Relative weight in the IMACS schema |
|----------|--------------------------------------|
| **DM diagnosis** | High |
| **Dysphagia** | High |
| **Male sex** | Intermediate |
| **Anti‑SAE1 positivity** | Intermediate |
| **Other myositis antibodies (e.g., anti‑TIF1‑γ, anti‑NXP‑2)** | High |
| **Age > 60 yr** | High |

**How anti‑SAE1 modifies risk**

- Anti‑SAE1 antibodies are not among the “high‑risk” myositis antibodies (TIF1‑γ, NXP‑2, Mi‑2) that are strongly linked to cancer.  
- In the large review cited in the case report (215 patients with anti‑SAE1 DM), *malignancy was reported in 18 %*, compared with an overall cancer incidence of ≈15‑20 % in all DM cohorts.  
- When combined with other intermediate‑risk features (male sex, age in the 40‑s, dysphagia), anti‑SAE1 moves a patient from a “moderate” to a “high‑risk” category under the IMACS model. The case patient had **four risk factors** (DM, dysphagia, male gender, anti‑SAE1), satisfying the definition of *high‑risk*.

**Recommended cancer‑screening protocol for anti‑SAE1‑positive, high‑risk DM**

1. **Baseline comprehensive evaluation (within the first 3–6 months of diagnosis)**
   - **History & physical** focused on lymphadenopathy, breast, genital, and skin lesions.
   - **Laboratory panel**: CBC, CMP, ESR/CRP, LDH, and a standard tumor‑marker panel (CEA, CA‑19‑9, CA‑125, PSA in men).  
   - **Cross‑sectional imaging**:
     - **CT chest‑abdomen‑pelvis with contrast** (or whole‑body PET/CT if available) to detect solid organ malignancies and nodal disease.  
     - **PET‑CT** is especially useful in DM because it can also monitor myositis activity, as was done in the case (no metabolically active tumor identified).
   - **Endoscopic studies**:
     - **Upper GI endoscopy** and **colonoscopy** to rule out occult gastrointestinal cancers, given the known association of DM with GI malignancies.

2. **Interval surveillance (for the first 2–3 years, the period of highest oncologic risk)**
   - **Clinical review** every 3‑4 months with targeted physical examination and symptom review (weight loss, new skin lesions, night sweats, etc.).  
   - **Laboratory repeat** of tumor markers and inflammatory markers at each visit.  
   - **Imaging**:
     - **CT chest‑abdomen‑pelvis** or **PET‑CT** annually for the first 2 years; thereafter, imaging can be spaced to every 2 years if prior studies remain negative and the patient remains asymptomatic.  
   - **Endoscopy**: If the baseline endoscopies were negative, repeat colonoscopy is recommended at 5 years (or earlier if symptoms arise); upper endoscopy can be repeated at 3‑5 years based on clinical judgment.

3. **Trigger‑driven re‑evaluation**
   - Any new systemic symptom (e.g., unexplained weight loss, persistent cough, new skin nodules) should prompt an **immediate repeat imaging** (CT or PET) irrespective of the surveillance calendar.

**Key teaching points**

- **Anti‑SAE1 antibodies are an intermediate‑risk serologic marker**; they do not confer the same high cancer risk as anti‑TIF1‑γ or anti‑NXP‑2, but in the presence of other high‑risk features (DM itself, dysphagia, male sex) they push the patient into the high‑risk category.  
- **Screening must be aggressive and multimodal**: combine thorough history/physical, broad laboratory tumor markers, contrast‑enhanced cross‑sectional imaging, and GI endoscopy.  
- **Follow‑up is essential**: most DM‑associated cancers are diagnosed within the first 2 years; therefore, repeat imaging at least annually during that window is recommended.  
- The case exemplifies this approach: the patient underwent CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, PET‑CT, tumor‑marker panel, and planned upper/lower GI endoscopy, all of which have remained negative at 12 months, illustrating that diligent early screening can provide reassurance and a baseline for future surveillance.

In summary, anti‑SAE1 positivity upgrades a dermatomyositis patient to **high‑risk** for malignancy when accompanied by dysphagia and male sex, and warrants a **structured, baseline work‑up plus scheduled annual imaging (CT or PET) and GI endoscopy** for the first 2‑3 years, with symptom‑directed reassessments thereafter.
