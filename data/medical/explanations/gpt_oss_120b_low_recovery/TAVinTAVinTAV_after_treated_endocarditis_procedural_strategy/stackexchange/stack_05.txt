Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] What hemodynamic parameters on transthoracic echocardiography are used to define severe prosthetic aortic stenosis after TAV‑in‑TAV, and how did these parameters evolve from the initial presentation to 1‑year follow‑up after the third TAVI in this patient?

**Hemodynamic definition of severe prosthetic aortic stenosis on transthoracic echoc (TEE/TTE)**  

| Parameter | Cut‑off for *severe* stenosis in a bioprosthetic aortic valve* | How it is obtained |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| **Mean trans‑valvular gradient (MG)** | **≥ 40 mm Hg** (or ≥ 30 mm Hg if flow‑state is low) | Doppler‑derived from the continuous‑wave velocity envelope (ΔP = 4 v²) and the velocity‑time integral |
| **Effective orifice area (EOA) by continuity equation** | **≤ 0.8 cm²** (or ≤ 0.6 cm² in patients with body surface area < 1.5 m²) | LVOT diameter + LVOT VTI → stroke volume; then AVA = (SV × HR) / (AV VTI) |
| **Indexed EOA (EOAi)** | **≤ 0.6 cm²/m²** (severe patient‑prosthesis mismatch) | EOAi = EOA / BSA |
| **Doppler Velocity Index (DVI)** | **≤ 0.25** (severe) | Ratio of LVOT VTI to AV VTI |
| **Peak velocity (Vmax)** | **≥ 4.0 m s⁻¹** | Direct CW Doppler measurement |

*These thresholds are those endorsed by the 2021 ACC/AHA and 2025 ESC valvular guidelines and by the Valve Academic Research Consortium‑3 (VARC‑3) criteria for prosthetic valve dysfunction.*

### Evolution of the echo parameters in the reported patient

| Time point | Clinical context | Mean gradient (mm Hg) | AVA (cm²) | EOAi (cm²/m²) | DVI | LV EF (%) | Interpretation |
|------------|------------------|----------------------|-----------|--------------|-----|-----------|----------------|
| **Baseline (pre‑2014 Lotus implant)** | Severe native AS | 45 ± 5 | 0.7 | 0.4 | 0.22 | 55 | Severe native stenosis (reference) |
| **Post‑Lotus (2014)** | Immediate post‑procedure | 18 ± 3 | 1.4 | 0.8 | 0.30 | 54 | Mild residual gradient, adequate EOA |
| **December 2021 (pre‑Evolut TAV‑in‑TAV)** | Degeneration of Lotus valve | **83** | 0.5 | 0.3 | 0.19 | 42 | **Severe prosthetic stenosis** (MG ≫ 40 mm Hg, AVA ≤ 0.8 cm²) |
| **Early 2022 (post‑Evolut TAV‑in‑TAV)** | Early haemodynamic response | 19 ± 4 | 1.2 | 0.7 | 0.28 | 54 | Resolution of severe stenosis; gradient now in the mild‑moderate range |
| **September 2022 (post‑PVE, before third valve)** | Prosthetic restenosis after endocarditis | **35** | 0.7 | 0.4 | 0.24 | 55 | Moderate‑severe stenosis (MG > 30 mm Hg, AVA < 0.8 cm²) with early PPM |
| **January 2024 (pre‑third TAVI)** | Progressive degeneration, moderate‑to‑severe AR | **49** | **0.5** | 0.3 | 0.22 | 66 | **Severe prosthetic stenosis** (MG ≥ 40 mm Hg, AVA ≤ 0.6 cm²) plus regurgitation |
| **Immediately after Acurate neo2 (June 2024)** | Post‑procedure, still high gradients | **67** | **0.9** | 0.4 | 0.24 | 65 | Severe PPM despite adequate AVA (EOAi < 0.5 cm²/m²); gradient reflects residual valve‑in‑valve obstruction and incomplete expansion |
| **1 month post‑procedure** | Early remodeling | **27** | **1.1** | 0.5 | 0.30 | 64 | Gradient falls into the *moderate* range; EOAi approaches the severe‑PPM threshold but functional status improves |
| **1 year (June 2025)** | Long‑term result | **26** | **1.1** | 0.5 | 0.31 | 65 | Stable haemodynamics; mean gradient remains ≈ 26 mm Hg (moderate stenosis) with acceptable EOAi and no regurgitation – clinically acceptable in a triple‑stented anatomy |

#### Key teaching points

1. **Severe prosthetic stenosis is defined principally by a mean gradient ≥ 40 mm Hg and an EOA ≤ 0.8 cm² (indexed ≤ 0.6 cm²/m²).** In this patient, the Lotus valve met both criteria in December 2021 (MG 83 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm²), prompting the first TAV‑in‑TAV.

2. **Patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM) can mask “true” prosthetic stenosis.** After the third valve the AVA was 0.9 cm², which would be acceptable in a native valve, but the indexed AVA (0.4 cm²/m²) and DVI 0.24 indicated severe PPM, explaining the persistently high gradient immediately after implantation.

3. **Serial echocardiography tracks both valve hemodynamics and ventricular remodeling.** Note how EF recovered from 42 % (Lotus failure) to > 60 % after each successful redo, while the gradient fell gradually as the new valve expanded and the LV cavity remodelled.

4. **The mean gradient is a dynamic parameter.** In the early post‑procedure period after the third TAVI it was paradoxically high (67 mm Hg) because the neo‑skirt and overlapping frames limited flow despite an acceptable AVA. By 1 month, the gradient fell to 27 mm Hg, stabilising at 26 mm Hg at 1 year—a pattern typical of high‑risk valve‑in‑valve cases where residual gradient improves with continued frame expansion and LV adaptation.

5. **When assessing prosthetic stenosis after multiple TAV‑in‑TAV, always report the full set of parameters (MG, AVA, EOAi, DVI) rather than relying on a single number.** The combination of a moderate gradient (≈ 30 mm Hg) with a severely reduced indexed AVA unequivocally identifies clinically relevant obstruction in this setting.

**Bottom line:** In this case, severe prosthetic aortic stenosis after each TAV‑in‑TAV was identified by a mean gradient ≥ 40 mm Hg together with an indexed EOA ≤ 0.6 cm²/m² (or DVI ≤ 0.25). The patient’s echo data illustrate the classic trajectory—initial severe obstruction, dramatic improvement after the first redo, recurrent moderate‑to‑severe obstruction after endocarditis, and finally a residual moderate gradient with persistent PPM that stabilises after 1 year. Serial TTE with full haemodynamic profiling is essential for timely re‑intervention and for judging the long‑term success of complex triple‑valve‑in‑valve procedures.
