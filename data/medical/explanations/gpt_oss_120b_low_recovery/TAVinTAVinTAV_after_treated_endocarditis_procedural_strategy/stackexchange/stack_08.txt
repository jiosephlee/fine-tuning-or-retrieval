Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Identify the key components of Valve Academic Research Consortium‑3 (VARC‑3) criteria used to define procedural success in this case, and indicate which endpoints were met immediately post‑procedure versus at 1‑year follow‑up.

**VARC‑3 procedural‑success criteria – what they are and how they were met in this case**

The Valve Academic Research Consortium‑3 (VARC‑3) defines **procedural success** as the simultaneous fulfillment of three domains evaluated at two time points:

| VARC‑3 component | Definition | Evaluated **immediately** after implantation | Evaluated **at 1‑year** follow‑up |
|-----------------|------------|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Device‑success** | • Successful vascular access, delivery and deployment of the intended transcatheter heart valve (THV) <br>• No need for a second THV (i.e., no valve‑in‑valve rescue) <br>• Absence of procedural mortality, stroke, major bleeding, life‑threatening/major vascular complications, or aortic annular rupture <br>• Hemodynamic performance: mean aortic gradient < 20 mm Hg **or** ≥10 mm Hg reduction from baseline **and** no moderate or greater prosthetic aortic regurgitation | ✔︎ The Acurate neo2 (size S) was deployed as planned, with correct positioning, no need for a second valve, and no periprocedural death, stroke, major bleed, or annular rupture.  Immediate post‑procedure aortography showed no perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation. | ✔︎ At 12 months the valve remains in situ, there has been no valve‑related mortality, stroke, or major complications; the prosthesis is still functional. |
| **Hemodynamic success** | • Post‑procedure mean aortic gradient < 20 mm Hg **or** ≥10 mm Hg reduction from the pre‑procedure value **and** <br>• No ≥moderate aortic regurgitation (by echocardiography) | ✖︎ Immediate transthoracic echo showed a mean gradient of **67 mm Hg** with an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² – severe patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM). No regurgitation was present, but the gradient criterion was not met. | ✔︎ By 1 month the mean gradient fell to **27 mm Hg** (≥10 mm Hg reduction) and AVA rose to 1.1 cm² (indexed ≈ 0.5 cm²/m²). At 1 year the gradient remained stable at **26 mm Hg** and AVA 1.1 cm², with no ≥moderate regurgitation. Thus the hemodynamic criterion was satisfied after early remodeling and remained met at 1 year. |
| **Safety‑endpoint** | Composite of any of the following up to 30 days (or 1 year for durability): <br>• All‑cause mortality <br>• Stroke (disabling or non‑disabling) <br>• Life‑threatening/major bleeding <br>• Major vascular complication <br>• Need for new permanent pacemaker implantation (if not present before) <br>• Valve‑related rehospitalisation | ✔︎ No death, stroke, major bleed, or vascular complication occurred during the index hospitalization. The patient already had a permanent pacemaker, so no new device was required. | ✔︎ Throughout the 1‑year interval the patient remained alive, free of stroke, major bleed, vascular events, or valve‑related rehospitalisation (no repeat intervention required). |

### Summary of endpoints in this patient

| Time point | VARC‑3 components met |
|------------|----------------------|
| **Immediately post‑procedure (June 2024)** | • **Device‑success** – yes <br>• **Safety‑endpoint** – yes <br>• **Hemodynamic success** – **no** (gradient 67 mm Hg, severe PPM) |
| **1‑year follow‑up (June 2025)** | • **Device‑success** – sustained <br>• **Hemodynamic success** – yes (mean gradient ≈ 26 mm Hg, AVA ≈ 1.1 cm², no ≥moderate regurgitation) <br>• **Safety‑endpoint** – yes (no deaths, strokes, major bleeds, vascular complications, or new pacemaker requirement) |

Thus, the case fulfills the VARC‑3 definition of procedural success because, although the immediate hemodynamic target was not achieved, **device‑success and safety were met at the index procedure**, and **both hemodynamic and safety criteria were satisfied by the 1‑month mark and remained durable through 1 year**. This illustrates how VARC‑3 allows early “partial” success (device placement without complications) to be followed by favorable remodeling that completes the hemodynamic component of procedural success.
