Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] How does the concept of patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM) apply to serial transcatheter valve replacements, and what strategies (e.g., valve selection, high implantation, commissural alignment) were employed in this case to minimize PPM after the third TAVI?

**Patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM) in serial TAVI‑in‑TAVI procedures**

When a transcatheter valve is placed inside a previously implanted transcatheter prosthesis, the effective orifice area (EOA) is constrained not only by the new valve’s own annular design but also by the inner diameter of the “host” valve frame. With each successive implantation the lumen is progressively narrowed, so the risk of **severe PPM**—indexed EOA ≤ 0.65 cm²/m²—rises dramatically. In this case the patient’s second‑generation Evolut valve left a minimum lumen of 13.7 mm, which would have yielded an indexed EOA of roughly 0.45 cm²/m² if a conventional 23‑mm valve had been placed inside it. The third implantation therefore required a deliberate strategy to **preserve or enlarge the residual EOA**.

### Strategies employed to minimise PPM after the third TAVI

| Goal | How it was achieved in this case | Rationale |
|------|----------------------------------|-----------|
| **Choose a valve with the most favourable supra‑annular EOA** | Implantation of an **Acurate neo2 (size S)**, a self‑expanding valve whose leaflets sit above the stent frame and whose design is known to produce larger EOAs in narrow annuli. | Supra‑annular leaflets exploit the maximal internal diameter of the existing frame, mitigating the “double‑ring” effect that characterises TAV‑in‑TAV. |
| **High (shallower) implantation depth** | The Acurate neo2 was positioned **high‑on‑the‑Evolut leaflets** (approximately 3–4 mm above the previous valve’s inflow edge) rather than low in the annulus. | By anchoring the new valve higher, the residual neo‑skirt sits above the inner frame of the Evolut, leaving a larger central lumen for flow and avoiding “pinching” of the EOA that occurs when the device lands deep in a pre‑existing narrow conduit. |
| **Commissural alignment with the prior valve** | Pre‑procedural CT modelling defined the orientation of the Evolut leaflets; the Acurate neo2 was rotated so that its commissures **aligned with those of the Evolut**. | Aligned commissures prevent overlapping of the stent struts in the coronary plane, preserving the widest possible central orifice and also protecting future coronary access. |
| **Aggressive post‑dilatation** | After deployment, a 20 × 40 mm balloon was inflated under rapid pacing. | High‑pressure post‑dilatation expands both the new valve and, to a lesser extent, the underlying Evolut frame, increasing the effective lumen diameter and reducing residual gradient. |
| **CT‑guided sizing and landing‑zone selection** | Multiplanar MSCT measured the minimal lumen (13.7 mm) and evaluated coronary ostial distances. The chosen Acurate neo2 size S (outer diameter ≈ 23 mm) was the smallest that could still achieve full expansion within that envelope. | Precise sizing avoids oversizing (which could cause annular injury) while ensuring the new valve fully expands, maximising the EOA. |
| **Avoidance of valve‑in‑valve “stacking” of leaflets** | By aligning the new valve’s leaflets with the previous leaflets, the procedural team ensured that the leaflets of the third valve opened into the central orifice rather than being forced against the inner frame. | Leaflet‑to‑frame interference is a major contributor to PPM; alignment reduces this mechanical obstruction. |

### Outcome confirming the efficacy of these measures
- **Immediate post‑procedure**: despite a high mean gradient (67 mm Hg) and indexed AVA ≈ 0.4 cm²/m²—reflecting inevitable early PPM—the valve was competent (no regurgitation) and coronary flow was preserved.
- **One month**: the mean gradient fell to 27 mm Hg, indexed AVA rose to ≈ 0.6 cm²/m², indicating that the high implantation and post‑dilatation had allowed the previously constrained frame to expand and the supra‑annular leaflets to achieve a larger orifice.
- **One‑year**: the mean gradient stabilized at 26 mm Hg with AVA ≈ 0.9 cm² (indexed ≈ 0.5 cm²/m²), which, in a patient of advanced age and limited functional demand, is clinically acceptable and demonstrates that severe PPM was avoided.

### Take‑home points for future serial TAVI cases
1. **Never treat PPM as an after‑thought**; anticipate it from the first implant by selecting a valve with low frame height and supra‑annular leaflets.
2. **CT‑based 3‑D planning** is essential to measure the inner diameter of the existing valve, the coronary ostial heights, and to model the likely neo‑skirt geometry.
3. **High implantation** (shallow depth) and **commissural alignment** are the most potent maneuvers to preserve the residual EOA in a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV scenario.
4. **Post‑dilatation** should be performed aggressively, but safely, to expand the prior frame as much as the surrounding aortic wall permits.
5. **Document the implantation depth and alignment fluoroscopically** for future re‑interventions; each layer adds complexity to coronary re‑access and to any subsequent valve‑in‑valve.

In this patient, the deliberate combination of a supra‑annular valve (Acurate neo2), high implantation aligned with the existing commissures, and thorough CT‑guided planning successfully limited PPM despite a triple‑stented aortic root, providing durable hemodynamics and a good clinical outcome.
