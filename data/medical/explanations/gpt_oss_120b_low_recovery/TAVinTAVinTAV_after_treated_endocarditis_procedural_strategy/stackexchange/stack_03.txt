Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient with prior TAVI, infective endocarditis, and high surgical risk, outline the algorithmic decision‑making process that leads to selecting a repeat transcatheter valve implantation versus surgical explantation.

**Algorithmic decision‑making for re‑intervention after failed TAVI in a patient with prior prosthetic‑valve endocarditis (PVE) and prohibitive surgical risk**

1. **Confirm that active infection has been eradicated**  
   * Repeated blood cultures – negative.  
   * Trans‑oesophageal echo (TEE) – no vegetations, abscess or paravalvular leak.  
   * Multislice CT – no perivalvular fluid or prosthetic dehiscence.  

   *Rationale*: Current ESC‑IE (2025) guidelines state that repeat valve surgery or trans‑catheter treatment is only permissible after cure of PVE. In the case report the patient fulfilled all these criteria after a 6‑week IV ampicillin‑based course and subsequent oral consolidation.

2. **Quantify the severity of prosthetic valve dysfunction**  
   * Echocardiography: mean gradient > 40 mm Hg, AVA ≤ 0.6 cm², moderate‑to‑severe intraprosthetic regurgitation.  
   * CT: minimal lumen diameter of the existing trans‑catheter valve (13.7 mm) confirming a critically narrowed orifice.  

   *Rationale*: Objective haemodynamic criteria (VIRGIN, VARC‑3) define severe structural valve deterioration (SVD) that mandates re‑intervention.

3. **Assess surgical risk and anatomic feasibility of open explantation**  
   * Risk scores: EuroSCORE II ≈ 27 % (high).  
   * Comorbidities: COPD, CKD, prior GI bleeding, multiple PCI, permanent pacemaker, frailty.  
   * Surgical technical issues: three overlapping stent frames fixed to the aortic wall, likely requiring extensive root replacement.  

   *Rationale*: When EuroSCORE II > 8 % (or STS > 8 %) plus “hostile chest” (prior sternotomies, severe calcification, frailty), surgery is deemed prohibitive. The case report explicitly cites prohibitive surgical risk.

4. **Perform detailed CT‑based anatomic planning for a repeat TAVI**  
   * Aortic root dimensions (annular area, perimeter, LVOT height).  
   * Coronary ostial heights and sinus of Valsalva diameters → assess risk of coronary obstruction.  
   * Valve‑to‑coronary distance after adding a third frame (virtual “neo‑skirt” analysis).  
   * Frame overlap and commissural orientation → plan alignment to preserve coronary access.  

   *Rationale*: In TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV, the cumulative mesh can occlude coronaries; CT is the gold standard for pre‑procedural risk stratification (VARC‑3, 2023 consensus). In the case report, CT showed a safe coronary distance and guided selection of a low‑profile, supra‑annular Acurate neo2.

5. **Choose the trans‑catheter strategy based on the CT findings**  

| Scenario | Preferred Approach |
|----------|--------------------|
| **Adequate coronary clearance** (≥ 4 mm from neo‑skirt) and sufficient annular area for another supra‑annular valve | **Direct TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV** (self‑expanding valve with commissural alignment) |
| **Coronary obstruction risk** (height < 12 mm, sinus < 30 mm) | **Coronary protection** (wire + balloon or “BASILICA” leaf‑laceration) *or* consider **surgical explantation** if protection not feasible |
| **Severe patient‑prosthesis mismatch** (projected indexed AVA < 0.65 cm²/m²) | Consider **valve fracture** of the prior self‑expanding frame before implanting a new valve; if not feasible, surgical option (rare) |

   In the reported patient, CT demonstrated a safe coronary distance, an inner lumen of 13.7 mm, and a low risk of obstruction; therefore a direct TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV with a supra‑annular Acurate neo2 was elected.

6. **Finalize device selection and implantation strategy**  
   * Valve type: self‑expanding, supra‑annular leaflets (Acurate neo2) for maximal effective orifice area in a narrow lumen.  
   * Size: determined by CT‑derived annular perimeter (size S was appropriate).  
   * Implantation depth: **high implantation** aligned with the leaflets of the prior Evolut frame to avoid “neo‑skirt” encroachment on coronaries and to maximize residual EOAs.  
   * Commissural alignment: planned using CT fluoroscopic landmarks to preserve future coronary access.  
   * Adjuncts: optional balloon pre‑dilation (to assess compliance) and post‑dilation (to optimize expansion). No coronary protection required because CT showed > 4 mm clearance.

7. **Post‑procedural assessment**  
   * VARC‑3 criteria: absence of mortality, stroke, major vascular complications, coronary obstruction; trace or none regurgitation.  
   * Hemodynamics: mean gradient < 20 mm Hg, indexed AVA > 0.65 cm²/m², DVI > 0.25.  
   * Imaging surveillance: TTE at discharge, 1 month, 6 months, then yearly; repeat CT if gradients rise.

8. **If any step fails, re‑evaluate the surgical pathway**  
   * Inability to obtain safe coronary clearance → consider **BASILICA** or **valve fracture**; if still unsafe, discuss **high‑risk surgical explantation** (root replacement) with the patient and family, acknowledging the very high operative mortality.

**Summary applied to the case:**  
- Infection cleared → ✅  
- Severe SVD confirmed → ✅  
- EuroSCORE II ≈ 27 % and hostile anatomy → ✅ surgery prohibitive  
- CT showed adequate coronary clearance and sufficient annular dimensions → ✅ repeat TAVI feasible  
- Selected Acurate neo2, high implantation, commissural alignment → ✅ optimal EOAs and future coronary access  
- Procedure succeeded, gradients improved to 27 mm Hg at 1‑month and remained stable at 1‑year.

Thus, the algorithm moves from infection control → severity assessment → surgical risk → detailed CT planning → coronary‑obstruction risk stratification → device/technique selection → execution and surveillance. When all steps favor a catheter‑based approach, a repeat TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV becomes the preferred strategy, as illustrated in this patient.
