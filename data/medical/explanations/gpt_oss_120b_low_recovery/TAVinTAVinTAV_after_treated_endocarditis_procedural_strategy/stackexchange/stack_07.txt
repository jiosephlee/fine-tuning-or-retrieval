Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Compare the hemodynamic performance and design features of the Lotus, Evolut, and Acurate neo2 valves that make each suitable (or not) for use as a valve‑in‑valve platform in a sequential re‑intervention strategy.

**Answer – How the Lotus, Evolut and Acurate neo2 differ with respect to hemodynamics and design when they are used as the “inner” valve in a valve‑in‑valve (ViV) platform**

| Feature | Lotus (Boston Scientific, 2014) | Evolut R/PRO (Medtronic, 2022) | Acurate neo2 (Boston Scientific, 2024) |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Valve type** | *Mechanically‑expanded* with an **active‑locking** frame and a *re‑seating* mechanism. | *Self‑expanding* nitinol frame (re‑positionable) with a supra‑annular leaflet nest. | *Self‑expanding* nitinol frame with a short, “top‑hat” design and supra‑annular leaflets. |
| **Frame height & cell density** | Tall, cylindrical frame (≈20 mm) with **dense mesh** that provides excellent annular sealing but occupies most of the aortic root. | Moderate height (≈23 mm) and **large cells** in the upper third, facilitating coronary access but leaving a sizable frame in the sinus. | Low profile (≈15 mm) with **wide open cells** at the level of the sinus of Valsalva; the “crown” sits just above the annulus. |
| **Leaflet position** | *Intra‑annular* (leaflets sit within the frame). | *Supra‑annular* (leaflets sit above the frame). | *Supra‑annular* (leaflets sit on top of the short frame). |
| **Effective orifice area (EOA) after ViV** | The intra‑annular leaflet geometry combined with the tall frame **reduces the residual EOA**; in a Lotus‑in‑Lotus scenario the mean gradient often remains >30 mm Hg. | Supra‑annular leaflets give a **larger EOA** than intra‑annular devices, but the Evolut’s relatively tall frame can still “stack” with a prior valve, limiting the net orifice if the inner device is also self‑expanding. | The short frame and supra‑annular leaflets produce the **largest residual EOA** of the three, particularly when implanted at the level of a prior Evolut leaflets (as done in the case). Post‑dilatation usually brings the mean gradient <30 mm Hg, as seen at 1‑month follow‑up (27 mm Hg). |
| **Radial force / capacity for frame fracture** | Mechanically expanded with a *fixed* radial force; the frame is not amenable to fracture or further expansion. | Moderate radial force; can be modestly expanded with high‑pressure balloon post‑dilatation, but true “valve fracture” is limited. | Lower radial force but the nitinol frame **expands well with post‑dilatation**, allowing partial reopening of the underlying Evolut frame (the case report notes that high‑pressure post‑dilatation likely increased the Evolut’s lumen). |
| **Commissural alignment & coronary re‑access** | The locking mechanism pins the valve at a predetermined depth; **commissural alignment is poor**, and the dense mesh often obstructs the coronary ostia in a ViV. | The Evolut’s delivery system allows **rotation before release**, giving reasonable commissural alignment; however, the upper frame cells can still block coronary access after a second valve is placed. | The Acurate neo2 is designed for **precise commissural alignment** (crown‑to‑cusp targeting) which was exploited in the case to line the neo2 leaflets with the Evolut leaflets, preserving coronary flow and future access. |
| **Suitability as the *inner* valve in a ViV‑in‑ViV** | **Not ideal** – the tall, dense frame and intra‑annular leaflets increase patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM) and limit coronary clearance when stacked. | Acceptable when used as the *outer* valve (as in the 2022 TAV‑in‑Lotus) because its supra‑annular leaflets improve hemodynamics; however, if it becomes the *inner* valve, the combined frame height can be excessive. | **Best suited** as the *inner* (third) valve in a triple‑stented configuration. Its short height, wide open cells, and supra‑annular leaflets maximize the residual EOA and minimize obstruction of the coronary ostia. The case demonstrates a final mean gradient of 26 mm Hg and an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m², a reasonable result given three overlapping prostheses. |
| **Key hemodynamic takeaway from the case** | After the Lotus (first valve) the patient’s gradient rose to 83 mm Hg → severe stenosis despite a 27 mm valve. | The Evolut (second valve) reduced the mean gradient to 27 mm Hg early, but infection and degeneration later produced a gradient of 49 mm Hg. | The Acurate neo2 (third valve) initially showed a high immediate gradient (67 mm Hg) owing to residual frame overlap, but after post‑dilatation and “high‑implant” technique the 1‑month gradient fell to 27 mm Hg and remained stable at 1 year (26 mm Hg). |

### Why the Acurate neo2 was the only feasible choice for the third intervention

1. **Frame geometry** – The CT‑derived sinus diameter (13.7 mm) left very little room for an additional tall frame. The Acurate neo2’s low profile fits within this constrained space, preserving a central flow channel.

2. **Supra‑annular leaflets** – By placing the neo2 leaflets above the Evolut leaflets, the operators created a **“high‑implant”** configuration that kept the leaflets of the failing Evolut pinned against the stent cage, preventing further reduction of the orifice.

3. **Commissural alignment** – Pre‑procedural CT modeling allowed alignment of the neo2 crowns with the Evolut commissures, limiting the “neo‑skirt” that could otherwise impede coronary flow. This was essential because the patient already had three coronary stents.

4. **Radial expansion capacity** – Post‑dilatation with a 20 mm balloon both optimized the neo2 frame and **expanded the underlying Evolut**, increasing the composite effective orifice area without needing a risky valve fracture.

5. **Coronary safety** – The combination of wide‑cell design and high‑implant strategy kept the coronary ostia >5 mm from any stent strut, eliminating the need for protective wiring or BASILICA.

### Clinical Pearls for ViV‑in‑ViV Planning

| Pearls | Application |
|-------|--------------|
| **Always start with the “future‑proof” valve** – when the first TAVI is performed in a patient likely to need re‑intervention, choose a short, supra‑annular valve (e.g., Acurate neo2) to leave room for later devices. | The case illustrates that the initial Lotus made later ViV more challenging. |
| **Measure the narrowest lumen** (CT‑derived minimal sinus diameter) and compare it with the outer diameter of the intended inner valve; a >2 mm clearance is a practical target. | The 13.7 mm lumen dictated a small‑size, low‑profile neo2. |
| **Plan commissural alignment** to line up the leaflets of the new valve with those of the previous valve; this maximizes the residual EOA and protects coronary access. | Alignment of neo2 with Evolut leaflets was a key procedural step. |
| **Use high‑implant technique** (deploy at the level of the prior valve’s leaflets) when the inner valve is supra‑annular; this pins the previous leaflets against the frame and prevents “double‑hinge” obstruction. | The authors deliberately implanted neo2 at the Evolut leaflets. |
| **Post‑dilatation is not optional** – a high‑pressure balloon can expand both the new valve and the underlying frame, improving gradients without fracture. | Gradient fell from 67 mm Hg immediately after deployment to 27 mm Hg after post‑dilatation. |
| **Coronary protection** – if CT predicts <4 mm clearance after the third valve, consider BASILICA or protective wiring; otherwise, a careful high‑implant can obviate it. | In this patient the CT showed adequate clearance, and no protection was required. |

**Bottom line:**  
- **Lotus** – mechanically expanded, intra‑annular, tall, dense frame → poor residual EOA and high risk of coronary obstruction in a ViV setting; unsuitable as an inner valve in a multi‑stage strategy.  
- **Evolut** – self‑expanding, supra‑annular, moderate height → acceptable as an *outer* valve (first or second stage) but adds considerable frame bulk when stacked.  
- **Acurate neo2** – short, low‑profile, wide‑cell
