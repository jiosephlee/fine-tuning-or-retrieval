Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] After completing antimicrobial therapy for Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis, what follow‑up imaging and laboratory assessments are recommended before undertaking a repeat TAVI, and why?

**Follow‑up assessment after completion of antimicrobial therapy for Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic‑valve endocarditis (PVE) – what to repeat before a redo TAVI and why**

1. **Blood cultures (at least two sets)**
   - **Purpose:** Confirm microbiologic eradication before any further instrumentation of the valve. Persistent bacteremia, even with low‑grade organisms such as E. faecalis, predicts recurrence after valve re‑intervention. In the reported case the heart‑team required *repeated* negative cultures before proceeding to the third TAVI.
   - **Timing:** Obtain the first set on the day of evaluation; a second set 24–48 h later increases sensitivity for low‑level bacteremia.

2. **Trans‑oesophageal echocardiography (TEE)**
   - **Purpose:** Detect residual vegetations, abscesses, or new paravalvular complications that are often missed on transthoracic imaging. TEE is the imaging gold standard for PVE and was used in the case to document complete resolution of infection before the repeat transcatheter procedure.
   - **Key findings to look for:** Absence of mobile echodensities on the prosthetic leaflets, no peri‑annular extension, and stable prosthetic geometry.

3. **Multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) of the aortic root and coronary ostia**
   - **Purpose:** Provides a three‑dimensional road‑map of the existing valve frames, assesses:
     - **Coronary clearance** (distance from the projected neo‑skirt to the coronary ostia) – critical in a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV scenario to avoid coronary obstruction.
     - **Effective orifice area of the current prosthesis** (minimum lumen diameter) – guides selection of the next valve size.
     - **Annular dimensions and leaflet‑frame interaction** – informs implantation depth and whether a “high‑implant” strategy is feasible.
   - **In the case:** MDCT confirmed a 13.7 mm minimum lumen diameter in the Evolut valve and demonstrated adequate coronary height, which allowed the team to plan a high‑positioned Acurate neo2 implantation without coronary protection.

4. **Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE)**
   - **Purpose:** Quantifies residual valve haemodynamics (mean gradient, aortic valve area, regurgitation) and left‑ventricular function. Even after infection is cleared, severe prosthetic stenosis or regurgitation mandates re‑intervention. In the case, TTE showed a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and AVA 0.5 cm², confirming the need for another valve.
   - **When to repeat:** At the end of antimicrobial therapy and again immediately before the planned TAVI to document any interval change.

5. **Inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, ESR)**
   - **Purpose:** Serve as adjuncts to the microbiologic and imaging data. A normal or markedly falling C‑reactive protein after therapy supports infection control, whereas persistent elevation should trigger repeat imaging and cultures. The case report did not detail these labs, but current guideline‑based practice incorporates them in the “end of therapy” work‑up.

---

### Why these assessments are essential before a repeat TAVI

| Assessment | What it rules out / informs | Impact on procedural planning |
|------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Blood cultures** | Ongoing bacteremia → contraindication to any prosthetic manipulation | Guarantees that implanting a new valve will not seed a fresh infection |
| **TEE** | Residual vegetations, abscess, or new paravalvular leaks | Presence would shift management toward surgery or prolonged antibiotics |
| **MDCT** | Coronary obstruction risk, annular geometry, valve‑to‑coronary distances, effective orifice size | Determines feasibility of another TAV‑in‑TAV, choice of valve type (supra‑annular vs intra‑annular), and implantation depth |
| **TTE** | Current haemodynamics (gradient, area, regurgitation) and LV function | Confirms severity of structural valve deterioration and helps set procedural goals (e.g., target gradient) |
| **CRP/ESR** | Systemic inflammatory activity | Supports the microbiologic verdict; persistent elevation may prompt repeat imaging |

In this patient, completion of a 6‑week IV ampicillin regimen, documented sterile repeat cultures, a negative TEE, and a reassuring MDCT (adequate coronary clearance and defined landing zone) collectively satisfied the safety criteria for a third transcatheter valve. Only after these steps did the heart team feel confident to proceed with a CT‑guided, high‑implantation strategy that ultimately yielded durable valve function at 1 year.

**Bottom line:** Before any redo TAVI in the setting of previously treated Enterococcus faecalis PVE, obtain *repeat sterile blood cultures*, a *TEE* to exclude residual infection, *MDCT* for anatomic feasibility and coronary protection, *TTE* to quantify prosthetic dysfunction, and assess *inflammatory markers*. This systematic approach ensures that the infection is truly eradicated and that the anatomy is suitable for safe and effective transcatheter re‑intervention.
