Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Given the patient’s EuroSCORE II of 27 % and extensive comorbidities, summarize the heart‑team decision‑making process that led to selection of a third transcatheter valve rather than redo surgical aortic valve replacement, highlighting how surgical risk, anatomical feasibility, and prior procedural complications were weighed.

**Heart‑Team Decision‑Making for a Third‑In‑Series TAVI (EuroSCORE II ≈ 27 %)**

1. **Quantitative surgical risk**  
   - EuroSCORE II = 27 % (predicted operative mortality ≈ 1 in 4) placed the patient in the *prohibitive*‑risk category for conventional redo‑sternotomy.  
   - The operative field would have required explantation of two chronically integrated transcatheter frames, extensive aortic root reconstruction, and re‑do coronary‑artery bypasses – a technically hazardous operation with a very high likelihood of peri‑operative mortality and morbidity (prolonged ventilation, renal failure, bleeding).  

2. **Anatomical feasibility of a transcatheter redo**  
   - **Multidetector CT** showed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm inside the 2022 Evolut valve, adequate for a 23‑mm Acurate neo2 (size S) to be implanted with acceptable oversizing.  
   - Coronary ostial heights and sinus dimensions remained >12 mm from the anticipated neo‑skirt, and the CT‑derived valve‑to‑coronary distance after the planned high implantation was >4 mm, indicating a low risk of coronary obstruction.  
   - The existing Lotus frame (2014) was fully endothelialised; however, its low profile and supra‑annular leaflets left a “virtual annulus” that could be accessed by a third valve without violating the coronary flow pathways.  

3. **Impact of prior procedural complications**  
   - The 2022 TAV‑in‑TAV was complicated by a femoral pseudo‑aneurysm requiring surgical repair, underscoring the patient’s vulnerability to vascular injury.  
   - Because the subsequent episode of prosthetic‑valve endocarditis was completely eradicated (negative blood cultures, TEE/CT without vegetations), the risk of re‑infection after another device‑in‑device implantation was deemed low.  
   - Importantly, a third surgical approach would have mandatory redo thoracotomy, cardiopulmonary bypass, and likely aortic root replacement—all carrying higher bleeding risk than a percutaneous approach, especially in a patient with recent gastrointestinal hemorrhage.  

4. **Balancing haemodynamic goals vs. patient‑prosthesis mismatch**  
   - The prior Evolut valve produced a severe gradient (mean ≈ 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm²).   CT‑simulation demonstrated that positioning the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut leaflets (high implantation) would maximize the residual effective orifice area and reduce PPM.  
   - The Acurate neo2’s supra‑annular leaflet design and low frame height were specifically chosen to preserve coronary access and limit further reduction of the annular area, a critical consideration after two previous frames.  

5. **Multidisciplinary consensus**  
   - The heart team (interventional cardiology, cardiac surgery, imaging, anesthesiology, infectious disease, and the patient’s primary physician) reviewed the quantitative risk scores, CT anatomy, and the patient’s frailty profile.  
   - **Key statements from the meeting**:  
     *“Surgical explantation would be technically prohibitive and would expose the patient to an unacceptable mortality risk (>30 %).”*  
     *“CT shows a safe landing zone for a third transcatheter valve with adequate coronary clearance and no residual infection.”*  
     *“A high‑implantation, commissure‑aligned Acurate neo2 offers the best chance of acceptable haemodynamics while preserving future coronary access.”*  

6. **Final decision**  
   - **Proceed with a third TAVI (TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV)** using CT‑guided high implantation of an Acurate neo2, accepting a modest residual gradient in exchange for a 1‑year survival benefit and avoidance of a high‑risk operation.  

**Take‑home point:** In patients with prohibitive surgical risk (EuroSCORE II > 25 %), extensive prior TAVI history, and resolved prosthetic‑valve endocarditis, the heart team can justifiably select a third transcatheter valve. The decision hinges on (i) objective surgical risk, (ii) detailed CT assessment confirming a safe landing zone and coronary clearance, and (iii) a realistic appraisal of the hemodynamic trade‑offs versus the morbidity of redo surgery. This case exemplifies how multidisciplinary, imaging‑driven planning can extend the lifespan of the aortic valve apparatus while preserving the patient’s functional status.
