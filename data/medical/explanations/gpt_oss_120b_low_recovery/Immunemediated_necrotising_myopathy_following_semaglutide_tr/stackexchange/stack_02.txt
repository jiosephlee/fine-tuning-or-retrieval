Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Which autoantibodies are most commonly associated with IMNM, and what is the significance of the patient’s seronegative myositis panel in this case?

**Answer – Autoantibodies in Immune‑Mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM) and the Meaning of a Seronegative Myositis Panel**

The two auto‑antibodies that most reliably identify IMNM are:

| Auto‑antibody | Typical clinical Associations | Approximate frequency in IMNM |
|---------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Anti‑HMG‑CoA reductase (anti‑HMGCR)** | Often follows exposure to statins; can also occur in statin‑naïve patients with metabolic disease | ~30–40 % of all IMNM cases |
| **Anti‑signal recognition particle (anti‑SRP)** | Frequently presents with very rapid CK rise, severe weakness, and may have cardiac involvement | ~20–30 % of all IMNM cases |

Together these two antibodies account for roughly **half of all patients with IMNM**. The remaining cases fall into two groups:

1. **Seronegative IMNM** – no detectable anti‑HMGCR, anti‑SRP, or any of the broader myositis‑specific antibodies (MSA) such as Jo‑1, PL‑7, Mi‑2, MDA‑5, TIF1‑γ, etc. This constitutes **≈30 %** of IMNM patients, as highlighted in the case report (“an estimated third of patients with IMNM are seronegative”).  
2. **Patients with yet‑to‑be‑identified antibodies** – emerging data suggest additional targets (e.g., antibodies to HSP‑70, myosin, etc.) that are not part of standard commercial panels.

---

### Why Does the Patient’s Seronegative Myositis Panel Matter?

1. **Diagnostic Confirmation Relies on Muscle Histology**  
   In the absence of a defining auto‑antibody, the **gold‑standard diagnosis is a muscle biopsy** showing:  
   - Prominent necrotic fibers with regeneration,  
   - Minimal endomysial lymphocytic infiltrates,  
   - Evidence of complement (C5b‑9) deposition on sarcolemma.  

   The case report provides precisely these findings (necrosis, CD68⁺ macrophages, sparse CD3⁺ T‑cells, membrane‑attack‑complex positivity), establishing IMNM despite a negative serologic work‑up.

2. **Implications for Etiology**  
   - **Drug‑induced IMNM**: When anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP are absent, clinicians should heighten suspicion for an external trigger (e.g., statins, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or, as in this report, a GLP‑1 receptor agonist). The temporal relationship (symptom onset within hours of semaglutide, progressive CK rise over months) and dramatic response after drug withdrawal support a **drug‑associated, seronegative phenotype**.  
   - **Prognosis**: Seronegative patients often have a **more favorable response to a short course of immunosuppression** and may not require prolonged multi‑drug regimens that are typical for seropositive IMNM. This aligns with the patient’s rapid CK normalization and functional recovery after a 5‑day methyl‑prednisolone pulse, oral prednisone taper, and a single course of IVIG.

3. **Therapeutic Decision‑Making**  
   - **Auto‑antibody positivity** (especially anti‑SRP) is associated with more severe disease, higher relapse rates, and may prompt early use of steroid‑sparing agents (e.g., rituximab, methotrexate).  
   - **Seronegative status**, as in this patient, allows clinicians to **opt for a limited immunosuppressive trial** (high‑dose steroids ± IVIG) and monitor closely for relapse, rather than committing to long‑term agents from the outset.

4. **Future Monitoring**  
   - Although the current panel is negative, **repeat serology** can be considered if clinical relapse occurs, because low‑titer antibodies may emerge later.  
   - Emerging next‑generation assays (e.g., line blot arrays, immunoprecipitation with radiolabeled antigens) may uncover novel antibodies not captured in standard panels.

---

### Take‑Home Points for Trainees

- **Anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP are the hallmarks of IMNM; together they explain ~60 % of cases.**  
- **Approximately one‑third of IMNM patients are seronegative; diagnosis then hinges on characteristic muscle‑biopsy findings.**  
- **A seronegative result should prompt a search for external triggers (statins, biologics, GLP‑1 agonists, etc.) and may predict a more steroid‑responsive, self‑limited course, as seen in this semaglutide‑associated case.**  
- **Management:** Start with high‑dose glucocorticoids; add IVIG if weakness is severe or CK remains >5 × ULN. Reserve long‑term steroid‑sparing agents for relapse or seropositive disease with poor response.

In this case, the absence of anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP reinforced the interpretation that the IMNM was **drug‑induced and seronegative**, guiding a time‑limited immunosuppressive strategy and emphasizing the importance of drug cessation (semaglutide) in achieving durable remission.
