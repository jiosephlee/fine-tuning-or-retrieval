Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Discuss the role of GLP‑1‑receptor agonists in muscle metabolism. Why might semaglutide precipitate muscle injury, and how does this differ from the mechanism of statin‑associated HMGCR‑positive IMNM?

**Clinical Pearls – GLP‑1‑receptor agonists and muscle injury**

**1. GLP‑1‑receptor agonists (GLP‑1 RAs) and skeletal‑muscle metabolism**  
- GLP‑1 receptors are expressed on myocytes and satellite cells. Activation stimulates cyclic‑AMP/PKA pathways that favor **protein synthesis** and inhibits catabolic signaling (e.g., FoxO‑mediated ubiquitin‑proteasome activity). In animal studies, chronic GLP‑1 RA exposure modestly preserves lean mass during caloric restriction.  
- In humans, the most reproducible adverse signal is **loss of lean body mass** with rapid weight loss, especially when calorie intake is aggressively curtailed. The mechanism appears to be an indirect effect of **negative energy balance**, not a direct myotoxicity.  

**2. How semaglutide can precipitate immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM)**  
- The case of a 60‑year‑old woman illustrates a **temporal association**: a single 0.25 mg dose of semaglutide → acute nausea/vomiting → hospitalization, modest CK rise (337 U/L), then a **progressive, sub‑acute rise in CK (up to 4 000 U/L) and proximal weakness over 4 months**.  
- Muscle biopsy showed necrotic/regenerating fibers with **macrophage infiltration (CD68^+), complement (MAC) deposition, and sparse CD3^+ T cells** – the histologic hallmark of IMNM. Autoantibody panels (HMGCR, SRP, Jo‑1, etc.) were negative, placing her in the seronegative IMNM group.  
- The most plausible pathogenesis is **drug‑triggered loss of tolerance**. GLP‑1 RAs can modulate immune function (they are expressed on T‑cells and dendritic cells and can bias cytokine production). In a predisposed host (e.g., underlying sub‑clinical autoimmunity, hepatic steatosis, prior inflammatory stress), a single exposure may have acted as a **hapten or adjuvant**, exposing muscle antigens to the immune system and initiating a complement‑mediated necrotising process.  

**3. Contrast with statin‑associated HMGCR‑positive IMNM**  
| Feature | GLP‑1 RA‑associated IMNM (case) | Statin‑associated HMGCR‑positive IMNM |
|--------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Trigger** | Single semaglutide dose; immune modulation rather than direct enzyme inhibition | Chronic statin exposure → up‑regulation of HMG‑CoA reductase in regenerating muscle |
| **Autoantibody** | Negative for HMGCR, SRP; seronegative IMNM | Positive anti‑HMGCR (high‑titer) |
| **Onset** | Delayed (weeks‑months) after exposure; sub‑acute progressive weakness | Often after months of statin use; can present after discontinuation |
| **CK trajectory** | Gradual rise, peaks months later; normalizes after drug withdrawal + short immunosuppression | Very high CK (often >10 000 U/L) at presentation; may stay elevated despite statin cessation, requiring prolonged immunosuppression |
| **Response to therapy** | Short course of steroids + IVIG led to sustained remission; immunosuppression tapered rapidly | Typically needs long‑term high‑dose steroids, steroid‑sparing agents (azathioprine, methotrexate, rituximab) for months to years |
| **Pathophysiology** | Likely immune dysregulation secondary to GLP‑1 receptor signaling on immune cells → complement‑mediated necrosis | Autoantibody (anti‑HMGCR) formation against over‑expressed enzyme → complement activation and necrosis |

**4. Practical take‑aways for the bedside**  
- **When a patient on a GLP‑1 RA develops unexplained proximal weakness or CK rise, consider IMNM**—especially if CK remains elevated weeks after drug discontinuation and the pattern is not typical for pure rhabdomyolysis (i.e., CK does not fall rapidly with hydration).  
- **Obtain a thorough medication history**, including the date of the first GLP‑1 RA dose, and screen for autoantibodies (HMGCR, SRP, myositis panel). A negative panel does not exclude IMNM; biopsy is the gold standard.  
- **Management** mirrors other drug‑induced IMNMs: stop the offending agent, initiate high‑dose corticosteroids, and consider IVIG early in severe cases. Because the immune trigger is removed, many patients (as in this case) can be weaned off steroids within weeks to months, unlike HMGCR‑positive disease that often requires chronic immunosuppression.  

**Bottom line:** GLP‑1 RAs are generally safe, but they can act as immune triggers leading to seronegative IMNM, a presentation distinct from the classic statin‑related, HMGCR‑positive necrotising myopathy. Recognizing the pattern—sub‑acute CK rise, proximal weakness, biopsy‑proven necrosis, and rapid remission after drug withdrawal and short‑term immunosuppression—allows timely treatment and prevents unnecessary prolonged immunosuppression.
