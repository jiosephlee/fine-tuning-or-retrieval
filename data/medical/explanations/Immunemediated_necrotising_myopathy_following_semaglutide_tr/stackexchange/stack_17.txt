Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?

### [Management] What nonpharmacologic measures are essential in the acute management of IMNM with bulbar involvement?

When immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy presents with bulbar signs (dysphagia, dysphonia, impaired secretion clearance), the immediate priorities are airway protection, nutrition and aspiration prevention, and prevention of respiratory failure and systemic complications — measures that are as important as starting immunotherapy.

Assess and monitor the airway and breathing continuously. Early involvement of ICU, ENT and speech‑language pathology is essential because bulbar dysfunction can rapidly progress to loss of airway protection. Perform a bedside swallowing screen and urgent instrumental evaluation (videofluoroscopic or fiberoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing) if available; keep the patient nil per os until the safety of oral intake is confirmed. Low thresholds for elective airway control are appropriate: progressive dysphagia with pooling/aspiration, ineffective cough, increasing drooling, or signs of fatigue/respiratory compromise should prompt consideration of definitive airway protection (endotracheal intubation) because emergent intubation in a weakened patient is higher risk.

Provide safe enteral nutrition and hydration while avoiding aspiration. If oral feeding is unsafe but the course is expected to be short, place a nasogastric tube; if prolonged dysphagia is likely, arrange percutaneous enteral access (PEG) once the patient is medically stable. Manage secretions actively with anticholinergic agents judiciously, frequent suctioning, and chest physiotherapy to reduce aspiration risk.

Monitor respiratory function objectively and frequently. Measure forced vital capacity and maximal inspiratory/expiratory pressures serially; large or rapid declines (FVC falling toward or below ~15–20 mL/kg, or rapid decline in FVC/MIP, rising CO2, hypoxaemia) indicate impending respiratory failure and need for ventilatory support. Use pulse oximetry and arterial blood gases as indicated, and consider prophylactic noninvasive ventilation for hypoventilation if appropriate.

Prevent systemic complications of severe myonecrosis. Aggressively hydrate and monitor renal function and urine output (and urinalysis for myoglobinuria) to prevent or mitigate acute tubular injury from muscle breakdown. Provide venous thromboembolism prophylaxis and pressure‑injury prevention in deconditioned patients. Early involvement of physical and occupational therapy for passive/active mobilization as tolerated reduces deconditioning and long‑term disability.

Coordinate multidisciplinary care and monitor for cardiac involvement. Because IMNM can be associated with myocarditis or arrhythmias (as in this case), obtain cardiac monitoring and cardiology consultation when troponin/proBNP are abnormal or if symptoms/signs suggest cardiac involvement.

In short, while initiating immunosuppression and IVIG, treat the patient with bulbar IMNM as you would any neuromuscular patient at high risk for aspiration and respiratory failure: protect the airway early, institute safe alternate nutrition, monitor respiratory mechanics closely with low thresholds for ventilatory support, manage secretions and prevent systemic complications, and engage a multidisciplinary team for swallowing, respiratory, nutritional and rehabilitative care.
