### [Differential Diagnosis] In a young adult with hyperglycaemia, glycosuria, negative islet autoantibodies, preserved stimulated C-peptide, and a multigenerational history of early-onset diabetes, which diagnosis should be strongly considered and what is its typical inheritance pattern?

Think of maturity‑onset diabetes of the young — specifically HNF1A‑MODY (MODY3). The clinical pattern — young age at presentation, marked hyperglycaemia with glycosuria, negative islet autoantibodies, preserved (stimulated) C‑peptide, and a multigenerational history of early‑onset diabetes — points away from autoimmune type 1 and from classic insulin‑resistant type 2 disease and toward a monogenic β‑cell defect. HNF1A mutations classically reduce β‑cell insulin secretion (and lower renal SGLT2 expression, explaining glycosuria), are the commonest MODY subtype, and are inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion; confirming diagnosis by targeted genetic testing alters therapy, prognosis and family counselling.
