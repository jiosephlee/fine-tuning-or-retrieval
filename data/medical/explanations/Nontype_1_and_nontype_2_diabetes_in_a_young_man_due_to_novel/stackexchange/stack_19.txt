Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A

### [Morning Report] Using ACMG criteria, what lines of evidence support classifying a novel missense HNF1A variant as likely pathogenic (e.g., location in a functional domain, segregation, in silico prediction, absence from population databases)?

Teaching answer:

Interpretation of a novel missense HNF1A change as “likely pathogenic” requires combining multiple independent ACMG lines of evidence rather than reliance on any single datum. In this case the following ACMG criteria are met and together support a likely pathogenic classification.

First, the variant lies in the HNF1A DNA‑binding domain, a well‑established functional region enriched for disease‑causing missense changes (ACMG PM1, moderate). Pathogenic HNF1A mutations concentrate in this domain and alterations there have a biologically plausible effect on transcriptional activity relevant to beta‑cell function.

Second, the substitution is absent from large population databases (gnomAD/ExAC/1000G), supporting rarity incompatible with a common benign allele (ACMG PM2, moderate). A true MODY‑causing allele should not be present at appreciable frequency in population reference sets.

Third, the variant segregates with early‑onset diabetes in the pedigree (affected mother, maternal grandmother, affected brother), providing familial cosegregation evidence (ACMG PP1, supporting). Even modest segregation in multiple affected relatives increases the posterior probability of pathogenicity for an autosomal dominant condition like HNF1A‑MODY.

Fourth, computational pathogenicity predictors give strong deleterious signal (high AlphaMissense score 0.995 and concordant in‑silico tools), supplying supporting in‑silico evidence (ACMG PP3, supporting).

Fifth, the residue is adjacent to or at a site where other pathogenic missense changes have been reported, permitting application of PM5 (moderate) or PM5_supporting depending on the exact context (novel amino‑acid change at/near a known pathogenic residue increases the probability that the residue/region is functionally constrained).

Finally, the clinical phenotype is highly consistent with HNF1A‑MODY (young onset, negative islet autoantibodies, glycosuria, progressive β‑cell dysfunction, family history, treatment response), which meets ACMG PP4 (supporting) because the observed phenotype is specific for defects in this gene.

Putting these together — two or three moderate criteria (PM1, PM2, PM5) plus several supporting criteria (PP1, PP3, PP4) — reaches the ACMG threshold for likely pathogenic. Functional assay data (PS3, strong) are lacking, so a definitive “pathogenic” call would require additional strong evidence (well‑validated functional studies or stronger segregation). In practice, this composite genetic plus clinical evidence justifies reporting the variant as likely pathogenic and using it to guide diagnosis, cascade testing, and precision therapy for HNF1A‑MODY.
