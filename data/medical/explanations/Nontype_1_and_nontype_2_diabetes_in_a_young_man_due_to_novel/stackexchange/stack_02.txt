Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A

### [Morning Report] Which clinical features in this case most strongly argue against classic type 1 diabetes at presentation?

The features that most strongly argue against classic type 1 diabetes in this case are the combination of negative islet autoantibodies, demonstrable endogenous insulin secretion, and a clear autosomal‑dominant family history of early‑onset diabetes.

Clinically, classic type 1 diabetes at presentation is usually (though not always) accompanied by positive autoantibodies (GAD, IA‑2, ZnT8) and rapid autoimmune β‑cell loss producing very low or absent C‑peptide and a propensity to present with ketosis/DKA. This patient had negative GAD and IA‑2 antibodies, no DKA despite marked hyperglycaemia, and a stimulated C‑peptide of 0.83 nmol/L (above usual cutoffs used to define preserved β‑cell function). Those findings point away from active autoimmune β‑cell destruction and toward a non‑autoimmune cause of insulin deficiency. The pedigree of multiple first‑degree relatives with early‑onset diabetes strongly favors a monogenic (MODY) process rather than sporadic type 1 disease.

Put together, negative autoantibodies + preserved stimulated C‑peptide + autosomal‑dominant early‑onset family history are the most discriminating features arguing against classic type 1 diabetes in this patient.
