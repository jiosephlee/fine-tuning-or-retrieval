### [Pathophysiology] Which circulating biomarker is often lower in HNF1A-MODY than in type 1 or type 2 diabetes and can support the diagnosis when genetic testing is not immediately available?

The circulating biomarker is high‑sensitivity C‑reactive protein (hs‑CRP). HNF1A is a hepatic transcription factor that directly regulates CRP synthesis; pathogenic HNF1A variants reduce hepatic CRP expression, so patients with HNF1A‑MODY tend to have lower baseline hs‑CRP than people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

Clinically this is useful as a screening aid when genetic testing is not immediately available: markedly low hs‑CRP in a young patient with non‑autoimmune diabetes and a suggestive family history raises the pretest probability of HNF1A‑MODY and prioritises sequencing. Typical proposed cut points vary by study, but an hs‑CRP in the very low range (for example <0.5 mg/L, with some studies using <0.75 mg/L or <0.2 mg/L to increase specificity) is more consistent with HNF1A‑MODY than with common diabetes. Important caveats are that acute inflammation, infection, smoking, obesity, statin use and other hepatic conditions alter hs‑CRP and can confound interpretation; a single low value is supportive but not diagnostic, and normal or only mildly reduced hs‑CRP does not exclude HNF1A‑MODY. Genetic testing remains the gold standard.
