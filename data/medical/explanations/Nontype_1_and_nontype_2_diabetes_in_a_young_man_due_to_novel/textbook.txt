# Beyond Type 1 and Type 2: HNF1A-MODY in a Young Adult With Hyperglycemia and Glycosuria

## Case Vignette
A young man was referred after routine screening revealed hyperglycemia and glycosuria. He had a BMI of 24.6 kg/m2, no acanthosis nigricans, and no prior medical history. Family history was notable for diabetes diagnosed in the maternal grandmother (age ~30 years), mother (late 20s), and younger brother (age 20). Laboratory evaluation showed HbA1c 10.2% (88 mmol/mol), fasting plasma glucose 12.4 mmol/L, and 2-hour OGTT glucose 19.8 mmol/L. Islet autoantibodies (GAD65 and IA-2A) were negative. A glucagon stimulation test demonstrated C-peptide 0.39 nmol/L at 0 minutes and 0.83 nmol/L at 6 minutes, indicating preserved but inadequate endogenous insulin secretion. Sudomotor testing indicated markedly reduced electrochemical skin conductance over the hands. Soon after diagnosis, next-generation sequencing revealed a heterozygous novel HNF1A variant, c.809A>G (p.N270S), located within the DNA-binding domain and classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic by ACMG criteria. Initial therapy with basal insulin, metformin, and a DPP-4 inhibitor achieved early glycemic improvement. Following genetic confirmation, insulin was discontinued and a GLP-1 receptor agonist was introduced, with HbA1c falling to 6.0% within 6 months and sustained below 6.5% thereafter on metformin plus DPP-4 inhibitor.

## Clinical Approach: Early-Onset Diabetes Beyond Classic Categories
Young-onset diabetes presents a diagnostic challenge because classic features of type 1 diabetes (T1D) and type 2 diabetes (T2D) often overlap. A structured approach focuses on:

- Age at onset and tempo: Rapid symptomatic hyperglycemia and ketoacidosis favor T1D; insidious hyperglycemia with obesity and insulin resistance suggests T2D. This patient had marked hyperglycemia without ketoacidosis.
- Family history: An autosomal dominant pattern across generations strongly suggests monogenic diabetes. Multiple first- and second-degree relatives with early-onset diabetes in this case raise suspicion for MODY.
- Phenotype of insulin resistance: Central obesity, acanthosis nigricans, hypertension, and hypertriglyceridemia favor T2D. In the modern era, however, overweight and metabolic features are not uncommon even in MODY, so their presence does not exclude monogenic disease.
- Autoimmunity and endogenous insulin: Negative islet autoantibodies and preserved C-peptide (particularly beyond the honeymoon period) make autoimmune T1D less likely. The stimulated C-peptide here (0.83 nmol/L, roughly 2.5 ng/mL) indicates residual β-cell function sufficient to consider non-autoimmune etiologies.
- Glycosuria disproportionate to blood glucose: In HNF1A-MODY, the renal threshold for glucose is reduced due to decreased SGLT2 expression, making glycosuria a helpful clue even at modest hyperglycemia.
- Organ involvement: Renal cysts or structural kidney disease point toward HNF1B-MODY; mild, stable fasting hyperglycemia from early life suggests GCK-MODY; perinatal macrosomia and neonatal hypoglycemia suggest HNF4A-MODY.

In this patient, the combination of early-onset diabetes, strong multigenerational family history, negative autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide, and disproportionate glycosuria focuses the differential on MODY, with HNF1A-MODY most likely. The patient’s elevated triglycerides and low HDL introduce diagnostic noise but can coexist with MODY.

The clinical differential to consider includes:
- T1D and latent autoimmune diabetes of adults (LADA): Usually antibody-positive; progressive insulinopenia over months to years; often lean phenotype initially.
- T2D of youth: Obesity, acanthosis, strong features of insulin resistance; family history common but not necessarily autosomal dominant across generations.
- Other MODY subtypes: GCK (mild stable fasting hyperglycemia), HNF4A (macrosomia/neonatal hypoglycemia), HNF1B (renal cysts/dysplasia, hypomagnesemia), and less common genes (KCNJ11/ABCC8, PDX1, PAX4, NEUROD1).
- Secondary diabetes: Pancreatitis, hemochromatosis, cystic fibrosis, endocrinopathies, or drug-induced (e.g., glucocorticoids).

Use of an evidence-based MODY probability calculator can improve pretest selection for genetic testing, but tools derived from predominantly European cohorts may overestimate risk in non-European populations. Clinical judgment remains paramount.

## Pathophysiology: HNF1A, β-Cell Failure, and the Signature Renal Phenotype
HNF1A (hepatocyte nuclear factor 1-alpha) is a homeodomain transcription factor on chromosome 12q24, expressed in pancreatic islets, liver, kidney, and intestine. The protein contains an N-terminal dimerization domain and a bipartite DNA-binding region (POU-specific and homeodomain), followed by a C-terminal transactivation domain. The case variant, p.N270S, lies within the DNA-binding homeodomain, where many pathogenic mutations cluster, and is predicted to impair DNA-binding and downstream transcription.

In β-cells, HNF1A regulates a network of genes essential for glucose sensing, intermediary metabolism, and insulin secretion:
- Glucose uptake and sensing: Reduced GLUT2 (SLC2A2) expression impairs glucose entry and blunts the rise in ATP/ADP ratio that normally closes KATP channels, diminishing insulin granule exocytosis.
- Glycolytic and mitochondrial pathways: Decreased expression of key metabolic enzymes (e.g., pyruvate kinase) lowers ATP generation from glucose, further attenuating stimulus-secretion coupling.
- Transcriptional hierarchy: HNF1A cross-talks with HNF4A and HNF1B, integrating developmental programs for β-cell maturation and function; haploinsufficiency results in progressive β-cell dysfunction rather than absolute agenesis.

A distinctive extrapancreatic manifestation is the kidney phenotype:
- HNF1A positively regulates SGLT2 (SLC5A2) in the proximal tubule. Reduced SGLT2 expression lowers the renal threshold for glucose reabsorption, causing glycosuria at lower plasma glucose concentrations than expected. This finding can antedate frank hyperglycemia and is a clinical hallmark of HNF1A-MODY.
- Hepatic gene regulation by HNF1A includes CRP; circulating high-sensitivity CRP (hsCRP) levels tend to be lower in HNF1A-MODY than in T1D or T2D, a useful but non-diagnostic biomarker.

The natural history is one of progressive β-cell failure beginning in adolescence or early adulthood, with prominent postprandial hyperglycemia. Ketoacidosis is unusual at presentation because residual insulin secretion is often maintained for years.

## Diagnostic Workup: From Bedside Clues to Genetic Confirmation
A methodical evaluation maximizes diagnostic yield and stewardship of genetic testing:

1) Clinical and biochemical assessment
- Document age at diagnosis, family history spanning three generations, and any syndromic features (renal anomalies, hepatic dysfunction, neurodevelopmental signs).
- Examine for insulin resistance stigmata and diabetic complications (notably, small-fiber autonomic dysfunction can occur even early with severe hyperglycemia, as in this case’s abnormal sudomotor testing).
- Laboratory testing:
  - HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose; OGTT when needed to characterize postprandial excursions.
  - Islet autoantibodies (GAD65, IA-2A; consider ZnT8 where available). Seronegativity supports non-autoimmune diabetes but is not definitive; a minority of MODY patients may have low-titer antibodies.
  - C-peptide: A stimulated C-peptide >0.2 nmol/L (≈0.6 ng/mL) outside the immediate post-diagnosis period argues against absolute insulin deficiency; higher thresholds (e.g., >0.6 nmol/L) reflect more robust residual secretion. The glucagon stimulation test is a pragmatic measure of reserve.
  - Urinalysis for glycosuria and ketonuria. Disproportionate glycosuria is a classic HNF1A clue.
  - Lipid panel and, where informative, hsCRP (low levels support HNF1A-MODY but lack specificity).
  - Screen for microvascular complications at baseline if hyperglycemia is severe or longstanding.

2) When to pursue genetic testing
- Consider MODY testing in patients with diabetes diagnosed before 25–35 years, negative islet autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide, and a family history consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Absence of obesity and insulin resistance features increases specificity but is no longer required.
- Next-generation sequencing panels targeting established MODY genes maximize yield and efficiency. Variant interpretation should follow ACMG guidelines: integrate population frequency, predicted impact (e.g., AlphaMissense), location within functional domains, segregation with disease in the family, and phenotype concordance. ClinVar and other databases document prior evidence but absence of a listing does not imply benignity.
- In this case, the novel heterozygous HNF1A c.809A>G (p.N270S) variant lies in the DNA-binding homeodomain, neighbors known pathogenic residues, shows supportive in silico pathogenicity, and segregates with early-onset diabetes—evidence meeting pathogenic/likely pathogenic criteria.

3) Differential diagnosis refinement
- GCK-MODY typically presents with stable, mild fasting hyperglycemia (usually HbA1c 5.7%–7.5%) from childhood, minimal progression, and little to no glycosuria—distinct from the marked postprandial hyperglycemia seen here.
- HNF4A-MODY resembles HNF1A but may feature neonatal hypoglycemia and macrosomia; lipid profiles can differ.
- HNF1B-MODY often includes renal cysts, structural anomalies, hypomagnesemia, or pancreatic hypoplasia—features absent in this case.

4) Family implications
- Cascade testing of first-degree relatives is essential; each child of an affected individual has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant. Identification enables early diagnosis, prognosis, and tailored therapy.

## Management Principles: Treat the Biology You Find
Therapeutic selection in HNF1A-MODY aligns with the underlying β-cell physiology and extrapancreatic phenotype.

Glycemic therapy
- Sulfonylureas (SUs) are traditional first-line agents. By directly closing KATP channels, they bypass impaired glucose sensing, often achieving excellent control at low doses. Patients with HNF1A-MODY are characteristically sensitive to SUs; start low and uptitrate carefully to avoid hypoglycemia. Over time, progressive β-cell decline may necessitate add-on therapy or transition away from SUs if hypoglycemia becomes problematic.
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, and promote weight loss. Small randomized data in HNF1A-MODY show glycemic efficacy comparable to SUs with less hypoglycemia. They are attractive in patients with overweight, hypoglycemia susceptibility, or intolerance to SUs, as illustrated by this case’s successful transition to a weekly GLP-1 RA.
- DPP-4 inhibitors provide modest glycemic efficacy and are weight neutral; they can be considered as adjuncts when aiming to preserve β-cell responsiveness with low hypoglycemia risk.
- Metformin targets hepatic insulin resistance and can be appropriate when metabolic features coexist, though it does not address the primary β-cell transcriptional defect.
- Insulin is indicated for severe hyperglycemia at diagnosis, during intercurrent illness, pregnancy, or late in the course when oral/incretin therapies are insufficient. Early insulin use can be de-escalated once genetic diagnosis and clinical response allow.
- SGLT2 inhibitors should be used cautiously, if at all, in HNF1A-MODY. Endogenous SGLT2 expression is already reduced; additional pharmacologic blockade can precipitate profound glycosuria, dehydration, euglycemic ketoacidosis, and genitourinary infections. Case reports describe severe adverse events; benefits rarely outweigh risks in this genotype.

Lifestyle and risk factor management
- Medical nutrition therapy and regular physical activity remain foundational. Avoid excessive carbohydrate loads that exacerbate postprandial excursions.
- Standard cardiovascular risk reduction applies. Although macrovascular risk follows aggregate risk factors, microvascular complication rates in HNF1A-MODY correlate with glycemic exposure similarly to T1D/T2D; therefore, diligent glycemic control is crucial.
- Routine screening for retinopathy, nephropathy (albuminuria), and neuropathy should follow established diabetes guidelines. The early sudomotor dysfunction in this case underscores the need to assess small-fiber neuropathy when symptoms or signs suggest autonomic involvement.

Special populations and situations
- Pregnancy: For women with HNF1A-MODY, insulin is typically preferred due to limited safety data for most non-insulin agents and the need for tight glycemic control. Fetal growth should be monitored; parental genotype and fetal inheritance can influence risks and management.
- Pediatrics and adolescence: Genetic diagnosis permits early, low-dose SU introduction or consideration of incretin-based therapy to avoid insulin when safe.

Follow-up and monitoring
- Track HbA1c and, when informative, home glucose profiles focused on postprandial peaks. Periodically reassess C-peptide to gauge β-cell reserve.
- Anticipate the need to adjust therapy as β-cell function declines with age.
- Implement cascade genetic testing and provide genetic counseling to family members.

## The Case Revisited: Precision Therapy Guided by Genotype
Initial combination therapy with basal insulin, metformin, and a DPP-4 inhibitor was warranted to quickly address severe hyperglycemia while autoimmunity and β-cell reserve were clarified. Genetic confirmation of HNF1A-MODY, along with preserved stimulated C-peptide and clinical response, supported de-escalation from insulin and a shift to a GLP-1 RA. The robust improvement to HbA1c 6.0% within 6 months and subsequent maintenance on metformin plus a DPP-4 inhibitor illustrates how genetic diagnosis enables tailored, less burdensome therapy while avoiding agents (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors) that may be hazardous in this genotype.

The novel p.N270S HNF1A variant’s location in the DNA-binding domain, segregation with disease, and strong in silico pathogenicity strengthen its classification and clinical relevance. This finding expands the mutational spectrum informing precision medicine in diverse populations.

## Prognosis and Long-Term Outcomes
With appropriate therapy, individuals with HNF1A-MODY can achieve excellent glycemic control and reduce their risk of microvascular complications to levels comparable to other forms of well-controlled diabetes. Without adequate control, retinopathy and nephropathy risks mirror those seen in T1D/T2D over time. Cardiovascular risk is determined largely by traditional factors; aggressive management of lipids, blood pressure, and lifestyle is advised. Early genetic diagnosis improves quality of life, reduces exposure to unnecessary insulin, and facilitates preventive care for at-risk relatives.

## Clinical Pearls
- Consider MODY in any young person with diabetes who is antibody-negative, has preserved C-peptide, lacks ketoacidosis at onset, and has an autosomal dominant family history across generations.
- Disproportionate glycosuria at modest hyperglycemia is a classic clue to HNF1A-MODY, reflecting a lowered renal glucose threshold from reduced SGLT2 expression.
- Low hsCRP is a supportive biomarker for HNF1A-MODY but should not be used in isolation.
- HNF1A-MODY responds dramatically to low-dose sulfonylureas; GLP-1 receptor agonists are effective alternatives with less hypoglycemia and weight benefits.
- Avoid SGLT2 inhibitors in HNF1A-MODY due to increased risks of dehydration and (eu)glycemic ketoacidosis on top of an already reduced SGLT2 reserve.
- A stimulated C-peptide >0.2 nmol/L beyond the immediate onset period argues against absolute insulin deficiency and supports non-autoimmune etiologies.
- Genetic testing with NGS panels and ACMG-guided variant interpretation is the diagnostic gold standard; cascade testing and counseling are integral to care.
- MODY probability calculators are helpful triage tools but may miscalibrate in non-European populations; clinical judgment remains essential.

## Take-Home Points
- HNF1A-MODY is the most common form of monogenic diabetes and is often misclassified as T1D or T2D.
- Pathogenic HNF1A variants impair β-cell glucose sensing and insulin secretion and lower the renal glucose threshold via reduced SGLT2 expression.
- Early, accurate genetic diagnosis directs effective, lower-risk therapies and informs family screening, embodying the promise of precision medicine in diabetes care.
