Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 2: ## Pathophysiology of Valve Degeneration and Prosthetic Valve Endocarditis in Transcatheter Devices  

Understanding why a transcatheter aortic valve prosthesis fails is essential for interpreting the clinical course of this patient and for planning repeat interventions. Failure can arise from two inter‑related processes: **structural valve deterioration (SVD)** that slowly compromises leaflet motion and **prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE)** that accelerates structural damage through inflammation and bacterial invasion. The interplay of these mechanisms was evident after each successive implantation—first a Lotus™ valve, then an Evolut™ R/PRO™, and finally an Acurate neo2™—and informs the hemodynamic picture seen on echocardiography and computed tomography (CT).  

### Design Features of the Transcatheter Valves Used  

**Lotus™ (Boston Scientific)** – The Lotus was an early-generation balloon‑expandable device with a **cobalt‑chromium alloy stent** that was fully retrievable before final release. Its leaflets were mounted **intra‑annularly**, i.e., within the radiopaque frame at the level of the native annulus. The intra‑annular location yields a relatively low profile but limits the effective orifice area because the leaflets must open through a smaller circular opening defined by the prosthetic ring. The frame’s dense mesh provides strong radial force, which is useful for sealing calcified native annuli but also concentrates stress on the leaflets.  

**Evolut™ R/PRO™ (Medtronic)** – The Evolut family is a **self‑expanding nitinol** platform designed for **supra‑annular leaflet** positioning. The nitinol frame expands continuously after deployment, conforming to the aortic root geometry while preserving a larger flow area above the annulus where the leaflets are seated. This design generally yields lower transvalvular gradients compared with intra‑annular devices because blood passes through a wider supra‑annular orifice. The Evolut’s “repositionable” version includes markers that assist alignment of its three commissural posts relative to the native sinuses—a feature used in this case to minimize coronary obstruction risk.  

**Acurate neo2™ (Boston Scientific)** – The neo2 is another self‑expanding nitinol system, but its frame has a **shorter height** and an “open‐cell” architecture that facilitates **commissural alignment** and future coronary access. Like Evolut, its leaflets sit **supra‑annularly**, giving a relatively large effective orifice area even in narrow anatomies. The neo2 also incorporates pericardial tissue treated with anti‑calcification processes intended to extend durability. Its low-profile cage allows deployment within previously placed prostheses without excessive stack height—a critical consideration when three stent frames become superimposed.  

These distinct designs explain why each successive valve could be accommodated within the same annulus yet produced different hemodynamic profiles. Importantly, when multiple frames overlap, each new device exerts **radial pressure on the earlier prosthesis**, producing repeated cyclic stress on previously implanted leaflets.  

### Structural Valve Deterioration (SVD) in Transcatheter Bioprostheses  

SVD refers to any intrinsic loss of prosthetic leaflet integrity that impairs function, and it evolves through several histopathologic steps:

1. **Calcific stiffening** – Calcium phosphate deposits infiltrate the collagen matrix of pericardial tissue, rendering leaflets less compliant. In transcatheter valves, calcium deposition may be accelerated by high implantation pressures or by residual calcium from the native annulus that is forced against the leaflet surface during expansion.  

2. **Collagen degeneration & fibrosis** – Over time, enzymatic remodeling (matrix metalloproteinases) degrades collagen fibers while fibroblasts lay down disorganized extracellular matrix, producing thickened “fibrotic” leaflets that open sluggishly. Metabolic derangements common in older patients—hyperlipidaemia, diabetes—and chronic low‑grade inflammation promote this process.  

3. **Leaflet tearing or perforation** – Repeated cyclic bending produces microcracks that propagate with each cardiac cycle; eventual rupture leads to acute regurgitation. In patients with overlapping frames—as in this case—each extra frame increases the curvature imposed on underlying leaflets during systole, augmenting mechanical fatigue.

When two or more transcatheter devices occupy the same segment, **radial stress is transmitted through successive nitinol or cobalt–chromium cages** into every layer of tissue beneath them. This "sandwich effect" creates focal points of excess compression where leaflets are pinched between outer frames (e.g., between Evolut and Acurate neo2). Such focal compression hastens collagen fiber disruption and accelerates calcification at contact zones—phenomena observed on CT as reduced minimum lumen diameters (13.7 mm in this patient’s Evolut frame).  

### Hemodynamic Consequences of Combined Severe Stenosis and Intraprosthetic Regurgitation  

When SVD progresses toward severe stenosis, **the effective orifice area falls below 0·6 cm²**, substantially raising flow velocity across the valve. Doppler measurements then reveal mean gradients >40 mm Hg—a threshold met by this patient after each degeneration episode (83 mm Hg before TAV‑in‑TAV, 49 mm Hg before third implantation). Simultaneously, **intraprosthetic regurgitation** develops when coaptation is compromised either by thickened leaflets or by vegetations that create gaps at the closing line (“para‑leaflet leakage”). The coexistence of high gradients and regurgitation imposes a double load on the left ventricle: pressure overload from stenosis plus volume overload from regurgitation → progressive decline in ejection fraction (EF fell from 54 % pre‑Lotus to 42 % before the first redo TAVI). Restoration of forward flow after each intervention improves EF transiently; however, persistent structural compromise inevitably recurs if underlying degradation continues unchecked.

### Prosthetic Valve Endocarditis (PVE) After TAVI  

PVE occurs when microorganisms colonise prosthetic material after hospital discharge (>60 days post‐procedure). In TAVI patients three pathways are most relevant:

* **Entry via large-bore vascular access** – The femoral approach necessitates sheath sizes up to 24–28 Fr; any breach in sterility can seed bacteria along the catheter tract into the bloodstream.
* **Seeding from indwelling hardware** – Permanent pacemaker leads traverse venous circulation into right heart chambers; bacteria adherent to lead surfaces may embolise systemically.
* **Hematogenous seeding from distant infections** – Gastrointestinal bleeding episodes or urinary tract infections provide portals for transient bacteremia.

Enterococcus faecalis was isolated in this patient’s blood cultures—a classic cause of subacute endocarditis in elderly males with genitourinary or gastrointestinal pathology. Its pathogenicity derives from several virulence factors:

* **Surface adhesins (e.g., Esp)** that bind fibrinogen and extracellular matrix proteins deposited on foreign material.
* **Aggregation substance** promoting interbacterial clumping within thrombi on prosthetic surfaces.
* **Biofilm formation**, which protects colonies from host immune defenses and antibiotics,
* Intrinsic low-level resistance to many β‑lactams necessitating prolonged high-dose ampicillin therapy combined with synergistic agents when needed.

These traits enable Enterococcus species to colonise even well-endothelialised prostheses such as transcatheter valves whose metal frames provide scaffolding for fibrin deposition after deployment.

### Interaction Between Infection‑Related Inflammation and Accelerated SVD  

Infection adds an inflammatory milieu that dramatically speeds up structural deterioration:

1. **Cytokine‐mediated matrix remodeling** – Interleukin‑1β and tumor necrosis factor‑α upregulate matrix metalloproteinases within perivalvular tissue → accelerated collagen breakdown.
2. **Bacterial proteases** – Enterococci secrete gelatinases capable of degrading structural proteins within leaflets.
3. **Calcification promotion** – Inflammatory cells release oxidative species that act as nucleation points for calcium phosphate crystal formation.
4. **Mechanical tethering by vegetations** – Early vegetations act as “pseudoleaves” restricting motion of true leaflets; limited opening increases shear stress on already compromised tissue leading to earlier fibrosis.

Thus, after successful antimicrobial therapy eliminated active infection, residual inflammatory scarring left behind thickened yet still dysfunctional leaflets—accounting for the rapid progression observed three months post‐antibiotics when mean gradients rose from ~20 mm Hg back to >35 mm Hg despite prior successful TAV‐in‑TAV.

### Patient‑Specific Predisposing Factors for PVE  

The present patient harboured several recognized risk enhancers:

* **Repetitive large-bore femoral access with subsequent pseudoaneurysm** – Surgical repair creates additional scar tissue around arterial puncture sites; these loci serve as nidus for bacterial entry during bacteremia.
* **Permanent pacemaker lead crossing tricuspid valve** – While right-sided endocarditis is more typical for leads, hematogenous spread can involve left-sided structures via high-flow jet forces during systole.
* **Type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease** – Both conditions impair innate immunity and compromise mucosal barriers—increasing susceptibility to bacteremia originating from respiratory or urinary sources.
* **Repeated procedural trauma** – Each new catheter manipulation perturbs endothelial integrity within the aortic root; cumulative injury predisposes bacterial adherence once bacteremia ensues.
* **Prior gastrointestinal bleeding episodes** – Bleeding episodes often entail endoscopic interventions that themselves risk transient bacteremia with enteric flora such as Enterococcus faecalis.

Together these elements lowered the threshold for colonisation of newly placed prosthetic material after the second TAVI procedure.

### Why PVE Can Produce a Rapid Rise in Transvalvular Gradients  

In native infective endocarditis acute gradient elevation occurs when vegetations physically obstruct flow or when leaflet flexibility is lost due to edema/inflammation (“functional stenosis”). A similar mechanism operates in PVE:

* **Leaflet thickening & rigidity** caused by inflammatory infiltrates sharply reduces opening area even before gross calcification appears.
* **Vegetative mass protruding into LVOT** creates an additional physical barrier—in Doppler terms this appears as a narrowed effective orifice located upstream of the prosthesis (“pseudo‐stenosis”).
* **Rapid loss of coaptation leads to intraprosthetic regurgitation**, which augments systolic pressure gradients because part of stroke volume recirculates across an already narrowed aperture during ejection.
* Superimposed on pre‑existing SVD—the baseline effective orifice might already be <0·5 cm²—any further reduction translates into exponential gradient elevation according to Bernoulli’s equation (ΔP ∝ V²).

Consequently, once infection settled but structural damage persisted, even modest additional thickening produced mean gradients rising from 35 mm Hg at diagnosis to 49 mm Hg at one year later despite aggressive medical therapy—a pattern vividly captured on serial echocardiograms throughout this report.

---

In summary, structural deterioration of transcatheter bioprostheses proceeds through calcific stiffening, collagen degeneration, fibrotic remodeling and mechanical fatigue—all processes amplified by repeated radial stresses incurred when new valves are implanted inside existing frames. When prosthetic valve endocarditis supervenes—particularly with organisms such as Enterococcus faecalis capable of biofilm formation—the inflammatory cascade hastens leaflet failure and precipitates abrupt hemodynamic compromise manifested as steeply rising transvalvular gradients combined with intraprosthetic regurgitation. Recognising these intertwined pathophysiologic pathways is fundamental not only for diagnosing failure but also for guiding nuanced imaging assessments and procedural planning required for complex salvage strategies such as TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV procedures described herein.
