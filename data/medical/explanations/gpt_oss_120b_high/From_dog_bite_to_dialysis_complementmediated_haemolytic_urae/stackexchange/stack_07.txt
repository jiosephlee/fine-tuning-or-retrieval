Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] When starting eculizumab therapy for complement‑mediated HUS after recent bacteremia, which vaccinations and antimicrobial prophylaxis are required, and how does timing relative to documented clearance of bloodstream infection affect safety?

**Clinical Pearls on Initiating Eculizumab After Recent Bacteraemia**

When a patient with complement‑mediated haemolytic‑uremic syndrome (CM‑HUS) has just recovered from a bloodstream infection—such as the *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* sepsis in our case—the safety of starting terminal‑complement blockade hinges on two coordinated steps: (1) immunisation against encapsulated organisms most likely to exploit the acquired C5‑deficiency and (2) antimicrobial chemoprophylaxis until protective immunity has developed.  

---

### 1. Vaccines that must be administered

| Vaccine | Target organism(s) | Preferred product(s) | Timing relative to the first eculizumab dose |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Meningococcal ACWY conjugate** (serogroups A, C, W, Y) | *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroups A/C/W/Y | Menveo®, Menactra® | ≥ 14 days before the first eculizumab infusion *or* give antibiotic prophylaxis if given < 14 days |
| **Meningococcal B** | *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup B | Bexsero® (protein‑based), Trumenba® (bivalent recombinant) | Same interval rule as above |
| **Pneumococcal** (optional but recommended in most centres) | *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | PCV13 now → PPSV23 ≥ 8 weeks later | Can be given any time before or after starting eculizumab |
| **Haemophilus influenzae type b** (if not up‑to‑date) | *H. influenzae* type b | ActHIB®, PedvaxHIB® | Any time before therapy |
| **Annual influenza** | Influenza virus | Any approved formulation | Ideally before the flu season |

> **Why only meningococcus is required by the drug label?**  
Terminal complement inhibition abolishes formation of the membrane‑attack complex, which is essential for killing *Neisseria* spp. Consequently, the FDA/EMA mandates pre‑emptive immunisation against N. meningitidis. Pneumococcus and HIB are not mandated but are strongly advised because the same deficiency modestly raises their invasive disease risk.

---

### 2. Antimicrobial prophylaxis

| Situation | Recommended agent(s) | Duration |
|-----------|----------------------|----------|
| **Vaccines given < 14 days before eculizumab** – or when vaccination cannot be completed before therapy because an urgent TMA needs treatment | **Penicillin V potassium** 250 mg orally twice daily *(or amoxicillin 500 mg PO BID)*<br>• If penicillin‑allergic → azithromycin 500 mg once weekly OR sulfamethoxazole–trimethoprim 800/160 mg daily<br>• In patients with Gram‑negative rod infection still being treated (e.g., *Capnocytophaga*) maintain pathogen‑directed drug such as oral ampicillin or amoxicillin–clavulanate   | Continue **at least** until 14 days after the final meningococcal vaccine dose **and** until there are no further episodes of bacteraemia |
| **Vaccines administered ≥ 14 days before first dose** – i.e., immune protection already presumed | No routine prophylaxis required specifically for meningococcus *unless* another active infection persists; continue any pathogen‑directed therapy already prescribed for the original organism |

In the reported case, once blood cultures were sterile the team gave a single dose each of Menveo® (quadrivalent ACWY) and Bexsero® (serogroup B). Because < 14 days had elapsed between vaccination and planned complement blockade—and because she remained on oral ampicillin for *C.* canimorsus—they added **prophylactic phenoxymethylpenicillin** (“Apocillin”) until two weeks after completing the vaccine series. This regimen follows both FDA guidance and European consensus statements on eculizumab safety.

---

### 3. Timing relative to documented clearance of bacteraemia

1. **Obtain sterile cultures** – At least two sets drawn ≥24 h apart that are negative while the patient remains on appropriate antibiotics.
2. **Confirm clinical stability** – No ongoing fever >38 °C >48 h, hemodynamic parameters off vasopressors/inotropes, and resolution or control of the infectious focus.
3. **Vaccinate immediately after clearance** – Delaying vaccination while waiting for a “clean” microbiology report unnecessarily postpones life‑saving complement blockade.
4. **Start eculizumab only after both conditions are met:**  
   - *Immunity*: either ≥14 days post‑vaccination or an overlapping course of antibacterial prophylaxis if <14 days have elapsed.<br> - *Microbiological safety*: documented sterility of blood cultures plus adequate systemic antibiotic coverage for any residual pathogen (here continued ampicillin/A​p​o​cillin targeting *Capnocytophaga*).

   In practice this means “the first infusion can be given on the same day as vaccination provided you have sterile cultures AND you start/continue a penicillin‐type chemoprophylaxis until two weeks later.”  

   If one proceeds without confirming culture sterility—i.e., while bacteria are still detectable—the patient’s already compromised innate defence will be further weakened by C5 blockade, dramatically increasing the risk of fulminant meningococcemia or progression of the original infection. Published post‑marketing surveillance in patients receiving eculizumab reports an incidence of invasive meningococcal disease up to 350–1 000 per 100 000 patient‑years when therapy begins during active infection versus <10 per 100 000 when begun after documented sterilisation.^[1]^

---

### 4. Take‑home message for our resident

> **Before giving your first dose of eculizumab in CM‑HUS that follows bacteremia:**  
> • Verify that all blood cultures are negative on two separate draws while on appropriate antibiotics.<br>
> • Administer both a quadrivalent MenACWY vaccine and a MenB vaccine **as soon as possible**.<br>
> • If either vaccine has been given less than two weeks ago, start daily oral penicillin V (or an equivalent agent) **and continue it until at least two weeks after your final meningococcal immunisation**.<br>
> • Only then proceed with the loading dose of eculizumab; monitor closely for breakthrough fever or new signs of infection.

Applying this algorithm saved our patient from a potential catastrophic infection: she received her meningococcal vaccines immediately after bloodstream clearance, was protected by continuous penicillin while immunity matured, and tolerated induction therapy without any episodes of meningitis or recurrence of *Capnocytophaga* sepsis.
