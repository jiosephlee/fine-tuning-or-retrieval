Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] How is the PLASMIC score calculated, what does a score of 4 indicate regarding the likelihood of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), and how does this result influence early therapeutic decisions?

**PLASMIC score – a bedside triage tool for suspected TTP**

| Variable (1 point each) | Definition |
|--------------------------|------------|
| **P** – Platelets | < 30 × 10⁹/L |
| **L** – Hemolysis | Elevated LDH (or indirect bilirubin), undetectable haptoglobin **or** reticulocytosis > 2.5 % |
| **A** – Absence of active cancer | No current solid‑tumour or hematologic malignancy |
| **S** – No solid‑organ or stem‑cell transplant | No history of transplantation |
| **M** – Mean corpuscular volume | < 90 fL (normocytic) |
| **I** – INR | < 1.5 |
| **C** – Creatinine | ≤ 2 mg/dL (≈ 177 µmol/L) |

The total yields a score from **0–7**.  

*Interpretation* (original validation cohort)

* **0–4 points** – **Low pre‑test probability** for severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10%). The negative predictive value exceeds 95 %; most patients do **not** have classic TTP.
* **5 points** – **Intermediate probability** (≈15‑20 % have ADAMTS13 < 10 %).
* **6–7 points** – **High probability**; >50 % will have severe ADAMTS13 deficiency and benefit from emergent plasma exchange (PEX).

---

### What does a PLASMIC score of **4** mean?

In our patient the seven criteria summed to **4/7**, placing her in the *low‑risk* category. This conveyed that classic thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura was unlikely—later confirmed by an ADAMTS13 activity of **43 %**, well above the <10 % threshold diagnostic for TTP.

---

### How does this influence early therapeutic decisions?

1. **Urgency of plasma exchange**  
   *High‑risk* scores (≥6) compel immediate empiric daily PEX while awaiting ADAMTS13 results because untreated TTP carries ≈90 % mortality.  
   *Low‑risk* scores (≤4) suggest that routine empiric PEX may be unnecessary; clinicians should first seek alternative explanations for MAHA–thrombocytopenia (e.g., infection‑triggered complement‑mediated HUS, DIC).

2. **Diagnostic focus**  
   A low score directs attention toward other TMAs—particularly in the setting of sepsis or known triggers—prompting early complement studies, Shiga toxin assays, and renal biopsy when feasible.

3. **Balancing safety and certainty**  
   Despite a low PLASMIC score, many physicians start a *brief* course of PEX (“bridging”) if the clinical picture remains ambiguous or if the patient is deteriorating rapidly—exactly what occurred in this case. The rationale is that missing TTP even briefly can be catastrophic; PEX provides coverage until the definitive ADAMTS13 assay returns.

4. **Switching therapy once TTP is excluded**  
   When ADAMTS13 activity proved normal, PEX was stopped and targeted therapy for complement‑mediated HUS—eculizumab—was instituted, leading to hematologic remission and partial renal recovery.

---

### Take‑home pearls

* The **PLASMIC score** uses seven readily available laboratory/clinical items to estimate the likelihood of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency.
* A **score of 4** places the patient in the *low probability* group; it does not rule out TTP outright but makes it statistically unlikely.
* In practice, a low score encourages clinicians to prioritize alternative diagnoses while still maintaining a low threshold for *temporary* PEX when uncertainty persists.
* Prompt cessation of PEX after an ADAMTS13 result ≥10 % avoids unnecessary exposure to its risks and allows timely initiation of disease‑specific therapy such as eculizumab for complement‐mediated HUS—as illustrated by this dog‑bite–associated case.

By integrating the PLASMIC estimate with rapid laboratory confirmation and clinical context, we can tailor early management—saving patients from both under‑treatment of true TTP and overtreatment when another TMA explains the presentation.
