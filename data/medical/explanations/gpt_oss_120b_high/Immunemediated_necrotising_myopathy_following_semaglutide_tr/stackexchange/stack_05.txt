Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] According to WHO‑Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) causality categories, what elements of this case satisfy the criteria for labeling semaglutide as a ‘probable’ cause of the patient’s IMNM?

**Answer – Why this case fulfills the WHO‑Uppsala Monitoring Centre ‘probable’ causality criteria for semaglutide‑associated IMNM**

The WHO‑Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) classifies a drug‑event relationship as **probable** when three core elements are present:

1. **A plausible temporal relationship** between drug exposure and onset of the adverse event.  
2. **The event is unlikely to be explained by the underlying disease or any other medication**.  
3. **A clinically relevant de‑challenge (withdrawal) response** is observed, and a rechallenge is either not performed or considered unethical.

Below is how each of these elements is satisfied in the present case:

| WHO‑UMC criterion | How the case meets it |
|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **1. Reasonable time‑relationship** | • The patient received **a single 0.25 mg subcutaneous dose of semaglutide**.<br>• Within **hours she developed severe GI symptoms**, required hospitalization, and her CK was already modestly elevated (337 U/L).<br>• Four months later—while off the drug—she manifested **progressive proximal weakness**, dysphagia/dysphonia, and a **12‑fold rise in CK (peak ≈ 4 000 U/L)**, a pattern consistent with an immune‐mediated process that began after drug exposure rather than an acute toxic rhabdomyolysis which would have resolved rapidly. |
| **2. Alternative explanations are unlikely** | • **No exposure to known myotoxic agents**: she never used statins, red‑yeast rice, mushrooms or other supplements linked to IMNM.<br>• **Serologic work‑up was negative** for anti‑SRP, anti‑HMGCR and all other myositis‐specific antibodies, making an idiopathic seropositive IMNM less likely.<br>• **Autoimmune connective‐tissue disease was excluded** – ANA negative, only a low‑titer isolated SS‑B without clinical Sjögren’s features.<br>• **Her chronic liver disease cannot account for the marked CK surge**—transaminases were already modestly elevated before semaglutide and remained relatively stable while CK normalized.<br>• **Cardiac findings were mild, transient, and resolved**, arguing against myocarditis as the primary driver of muscle injury.<br>• Thus, *other diseases or drugs do not plausibly explain* the necrotising myopathy. |
| **3. Positive de‑challenge (withdrawal) response** | • After the single dose she discontinued semaglutide permanently.<br>• Following immunosuppressive therapy (IV methylprednisolone → oral prednisone + IVIG), her CK fell dramatically within two weeks and remained normal throughout follow‑up (≥ 10 months).<br>• No further worsening occurred despite continued exposure to all other medications, supporting that removal of semaglutide was a key factor in clinical improvement.<br>• A **rechallenge was deemed unethical** because re‑exposure would likely precipitate a severe recurrence of IMNM; therefore WHO‑UMC’s “rechallenge not performed or not ethical” condition is satisfied. |
| **4. Biological plausibility** *(supportive but not required for ‘probable’)* | • GLP‑1 receptor agonists have been reported to cause muscle injury (e.g., tirzepatide‐associated rhabdomyolysis) and are known to influence muscle metabolism.<br>• The histopathology showed necrotic fibres with macrophage infiltrates and complement deposition—findings observed in drug‑induced necrotising myopathies such as statin‐related HMGCR disease—providing a mechanistic bridge between drug exposure and immune activation. |

### Bottom line
The case fulfills all WHO‑UMC requirements for a **probable** adverse drug reaction:

- *Temporal proximity* between semaglutide initiation and symptom onset,
- *Exclusion of alternative etiologies* (no statins, seronegative IMNM, no other myotoxic exposures),
- *Improvement after drug withdrawal* (CK normalization with immunosuppression while remaining off semaglutide),
- *Inability/unethical nature of rechallenge*.

Consequently, semaglutide can be labelled as a *probable* trigger of immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy in this patient—a classification that underscores vigilance when prescribing GLP‑1 receptor agonists but also respects the limits of definitive proof without re‑exposure.
