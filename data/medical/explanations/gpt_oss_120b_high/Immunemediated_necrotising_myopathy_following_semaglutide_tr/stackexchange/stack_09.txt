Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] In seronegative IMNM patients who achieve remission after a short course of steroids and IVIG with withdrawal of the offending agent, how long should clinicians continue surveillance for disease relapse, and which biomarkers or clinical assessments are most useful during follow‑up?

**Answer – Follow‑up after drug‑induced seronegative IMNM**

In a patient such as the one described—seronegative immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) that remitted after a brief pulse of methyl‑prednisolone plus a single course of IVIG, together with prompt discontinuation of the offending GLP‑1‑RA—most experts would continue structured surveillance **for at least 12 months** after the last immunosuppressive dose, and many would extend it to **24 months** in the absence of relapse. The rationale is that relapses in IMNM tend to cluster in the first year after tapering therapy, even when the initial insult is drug‑related rather than idiopathic. A period of “observation only” beyond two years can be considered safe provided that both laboratory markers and clinical examinations remain unequivocally normal.

Below is a practical schedule derived from the published case and from the broader IMNM literature:

| Time from last immunosuppressive dose | Visit frequency | Key assessments |
|----------------------------------------|----------------|----------------|
| **0–4 weeks** | Every 2 weeks | Serum CK, aldolase (optional), ESR/CRP; focused MRC strength exam (proximal muscles); bedside dysphagia screen |
| **Weeks 5–12** | Monthly | Same laboratory panel; repeat manual muscle testing (MRC‐8 or MMT‑8); patient‑reported outcome (HAQ‑DI or PROMIS physical function); review of any new systemic symptoms |
| **Months 4–12** | Every 3 months | Serum CK; targeted strength exam; functional questionnaire; consider repeat MRI of thighs only if CK rises >2× ULN or symptoms recur |
| **Months 13–24** | Every 6 months (if previous visits all normal) | Serum CK; brief strength check; dysphagia query. If completely quiescent for ≥12 months you may space visits further or discharge from formal neuromuscular follow‑up, returning only for new symptoms. |

### Why this schedule works

1. **Serum creatine kinase is the most sensitive disease activity marker in seronegative IMNM.** In the reported case, CK fell from >3 900 U/L to within normal limits within two weeks of treatment and stayed normal even during a severe unrelated infection—a useful illustration that a rising CK often precedes clinical deterioration.

2. **Aldolase adds modest incremental sensitivity** when CK is borderline or when patients have concomitant hepatic enzyme elevation that can obscure interpretation of AST/ALT.

3. **Inflammatory indices (ESR/CRP) are not diagnostic but help flag concurrent infections or other inflammatory triggers** that might precipitate a flare; they are inexpensive and readily available at each visit.

4. **Manual muscle testing (MRC or MMT‑8) remains essential** because subclinical weakness may appear before biochemical changes—especially in seronegatives who lack an autoantibody titre to follow.

5. **Patient‑reported outcome measures** (HAQ‑DI, PROMIS PF) capture functional impact more sensitively than strength scores alone and are quick to obtain in clinic or via telemedicine—the same modality used for her initial neurology consult.

6. **Dysphagia assessment is mandatory early on** because bulbar involvement was prominent in this case and may recur independently of limb weakness.

7. **Imaging (muscle MRI) is reserved for equivocal laboratory results or recurrent symptoms**, as it reliably detects edema/active necrosis but adds cost and logistic burden when routine labs are normal.

### Special considerations for drug‑induced disease

- **Offending‑agent avoidance eliminates the ongoing antigenic stimulus**, which likely reduces long‑term relapse risk compared with idiopathic HMGCR/SRP‐positive IMNM where chronic autoimmunity persists.
- Nevertheless, published series report *late* relapses even after firm drug removal—hence an observation window extending beyond one year remains prudent.
  
### Practical take‑home points

1. **CK every visit is non‑negotiable** in seronegative IMNM; treat any confirmed rise >2× ULN as a signal to reinstitute low‑dose steroids or consider another IVIG cycle depending on clinical context.
2. **Clinical exam drives management decisions** when laboratory trends are flat but patients report new fatigue or difficulty swallowing.
3. **A minimum one‑year “intensive” surveillance period followed by quarterly then semi‑annual checks provides a safety net without excessive burden**, as demonstrated by the patient who remained relapse‑free at her 10‑month outpatient review while off all immunosuppression.
4. **Document medication exposure meticulously** – confirming permanent cessation of semaglutide (or any other suspect drug) simplifies counseling about future pharmacotherapy choices and informs pharmacovigilance reporting.

In summary, for seronegative IMNM triggered by an agent such as semaglutide—once remission is achieved with short‑course steroids plus IVIG—the safest approach is structured follow‑up for at least twelve months using serial CK measurements paired with regular strength and functional assessments, extending surveillance toward two years if any uncertainty remains regarding disease quiescence. This regimen balances the relatively favorable prognosis of drug‐induced disease against the known propensity of IMNM to flare early in its natural history.
