Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Which autoantibodies are most strongly associated with immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy, and how does their presence—or absence—affect diagnostic confidence and therapeutic decisions?

**Clinical Pearls – Immune‑Mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM)**  

**Key autoantibodies**

| Autoantibody | Frequency in IMNM | Typical clinical phenotype | Therapeutic implications |
|--------------|-------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Anti‑HMG‑CoA reductase (anti‑HMGCR)** | ≈ 45 % of all IMNM cases | Often preceded by statin exposure (but can be “statin‑naïve”); sub‑acute proximal weakness, CK often >5 000 U/L; relatively good response to glucocorticoids ± IVIG | Steroids are usually sufficient; IVIG is added when CK remains high or weakness persists; relapses may be prevented with a steroid‑sparing agent (azathioprine, methotrexate). |
| **Anti‑signal‑recognition particle (anti‑SRP)** | ≈ 30 % of all IMNM cases | More severe and rapidly progressive weakness, frequent dysphagia & respiratory involvement; CK frequently >10 000 U/L; higher relapse rate | Early aggressive therapy is favoured – high‑dose steroids plus IVIG ± rituximab. Steroid‐sparing agents are added sooner because monotherapy often fails. |
| **Other myositis‑specific antibodies** (e.g., Jo‑1, PL‑7, PL‑12, Mi‑2, MDA5, TIF1‑γ) | Rare in “pure” necrotising myopathy | Usually accompany dermatomyositis or antisynthetase syndrome rather than isolated IMNM. | Their presence redirects evaluation toward those overlap syndromes and may influence choice of immunosuppressants (e.g., tacrolimus for antisynthetase disease). |

> **Bottom line:** Anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP are the two antibodies most tightly linked to IMNM. Detecting either strongly points toward this diagnosis and helps tailor therapy.

---

### How antibody status shapes our diagnostic confidence

1. **Positive anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP**  
   - Provides a *serological anchor* that the necrotising process is immune mediated rather than a toxic‐myopathy or metabolic myopathy.  
   - Allows us to skip extensive evaluation for secondary causes (e.g., endocrine disorders, infections) once CK elevation, proximal weakness and compatible MRI/EMG are documented.  
   - Confirms a *myopathy* distinct from classic polymyositis/dermatomyositis because those entities rarely harbour these antibodies.

2. **Seronegative IMNM** (~⅓ of cases)  
   - The lack of a defining antibody lowers pre‑test certainty and mandates a **muscle biopsy** for definitive diagnosis—as illustrated by the present case where an extensive myositis panel was negative for SRP, HMGCR and all other standard antibodies yet the biopsy showed necrotic fibers with macrophage predominance and scant CD3⁺ T cells—classic for IMNM.  
   - In seronegative patients we must also rule out *drug‐induced toxic rhabdomyolysis*, *metabolic myopathies*, or *inherited muscular dystrophies* by integrating CK trends, temporal relationship to drugs (here semaglutide), imaging findings, and clinical context.

3. **Low‐titer isolated SS‑B positivity**  
   - As seen in the case report, an isolated SS‑B without ANA or SS‑A does not currently influence IMNM classification; it should be regarded as an incidental finding unless Sjögren features emerge later.

---

### Impact on therapeutic decision‑making

| Antibody status | Initial therapy | When to intensify |
|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Anti‑HMGCR positive** | Prednisone 1 mg/kg ± IVIG (2 g/kg) if CK >5 000 U/L or dysphagia present | Add methotrexate/azathioprine if CK plateau >2× ULN after 4–6 weeks; consider rituximab if refractory |
| **Anti‑SRP positive** | High‑dose steroids + IVIG upfront (often 2 g/kg) | Early rituximab or cyclophosphamide if weakness progresses despite 2 weeks of steroids/IVIG |
| **Seronegative** | Same regimen as anti‑HMGCR (steroids ± IVIG) guided by severity | Escalate based on functional response rather than serology—persistent CK elevation or failure to regain ambulation warrants IVIG re‑dose or addition of steroid sparers; monitor closely for relapse because seronegative disease can behave either way |

> In the report under discussion, despite being seronegative for both HMGCR and SRP, the patient responded dramatically to a short course of corticosteroids plus a single course of IVIG and remained relapse free after drug withdrawal. This illustrates that **treatment principles apply regardless of antibody status**, but seronegative disease obliges us to rely more heavily on the biopsy and clinical course for therapeutic judgment.

---

### Practical algorithm for the bedside

1. **Clinical suspicion** – proximal weakness ± dysphagia + CK >1 000 U/L → obtain MRI/EMG.
2.<ins>**Serology** – send extended myositis panel (incl. HMGCR & SRP).  
   • Positive → diagnose IMNM → start steroids ± IVIG per antibody type.  
   • Negative → proceed to muscle biopsy.
3.<ins>**Biopsy** – look for necrotic/regenerating fibers with macrophage infiltration, rare CD3⁺ cells, MAC deposition → confirms IMNM.
4.<ins>**Therapy** – initiate high‐dose prednisone; add IVIG early if CK >5 000 U/L or severe dysphagia; consider early rituximab for SRP or refractory disease.
5.<ins>**Follow‐up** – serial CK, functional assessment & taper steroids when CK normalizes (<2× ULN); monitor for relapse especially in seronegatives who may need longer immunosuppression.

---

### Take‑Home Message

- **Anti‑HMGCR and anti‐SRP antibodies are the hallmarks of immune‐mediated necrotising myopathy**, providing high diagnostic confidence and guiding intensity of immunotherapy.
- **Absence of these antibodies does not exclude IMNM**—about one third are seronegative as exemplified by the semaglutide case—and obliges confirmation by muscle pathology.
- **Therapeutic strategy is shaped more by clinical severity than by antibody status**, yet knowledge of which antibody is present helps anticipate disease aggressiveness (SRP→more aggressive) and informs choice of adjunctive agents (IVIG early in HMGCR vs rituximab early in SRP).

By integrating serology with histopathology and clinical phenotyping—as was necessary in the presented patient—we achieve both diagnostic clarity and rational treatment planning in this rare but treatable entity.
