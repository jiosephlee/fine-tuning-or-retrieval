Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What histopathologic features on muscle biopsy distinguish immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from other inflammatory myopathies such as polymyositis and dermatomyositis?

**Clinical Pearls – How a Muscle Biopsy Distinguishes Immune‑Mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM) from Polymyositis (PM) and Dermatomyositis (DM)**  

| Feature | Immune‑Mediated Necrotising Myopathy | Polymyositis | Dermatomyositis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dominant lesion** | Widespread myofiber necrosis with regeneration → necrosis far out‑numbers inflammation | Endomysial CD8⁺ T‑cell–mediated invasion of otherwise intact fibers | Perifascicular / perimysial/vascular inflammation with **perifascicular atrophy** |
| **Inflammatory infiltrate** | **Paucity of lymphocytes**; predominance of **macrophages** (CD68⁺, acid‑phosphatase⁺) performing myophagocytosis | Dense **endomysial infiltrate** of CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells; often accompanied by up‑regulated MHC‑I on all fibers | Perivascular and interfascicular infiltrate rich in **CD4⁺ T cells**, B cells, plasmacytoid dendritic cells |
| **Complement (C5b‑9/MAC) deposition** | **Sarcolemmal MAC** on both necrotic and non‑necrotic fibers → direct antibody‑mediated injury | Usually absent or focal; complement plays little role | MAC deposits primarily **on capillaries** and occasionally on endomysial vessels; not on sarcolemma |
| **MHC class I expression** | May be up‑regulated but not a defining feature | **Diffuse strong MHC‑I** expression on all fibers (helps confirm an immune process) | Often up‑regulated but less striking than PM |
| **Perifascicular changes** | None – fiber loss is random rather than perifascicular | None | **Perifascicular atrophy**, “puzzled” → key DM clue |
| **Helpful immunostains** | CD68⁺ macrophages, acid phosphatase+, sarcolemmal C5b‑9 → confirms IMNM; occasional sparse CD3⁺ T cells are permissible | CD8 immunostain highlighting invading cells; MHC‑I staining diffuse | C5b‑9 highlighting capillary walls, MXA or MxA staining for type I interferon signature |

### How These Findings Were Illustrated in the Semaglutide‑Associated Case

1. **Necrotic & regenerating fibers** – The biopsy showed “dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibres,” the hallmark pattern in IMNM where active fiber death dominates the picture.
2. **Macrophage predominance** – Immunohistochemistry demonstrated strong CD68 positivity and acid phosphatase activity, indicating robust macrophage‐mediated phagocytosis (“myophagocytosis”). This is far more conspicuous than the scant lymphocytes seen in PM/DM.
3. **Sparse lymphocytes** – Only rare CD3⁺ T cells were identified, satisfying the “paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates” criterion that separates IMNM from polymyositis (dense endomysial CD8⁺ infiltrates).
4. **Sarcolemmal MAC deposition** – The membrane attack complex stained positively on both necrotising *and* non‑necrotising fibres—a pattern characteristic of antibody‑driven injury in IMNM. In DM the MAC is confined mainly to capillary endothelium; in PM it is typically absent.
5. **Lack of perifascicular atrophy or perimysial B‐cell zones** – No mention of perifascicular fiber loss or B‑cell–rich cuffs was made, arguing against dermatomyositis.

Together these morphologic clues—extensive fiber necrosis out of proportion to inflammation, macrophage‐rich phagocytosis, sarcolemmal MAC deposition, and only occasional T cells—fulfilled the ENMC/2021 consensus criteria for immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy and excluded polymyositis or dermatomyositis.

### Take‑Home Points for Residents

- **IMNM = “necrosis with few fighters.”** When you count fibers first, necrosis dominates; when you count immune cells first, they are few.
- **MAC matters:** Sarcolemmal C5b‑9 is a red flag for IMNM (or seronegative anti‑SRP/HMGCR disease); capillary C5b‑9 points toward DM.
- **Macrophages are the star:** Strong CD68/acid phosphatase staining signals myophagocytosis—central to IMNM pathology.
- **PM’s hallmark is an army:** Dense endomysial CD8⁺ T‐cell invasions into intact fibers, plus diffuse MHC‑I upregulation.
- **DM’s signature is geography:** Perifascicular atrophy with perivascular/perimysial inflammation and capillary MAC—often accompanied by a cutaneous rash.

In practice, when confronted with a patient who has an indeterminate CK elevation and proximal weakness, request:
1. Routine H&E staining to assess the ratio of necrosis → inflammation,
2. Immunostains for CD68 (macrophages), CD3/CD8 (lymphocytes), and C5b‑9 (MAC),
3. Optional MHC‑I stain if the diagnosis remains ambiguous.

These histopathologic fingerprints will reliably separate IMNM from its polymyalgia/myositis cousins and guide the clinician toward appropriate immunosuppressive therapy—as illustrated by this patient’s rapid response to steroids plus IVIG after semaglutide was withdrawn.
