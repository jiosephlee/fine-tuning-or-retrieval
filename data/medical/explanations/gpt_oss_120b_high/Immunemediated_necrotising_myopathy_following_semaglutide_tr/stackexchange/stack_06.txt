Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Discuss plausible pathophysiologic mechanisms by which GLP‑1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide could precipitate an autoimmune muscle injury like IMNM.

**Pathophysiologic pathways by which a GLP‑1 receptor agonist such as sema­glutide could trigger an immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM)**  

The presentation described by Lam et al. – a single dose of semaglutide followed weeks later by progressive proximal weakness, a >10‑fold rise in CK, macrophage‑rich necrotic fibers with membrane‑ attack complex (MAC) deposition, and recovery after drug withdrawal plus a short course of steroids/IVIG – is archetypal for a drug‑associated autoimmune myopathy.  Several non‑exclusive mechanisms are biologically plausible:

| Proposed Mechanism | Rationale & Supporting Data from the Case |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **1. Hapten‑mediated neo‑antigen formation** | • Semaglutide (or a metabolite) may bind covalently to exposed muscle proteins after the initial hypovolaemic insult (the patient’s early acute kidney injury and modest CK rise). <br>• The resulting hapten–protein complex can be taken up by antigen‑presenting cells, breaking peripheral tolerance and generating **novel auto‑antibodies** that are not covered by routine panels (the biopsy showed MAC deposition despite negative anti‑SRP/HMGCR). <br>• Adaptive immunity requires weeks to months to mature—consistent with the 4‑month latency between exposure and overt myopathy. |
| **2. Amplification of innate immune danger signals** | • Acute GI toxicity → dehydration → renal hypoperfusion → necrotic myocytes release **damage‑associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)** such as mtDNA and HMGB‐1.<br>• DAMPs engage Toll‑like receptors on resident macrophages and circulating monocytes, driving IL‑6, TNF‑α, and IFN‑γ production.<br>• GLP‑1 receptors are expressed on monocytes/macrophages; in certain inflammatory milieus they can paradoxically enhance cytokine release rather than suppress it (Müller et al., *J Immunol* 2016). <br>• The biopsy’s CD68⁺ macrophage infiltrate and rare CD3⁺ T cells mirror this innate–adaptive bridge. |
| **3. Altered regulatory T–cell balance** | • Chronic metabolic disease (obesity, NAFLD) is associated with defective Treg function.<br>• GLP‑1RA therapy modulates the cAMP pathway in T cells; while generally anti‑inflammatory, it can also shift the Th17/Treg equilibrium toward **Th17 dominance** under stress conditions.<br>• A skewed Th17 response promotes autoantibody production and complement activation – exactly what is visualised as MAC positivity on non‑necrotic fibers in this patient. |
| **4. Unmasking of cryptic muscle antigens through rapid protein turnover** | • GLP‑1 agonists influence skeletal‐muscle metabolism—promoting modest catabolism initially before net anabolism develops.<br>• Sudden changes in protein synthesis/degradation may expose otherwise sequestered epitopes (e.g., α‐actinin, troponin I) that become immunogenic when presented alongside adjuvant signals from the preceding systemic stress. |
| **5. Parallel with other drug‐induced IMNMs** | • Statins provoke HMGCR antibodies through similar “first hit” muscle injury followed by loss of tolerance (as highlighted in the report).<br>• The current case mirrors that paradigm but without detectable HMGCR or SRP antibodies—suggesting either a yet unidentified target antigen or an antibody class not captured by commercial panels. |

### Integrating the mechanisms with the case

* Timing – A single dose provoked an immediate gastrointestinal insult; subsequent subclinical myocyte injury likely generated DAMPs that primed the immune system.
* Latency – The 4‑month interval allowed for hapten formation → antigen presentation → adaptive autoimmunity.
* Histology – Prominent macrophages (CD68⁺), sparse CD3⁺ T cells, and diffuse MAC deposition are hallmarks of an antibody/complement–driven process rather than pure toxic necrosis.
* Serology – Negative standard myositis panel does not exclude drug‐related autoimmunity; novel antibodies may have been generated against semaglutide–modified muscle proteins.
* Clinical course – Rapid CK fall after corticosteroids/IVIG and permanent drug avoidance mirrors other drug‐induced IMNMs where removal of the inciting antigen restores tolerance.

### Take‐home pearls

1. **Drug‐induced IMNM can follow a single exposure** when that exposure triggers acute metabolic stress plus immunologic priming.
2. **Absence of classic myositis antibodies does not rule out an autoimmune process**—muscle biopsy with MAC staining is diagnostic.
3. **GLP‑1R agonists act beyond glucose control**; their receptors on immune cells permit both anti- and pro-inflammatory signaling depending on context.
4. **Early drug withdrawal combined with brief immunosuppression often yields complete recovery**, as demonstrated by the patient’s return to baseline function within months.

In practice, when a patient receiving a GLP‑1RA develops unexplained CK elevation or proximal weakness—even weeks after the initial dose—consider IMNM in the differential, obtain a muscle biopsy if CK remains >10× ULN, discontinue the agent promptly, and initiate pulse steroids ± IVIG while awaiting definitive pathology.
