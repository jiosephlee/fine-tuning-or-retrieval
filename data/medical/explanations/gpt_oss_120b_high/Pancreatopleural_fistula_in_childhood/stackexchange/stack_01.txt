Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] Given this child's recurrent right‑sided hemorrhagic pleural effusion after blunt abdominal trauma, which laboratory finding in the pleural fluid is most diagnostic for a pancreatopleural fistula?

**Answer – A dramatically elevated pleural‑fluid amylase (pancreatic‐type) is the single most diagnostic laboratory clue for a pancreatopleural fistula.**  

In the presented child the pleural tap yielded dark‑red fluid with an **amylase of 6 118 U/L** (and a lipase of 7 211 U/L). In the literature this degree of elevation—usually quoted as **≥1 000 U/L**—is considered pathognomonic for pancreatic‑origin effusions. The result reflects direct leakage of pancreatic juice into the thoracic cavity through a disrupted duct or pseudocyst that has tracked across the diaphragmatic hiatus.  

*Why amylase beats other markers*  

- **ADA** may be modestly raised in tuberculous effusions (as was initially suspected in this case), but it does not reach the levels seen with pancreatic secretions and lacks specificity.  
- **Lipase** often parallels the rise in amylase and supports the diagnosis, yet isolated lipase elevation can occur in other conditions (e.g., acute pancreatitis without fistula).  
- **Amylase typing** distinguishes pancreatic iso‑amylase from salivary iso‑amylase; a predominance of pancreatic iso‑amylase further excludes esophageal perforation or malignancy, which usually produce salivary‑type elevations.  

Thus, when confronted with recurrent hemorrhagic or bloody‑appearing effusions—especially after blunt abdominal trauma—measurement of pleural‑fluid amylase should be obtained promptly. A result >1 000 U/L (pancreatic type) essentially clinches a diagnosis of pancreatopleural fistula and directs subsequent imaging (CT/MRCP) and management (bowel rest, somatostatin analogues, drainage).  

**Key teaching point:** In any child (or adult) with unexplained recurrent unilateral pleural effusion, *always send a specimen for amylase*; a markedly elevated level is the hallmark laboratory test that shifts the differential from pulmonary disease to pancreatic fistula.
