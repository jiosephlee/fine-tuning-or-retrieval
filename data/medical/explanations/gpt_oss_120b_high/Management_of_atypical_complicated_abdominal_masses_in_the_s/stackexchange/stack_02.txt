Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the normal physiologic pattern of serum CA‑125 levels throughout pregnancy, and how should a modest elevation (e.g., 42 U/mL) be interpreted when assessing a second‑trimester pregnant woman with an adnexal mass?

**Normal physiologic pattern of serum CA‑125 during pregnancy**

| Trimester | Median CA‑125 (U mL⁻¹) | Typical range |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| **1st**   | ≈ 23 U/mL            | modest rise above non‑pregnant baseline (<20 U/mL); may reach ≥35 U/mL in some women |
| **2nd**   | ≈ 14 U/mL            | generally close to non‑pregnant “normal” zone |
| **3rd**   | ≈ 21 U/mL            | a second physiological peak before delivery |

These data come directly from the case discussion (“the serum CA‑125 usually reaches two peaks…first trimester median 23 U/mL … third‐trimester median 21 U/mL … versus second‑trimester median 14 U/mL”). The early rise reflects decidualisation of endometrial tissue and placental mesothelial activation; the late rise parallels increasing uterine stretch and peritoneal irritation as term approaches. In isolation, a value ≤35 U/mL—even when slightly above the second‑trimester median—has limited specificity for malignancy because many benign conditions (endometriosis, adenomyosis, leiomyoma degeneration, infection or inflammation) can elevate CA‑125 modestly.

---

**Interpretation of a modest elevation (~42 U/mL) in a second‑trimester adnexal mass**

1. **Context matters more than the cut‑off:** A single measurement above the conventional oncology threshold (≥35 U/mL) does *not* diagnose ovarian cancer in pregnancy. The case illustrates this point—CA‑125 rose from 36 → 41.8 U/mL over two weeks without other biochemical abnormalities (normal CEA, CA19‑9, LDH) and without sonographic features typical of malignancy (no papillary projections, ascites or lymphadenopathy).

2. **Benign contributors are common:**  
   - **Decidualised endometrioma** – hormonally driven cystic change often yields CA‑125 elevations >19 U/mL.  
   - **Cystic degeneration of leiomyomas** – peritoneal irritation from necrosis or torsion can raise CA‑125 (the case’s pedunculated leiomyoma was necrotic at surgery).  
   - **Inflammation/Infection** – low‑grade fever and a concurrent Klebsiella urinary tract infection provide an additional source of mesothelial activation that can push levels into the low‑40s range.

3. **Trend vs absolute value:** Serial measurements are helpful. In this patient the upward trend prompted further imaging but did not alone dictate surgery; it signaled that whatever process was present was active rather than quiescent.

4. **Imaging remains decisive:** Ultrasound showed large unilocular cystic lesions with peripheral vascularity; MRI confirmed thick walls and solid components without malignant features such as irregular septations or nodal enlargement. Because imaging suggested benign pathology and because the masses were symptomatic and posed mechanical compromise, operative intervention was pursued irrespective of the modest CA‑125 rise.

5. **Practical teaching point:** When you encounter an adnexal mass in the second trimester with a CA‑125 around 40–45 U/mL:
   - Recognize this level falls within the expected physiological variability plus possible benign pathology.
   - Correlate meticulously with ultrasound/MRI findings.
   - Consider concurrent inflammatory states that can artifactually elevate CA‑125.
   - Use serial CA‑125 only as an adjunct—significant rising trends coupled with worrisome imaging may increase concern, whereas stable or slowly rising values often reflect benign processes.
   - Reserve definitive oncologic workup for cases where imaging shows solid papillary architecture, ascites, rapid growth >5–6 cm over weeks, or systemic signs disproportionate to benign disease.

In summary, during straight‐line gestation CA‑125 physiologically peaks in early pregnancy then settles near baseline until a modest resurgence near term. A solitary value of ~42 U/mL at 21 weeks should be interpreted as *potentially benign*—a flag for careful imaging and clinical correlation rather than an automatic trigger for malignancy workup or altered obstetric management.
