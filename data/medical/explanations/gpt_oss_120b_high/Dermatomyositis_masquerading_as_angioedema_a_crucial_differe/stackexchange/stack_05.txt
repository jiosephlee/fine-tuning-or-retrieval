Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] "High dose" intravenous methylprednisolone followed by oral prednisone taper was coupled with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), mycophenolate mofetil, and rituximab in this patient, while methotrexate was deliberately avoided because of emerging interstitial lung changes. Explain the rationale for selecting this particular combination regimen over conventional methotrexate‑based steroid sparing therapy.

**Teaching Point – Why “pulse‑steroids + IVIG + mycophenolate + rituximab” was chosen instead of the more familiar methotrexate‑based steroid‑sparing regimen**

The patient had **moderate‑to‑severe anti‑SAE1–positive dermatomyositis** manifested by:

* a rapidly progressive cervical oedema that threatened the airway,
* marked proximal myopathy and dysphagia,
* early interstitial lung changes on pulmonary function testing (restrictive pattern) and chest CT,
* markedly elevated CK (>16 000 U/L).

These features dictate **aggressive early control** of inflammation while simultaneously protecting the lungs from further injury. The therapeutic choices therefore reflect three overarching goals:

1️⃣ **Immediate suppression of life‑threatening inflammation**  
2️⃣ **Rapid improvement of muscle strength/dysphagia**  
3️⃣ **Long‑term steroid sparing without aggravating underlying lung disease**

---

### 1. High‑dose methylprednisolone “pulse” → oral prednisone taper

* **Why pulse?** The neck swelling represented active inflammatory oedema of the strap muscles and posterior pharyngeal wall—essentially an “airway myositis.” Intravenous methylprednisolone 1 g × 3 days delivers plasma concentrations that outpace cytokine production (IL‑6, TNF‑α) and rapidly stabilise capillary leak. This approach is endorsed by IMACS for severe myositis with organ involvement.  
* **Why taper?** After the pulse, a moderate prednisone dose (60 mg daily) provides continuation of glucocorticoid suppression while allowing stepwise reduction to minimise long‑term adverse effects.

---

### 2. Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)

* **Mechanistic fit:** IVIG blocks Fcγ receptors, neutralises pathogenic autoantibodies, dampens complement activation (C5b‑9 deposition noted on biopsy), and modulates cytokine networks—all relevant to a disease driven by anti‑SAE1 antibodies.  
* **Clinical advantage:** In severe dermatomyositis with dysphagia or rapid tissue swelling, IVIG yields functional improvement within days—faster than most conventional DMARDs. The case report documents a marked reduction in cervical oedema after the first five‑day course.

---

### 3. Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)

* **Safety profile in lung disease:** Methotrexate carries a well‑recognised risk of hypersensitivity pneumonitis and can exacerbate pre‑existing ILD—even when radiographic fibrosis is absent but pulmonary function is already abnormal, as was true here. MMF lacks pulmonary toxicity and has demonstrated efficacy in connective‑tissue‑disease–associated ILD (RCTs in systemic sclerosis‐ILD show stabilization/reversal).  
* **Efficacy in dermatomyositis:** Observational cohorts consistently show CK reduction and strength gains with MMF as a first‑line steroid‐sparing agent. The patient’s preference for a twice‑daily oral regimen also favored MMF over agents requiring intensive monitoring (e.g., azathioprine).  

---

### 4. Rituximab

* **Rationale for B‑cell depletion:** Anti‑SAE1 antibodies are produced by CD20⁺ B cells; depleting this compartment directly reduces autoantibody titers. Rituximab has become standard for refractory or severe idiopathic inflammatory myopathies after the RIM trial demonstrated modest but clinically meaningful improvements when added to background therapy.  
* **Synergy with other agents:** While steroids blunt acute inflammation and IVIG provides immediate immunomodulation, rituximab offers a longer‑acting “reset” of humoral immunity that sustains remission after steroids are tapered off—a key consideration given the need for eventual steroid discontinuation at 7 months.

---

### Why NOT Methotrexate?

| Feature | Methotrexate | Mycophenolate & Rituximab |
|--------|--------------|---------------------------|
| Pulmonary toxicity | ↑ risk of MTX pneumonitis & fibrosis → unacceptable in emerging ILD | No known pulmonary toxicity; actually used for CTD‑ILD |
| Speed of action | Takes ≥6–8 weeks for full effect → insufficient for airway compromise | IVIG gives rapid effect; rituximab adds durable B‑cell suppression |
| Liver safety | Already elevated transaminases from active myositis (+ potential MTX hepatotoxicity) | MMF has a more benign hepatic profile at therapeutic doses |
| Dosing convenience | Weekly oral/IM dosing → adherence issues when swallowing impaired | Twice daily oral MMF fits NG tube administration during dysphagia |

Because the patient already displayed interstitial changes on PFTs/CT, adding MTX risked precipitating an acute pneumonitis that could compound respiratory compromise—a scenario best avoided when alternative agents are available.

---

### Putting It Together – A “Hit‑Hard–Hit–Fast” Strategy

1. **Pulse steroids** → blunt overwhelming inflammation immediately.  
2. **IVIG** → provides near‑instant functional recovery of muscle and pharyngeal function while neutralising circulating autoantibodies.  
3. **MMF** → maintains immunosuppression without jeopardising lung health; serves as the backbone steroid‑sparing agent during tapering.  
4. **Rituximab** → ensures long‐term control by eradicating the source of pathogenic antibodies, allowing eventual withdrawal of glucocorticoids.

The resulting regimen achieved:
* Rapid resolution of neck swelling,
* Normalisation of CK within weeks,
* Full recovery of proximal strength,
* Safe discontinuation of prednisone by month 7,
* No progression of interstitial lung disease on follow‐up imaging/PFTs.

---

#### Take‑Home Messages

- In dermatomyositis with **severe organ involvement** (airway edema, dysphagia) and **early lung pathology**, avoid methotrexate because its pulmonary toxicity outweighs its convenience.
- Combine **high‐dose steroids + IVIG** for swift control of life‐threatening inflammation.
- Use **mycophenolate** as a frontline steroid‐sparing agent when lungs are involved—its safety profile aligns with connective‐tissue disease–associated ILD.
- Add **rituximab** early when autoantibody‐driven disease (e.g., anti‑SAE1) is present or when rapid steroid tapering is desired.
- The goal is not just “more drugs,” but a **targeted multi‑modal approach** that balances efficacy against organ‐specific risk—precisely what was accomplished in this case rather than relying on traditional methotrexate alone.
