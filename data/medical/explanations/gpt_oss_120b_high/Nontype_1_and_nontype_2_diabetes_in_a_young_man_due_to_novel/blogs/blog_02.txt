Title: Blog about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

# A New HNF1A Variant (p.N270S) from Taiwan: Interpreting Novel Mutations with ACMG Guidelines  

---

## Why this case turned heads  

When a 22‑year‑old Taiwanese man walks into clinic with an HbA1c of 10 % and a strong three‑generation pedigree of early‑onset diabetes, you might first think classic type 2 diabetes amplified by lifestyle factors—or even type 1 diabetes because he’s already losing weight and has polyuria. What made his presentation truly surprising was that routine autoantibody panels were negative, his BMI hovered at a modest 24 kg/m², and he had glycosuria despite a relatively preserved C‑peptide. The subsequent next‑generation sequencing (NGS) panel uncovered **a heterozygous missense change – p.N270S (c.809A>G) in exon 4 of HNF1A** – a variant never before reported in ClinVar, dbSNP or any public repository.  

The moment the lab flagged the mutation as “novel,” the clinical team faced a classic precision‑medicine dilemma: **How do we decide whether this unknown change actually *causes* disease?** The answer lies in a systematic application of the ACMG/AMP variant‑interpretation framework – a set of rigorously defined criteria that turn raw sequence data into an actionable diagnosis.

---

## A quick refresher: MODY and HNF1A  

Monogenic diabetes (MODY) accounts for 1–6 % of diabetes diagnosed before age 25. Of the >15 genes implicated, **HNF1A**, encoding hepatocyte nuclear factor 1‑α, is by far the most common culprit (≈30–65 % of MODY cases). Classic HNF1A‑MODY features include:

* Early‐onset hyperglycaemia without marked insulin resistance  
* High postprandial glucose excursions → glycosuria at relatively low plasma glucose  
* Negative pancreatic autoantibodies  
* Family history consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance  

Therapeutically, patients respond exquisitely to low‑dose sulfonylureas or GLP‑1 receptor agonists, often allowing insulin discontinuation – but only once we can **confidently label** the underlying genotype as pathogenic.

---

## Applying ACMG/AMP criteria to p.N270S  

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics together with the Association for Molecular Pathology published a checklist of evidentiary categories that can be weighted as **Pathogenic (P)**, **Likely Pathogenic (LP)**, **Uncertain Significance (VUS)**, **Likely Benign**, or **Benign**. Below is how each relevant criterion was satisfied for our Taiwanese patient’s HNF1A p.N270S mutation.

### 1️⃣ Population data – “Absent is suspicious”  

| Criterion | Evidence | ACMG Code |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **PM2**    | The variant is *absent* from gnomAD overall and from East‐Asian subpopulations (including Taiwan Biobank). No entry exists in 1000 Genomes or ExAC.|  

Population rarity alone does NOT prove pathogenicity but fulfills the *moderate* weight PM2 because the allele is not seen in any large reference cohort (>140 000 individuals).

### 2️⃣ Computational predictions – “Strong in silico support”  

* **AlphaMissense score:** 0.995 *(near‐perfect deleterious prediction)* → supports pathogenicity (**PP3**).  
* Additional tools (REVEL = 0.88, CADD = 27) align with this conclusion though they are not formally required when AlphaMissense already meets threshold.

The convergence of multiple algorithms gives high confidence that substitution N270S destabilizes protein function.

### 3️⃣ Functional domain relevance – “Location matters”  

Exon 4 encodes part of the **DNA‑binding domain** of HNF1α—a recognized hotspot where ≈70 % of disease‐causing missense changes reside. Variants altering residues adjacent to known pathogenic sites have repeatedly been shown to impair transcriptional activation of GLUT2 and insulin‐promoting genes. This satisfies **PM1** (“mutational hot spot/critical functional domain”).

### 4️⃣ Segregation data – “Does it run in the family?”  

Testing revealed that:

* The patient’s mother and maternal grandmother both carry p.N270S and have early‐onset diabetes.
* The younger brother also carries the variant and was diagnosed at age 20.
* No unaffected carrier was identified among tested relatives.

Three independent affected relatives sharing the same allele give **PP1_Strong** (“co‐segregation with disease”) under ACMG recommendations when ≥5 meioses are documented – here we have three informative meioses plus clear phenotype concordance, meeting *moderate–strong* evidence thresholds.

### 5️⃣ Phenotypic fit – “Is this what we’d expect?”  

The patient’s presentation aligns perfectly with classic HNF1A‐MODY:

* Hyperglycaemia before age 25  
* Glycosuria out of proportion to plasma glucose  
* Negative pancreatic antibodies  
* Moderate C‑peptide preservation  

Thus we assign **PP4** (“patient’s phenotype highly specific for disease gene”).

### 6️⃣ Other lines – “What about functional assays?”  

No direct functional studies exist for N270S yet. In their absence we cannot invoke PS3 (“well‑established functional impact”). However, given accumulated moderate–strong evidence from other criteria, this omission does not preclude classification as LP/P.

#### Final classification  

Applying ACMG’s point system yields:

- **PM2** + **PM1** + **PP3** + **PP4** + **PP1_Strong** ⇒ *Likely Pathogenic*, bordering on *Pathogenic*.  

The authors of the case report felt comfortable reporting it as **Pathogenic**, which is defensible given that multiple moderate criteria dominate and segregation is robust.

## When ClinVar says “nothing…”  

ClinVar is an invaluable repository but it reflects only variants that have been submitted and reviewed by the community. A *null entry* does NOT equal benignity—it simply means no one has reported it yet. For patients from underrepresented ancestries—like East Asian populations—this lacuna is especially common. Relying solely on database presence would have left our clinician uncertain about management decisions for months longer than necessary.

Key take‑aways regarding public databases:

* Always cross‑check multiple sources: gnomAD for allele frequency, dbSNP for historic naming, regional biobanks for ethnicity‐specific frequencies.
* Document whether any related lineages have been entered into ClinVar after your own interpretation—this helps close gaps for future patients.
* Encourage local labs to submit novel variants alongside phenotypic details (phenotype ontology tags recommended by ClinGen).

---

## Adding functional context: why DNA‑binding matters  

HNF1α’s DNA‑binding domain engages promoter regions controlling key β‑cell genes—most notably *GLUT2*, *insulin*, *pyruvate kinase*. Missense changes here can blunt transcriptional activation even without gross protein instability:

* Loss-of-function reduces GLUT2 expression → lower hepatic glucose uptake → higher postprandial glucose spikes.
* Impaired β‑cell gene regulation explains progressive reduction in insulin secretion seen on glucagon stimulation testing.
  
Therefore a missense change at residue N270—a residue directly interacting within the recognition helix—has a biologically plausible route to MODY phenotype even if exact functional assays are pending.

---

## From genotype to bedside: how interpretation reshaped care  

Once p.N270S received an LP/P designation:

1️⃣ Insulin was tapered off after one month once fasting glucose improved (<9 mmol/L).  
2️⃣ A GLP‑1 receptor agonist (**dulaglutide**) was introduced alongside metformin—a regimen shown superior to sulfonylureas regarding hypoglycaemia risk in HNF1A carriers whose β‑cell reserve is waning.  
3️⃣ Six months later HbA1c fell from 10 % → 6 %, allowing medication simplification back to metformin + saxagliptin for maintenance.

Had we persisted under an initial “type 2” label without genetic clarification, he likely would have remained on higher insulin doses (+risk of hypoglycaemia) or sulfonylureas without recognizing his heightened susceptibility to SGLT2 inhibitor–related euglycaemic ketoacidosis—a distinct hazard in HNF1A defects due to already reduced renal SGLT2 expression.

## A pragmatic roadmap for clinicians facing an “unreported” variant  

When your sequencing lab flags a missense change that you cannot find in public databases:

| Step | Action | What you’re looking for |
|------|--------|--------------------------|
| **① Verify population frequency** | Query gnomAD (overall + subpopulation), local biobanks | PM2 if absent/ultra‑rare (<0.001%) |
| **② Run multiple computational predictors** | AlphaMissense + REVEL + CADD + SIFT/PolyPhen | PP3 if ≥3 tools support deleteriousness |
| **③ Map the variant onto protein domains** | Use UniProt or Ensembl protein view | PM1 if located within known mutational hot spot |
| **④ Gather segregation data** | Test first-degree relatives when possible | PP1_Strong/Moderate based on number of affected carriers |
| **⑤ Correlate phenotype** | Compare clinical picture to gene‐specific disease spectrum | PP4 if highly specific |
| **⑥ Check literature & functional studies** | Search PubMed & GeneReviews for any functional assay | PS3 if functional impairment demonstrated |
| **⑦ Assemble evidence per ACMG matrix** | Apply codes → tally tiered strength → assign final classification | Final VUS vs LP/P decision |
| **⑧ Communicate clearly** | Document interpretation process & rationale for treatment plan | Enables multidisciplinary consensus & future reclassification |

Remember that some criteria can be upgraded/downgraded based on additional evidence—for example, detection of de novo occurrence (**PS2**) or strong phenotypic specificity (**PP4**) can tip a VUS toward LP/P even when computational scores are modest.

---

## Take‑home Messages  

- **Novel doesn’t mean benign:** Absence from ClinVar or dbSNP should prompt systematic ACMG evaluation rather than dismissal.
- **Population context is king:** Confirm rarity using ancestry‐matched reference datasets; ultra‑rare status contributes moderate evidence toward pathogenicity.
- **In silico scores matter—but aren’t sole proof:** High AlphaMissense (>0.9) strongly supports deleterious effect when paired with other lines.
- **Domain knowledge accelerates interpretation:** Missense changes within HNF1A’s DNA‑binding domain are frequently pathogenic because they disrupt transcriptional regulation.
- **Family segregation can clinch it:** Demonstrated co‑segregation across ≥3 affected relatives upgrades PP1 to strong evidence.
- **Phenotype alignment validates genetics:** Early‐onset hyperglycaemia plus glycosuria plus negative antibodies = textbook HNF1A-MODY pattern → PP4.
- **Precision treatment follows precise diagnosis:** Recognizing LP/P status enabled cessation of insulin and transition to GLP‑1RA/metformin—a regimen more physiologically appropriate.
- **Contribute back:** Submitting newly classified variants—and their phenotypes—to ClinVar enriches resources for future clinicians working across diverse ethnicities.

---

By walking through each ACMG criterion using real-world data from this Taiwanese family, we see how a seemingly opaque “novel” missense change becomes a clear therapeutic target within weeks—not months or years—once interpreted methodically. As NGS panels become routine worldwide, especially among underrepresented populations, mastering this framework will be essential for delivering true precision medicine today.
