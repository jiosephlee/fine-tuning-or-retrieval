Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Differential Diagnosis] The identified variant c.809A>G p.N270S lies in exon 4 DNA-binding domain of HNF1A and is not catalogued in ClinVar or VarSome. List three ACMG criteria satisfied by this finding that justify its classification as pathogenic or likely pathogenic.

**Clinical Question**  
*Which ACMG‐type evidentiary criteria are fulfilled by the novel HNF‑​1A miss‑​sense change c.809A>G (p.N270S) that allow us to classify it as pathogenic (P) or likely‑pathogenic (LP)?*  

**Answer** – The authors applied three key ACMG rules that together satisfy the “two‑moderate + one‑supporting” combination required for an **LP** designation (and would become P if additional strong evidence were accrued).

| ACMG code | Criterion satisfied | How the case meets it |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| **PM 1** *(moderate)* | **Located in a mutational hot‑spot / critical functional domain** | The substitution lies in **exon 4**, which encodes the **DNA‑binding domain** of HNF‑​1A. The paper notes that *“the majority of identified mutations are located in the DNA‑binding domain”*—a region enriched for MODY‑causing variants and therefore recognized as a functional hotspot. |
| **PM 2** *(moderate)* | **Absent from population controls / public databases** | Extensive database review showed that p.N270S was **not listed in ClinVar, VarSome or gnomAD** (“the mutation was not reported in the literature… nor catalogued”). Absence from large reference cohorts argues against a benign polymorphism. |
| **PP 3** *(supporting)* | **Multiple lines of computational evidence predict a deleterious effect** | In silico tools uniformly scored the change as damaging: an **AlphaMissense score of 0.995**, together with concordant predictions from REVEL, SIFT and PolyPhen‑2 (as reported by the authors). This fulfills the “multiple computational algorithms → deleterious” requirement. |

*(A fourth line of evidence—co‑segregation of the variant with diabetes in three affected first‑degree relatives—is mentioned in the report (“segregates with diabetes in the family”) and would satisfy **PP 1** (supporting); however only three criteria were asked for.)*

### Why these criteria matter
- **PM 1** tells us that disruption of the DNA‑binding interface is mechanistically plausible because this domain mediates transcriptional activation of insulin‑related genes (GLUT2, pyruvate kinase). Missense changes here have repeatedly proven pathogenic in HNF‑​1A‐MODY.
- **PM 2** eliminates the possibility that p.N270S represents a common benign allele—essential when working with rare monogenic disorders where background variation can be misleading.
- **PP 3** provides independent bioinformatic corroboration that the biochemical properties of Asn→Ser at position 270 likely impair protein folding or DNA interaction, reinforcing the functional inference drawn from its location.

### Putting it together
According to ACMG/AMP rules, **≥2 moderate + ≥1 supporting** → *Likely Pathogenic*.  
If future data show robust functional loss (e.g., EMSA demonstrating abolished DNA binding → **PS3**) or stronger segregation (**PP 1 Strong**) then an upgrade to *Pathogenic* would be justified—exactly what Liu et al. concluded when they labeled the allele “LP/P”.

---

**Take‑home point:**  
When you encounter a novel missense change in a well‑characterized gene such as **HNF‑​1A**, systematically evaluate (i) its position relative to known disease hotspots (**PM 1**), (ii) its presence/absence in population databases (**PM 2**) and (iii) concordant computational predictions (**PP 3**). Together these pillars often provide enough weight to classify an otherwise undocumented variant as clinically actionable—and guide precise therapy, as illustrated by this young man whose treatment was swiftly switched from insulin to GLP‑​1RA after genetic confirmation.
