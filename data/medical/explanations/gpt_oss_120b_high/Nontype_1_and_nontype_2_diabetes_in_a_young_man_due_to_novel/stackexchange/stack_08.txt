Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Morning Report] The glucagon stimulation test showed a fasting C-peptide of 0.39 nmol/L rising to 0.83 nmol/L at 6 minutes. How does this result influence diagnostic certainty for MODY versus type 1 diabetes, and how does it guide therapeutic choice?

**Morning‑Report Pearls – Interpreting a Glucagon‑Stimulated C‑Peptide in a Young Adult with Early‑Onset Diabetes**

**Key Finding**  
Glucagon stimulation produced a fasting C‑peptide of **0.39 nmol/L** that rose to **0.83 nmol/L at 6 min**. In most laboratories a *stimulated* C‑peptide > 0.60 nmol/L is taken as evidence of **preserved endogenous insulin secretion**; values below this are regarded as indicating marked β‑cell failure.

---

### 1️⃣ What does the pattern tell us about **MODY vs. type 1 diabetes**?

| Condition | Typical fasting C‑peptide | Stimulated response | Expected clinical adjuncts |
|-----------|----------------------------|----------------------|----------------------------|
| **HNF1A‑MODY** (early–mid disease) | Low‑normal or mildly reduced (≈0.3–0.8 nmol/L) | **> 0.60 nmol/L** (often ~0.8–2 nmol/L) – modest rise | Negative autoantibodies, strong autosomal‑dominant family history |
| **Type 1 diabetes** (established) | Very low or undetectable (< 0.2 nmol/L) | Minimal change (< 0.05 nmol/L) – essentially flat | Positive GADA/IA‑2/ZnT8 antibodies, rapid decline in C‑peptide over months |

In the presented case the **stimulated level reaches 0.83 nmol/L**, crossing the preservation threshold despite a low fasting value. This pattern is *inconsistent* with advanced autoimmune β‑cell loss and *compatible* with **partial β‑cell dysfunction typical of HNF1A‐MODY**, where secretory capacity declines gradually rather than being abruptly abolished.

When paired with:

* **Negative islet autoantibodies** (GADA, IA‑2A),
* A **multigenerational early‑onset diabetes pedigree**, and
* Absence of ketoacidosis,

the glucagon test **raises the pre‑test probability for MODY from modest to high**, supporting proceeding to definitive genetic testing—as was done in the report (NGS uncovered a pathogenic p.N270S HNF1A variant).

---

### 2️⃣ How does this result shape **therapeutic choice**?

| Therapeutic option | Rationale based on C‑peptide results |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Insulin (continuous)** | Needed when β‐cell output is negligible (stimulated C‑peptide < 0.30 nmol/L) → typical of type 1 or long‐standing MODY with exhausted reserve |
| **Sulfonylurea (low dose)** | Exploits residual ATP‐sensitive K⁺ channel activity; effective when *any* endogenous secretion remains (stimulated C‑peptide > 0.60 nmol/L). Historically first line in HNF1A‐MODY |
| **GLP‑1 receptor agonist** (e.g., dulaglutide) | Enhances glucose‑dependent insulin release *and* reduces glucagon; useful when β‐cell reserve is modest but declines further—avoids hypoglycaemia seen with sulfonylureas in borderline patients |
| **Metformin ± DPP‑4 inhibitor** | Addresses hepatic gluconeogenesis & incretin tone without relying on β‐cell mass; safe adjunct once glycaemia is near target |

In this patient:

* The modestly preserved stimulated C‑peptide signaled that the pancreas could still respond to secretagogues.
* After confirming an HNF1A mutation, clinicians **discontinued basal insulin**, switched first to a GLP‑1RA (**dulaglutide**) to harness residual secretion while mitigating hypoglycaemia risk, and later simplified therapy to metformin + saxagliptin once HbA₁c fell to 6 %.
* Had the stimulated C‑peptide remained < 0.30 nmol/L despite negative antibodies, a decision to maintain insulin would have been justified, as genuine type 1 diabetes would be far more likely.

---

### Take‑Home Message

- A **glucagon‐stimulated C‑peptide > 0.60 nmol/L** argues strongly for *preserved β‑cell function*. In a young adult with negative autoantibodies and an autosomal dominant family history, this finding tips the balance toward **HNF1A‐MODY**, not type 1 diabetes.
- Demonstrated residual secretion enables **non‑insulin strategies**—low‐dose sulfonylureas or GLP‑1 receptor agonists—​which are both more physiologic and cost‐effective for HNF1A‐MODY.
- The test should be viewed as a *supportive biological filter*: it does not replace genetic confirmation but sharpens diagnostic certainty enough to justify molecular testing and tailor therapy away from lifelong insulin when appropriate.

> **Clinical pearl:** In any young adult with unexplained hyperglycaemia, obtain a glucagon stimulation test *early*. A stimulated C‑peptide above the preservation cutoff markedly raises suspicion for MODY and opens the door to precision therapy—​a classic illustration of “biology before branding.”
