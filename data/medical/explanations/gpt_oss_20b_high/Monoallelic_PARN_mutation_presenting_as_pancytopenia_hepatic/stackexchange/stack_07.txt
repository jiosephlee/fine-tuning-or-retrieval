Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Differential Diagnosis] "Name at least five non‑genetic causes that must be excluded when evaluating hepatic steatosis and cirrhosis in a young adult before diagnosing hepatic involvement secondary to a telomere biology disorder."

**Non‑genetic etiologies that must be ruled out prior to attributing hepatic steatosis/cirrhosis in a young adult to a telomere‑biology disorder**

| Category | Typical presenting clues | How it was excluded in this patient | Relevance |
|----------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|-----------|
| **Alcoholic liver disease** | History of significant alcohol intake; AST > ALT with AST/ALT > 2; supportive imaging of fatty change | No reported alcohol use; AST/ALT ratio was <1 on presentation | Alcohol exposure can mimic fatty infiltrates and fibrosis |
| **Non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease / NASH** | Obesity/overweight or diabetes mellitus; metabolic panel with dyslipidemia; elevated ALT/AST | BMI was not markedly elevated; lipid profile was within normal limits; transient elastography revealed steatosis but no evidence of insulin resistance | NAFLD is the most common cause of steatosis worldwide |
| **Chronic viral hepatitis (HBV/HCV)** | Positive HBsAg/HBcAb or HCV antibody/RNA PCR | All viral serologies—including HBV surface antigen and HCV antibody—were negative | Persistent viral infection causes progressive fibrosis |
| **Drug‑induced hepatotoxicity** | Recent exposure to known hepatotoxic agents (amiodarone, methotrexate, statins); temporal relationship with onset of symptoms | No medications identified that are known hepatotoxins at toxic doses; medication review was unremarkable | Certain drugs cause centrilobular necrosis → fibrosis |
| **Autoimmune hepatitis** | Positive ANA, SMA ± LKM‑1 antibodies; hypergammaglobulinemia; often younger females | Full autoimmune panel including ANA and SMA was negative; serum IgG within normal range | Autoimmune inflammation leads classically to bridging fibrosis |
| **Other acquired cholestatic/inflammatory causes** *(auxiliary screening)*<br>- Primary sclerosing cholangitis <br>- Cholangiocarcinoma <br>- Sarcoidosis <br>- Thyroid dysfunction ± malnutrition <br>—(Each evaluated by imaging / biopsy where feasible)* | Imaging evidence of ductular ectasia or masses; elevated ALP/γ‑GT ≥ 2–3× ULN → needs MRCP/ERCP* | Imaging (CT/PET-CT) did not reveal obstructive lesions or inflammatory cholangiopathy |

**Take‑home point:** In any young adult with unexplained steatosis and cirrhosis, a systematic exclusion of these common acquired causes—outlined above—is mandatory before invoking a telomere‑biology disorder. Even after correction of nutritional deficiencies—as done here—the persistence of organomegaly and fibrosis necessitates comprehensive serologic & imaging studies that effectively ruled out the above etiologies and directed us toward genomic evaluation for TBD.
