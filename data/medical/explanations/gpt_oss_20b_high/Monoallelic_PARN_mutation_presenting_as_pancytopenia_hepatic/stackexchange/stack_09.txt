Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] "What ACMG criteria were met by the p.Cys205Arg missense variant identified in this patient’s PARN gene, and how do these criteria inform its classification as a variant of uncertain significance?"

**ACMG/AMP assessment of the PARN c.613T>C (p.Cys205Arg) variant**

| ACMG/AMP criterion | How it applies to p.Cys205Arg | Evidence from the case report |
|--------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **PM1 – Located in a critical functional domain** | Cys205 lies within the highly conserved R3H
deadenylase domain (residues 178–245), a region where all known pathogenic PARN variants cluster and no established benign variants exist “within this domain.” | The authors note that Cys205 is highly conserved and resides in the R3H domain; no benign entry for this residue is reported in ClinVar or population databases. |
| **PM2 – Absent/very rare in controls** | The allele occurs at < 0.001 % frequency in gnomAD and other large population databases, indicating it is not a common polymorphism. | The report states “absent or extremely rare” in population databases. |
| **PP3 – In silico predictive evidence** | Multiple computational tools (LRT, Mutation Assessor, MutationTaster, SIFT) predict a damaging effect on protein function. | “Predicted to be damaging by four of five tools.” |
| **PP2 – Gene‑specific constraint** | *PARN* has a low tolerance for missense variation; most disease‑causing alleles are missense/haploinsufficiency‑related. | The authors refer to *PARN* as a “gene with a low rate of benign missense variation” and “missense variants are a common mechanism of disease.” |

No other ACMG evidence was available:

* **Functional studies** – none reported.
* **Segregation / de novo status** – not assessed due to lack of family testing.
* **Population founder effects** – none identified.
* **Multiple affected individuals / independent occurrences** – only sporadic reported cases exist.

#### How these findings determine a Variant of Uncertain Significance

The ACMG/AMP framework assigns quantitative weights:

* *Moderate* evidence → 1 point each
* *Supporting* evidence → 0.5 point each

Adding up:
 • PM1 = 1 point  
 • PM2 = 1 point  
 • PP3 = 0.5 point  
 • PP2 = 0.5 point  

Total score = **3 points** = *VUS*.  
A classification of *Likely Pathogenic* would require at least one strong criterion or five moderate/superior scores (e.g., ≥ 4 points with at least one strong).  

Thus, even though the variant resides in a critical domain and shows predicted deleteriousness with high constraint on salt-induced dysregulation at that site, the absence of functional assays or segregation data prevents upgrading beyond uncertain significance.  

Future data—functional telomere length assays demonstrating defective TERC maturation or demonstration that the variant segregates with premature‑ageing features in additional family members—would shift interpretation toward *pathogenic*, potentially guiding more aggressive surveillance or consideration for bone‑marrow transplantation if marrow failure evolves.
