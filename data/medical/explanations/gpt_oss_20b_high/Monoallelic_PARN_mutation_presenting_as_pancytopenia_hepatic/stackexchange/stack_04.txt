Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] "What functional consequence would you expect when a missense mutation occurs within the R3H domain of PARN concerning TERC RNA processing and overall telomerase activity?"

**Functional consequence**

The missense change p.Cys 205Arg resides in the highly conserved **R3H RNA‑binding/exonuclease domain** of PARN (residues 178–245).  PARN removes a post‑transcriptionally added oligoadenylate tail from newly transcribed **TERC** so that it can acquire its correct 5′–7‑15 restructured intramolecular domains required for stable association with **TERT**.  

A deleterious alteration at Cys 205 is predicted (LRT/MutationTaster/SIFT etc.) to disturb either metal coordination or the catalytic core, thereby **reducing or abolishing exoribonuclease activity** on nascent TERC transcripts.  Consequences include

1. **Failure to trim the poly(A) tail** → accumulation of immature 5′–7 nt leucine-rich repeats that cannot fold into the proper secondary structure;
2. **Decreased pool of mature (~451‑nt) TERC** available for holoenzyme assembly;
3. **Impaired recruitment of TERT** and consequent **markedly reduced telomerase enzymatic activity**;
4. **Accelerated leukocyte and stem‑cell telomere attrition**, which underlies the pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and pulmonary fibrosis seen in this patient’s psoriasis-like spectrum disorder.

In other words, a missense variant within the R3H domain is expected to establish a practical loss‑of‑function state (haploinsufficiency), producing deficient TERC maturation and downstream reduction in overall telomerase activity—essentially reproducing the molecular defect identified by your genomic workup (c.613T>C p.Cys 205Arg) and explaining her clinical presentation as a telomere biology disorder despite being labeled a Variant Of Uncertain Significance at present clinical interpretation.
