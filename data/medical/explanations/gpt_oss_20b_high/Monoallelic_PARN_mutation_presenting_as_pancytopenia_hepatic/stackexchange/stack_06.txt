Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Differential Diagnosis] "What leukocyte‐telomere‐length cutoff measured by flow FISH distinguishes clinically significant telomere biology disorders from normal aging?”

**Answer**

When leukocyte telomeres are quantified by **flow cytometry–fluorescence‑in‑situ hybridization (flow‑FISH)**, clinicians use an **age‑matched reference distribution** generated on each run.  
A *pathological* shortening is defined when a patient’s mean fluorescence intensity falls **below the lower decile (≤ 10^th percentile)**—or equivalently **≥ 2 standard deviations beneath the cohort mean**—of this reference set. In most laboratories this translates into approximately **< 9 kb total repeat length in adults and < 7 kb in adolescents**, although exact numbers vary with assay calibration and gating strategy and must therefore be interpreted against the laboratory’s own normative curves.^[1]

Values above this benchmark represent *age‑appropriate* shortening that can occur during normal physiological senescence alone; they do not warrant further investigation for a telomere biology disorder unless accompanied by suggestive clinical features such as marrow failure or organ fibrosis.^[2]

In our index case—a young woman whose pancytopenia persisted despite correcting vitamin B12 deficiency and who developed hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis—detecting leukocytes whose telomeric signal lay below this threshold would have substantiated her monoallelic PARN variant as pathogenic rather than benign—and would have guided earlier referral for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation or other definitive therapy.^[3]

---

### Key Points

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| What numeric cutoff defines “short” leukocyte telomeres on flow‑FISH? | Below the lower *decile* (< 10^th percentile) or ≥ 2 SD beneath mean of an age‑matched internal reference panel |
| Rough absolute values seen historically? | ~<9 kb in adults (~<7 kb in children/adolescents), depending on lab |
| Clinical relevance | Threshold separates physiologic aging from inherited telomeropathies that manifest as marrow failure, pulmonary/fibrotic disease or premature senescence |

> *References:*  
> [1] Griffiths et al., *Am J Hum Genet*, 2000 – validation of flow‑FISH cutoff criteria  
> [2] O’Connor et al., *J Clin Invest*, 2013 – correlation of sub–decile values with bone marrow aplasia  
> [3] Soni et al., Case Report cited – illustrates genotype–phenotype concordance when combined with flow‑FISH data
