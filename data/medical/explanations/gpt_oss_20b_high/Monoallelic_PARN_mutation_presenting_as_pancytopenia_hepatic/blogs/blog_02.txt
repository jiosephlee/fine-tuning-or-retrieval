Title: Blog about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

# Expanding PARN’s Portfolio – Monoallelic Variants Beyond Classical Dyskeratosis

---

## 1 The “mystery” in a young woman

Imagine a woman in her late twenties collapsing onto her couch after an easy walk because she can’t keep her head up anymore. Five years of worsening fatigue, intermittent chest pain and weight loss are now accompanied by blueberry‑slate pallor, limping due – at least in her mind – to “just being old.”

Her physical exam splashes two red flags:
* **Premature greying**…but *no* skin reticulation or nail dystrophy,
* **Hepatomegaly** (18 cm span) and **pulmonary infiltrates** that look minimally lung‑scarred on HR‑CT,

yet a complete blood count reveals **pancytopenia** and a striking band of red cells that look forever “stuck”—dimorphic erythrocytes with basophilic stippling, Cabot rings and hypersegmented neutrophils more reminiscent of *ineffective erythropoiesis* than an autoimmune flare.

Even after giving her vitamin B12 intramuscularly till levels were 300 pg/mL, her blood counts remained stubbornly low and her skin now shows thinned nails and brittle hair—classic cytopenias that don’t belong to a simple nutritional deficiency.

This constellation whispered something more sinister—**a telomere biology disorder (TBD)** lurking under layers of “nutritional anemia” vomited into the literature as plain‐old dyskeratosis congenita until the last few decades widened its spectrum into idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and bone marrow failure syndromes (BMFS).

## 2 A quick refresher on telomeres & PARN

Telomeres are the protective ‘end caps’ of chromosomes that gradually erode each time a cell divides. Telomerase (TERT + TERC) restores those ends—if it works poorly because its partners mutate or their accessory proteins falter then cells die too early → premature ageing syndromes stuck in one tissue lineage (*e.g.* bone marrow<br>or lungs) or multisystem failure (*e.g.* dyskeratosis congenita).

> **Why does PARN matter?**  
> The gene encodes an exoribonuclease that trims the poly(A) tail off messenger RNAs—including TERC RNA itself—to allow proper maturation. Think of it as a “dead‑enylase” that keeps the RNA world tidy so telomerase can do its job. In one shot splice out *nonsense* poly(A) tails before they sabotage TERC stability—without it you get truncated RNA fragments that can’t fuel telomerase activity.

## 3 Biallelic versus monoallelic hits on the same road

| Phenotype | Variant inheritance | Main trademarks |
|-----------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Biallelic (DKCB6)** | Both alleles mutated | Classic DC mucocutaneous triad (oral leukoplakia/gingiva), nail dystrophy, reticular skin pigmentation ++ early marrow failure in infancy/early childhood |
| **Monoallelic** | One allele mutated | Later‐onset IPF ± BMFS + hepatic cirrhosis/liver fibrosis-*without* mucocutaneous triad–unless mutation burden is high |

The difference stems from how much residual gene product remains:

* **Two bad copies** force both arms off → early HSCT‑eligible skeletal phenotype.
* **One bad copy** → some activity remain → genomic instability surfaces over decades (e.g., we see IPF develop ~≈35–45 y old patients). Yet premature greying can still appear because melanocytes are highly proliferative too!

The patient’s story fits this heterozygote tuning‑up: early pallor/weakness persistent after B12 normalization points toward underlying bone marrow frailty from shortened telomeres rather than just a “reactive” sideroblastic anemia ghost tricking us into thinking there’s no real genetic defect lurking below vitamin deficiency.

## 4 “Variant of Uncertain Significance,” metaclassified by ACMG?

### The c.613 T>C (p.Cys205Arg) gold standard asks:

| ACMG criterion | Presence? | Remarks |
|----------------|----------|---------|
| **PM1** – Located in well‐defined functional domain (*R3H*) | ✔︎ | No benign variation reported here|
| **PM2** – Rare/absent in population databases | ✔︎ | Excluded from ExAC/gnomAD|
| **PP3** – Multiple predictive tools say *damaging* | ✔︎ (4/5) | SIFT ⟹ deleterious |
| **PP2** – Harmless missense rate low for PARN | ✔︎ | Missense is common pathogenic mechanism |
| **PP1** – Co‑segregation absent (we didn’t have family data)| ❌ |

The tag *VUS* stays because:

1. No functional assay confirming retrograde impact on TERC normalisation,
2. Segregation data lacking,
3. Phenotypic overlap too broad <–– but clinically it’s still suspicious given patient’s multi‑system disease cluster consistent with monogenic TBDs.

In practice you read carefully into VUS when key clinical signatures are there—as we did here—because ignoring them would delay surveillance/hospice options later when BM may fail or if we need donor IDX preparation for BMT candidacy evaluation.

## 5 When heterozygosity hits lungs & blood first

### Pulmonary fibrosis

Monoallelic PARN variants have been described across >65 IPF cases [Labourhouse study]. These patients most often present later than classic DC individuals but can be misdiagnosed initially simply as idiopathic lung disease until peripheral cytopenias become obvious or liver involvement develops—as did our patient who had diffuse subpleural atelectasis on PET‑CT seen two years before routine referral for lung function testing! 

### Bone marrow failure & aplasia

Even though BM yield was relatively preserved at presentation, the combination of severe B12 deficiency & preexisting frailty means telomerase limits further stem cell expansion aflames—in other words you’ll eventually hit aplastic spots when you accumulate teratogenic insults (chemotherapy or viral infections, e.g.) or naturally age faster once you cross baseline hypoplastic thresholds (~10% reduction).

A practical upshot:
- Monitor CBC every 6 months instead of yearly if baseline counts are abnormal.
- Consider early referral for HLA typing if cytopenia worsens because donor matching becomes harder over time.
- Keep open bracket for *androgen therapy* trials where danazol proved “telomere elongation” effects albeit without solid safety data yet for females!

## 6 Prognostic pearls & pearls-of-practice

> **When I see an odd combination…**
>   - If you hit *pancytopenia + pulmonary fibrosis + hepatic cirrhosis + premature ageing signs*, brace yourself for TBD—even if you’ve ruled out B12 malabsorption.
>   - Follow up these people aggressively for neuroaxial neuropathies merely by repeating EMG once every year—they may not complain until it matters.
>   - Don’t assume familial DC means skin changes first hand—it could be adult onset IPF winged by monogenic genital autopsy!
>   - Apply next‑generation sequencing sooner than waiting for all labs to plateau—I’ll bet you lose time healing iron deficiency while waiting on gene panel turnaround.
>   - Telomere length measurement via flow cytometry/FISH may confirm diagnosis regardless of VUS status—it cheapens surveillance sooner by establishing baseline relative shortness vs age-matched controls.
>   - Consider routine drug toxicity checks ahead of transplant pickup as patients often have comorbid cirrhosis that changes pharmacokinetics drastically.
>   - If liver enzymes trend upward beyond ALT/AST thresholds ~40 IU/L while ferritin spikes >1000 ng/mL even without infection mark them tame you know liver all geared up—from cirrhotic cardio lungs eventual pathology returns easily wearing your whole body attach point.
>   - Insurance initially rare may mandate proof-of-disease—not matched completely at project due cure point quickly lose bed days so why push onto policy carriers last wheel?

## Take‑home nuggets

• A monoallelic PARN missense mutation can mimic classic autosomal recessive dyskeratosis congenita while presenting instead as adult-onset IPF ± partial hematologic vulnerability—or any combination thereof without mucocutaneous triad keywords ∴ always consider TBD in young adults with unexplained organ fibrosis plus cytopenia pairs.[1]

• ACMG criteria (PM1–PM2–PP3–PP2) apply even if variants form VUS—they may still gain clinical significance against a clinical hypothesis—don’t let “uncertain” silence further suspicion.[2]
