## Pathophysiology and Epidemiology of Behçet’s Disease  

Behçet’s disease (BD) is an idiopathic multisystem vasculitic disorder typified by recurrent oral ulceration accompanied by genital ulcers, skin lesions and ocular inflammation. Because its presentation overlaps widely recognized conditions such as infectious meningitis or systemic connective‑tissue diseases, several classification frameworks have been devised so clinicians can identify it reliably during routine clinical practice while waiting for further investigations. The current consensus relies on the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD). Under this system points are assigned based on discrete clinical manifestations – two points each for oral aphthae confirmed over several occasions, genital ulcers confirmed over several occasions or one single episode together with two further points if ocular involvement or major mucocutaneous eruptions having nonspecific morphology are noted – whereas cutaneous pseudofolliculitis receives one additional point when present outside familiar eczema sites.[^12] A total score ≥ 4 establishes “definite” BD allowing appropriate therapeutic intensification while remaining sufficiently sensitive so that patients presenting concurrently with neurologic manifestations do not await prospective confirmation from ancillary imaging tests before initiating treatment plans that address both systemic inflammation and potential central nervous system complications.[^13] In our patient, two points were garnered from oral aphthae reported repeatedly over his history and retrospectively verified upon assessment today plus an additional two points from genital ulceration observed during admission day five promptly followed by an inadvertent follicular eruption inducing an extra point for cutaneous disease – summing five points confirms definite disease per ICBD before obtaining additional laboratory confirmation.[^14]  

Genetic susceptibility plays an integral role even though autonomic autoinflammation remains poorly elucidated mechanistically. The strongest single allele association identified worldwide is HLA‑B51 transmitted in an autosomal dominant fashion when both parents provide carriage while its risk magnitude varies dramatically among different ethnicities—it confers roughly threefold relative risk among Turkish individuals versus near‑neutral odds among Northern European controls.[^15] Yet despite this compelling association residing more strongly within populations sharing long ancestral residence along eastern Eurasia's “Silk Road,” many patients lacking detectable B51 alleles nevertheless meet standard clinical diagnostic thresholds (“HLA‑negative” cohorts appear clinically indistinguishable). Thus B51 appears paradoxically both predictive yet insufficient alone—a consequence aligning well with our Asian gentleman’s positive typing measured after genotype screening.[^16]  

Underlying immunopathogenesis comprises dysregulated neutrophil recruitment together with dysregulated cytokine exchange characteristic of acute systemic inflammation rather than classical autoimmune patterning driven predominantly through autoantibodies.[^17] Polymorphonuclear leukocytes isolated from circulating blood samples exhibit augmented motility through interleukin‑8–rich chemokine gradients while spontaneously secreting granulocyte proteases independent of classical T‐cell activation pathways.[^18] Concomitantly infected‐dying tissue releases danger signals inciting monocyte/macrophage responses producing pronounced interleukin‑1β (“IL‑1β”) and interleukin‑6 (“IL‑6”) release – elevations mirrored clinically via markedly raised C‑reactive protein produced hepatically through IL‑6 stimulation — an observation connecting systemic inflammation severity directly to elevated intracellular mediator levels present even prior any overt organ damage noted clinically.[^19] In neuro‐Behçet’s disease particular attention has been drawn toward increased CSF IL‑6 values found during acute episodes possibly modulating adrenomedullary function thereby substituting atypical ADH regulation producing SIADH—a hypothesis embraced when systemic cytokines influence hypothalamic osmoreceptor activity under chronic cerebral angiogenic insult setting postulated throughout literature.[^20]

## Case Presentation & Initial Assessment  

A man in his sixties presented to the emergency department after a three‑day history of fever and progressively worsening headache accompanied by persistent hiccups and arthralgia involving his shoulders and ankles. He reported an accompanying loss of appetite since symptom onset. At arrival he was alert but appeared fatigued; no focal neurological deficits were noted during bedside examination.  

Early in the encounter clinicians noted several classic mucocutaneous manifestations characteristic of Behçet’s disease (BD). The patient exhibited multiple painful oral aphthae visible on inspection of the oral cavity; erythematous folliculitis‑like eruptions distributed over his face and trunk were prominent upon careful dermatologic review. A genital ulcer – later visible on examination of the scrotum – further supported a systemic inflammatory process involving mucosal surfaces common to BD.

Vital‑sign monitoring revealed a heart rate of 119 beats per minute against a systolic blood pressure of approximately 135/73 mm Hg, indicating tachycardia without hypotension—a pattern compatible with an euvolemic status rather than hypovolemic shock or hypervolemic states such as heart failure. Physical evaluation showed absent peripheral edema and dry skin patches were absent, reinforcing clinical euvolemia despite mild tachycardia likely driven by febrile stress rather than volume depletion or excess.

Routine laboratory studies immediately highlighted profound hyponatraemia ([Na⁺] = 124 mmol/L) accompanied by a low serum osmolality (267 mOsm/kg), classically denoting a hypo‑osmolar state produced by excess water retention rather than extrarenal fluid loss. Urine–studies were equally telling: an elevated urine osmolality (900 mOsm/kg) combined with a high urinary sodium concentration (88 mmol/L) confirmed inappropriate water reabsorption under euvolemic conditions—a hallmark finding supporting syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH). Serum thyroid‑stimulating hormone, free thyroxine and plasma cortisol concentrations were all within normal ranges ([TSH] = 1.11 mIU/L; [free T₄] = 1.25 ng/dL; cortisol = 42 µg/dL), effectively ruling out hypothyroidism or adrenal insufficiency as underlying causes manifesting similarly biochemically.

Further endocrine testing confirmed an elevated plasma antidiuretic hormone level ([21–24] pg/mL) consistent with central overproduction rather than ectopic secretion or assay artifact—a criterion required for finalising an SIADH diagnosis per Consensus Guidelines (avoidances such as hyperglycemia‑induced dilution were accounted for through correction equations).

The convergence of inflammatory mucocutaneous lesions suggestive rapidly evolving BD and objective laboratory evidence confirming central dysregulation of water handling through excessive ADH prompted an expedited diagnostic pathway directed toward acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease complicated by SIADH – conditions whose overlapping clinical profiles demand high index indexing during initial triage.【source】
