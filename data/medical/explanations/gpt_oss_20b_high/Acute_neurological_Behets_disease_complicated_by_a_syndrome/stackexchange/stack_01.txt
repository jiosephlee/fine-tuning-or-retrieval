Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] "According to the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD), what is the minimum total score required for a definitive diagnosis and which mucocutaneous manifestations contribute how many points respectively?" 

**Answer**

*According to the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD)*

| Clinical domain | Points awarded |
|-----------------|---------------|
| **Mucocutaneous** | |
| • Recurrent **oral aphthae** | **+2** |
| • Recurrent **genital aphthae** | **+2** |
| • Any **cutaneous lesion** (erythema nodosum, pseudofolliculitis/follicular pustules, papulopustular eruptions…) | **+1** |

A score **≥ 4** constitutes a *definite* Behçet’s disease diagnosis.  
In the presented patient, oral aphthae (+2), genital ulcers (+2) and folliculitis‑like rash (+1) gave a total of **five points**, meeting the ICBD threshold (≥ 4) for definite BD.[*See Abstract*]

---

These weighted scores reflect the relative specificity of each manifestation for Behçet’s disease; mucocutaneous signs are among the most frequently appreciated features and form an essential part of early diagnostic confidence—especially when neurological symptoms coexist, as illustrated in this case where neuro‑Behçet’s was suspected before full clinical confirmation.[*See Case Report*]
