Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] "List at least three entities that should be excluded before labeling this illness as acute neuro‐Behçet syndrome when faced with fever, headache, mild lymphocytic CSF pleocytosis and hypoosmolar hyponatraemia." 

**Key differentials that must be ruled out before calling this an “acute neuro‑Behçet”**

| Entity | Why it fits the presentation | How it was excluded in this patient |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Infectious meningo‑encephalitic processes**<br>• Bacterial meningitis (e.g., *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*)<br>• Viral encephalitides (HSV 1/2 or VZV)<br>• Tuberculous or fungal meningitis | Fever + headache + slightly elevated LP cell count ≤ 15 cells/µL are classic markers.<br>CSF osmolality rarely affected by bacterial causes but serum sodium crosses <135 mmol/L occasionally due to fever‑induced diuresis.| • Gram staining/culture = negative.<br>• CSF PCR panels for HSV/VZV were “negative”.<br>• Acid‑fast bacilli smears/PCR DNA test for MTB were “negative”.<br>• No neutrophilic predominance in the LP.| 
| **Primary central‑nervous‑system lymphoma or metastasis** | Neuro‑BD can mimic a silent mononuclear parapneumonic picture while some paraneoplastic syndromes associated with idiopathic IgG4‑related hypophysial disease present likewise.</li>\n&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(Elevated sIL‑2R level suggested investigations.) | • Brain MRI showed **no focal infiltrates** nor contrast enhancement.<br>• Serial cerebrospinal fluid cytology revealed **no malignant cells**)<br>• Mild sIL‑2R elevation falls below the threshold typical for NHL.| 
| **Neurosarcoidosis** | Granulomatous involvement often produces aseptic Meninginetisis with lymphocyte‐predominant pleocytosis plus mild systemic constitutional signs encouraging similar biochemical picture.</li>\n&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(Fiirst cystic lesions localized to hypothalamus). | • Serum angiotensin‐converting enzyme = 11 U/L (**normal**).<br>• Lysozyme = 5​µg/mL (within reference range).<br>• Chest CT/MRI did **not reveal hilar adenopathy** or parenchymal opacities.| 

*Additional conditions worth evaluating in similar presentations*  

– **Other connective‐tissue vasculitides** such as granulomatosis with polyangiitis → ANCA panel was negative in our patient.<br>– **Autoimmune seronegative encephalopathies** (e.g., anti‑NMDA R antibodies) → an appropriate panel would dampen bias if patients develop seizures/memory loss.<br>– **Endocrine causes of SIADH** e.g., hypothalamic pituitary tumors → MR without hypothalamic lesion ruled these out.  

Only after these entities are clearly ruled out—using microbiologic workup, imaging ± cytology—and once a stable set of mucocutaneous signs satisfy criteria for Behçet’s disease does acute neuro‑Behçet become a valid diagnosis…the very scenario portrayed in the Kakehi et al paper where fever‐driven ‘aseptic’ meningitis evolved into self‑limited neuro‑BD managed successfully by colchicine alone.*
