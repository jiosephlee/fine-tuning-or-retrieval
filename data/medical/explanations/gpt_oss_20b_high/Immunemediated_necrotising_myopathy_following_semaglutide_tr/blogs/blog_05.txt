## Introduction  

Semaglutide has become a staple in both Type 2 diabetes and obesity management thanks to its excellent glucose‐lowering and weight‐loss profiles. Its side effect profile has been largely restricted to nausea, vomiting and mild hypoglycaemia — things we can all act on quickly and predictably. Yet what happens when a patient presents with progressive proximal weakness, dysphagia and an explosive creatine kinase rise after a single injection? This is the scenario that unfolded in our patient’s journey through the emergency department, intensive care unit, rheumatology ward and ultimately back home functioning well again after a short stint of steroids plus IVIG **and** discontinuation of semaglutide.

* Identifying causality from a single dose plutuses a question that many clinicians meet daily when confronted with emergent drug reactions—but most will resort either to “clinical suspicion” or finer pharmacovigilance tools half upon instinct.*
