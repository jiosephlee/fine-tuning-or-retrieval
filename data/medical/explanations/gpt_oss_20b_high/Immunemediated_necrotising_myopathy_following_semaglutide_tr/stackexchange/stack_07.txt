Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Why did pro-B-type natriuretic peptide rise markedly while left ventricular ejection fraction remained preserved during this illness episode?

**Why NT‑proBNP was markedly elevated while LVEF stayed normal**

In this patient the marked increase in NT‑proBNP (4 358 pg/mL vs. a reference ≤125 pg/mL) occurred in the setting of a brief episode of immune‑mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) precipitated by semaglutide use. **The key driver is subclinical myocardial inflammation** rather than frank systolic failure:

| Feature | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Cardiac imaging** | Echo demonstrated a preserved ejection fraction (65–70 %). Cardiac MRI showed mild hypokinesis of the mid‑anteroseptal/anterior wall with diffuse myocardial oedema but no late gadolinium enhancement—findings characteristic of *indeterminate* or *early* myocarditis / stress‑cardiomyopathy. |
| **Wall stress & stretch** | Even mild regional edema increases myocyte extracellular fluid volume and mechanical strain on ventricular walls. This local stretch stimulates atrial/ventricular myocytes to release natriuretic peptides independently of a drop in global LV systole. |
| **Neurohumoral activation** | The systemic inflammatory state—and transient tachyarrhythmia (paroxysmal atrial fibrillation) –augments sympathetic tone, raising preload and LV end‑diastolic pressure without immediate loss of contractility.|
| **Renal clearance** | The patient’s creatinine never rose above baseline thereafter, so decreased clearance does **not** account for the disproportionate NT‑proBNP rise (which would be only modest). |

Thus, *acute inflammatory edema* led to elevated intramyocardial pressure → heightened secretion of NT‑proBNP while overall pump function remained adequate until subsequent recovery. When the myocardium healed under immunosuppression, repeat imaging showed normalization of wall motion and resolution of BNP elevation, confirming reversible inflammatory involvement rather than permanent ventricular dysfunction.
