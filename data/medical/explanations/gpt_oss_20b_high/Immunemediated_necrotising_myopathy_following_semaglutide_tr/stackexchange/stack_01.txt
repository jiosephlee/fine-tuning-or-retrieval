Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] What histologic finding most reliably distinguishes immune-mediated necrotising myopathy from polymyositis or dermatomyositis?

**Answer**

The hallmark that separates immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from polymyositis or dermatomyositis on muscle biopsy is **extensive myofiber necrosis with accompanying regeneration yet a severe paucity of interstitial lymphocytes**—in particular, minimal endomysial/perimy‑seal CD8⁺ T‑cell infiltration.

In our patient’s biopsy (Figure 3 A–B) we saw diffuse clusters of necrotic fibers surrounded by macrophages (acid phosphatase/CD68+) and newly formed regenerating fibers, but only sparse CD3⁺ T-cell “hot spots” (Figure 4 A). This pattern contrasts sharply with:

| Condition | Typical histology |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Polymyositis** | Endomysial invasion by sheet‑forming CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells, markedly active inflammation |
| **Dermatomyositis** | Perifascicular atrophy, perivascular capillary dropout/driven complement deposition; abundant B‑cell/T‑cell clusters |
| **IMNM** | Predominant fibre necrosis + regeneration; few or no interstitial lymphocytes; macrophage phagocytosis prominent |

Thus, a biopsy showing widespread fibre necrosis/regeneration **without substantial inflammatory infiltrate** is the most reliable morphologic discriminator for IMNM relative to polymyositis or dermatomyositis.
