## Same Old “Fly‑the‑Bugs” Throne Meets New Genetic Reality
Fast facts remind us:
* PPF is an *extremely* uncommon diagnosis (~47 paediatric reports worldwide).  
* It almost always arises from either **acute pancreatitis**, chronic organ dysfunction, *or* direct pancreatic injury—yet few clinicians think “pancreas first” when chest pain dominates presentation.^[Majori et al.]  
* Our boy’s first sign was blood‑stained right pleural fluid that spiked occasionally weeks after blunt abdominal assault—and he was never formally labelled “chronic” anywhere before that moment.*  

Why did the pancreas react theatrically after seemingly harmless contact?
Because many paediatric patients actually carry remodelled genes predisposing their glands towards uncontrolled trypsin activity, washed down ducts, or structural weakness—all invisible unless you deliberately fish them out.^[Catts & Wickramasinghe]

## When Should You Dial In the Gene Panel?
You don’t wait until surgery ensues altering life forever. Consider paneling patients who meet any one of these criteria:
* First attack under ten years old *or*
* Two total attacks under twenty-days Elapsed time *or*
* Recurrent over three episodes per year *or*
* A single attack accompanied by unexplained hemodynamic instability (*e.g.*, sudden hypoxia shutdown)*  
These thresholds dovetail reporting standards adopted by recent American Gastroenterological Association consensus letters where crucial genotype information can guide emergent decisions within hours.^[AGA Guidelines]
