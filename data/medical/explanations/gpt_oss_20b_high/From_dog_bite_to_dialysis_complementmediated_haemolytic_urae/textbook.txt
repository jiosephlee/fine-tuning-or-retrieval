## 1 Epidemiology & Pathophysiology of *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* Infection  

*Capnocytophaga canimorsus* is a strictly canine‑associated, slow‑growing Gram‑negative rod that has become an internationally recognised zoonotic pathogen mainly through dog bites. Its pathogenicity lies not only in its capacity for rapid systemic spread when introduced into injured tissues but also in its sophisticated evasion strategies that target innate immunity—particularly the alternative complement cascade—which underwrite its association with fulminant sepsis and complement‑mediated thrombotic microangiopathy (CM‑HUS) as illustrated by our patient.  

---

### Dog‑bite epidemiology and human exposure risk  

Dog bites are common injuries worldwide; every year millions of persons present with bite wounds at emergency departments. Although most dog‑bite wounds result in uncomplicated cellulitis or minor complications, a minority precipitate severe systemic infections affecting endothelium, blood cells, liver and kidneys. *C.* canimorsus is resident in the dogs’ oral cavity and upper respiratory tract; high‑grade bacterial inoculation ensues when a bite pierces mucous membranes or breaks exposed skin. The risk for invasive disease escalates when bites involve areas prone to contamination (mouth, face) or when there are comorbidities that predispose to bacteremia (e.g., alcoholism, diabetes). In healthy adults—a cohort that includes our patient—the incidence is low but potentially lethal; mortality rates approach 30 % when bacteremia occurs despite adequate antibiotic coverage.

---

### Microbiology of *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*: Gram‑negative rod morphology and encapsulation  

*C.* canimorsus is a facultative anaerobic Gram‑negative rod (0.3–2 µm × 1–4 µm) with a thin capsule rich in polysaccharides (including sialylated components). The capsule confers resistance against phagocytosis by neutrophils and serum killing—a key virulence determinant—yet many isolates remain susceptible (“capsule negative” strains) permitting rapid clearance if host defenses are intact. Cultures are notoriously slow-growing (often >48 h), prompting reliance on molecular diagnostics for early detection, particularly because standard blood cultures may remain negative for several days after initial sampling—as seen in our patient where *C.* canimorsus grew after seven days despite early empirical therapy.

---

### Clinical spectrum ranging from localized cellulitis to fulminant sepsis  

Infection manifestations span from mild localized cellulitis at the bite site through toxic shock syndrome, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), multiorgan failure and thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA). Classic early signs are fever (often >40 °C), tachycardia and localized erythema that rapidly progresses when untreated. Sepsis progresses quickly; laboratory findings usually reveal leukopenia or leukocytosis with neutrophil left shift, thrombocytopenia secondary to consumptive coagulopathy, elevated lactate from tissue hypoxia—precisely what was documented on admission for our patient—and rising creatinine indicating acute kidney injury (AKI).

---

### Overview of innate immunity with emphasis on the alternative complement pathway  

Innate immune defense against Gram‑negative bacteria hinges upon pattern recognition receptors detecting lipopolysaccharide (LPS) via toll‑like receptor 4 (TLR4) and subsequent activation of all three complement pathways—classical , lectin , and alternative . The alternative pathway provides a self‑amplifying circuit whereby spontaneous hydrolysis of C3 generates C3(H₂O), which associates with factor B; factor B is then cleaved by factor D producing Bb fragments that associate with C3(H₂O) yielding fluid-phase C3 convertase C3(H₂O)Bb . Local deposition of C3b leads to amplification via covalent attachment on bacterial surfaces forming cell-bound convertases C₃bBb . Critical regulators—including factor H , factor I , membrane cofactor protein (CD46) – limit such amplification by promoting C3b cleavage into inactive iC3b .

In healthy hosts this cascade eliminates pathogens before systemic spread occurs while sparing self‐tissues due to these regulatory checkpoints.

---

### Mechanisms by which *C.* canimorsus activates complement leading to endothelial injury  

The murine lipopolysaccharide profile of *C.* canimorsus contains unique acyl chains that potently stimulate TLR4 yet also instigate rapid complement activation via both classical/lectin pathways due to natural antibodies against sialic acids as well as through direct interaction with albumin-binding protein A-type moieties capable of binding surface/extracellular matrix components conducive for coating by factor B/C3b complex formation. Once deposited frame onto bacterial surfaces , it recruits factor B → factor D cleavage → generation gradient bursts generate Bb , enabling uncontrolled amplification even if soluble regulators are intact. Moreover:

1. **Capsular interference** – some strains suppress antibody deposition via capsule-mediated steric hindrance which delays classical pathway initiation but allows alternative pathway priming directly by LPS.
2. **Regulatory protein consumption** – massive surface opsonization drives consumption failure → depletion in plasma.
3. **Selective inhibitor** – some isolates express neuraminidase releasing sialic acids that degrade properly functioning factor H binding sites thereby permitting unchecked convertase maturation.
   
The net result is widespread endothelial activation characterized by upregulated adhesion molecules (P-selectin), dysfunctionally increased permeability and shear stresses—notably promoting subendothelial platelet aggregation secreted VEGFs resulting alve pressure ratio reversal culminating into TMA lesions evidenced histologically by fibrin thrombi within glomerular capillaries—a picture observed in our patient’s renal biopsy.



---

### “Second hit” model—how infections trigger latent complement dysregulation  

In many young adults without overt alloimmune disease an underlying “subclinical” alternative‑pathway dysregulation exists due largely to inherited trait defects or transient autoantibody formation against factor H . Until an intensifying stimulus (“second hit”) surpasses compensatory regulation threshold these individuals remain asymptomatic because their baseline regulator levels marginally control low‐level activation products generated during steady state.

Septic insults harness great amounts of LPS or pathogen-associated molecular patterns provoking strong TLR signals together with widespread tissue hypoxia/ischemia—all fueling massive unscheduled activation through:

* **Excessive convertase assembly** outpacing regulatory control.
* **Systemic consumption** of soluble regulators amid sustained inflammation.
* **Secondary production** of inflammatory cytokines exacerbating endothelial susceptibility.
* **Immune complex formation** around foreign antigens (*e.g.*, canine IgG or antigens presented from oral flora), further inciting classical pathway activation.
   
Once this flood overwhelms regulation beyond constitutional rebuilding capacity (~hours), endothelial cells suffer fragmentation producing schistocytes that spill into circulation—a hallmark measurable MAHA component described precisely at day 12 after ICU admission for this patient when her haptoglobin dropped fascinatingly leading clinicians towards TMA evaluation although routine variables initially made this consideration unlikely.



---

### Genetic susceptibility in CM‑HUS : CFH/CFI/CFB variants or anti‑factor H antibodies  

Familial clustering studies have identified pathogenic variants within:

* **CFH** (the major soluble regulator inhibiting surface bound convertases expression).
* **CFI** (serine protease facilitating rapid clearance).
* **CFB** (factor aiding conversion/amplification); pathogenic variants lead paradoxical hyperactivity 
* **CD46**, especially homozygous loss-of-function changes  
   
Complement gene testing frequently identifies one variant among ~50–70 % cases presenting eventually as CM-HUS upon triggering event—e.g., infection or pregnancy—without prior clinical manifestation owing parenteral immunity suppression until overwhelming challenge arises.Date Here no pathogenic variant was found thus raising suspicion that transient anti‑factor H antibody production due either facilitated viral capsid mimicry or immune complex depot might be involved; however absent auto‐iabs at baseline suggests infection itself served as primary “second hit.” 

Understanding these genetic underpinnings informs risk stratification because individuals harboring certain CFH variants experience more refractory disease likelihood requiring lifelong maintenance therapy post‐induction detoxification—as partially illustrated by decisions regarding ongoing eculizumab infusion post-corticosteroid tapering in our patient’s plan.



---

In sum, *C.* canimorsus inflicts severe systemic pathology through superlative capacity do activate alternative complement irreversibly at sites where it encounters host immunity disorders—even if subclinical pre-existing genotypes are silent until triggered clinically—as seen clinically where fatal sepsis evolved into exuberant CME-mediated TMA critical rather than solely infectious shock processes alone would dictate therapeutic strategy.

## 2 Initial Presentation & Emergency Department Evaluation  

The index event began abruptly when the patient was bitten on the upper lip by her domestic dog (“day‑0”). Although she printed a tetanus booster immediately thereafter, she did not begin a written course of phenoxymethylpenicillin because she could not obtain a prescription on the same day—a missing element that set the stage for unchecked bacterial growth over the next few days. By “day‑4,” she developed an explosive fever (>40 °C), profound malaise and oliguria complicated further by a sudden fall in capillary glucose levels (<0.6 mmol L⁻¹). The primary care setting attempted aggressive oral glucose replacement followed by subcutaneous glucagon injections without success, prompting urgent transfer straight to our tertiary emergency department despite the absence of obvious focal infections on physical examination beyond localized erythema around the bite site and mottling of both lower extremities.

### Vital Signs & Quick Sepsis Screening  
On arrival she was conscious but tachypneic (RR ≈ 28 breaths min⁻¹) with a systolic blood pressure just above the Surviving Sepsis Campaign threshold (>90 mm Hg). Her heart rate was moderately elevated (~110 bpm). Mental status remained intact i.e., no confusion was noted. Using these three parameters—a systolic arterial pressure ≤100 mm Hg ‑ *absent*, heart rate ≥90 bpm ‑ *present*, respiratory rate ≥22 breaths per minute ‑ **present**, altered mentation ‑ *absent*—the quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (qS­OFA) score calculated as **1/3**, signalling a modest likelihood of organ dysfunction secondary to infection while highlighting tachypnea as a prominent marker in this patient’s presentation.(1) This prompted activation of an aggressive “sepsis bundle” pathway even though classic SIRS criteria were only partially met given her preserved mental status and borderline hypertension.

### Complete Blood Count  
A CBC performed immediately suggested a paradoxical hematologic picture consistent with overwhelming infection rather than inherent marrow pathology:
| Parameter | Value | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Total WBC | **3·5×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹** | Leukopenia |
| Neutrophils | **~65 %** | Absolute ANC ≈ 2·3×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹ – *neutrophilic predominance* |
| Lymphocytes | ↓ | Lymphocytopenia associated with sepsis |
| Platelets | **80×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹** | Thrombocytopenia |

The combination denotes consumptive loss secondary to cytokine‑mediated redistribution coupled with platelet consumption characteristic of disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). The reduced leukocyte count mirrors profound systemic inflammation often seen in capnocytophagosis where capnodysin induces cellular lysis.(2)

### Coagulation Profile – Evidence for DIC  
Heparin‑free assays revealed a classic ISTH DIC phenotype:
* Prothrombin time (PT) = prolonged by +7 seconds  
* Activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) = prolonged (+32 seconds)  
* Fibrinogen = low at **0·8 g·dL⁻¹**  
* D‑dimer = markedly elevated (**>5 µg·mL⁻¹**)  

Additional data showed mild thrombocytopenia together with clinical findings of petechiae–ecchymoses pending full diagnostic confirmation (Fig. A). Requiring only timely bedside testing—crucial because organ failure often precedes overt laboratory relapse—these findings fulfilled two ISTH red‑flag arguments (“fibrin degradation products exceeding ten times upper reference level” and “low fibrinogen”), thereby satisfying partial criteria for overt DIC formation secondary to bacterial endotoxin activation.(3)

### Renal Function – Early AKI Markers  
Baseline serum creatinine was unknown clinically until she had been admitted previously without any history suggestive of chronic kidney impairment—presumably around **90 µmol·L⁻¹** based on outpatient data—but upon presentation it had risen sharply:
| Kidney Parameter | Baseline / Admission |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Serum creatinine | **≈ 175 µmol·L⁻¹** |
| Blood urea nitrogen | **≈ 65 mg·dL⁻¹** |
| Urine output | Initially oliguria → anuria reported within hours |

The abrupt increase masked both prerenal contributions driven by systemic vasodilatory shock (+ evaluated lactate peak ≈ 5·7 mmol·L⁻¹) and intrarenal microvascular thrombi causing acute tubular necrosis—a hallmark sequelae waiting confirmation spectra during longer observation.(4)

### Imaging Studies – Rule Out Necrotising Fasciitis / Vascular Catastrophe  
Given mottled skin pigmentation coupled with pain disproportionate to swelling—a classic red flag for rapidly evolving soft‑tissue infection—the team pursued urgent contrast‑enhanced computed tomography (CT)/fascia surface imaging over both lower limbs.
Images demonstrated:
* Normal fascial plane integrity without gas collection  
* No evidence of deep venous thrombosis or arterial occlusion  
* Thoracic/metabolic abscesses absent  

Therefore necrotising fasciitis was highly unlikely even though Global Pathology suggests any skin compromise following a dog bite warrants exploration.(5)

Similarly, routine brain MRI/angled CT angiography denied intracranial hemorrhage or ischemic thrombotic episodes—an insight critical because septic emboli would alter management strategies dramatically via additional antibiotics/supportive monitoring tailored toward Aspergillus–strores destructive bacteria like *C.* *capnocytophaga*.  

### Empirical Antimicrobial Therapy – Targeting Dog‑Bite Flora  
Recognizing that canine oral microbiota is dominated by *Pasteurella multocida*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*, Moraxella spp., Anaerobe groups {Flavobacterium/Fusobacterium} and fastidious *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*, clinicians preferred empiric broad‐spectrum coverage encompassing Gram‑positive cocci/extensively drug‑susceptible isolates alongside Gram‐negative rods capable otherwise manifesting remarkable resistance patterns.
Accordingly triple dosing piperacillin/tazobactam commenced within **<60 min** post–arrival (120 mg tazobactam per gram piperacillin intravenously every six hours), offering robust activity against β‑lactamase–producing organisms while retaining efficacy against slow growers such as *Capnocytophaga*. Empiric intravenous access provided route comfort throughout close monitoring until definitive bacteremia culture results materialised seven days later revealing true causative pathogen (*C.* *canimorsus*).

This vancomycin‐avoidance strategy minimized risk without compromising efficacy given bacteremia surfaced beyond tetanus vaccination’s protective window where protective host antibodies were absent.(6)

---

In summary, the patient's immediate evaluation highlighted four cardinal checkpoints common for any severe zoonotic infection:
1.) Rapid assessment via qS­OFA underlines organ compromise even when classic SIRS is equivocal;  
2.) Picture-perfect CBC‐derived evidence foreshadows septic consumption deficits;
3.) Established ISTH parameters unmistakably signal emerging DIC responsible for subsequent multiorgan insult;
4.) Prompt bedside imaging secures exclusion of catastrophic soft tissue dysfunction;
5.) All elements informed timely transition into critical care while empirically targeting notorious canine pathogens—including *C.* *canimorsus*.
