Title: Blog about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

# From Canine Bite to Complement Crisis  

---

When a woman arrives alive and alert a few hours after being bitten on her upper lip by her own dog you would normally think she needs broad‑spectrum antibiotics and supportive care – that’s pretty standard practice when thinking “dog‑bite sepsis”. Fifteen minutes later she keeps piecing together patchy fevers until her vitals settle enough for admission, her platelets drop into the teens × 10⁹ /L range, she becomes anephric overnight and finally her hemoglobin plunges without any documented bleed while schistocytes appear on a smear taken seven days later. That “late” picture is what turned out not just to be septic shock gone quiet – it was a capnopathologic fulminant thrombotic microangiopathy driven by uncontrolled alternative‑pathway activation downstream of *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*. In essence this simple animal bite turned into an orchestrated cascade where immune dysregulation trumped even profound sepsis education most clinicians would anticipate.”

## Case Snapshot
