# From DIC to MAHA – A Dynamic Clinical Trajectory in Sepsis‑Triggered TMA  

---

## TL;DR  
A woman who slipped into **septic shock** after a dog bite developed **DIC** and AKI that looked like overt sepsis—but when her **anemia dropped sharply** and **platelets never fully recovered**, she hit an unsuspected climax: **CM‑HUS** triggered by *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*.  
Key take‑away:  
* Keep hunting for evolving hemolysis **even when septic markers improve**  
* Daily CBC smear + haptoglobin checks turn out cheaper than many fancy algorithms  
* When doubt arises, prepare plasma exchange quickly while awaiting definitive diagnostics (*ADAMTS13*, kidney biopsy)  
* Initiate eculizumab **after infections are cleared** yet before irreversible renal damage ensues  

---

## What Makes This Case Surprising  
Most people think “septic shock → DIC → death” is a linear decline. In real life, patients often pick up a *second act* while their original illness improves—microangiopathic hemolysis that unfolds almost unnoticed because everyone is busy chasing lactate levels and organ perfusion pressures.  

The headline twist here is that our patient’s earliest video‐talk pitched her into massive multisystem failure driven by *Capnocytophaga*, yet almost three weeks into care she developed **isolated cytopenias** against the backdrop of improving inflammatory markers—but still rampant platelets exhaustion—and it turns out she's battling *complement‑mediated hemolytic uremic syndrome*.  

That adds two hoops reluctantly required by every intensivist & nephrologist practising today:
1️⃣ Recognize late‑onset TMAs after apparent bacterial recovery  
2️⃣ Treat productively without stepping on a lethal rabbit hole (severe infections plus terminal‐complement inhibition)

## Why KM4 Sepsis Masks Complement‑Driven Events  

In any severe infection you see:
* Disrupted endothelium  
* Cytokine surge (“smoky knives”) enhancing clotting cascade  
* Elevated fibrinolytic products  

During *acute* stabilization you’ll notice SHARP declines in PT/PTT/APTT followed by normalization—but microscopic “helmet cells” hidden behind routine red cell counts often remain silent until you look hard enough. Consequently remaining thrombocytopenia feels “expected” while an occult Congo‐red lung membrane emerges later.

### Monitoring Checkpoints That Saved Her Renal Function
In any ICU setting where bacterial cultures are pending longer than expected—or cases fueled by fastidious organisms upper airway flora are suspected—you should consider implementing these cheap "rules":

1️⃣ **Daily CBC + Morphologic Review**  
   * Even one minute an eye scan can detect ≥5 % schistocytes—pocket meta‑analysis suggests >=5% strongly predictive of ongoing MAC ↔ CM‑HUS.*  
2️⃣ **Every Other Day Haptoglobin Measurement** (*if nightly labs allow*)***  
   * Drop ⩽30% IV baseline signals intravascular hemolysis (*fast shift <24h*) which precedes ↓Hb by several days.*   
3️⃣ **Serial LDH/Bilirubin Profiles Around Day 6–8 After Stabilization**  
   * These quantify ongoing endothelial breakdown.*  

When your prohepatitic picture clears while nicking red cells remains continuous—that's your “red flag.” Get PLASMIC score instantly if you have digital algorithm ready—and work towards ADAMTS13 early!

## Differential Diagnosis Roadmap When You See Schistocytes Later On  

| Potential Etiology | First Lines Of Testing / Key Features |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)** | Low ADAMTS13 (<10%) + <10 hr rise LDH + high platelet consumption ‑ did quickly yes/no test integrate into ED workflow |
| **Shiga toxin–produced HUS** | Recent diarrheal prodrome + stool PCR negative unequivocal withdrawal footnote |
| **Complement‐Mediated HUS** (CM-HUS)/Atypical HUS | Normal or low-normal C3/C4 ok ➜ re-evaluate functional alternative pathway (AH50); normal factor B/H/I skip BCAP inhibitors ✅|
| **Drug–induced Antiphospholipid syndrome** or other drug TMAs ✔ Test ANA/ENA panels╫ comparably reassess promptly |

The point isn’t you get all tests perfect —the diagnostic journey pragmatically rules them out stepwise hits towards CM-HUS after negative results confirm high reliability defaults.

## Management Decision Framework in Practice 

### Step #1 — Wash Out Infection 
Initiating eculizumab during active Gram‑negative bacteremia risks catastrophic necrotizing infections recalcitrant beyond routine coverage. My bedside mantra was “clear cultures first.” In this instance cultures turned negative long enough that I waited two consecutive “culture cleared” orders before giving first dose ‑ adding meningococcal coverage immediately afterwards ! 

### Step #2 — Plasma Exchange Bridging 
If suspecting any form of acute Microangiopathy you do NOT delay! Simple four–session logic works best:
- Session 1 bridge while awaiting ADAMTS13 results 
- Continue through sessions 2–3 if NB plateau persistency persists 
Proudly used Octaplas® xenogenic plasma solutions — easier logistics compared representation edge excluded all sIgM overdose concerns.



### Step #3 — Terminal Complement Blockade 
Once infection control confirmed ➜ start standard induction dosing 
(900 mg IV weekly ×4 weeks). My follow‐up loop includes:
