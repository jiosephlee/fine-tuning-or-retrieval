Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

Chapter 2: ## 2 Initial Presentation & Emergency Department Evaluation  

The index event began abruptly when the patient was bitten on the upper lip by her domestic dog (“day‑0”). Although she printed a tetanus booster immediately thereafter, she did not begin a written course of phenoxymethylpenicillin because she could not obtain a prescription on the same day—a missing element that set the stage for unchecked bacterial growth over the next few days. By “day‑4,” she developed an explosive fever (>40 °C), profound malaise and oliguria complicated further by a sudden fall in capillary glucose levels (<0.6 mmol L⁻¹). The primary care setting attempted aggressive oral glucose replacement followed by subcutaneous glucagon injections without success, prompting urgent transfer straight to our tertiary emergency department despite the absence of obvious focal infections on physical examination beyond localized erythema around the bite site and mottling of both lower extremities.

### Vital Signs & Quick Sepsis Screening  
On arrival she was conscious but tachypneic (RR ≈ 28 breaths min⁻¹) with a systolic blood pressure just above the Surviving Sepsis Campaign threshold (>90 mm Hg). Her heart rate was moderately elevated (~110 bpm). Mental status remained intact i.e., no confusion was noted. Using these three parameters—a systolic arterial pressure ≤100 mm Hg ‑ *absent*, heart rate ≥90 bpm ‑ *present*, respiratory rate ≥22 breaths per minute ‑ **present**, altered mentation ‑ *absent*—the quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (qS­OFA) score calculated as **1/3**, signalling a modest likelihood of organ dysfunction secondary to infection while highlighting tachypnea as a prominent marker in this patient’s presentation.(1) This prompted activation of an aggressive “sepsis bundle” pathway even though classic SIRS criteria were only partially met given her preserved mental status and borderline hypertension.

### Complete Blood Count  
A CBC performed immediately suggested a paradoxical hematologic picture consistent with overwhelming infection rather than inherent marrow pathology:
| Parameter | Value | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Total WBC | **3·5×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹** | Leukopenia |
| Neutrophils | **~65 %** | Absolute ANC ≈ 2·3×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹ – *neutrophilic predominance* |
| Lymphocytes | ↓ | Lymphocytopenia associated with sepsis |
| Platelets | **80×10⁹ cells mL⁻¹** | Thrombocytopenia |

The combination denotes consumptive loss secondary to cytokine‑mediated redistribution coupled with platelet consumption characteristic of disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). The reduced leukocyte count mirrors profound systemic inflammation often seen in capnocytophagosis where capnodysin induces cellular lysis.(2)

### Coagulation Profile – Evidence for DIC  
Heparin‑free assays revealed a classic ISTH DIC phenotype:
* Prothrombin time (PT) = prolonged by +7 seconds  
* Activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) = prolonged (+32 seconds)  
* Fibrinogen = low at **0·8 g·dL⁻¹**  
* D‑dimer = markedly elevated (**>5 µg·mL⁻¹**)  

Additional data showed mild thrombocytopenia together with clinical findings of petechiae–ecchymoses pending full diagnostic confirmation (Fig. A). Requiring only timely bedside testing—crucial because organ failure often precedes overt laboratory relapse—these findings fulfilled two ISTH red‑flag arguments (“fibrin degradation products exceeding ten times upper reference level” and “low fibrinogen”), thereby satisfying partial criteria for overt DIC formation secondary to bacterial endotoxin activation.(3)

### Renal Function – Early AKI Markers  
Baseline serum creatinine was unknown clinically until she had been admitted previously without any history suggestive of chronic kidney impairment—presumably around **90 µmol·L⁻¹** based on outpatient data—but upon presentation it had risen sharply:
| Kidney Parameter | Baseline / Admission |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Serum creatinine | **≈ 175 µmol·L⁻¹** |
| Blood urea nitrogen | **≈ 65 mg·dL⁻¹** |
| Urine output | Initially oliguria → anuria reported within hours |

The abrupt increase masked both prerenal contributions driven by systemic vasodilatory shock (+ evaluated lactate peak ≈ 5·7 mmol·L⁻¹) and intrarenal microvascular thrombi causing acute tubular necrosis—a hallmark sequelae waiting confirmation spectra during longer observation.(4)

### Imaging Studies – Rule Out Necrotising Fasciitis / Vascular Catastrophe  
Given mottled skin pigmentation coupled with pain disproportionate to swelling—a classic red flag for rapidly evolving soft‑tissue infection—the team pursued urgent contrast‑enhanced computed tomography (CT)/fascia surface imaging over both lower limbs.
Images demonstrated:
* Normal fascial plane integrity without gas collection  
* No evidence of deep venous thrombosis or arterial occlusion  
* Thoracic/metabolic abscesses absent  

Therefore necrotising fasciitis was highly unlikely even though Global Pathology suggests any skin compromise following a dog bite warrants exploration.(5)

Similarly, routine brain MRI/angled CT angiography denied intracranial hemorrhage or ischemic thrombotic episodes—an insight critical because septic emboli would alter management strategies dramatically via additional antibiotics/supportive monitoring tailored toward Aspergillus–strores destructive bacteria like *C.* *capnocytophaga*.  

### Empirical Antimicrobial Therapy – Targeting Dog‑Bite Flora  
Recognizing that canine oral microbiota is dominated by *Pasteurella multocida*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*, Moraxella spp., Anaerobe groups {Flavobacterium/Fusobacterium} and fastidious *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*, clinicians preferred empiric broad‐spectrum coverage encompassing Gram‑positive cocci/extensively drug‑susceptible isolates alongside Gram‐negative rods capable otherwise manifesting remarkable resistance patterns.
Accordingly triple dosing piperacillin/tazobactam commenced within **<60 min** post–arrival (120 mg tazobactam per gram piperacillin intravenously every six hours), offering robust activity against β‑lactamase–producing organisms while retaining efficacy against slow growers such as *Capnocytophaga*. Empiric intravenous access provided route comfort throughout close monitoring until definitive bacteremia culture results materialised seven days later revealing true causative pathogen (*C.* *canimorsus*).

This vancomycin‐avoidance strategy minimized risk without compromising efficacy given bacteremia surfaced beyond tetanus vaccination’s protective window where protective host antibodies were absent.(6)

---

In summary, the patient's immediate evaluation highlighted four cardinal checkpoints common for any severe zoonotic infection:
1.) Rapid assessment via qS­OFA underlines organ compromise even when classic SIRS is equivocal;  
2.) Picture-perfect CBC‐derived evidence foreshadows septic consumption deficits;
3.) Established ISTH parameters unmistakably signal emerging DIC responsible for subsequent multiorgan insult;
4.) Prompt bedside imaging secures exclusion of catastrophic soft tissue dysfunction;
5.) All elements informed timely transition into critical care while empirically targeting notorious canine pathogens—including *C.* *canimorsus*.
