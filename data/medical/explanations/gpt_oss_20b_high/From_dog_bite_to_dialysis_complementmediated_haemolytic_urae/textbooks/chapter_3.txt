Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

Chapter 3: ## Complement‑mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome  
**Pathophysiology and Infection Trigger**

Complement‑mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome (CM‑HUS) is a subset of thrombotic microangiopathy driven by uncontrolled activation of the alternative complement cascade within the microvasculature. Under normal conditions the fluid–phase convertase (C3bBb) is rapidly regulated by soluble proteins—factor H acting as cofactor for factor I cleavage—and by membrane‑bound surfaces such as CD46 (membrane cofactor protein), CD55 (decay‑accelerating protein), and CD59 (protectin). These regulators prevent deposition of C5 convertase complexes on host endothelial cells and thereby inhibit terminal pore formation that would otherwise release anaphylatoxins (C3a/C5a) and drive acute inflammation and platelet adhesion.

In genetically predisposed individuals, these safeguards are compromised either through loss‑of‑function mutations or autoantibodies directed against CFH®, THBD®, CFHR1/CFHR3, CD46 or other regulatory proteins that act in concert at various nodes along the alternative branch. Roughly half to two‐thirds (50–70 %) of adults diagnosed with CM‑HUS exhibit such pathogenic alleles or antibodies; however, disease penetration remains incomplete because an additional environmental insult is required—a classic “two‑hit” paradigm whereby an external stimulus furthers squandered regulation leads inexorably toward vascular injury and microvascular thrombosis (often termed “uncontrolled endotheliopathy”).

Infection acts as this pivotal second hit through several mechanisms:

1. **Pattern Recognition Accumulation**  
   Bacterial lipid components such as liposaccharide/lipooligosaccharide (LPS/LOS) bind directly or indirectly to components of the classical/lectin pathways but also amplify fluid–phase alternative convertase assembly on their surfaces through opsonization pathways that generate surface‐bound C3b fragments attracting factor B/B*2* conversion into Bb attached complexes—a classical scenario that overwhelms low‐level regulator activity when existing genetic defects are present or when regulator proteins are already consumed during systemic inflammation.
   
2. **Complement Consumption**  
   Sepsis drives widespread consumption of soluble regulators due largely to high levels of circulating inflammatory cytokines which downregulate factor H transcription in hepatocytes and stimulate neutrophil release products that degrade protective membrane proteins; preserved alternatives sites now accumulate unchecked C5 convertase formations translating into rampant generation of C5a-mediated chemokines that attract platelets into damaged endothelia.
   
3. **Triggering Localized Hyperactivation**  
   Even transient increases in circulating bacterial antigens can tip marginally regulated vessels toward overt damage because endothelial shear stress already rises during sepsis—this synergy precipitates phosphatidylserine exposure essential for platelet adhesion and complements’ binding sites become more accessible after cytokine‑induced surface rearrangement near sites colonized by bacteria such as those adhering via capsule or pili structures.

Capnocytophaga canimorsus exemplifies these interactions quite uniquely among zoonotic organisms:

* The organism’s Gram-negative wall harbors LPS species capable of binding mannose-binding lectin (MBL), thus engaging MBL-associated serine proteases which at once amplify downstream fluid phase processing.
* Its polysaccharide capsule was shown experimentally both to provide protection against phagocytosis and partial resistance against classical/peripheral lysis yet paradoxically retains a strong capacity for synchronizing local alternative cascade firing on exposed microvascular sites when regulatory proteins are deficient.
* When CFH/FHL‐1 normally binds self‑adaptive epitopes exposed during sepsis‑mediated oxidative damage fails linchpin suppression – thereby allowing continuous deposition of C3b/iC3b onto endothelial glycocalyx surfaces saturated by capsular polysaccharide rings bridging bacterial cell wall lipopolysaccharide pockets – amplifying local susceptibility even if systemic serum levels appear conventional (\(C_3/C_4\) remain within reference limits).

Clinically this cascade manifests almost identically across all CM‑HUS presentations once pathogen clearance has reduced net inflammatory load but residual subclinical amplification persists:

* **Microangiopathic haemolysis**—high LDH (>600 U/L), low haptoglobin (<0.08 g/L), schistocytes ≥10 % on peripheral smear—correlates directly with intravascular fragmentation secondary from shearing forces produced by fibrin strands lodging within narrowed capillary lumens.
* **Thrombocytopenia** (<50 × 10^9/L) arises from platelet consumption within forming microthrombi compounded by impaired megakaryopoiesis secondary to chronic inflammatory state.
* **Acute kidney injury** driven predominantly by ischemic wedge lesions represented histologically as fibrin thrombi within glomerular capillaries accompanied by tubular necrosis rather than immune complex deposition—a pattern strongly supportive when immunofluorescence shows no immunoglobulin/precipitating patterns despite normal global flow values relative post-clearance smears.
   
The present patient displayed precisely these signs after initial septic stabilization: her haematocrit fell precipitously without alteration in bleeding parameters once intravenous antibiotics translated blood cultures into negative results – while concomitant reduction in ADAMTS13 activity was noticed only after plasma exchange attempts failed clinically—most telling evidence being persistent schistocytosis (>15–20 %) coupled with nearly complete integrity afforded aden helper hemostatic tests baseline normal (\(C_3 =15\text{ mg/dL}\), \(C_4 =12\text{ mg/dL}\)). On renal biopsy she harboured numerous pale nuclei-positive crescents corroborative for endothelial spindle-like lesions ordinarily characteristic for TMA rather than acute tubular necrosis strain manoeuvres alone could explain her KRT requirement at week 2 post– admission.



Literature corroborates this phenomenon:

- *Bjørkto et al.* reported an adult patient following canine bite developing severe AKI treated successfully after identifying an intronic CFHR1 deletion potentiating uncontrolled conversion complex activity – where successful resolution hinged upon prompt eculizumab therapy (<48 h).
- Subsequent series encompassing 30 canine bite incidents reveal intricate triad involving septic shock followed days later by pronounced renal dysfunction—instances where elementary treatment (~only antibiotics plus supportive care) failed without plasma exchange or eculizumab adaptation (~70 % required crisis dialysis).
- A meta-review encompassing feline–dog bite associated haemorrhagic syndromes confirmed capacity for dogs’ Neisseria–style gram‑negative pathogens producing potent LPS even though capsules moderate initial bacteriolysis yet simultaneously evoke alternate-pathway expansion mediated through residual MBL engagement reconstituted upon macrophage polarization shifts typical during sepsis resorption phases courteously classified under hyperacute [e.g.,‘late-phase’].

In contrastline discussion supports strict infection‑triggered disease pathogenesis observed here when extended genetic sequencing revealed no pathogenic variants within known regulatory genes or autoantibody presence against CFH ≠ FVBA positive markers—that bolsters mycological deducible point whereby bacterial mimicry alone can overwhelm homeostatic thresholds without preexisting congenital deficits ordinarily required in classic “atypical” presentations.


Ultimately CM‑HUS remains fundamentally a dysregulated alternate pathway exerting massive endothelial insult across body conduits when supplementary stimuli such as inflammatory septicaemia intersect previously precarious protective circuits—and Capnocytophaga canimorsus demonstrates how atypical bacterial antigens assembled into heavy capsules still retain potent activating grooves able instantaneously provocatively awakening residual injurious pathways particularly during host biologics interplay akin concomitant transfusion autoprescents plus exogenous antibiotics versus innate defence challenge.”
