Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### ["Differential Diagnosis"] "How does an isolated drop in hemoglobin coupled with persistent thrombocytopenia help differentiate Capnocytophaga-induced atypical HUS from Shiga-toxin-associated HUS?

**Key teaching point:**  
In a patient who has recently recovered from severe sepsis/ DIC and then develops a *sudden* fall in haemoglobin while both platelet counts remain low (“persistent thrombocytopenia”) this laboratory picture should make you suspect **infection‑triggered complement‑mediated thrombotic microangiopathy (CM‑HUS)** rather than **Shiga‑toxin–associated HUS (STEC‑HUS).**  

**Why this pattern favors CM‑HUS**

| Feature | CAPNOCYTOPHAGA‑Induced CM‑HUS | STEC‑Associated HUS |
|---------|---------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Antecedent illness** | Bacterial sepsis (dog bite → Capnocytophaga canimorsus) | Gastroenteritis → bloody diarrhoea 48–72 h before onset |
| **Timing of anaemia** | Often *delayed*, appears once systemic inflammation subsides (as seen 6 days after improvement) | Usually present at initial TMA presentation |
| **Platelet behaviour** | Persistent consumption → platelets stay markedly depressed despite blood transfusion | Platelet count falls rapidly at onset but may recover sooner once haemostasis improves |
| **Evidence of ongoing haematological injury** | Isolated haemoglobin drop + rising LDH + undetectable haptoglobin + schistocytes only after septic phase clears → supports *new* intravascular haemolysis driven by endothelial damage | Anaemia co‑occurs immediately with diarrhoea/diarrhoeal prodrome |
| **Diagnostic work‑up needed** | Negative stool PCR for Shiga toxin ❌; normal ADAMTS13 activity (>10 %); normal ANA/ANCA → rule out TTP/diseases unrelated to shiga toxin | Positive Shiga toxin PCR or FITC‑ELISA definitive |
| **Clinical implications** | Prompt recognition drives early eculizumab/high‑dose steroids + plasma exchange ± antibiotics → renal recovery | Avoid unnecessary complement blockade; manage supportive care unless secondary infection arises |

**Case evidence**

* The patient improved clinically from septic shock and DIC but then suffered a rapid haemoglobin fall (111 → 68 g/L over 6 days) without evidence of bleeding—a hallmark sign that new intravascular haemolysis had begun.
* Platelet count remained persistently low (30–40 ×10⁹/L), indicating continuous platelet consumption typical of a microangiopathic process driven by endothelial injury.
* Direct antiglobulin test turned negative on repeat testing, arguing against autoimmune haemolytic anaemia.
* Peripheral smear showed 15–20 % schistocytes only after this haemolytic episode began.
* ADAMTS13 activity was 43 % → not consistent with TTP.
* Stool PCR for Shiga toxin was negative → Rules out STEC‐HUS.
* Kidney biopsy revealed ischemic glomeruli with fibrin thrombi—all consistent with TMA caused by uncontrolled alternative pathway activation seen in CM‑HUS.

Thus, **the combination of a late-onset isolated anaemia following resolution of septic inflammation plus ongoing thrombocytopenia—in the absence of diarrhoeal prodrome or shiga toxin positivity—points toward Capnocytophaga‐triggered complement‑mediated HUS over Shiga‐toxin–associated HUS.** Recognizing this pattern allows initiation of appropriate therapy (plasma exchange followed by eculizumab) at a critical point where renal recovery is still feasible.
