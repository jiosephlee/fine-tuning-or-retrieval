Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### ["Morning Report"] "In a septic patient presenting AKI, low platelets, schistocytes on smear and falling hemoglobin despite resolving coagulopathy—what immediate laboratory studies would you order upon admission?

**Immediate diagnostic algorithm for a septic patient who develops new‐onset AKI with thrombocytopenia, net falling hemoglobin and schistocytosis**

1. **Full CBC + peripheral blood smear**  
   *Reticulocyte count* – distinguishes recent hemoglobin loss from low production.  
   *Schistocyte quantification* – confirms microangiopathic hemolysis in real time (≥1–2 %).  

2. **Hemolysis panel** (performed serially every 12–24 h)  
   • **LDH** – ↑ >2× upper limit indicates intravascular hemolysis  
   • **Indirect bilirubin** – ↑ bilirubin accompanies cell lysis  
   • **Haptoglobin** – ≥3‑fold decrease is diagnostic of ongoing MAHA  
   These values rapidly gauge severity (as seen when haptoglobin became undetectable in our patient).

3. **Coagulation screen**  
   • PT/INR, aPTT  
   • Fibrinogen & D‑dimer  
   Normalization of these parameters differentiates resolving DIC from persistent consumptive coagulopathy associated with TMA—exactly what our patient experienced when coagulopathy resolved yet hemolysis continued.

4. **ADAMTS13 activity** (turn‑around ≤24 h)  
   *Rationale*: Severe deficiency (<10 %) unequivocally diagnoses thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), which requires urgent plasma exchange regardless of other data—our patient’s 43 % activity ruled out TTP promptly before initiating plasma exchange as bridge therapy.\(^1\)

5. **Complement assessment** – screening for complement‑mediated HUS (CM‑HUS)  
   • Serum C3 & C4 levels – normal values do **not** exclude CM‑HUS but help document baseline complement status.\(^2\)  
   • Functional assay of total complement activity (CH50) *or* alternative pathway activity (AH50). A markedly reduced AH50 during active disease supports alternative‑pathway activation as seen when our patient’s AH50 fell during treatment.\(^3\)

6. **Stool PCR for Shiga toxin** – mandatory exclusion of STEC‐HUS even in adults without prodromal diarrhea.\(^4\)

7. **Direct antiglobulin test (DAT)** – IgG or IgM positive assays can mimic MAHA due to immune‐mediated hemolysis common in transfusion or antibiotic exposure.\(^5\)

8. **Antibiotic–sensitive blood cultures + Gram stain** – identify infective trigger (“Capnocytophaga canimorsus” in our index case) that may precipitate CM‑HUS or trigger other systemic effects.

These investigations provide an evidence‐based roadmap:

* If ADAMTS13 <10 %, start plasma exchange without delay (*e.g.*, our patient received PEX once the low activity was confirmed).  
* Normal ADAMTS13 activity plus persistent schistocytosis and rising LDH directs attention toward alternative complement activation → prompt completion of the panel above → consultation of nephrology/hematology → consider eculizumab if LA remains unchecked.\(^6\)

Early execution of this diagnostic sequence—ideally within the first 24 h in an ICU setting—prevents unnecessary delays between recognition of microangiopathic injury and definitive intervention with complement blockade or plasma exchange when appropriate​.


---

> **Key take‐away:** In sepsis evolving into AKI with unexplained anemia & thrombocytopenia, “triage by lab” begins with an urgent *ADAMTS13* level alongside standard coagulation panels and a basic complement screen; these tests permit life‑saving decisions between plasma exchange for TTP versus rapid initiation of terminal complement inhibition for CM‑HUS triggered by infection—as demonstrated by this Capnocytophaga case.\(^1–^6\)
