Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### ["Clinical Pearls"] "Penicillin exposure can yield hostile direct antiglobulin positivity while mechanical hemolysis shows schistocytes—how would peripheral smear morphology combined other labs distinguish warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia from microangiopathic hemolysis?

**How a peripheral‑smear morphology‐plus‑lab panel separates warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (WAIHA) from microangiopathic haemolysis**

| Feature | Warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia | Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (TMA / CM‑HUS) |
|--------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| **Peripheral‑smear** | Predominantly *spherocytes* or mildly altered red cells; **≤ 1 % schistocytes** | **> 5 % (> 15 % abnormal cases)** *schistocytes*—fine angular fragments (“helmet”‐shaped), bite/cerebral crescents |
| **Platelet count** | Usually normal or only mildly reduced unless underlying SLE/Sjögren | Markedly reduced (< 150×10⁹/L); often < 50×10⁹/L |
| **Direct antiglobulin test (DAT)** | Positive for *IgG* (and often anti‑IgM) – true antibody coating | Negative when no autoantibody present – drug‑induced *false‑positive* “hostile” results seen with peniccillins (as in this case where Apocillin triggered an early IgG+ assay that subsequently became negative once the drug was cleared) |
| **Reticulocyte response** | Sharp increase reflecting marrow compensation (“high retic”) | Can be blunted by concurrent marrow suppression early in sepsis or CKD |
| **Complement consumption** | Low C3/C4 only if anti‑C3 cold AGs present | Often normal early in CM‑HUS but functional alternative pathway activity may be reduced (seen here after plasma exchange) |
| **ADAMTS13 activity** | Not required – values usually > 10 % | Low (< 10 %) suggests TTP (excluded here with 43 %) |
| **Clinical context** | Usually painless splenomegaly or underlying autoimmune disease | Thrombosis/parenchymal injury—in our patient AKI + oliguria + skin ischemia |

### Why morphology trumps a single test

1. **Penicillin‑associated “hostile” DATs**  
   Many β‑lactams create transient drug‐dependent antibodies that bind only when the drug is present on the red‑cell surface. A mere pulse or poor compliance can therefore yield a *positive IgG‐DAT* even when no pathological autoantibody exists. In our patient, a repeat Coombs test after clarifying medication exposure was negative—clear evidence that the initial finding was a drug artifact rather than true WAIHA.

2. **Schistocyte burden**  
   A quantitative surrogacy for intravascular crushing is straightforward: ≥15 % schystocytes almost always signifies a microangiopathic process such as DIC—infections like Capnocytophaga can ignite complement activation leading to endothelial injury—and ruled out pure immune haemolysis which never generates substantial fragmentation.

3. **Platelet involvement**  
   The platelet fall seen here (30–40×10⁹/L) points toward consumptive coagulopathy rather than isolated autoimmune destruction of red cells which typically spares platelets unless concomitant disorders exist.

4. **Complement & ADAMTS13 workup**  
   A normal-to-mildly reduced C3/C4 does not exclude complement‐mediated HUS because acute consumption may be transient or masked by ongoing therapy—all this was confirmed by an alternative pathway assay showing very low activity post‑PEX and eculizumab initiation. Conversely, an ADAMTS13 level at 43 %, well above the >10 % threshold needed for non‑TTP diagnosis, argues against thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura while supporting CM‑HUS after ruling out Shiga toxin source.

### Practical pearls

1. **Always combine smear analysis with serial retics and platelet counts before initiating steroids**—an unnecessary pulse dose increases infection risk without benefit when the culprit is not autoantibody driven.
2. **If a patient presents after recent β‑lactam therapy**, repeat the DAT after clearance or use a drug neutralization protocol before trusting any antibody–positive result.
3. **Quantify schists precisely:** Count ≥50 blasts/macroscopic fields using a wet fixed slide under oil immersion; ≥5 % typically mandates evaluation for MAHA/TMI regardless of other findings.
4. **Prioritize early ADAMTS13 and complement testing:** A rapid assay will steer you away from unnecessary plasma exchange if activity is high enough (>10 %) while confirming complement blockade targets when below shutdown point (>90 %).

In summary, this case illustrates how emergent “hostile” DAT positivity triggered by penicillin exposure could easily misdirect management toward corticosteroid therapy if unverified against smears showing overwhelming schistosclae and thrombocytopenia indicative of CM‑HUS driven by Capnocytophaga infection—a diagnosis correctly confirmed only after integrating morphology with comprehensive laboratory profiling.(NEJM Clinical Pearls style)
