Title: Blog about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

# Cervical Oedema That Is Not an Infection  

## A Little‑Known Giant in the Neck

When a young man presents to the ED with a three‑week history of progressive neck swelling, you imagine Ludwig’s angina or a soft‑tissue infection first. In our community hospital we raised antibiotics and CT dials expectedly. The twist wasn't an abscess or malignancy – it was dermatomyositis masquerading as a pseudo‑angioedema. This case shows that sometimes the “simple” neck flare hides a diffuse autoimmune catastrophe that can threaten the airway before you even get an infectious screen back.

---

## The First Look – What Was Unusual?

* **Persistently swollen neck** – striking diffuse edema of strap muscles extending into retropharyngeal space.  
* **No systemic infection** – afebrile, saturations normal, WCC only mildly elevated (9 × 10^9 L).  
* **Proximal weakness** – hip flexor power downgraded to 4/5 within days of presentation.  
* **Subtle cutaneous clues** – periungual erythema on capillaroscopy, mild facial erythema over nasolabial folds, but no classic Gottron’s papules or heliotrope rash.  
* **Laboratory surprise** – CK catapulted to **16 184 U/L**, far exceeding any inflammatory‐mediated angioedema expectation.

The last item made us toss the “angioedema” hypothesis out the window sooner than usual. In practice we tend to wait for labs before contemplating steroids; here we had a biologic alarm and had to act on it fast.

---

## Differential Diagnosis – The Classic Wall‑Off

| Pt’s history | Initial suspects |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Neck swelling + aspiration risk | Ludwig’s angina / deep‐neck space infection |
| No palpable mass or abscess | Microbial cellulitis or sialadenitis |
| Pruritic facial rash | Angiotensin‑converting enzyme inhibitor excess |
| Elevated temperature/CRP | Bacterial meningitis/thyroid storm |

The skin sign that slipped past many ENT surgeons was a nailfold microhaemorrhage pattern typical of connective‐tissue disease—rarely considered when you’re focussing on pharyngeal flora. On top of that we had a **positive ANA** (1:1280 speckled) and a myositis panel murmuring “SAE‑1”—the 'SMARTE' indicator you'd expect in an autoimmune myopathy bull shaking your ENT colleagues into silence.

---

## Work‑Up Snapshot

* **CT Neck (contrast)** – diffuse soft‑tissue edema without discrete abscesses → ruled out bacterial focus on day one.
* **Serum CK** – >10× ULN → strongly argues against purely histamine‐driven angioedema.
* **Myositis antibody panel** – EVP® Euroline test → high intensity anti‑SAE1 titre (**173 > 35**).
* **MRI Thigh + PET** – widespread peripheral muscle uptake plus active fascia infiltration consistent with dermatomyositis rashless subtype.
* **Muscle biopsy** – peri‑fascicular necrosis + C5b–9 deposition → classic dermatomyopathy blue‑print.
* **Complement levels / C1 esterase inhibitor** – normal → excludes acquired HAE.
* **Chest CT + PFTs** – mid‑lower zone nodules yet no fibrosis at baseline; mild restrictive pattern likely nonspecific initially but followed up serially.
 
A single lab value hammered home the diagnosis—high CK in the context of localized soft tissue edema is akin to reading “myositis” instead of “infection” from an ultrasound report gunshot wound.

---

## Management Course – Rapid Steroids First

In this scenario airway compromise risks climb steeply within hours because neck fibrosis can encroach on laryngeal structures and dysphagia will worsen rapidly.

1. **IV methylprednisolone 1000 mg daily × 3 days** for immediate anti‑inflammatory effect & swollen tissue reversibility.  
2. Transition to oral prednisone taper starting at 60 mg daily – reducing by 5 mg every 2–3 weeks based on CK & symptom trends.  
3. Send off IVIG cycle (2 g/kg over five days) followed by maintenance every four weeks as adjunct rescue therapy.  
4. Initiate **mycophenolate mofetil 1 g BID** as steroid‐sparing agent early due to LT infiltration risk with methotrexate (IL–D lung function abnormal).  
5. Administer two rituximab infusions (1000 mg IV) two weeks apart as we tend toward sustained remission—especially useful after steroid taper decreases breakthrough symptoms.  
6. Allergy risk control – stop TMP/SMX prophylaxis due to hemolytic anaemia; switch to nebulised pentamidine quarterly instead of oral PCP prophylaxis because baseline glucocorticoids persist longer than anticipated.

> 💡 A quick high‑dose steroid pulse can buy time for diagnostics because delayed treatment in dermatomyositis—particularly with airway involvement—surpasses what any antibiotic ever could save you from a “Ludwig’s” emergency collapse.

---

## Outcome – Airway First, Then Muscle Recovery Sequentially

By day 21 postpartum:

* Neck edema shrank markedly though still not completely resolved at discharge.
* Hip flexor strength partially improved; dysphagia warranted NG tube feeding for another two months.
* Creatine kinase fell from >16k U/L to almost normal (**37** U/L) after three weeks of therapy irrespective of taper speed.
* Within five months: full resolution of cervical edema, uncomplicated swallow function inferred by nasogastric removal before six months.
* Longitudinal pulmonary CT at month 11 showed near normalization—likely attributable both to therapy and natural course rather than active ILD progression.

While he survived this critical window without mechanical ventilation or lateral pharyngoesophageal collapse documented on laryngoscopy—a heartbreaking tool not used—we relied on serial clinical assessment and repeat imaging taken wisdom by clinicians rather than invasive tests whenever possible.

---

## Clinical Pearls & Take‑Home Messages

- **Dermatomyositis can present as painless neck pseudoburst**, especially when accompanied by proximal weakness—even if skin rash is minimal or absent.
- A markedly elevated CK (>10× ULN) in any patient presenting with non‑exudative edema should redirect your bedside suspicion away from classic infections toward inflammatory myopathy.
- Subtle nailfold changes are often ignored but can provide crucial clues when cutaneous hallmark features are not yet manifest.
- When airway involvement is possible (dysphagia or retropharyngeal extension), initiate a high‑dose IV steroid trial before awaiting full immunologic workup—it’s more life-saving than sitting around for lab results.
- Keep in mind that anti‑SAE1 positivity associates with dysphagia and pulmonary infiltrates; hence simultaneous thoracic imaging is essential even if chest X-ray looks clean at first glance.
- Even when initial ENT evaluation uncovers no obvious pathology on CT or flexible nasendoscopy, dexamethasone may still hold the key if autoimmunity is suspected—it can quell inflammatory edema that otherwise would thicken tissue thickness overnight.
- In patients under twenty–five years old? Not relevant here but pertinent for younger ages as this condition also pops up sporadically—they flourish differently long term.

---

## Bottom Line?

Don’t let the words “swelling” hide fibrosis behind an autoimmune veil! An unexpected triad of neck edema + proximal weakness + huge CK load arouses suspicion for dermatomyositis masquerading as pseudo‑angioedema or Ludwig’s angina—a scenario amenable only to prompt immunomodulation rather than antibiotics alone. Keep your differential broad enough that you’ll start steroids early while still chasing pitfalls like true infection—and watch your airway—all while gearing up for an aggressive but survivable rheumatologic disease course.”
