### A startling swap between “angioedema” and immune‑mediated inflammation  

Picture this: a healthy man in his forties walks into an emergency department clutching his jaw because his neck feels “puffy.” He has no fever, his pulse is normal, he denies allergies or medication changes—and yet the picture on his health record resembles Ludwig’s angina more than a soft‑tissue allergic flare – a swelling that sits deeply among his strap muscles and climbs into the retropharyngeal space (see Fig ​1). A quick facial exam spots subtle peri‑ungual erythema but nothing else that screams lupus or arthritis—yet the creatinine kinase turns out sky‑high at 16 184 U/L overnight. As you read through the case report I’ll weave through how every imaging system—from instant contrast CT through the story‑telling plots of MRI and finally whole‑body PET—guided us past confusion toward a dramatic treatment plan based on anti‑small ubiquitin‑like modifier activating enzyme‐102 antibody–positive dermatomyositis (SAE1+ DM).

### Why multimodal imaging clicks better than single‐shots  

When faced with **cervical swelling without an obvious infectious nidus**, clinicians often rely first on **CT** because it’s fast enough at an emergency desk and readily available when ward staff are racing against three days’ worth of walkaway airways door closures. But while CT tells us *where* fluid sits—or what doesn’t exist—it rarely spells out *why* that fluid lives there unless you’re armed with question cues along your learning curve (i.e., absence of rim enhancement argues against abscess certainty while diffuse low attenuation hints towards inflammatory edema).

Once there’s doubt leveled against infection or histaminergic causes (the patient was afebrile, CRP was modest rather than astronomical), elective referral for **magnetic resonance imaging** becomes purposefully targeted toward skeletal musculature audit alike a “soft tissue nutcracker”. ### Key Takeaways (Clinical Pearls)

| Brief Clinical Pearls And Decision Triggers |
| :---|
| • When cervical soft-tissue edema is persistent without fever or classic signs of infection think dermatomyxis beyond standard allergic/bradykininades dupletotenoiche — order CK promptly alongside imaging immediately |
| • Contrast-enhanced CT efficiently rules out abscess or vascular lesion — look for diffuse strap-muscle thickening without rim-enhancement |
| • MRI STIR sequences are detective gold standard for skeletal-muscle inflammation – look cues:** perifascicularly hyperintense signal** |
| • Whole-body FDG-PET offers dual benefit ‑ mapping active inflammation across systemic musculature AND serving oncologic staging especially when autoantibodies put patient in medium-high cancer risk |
| • Multimodal radiologic assessment streamlines biopsy site selection toward most inflamed areas → better diagnostic yield |
