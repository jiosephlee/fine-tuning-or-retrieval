Title: Blog about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

# Blending Pulse Steroids With Biologics For Severe Dermatomyositis

This one starts classic‑looking in the emergency department – a man in his forties comes in with painful cervical swelling best described as “Ludwig’s angina”. What follows is a tongue‑in‑cheek reminder that **neck edema can be the herald of dermatomyositis** rather than an infectious deep space abscess or idiopathic angioedema. When the picture is atypical—no fever, no frank purulence, an otherwise well patient—the head‐to‑toe skin check becomes the bedside strate­gy that pivots management entirely toward aggressive medical therapy instead of surgical drainage or antihistamines alone.



## What Makes This Case Interesting

- **Angioedema masquerade** – Subcutaneous edema confined largely to the neck mimicked anaphylaxis or bradykinin‑mediated angioedema despite normal C1 esterase activity and normal complement fractions.
- **Neck swelling caused by muscle inflammation** – Pathological work‑up revealed diffuse myofascial inflammation that produced “pseudo‑angioedema” rather than vascular leakage.
- **Serum CK spiking >16 000 U/L** – The bread‐and butter laboratory value that tipped off clinicians toward an inflammatory myopathy amid an otherwise misleading ENT work‑up.
- **Anti‑SAE1 positivity** – A relatively uncommon autoantibody associated most consistently with dysphagia and interstitial lung disease within dermatomyositis populations.
- **Aggressive combination therapy** – Pulse methylprednisolone followed by a structured prednisone taper plus intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) *and two rituximab infusions* defeated airway‐relevant disease activity within weeks.



## Clinical Vignette Recap

| Day | What Happened | Key Finding |
|-----|---------------|-------------|
| ED arrival | Progressive neck swelling + proximal hip flexor weakness | Neck CT = diffuse strap muscle edema extending into retropharyngeal space |
| Same day | ENT flexible nasendoscopy | No anatomical abnormality; cough/high secretions noted |
| Next day | Serum labs sent | Creatine kinase ↑ → 16 kU/L (normal <200); axillary CRP elevated |
| Initial interpretation | “Ludwig’s angina” → CTA → “Idiopathic angioedema” | Unexplained neutropenia absent |
| Re‑examined† | Nailfold capillaroscopy = periungual erythema + microblan vascular changes | Erythematous face + rash on lateral thigh |

†The subtle periungual findings are one example of “skin-findings-on-the-fly” clinicians should always keep an eye out for when puzzling musculoskeletal presentations appear.



## Diagnostic Toolbox That Separated Angioedema From Dermatomyositis

| Modality | Result | Why It Matters |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| Serum CK | **16 184 U/L** | Acute skeletal muscle injury index far exceeding what you’d expect with isolated angioedema |
| ANA screen | Speckled pattern titer 1:1280 | Encourages broad autoimmune evaluation |
| Myositis panel | **Anti‑SAE1 strongly positive** (signal 173) • Negative Mi‑2α/TIF1γ/Jo‑1/others | Stronger evidence pointing directly toward dermatomyositis spectrum |
| Muscle biopsy | Perifascicular necrosis + C5b‑9 deposition on endomysial capillaries | Classic histologic fingerprint distinguishing classic inflammatory myopathies |
| MRI thighs & PET scan | Active perifascicular inflammation w/ FDG uptake diffusely across head + neck, deltoids, forearms & pelvis | Confirms loooong deep tissue involvement rather than peripheral subcutaneous edema alone |
| Lung imaging / PFTs | Restrictive pattern w/ multiple non‑cavitating pulmonary nodules & patchy peribronchial consolidation |

Sequence matters – getting serum CK promptly reversed an initial misguided surgical extension on the basis of >70% “deep space infection” probability score.



## Why Anti‑SAE1 Is Emblematic For This Disease Course

Patients harboring anti‐SAE1 autoantibodies often present late‐onset proximal weakness *and*, crucially,
**dysphagia complemented by interstitial lung disease** in nearly half they turn out present:
> *In a semi–real world sample of SAE1 + dermatomyositis,* dysphagia = 46 %, ILD = 43 %.  
> *Malignancy,* though less frequent than other DM autoantibody cohorts (~18 %), mandates extensive screening per IMACS guidelines because SAEs contribute both oncologic signalling pathways and taste‑bottleneck immune dysfunction.*

That’s why our patient underwent:
* PET—to rule out occult adenopathy even though no active metabolically bright node was present,
* Multiple tumour marker panels,
* Baseline thorax CT,
* Gastrointestinal endoscopies planned shortly thereafter.



## Treatment Strategy Map

High intensity treatment focuses on rapid aero‑thymic decompression followed by long-term control without cumulative organ toxicity—all packaged under an interprofessional framework touching rheumatology, immunology and ENT realms.


### A. Pulse Steroids + Taper

> **Day 0–3** IV methylprednisolone  =  1000 mg/day  
> Rationale – Clearance of edematous tissue deposits within hours → airway protection milestones  
> **Day 4** Start oral prednisone ####30–60   mg/day (titrated)
> Every **2–3 weeks**, reduce dose by ~5 mg applying how soon signs resolve / CRP/CK trend
>  
> *Avoid abrupt termination.*

### B. Intravenous Immunoglobulin (`IVIG`)

> Initial loading = 2 g/kg ÷5 days → glomerular support + blockade of pathogenic autoantibodies 
> Follow-up weekly/monthly infusions (~2 g/kg) spaced ~4 weeks apart through **6–7 months** depending on clinical remission status.


### C.`Rituximab`

> Two doses = 1000 mg IV , spaced ≈ 14 days   
> Goal – B cell depletion tempts sustained antibody blockade downtrading long term dose exposures compared some standard csDMARDs.



### D.`Steroid–Sparing Agent`

> **Mycophenolate mofetil**(MMF) ‖ *Choice rational*:  
> * Reduced risk vs methotrexate in presence pft ≥10% restrictive component (+aspiration)*
> * Oral twice-daily dosing ‑ patient preference & comorbidity safety profile 
> 
> Alternatives (*not chosen*) -> Azathioprine/tacrolimus faced intolerability / slower onset  
> Methotrexate avoided upfront simply because it carries potential cumulative pulmonary toxicity especially when baseline ILD is detected.


### E.`PCP Prophylaxis Customization`

When ongoing B cell depletion + glucocorticoid exposure pushes us beyond risk tiers for *Pneumocystis jirovecii*, guideline‐driven TMP‑SMX is standard start:
> - Started initially at discharge,
> - Discontinued due hypertension? Anemia increasingly derived?
>
Actual pathology:
* Patient developed hemolytic anemia undetermined G6PD status -> unloved TMP‑SMX eliminated
→ Switched successfully onto quarterly nebulized pentamidine regimen.

## 📌 Key Take-Homes

– 🩺 A sudden cervical soft-tissue ‘angioedema’ compellingly should raise dermatomyositis—especially if skin=nail changes exist even subtly.
– ⚡️ Get a serum creatine kinase reflex immediately before expanding ED maneuvers—not merely a routine test for classic presentation!
– 🔬 Anti‑SAE1 positivity defines a phenotype predisposing heavily toward airway compromise/dysphagia & ILD → intricately integrate ENT consult early.
– 💉 Aggressive pulsed methylprednisolone nightly ➜ steep prednisone taper matched against serial CK/CRP trends gets you past acute airway danger fast-track epoch versus surgery alone.
– 💊 When pulmonary impairment flags appear early—pick MMF over MTX—and monitor quadraticlimbs signs intensively mean HFs!
– 💉***If*** your patient accrues hemolysis on TMP‑SMX during prolonged dual therapy → swapped #PCPProxiprim from oral ^→ nebulized pentamidine needle devilically safely.
