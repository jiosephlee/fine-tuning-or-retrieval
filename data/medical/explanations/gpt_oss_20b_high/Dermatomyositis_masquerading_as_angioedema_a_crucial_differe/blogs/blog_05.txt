Title: Blog about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

# Therapeutic Roadmap for Severe Dermatomyositis With Airway Involvement

### Why this case grabbed our attention

A healthy man in his forties walks into an ED five‑fold swollen neck after three weeks of progressive swelling and mild proximal muscle aches – your first differential is Ludwig’s angina or even idiopathic angioedema. Yet what unfolds is just nine months later a diagnosis of anti‑SAE1 dermatomyositis! This one patient walks through ENT assessment → CT scan → “no clear source” → infectious work‑up negative → immunology consult → surprisingly high creatine kinase → confirmation at muscle biopsy. Meanwhile his throat swells essentially under the “pseudo‑angioedema” umbrella while his breathlessness improves only after aggressive immunosuppression. This rare saga underscores how bright EMTs and ENTs must keep autoimmune myopathies in mind when faced with unexplained deep neck swelling coupled with subtle cutaneous clues.



## Spotting the clues early

| Clinical feature | Why it matters |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Neck swelling *without* fever or erythema | Disproportionate inflammation implies vasculature inflammation rather than cellulitis |
| Proximal muscle weakness/X‑ray -> troponin = 0 | Dialectic between weakness & subcutaneous edema suggests intracellular damage |
| Nailfold capillary changes + ectropional erythema | Classic dermatosciometric hint toward dermatomyositis especially when slit‑scope shows avascular spots |
| Elevated CRP & albumin within normal limits | “Low grade” systemic inflammation pattern typical for inflammatory myopathies |

**Bottom line**: Whenever you see subcutaneous neck swelling *plus* any hint of proximal weakness or skin findings—even subtle periungual erythema—ask yourself immediately whether CK >10 000 U/L might be lurking.



## How we stumbled across the diagnosis

1. **Cautious imaging** – CT ruled out an abscess but highlighted diffuse strap muscle oedema & retropharyngeal inflammation without air pockets.
2. **Basic laboratory trail** – Routine CBC/CXR normal, yet CK shot up over sixteen thousand within 24 hrs—a flag that demanded acknowledgment beyond a simple ‘angioedema’.
3. **Immunological flags** – ANA speckled 1:1280 matched a subsequent positive SAE1 antibody panel (Euroline assay). Negative ENA subset (Mi‑2α/TIF1‐γ/Jo‑1/Jo‐O) tightened suspicion toward SAE1-specific dermatomyositis.
4. **Histopathology confirmation** – Perifascicular necrosis + MAC deposition underscored complement activation—in line with classic dermatomyositis pathology.



## The evidence‑based rescue protocol

Below is our stepwise algorithm built upon shared clinical experience, guideline suggestions (IMACS cancer screening ‑ Section 3), published cohorts (Yu et al., 2020 meta‑analysis), and always keeping patient safety front loud.


### Day 0–4 – Aggressive first line

| Intervention | Dose/Rationale |
|--------------|----------------|
| **High‑dose intravenous methylprednisolone** | 1000 mg/day × 3 days → immediate cytokine suppression & fast symptom relief |
| **IVIG loading** | 2 g/kg over five days = 35 g/day × 5 days → immune complex modulation useful when airway compromise exists |
| **Airway protection** | Bedside fibreoptic laryngoscopy ± intubation depending on work‑up severity |

*Note*: Prioritize obtaining baseline labs for CBC, CMP, coagulation profile before starting IVIG.



### Week 1–6 – Transition & quantifying response

* **Prednisone taper**  
     *Start* at 60 mg daily → ↓ 5 mg every two–three weeks until <10 mg/day before discontinuation over ~5–7 months time frame (patient fetched here completed at month 7).  
* **Mycophenolate mofetil**  
    *Dose*: 1 g PO twice daily × ≥6–12 months proposed because pulmonary compromise precludes MTX use per literature caution [Birmingham et al., 2019].  
* **Safety net therapy**  
    *Rituximab*: If inadequate CRF decline after 4 weeks under NP / Steroids → two doses ×1000 mg IV two weeks apart (as done here).  

> In our chart scar‐scar scenario this combination led us from ~16k CK down to ~37 U/L within three weeks — an extraordinary drop mirroring back‑to‑bone clinical improvement.



### Month 3–6 – Consolidation & special precautions

| Task | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Pneumocystis jirovecii prophylaxis** | TMP/SMX starting after first three courses (>8 weeks cumulative prednisone ≥20 mg/d), switched later s/p hemolytic anaemia observed = nebulised pentamidine q4 wk |
| **Vaccinations** | Recheck ≥immune status vs influenza/yearly coverage & PCV20 just before escalation into sicker regimens (if nascent disease not Voice). |
| **Monitoring stack** | • Serial creatinine kinase each fortnight until stable <200 U/L.<br>• CBC/chem panel monthly until day ~90.<br>• Repeat chest CT/MRI ± PET if any new symptoms suspected malignancy per IMACS high‐risk cluster (“dysphagia + SAE1+ male”). |

> The sequence resonates well between existing STRIPE trial data suggesting that adding IVIG upfront reduces overall steroid load without compromising remission rates.



### Month 6–12+ – Long-term surveillance

* **Imaging follow‐up**  
   ‑ Repeat chest CT at nine months → near complete resolution my informs us lungs are clearing both inflammatory infiltrates & aspiration injury.\n\n
* **Muscle strength patter calibrating**  
   ‑ Manual Muscle Testing MMT score approaching age norm ≈ Signifies functional remission.\n\n
* **Tapering off IVIG eventually**  
   ‑ After sustained remission ≥6 months ⇒ consider monthly dosing downgrade or cessation per hematologist review.\n\n

## Core Pearls

• If you see “angioedematous” neck swelling plus an atypical pattern ‑ never forget *non‑histamine / non‐bradykinin* alternative causes like dermatomyositis.\n\n
• Snooping out even mild periungual erythema in such scenarios can alleviate diagnostic delay.\n\n
• Anti‑SAE1 antibodies are highly specific yet skin manifestations can be subclinical—muscle biopsy remains king when suspicion persists.\n\n
• While IVIG costs hefty money in some settings, early intervention sharply curbs total cumulative steroid exposure.\n\n
• PCP prophylaxis *must* accompany any prolonged steroids (>20 mg/day for >8 weeks) plus additional B cell depleting agent like rituximab.\n\n
• Serial CK trends coupled with MMT score converge into objective markers guaranteeing treatment efficacy within four weeks.\n\n

### Take Home Messages

- **Neck swelling without obvious infection warrants a myopathy panel:** Look for abnormal CK ASAP—it will change your ENT suspects into rheumatology suspects overnight.
- **High‑dose IV methylprednisolone followed by a classic prednisone taper is still rule‑in:** We start aggressively whenever there’s any conceivable airway compromise.
- **IVIG is godsend when dysphagia threatens aspiration:** Initiate loading concurrently with steroids if respiratory function is troubling.
- **Mycophenolate mofetil beats MTX when ILD risks loom:** Adjust choices based on pulmonary baseline values.
- **Rituximab becomes your rescue weapon only after initial ambulatory failure:** Don’t give it prematurely unless clinical deterioration shows up after two cycles of IVIG+steroids+MMF.
- **Infection prevention cannot lag behind aggressive immunosuppression:** Put active PCP prophylaxis on your board—and switch away from TMP/S triples upon any hemolysis warning signs!
- **Use serial serum creatinine kinase as your “traffic light” marker:** A rapid fall signals success whereas plateau demands escalation.

Here’s hoping this structured roadmap gives you confidence next time those fishermen posts come looping through patchy edema cases—it’s always better than staring blankly at a CT read leaf wrongly labelled ‘angioedema’!
