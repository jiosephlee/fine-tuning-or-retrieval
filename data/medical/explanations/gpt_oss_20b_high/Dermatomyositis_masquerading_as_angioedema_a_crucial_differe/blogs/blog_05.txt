# Cancer Screening Checklist When Facing Myopathy Impact  

---

## Why think about tumours when you see a rash and muscle weakness?

Dermatomyositis (DM) is classically a “rash‑first” disease—helio­tope rashes, Gottron’s papules and nailfold changes often herald muscle inflammation years before the headache‑like pain beyond your shoulder blades becomes obvious. Yet under that fishy skin lies a very real cancer clock ticking hands down every week it takes days longer than you think. In adult males aged 45–64—the very demographic of our index patient—a prospective registry found roughly **18 %** harbouring an occult malignancy within five years of DM diagnosis when anti‑SAE1 antibodies were present. 

This isn’t just academic nuance—any snapshot from a clinical trial tells us DM is one of the most common connective tissue diseases where “watchful waiting” turns into *watchful fighting* against nascent tumours.  

---

## The IMACS scaffold—how do we move from “maybe” to “definitely urgent”?

IMACS (International Myositis Assessment & Clinical Studies Group) built a pragmatic rubric around three classic variables:

| Category | Examples | Weight |
|---------|----------|--------|
| **Myositis type** | Dermatomyositis = **high** | |
| **Clinical features** | Dysphagia = **high**, symmetric proximal weakness = **moderate** | |
| **Specific auto‑antibodies** | Anti‑SAE1 = **intermediate**, anti‑TIF‑γ/anti‑MDA5 = **intermediate**, others < 5 % | |

**Rule:**  
- Two *high* risk factors → *high‐risk* group → full immediate cancer work‑up within weeks.  
- One *high* + two *intermediate* → still *high‐risk*.  
- Any isolated *intermediate* alone = low–moderate—no immediate imaging unless organ involvement surfaces.

Our patient clearly overlapped into that top bin:
* Dermatomyositis – high  
* Dysphagia – high  
* Male sex – intermediate  
* Anti‑SAE1 positivity – intermediate  

Total score = two highs + two intermediates → unequivocally “high‐risk”.

## From rash to referral desk—case follow‑through

> A man in his forties presents with “neck swelling” billed initially as Ludwig’s angina—afterward recognized as pseudo‑angioedema secondary to inflammatory myofascial edema from dermatomyositis.

Key lab values on day 2 mirrored classic inflammatory myopathies:
- CK ~ 16 000 U/L (normal < 200)  
- ALT ≈ 140 U/L, AST ≈ 400 U/L  
- LDH ≈ 700 U/L  
- ANA titer 1:1280 speckled pattern  

Imaging crescendoed:
- Chest CT showed multiple pulmonary nodules without classic fibrosis—a red flag prompting *PET/CT*, which came back clean for metastases.
- Video fluoroscopy confirmed severe pharyngeal dysphagia—a real early sign of airway compromise.
- MRI/MRS equivocal—multifocal active myositis confined mainly to thigh compartments rather than cardiac muscle yet still indicated systemic inflammatory burden.
  
Screening inventory in this hotbox patient?
| Test | Rationale | Result |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Full body PET/CT | Detect occult primary/metastasis | Negative |
| Chest CTA/CT | Evaluate ILD or pleural nodules | Nodules only |
| Colonoscopy & gastroscopy | Colorectal occult carcinoma<br>Upper GI neoplasm screen | Pending |
| Tumour markers panel<sup>†</sup> | Longitudinal trend capture malignant rise | Normal at baseline |

<sup>†</sup>CEA, CA19‑9, CA125 & PSA responsibly drawn because they are standardised across guideline tiers even if baseline normal does not exclude later anomalies.

## Essentials checklist—for every practitioner fearing hidden tumours

### Baseline immediately upon confirmation of idiopathic inflammatory myopathy

1. **Chest imaging**  
   * Chest CT whether or not ILD present *if organotropism* flag exists (e.g., lymphadenopathy > 4 mm); 
   * PET/CT optional if unremitting symptoms unseen on conventional imaging despite steroid trial.
2. **Endoscopic evaluation**  
   * Gastroscopy & colonoscopy regardless of gastrointestinal symptoms—colorectal cancers lag behind new-onset DM by months!
3. **Tumour marker panel**  
   * CEA, CA19‑9 — especially adult pneumonias > 50 y;
   * CA125 — give cross‑check between pleural effusions and ascites;
   * PSA — men over 50 may stalk silent prostate carcinoma hidden behind CK spikes.
4. **Full history & physical emphasis resolution**  
   * Look for subtle facial telangiectasias, Heliotrope rash—even absent subhepatic jolts!

### Follow-up cadence—how does it shift over time?

| Timepoint post-diagnosis | What changes? |
|-------------------------|---------------|
| Months 0–3 — active therapy pause after initial steroid pulse ± immunomodulators | Repeat tumour markers every month; repeat chest imaging only if new respiratory complaints or CK rises >50%. |
| Months 3–6 — Prednisone taper / steroid-sparing agent plateau     | Endoscopic surveillance step down after two years symptom-free unless malignancy suspected; tumour markers trended quartely until plateaus present.| 
| Months >6                                 | Baseline Pan-INV mapped aircraft timeline ± yearly surveillance imaging <12 months interval if disease quiescent.| 

The major driver remains compressed therapy windows; if during steroid taper you hit muscle flares or bruised cachexia abates within weeks—immediately reset imaging truck!
