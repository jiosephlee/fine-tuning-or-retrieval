Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Morning Report] A type III overnight polysomnography shows an AHI of 6/h but predominantly central events—what methodological limitation likely underestimates true severity?

**Methodological limitation:** The use of a **type‑III portable study, which lacks electroencephalographic (EEG) monitoring**, causes the apnea‑hypopnea index (AHI) to be calculated over the entire recording period rather than true sleep time. Because periods of wakefulness are included in the denominator, the AHI—and therefore the apparent severity of respiratory events—under‑estimates what would be observed on an overnight laboratory type‑I polysomnography with accurate sleep staging.[10]  

In this patient’s case, a type‑III study reported an AHI of 6 h^–1 with 72 % central events. Without EEG data, we cannot determine how much of the 8‑hour recording represented actual sleep; if much time was spent awake or cycling through REM/NREM stages with differing airflow patterns, the real event burden per hour of sleep would be higher. Thus, relying on a type‑III study likely obscures the true severity of her central hypoventilation syndrome secondary to Chiari malformation type I.
