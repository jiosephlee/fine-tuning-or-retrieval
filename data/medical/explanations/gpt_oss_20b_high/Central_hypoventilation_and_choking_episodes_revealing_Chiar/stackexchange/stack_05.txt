Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Morning Report] A type III overnight polysomnography shows an AHI of 6/h but predominantly central events—what methodological limitation likely underestimates true severity?

**Answer**

The overnight study was a Type III portable polysomnogram that recorded only airflow, respiratory effort, SpO₂ and heart rate—but **no EEG**. Because no objective marker of when the patient was actually asleep was available, the investigators had to calculate the apnoea‑hypopnoea index (AHI) from *total recording time* rather than from *actual sleep time*. In other words,

\[
\text{AHI}_{\text{reported}} = \frac{\text{# events}}{\text{total recorded hours}}
\]

Instead of

\[
\text{AHI}_{\text{gold standard}} = \frac{\text{# events}}{\text{sleep hours}}
\]

The inclusion of wake periods increases the denominator, thereby **underestimating** the density of respiratory events. In a patient whose study shows predominantly central apneas—the hallmark in this Chiari I case—this methodological limitation can make a truly severe disorder appear mild (AHI 6/h). 

**Take‑home point:** For suspected central hypoventilation or unexplained desaturations, a full Type I PSG with EEG staging—and ideally capnography—should be obtained so that AHI is calculated per hour of actual sleep and represents true disease severity.
