## What Makes This Case Eye‑Opening  
Picture an otherwise healthy woman who spends her nights endlessly worrying about falling asleep somewhere inattended—a classic “sandwich slip” scenario built into every emergency medicine training module—and yet she never feels short‑of‑breath even when her oxygen plunges into the 70%‑80% range every few minutes on her pulse oximeter during REM sleep and brief periods when she’s distracted while eating whole foods. A decade‑long history of choking episodes linked bidirectionally—both solid foods and liquids—combined with constant “pulsing” occipital headaches gives clinicians a baffling puzzle considered impossible until an old head CT surface when read through fresh eyes showed a crowded foramen magnum and cerebellar tonsillar his cranial descent—a textbook Chi‑1 malformation concealed behind decades‑old trauma‐related imaging had finally surfaced outright enough that it explains rapid progressive dysphagia/pulmonary apnoea syndrome masquerading as acquired *Ondine’s curse*.  

The quietest symptom—the lack of dyspnea during severe desaturation—is perhaps the biggest clue directly pointing toward **central hypoventilatory failure** caused by a structural brainstem lesion rather than airway irritation or pulmonary disease.
