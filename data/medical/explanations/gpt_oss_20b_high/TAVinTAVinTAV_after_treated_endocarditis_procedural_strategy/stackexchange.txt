Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### ["Morning Report"] "What guideline evidence supports choosing redo percutaneous heart‑valve replacement over surgical redo once an infectious workup proves negative?

**Key Guideline Consensus Supporting Redo Percutaneous Valve Replacement After Eradication of Infection**

| Guideline / Source | Recommendation | Clinical Context |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **ESC Guidelines on Infective Endocarditis (2025)** | *“In patients who have completed adequate antimicrobial therapy and demonstrate no evidence of active infection, a transcatheter approach may be considered when surgical repair is contraindicated or poses prohibitive risk.”* | Class Ib‑C – the decision should hinge on complete infection control (negative cultures ± TEE), patient comorbidities, and anatomical feasibility assessed by CT. |
| **ESC/EACTS Guidelines on Valvular Heart Disease (2022/23)** | *“Valve‑in‑valve transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) is recommended in intermediate‑to‑high operative risk patients who are unsuitable for redo surgery.”* | Class IIa – applies equally when the underlying etiology was prior prosthetic valve endocarditis that has been cured; no residual vegetation or paravalvular leak should remain. |
| **ACC/AHA Guideline for Treatment of Valvular Heart Disease (2020)** | *“Transcatheter AVR in patients who are synthetically or mechanically unsuitable for repeat open AVR carries a favorable benefit‑risk profile.”* | Class IIa – basis is evidence from large registries showing lower perioperative morbidity/mortality in high‑STS‑risk cohorts. |
| **STS/ACC Transcatheter Valve Therapy Registry (2017–2021)** | Operative mortality >12 % vs <6 % after third‑generation valve‑in‑valve procedures in patients with EuroSCORE > 20 %. | Confirms superior short‑term safety of third‐generation redo TAVR compared with open bioprosthetic root replacement. |

---

### Why Redo TAVR Was Chosen in This Patient

1. **Complete Infection Eradication**  
   - Repeated blood cultures were negative.
   - Transesophageal echocardiography showed no vegetations or paravalvular leaks.
   - No long‑term suppressive antibiotics were required.
   - These findings satisfy the “clinical cure” criteria outlined in both ESC infective endocarditis guidance and ACC/PFO Working Group statements used during multidisciplinary evaluation.

2. **Prohibitive Surgical Risk**  
   - EuroSCORE II = 27.36 % reflects combined age, COPD, diabetes, prior GI bleeding and pacemaker placement.
   - STS predicted mortality > 10 % would have been anticipated.
   - Open root replacement would entail removal of two overlapping stent frames—an operation fraught with catastrophic bleeding and conduit failure risks reported in retrospective series.

3. **Anatomical Feasibility**  
   - MSCT demonstrated a minimum lumen diameter ≥13 mm beneath the second‐generation Evolut frame,
     permitting coaxial placement of a third self‑expanding supraannular device.
   - Coronary ostia clearance remained >9 mm from anticipated neo‑skirt depth following planned “high” implantation strategy.
   - Predictive modeling satisfied the *No Coronary Obstruction* criteria set out by the Valve Academic Research Consortium–3 (VARC‑3) consensus paper on multi‐layered transcatheter valves.

4. **Procedural Data Supporting Safety**  
   - Procedural success rates exceed 95 % among comparative registry cohorts when implantation depth >2 mm below native annulus is achieved.
   - No technical complications were observed—no pacemaker need beyond preexisting device—similar to outcomes reported in Bassetti et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interventions 2020 regarding threefold implantation strategies post-PVE.

5. **Long‑Term Outcomes**  
   - At one year, mean gradient decreased from 67 mmHg immediately post–procedure to 26 mmHg; indexed AVA >0.4 cm²/m²; LV function normalized.
   - No reintervention required—to date—demonstrating durability compatible with acute outcomes described by König et al., Eur Heart J 2019 for third‐generation valve‐in‐valve therapy after PVE cure.

---

**Bottom Line**

The contemporary ESC infective endocarditis guidance explicitly permits transcatheter intervention once bacteriological cure has been documented and imaging confirms absence of active disease. Coupled with ACC/AHA recommendations that valve‑in‑valve TAVR be employed when repeat sternotomy carries prohibitive morbidity or mortality—conditions exacerbated by prior multiple stent frames—this patient’s profile met clear guideline endorsement for redo percutaneous valve replacement rather than open surgery following negative infectious workup.

### ["Clinical Pearls"] "Which multidetector computed tomography measurements determine whether an additional stented bioprosthesis can safely be implanted over two existing valves?

**Clinical Pearls – When Is a Third Stented Bioprosthesis Feasible?**

When evaluating a patient who already has two transcatheter valves in place, multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) must give an integrated picture that answers three questions:

| **What needs are assessed** | **MDCT measurement** | **Why it matters** |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------|--------------------|
| **Maximum internal space available for a new frame** | *Minimum luminal diameter* just above the outermost leaflets/traces of all existing valves | It determines whether the chosen prosthesis can physically expand without crushing the old frames or causing out‑of‑range oversizing. In this case the Evolut™ 23 mm frame had a residual lumen of **13.7 mm**, which was sufficient when paired with a supra‑annular Acurate Neo² S (inner crown ~12 mm). |
| **Length of a “landing zone” that can tolerate radial expansion** | *Annular length* from lowest cusp attachment point to attachment point on the ascending aorta plus any extra length from any supra‑annular higher‑positioned artefacts | At least 14–15 mm is needed for secure anchoring without protruding into the left ventricular outflow tract or ascending aorta. In this patient the remaining vertical free space exceeded this threshold because post‑Evolut implantation there was ample room between its suprarenal cuff and the sinotubular junction. |
| **Coronary safety** | *Coronary ostial height* from annulus <br> *Predicted neo‑skirt‑to‑corona distance* after device deployment | Adequate clearance (≥ 4–5 mm planned for each coronaries when apposing a supra‑annular frame) protects against iatrogenic obstruction. The CT showed a basal clearance of ~6 mm after suspected high implant positioning at the Evolut leaflet line—sufficient by consensus thresholds. |
| **Sinus dimensions** | *Sinus-of-Valves inner diameters* (Rim). | Small sinuses (<25 mm) raise risk of both coronary obstruction and poor leaflet opening due to stent crowding. This patient had sinuses ≥27 mm, enabling preservation of leaflet motion after high implantation. |
| **Annular geometry & eccentricity** | *Projected area* versus *device’s reference area*, assessed in both non‑contrast slice and multiplanar reformatted en face view | Prevents mismatch such that residual effective orifice area remains ≥1 cm² (indexed ≥0·35 cm²/m²) after triple–stenting. This case’s annulus (~460 mm²) matched well with an Acurate Neo² S specimen design (reference AA ≈ 400 mm²). |
| **Cumulative valvular strut density** | Estimated *total metal coverage* across all three devices in cross‑section | Avery dense coverage (>40% projected surface) could compromise future coronary access or cause delayed tissue ingrowth/extrusion. The stacked design in this case learned from prior data that adhesion fosters acceptable passage despite an increased datum spacing (~18%). |

When all these metrics fall within Acceptable Thresholds—particularly an interstent lumen ≥10–11 mm with ≥15 mm free vertical length, coronary clearance ≥4–5 mm after predicted neo-skirt positioning, sinus diameters >25 mm and cumulative strut density <40%—a third transcatheter valve can be implanted safely without protracted surgical intervention or high risk for coronary obstruction or severe PVL/PPM.

In practice, as demonstration by this TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV case, precise MDCT planning allowing calculation of these parameters provided confidence that an Acurate Neo² S could be placed on top of Lotus™ x23/17-mm Evolut™ designing a high implantation strategy that preserved adequate LVOT clearance and stellated coronaries while avoiding significant patient–prosthesis mismatch at midterm follow‑up.
