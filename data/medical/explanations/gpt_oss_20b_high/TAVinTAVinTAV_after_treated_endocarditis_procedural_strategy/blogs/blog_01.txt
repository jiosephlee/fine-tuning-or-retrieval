# When It Comes Back Again — TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV After Endocarditis  

The last advance described here isn’t a brand new valve technology or an FDA announcement— it’s the story of **the same patient who had three different transcatheter valves stacked side‑by‑side over ten years**, survived a serious infection, and remained symptom‑free a year later… Yet it also raises a hard question clinicians constantly wrestle with today: **When do you keep trying percutaneous solutions instead of calling the surgeons?**  

---

## The Recurrent Redundancy That Prompted This Blog  

Three consecutive transcatheter procedures are already rare enough, but adding an episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis into the mix brings an extra layer of complexity that most registry data haven’t captured adequately. The majority of our teaching notes insist “any treatment of perivalvular infection leans toward open surgery.” That maxim was written early in the decade when most patients were considered either low­risk enough for early surgery or too frail for anything but palliative therapy. Fifty years into this decade our reality has shifted dramatically—with more transcatheter implants surviving longer—and patients are now living long enough to “hit” LQ score failures multiple times while suffering from infections when those failures occur.

#### How Long Do Subclinical Infections Matter?

In December 2021 he had `severe bioprosthetic stenosis` after his first Lotus expired—immediate score milestones quickly raising gradients across his device (*mean gradient 83 mm Hg*). Ten months later `persistence fever + culture positive` provoked our team into diagnosing prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). By July 2023 serial imaging showed clear resolution—no vegetations on TOE and normal LAD‐regained central pressures on MSCT found nothing suspicious—to permit us holding onto our transcatheter jira strategy.



---

### Surgical Guidance vs Real‐World Practice  

Summer \[ESC Guidelines 2025\] still insists that **open replacement is preferred** for native…and especially prosthetic …endocarditis when feasible—particularly if there’s heart failure deterioration or uncontrollable septic focus—but they also recognize “individualized” decisions upon completeness control through antibiotics‐based de-escalation periods combined with imaging confirmation (`TEE/CT‐confirmed negative`).  
Our patient fit their “individualized” bracket perfectly:
* Infection cleared (<30 days off antibiotics)
* No paravalvular leak detected on echo/PT
* Coronary anatomy clear on MSCT (>12 mm from clips)
* Anatomic corridor reminiscent of planned post‐treatments

But his higher EuroSCORE>20% made probability calculus steeply tilt toward non-surgical management once his infectious episode subsided.

#### Delivering Pressure That Pops Into Place

### Clinical Pearls (& Key Take‑Hows)

#### 🔑 Core Decision Drivers  

• Ensure complete clearance from infective focus via blood cultures + negative transthoracic/transesophageal echo prior to any mechanical line insertion  
• Perform high-dimension CT overlay targeting coronary heights (>12 mm) AND intra-lumen diameter (>12–14 mm)  
• Favor supra-annular valves when multiple layers stack—lessened neo-skirt meniscus over coronary ostia  
• Align commissures using navigator software checkpoints built from CT landmarks—to preserve future coronary access  
• Use rapid pacing balloons during all dilatations—maximizes safety buffer during frame friction exposure
