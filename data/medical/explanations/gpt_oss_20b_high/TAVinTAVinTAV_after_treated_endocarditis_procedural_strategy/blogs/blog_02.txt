# Sculpting Success — CT‑Guided Planning For Triple Transcatheter Valves  

## Why This Case Rocks  

When I first read about a patient who had TAVI inside an already deployed TAVI inside another TAVI—plus an episode of *Enterococcus* endocarditis that was successfully treated—we all know that “redo” doesn’t usually ask for such extreme engineering precision. Yet this gentleman’s story demonstrates that meticulous preprocedural imaging can turn what looks like a towering stack of valves into an elegant masterpiece rather than an anatomical disaster.

In 2014 he received a 27 mm Lotus valve because surgery was too risky for him at then. Eight years later, degeneration forced a TAV-in-TAV with an Evolut 23 mm device (Sept 2022). Three months after that he slipped into fever and got diagnosed with prosthetic valve endocarditis caused by *E. faecalis*. Antibiotics cleared the infection; no suppressive therapy was needed because cultures were sterile and there were no vegetations on repeat echo/CT.

Fast forward two years—he’s again sitting feeble on exertion but now MRI/echo show severe restenosis and regurgitation in his two-layer prosthesis (mean gradient ~49 mm Hg). He’s still a prohibitive surgical candidate (EuroSCORE II ~27%) but he hopes for another transcatheter fixling shabby door did he? Right: we took him back to the cath lab in June 2024 for what history would call **TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV**—deploying an Acurate Neo–2 self-expanding valve on top of a Lotus + Evolut sandwich while preserving coronaries and maximizing effective orifice area (EOA).

The mission here wasn’t just “drop another hornet hive onto that nest.” It was *planual*, immediate imaging wizardry so you can script each bead of nuclear baseball = MDSPE approach in protagonist perspective — We’ll walk through how we used multiphase multi-slice computed tomography (MSCT) as our Swiss Army knife for this triple-layer device separation puzzle.



## The Key Imaging Objectives  

Before any wire goes through, you must answer four brutal questions:

1. **Is there room for a third valve?**  
   - Minimum lumen diameter where the new valve will sit  
   - Frame-to-coronary clearance  
2. **If so where should it be positioned?**  
   - Coronary ostia height vs “neo-sinus” size
   - Implications of high vs low implantation
3. **Which prosthesis performs best?**  
   - Annulus sizing & EOA prediction  
   - How each prior design influences leaflet dynamics
4. **What’s the CYP3A risk or potential post-dilatation requirement?**  
   - Overhang proportions
   - Interaction among metal struts

Answering these required combining standard annular planimetry with custom measurements around prior frames—a bit like playing hide-and-seek with metal.



## Step‑by‑Step CT Workup  

| Step | What & Why | Practical Tips |
|------|------------|----------------|
| 1️⃣ **Acquire high‐resolution MSCT** | ≥0.5 mm slice thickness, ECG-gated at mid-diastole | Make sure scanners are preloaded with high spatial resolution cardiac protocol |
| 2️⃣ **Reconstruct annulus in multiplanar view** | Measure internal perimeter & area in five segments; confirm slice oriented edges match prior valve rings | Use dedicated software (Materialise Mimics/Velocity) or Vizceral if available |
| 3️⃣ **Define ‘effective landing zone’** | Identify where three prostheses engraft together plus extra stent legend above ≥2 mm above coronary ostium | Capture cross-sections both just beneath Evolut frame and at proposed neo-sinus level |
| 4️⃣ **Compute minimum lumen diameter** | Measure diameter across the narrowest part ≥25 mm below coronary ostium | In this case it measured ~13.7 mm – adequate for S-sized neo2 (~18 mm internal nominal) if properly angulated |
| 5️⃣ **Coronary clearance check** | Get coronary ostial height vs sinotubular junction; measure distance from proposed implant landing ring to ORIGIN OF CORONARY ORIS | Our target: ≥4–6 mm margin – achieved by retaining Evolut leaflet position via high implantation |
| 6️⃣ **Visualise overlapping stents** | Use coronal/sagittal reconstructions + orthogonal ‘stent‑wire map’ streaming instrumentation contact levels | Take care with metallic artefacts hiding true lumen; thick struts sometimes appear artificially narrowed |
| 7️⃣ **Simulate virtual deployment** | Drag-and-drop highest-resolution images into Malta hybrid software / EchoPAC “Valve View” / optional custom script | Align Acurate Neo–2 rotation commensurate with Evolut commissures so that leaflets overlie optimal frame slots |
| 8️⃣ **Assess commissural alignment feasibility** | Ensure commissure columns are separate enough for future coronary access post deploy + simulate radial forces (%) that might fracture earlier frames if needed later | In this case Neo-2 symmetric base allowed aligning with Evolut leaflets – giving us an 'open door'*

> ### Pitfall Alert  
> When multiple frames overlap, volumetric reconstruction can overestimate lumen because struts cause attenuation scatter artifacts. Always look at *slice overlay* images by manually aligning them perpendicular to each stent's struts—any misalignment misreports the real space.*

## Practical Virtual Deployment Paradigm  

1) Load MSCT data into virtual platform.
2) Place Acurate Neo–2 at diameters suggested by nominal sizing tables (~S-size given LVA<18 mm).
3) Adjust depth until simulated frame sits partially overlaps Evolut's skirt but leaves coronary clearances intact.
4) Stress-test using synergy between Evolut radial stiffness and Neo–2 supra-annular leaflets — lean toward high implantation (~14 mm above annulus), mimicking our actual outcome.
5) Confirm projected EOA >1–1¼ cm² when two layers are accounted for – 
6) Label recommended balloon pre-dilation sizes based on residual frame constraint (22/40 mm preceding Deploy; then post-dilation 20/40 mm).

The simulation largely agreed with our intraoperative logistics—in real life we used high speed left radial + femoral access; felt balloon dilation under ramp ~180 bpm – all praised by teleradiography who saw coronaries flush.



## Taking Out the Valve Supreme 

During procedure:

- Left radial plus left femoral across principle
- Safe crossing/guidewire orientation via Safari catheter into LV
- Rapid pacing + balloon pre-dilation 22/40 mm inside box above existing Evolut skirt → ensures coil expandibility & minimal Rupture risk.
- Under fluoroscopy guide align Acurate Neo–2 w/ respect to earlier commissures (vertical dotted lines)
- Post-dilate carefully w/20/40 mm balloon at ~12 rpm before letting off final release

Key technical decision: Avoid coronary protection wires because pre-Doppler aortography proved coronaries unobstructed—even after third implant swirling around facial enamel difference changes thanks to oriented alignment.

Outcome basically is "high improvement".

At routine follow-ups—one-year—he is asymptomatic, MV+EF>65%, mean gradient <30 mm Hg; no PPM concerns clinically meaningful beyond minimal first-month spike.



## Clinical Pearls & Take-home Messages  

- **Serial TAVI demands more than bigger valves – it's geometry management**  
  • Pre-procedural MSCT must quantify not only annulus but also inter-valve spacing, frame overlap lengths, and coronary distances separately per layer.
  • Virtual deployment should incorporate commissural alignment goals both now & future coronary access.
  • Be prepared for evolving PPM patterns—three overlapped ridges reduce EOA more than simple single-layer cases.
  • Post-dilatation pressure may inadvertently relax earlier frames—monitor intraluminal pressure gradients intraoperatively rather than only echo estimates.
  • Inpatient infection clearance should be documented via negative cultures + sterile echo + flawless MSCT before reintervention; once resolved, redo TAVI is as reasonable as first-time placement when surgical risk is prohibitive.
  • Emerging software (e.g., GHMware Smart Heart Valve simulator, dedicated ValveFit plug-ins) streamline translational planning from raw Hounsfield units to interactive geometry mapping—even though not yet standard in all cath labs.
  • Avoid reliance solely on annular MV reference tables when layering devices—a manufacturer’s nominal size may underestimate required EOA when multiple stiff frames exist.
