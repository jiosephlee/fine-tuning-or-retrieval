Chapter 2: ## Patient Case Timeline and Clinical Presentation

### Baseline demographics and comorbidity profile
The patient is a man in his late seventies who presents with multiple cardiovascular risk factors that shaped his therapeutic journey. His chronic medical history includes arterial hypertension refractory enough to necessitate long‑term antihypertensive medication, type‑II diabetes mellitus managed medically, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease contributing to exertional limitation, and an extensive coronary artery disease burden that required four percutaneous coronary interventions between 2009‑2014 (right coronary artery, left anterior descending artery twice, circumflex artery). In addition to these medical conditions he underwent dual‑chamber pacemaker implantation in October 2014 because of newly diagnosed third‑degree atrioventricular block—a complication common after transcatheter aortic valve replacement owing to proximity of the conduction system to the annulus. Recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding episodes began during the last two years and were attributed mainly to antiplatelet therapy following coronary interventions combined with peptic ulcer disease secondary to chronic steroid exposure for COPD management.

Overall these comorbidities conferred a prohibitive surgical mortality risk according both European Society of Cardiology surgical score calculations (>25 %) and contemporary contemporary morbidity indices such as EuroSCORE‑II (~27 %). Consequently subsequent decisions favored transcatheter solutions wherever clinically feasible despite their inherent complexity when multiple reinjections are required.



### First‑generation Lotus Valve implantation – technical notes & early outcomes
In October 2014 he underwent transfemoral transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) under local anesthesia under guidance by rapid ventricular pacing using a balloon crimped Sapien‐like proprietary device—a self‑expanding Lotus™ valve measuring diameters set respectively at **27 mm** based on annulus sizing performed during admission survey examinations (transesophageal echocardiography plus multislice computed tomography). This platform combines respiratory gating mechanics coupled with fluorescence‐based seal technology aimed at minimizing paravalvular leak—a crucial advantage in patients who are poorly cannulated due their fragile anatomy following multi-drug heart failure treatment regimen’s slowed tissue healing mechanisms typical in COPD patients receiving systemic steroids.This lap­aroscopic planning achieved an apparently optimal flex point placement resulting in well-seated leaflet geometry without mechanical obstruction evident during immediate intraoperative fluoroscopic assessment.
