Title: Blog about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

# Interpreting CA‑125 During Gestation  

Ever seen a “normal” laboratory value raise a red flag just because it sits slightly over its textbook threshold? In this case, a modest rise from **36 → 41 U/mL** at 21 weeks gestation set off alarm bells—yet it turned out not to signal ovarian cancer at all, but a twisting pedunculated uterine leiomyoma that was causing acute pain and dyspnea.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## What Made This Case Interesting  

A 43‑year‑old nulliparous woman arrived at the ED with sudden left upper quadrant pain and exertional shortness of breath. Ultrasound revealed **two gigantic cystic masses**—one spanning the left subphrenic space into the uterine fundus (>18 cm) and another filling the pouch of Douglas (~9 cm) attached to an intramural/subserosal leiomyoma. A slight elevation in **CA‑125** was noted serendipitously during routine lab work (the value had climbed two weeks earlier). Problem set curve straightened out once an MRI showed thick walls, focal solid components, and continuity with a degenerated uterine myoma—all classic signatures of *degenerating* rather than *malignant* disease.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Understanding Ca‑125 Physiology in Pregnancy  

| Trimester | Median Ca‑125 | Physiologic Variation |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------------|
| 1st | ≈ 23 U/mL | Peak attributable to placentation & endometrial expansion |
| 2nd | ≈ 14 U/mL | Generally stable |
| 3rd | ≈ 21 U/mL | Second physiologic rise |

Key takeaways:

1. **Every pregnant patient has her own baseline flare**—ca‑125 routinely dips below <35 U/mL in most women until near term when it rebounds somewhat higher.
2. Even *benign* processes (endometriosis, hepatotoxicity, pelvic inflammatory disease, fibroid degeneration) can push values past this cut point.
3. In isolation, an isolated spike above 35 U/mL does **not equal cancer** unless accompanied by red flags on clinical exam or imaging.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Serial Monitoring Beats Absolute Numbers  

In practice you rarely act because *any* value jumps over an arbitrary threshold without context. Instead:
* Track changes within individual patients—e.g., an uncomplicated pregnancy drifting steady around 16 U/mL versus one trending upward steadily through serial checks.
* If levels remain flat below 20 except for one bump tied to new symptoms (as here), focus on that bump's correlation with clinical changes—and check imaging immediately.
* Circulate your numbers mentally when ordering investigations so you can decide if extra testing is truly warranted.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Imaging Strategy During Pregnancy  

1️⃣ **Ultrasound** remains first line—color Doppler helps delineate vascularity seen here (peripheral flow around both masses).  
2️⃣ If uncertainty lingers, move safely to **non‑contrast MRI** after the first trimester—no gadolinium needed for diagnosing subserosal/cystic lesions in this setting.  
3️⃣ All modalities together sharpen differentiation between *decidualised endometrioma*, *cystically degenerating leiomyoma*, and *gynaecomastia*.  


In this patient both ultrasound AND MRI pointed towards a fibroid-originated lesion—especially given continuity between the pouch-of-Douglas mass and the posterior intramural myoma.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## When Did We Decide Surgery Was Needed?  

Critical decision nodes (often surfacing quietly behind seemingly trivial labs):

| Factor | Significance |
|--------|--------------|
| Symptom burden | Severe pain + dyspnea indicating mechanical obstruction |
| Progressive size | Likely rapid growth given advanced maternal age |
| Fever + infectious workup | Klebsiellauria suggested local inflammation/infection risk |
| Serial ↑ Ca‑125 | Trend hinting ongoing tissue turnover/degeneration |
| Imaging confirming degenerative solid components | Solid ↔ necrotic cores amenable only via resection |

These worked together against “wait‐and‐see” which would carry significant morbidity if torsion or rupture occurred.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Operative Course & Aftermath  

A mid‑pregnancy midline laparotomy was chosen (classic approach for sizeable masses) instead of a laparoscopy because:
* Masses were >15 cm with unclear borders;
* She was already experiencing restrictive lung mechanics;
* A quick definitive debulking would reduce maternal systemic stress while preserving uterus integrity.

Procedure:
1. Free peritoneal fluid sent for culture/cytology → all negative later.
2.Bedside transvaginal US confirmed viable fetus throughout surgery under continuous fetal HR monitoring.
3.Pedunculated upper mass was excised using dual clamps → root closed with absorbable sutures – limiting uterine trauma.
4.Pelvic lower mass was left untouched – deemed safe (no imminent torsion/abscess risk) yet too deep for safe removal without risking scar rupture later.



Post-op management included iron repletion & transfusion for anaemia drop immediately after surgery because massive intramuscular blood loss wasn't detected intraoperatively but manifested on CBC days later—a reminder that occult bleeding may still loom.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Outcome Highlights  

* Immediate relief from dyspnea & abdominal pain within days post-op  
* No intraoperative complications  
* Vaginal umbilical artery Doppler + CTG remained reassuring throughout hospitalization  
* Elective C‐section planned at exactly 37 weeks once fetal lungs matured fully  
* Neonate born healthy → birth weight fell into 86th percentile at time point per WHO chart  
* Persistent palpable pelvic mass regressed slightly (<1 cm decrease) after delivery – consistent with regression pattern seen in postpartum uterus



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Clinical Pearls / Take‐Home Messages  

• **Do not act solely on an absolute level—use serial trends** relative to each patient's physiologic range.  
• **Imaging always precedes invasive decisions**; even slight marker shifts must be correlated against ultrasonography/MRI findings before labeling pathologic suspicion as malignant.  
• **Remain cautious when CA‑125 rises modestly above <35 U/mL** during pregnancy; its presence alone rarely dictates surgery unless coupled by new symptoms or imaging clues suggestive of active degeneration/necrosis/torsion/ rupture risk.  
• **If treatment is warranted**, aim for the shortest operative time possible while preserving uterine integrity—with fetal monitoring continuous irrespective of gestational age or operation type.  
• **Consider low-dose progesterone throughout postoperative period**, especially after myomectomy during late second trimester – evidence indicates it may help mitigate preterm labor risk related surgical insult.
