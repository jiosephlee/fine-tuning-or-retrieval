Title: Blog about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

# When Is Myomectomy Safe In Gestation?

A forty‑year‑old first‑pregnancy woman presented at **21 + 6 weeks** gestation with **severe left‑upper quadrant pain and pelvic discomfort** that kept her from sitting upright – she even complained of mild dyspnoea from “being held back” by her own belly. Ultrasound revealed an **18 × 12 × 7 cm subphrenic cyst** attached to the uterine fundus and a **9 × 8 × 8 cm pelvic cyst** tethered near a posterior intramural‑subserosal myoma. The picture looked like *two gigantic decidualised endometriomas*, yet the CA‑125 was climbing (41 U/mL) and her urine grew *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. After a multidisciplinary tag‑team conversation (obster‑gyn‑surgeon‑anesthesia) we chose **early open myomectomy** – something most obstetricians consider taboo until after delivery – because our red flags outweighed the usual “wait”.

## The Unexpected Giants

When ultrasonography spat out two unilocular cysts larger than any typical ovarian cyst you’d see outside pregnancy, our differential slipped into rhinology territory – *deciduoid outtakes* versus *pseudocystic degenerating fibro‑tums*.

So everyone still thought “adnexal”—until laparoscopy turned out an enormous pedunculated fibroid dripping blood into its own twisted stalk – the puzzle piece that made us all worry relief about…watching it grow vs watching it collapse.



## Red Flags That Nudged Us Toward Surgery

1. **Intense bilateral pain & mechanical dyspnoea**  
   - VAS 5/10 in two quadrants simultaneously is not “just a flare.”  
   - Compression of diaphragm ➜ impaired maternal ventilation ➜ potential fetal hypoxia if it worsened further along pregnancy.
2. **Torsion / necrosis = life‑threatening emergency**  
   - Haemodynamic changes + ascending fever + rising WCC suggested an inflamed pedicle draining neutrophils into peritoneum—typical precursors of shock if left unattended.
3. **Rising CA‑125 trend (+ bacteriuria)**  
   - While CA‑125 often peaks early/later trimesters, any rise paired with infection raises suspicion that something isn’t benign.
4. **Anticipated obstetric compromise**  
   - A massive centimetric intramural belly will limit uterine expansion later in gestation → risk of preterm contraction or placental insufficiency → forced preterm delivery complicated by hemorrhage.



In short: symptomatic massive fibroids *with* signs of degeneration/necrosis are “elective emergencies.” Delay means you're hurting mom AND baby.

### Bottom line?

Large symptomatic fibroids during second trimester demand *multidisciplinary urgency*. Absent robust guidelines mean every centre must build its own consensus bundle mapping red flags such as pain severity, ascitic trends, pedicular necrosis evidence, and fever/infection markers against gestational age safety margins so decisions feel like science more than guesswork.
