Title: Blog about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

# Cystic Crossroads  
**Differentiating Endometriomas From Leiomyomas With Ultrasound And Non‑Contrast MRI**

---

## 👀 Why This Case Stumped Us

Picture it: a nulliparous woman at ~21 weeks gestation groans over a massive left upper abdomen—pain sharp enough for her visu analogue score (VAS) 5—and breathlessness so potent it almost feels like a congenital lung disease rather than a uterine oesophageal stretch injury. Two gigantic unilocular cysts sit where your ovaries should—one dwarfs the left upper quadrant all the way up to the fundus, the second blooms right into the cul-de-sac beside what will soon be a full-term baby’s head—yet their true origin is anything but obvious until you go under the knife **and discover one is an atypical pedunculated leiomyoma twisting itself into hemorrhagic necrosis**! That twist not only explains her fever and anemia but also steered definitive management away from a routine “remove ovarian cyst” mentality toward carefully balancing life‑saving surgery against preserving an ongoing pregnancy.

## 🎚️ Imaging Jeopardy ― Lessons From Ultrasound

Top‑down bowel obstetrics UI would usually not gamely delimit *which* side epithelium or muscle architecture constitutes your culprit—especially when maternal size issues mask ovarian geometry—or something like this could easily mislead even a seasoned sonographer into characterized “decidualised endometrioma.” Here’s what you need to factor into your differential when seeing two massive lesions in pregnancy:

| Feature | Classic Endometrioma | Vanishing Leiomyoma ‑ Degeneration |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Wall Thickness** | Usually ≤5–10 mm; simple smooth contour | May be ≥10–15 mm if infarction/adpated | 
| **Internal echoes** | “Shading” signal – low-level heterogeneous echoes from old haemorrhage | More superimposed heterogeneity from red‑cell debris / mucinous fluid |
| **Vascularity** | Peripheral color Doppler pooling mainly tiny venules; minimal arterial flow | Often peripherally prominent arterial pulsations intruding through thickness |
| **Calcification** | Very rare unless old rupture ensues | Occurs especially as subserosal borders harden over time |

In our patient’s pitfall case:
* Both lesions had peripheral flow—but overall intense feeders suggested trophogenic activity more typical of *leiomyomata*.  
* Thick borders (~14 mm) were not what we'd expect from an “endometriotic” phenotype dragging around primordial ovaries alone.)

## 🔎 Low‑Field Non‑Contrast MRI — A Safeguard During Pregnancy

Do remember **gadolinium** is absolutely off‑limits unless you have no alternative after 28 weeks' gestation—so our go‐to becomes *T1/T2 fat‑sat sequences at ≤ 1.5 T*. What did we glean clinically?

```
MRI Features:
      • Cortical '; Wall thickening = 14 mm          │   ➜   Fired 
      • Focal hyperintense T2‑cluster = ‘fibrocollagenous core’ 
          ↔    Subtle absorption by inflammation/necrosis           •     
      • Lack of large venous ‘signal void’ cluster indicates   
          no massive thrombotic component << below threshold >>

```

If you see:
* **“Very bright” T1 signals** consistent with chronic blood → favour *endometriosis*.
* In contrast our scan yielded **high T2 areas mixed with moderate-intensity foci**, portraying internal firm tissue surrounded by necrotic fluid — exactly what you’d anticipate from a miO―ology undergoing liquefaction and thrombosis *– basically your changed pedunculated grow into itchy latex.*

## 💉 Operative Decision Kit ― When Does It Pay Off?

In pregnant patients surgical breech might feel akin playing Russian roulette final round—but let’s parse out why earlier laser action saved *both womb & airway* here.

### Gestational Window
* Earlier second trimester grants anesthetic control while uterus is still malleable yet below emergence risks inherent preterm parturition.
* Avoids surge-friendly third-trimester hemorrhagic shift that can compromise antegrade UFDF arterial reserve *and new-onset intrauterine hypertension.*

### Risk Stratification
| Surrogate marker                             | Threshold                        |
|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Size ≥15 cm + symptomatic                  | High probability surgery         |
| Symptomatic h/o weight/distress           >   ±anthere region Fighter(s)   |

### Essential Exigencies
* Untethered pedunculated volvulus → potential infraction-and-rebleeding loop  
* Fever + anemia --> Infective/peripheral oxygen deficit states +
                CI sensitivity deficits -> ACS bedscarpop adaptation.
              
### Aftermath Care  
Prophylactic progesterone started intraoperatively maintains uterine quiescence while antibiotics guard against organism translocates across abdominomorph skeleton=→ postpartum infection risk reduced dramatically⟹CK correlated obstetron 😉

## ✅ Clinical Pearls – Take Home Messages

• Employ low field (< 1.5 T) non‑contrast MRI beyond first trimester safely when US finds large unilocular adnexal lesions whose demarcation is unclear!  

• Thick (>10 mm) pigmented walls plus discrete internal solid foci favor *degenerating subserosal/pedunculated *leio­myo­mas over simple endometrio­lomatous cysts.*  

• Color Doppler peripheral pulsatile vessels are more likely an artefact of myogenic feeding rather than living globular drop ­pattern obviously identical relative area crescents − think about vessel architecture!  

• Beware CA‑125 climbs during first trimester + third-trimester spikes unrelated to proper malignancy – look heavy trends vs baseline instead!  

• Open courtesy – Keep care macroscopically focused when balancing parametrium compressive pathology vs birthing demands — supportive bimanual exam + minimal laparotomy provided most perfect therapeutic dosage!  

• Preserving less becoming best idea – Leaving pitch catalyzed oophorospinal metastasis often yields better outcomes than conquering all lesions across uterus impetus `intramural` risk profile!

#### Bottom Line

Large abdominal gestational cystic masses routinely masquerade as giant ovarian formations or even huge tumours ready for laparoscopy—to avoid iatrogenic harms keep ductal confidence high using validated ultrasound phenotypes coupled with safe non‑enhanced MR imaging cortical patterns; treat only those whose morbidity outweighs rhythm-less physiologic gestation clearance—and always ditch one antibiotic culture while sustaining meticulous fetal monitoring throughout any operative venture!
