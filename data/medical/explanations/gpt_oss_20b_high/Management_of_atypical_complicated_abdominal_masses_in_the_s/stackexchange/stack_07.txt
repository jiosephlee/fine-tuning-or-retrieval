Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Morning Report] "During laparotomy for myomectomy in the second trimester," what measures are recommended to maintain fetal viability intra‑operatively?

**Clinical Teaching Note – Morning Report**

**Question:** *During laparotomy for myomectomy in the second trimester, what measures are recommended to maintain fetal viability intra‑operatively?*  

**Answer**

1. **Baseline Fetal Assessment Before Incision**  
   * Verify evidence of active cardiac activity by trans‑abdominal Doppler ultrasound (or CTG if available). Record interval heart rate so any later change can be attributed to the procedure itself.

2. **Continuous Fetal Monitoring During Surgery**  
   * Repeat bedside ultrasound every 20–30 min (or continuous CTG in centers that can do so).  
   * Look for bradycardia (<110 beats/min), variation >25 bpm on CTG, or cessation of beats – each is an immediate red flag requiring rapid intervention (e.g., passively warmed return of blood volume).

3. **Maintain Maternal Hemodynamics**  
   * Keep systolic BP ≥90 mmHg (goal 100–110 mmHg) with isotonic crystalloids or colloid preload.  
   * Use vasopressors that spare uterine perfusion—phenylephrine is preferred over norepinephrine/epinephrine if rapid titration is needed.  
   * Monitor central venous pressure or pulse pressure variation when possible; avoid over‑resuscitation that could provoke uterine overdistension or hypervolemia‑induced premature contractions.

4. **Oxygenation & Thermoregulation**  
   * Target SpO₂ >95 % with supplemental O₂ during induction and throughout the case.  
   * Maintain normothermia using warmed IV fluids, active warming blankets, and warming ambient room temperatures (~24 °C). Hypothermia reduces metabolic demand and can precipitate bradycardia in the fetus.

5. **Anesthetic Considerations**  
   * Prefer a balanced anesthetic with minimal opioids and short‑acting muscle relaxants to preserve spontaneous respiration and minimal systemic depressant effect on the fetus.  
   * Avoid agents known to provoke uterine contraction or vasoconstriction unless clinically mandated (e.g., routine oxytocin bolus should be avoided unless vaginal delivery or abortion is imminent).  

6. **Surgical Technique & Duration**  
   * Keep the procedure brief (<3 h when feasible). The reported case achieved a 2 h 25 min operation by careful clamping and suture ligation of the pedunculated leiomyoma while leaving the pelvic mass intact to reduce distention of the abdomen—all factors supporting favorable fetal outcomes.  

7. **Administer Antenatal Prophylaxis When Indicated**  
   * The authors administered intramuscular progesterone intra‑operatively and continued daily until delivery—an established strategy for maintaining a short cervix or preventing early preterm labor when miscarriage risk exists during obstetric procedures in late pregnancy (≥20 weeks).  

8. **Post‑Incision Scan Before Closure**  
   * Re‑check fetal heart rate immediately before abdominal closure—to confirm no iatrogenic compromise occurred during dissection or manipulation of major vessels adjacent to uterus.

9. **Documentation & Immediate Post‑op Care**  
   * Record all baseline/final FHRs, hemodynamic parameters, anesthesia drugs, fluid balance, and any episodes of tachycardia/bradycardia.  
   * In the postoperative period re‑check fetal status at 24 h plus daily until delivery due to potential delayed decompensation following a major abdominal operation.

**Key Point:** In this 21⁺⁶‑week gestation patient a successful laparotomy with myomectomy required meticulous maternal stabilization paired with real‑time fetal monitoring at multiple time points — pre‑incision verification of cardiac activity, intermittent checks throughout surgery, and a post‑closure scan — combined with judicious fluid/vasopressor management and avoidance of procontraction agents—all measures upheld in this case’s outcome of an uncomplicated elective cesarean at 37 weeks for a vaginal birth‐obstruction risk from residual myoma.*
