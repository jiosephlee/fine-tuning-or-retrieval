Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What aspects of the ultrasound and non‑contrast MRI findings led clinicians to favor a myomatous origin for the caudal mass rather than an adnexal lesion?

**Key imaging clues that point to a myomatous (fibroid) origin for the caudal pelvic mass**

| **Ultrasound** | **Non‑contrast MRI** | **Why it favors a leiomyoma** |
|-----------------|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| • The lower 9.5 × 8 × 8 cm lesion is **adherent** to a posterior intramural‑subserosal uterine myoma (58 × 71 mm) rather than arising from an ovary or tube. | • The “cleavage” between the caudal cyst and adjacent structures is unclear – the lesion appears *continuous* with the posterior uterine wall. | • A true adnexal mass usually has a discrete hilum or a pedunculated stalk; continuity suggests it is part of the uterus. |
| • Regularly circumscribed margins with a **thickened wall** and an inner cystic cavity surrounded by peripheral **vascularity** on colour Doppler. | • The lesion has a 1.4‑cm thick rim that is hypointense on T2‑fat‑saturated images and contains foci that are hyperintense on both T1 and T2 fat‑saturated sequences – classic “fibrous nodules” seen in degenerated fibroids. | • Adnexal cysts (e.g., endometriomas) show homogeneous low‑signal fluid on T1 or uniform high‑T2 signal without internal solid elements strongly linked to fibrous tissue. |
| • No distinct ovarian hilum or identifiable ovarian vessels seen within the bulk of the lesion. | • No separate adnexal organ or “ovarian capsule” is visualized; the enhancement pattern (solid parts still iso‑to–hypointense on T2, no rim enhancement because contrast is omitted) is consistent with interstitial fibroids rather than cystic ovaries or tumors. | • The absence of an ovarian capsule plus continuity with uterine muscle fibers shifts differential from ovary toward uterine sarco‑fibroid structures. |
| • Peripheral vascular pattern typical for subserosal fibroids rather than the richer, central plexus seen in many ovarian masses. | • On T2–fat‑sat imaging, fluid within the degenerated portion is markedly inhomogeneous – degenerative changes such as hyaline or red degeneration cause this pattern more commonly in leiomyomas than in simple epithelial ovarian cysts. | • Degenerative changes are rarely seen in uncomplicated adnexal lesions without underlying neoplastic atypia.* |

**Bottom line:** In this case both US and MRI demonstrated an intimate relationship between the caudal lesion and the posterior uterine wall without a clear separation plane or discrete adnexal structure—a hallmark of a cystically degenerating subserosal leiomyoma rather than an independent adnexal tumor such as an endometrioma or ovarian cyst.*
