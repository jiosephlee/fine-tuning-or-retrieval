Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] Which imaging modalities are considered safe and provide the most accurate assessment of large abdominal masses in a second‑trimester pregnant patient?

**Imaging strategy for a large abdomin­pelvic mass in the second trimester**

The most reliable and safest approach combines **(1) transabdominal/transvaginal ultrasonography with color‑doppler** and **(2) non‑contrast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** performed at ≥ 1.5 T after the first trimester.

- **Ultrasound** is the immediate first‐line study because it is rapid, widely available and free of ionising radiation. Colour‑doppler augments its utility by revealing intralesional vascularity—essential for distinguishing vascular leiomyomas from cystic lesions such as endometriomas or germ‑cell tumours. In the reported case ultrasonography identified two massive unilocular cysts and raised doubts about their origin, prompting further investigation.
  
- **Non‑contrast MRI** delivers superior soft‐tissue contrast and multiplanar capability without any gadolinium exposure (contraindicated during pregnancy). It precisely defines lesion extent relative to the gravid uterus, identifies solid components or haemorrhagic degeneration, and clarifies continuity with uterine myometrium—as confirmed in the case where MRI confirmed a caudal mass adherent to an intramural-subserosal myoma. Current safety data support 1–3 T magnetic fields after 12 weeks gestation.

Other modalities such as CT remain contraindicated owing to fetal ionising radiation unless mandatory for urgent surgical planning (in which case extreme dose optimisation would be required). Thus, **ultrasound plus non‑enhanced MRI** constitute the gold standard pair for evaluating large abdominal masses safely in second‑trimester pregnancy.
