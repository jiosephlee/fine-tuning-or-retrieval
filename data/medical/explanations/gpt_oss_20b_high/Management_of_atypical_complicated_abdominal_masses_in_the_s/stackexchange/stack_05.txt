Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] "When is surgical removal of a uterine fibroid indicated during pregnancy?" Discuss the risk factors and clinical scenarios that justify operation despite potential obstetric risks.

**Clinical Pearls – When is a fibroid removed during pregnancy?**

| **Scenario** | **Why intervene?** | **Key Points from this Case** |
|--------------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| **Complication‐driven pain / dyspnea** | Rapid enlargement or torsion produces ischemia and necrosis → abdominal pain, fever, leukocytosis → imminent rupture or sepsis → threat to both mother and fetus | 21‑week patient had severe epigastric and pelvic pain + dyspnea from a 18 × 12 × 7 cm pedunculated leiomyoma that was twisted and leaking – causing mild fever and a rising CRP |
| **Torsion / impending rupture** | Twisted pedicle cuts arterial perfusion → tissue necrosis → inflammation → potential hemorrhage → preterm delivery | The surgeons noted a “twisted” stalk on intra‑op exploration—prompting immediate excision before irreversible damage |
| **Infection / septic change** | Necrotic tissue becomes secondarily infected → systemic signs → maternal morbidity | Urine culture grew *Klebsiella*, reinforcing the urgency of removing a potentially infected myoma |
| **Suspicion of malignancy** | Progressive growth + solid‑cystic features + CA‑125 rise > 35 U/mL | The upper lesion had focal hyperintense solid components on MRI + a rising CA‑125 (.841 U/mL above 2‑week baseline); although benign pathology was later confirmed, this trend triggered operative exploration |
| **Mass effect limiting fetal growth / future labour** | Large intramural masses can curtail uterine expansion ⇒ small-for‑gestational‑age infant + obstructed labour | The caudal posterior lesion would have impeded fetal head descent; hence an elective cesarean was planned once fetal maturity was reached |

---

### Decision Algorithm In Practice

1. **Early assessment**
   * Detailed US ± non‑contrast MRI (first trimester ≤ 1.5 T); look for stalks, necrotic zones, solid components.
   * Serial tumour markers only useful if trends diverge from physiologic pregnancy peaks (e.g., CA‑125 rising beyond normal second‑trimester medians).

2. **Risk stratification**
   * Size ≥ 5 cm or fibroid diameter > 10 cm × location = ↑ risk for torsion/rupture.
   * Pedunculated subserosal lesions especially prone to twisting.
   * Intramural lesions abutting placenta/uterus fundus limit expansion ↓ fut congenital smallness/obstruction risk.
   * Maternal factors—age > 35 yrs, obesity (BMI>30) increase symptom burden & morbidity risk profiles.

3. **Timing & modality**
   * Avoid elective myomectomy after 20 weeks unless life‑threatening symptom or strong malignancy suspicion.
   * First/early second trimester favor laparoscopic approach for small subserosal/pedunculated lesions (< 5 cm).
   * Open laparotomy recommended for large (> 8 cm), multiple or intramural masses where laparoscopy risks excessive blood loss or inability to clamp vessel safely—as seen in this case’s 18‑cm pedunculated myoma.
   * Continuous intraoperative fetal heart rate monitoring is mandatory if uterus approached between 16–22 weeks [Zhao et al].

4. **Perioperative measures**
   * Antepartum prophylactic high‑dose progesterone post‑op reduces post‑myomectomy preterm birth rates [Sakinci et al].
   * Antimicrobial coverage tailored to culture results reinforces infection control given vulnerable status post‑torsion/necrosis.

---

### Take‑Home Points

* **Symptomatic complications**—especially torsion or infection—are clear indications for surgery even during mid‑pregnancy despite obstetric risks.
* **Malignancy suspicion** through imaging features plus aberrant tumour marker kinetics justifies operative evaluation often earliest possible.
* When surgery is performed, it should be *targeted*—only removing the threatening lesion(s)—and planned with multidisciplinary equipoise between minimizing maternal trauma and preserving gestation.
* Excellent outcomes can be achieved when operative intervention alleviates mechanical compression while allowing pregnancy continuation—as demonstrated by this patient’s uneventful delivery at term via cesarean after residual fibroid regression postnatal period.
