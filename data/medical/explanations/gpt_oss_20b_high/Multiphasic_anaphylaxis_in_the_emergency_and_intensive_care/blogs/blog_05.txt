# Tryptase Timings Matter — Best Practices for Sampling Triggers Without Delaying Urgent Treatment  

When you arrive at an ER gateboy’s doorway shouting **“Epi now!”**, your mind spikes white‑with‑adrenaline fireball­ly-fast – despite that everything else seems secondary. Yet often what sits right behind that tonic bomb is a single vial of blood that can tell us whether this lady has an underlying mast‑cell disorder screaming while she’s already being treated piecewise through oxygen and IV fluid rails – the very same patient who came back over six episodes later clutching her throat during another flare-up after qualifying hyper‑tryptasemia was finally discovered weeks later only because her **baseline wasn’t captured early enough**.

In our multicenter audit paper “Multiphasic Anaphylaxis…” we ran into exactly that problem—a woman who had just one tryptase nailed way too late during her third attack and clinicians were forced to **re‑interpret two surrogates:** what was “baseline” versus what was “peak.” It’s a reminder that picking the doorbell rings fast goes hand‑in‑hand with selecting the exact hemodynamic windows to empty your coffined bucket called blood drawing equipment!
