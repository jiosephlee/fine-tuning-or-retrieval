Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

Chapter 5: ## Outcomes, Follow‑Up & Clinical Significance

At presentation the patient’s glycated haemoglobin (HbA₁c) was markedly elevated at 10.2 % (88 mmol/mol), with fasting plasma glucose of 12.4 mmol/L and an abnormal two‑hour OGTT peak of 19.8 mmol/L. Twelve days after initiating a baseline regimen that combined basal insulin degludec (8 U/24 h), metformin (1500 mg/day) and a DPP‑4 inhibitor (saxagliptin 5 mg/day), his HbA₁c had fallen to 9.1 %. The rapid decline within merely four weeks reflected good adherence but also indicated that residual β‑cell function was still present—a finding reinforced by a stimulated C‑peptide of 0.83 nmol/L during glucagon testing and negative islet autoantibodies. These data prompted cessation of insulin once HNF1A‑MODY was confirmed genetically; insulinemic therapy was replaced by once‑weekly dulaglutide (GLP‑1 receptor agonist) while maintaining metformin and saxagliptin.

Over the next five months on dulaglutide monotherapy plus background oral agents, the patient’s glycaemic control continued improving steadily—HbA₁c reached 6.0 % at month six post‑switch—demonstrating near–normoglycaemia without hypoglycaemic episodes or adverse reactions such as nausea commonly associated with GLP‑1 RA initiation. Given the sustained target attainment and a return interval exceeding three months between doses, therapy was subsequently simplified further by eliminating dulaglutide in favour of a dual oral regimen (metformin 1500 mg/day + saxagliptin 5 mg/day). At present the patient remains well controlled with an average HbA₁c <6.5 %, no microalbuminuria or retinopathy detected on baseline examination, and normal weight status (BMI 24.6 kg/m²).

From a precision‑medicine perspective this clinical course underscores several key lessons:

1. **Genotype-guided drug selection** – In HNF1A‑MODY sulfonylureas are traditionally first line because they directly stimulate residual β‑cell ATP‐sensitive K⁺ channels. However, GLP‑1 receptor agonists offer comparable efficacy with a lower hypoglycaemia risk profile—essential when β‑cell reserve is already diminished—as exemplified by our patient’s uncomplicated transition from basal insulin+oral agents to dulaglutide alone without hypoglycaemic episodes or renal side effects commonly observed in type 2 diabetes cohorts exposed to SGLT₂ inhibitors.

2. **Early genetic confirmation expedites treatment optimisation** – If HNF1A mutation had been delayed until after successful glycaemic response to basal–bolus insulin, unnecessary exposure to exogenous insulin would have continued because clinicians might still be uncertain whether endogenous β‑cell secretion could be preserved long enough for oral therapy alone—a scenario avoided here by early next‑generation sequencing shorted both cost per patient and time away from evidence‐based pharmacotherapy tailored for MODY physiology.

3. **Cascade screening potential** – Identification of the novel p.N270S pathogenic variant provides actionable information for family members who share the same autosomal dominant inheritance pattern; proactive monitoring can prevent late presentation with uncontrolled disease among siblings or unaffected relatives carrying this mutation.

4. **Evidence generation for rare subtypes** – The clinical response documented herein adds real‐world efficacy data supporting GLP‑1 RA usage beyond conventional type 2 diabetes trials—data valuable for refining therapeutic algorithms used in future consensus guidelines for MODY subtypes where evidence is sparse.

In summary, this case demonstrates how timely molecular diagnosis can transform therapeutic strategy—from an initial multi‑agent basal insulin approach toward a targeted pharmacologic regimen that normalises glucose control safely while preserving patient quality of life—and illustrates broader applicability across other monogenic diabetes entities where tailored precision medicine offers tangible benefits over empiric stepwise approaches typical of type 2 diabetes management.[citation needed]
