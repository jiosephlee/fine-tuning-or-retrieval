Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

Chapter 4: ## Management Strategy & Therapeutic Decisions

The therapeutic journey in this case illustrates how a clinical strategy grounded in initial empirical control can evolve into a precision‑guided regimen once the underlying genetics are clarified. The care pathway progressed through three distinct phases—baseline rapid‑control therapy presumed for type 2 diabetes (T2D), transition to a GLP‑1 receptor agonist–driven approach following molecular confirmation of HNF1A‑MODY, and finally simplification of the regimen while maintaining tight glycaemic control.

### Initial Intensive Regimens Employing Basal Degludec Plus Oral Agents Utilised Prior Diagnostic Clarification

At presentation, the young adult displayed markedly elevated fasting plasma glucose (12.4 mmol/L), an HbA1c of 10.2 % and a 2‑hour post‑OGTT glucose of 19.8 mmol/L—all severe biochemical markers typical of both type I and type II diabetes when viewed alone. Although negative islet autoantibodies suggested a non‑type I phenotype, the rapid hyperglycaemia combined with low stimulated C‑peptide (0.83 nmol/L) warranted aggressive early control before definitive diagnostic conclusions could be reached. Pending confirmatory studies, clinicians adopted a conventional T2D paradigm:

* **Basal Insulin Degludec** – Eight units daily addressed fasting hyperglycaemia and provided broad coverage over a steady state period.
* **Metformin** – At a dose of 1500 mg/day it enhanced hepatic insulin sensitivity and suppressed gluconeogenesis.
* **Saxagliptin** – A DPP‑4 inhibitor that prolongs endogenous GLP‑1 activity offered an additional stimulus for glucose‑dependent insulin secretion without increasing hypoglycaemic risk.

This tripartite approach was intended not only to lower blood glucose rapidly but also to minimise endogenous glucagon elevation—a key driver of hepatic glucose output in poorly controlled diabetes mellitus—while preserving remaining β‑cell function within the first critical weeks following diagnosis.

One month later the patient’s HbA1c had fallen modestly from 10.2 % to 9.1 %, indicating partial efficacy but also raising questions about optimal long‑term strategy given his family history and negative autoimmunity panel.

### Transition Following Genetic Confirmation: From Basal Insulin to GLP‑1 Receptor Agonist Therapy

Genetic evaluation identified a heterozygous p.N270S mutation in HNF1A—a pathogenic variant situated within the DNA‑binding domain responsible for regulating genes essential to β‑cell function (e.g., GLUT2, pyruvate kinase). Understanding that HNF1A mutations compromise insulin gene transcription and compromise β‑cell responsiveness led clinicians toward therapies that augment residual β‑cell capacity rather than impeding it or forcing exogenous insulin administration indefinitely.

The decision thus was dual:

1. **Discontinuation of Basal Insulin** – Given that β‑cell secretory reserve remained evident (stimulated C‑peptide above 0.6 nmol/L), continued exogenous insulinemia was deemed unnecessary once adequate homeostasis could be achieved.
   
2. **Initiation of Dulaglutide** – A long‑acting GLP‑1 receptor agonist administered once weekly offers several mechanistic advantages in HNF1A‑MODY:
   * Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion via cAMP pathways independent of K_ATP channels that are dysfunctional in these patients.
   * Suppresses postprandial glucagon release without provoking hypoglycaemia.
   * Promotes weight neutrality or modest loss—potentially beneficial against polyphagia observed at baseline.
   * May exert direct cytoprotective effects on islet cells by upregulating anti‑apoptotic genes mediated by PDX‐single protein interaction pathways altered by HNF1A loss.

Within three months under dulaglutide alone (plus adjunctive metformin remaining), the patient’s HbA1c plummeted from 9.1 % down to an impressive 6.0 %, illustrating the superior match between drug mechanism and disease biology afforded by precision medicine.

### Long-Term Maintenance and Simplification 

After sustained improvement over six months—confirmed by consistent HbA01 levels below 6½ %—clinicians considered further simplification without compromising metabolic control or patient convenience:

* **Metformin** remained as it continues to address hepatic steatosis risk indicated by elevated triglycerides (4.31 mmol/L) while still supportive against ectopic fat deposition contributing to impaired β-cell function.
  
* **Saxagliptin** was retained at its initial dose; its modest effect on incretin turnover effectively loops into maintaining baseline postprandial regulation now that glucagon secreting goes dampened physiologically under dulaglutide washout influence.
  
GLP‑1 agonists were withdrawn given tolerable adverse effect profile under their controlled dosing schedule such that long-term maintenance could rely on oral twinstones without exposing patients toward injection burden or rare pancreatitis risk associated with chronic parenteral therapy at high pharmacologic concentrations only transiently employed during race around identification of pathogenesis.

The resulting regimen—metformin plus saxagliptin—kept the patient achieving below target HbA01 readings while significantly simplifying life‐style adherence dynamics, illustrating an evolution from empiric intense therapy toward a judicious treatment plan fine-tuned by disease genotype knowledge.

### Concluding Perspective

Initially adopting a broad T2D aggressive control schema ensured prompt reduction of potentially ketosis-prone hyperglycemia until diagnostic certainty awaited genome sequencing results.
