Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

Chapter 3: ## Genetic Pathogenesis & Variant Interpretation

Hepatocyte nuclear factor‑1α (HNF‑1α) is a transcription factor encoded by **HNF 1A**, located on chromosome 12 q24. It possesses an N‑terminal dimerization/dna‑binding motif followed by a carboxy‑terminal transactivation domain that harbors one or more activation‐repression motifs modularly engaged through interaction partners such as co‑activators p300/TATA binding protein complexes identified mainly within pancreatic β cells, hepatic hepatocytes, renal tubules and intestinal mucosae.  
The mutated protein in this patient substitutes an alanine residue at nucleotide 809 (c.809 A> > G) generating p.Asn 270→Ser located inside the DNA‑binding helix–turn–helix region—a ∼15 % consensus motif highly conserved across vertebrates (>96 % identity between human Amniota) which directly contacts the consensus “GTTC” sequence upstream of target promoters [e.g., INS, GLUT‑2/SLC2A2 and PKLR]. Loss‐of‐function mutations in this segment impede chromatin opening or primer recruitment both downstream signaling events critical for early life β‑cell differentiation and mature glucose‐stimulated insulin release pathways [18]. Consequently patients develop both basal hyperglycaemia—as insulin transcription reaches residual cellular levels—and marked post‑prandial excursions because NO-mediated GLUT −02 uptake becomes blunted [19]. In renal proximal tubules dysregulated SGLT − 2 expression explains intrarenal hypo‑threshold glucose reabsorption responsible for persistent glycosuria seen before plasma levels exceed the traditional range ≥180 mg/dL [20].

The clinical phenotype observed—in particular pronounced fasting hyperglycaemia at age 24 coupled with normal body mass index (BMI ≈24 kg/m²), lack of autoantibodies against GADA/Ia₂/ZnT8 glands, progressively impaired C‑peptide responses after glucagon despite relatively preserved residual secretion (<0·8 nmol/L)—reads coherently against an autoimmune process and fits well into an autosomal dominant monogenic framework designated maturity‑onset diabetes of youth (MODY). Most notable is the family pedigree reporting maternal grandmother, mother and younger brother diagnosed between ages 30–20 years—three generations harboring identical disease characteristics—this stands against sporadic type Ⅱ diabetes whereas childbearing anonymity suggests dominant inheritance [21].

To formally address whether the variation c.*809 A> > G qualifies as “pathogenic,” American College Of Medical Genetics (ACMG) criteria were applied systematically:

| Criterion | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **PM2** absent from gnomAD South Asian population (>280 k exomes) | shows rarity ― supportive |
| **PP3** Multiple computational tools (PolyPhen‑2 = probably damaging = score 0·99; SIFT = damaging) coupled with an AlphaMissense score 0·995 | establishes predicted deleteriousness ‑ strong support |
| **PS4** Co‑segregation with diabetes across three generations | statistical enrichment beyond chance — moderate/strong depending on family size |
| **PM5** Missense change affecting an amino acid residue within a canonical functional domain documented among other PD variants | additional plausibility |

Combining PM2 + PP3 + PS4 yields a **likely pathogenic** designation per ACMG short codes (“LP”), comfortably distinguishing it from “variant of uncertain significance.” The absence from ClinVar/VarSome provides additional novelty credit yet does not alter status slated toward actionable testing policies endorsed by most laboratories implementing MODY screening panels [22].

In practice this classification has therapeutic import: Established guidelines advocate first-line sulfonylureas—owing their K_ATP channel agonism—as effective because residual insulin machinery remains intact until later stages [23]. However our patient’s baseline glucagon-stimulated C‑peptide was modest (~0·83 nmol/L); GLP‑‑R agonists were therefore selected after confirmatory genetics given their lesser hypoglycaemic profile while preserving β‑cell integrity—a strategy supported by double-blind crossover data showing comparable glycaemic improvement versus glimepiride yet lower incidence of shunt-related hypoglycaemic events [24].
