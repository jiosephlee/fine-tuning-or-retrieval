Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] What are the preferred first-line and alternative glucose-lowering therapies in HNF1A-MODY, when might GLP-1 receptor agonists be favored over sulfonylureas, and why should SGLT2 inhibitors be used cautiously (or avoided) in this genotype?

First-line therapy
- Low‑dose sulfonylureas are the classic first‑line oral therapy for HNF1A‑MODY. These patients have a primary defect of insulin secretion but intact KATP‑channel responsiveness, so small doses of sulfonylureas often produce large glucose‑lowering effects and can maintain glycaemic control for years.

When to use alternatives (and which)
- GLP‑1 receptor agonists (GLP‑1 RAs) are a well‑supported alternative and are increasingly favored when there is:
  - progressive decline in β‑cell reserve (higher hypoglycaemia risk with sulfonylureas),
  - obesity or need for weight loss, or
  - a clinical preference to avoid frequent hypoglycaemia.
  A randomized crossover trial cited in the report found similar glycaemic efficacy between liraglutide and glimepiride in HNF1A‑MODY but substantially less hypoglycaemia with liraglutide.
- Metformin is appropriate as adjunctive therapy when features of insulin resistance or metabolic syndrome are present (as in this case) but is not genotype‑specific therapy.
- DPP‑4 inhibitors are modest alternatives when GLP‑1 RAs are not tolerable or contraindicated, though their potency is less.
- Insulin is indicated acutely for severe hyperglycaemia, DKA, or when endogenous insulin secretion is exhausted; after genetic diagnosis many patients can often be transitioned off insulin (as in this case where insulin was stopped and dulaglutide + metformin achieved A1c 6.0%).

Why GLP‑1 RAs may be favored over sulfonylureas
- Mechanism: GLP‑1 RAs stimulate glucose‑dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon and promote weight loss — reducing hypoglycaemia risk compared with sulfonylureas that stimulate insulin regardless of ambient glucose.
- Clinical tradeoffs: sulfonylureas are inexpensive and highly effective early, but cause more hypoglycaemia and may accelerate β‑cell exhaustion; GLP‑1 RAs preserve better safety (less hypoglycaemia), cardiovascular/weight benefits in many patients, and were effective in the presented patient (dulaglutide + metformin reduced A1c from 9.1% → 6.0%).

Why SGLT2 inhibitors should be used cautiously or avoided
- Pathophysiology: HNF1A is a transcriptional regulator of SGLT2 in the proximal tubule. Loss‑of‑function HNF1A mutations reduce SGLT2 expression and lower the renal glucose threshold, causing baseline glycosuria.
- Clinical consequences of adding an SGLT2 inhibitor:
  - Exaggerated glycosuria and volume depletion (risk of marked dehydration),
  - Increased risk of genital infections,
  - Reported cases of euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and severe dehydration in HNF1A‑MODY when SGLT2 activity is already low.
- Efficacy may also be diminished because the target protein (SGLT2) is downregulated.
- Therefore, the potential harms (volume depletion, DKA) often outweigh modest incremental glycaemic benefits in this genotype; if considered, use extreme caution, ensure adequate insulin reserve, counsel about ketone monitoring, and avoid during intercurrent illness or if insulin is being withdrawn.

Practical clinical pearls
- Suspect HNF1A‑MODY with young onset, strong autosomal dominant family history, glycosuria out of proportion to hyperglycaemia, negative autoantibodies and preserved C‑peptide.
- Start low‑dose sulfonylurea as first-line in many patients, but choose GLP‑1 RA when hypoglycaemia risk, weight, or cardiovascular considerations favor it — and particularly when β‑cell function is waning.
- Use metformin when insulin resistance coexists; reserve insulin for severe presentation and reassess after genetic confirmation.
- Avoid routine use of SGLT2 inhibitors in HNF1A‑MODY because of impaired SGLT2 expression and documented risks of dehydration and DKA; if used, do so only with careful counselling and close monitoring.
- The case illustrates these principles: initial insulin for severe hyperglycaemia, genetic diagnosis prompted transition to dulaglutide + metformin with excellent glycaemic response, and the authors appropriately caution against SGLT2 inhibitors for this genotype.
