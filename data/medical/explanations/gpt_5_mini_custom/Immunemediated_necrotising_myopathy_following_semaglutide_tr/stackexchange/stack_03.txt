Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Which muscle-biopsy histopathologic and immunohistochemical findings are characteristic of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy and help differentiate it from polymyositis or dermatomyositis (e.g., necrotic/regenerating fibers, macrophage-rich myophagocytosis, scant lymphocytes, membrane attack complex deposition)?

Answer:

The diagnostic muscle‑biopsy signature of immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) is dominated by myofibre necrosis and regeneration with a macrophage‑predominant inflammatory response and only scant T‑lymphocytes — in contrast to the lymphocyte‑driven patterns seen in polymyositis (PM) or dermatomyositis (DM).

Key histopathologic and IHC features that point to IMNM and help distinguish it from PM or DM

- Prominent scattered necrotic and regenerating fibres as the predominant lesion (rather than perifascicular atrophy or dense endomysial inflammation).
- Macrophage‑rich myophagocytosis: abundant CD68+ macrophages engulfing necrotic fibres; acid phosphatase positivity reflecting lysosomal activity. (Seen in this case: dispersed necrotic/regenerating fibres with CD68 and acid phosphatase staining and myophagocytosis.)
- Relative paucity of lymphocytes: only rare CD3+ T cells on IHC, and lack of the CD8+ endomysial cytotoxic T‑cell invasion of non‑necrotic fibres that defines PM. (This case showed rare CD3+ cells.)
- Deposition of membrane attack complex (C5b‑9, “MAC”) on the sarcolemma of necrotic and non‑necrotic myofibres — a marker of complement‑mediated myofibre injury commonly seen in IMNM. (This case demonstrated MAC positivity on necrotising and non‑necrotising fibres.)
- Diffuse upregulation of MHC‑class I on fibres is often present in IMNM (not specific, but supportive).
- Absence of features typical of dermatomyositis: lack of prominent perifascicular atrophy, absence of perivascular CD4+/B‑cell–predominant infiltrates and endothelial capillary loss; DM often shows complement deposition in endomysial capillaries and MxA expression in perifascicular fibres (not the pattern seen here).

Clinical correlation: IMNM often associates with anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP antibodies but up to ~30% are seronegative — so the biopsy pattern is essential for diagnosis and to guide immunotherapy. In this patient, the combination of necrotic/regenerating fibres, macrophage‑rich myophagocytosis, scant lymphocytes and sarcolemmal MAC deposition was diagnostic of IMNM and helped distinguish it from PM or DM.
