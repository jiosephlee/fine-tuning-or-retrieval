### [Pathophysiology] What are the 2022 WHO major and minor diagnostic criteria for systemic mastocytosis, and how can limited bone marrow tissue availability affect your ability to meet the major criterion?

The 2022 WHO diagnostic framework for systemic mastocytosis (SM) is unchanged in its two-step structure: one major criterion plus one minor criterion, or three minor criteria, are required to make the diagnosis.

The major criterion is demonstration, by histology, of multifocal dense aggregates of mast cells (classically defined as aggregates of ≥15 mast cells) in the bone marrow and/or in extracutaneous organs.

The four minor criteria are: (1) >25% of mast cells in the bone marrow or other extracutaneous organ showing atypical (often spindle-shaped) morphology or mast cell atypia on cytology; (2) detection of a pathogenic KIT mutation (most commonly D816V) in bone marrow, peripheral blood, or another extracutaneous organ; (3) aberrant immunophenotype of mast cells with expression of CD25 and/or CD2 (by flow cytometry or immunohistochemistry); and (4) a persistently elevated baseline serum tryptase level >20 ng/mL (provided there is no concomitant clonal myeloid neoplasm that would itself raise tryptase).

How limited bone‑marrow tissue affects the major criterion
The major criterion is a histopathologic one and therefore entirely dependent on obtaining an adequate marrow trephine (core) biopsy that samples enough marrow to detect focal, multifocal aggregates. A small or crushed core, a biopsy that misses involved areas, or reliance only on an aspirate smear can easily miss the characteristic dense mast cell clusters and therefore produce a false‑negative major criterion. In practical terms, an insufficient or non‑representative marrow specimen means you cannot confidently apply the major criterion and must rely on the minor criteria pathway.

Clinical consequences and approach
When tissue is limited, do not overcall absence of the major criterion; pursue the minor-criteria route. Obtain sensitive molecular testing for KIT D816V in peripheral blood (highly specific and may be detectable even when marrow is non‑diagnostic), perform flow cytometry/IHC for CD25/CD2 on any available marrow material, and document baseline tryptase (and repeat after recovery if levels were taken during/after anaphylaxis). If initial marrow is non‑diagnostic and clinical suspicion remains high, repeat a properly sized marrow trephine or sample an extracutaneous site (e.g., liver, lymph node) if involved. In short, inadequate marrow sampling can prevent fulfillment of the major criterion and should prompt additional molecular, immunophenotypic testing and repeat or alternative-site biopsy rather than concluding SM is absent.
