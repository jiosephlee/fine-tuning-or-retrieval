Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting

### [Management] Why can use of a 0.3 mg epinephrine auto-injector lead to underdosing in adults over 50 kg, and what clinical consequences have been associated with initial underdosing compared with 0.5 mg?

The recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose for anaphylaxis is 0.01 mg/kg up to a maximum of 0.5 mg. A 0.3 mg auto‑injector therefore delivers the target dose only up to a body weight of about 30 kg; for adults above ~50 kg it provides substantially less than  guideline dose (for a 70‑kg adult 0.3 mg = 0.0043 mg/kg, well below 0.01 mg/kg). In other words, a 0.3 mg autoinjector can produce a physiologic underdose in the typical adult because it fails to achieve the weight‑based therapeutic concentration needed to promptly reverse mast cell–mediated vasodilation, bronchospasm and edema.

Clinically, initial underdosing has been associated with need for escalation of care. Compared with patients who receive an initial 0.5 mg IM dose, those who initially receive 0.3 mg have required additional epinephrine doses and other interventions more often and have been more likely to require higher‑level care (eg, repeat IM injections, IV epinephrine, ICU admission or prolonged observation). Underdosing can prolong hypotension and hypoxemia, increase the risk of ongoing or recurrent (biphasic/multiphasic) episodes by allowing incomplete early mediator control, and may increase organ hypoperfusion and time to definitive stabilization. The patient in this report illustrates that pattern: initial 0.3 mg produced a transient response but recurrent episodes required further 0.5 mg injections and ICU monitoring.

Practical takeaway: for adults >50 kg aim for 0.5 mg IM epinephrine as first‑line therapy. If only a 0.3 mg auto‑injector is used and clinical response is incomplete or transient, give additional epinephrine promptly (repeat IM every 5–15 minutes as needed) and escalate monitoring/therapy rather than relying on a single 0.3 mg dose. Address system issues (stocking appropriate adult autoinjectors, staff familiarity) to avoid underdosing in time‑sensitive situations.
