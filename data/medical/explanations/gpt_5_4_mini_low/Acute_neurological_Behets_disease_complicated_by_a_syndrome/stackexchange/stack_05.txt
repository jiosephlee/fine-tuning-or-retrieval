Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the proposed pathophysiologic link between acute neuro-Behçet’s disease and SIADH, and how might IL-6-mediated neuroinflammation contribute to inappropriate ADH secretion?

The proposed link is that **acute neuro-Behçet’s disease causes central nervous system inflammation, and that inflammatory state—especially IL-6–driven neuroinflammation—can stimulate inappropriate ADH release**, leading to SIADH.

In this case, the patient had **aseptic meningitis on CSF**, elevated inflammatory markers, and **hypotonic euvolemic hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine and elevated ADH**, all consistent with SIADH occurring in parallel with acute neuro-BD. The authors suggest that **aberrant monocyte activation in Behçet’s disease promotes IL-6 production**, and IL-6 has been shown in other neuroinflammatory disorders to **increase ADH secretion**. Mechanistically, IL-6 may act at the hypothalamus and posterior pituitary to dysregulate osmoregulation and ADH release, so the patient continues to secrete ADH despite hypo-osmolality.

So, the teaching point is: **in acute neuro-Behçet’s, SIADH may reflect cytokine-mediated CNS inflammation rather than a structural hypothalamic lesion**. In this report, the normal MRI did not exclude the diagnosis, and the concurrent improvement in neurologic symptoms, mucocutaneous disease, and sodium after colchicine supports an inflammatory rather than a primary endocrine cause.
