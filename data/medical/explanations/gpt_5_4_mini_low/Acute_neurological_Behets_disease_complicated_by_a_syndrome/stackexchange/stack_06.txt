Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Persistent hiccups and elevated creatine kinase were notable findings in this case; how can severe hyponatraemia from SIADH explain these manifestations, and why are they important bedside clues?

Severe hyponatraemia from SIADH can present with symptoms that seem nonspecific until you recognize them as metabolic clues. In this case, the persistent hiccups and elevated creatine kinase were both important bedside signals that the sodium abnormality was clinically significant, not merely an incidental lab finding.

Hiccups can occur with profound hyponatraemia because low serum sodium may irritate the central hiccup reflex arc, especially when the cause is a CNS inflammatory process such as acute neuro-Behçet’s disease. In other words, the hiccups are not just a GI complaint; in a patient with headache, fever, and hyponatraemia, they should prompt consideration of brainstem or meningeal involvement and should raise suspicion for SIADH related to neuroinflammation.

The elevated creatine kinase likely reflected hyponatraemia-associated muscle injury, including mild rhabdomyolysis. Severe hypotonicity can injure skeletal muscle through cellular swelling and membrane dysfunction, and CK elevation may be the only early clue before frank weakness or myoglobinuria appears. Importantly, this patient had no focal weakness, so the CK rise could easily have been missed if it were not checked.

These findings matter because they tell you that the hyponatraemia is symptomatic and potentially dangerous, and they support the diagnosis of SIADH in the setting of active CNS inflammation. In this case, the resolution of hiccups, normalization of sodium, and fall in CK after treatment of the underlying Behçet’s inflammation strongly reinforce that link.
