Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What were the leading differential diagnoses for the two cystic masses in this case, and which sonographic and MRI features supported decidualised endometrioma versus cystic degenerating leiomyoma?

The leading differential diagnoses were **decidualised endometrioma versus cystic degenerating leiomyoma**. In practice, the **upper mass** was initially interpreted as an ovarian-type cystic lesion—most likely a **decidualised endometrioma**—whereas the **lower, pelvic mass** was favored to be a **posterior intramural-subserosal leiomyoma with cystic degeneration**.

The sonographic features that pushed the team toward **decidualised endometrioma** for the cranial lesion were its **large unilocular cystic appearance, regular margins, and peripheral vascularity**, together with the **slight CA-125 elevation** in the second trimester. Importantly, because of the gravid uterus and the mass size, **no definite stalk or feeding vessel** could be identified on ultrasound, which kept an ovarian origin in the differential.

By contrast, the **caudal mass** was more suggestive of **cystic degenerating leiomyoma** because it appeared **adherent to a posterior intramural-subserosal myoma** on ultrasound. MRI strengthened that impression: the lesion was seen in **continuity with the posterior uterine wall**, and the study showed **thick walls, inhomogeneous fluid content, and focal T1/T2 hyperintense solid components**, all features compatible with a **degenerating leiomyoma** rather than a simple adnexal cyst. The absence of adenopathy also argued against an overt malignant process.

So, in this case, the key discriminators were not just “cystic versus solid,” but **anatomic continuity with the uterus on imaging** and the **MRI signal characteristics of degeneration**. Pathology ultimately confirmed that both lesions were **benign uterine leiomyomas**, with ischemic change near a twisted pedicle in the cranial mass.
