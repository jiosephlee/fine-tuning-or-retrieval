Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Morning Report] What clinical factors in this case justified proceeding to surgery at 21 weeks and 6 days of gestation rather than continued expectant management?

Proceeding to surgery at 21 weeks and 6 days was justified because this was not an asymptomatic mass that could safely be watched; it was a **symptomatic, complicated, and diagnostically uncertain lesion** with several red flags.

The decisive clinical factors were:

- **Worsening maternal symptoms despite conservative measures**: she had severe acute abdominal pain and dyspnea from mechanical compression, and although analgesia and positioning helped transiently, the symptoms were significant and persistent.
- **Risk of acute complication from the mass itself**: imaging showed **very large, vascularized masses with mixed solid-cystic features**, and at surgery the cranial lesion proved to be **pedunculated, twisted, and leaking with ischemic necrosis**—findings consistent with imminent or ongoing complication. The team was appropriately concerned about **torsion, rupture, infection, and necrosis**.
- **Need for a definitive diagnosis**: ultrasound and non-contrast MRI could not conclusively distinguish between adnexal pathology and a degenerating leiomyoma; the slightly elevated and rising **CA-125** added to the diagnostic uncertainty, even though it was not diagnostic of malignancy.
- **Progressive mass effect with anticipated worsening**: the lesions were so large that further uterine enlargement and fetal growth would likely increase compression, pain, and respiratory compromise, while also raising the risk of hypoxia and degeneration of the masses.
- **Time-sensitive obstetric planning**: delaying intervention would not necessarily improve safety, whereas the masses already threatened maternal status and later pregnancy would only make surgery more difficult.

So, the rationale was the classic pregnancy-surgery balance: **persistent maternal symptoms plus complication risk and uncertain pathology outweighed the risks of second-trimester laparotomy**. In this case, the operation was both diagnostic and therapeutic, and pathology confirmed the concern for **benign leiomyomas with ischemic necrosis near a twisted pedicle**.
