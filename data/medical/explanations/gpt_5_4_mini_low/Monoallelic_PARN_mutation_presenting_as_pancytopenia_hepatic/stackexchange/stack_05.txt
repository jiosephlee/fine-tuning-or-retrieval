Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Morning Report] What is the differential diagnosis for a young woman with pancytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly, pulmonary fibrotic changes, premature greying, and hepatic fibrosis, and which elements of the workup in this case argue against autoimmune, infectious, malignant, and infiltrative causes?

In a young woman with **pancytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly, fibrotic lung disease, premature greying, and hepatic fibrosis**, the differential should first include **nutritional megaloblastosis or other causes of ineffective hematopoiesis**, **bone marrow failure syndromes**, **autoimmune cytopenias or connective-tissue disease**, **chronic infection**, **hematologic malignancy**, and **infiltrative/granulomatous disease**. In this case, however, the broader phenotype points strongly toward a **telomere biology disorder**—the dyskeratosis congenita spectrum—including **PARN-associated disease**, which can present in adulthood with marrow dysfunction, pulmonary fibrosis, liver fibrosis, and premature aging features even without the classic mucocutaneous triad.

Several elements of the workup argue against the major acquired causes:

- **Against autoimmune disease:** although the DAT was positive, the picture did not support immune hemolysis or autoimmune cytopenia. The **reticulocyte count was low**, not appropriately elevated for hemolysis; marrow showed **ineffective erythropoiesis rather than peripheral destruction**; and **ANA and autoimmune hepatitis serologies were negative**. There were no convincing systemic connective-tissue disease features to unify the findings.

- **Against infection:** the infectious evaluation was unrevealing, with testing for **viral hepatitis, HIV, CMV, and scrub typhus negative**, and the isolated **EBV IgG positivity** indicated prior exposure rather than active disease. The chronic multisystem fibrotic phenotype is also not explained by an acute or subacute infection.

- **Against malignancy:** bone marrow examination showed **trilineage hematopoiesis with erythroid hyperplasia**, but **no blasts, dysplasia, or infiltrative process**, and **cytogenetics were normal**. PET-CT did not show a focal hypermetabolic malignant process.

- **Against infiltrative/granulomatous disease:** PET-CT showed **no evidence of granulomatous disease or overt infiltrative malignancy**, and marrow histology likewise did not demonstrate infiltration. The liver and lung findings were fibrotic rather than mass-like or granulomatous.

So the teaching point is that when a young patient has **multiorgan fibrosis plus marrow failure and premature aging features**, and the usual autoimmune, infectious, malignant, and infiltrative causes are not supported by targeted testing, you should think of a **telomere disorder** early. In this case, exome sequencing identifying a **monoallelic PARN variant** provided the unifying explanation.
