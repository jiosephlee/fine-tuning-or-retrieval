Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] Why was a self-expanding Acurate neo2 chosen as the third prosthesis in this case, and what design features made it attractive in the setting of a narrow, triple-stented aortic valve anatomy?

The Acurate neo2 was chosen as the third prosthesis because the team needed a valve that could deliver the best possible hemodynamics in a severely constrained, triple-stented aortic root while also preserving coronary flow and future coronary access. In this patient, CT showed a very narrow residual lumen within the Evolut frame, so any additional transcatheter valve risked worsening patient-prosthesis mismatch and creating an even more obstructive neo-skirt. The Acurate neo2 was attractive because it could be implanted high and aligned with the prior Evolut leaflets, helping to pin the old leaflets outward against the frame, preserve the effective orifice area, and avoid further compromise of the coronary ostia.

Its design features were particularly useful in this anatomy: it is a self-expanding valve with supra-annular leaflet position, which favors a larger functional orifice in a small annulus or constrained landing zone; it has a relatively low frame height, which helps limit interaction with the coronaries; and it supports commissural alignment, an important advantage when future coronary reaccess may be needed. In short, the device was selected not just for deliverability, but because its geometry offered the best chance of acceptable gradients, coronary preservation, and workable lifetime valve management in a very narrow triple-valve configuration.
