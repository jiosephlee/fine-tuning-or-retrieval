Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient with recurrent dysfunction after two prior transcatheter aortic valves, what differential diagnosis should be considered for elevated transvalvular gradients and new regurgitation, and which features in this case argued against active infective endocarditis at the time of the third procedure?

In a patient with recurrent dysfunction after two prior transcatheter aortic valves, the differential diagnosis for **elevated transvalvular gradients with new regurgitation** should be broad. The major considerations are **structural valve deterioration** of one or more prostheses, **patient–prosthesis mismatch** from progressively smaller effective orifice area in a layered valve stack, **leaflet thrombosis**, **prosthesis malexpansion or underexpansion** related to frame interaction, **pannus or fibrotic ingrowth**, and **prosthetic valve endocarditis** with leaflet destruction or paravalvular complications. In a triple-valve anatomy, these mechanisms can overlap, so imaging and clinical context are essential.

In this case, several features argued **against active infective endocarditis at the time of the third procedure**. He had **completed a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy** for prior *Enterococcus faecalis* prosthetic valve endocarditis, had **no ongoing suppressive antibiotics**, and on reassessment had **no clinical evidence of infection**. Importantly, **repeat blood cultures were negative**, and both **transesophageal echocardiography and multislice CT showed no vegetations, abscess, or paravalvular complications**. Thus, the recurrent stenosis and regurgitation were interpreted as **post-infectious structural dysfunction and hemodynamic failure of the prostheses**, not ongoing infection, which is why the team proceeded with reintervention rather than treating presumed active endocarditis.
