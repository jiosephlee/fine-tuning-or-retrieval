Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What features on muscle biopsy support a diagnosis of dermatomyositis rather than immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy or inclusion body myositis?

On muscle biopsy, **dermatomyositis** is supported by a **perifascicular pattern of injury** with **perimysial/perivascular inflammation** and **complement-mediated microangiopathy**. In this case, the biopsy showed **necrotic, regenerating, and myophagocytosed myofibers in a perifascicular distribution**, **scattered perimysial inflammation with histiocytes**, and—most importantly—**C5b-9 (membrane attack complex) deposition in perifascicular endomysial capillaries**, which is a classic dermatomyositis feature.

This differs from the other inflammatory myopathies in a few key ways:

- **Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM):** biopsy typically shows **prominent myofiber necrosis and regeneration with minimal inflammatory infiltrate**, usually **without the characteristic perifascicular distribution** or capillary MAC pattern seen in dermatomyositis.
- **Inclusion body myositis (IBM):** biopsy usually shows **endomysial inflammation that invades non-necrotic fibers**, often with **rimmed vacuoles** and other degenerative changes; a **perifascicular capillary C5b-9 pattern** is not the defining feature.

So, in short: **perifascicular atrophy/necrosis, perimysial inflammation, and capillary C5b-9 deposition favor dermatomyositis** over IMNM or IBM.
