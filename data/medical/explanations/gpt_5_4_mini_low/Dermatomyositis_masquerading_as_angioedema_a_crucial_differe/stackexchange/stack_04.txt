Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the significance of anti-SAE1 antibody positivity in dermatomyositis, and which clinical manifestations are especially associated with this autoantibody phenotype?

Anti-SAE1 positivity identifies a distinct dermatomyositis autoantibody phenotype. Clinically, it often signals a presentation in which **cutaneous disease is prominent** and **muscle and extramuscular involvement can be substantial**, even when the classic rash is incomplete or subtle at first, as in this case. Importantly, it should prompt clinicians to think beyond “skin-limited” disease and to screen carefully for **dysphagia, interstitial lung disease, and malignancy**.

In the case report, the patient had **severe dysphagia**, marked **myositis with very high CK**, and **respiratory/aspiration-related abnormalities**, all fitting the SAE1-associated pattern of significant systemic involvement. The literature summarized in the report also shows that SAE1 dermatomyositis is especially associated with **heliotrope rash, Gottron’s papules, V-neck and shawl signs**, and that **dysphagia and interstitial lung disease** are common—reported in about **46%** and **43%** of cases, respectively—while **malignancy** was present in about **18%**. 

A useful teaching point from this case is that **edema or “pseudoangioedema” of the face or neck is uncommon but can occur**, and in dermatomyositis it reflects **inflammatory myositis and soft-tissue involvement**, not histamine- or bradykinin-mediated angioedema. Thus, anti-SAE1 positivity should heighten suspicion for a **more severe dermatomyositis phenotype with dysphagia and lung involvement**, even when the rash is not classic.
