Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] Why was this patient considered high risk for malignancy according to idiopathic inflammatory myopathy-associated cancer screening principles, and what screening strategy is appropriate in such a case?

He was considered **high risk for malignancy** because, under the **IMACS idiopathic inflammatory myopathy–associated cancer screening guideline**, he had **two high-risk features** and **two intermediate-risk features**:

- **High-risk features:**  
  - **Dermatomyositis** itself  
  - **Dysphagia**  
- **Intermediate-risk features:**  
  - **SAE1 antibody positivity**  
  - **Male sex**

In idiopathic inflammatory myopathy, cancer risk is greatest in dermatomyositis, particularly when there is dysphagia and cancer-associated autoantibody positivity. In this case, the combination of dermatomyositis plus significant dysphagia was enough to place him in the high-risk category, with SAE1 positivity and male sex adding further concern.

The appropriate screening approach is **enhanced baseline malignancy screening at the time of diagnosis**, followed by **ongoing surveillance**. In practice, that means:

- **Thorough history and physical examination**
- **Basic laboratory testing**
- **Cross-sectional imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis**  
- **Age- and sex-appropriate cancer screening**
- **Further directed testing if baseline studies are unrevealing or suspicion remains**, such as **PET scanning** and **upper and lower GI endoscopy**, as was planned in this case

For **high-risk patients**, the key principle is not a single negative test, but **comprehensive initial screening plus interval follow-up for occult malignancy**, typically over the first few years after diagnosis, when cancer risk is highest. This is exactly why this patient underwent CT imaging, tumor markers, PET-CT, and planned gastroscopy and colonoscopy.
