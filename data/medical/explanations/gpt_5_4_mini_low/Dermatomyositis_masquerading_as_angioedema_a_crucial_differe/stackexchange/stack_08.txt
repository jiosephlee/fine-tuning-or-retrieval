Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] What is the rationale for using pulse intravenous methylprednisolone, IVIG, mycophenolate mofetil, and rituximab in a patient with moderate to severe dermatomyositis with dysphagia and airway-related muscle involvement?

In this case, the combination was chosen because the patient had **moderate to severe dermatomyositis with organ-threatening muscle involvement**—specifically **severe pharyngeal dysphagia, cervical/airway-related muscle edema, and active multifocal myositis**. In that setting, treatment must do two things at once: **rapidly suppress active inflammation** and **maintain durable control while tapering steroids**.

**Pulse intravenous methylprednisolone** is used first because it provides the **fastest anti-inflammatory effect** in severe dermatomyositis. When swallowing and airway-adjacent muscles are involved, delay risks aspiration, nutritional failure, and progressive respiratory compromise. The 3-day pulse in this patient was appropriate for urgent disease control, followed by oral prednisone taper.

**IVIG** is often added in severe or rapidly progressive dermatomyositis, especially when there is **prominent dysphagia**, because it can produce a relatively **rapid clinical response** and is useful as an adjunct when immediate improvement is needed. In this case, the patient had severe pharyngeal dysfunction with aspiration risk and required nasogastric feeding, so IVIG was a rational escalation.

**Mycophenolate mofetil** serves as a **steroid-sparing maintenance agent** to help sustain remission and allow prednisone tapering. Dermatomyositis commonly relapses if treated with glucocorticoids alone, and this patient had extensive MRI and PET evidence of active myositis, so a conventional long-term immunosuppressant was warranted.

**Rituximab** was added because the disease burden was high and the case was antibody-positive dermatomyositis with severe extracutaneous involvement. Rituximab is commonly used in **refractory or severe idiopathic inflammatory myopathy** as a more potent steroid-sparing therapy when disease is extensive or when a faster, deeper immunologic suppression is desired. In the case report, the patient improved after this intensified regimen, with normalization of CK, recovery of strength, and eventual resolution of dysphagia.

So the rationale is that this was **not simple cutaneous dermatomyositis**, but **systemic, severe muscle disease threatening swallowing and airway function**. The regimen combined:
- **Pulse steroids** for immediate control,
- **IVIG** for rapid improvement in dysphagia/severe weakness,
- **Mycophenolate** for maintenance and steroid sparing,
- **Rituximab** for escalation in a high-burden autoimmune myopathy.

This is a classic example of why severe dermatomyositis requires **early, aggressive, multi-agent immunosuppression** to prevent aspiration, prolonged disability, and further multiorgan injury.
