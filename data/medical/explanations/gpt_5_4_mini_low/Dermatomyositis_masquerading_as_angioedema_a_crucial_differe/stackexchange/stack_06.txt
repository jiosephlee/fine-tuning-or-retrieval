Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] In this patient with neck swelling, coughing on nasendoscopy, and severe pharyngeal dysphagia on swallow study, what is the likely mechanism of his respiratory findings and chest CT abnormalities?

The respiratory findings were most likely due to **bulbar and pharyngeal muscle involvement from dermatomyositis, leading to impaired swallowing and recurrent microaspiration**.

In this case, the key clues were the **coughing and excess secretions on nasendoscopy**, the **severe pharyngeal dysphagia on videofluoroscopy**, and the **pyriform sinus residue with penetration to the vocal cords**. Those findings indicate failure of the oropharyngeal phase of swallowing, so saliva, thin liquids, and food were being aspirated into the airway. That explains both the **cough** and the **chest CT abnormalities**: the **patchy peribronchial consolidation** and **noncavitating nodules** were interpreted as **aspiration pneumonitis/inflammatory change**, not primary interstitial lung disease.

So the mechanism is not angioedema or a primary pulmonary process; it is **myositis-related oropharyngeal dysfunction causing aspiration**, with secondary airway-centered inflammatory opacities on CT.
