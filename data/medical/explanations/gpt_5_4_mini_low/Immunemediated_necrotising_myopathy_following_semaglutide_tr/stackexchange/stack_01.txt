Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient with progressive proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, dysphonia, and a creatine kinase level in the thousands, what clinical features should raise suspicion for immune-mediated necrotising myopathy rather than simple drug-induced rhabdomyolysis?

What should make you think of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, rather than a straightforward toxic rhabdomyolysis, is the **pattern and persistence of the weakness**.

In this case, the key clues were:

- **Progressive, symmetric proximal muscle weakness** rather than isolated acute myalgias or transient weakness
- **Bulbar involvement** — specifically **dysphagia and dysphonia**, which are red flags for an inflammatory myopathy
- **A subacute course over weeks to months**, with continued decline **after the drug had already been stopped**, instead of the rapid onset-and-recovery pattern expected with toxic rhabdomyolysis
- **Creatine kinase remaining elevated and even rising over time** — here, from a modest elevation initially to nearly 4,000 U/L four months later
- **Lack of a classic rhabdomyolysis picture**, such as severe myalgias, cola-colored urine, or rapid CK normalization with hydration
- **Objective evidence of myositis**, including MRI muscle edema and, most importantly, biopsy showing **necrotic and regenerating fibers with macrophage-mediated myophagocytosis and only sparse lymphocytic inflammation**, which is characteristic of IMNM

By contrast, simple drug-induced rhabdomyolysis usually presents as an **acute toxic injury**: CK peaks early, the inciting agent is withdrawn, hydration is given, and the CK generally falls quickly over days to a few weeks. In this case, the persistent progression off semaglutide and the bulbar/proximal weakness pointed away from rhabdomyolysis and toward an autoimmune necrotising myopathy that required immunosuppression.
