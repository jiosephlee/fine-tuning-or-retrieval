Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What muscle-biopsy findings are characteristic of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, and which specific histopathologic features in this case supported that diagnosis?

Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy is characterized on muscle biopsy by **prominent myofiber necrosis and regeneration with relatively sparse lymphocytic inflammation**. The key pathologic pattern is **necrotic and regenerating fibers out of proportion to inflammatory infiltrate**, often with **macrophage-predominant cleanup of dead fibers** rather than a dense T-cell infiltrate, and sometimes **membrane attack complex (C5b-9) deposition on muscle fibers**.

In this case, the biopsy supported IMNM because it showed:

- **Dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibers**
- **Myophagocytosis**
- **CD68-positive macrophage infiltrates**
- **Acid phosphatase positivity**, consistent with active fiber breakdown/repair
- **Membrane attack complex staining on necrotizing and non-necrotizing myofibers**
- Only **rare CD3-positive T cells** and limited **perivascular inflammatory cells**

Taken together, the biopsy showed a **necrotizing myopathy with minimal lymphocytic inflammation**, which is the classic histopathologic signature of IMNM.
