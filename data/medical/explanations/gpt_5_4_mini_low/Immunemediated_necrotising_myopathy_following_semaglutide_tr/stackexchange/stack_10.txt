Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Using a pharmacovigilance framework such as the WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre criteria, what elements support a ‘probable’ causal association between semaglutide and this patient’s myopathy, and what key limitations prevent a definitive conclusion?

Using the WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre framework, this case meets **“probable”** rather than “certain” causality because several elements fit a drug-related adverse event, but the evidence is not definitive.

The features supporting a **probable causal association** are:

1. **Reasonable temporal relationship**: her symptoms began after semaglutide exposure, with a striking initial intolerance after the first dose and then progressive proximal weakness, dysphagia, dysphonia, and CK rise over the ensuing months.
2. **Plausible clinical syndrome**: the biopsy showed **necrotizing myopathy with macrophage-rich myophagocytosis and membrane attack complex staining**, consistent with IMNM rather than simple toxic rhabdomyolysis.
3. **Lack of a better alternative explanation**: she had no statin exposure, no clear toxin or supplement trigger, negative connective-tissue-disease serologies, and no clear evidence of another myositis subtype or active malignancy in the reported evaluation.
4. **Clinically reasonable response after withdrawal**: semaglutide was not continued, and she improved with immunosuppression and IVIG, with normalization of CK and recovery of baseline function while avoiding GLP-1 receptor agonists.
5. **Positive dechallenge in the broad sense**: the course was compatible with removal of the suspected agent, although improvement was also confounded by treatment of the myopathy itself.

The **key limitations** preventing a definitive conclusion are equally important:

- **No pre-semaglutide CK or muscle evaluation**, so a smoldering autoimmune myopathy before exposure cannot be excluded.
- **Single-case observation**: there is no rechallenge, no dose-response relationship, and no comparator, so causality cannot be proven.
- **Rechallenge would be unethical** given the severity of the presentation.
- **Potential confounding by treatment**: recovery occurred after corticosteroids and IVIG, so improvement cannot be attributed to semaglutide withdrawal alone.
- **Seronegative IMNM remains possible**: negative HMGCR/SRP and other myositis antibodies do not rule out IMNM, and a subset of cases are seronegative.
- **Other contributors cannot be fully excluded**, including the acute systemic illness, deconditioning, and the possibility of an unrelated autoimmune process.

So, in pharmacovigilance terms, this is a **reasonable, temporally associated, dechallenge-supported signal**, but not proof of causation.
