Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Morning Report] How does the time course of this patient’s illness help distinguish semaglutide-associated toxic muscle injury from a possible semaglutide-triggered autoimmune necrotising myopathy?

The **time course strongly favors an immune-mediated necrotising myopathy rather than a direct toxic myopathy/rhabdomyolysis**.

In a **toxic semaglutide-associated muscle injury**, one would expect the muscle injury to occur **during active exposure** and then begin to **improve quickly after the drug is stopped**, with CK falling over **days to a few weeks** and without the need for immunosuppression. That is the pattern described in drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, including the tirzepatide case referenced in the report.

By contrast, this patient had only **a modest CK elevation at the time of the initial semaglutide-related hospitalization**, when her symptoms were dominated by gastrointestinal toxicity, hypovolemia, and AKI. The key clue is that she then **continued to deteriorate over the next 4 months while off semaglutide**, developing **progressive proximal weakness, dysphagia, dysphonia, and a 12-fold rise in CK**. That delayed, progressive course is much more typical of **autoimmune IMNM**, in which the inflammatory muscle injury continues **after the inciting exposure has ended**.

Her subsequent course also supports an immune process: she had **necrotising myopathy on biopsy** and improved only after **pulse steroids plus IVIG**, with sustained recovery and normalization of CK. So the temporal pattern is not “drug exposure → acute muscle injury → rapid resolution,” but rather “brief exposure → delayed progressive myopathy persisting off drug → response to immunosuppression,” which is the clinical behavior of a **triggered autoimmune necrotising myopathy**.
