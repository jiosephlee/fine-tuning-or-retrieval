Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] Why was this patient treated with pulse methylprednisolone followed by high-dose prednisone and IVIG, and what clinical features justified escalation beyond corticosteroids alone?

She was treated aggressively because this was not a mild, self-limited myositis; it was a severe immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy with bulbar involvement. In IMNM, prompt high-dose corticosteroids are used to rapidly suppress muscle inflammation and halt ongoing necrosis, so the initial 5-day pulse of methylprednisolone followed by prednisone 1 mg/kg daily was appropriate first-line therapy.

IVIG was added because several features signaled a high-risk, refractory presentation in which corticosteroids alone might be inadequate. She had:

- **Progressive, profound proximal weakness** to the point that she could no longer stand or sit independently  
- **Bulbar symptoms** — dysphagia and dysphonia — which indicate more severe disease and raise concern for aspiration and respiratory compromise  
- **Marked CK elevation** approaching 4000 U/L with continued clinical decline over months, consistent with active muscle injury  
- **Biopsy-proven IMNM**, showing necrotic and regenerating fibers with macrophage-mediated myophagocytosis and membrane attack complex deposition  
- **Possible cardiac involvement** on imaging and biomarker elevation, which further supported a systemic inflammatory process

In practice, IMNM often requires **early combination immunotherapy** rather than steroids alone, especially when weakness is severe or function-threatening. IVIG is particularly useful because it can produce a faster clinical response in severe necrotizing myopathy and can be steroid-sparing.

So, the escalation reflected the combination of **bulbar involvement, severe proximal weakness, ongoing CK elevation, and biopsy-confirmed necrotizing autoimmune myopathy**, all of which made corticosteroids alone insufficient.
