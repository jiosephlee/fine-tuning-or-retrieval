Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Morning Report] In a child with recurrent large unilateral, blood-stained pleural effusions and predominantly respiratory symptoms, what historical clue in this case most strongly suggested a pancreatic source rather than a primary thoracic cause?

The most important historical clue was the **recent blunt abdominal trauma from the bicycle handle, occurring about 1 month before the onset of recurrent pleural effusions**.  

That detail strongly pointed away from a primary thoracic process and toward **post-traumatic pancreatic duct injury with pancreatopleural fistula**, especially because the effusions were **recurrent, unilateral, and blood-stained** with predominant respiratory symptoms. In this case, the abdominal history emerged only after repeated questioning, and it ultimately aligned with the CT/MRCP findings of a tract from the pancreas to the pleural space.
