Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Pathophysiology] Although trauma was the likely trigger in this child, what underlying causes should be considered in a child with recurrent pancreatitis or recurrent pancreatopleural fistula, and which genetic mutations are classically associated with hereditary pancreatitis?

In a child with recurrent pancreatitis or a recurrent pancreatopleural fistula, trauma may be the immediate trigger, but it should prompt you to ask whether there is an underlying predisposition that made the pancreas vulnerable in the first place. In children, recurrent or chronic pancreatitis is much more often linked to **hereditary or genetic disease** than to alcohol or gallstone disease, and idiopathic cases may in fact reflect an unrecognized ductal abnormality or genetic mutation. Thus, in a child like this one, you should consider **hereditary pancreatitis**, **cystic fibrosis–related pancreatic disease**, and other genetic causes of chronic pancreatitis, especially if there is recurrence, a family history, or disease out of proportion to the triggering event.

Classically, hereditary pancreatitis is associated with mutations in:

- **PRSS1**  
- **SPINK1**  
- **CFTR**  
- **CTRC** (chymotrypsin C)

Among these, **PRSS1** is the prototypical hereditary pancreatitis gene, and the classic mutations often cited are **R122H** and **N29I**. In other words, although this boy’s pancreatopleural fistula was temporally linked to blunt abdominal trauma, recurrent disease should still raise suspicion for an underlying genetic pancreatitis predisposition, particularly **PRSS1- or SPINK1-related disease**, as highlighted in the case discussion.
