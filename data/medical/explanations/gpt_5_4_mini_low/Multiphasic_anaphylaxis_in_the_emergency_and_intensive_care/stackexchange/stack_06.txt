Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Pathophysiology] Given this patient’s severe, prolonged course and persistently elevated tryptase, what underlying mast cell disorder should be considered, and what clinical or laboratory findings in the case support that suspicion despite not meeting full formal criteria?

The underlying mast cell disorder to consider is **systemic mastocytosis**, or more broadly a **clonal mast cell disorder**. In this case, the suspicion is driven less by a single diagnostic test than by the overall pattern: **severe, prolonged, multiphasic anaphylaxis**, **persistently elevated baseline tryptase**, and additional findings that fit mast cell disease.

What supports the suspicion in this patient, even though she did not meet full WHO criteria, is:

- **Persistently elevated tryptase**, including an elevated level during the episode and an elevated **baseline** tryptase on follow-up. A chronically elevated baseline tryptase is a classic clue to an underlying mast cell burden or clonality, not just acute anaphylaxis.
- **Atypical severity and recurrence of anaphylaxis** after a single clarithromycin dose, with **multiple relapses despite appropriate epinephrine**, which is disproportionate and raises concern for mast cell instability.
- **Hepatomegaly** on abdominal sonography, a possible extracutaneous manifestation of systemic mast cell disease.
- Possible **neuropsychiatric symptoms**—depression and memory problems—which the authors note are in line with systemic mastocytosis.
- The allergology workup suggested a **high probability of systemic mastocytosis**, but the bone marrow material was limited, so the **major criterion could not be assessed**, and only **two minor WHO criteria** were documented—insufficient for formal diagnosis.

So the teaching point is: in a patient with **recurrent or unusually severe anaphylaxis plus persistently elevated baseline tryptase**, think of **systemic mastocytosis or another clonal mast cell disorder**, even if formal diagnostic criteria are not fully met.
