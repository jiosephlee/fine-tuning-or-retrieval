Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the significance of recent preoperative unfractionated heparin exposure in explaining an early-onset platelet fall before postoperative day 5 in suspected HIT after cardiac surgery?

Recent preoperative unfractionated heparin exposure is the key clue that makes a platelet fall **before postoperative day 5** compatible with HIT rather than arguing against it. In classic HIT after cardiac surgery, the platelet count usually begins to fall **between POD5 and POD10**, reflecting the time needed to generate new anti-PF4/heparin antibodies. But if the patient has had **heparin exposure in the preceding days to weeks**—as in this case, where heparin was given during preoperative cardiac catheterization—he may already have circulating antibodies at the time of surgery. Then, re-exposure to large-dose intraoperative heparin can trigger an **anamnestic, rapid-onset HIT**, producing thrombocytopenia essentially immediately or within the first few postoperative days.

In the reported case, the early platelet decline was therefore not “too early” for HIT; rather, it was explained by **recent preoperative UFH exposure** that likely sensitized the patient before cardiopulmonary bypass. This is a classic diagnostic pearl after cardiac surgery: **an early postoperative platelet fall should prompt review of all heparin exposures in the prior 1–2 weeks**, especially catheterization or line flushes, because they can convert an otherwise atypical timeline into highly plausible HIT, including autoimmune HIT with very severe thrombocytopenia and thrombosis.
