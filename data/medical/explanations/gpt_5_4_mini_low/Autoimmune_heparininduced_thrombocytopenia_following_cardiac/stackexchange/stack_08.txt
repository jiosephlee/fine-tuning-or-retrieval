Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] What was the rationale for choosing argatroban initially in this case, and what clinical and laboratory findings prompted the subsequent switch to therapeutic-dose fondaparinux with antithrombin supplementation?

Argatroban was chosen initially because the team needed an anticoagulant for suspected HIT that would not cross-react with heparin antibodies and would be rapidly titratable in a patient with very high bleeding concern. In this case, the patient had a recent cerebral infarct and profound thrombocytopenia, so a short-acting, intravenous direct thrombin inhibitor with aPTT monitoring was favored over fondaparinux at the outset. Importantly, argatroban does not depend on antithrombin, so it can be used even when HIT is causing consumptive coagulopathy and the antithrombin level may be reduced.

The later switch to therapeutic-dose fondaparinux was prompted by evidence that the HIT-associated hypercoagulable state was not adequately controlled on argatroban. Clinically and laboratorily, the patient had ongoing severe disease: fibrinogen continued to fall, d-dimer rose, schistocytes appeared on the blood film, and the platelet count improved only modestly despite IVIG. Those findings suggested persistent consumptive thrombosis consistent with autoimmune HIT. Fondaparinux was then selected because it is an effective off-label option for HIT, is simpler to administer once daily, and was judged more suitable for continued anticoagulation. Because fondaparinux is an antithrombin-dependent factor Xa inhibitor, supplemental antithrombin was given first when the measured antithrombin level was mildly low, to ensure adequate drug effect.
