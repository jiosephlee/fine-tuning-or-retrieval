Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] In a patient like this, why might plasma exchange be started before the diagnosis is fully clarified, and why is eculizumab considered the definitive therapy once complement-mediated HUS becomes the leading diagnosis?

Plasma exchange is often started **before the diagnosis is fully clarified** because, in an adult with **microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury**, the most time-critical alternative diagnosis is **thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)**. TTP can be rapidly fatal and is treated immediately with plasma exchange while ADAMTS13 testing is pending. In this case, the patient’s initial picture was clouded by severe sepsis, DIC, pancytopenia, and evolving renal failure; only later did the more classic TMA pattern emerge with schistocytes, falling haptoglobin, rising LDH, and persistent thrombocytopenia. In that setting, PEX is a reasonable **bridging therapy** because it may temporarily remove circulating autoantibodies or inflammatory mediators and buys time while the team excludes TTP and other causes.

Once **complement-mediated HUS (CM-HUS)** becomes the leading diagnosis, **eculizumab is the definitive therapy** because the disease is driven by **uncontrolled terminal complement activation**, not by a problem that plasma exchange can correct. PEX does not fix the underlying complement dysregulation; at best, it provides transient improvement. By contrast, eculizumab blocks **C5**, preventing formation of the membrane attack complex and halting the endothelial injury that drives TMA. That is why, in this case, the durable hematologic response and renal recovery followed complement blockade rather than plasma exchange alone. The case also illustrates an important practical point: because eculizumab increases susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria, it is ideally started when infection is controlled, with meningococcal vaccination and antimicrobial prophylaxis in place.
