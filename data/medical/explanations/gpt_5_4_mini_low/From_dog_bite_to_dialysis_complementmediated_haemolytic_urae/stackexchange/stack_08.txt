Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Morning Report] What renal biopsy findings in this case supported thrombotic microangiopathy, and how did the biopsy help distinguish ongoing TMA from isolated acute tubular necrosis due to septic shock?

The biopsy supported thrombotic microangiopathy by showing **fibrin thrombi within ischemic glomeruli**, along with **scattered crescent formation**. Those findings indicate **microvascular endothelial injury and intraglomerular thrombosis**, which are the histologic hallmarks of TMA. In this case, immunofluorescence showed **no immune-complex deposition**, and electron microscopy showed **no primary glomerulopathy**, making an immune-mediated nephritis or another primary glomerular disease unlikely.

At the same time, the biopsy also showed **severe acute tubular necrosis**, which is entirely compatible with the patient’s preceding septic shock and ischemic kidney injury. The key point is that **ATN explains tubular damage, but it does not explain fibrin thrombi in glomeruli**. Thus, the biopsy distinguished **concurrent ischemic ATN from ongoing TMA**: the tubular injury reflected shock, whereas the **fibrin thrombi and ischemic glomerular lesions proved active microangiopathic renal injury** from complement-mediated HUS.
