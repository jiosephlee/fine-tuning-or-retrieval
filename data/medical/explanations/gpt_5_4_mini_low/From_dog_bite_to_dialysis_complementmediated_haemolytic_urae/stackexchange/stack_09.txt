Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] What infection-prevention measures are required before and after starting eculizumab, and why are these especially important in someone recovering from Capnocytophaga bacteraemia?

Before starting eculizumab, the key infection-prevention step is **meningococcal protection**: patients should receive **both MenACWY and MenB vaccination ideally at least 2 weeks before the first dose**. If eculizumab must be started urgently, as in this case, then vaccination should be given **as soon as possible**, and the patient should receive **concurrent antimicrobial prophylaxis**—commonly **penicillin or amoxicillin** if tolerated—because vaccine-induced protection is not immediate.

After eculizumab is started, this protection must be **maintained throughout treatment**: patients need to **complete the vaccine series and boosters on schedule**, and many centers continue **antibiotic prophylaxis for the duration of complement blockade**, with close counseling to seek urgent care for fever or meningitic symptoms.

These measures are especially important after **Capnocytophaga bacteraemia** because the patient is recovering from an infection in which **complement-mediated host defense is central**. Eculizumab blocks terminal complement activity, which markedly increases susceptibility to **encapsulated bacteria**, classically **Neisseria**, but also raises concern for **recurrent or uncontrolled Capnocytophaga infection**. In other words, this patient has just demonstrated a complement-sensitive zoonotic infection, and eculizumab removes one of her major remaining defenses. That is why the case appropriately paired **vaccination, penicillin prophylaxis, and careful timing of complement blockade after blood cultures cleared**.
