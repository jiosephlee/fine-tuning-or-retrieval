Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In an adult who develops microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and acute kidney injury after sepsis, what are the major thrombotic microangiopathy syndromes that must be distinguished, and which key tests help separate TTP, STEC-HUS and complement-mediated HUS?

In an adult who develops **microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury after sepsis**, the thrombotic microangiopathies that must be distinguished are:

1. **Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)**
2. **Shiga toxin–producing E. coli–associated HUS (STEC-HUS)**
3. **Complement-mediated HUS (CM-HUS, formerly “atypical HUS”)**

The first diagnostic priority is to **exclude TTP**, because it is a hematologic emergency requiring urgent plasma exchange. The key test is **ADAMTS13 activity**:  
- **Severe deficiency, typically <10%**, strongly supports TTP.  
- In the case report, ADAMTS13 activity was **43.5%**, which essentially ruled out TTP.

Next, **STEC-HUS** is separated by evidence of Shiga toxin exposure/infection:  
- **Stool PCR for Shiga toxin** and/or stool culture are the key tests.  
- In this case, **stool PCR was negative**, arguing against STEC-HUS.

If ADAMTS13 is not severely reduced and Shiga toxin testing is negative, the remaining major diagnosis is **CM-HUS**, which is a **clinicopathologic diagnosis of exclusion**. Helpful supportive studies include:  
- **Complement studies**: C3/C4 may be obtained, but **normal values do not exclude CM-HUS**, as shown in this case.  
- **Alternative pathway functional testing** such as **AH50** may show reduced activity, though results can be affected by treatment.  
- **Kidney biopsy**, when safe, can confirm a **TMA pattern** with fibrin thrombi and ischemic glomerular injury, as in this patient.

So, in practice: **ADAMTS13 activity distinguishes TTP; Shiga toxin PCR distinguishes STEC-HUS; and normal ADAMTS13 plus negative Shiga toxin testing, with biopsy-proven TMA, supports CM-HUS even if C3/C4 are normal.**
