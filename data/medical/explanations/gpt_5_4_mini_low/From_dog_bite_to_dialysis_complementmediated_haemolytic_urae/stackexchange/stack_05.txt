Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] The patient initially had a positive direct antiglobulin test but was later found to have 15%–20% schistocytes on smear. How do you reconcile these findings, and what clues argue for microangiopathic haemolysis rather than autoimmune haemolytic anaemia?

The apparent contradiction is resolved by recognizing that a **positive direct antiglobulin test (DAT) does not prove autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA)** in this setting. In this case, the DAT was likely a **false-positive or incidental finding**, plausibly related to **penicillin exposure and/or recent transfusions**, and it was later negated on repeat testing. By contrast, the peripheral smear showing **15%–20% schistocytes** is a much stronger morphologic clue to **microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA)** from a thrombotic microangiopathy.

Several features favor MAHA over AIHA:

- **Schistocytes on smear**: fragmented red cells are the hallmark of mechanical injury in the microvasculature; a burden as high as 15%–20% is highly suggestive of TMA.  
- **Persistent thrombocytopenia**: MAHA in TMA classically accompanies platelet consumption, whereas isolated AIHA does not usually cause marked thrombocytopenia.  
- **Severe kidney injury with anuria and dialysis dependence**: renal involvement is central to TMA/HUS and is not a typical dominant feature of AIHA.  
- **Failure to respond to corticosteroids**: the hemoglobin drop did not improve with prednisone, arguing against immune-mediated hemolysis.  
- **Biopsy confirmation of TMA**: fibrin thrombi and ischemic glomerular injury supported a microangiopathic process.  
- **Hemolysis emerging as sepsis improved**: this temporal pattern is classic for a superimposed complement-mediated TMA rather than primary AIHA.

So, the key teaching point is that in a septic patient, a positive DAT can mislead, but **the combination of schistocytosis, thrombocytopenia, AKI, and steroid nonresponse should redirect the diagnosis from AIHA to MAHA/TMA**.
