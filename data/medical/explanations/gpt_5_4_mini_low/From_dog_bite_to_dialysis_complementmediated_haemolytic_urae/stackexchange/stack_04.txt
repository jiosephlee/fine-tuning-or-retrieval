Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Clinical Pearls] How should the ADAMTS13 activity result of 43.5% be interpreted in this patient, and why is the threshold of less than 10% clinically important?

An ADAMTS13 activity of **43.5% is not consistent with thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)**. In this patient, it argues strongly **against severe ADAMTS13 deficiency as the cause of her thrombotic microangiopathy**, and therefore away from TTP as the primary diagnosis. That is clinically important because her presentation later evolved into **MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury**, which can be seen in both TTP and complement-mediated HUS, but the treatment and urgency differ.

The threshold of **<10%** matters because **severe ADAMTS13 deficiency is the diagnostic hallmark of TTP**. When ADAMTS13 activity is below 10%, platelet-rich microthrombi form because von Willebrand factor multimers are not adequately cleaved. This is the setting in which **urgent plasma exchange is lifesaving**, often started empirically before the result returns. By contrast, an activity of 43.5% is much more compatible with **secondary TMA or CM-HUS**, as in this case, where the kidney biopsy and clinical course supported complement-mediated disease rather than TTP.

So, in this patient, the ADAMTS13 result was used to **exclude TTP and redirect management toward complement-mediated HUS**, with plasma exchange only as a bridge while eculizumab was arranged.
