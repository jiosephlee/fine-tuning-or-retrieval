Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] Why might SGLT2 inhibitors be relatively unfavorable in patients with HNF1A-MODY, despite their common use in other forms of diabetes?

In **HNF1A-MODY**, SGLT2 inhibitors are relatively unfavorable because the renal glucose-handling defect is already part of the disease biology. HNF1A normally regulates expression of **SGLT2 in the proximal tubule**; when HNF1A is mutated, **SGLT2 expression is reduced**, lowering the renal threshold for glucose and causing **glycosuria even without SGLT2 inhibitor therapy**.  

If you then add an SGLT2 inhibitor, you may **further increase urinary glucose loss** and thereby worsen **osmotic diuresis, dehydration, and the risk of ketosis or diabetic ketoacidosis**. In the case report, the authors specifically note that glycosuria was present and that the risks of SGLT2 inhibition in HNF1A-MODY—**dehydration, vascular collapse, genital infections, and DKA**—may outweigh the benefits.  

So, unlike in typical type 2 diabetes, where SGLT2 inhibitors are often beneficial, in HNF1A-MODY they can **accentuate an already abnormal renal glucose-wasting state** and carry disproportionate safety concerns.
