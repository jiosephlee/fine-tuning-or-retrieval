Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the major clues in the pedigree that support autosomal dominant inheritance, and why is this pattern important when evaluating early-onset diabetes?

The pedigree in this case has the classic hallmarks of **autosomal dominant inheritance**: diabetes appears in **successive generations** rather than skipping generations; there is **vertical transmission** from the maternal grandmother to the mother to the proband’s younger brother; and the affected relatives are not confined to one sex, consistent with a **non–sex-linked** trait. Another important clue is the **early age at diagnosis** in multiple family members—grandmother in her 30s, mother in her late 20s, brother at 20—which strongly suggests a familial monogenic form of diabetes rather than typical type 2 diabetes.

This pattern matters because **early-onset diabetes with autosomal dominant inheritance should prompt consideration of MODY**, particularly **HNF1A-MODY** in a family like this. Recognizing the inheritance pattern helps avoid misclassification as type 1 or type 2 diabetes, supports **genetic testing**, and has direct implications for management and family counseling. In this case, the pedigree, together with negative islet autoantibodies and preserved C-peptide, made MODY much more likely and led to the correct molecular diagnosis and more appropriate therapy.
