Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the pathophysiologic role of HNF1A in pancreatic beta-cell function, and how do HNF1A mutations lead to progressive hyperglycaemia?

HNF1A encodes hepatocyte nuclear factor 1-alpha, a transcription factor that is highly expressed in pancreatic β cells and is essential for normal β-cell gene expression and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. In the pancreas, HNF1A regulates transcription of key genes involved in sensing and responding to glucose, including **insulin itself**, **GLUT2**, and enzymes such as **pyruvate kinase**. It also contributes to the maintenance of β-cell differentiation and functional reserve.

When HNF1A is mutated, as in this case of autosomal dominant HNF1A-MODY, the β cell does not fail abruptly; rather, it undergoes a **progressive decline in function**. Early on, patients may still have measurable C-peptide and no ketoacidosis, but their insulin secretory response to glucose is blunted. Over time, impaired transcription of these target genes leads to worsening postprandial hyperglycaemia, then fasting hyperglycaemia, and eventually more overt diabetes. This progressive defect explains why the patient initially had preserved but insufficient C-peptide and why his glycaemia improved with therapies that enhanced endogenous insulin secretion rather than requiring permanent insulin replacement.

The case also illustrates another HNF1A effect: reduced renal glucose reabsorption, causing **glycosuria at relatively lower glucose levels**, because HNF1A normally regulates tubular glucose transport, including SGLT2 expression. Thus, HNF1A mutations cause diabetes through a dual mechanism—**β-cell transcriptional dysfunction with progressive insulin secretory failure, plus lowered renal glucose threshold**—which is why these patients can present as young adults with glycosuria, marked hyperglycaemia, negative autoantibodies, and a strong autosomal dominant family history.
