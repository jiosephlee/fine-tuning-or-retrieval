Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] In the management of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), why is surgical valve replacement generally preferred over transcatheter interventions like TAVI, and what factors might lead a clinician to consider TAVI instead?

In the management of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), surgical valve replacement is generally the preferred standard of care because it allows for definitive surgical debridement of infected tissue, including vegetations, abscesses, and any associated perivalvular complications. This "clean" surgical environment is essential to ensure the longevity of the new prosthesis and to minimize the risk of recurrent infection.

However, clinical management must be individualized, and transcatheter interventions like TAVI may be considered in specific scenarios. In this case, TAVI was selected over surgery due to the following factors:

1.  **Prohibitive Surgical Risk:** The patient presented with a high EuroSCORE II (27.36%) and significant comorbidities, including COPD, type 2 diabetes, and a history of recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, making open surgery high-risk.
2.  **Anatomical Complexity:** The patient had a "triple-stented" anatomy with two prior transcatheter valves deeply integrated into the aortic wall. Surgical intervention in this context would have been technically hazardous and likely necessitated a total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement.
3.  **Resolution of Active Infection:** A critical prerequisite for considering TAVI in PVE is the absence of active infection. This patient had completed a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy for *Enterococcus faecalis*, and both clinical assessment and imaging (TEE and MSCT) confirmed the resolution of the infection without the need for long-term suppressive antibiotics.
4.  **Feasibility of Transcatheter Reintervention:** Advanced preprocedural planning using multislice CT (MSCT) and 3D modeling demonstrated that a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure was anatomically feasible. Specifically, a strategy-driven approach—including high implantation of the Acurate neo2 valve—was planned to preserve coronary flow and optimize the effective orifice area despite the complex multi-prosthesis configuration.

In summary, while surgery remains the guideline-recommended strategy for PVE to ensure adequate debridement, TAVI is a viable alternative in patients with prohibitive surgical risk, complex anatomy, and a fully resolved infection where detailed imaging confirms procedural feasibility.
