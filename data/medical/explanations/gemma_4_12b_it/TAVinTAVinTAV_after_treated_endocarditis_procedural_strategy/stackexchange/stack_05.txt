Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] The clinical team utilized a 'high implantation' strategy for the third transcatheter valve. What were the two primary anatomical goals of this maneuver in a triple-stented aortic root?

In the management of complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures, a "high implantation" strategy is a deliberate maneuver used to optimize both hemodynamics and long-term access. In this case, the clinical team utilized high implantation of the third transcatheter valve to achieve two primary anatomical goals:

1.  **Preservation of the Effective Orifice Area (EOA):** By positioning the third valve at the level of the preceding Evolut valve leaflets, the team intended to fix those pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame. This maneuver prevents the leaflets from folding inward, which would otherwise result in a further reduction of the EOA and exacerbate prosthesis-patient mismatch.
2.  **Coronary Protection and Access:** High implantation serves to minimize the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia. This is a critical consideration in a triple-stented anatomy, as it helps preserve unobstructed coronary flow and maintains the feasibility of future coronary interventions, which are often complicated by the cumulative mesh burden of multiple overlapping valve frames.

These goals are achieved through meticulous preprocedural CT modeling, which allows the operator to balance the need for adequate expansion with the necessity of avoiding iatrogenic coronary obstruction.
