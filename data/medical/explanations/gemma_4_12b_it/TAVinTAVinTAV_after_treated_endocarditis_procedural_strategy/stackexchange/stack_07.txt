Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] During the procedure, the team performed balloon pre-dilation and post-dilation under rapid ventricular pacing. What are the primary objectives of these maneuvers in the context of a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure?

In the context of a complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, balloon pre-dilation and post-dilation under rapid ventricular pacing (RVP) are critical maneuvers designed to optimize the geometry of the nested prostheses, maximize the effective orifice area (EOA), and ensure procedural safety.

**Pre-dilation** is performed to prepare the "landing zone" by enlarging the lumen of the previously implanted prosthesis (in this case, the Evolut valve). Given the severe restenosis and a minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm identified on MSCT, pre-dilation ensures that the new prosthesis (Acurate neo2) can be deployed without excessive resistance or frame deformation. This reduces the risk of iatrogenic injury to the previous valve frame and helps ensure that the new frame sits correctly within the intended anatomical space.

**Post-dilation** is primarily performed to optimize the expansion of the newly implanted valve and to address the high risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM) inherent in serial TAVI procedures. In this case, the immediate post-procedural AVA was only 0.9 cm², indicating severe PPM. High-pressure post-dilation serves two specific roles here:
1.  **Maximizing EOA:** It ensures the neo-skirt and leaflets of the Acurate neo2 are fully expanded against the previous frames, maximizing the available flow area.
2.  **Mechanical Expansion of the Prior Frame:** Uniquely in a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV configuration, post-dilation can provide enough radial force to mechanically expand the prior Evolut frame. This expansion improves leaflet motion and central flow dynamics, which was evidenced in this patient by the significant reduction in mean gradient from 67 mm Hg to 27 mm Hg and the increase in AVA to 1.1 cm² over the first month.

**Rapid Ventricular Pacing (RVP)** is utilized during both maneuvers as a safety standard. By inducing a high heart rate (e.g., 180 beats per minute), the procedure achieves a state of near-complete cardiac standstill. This minimizes the transvalvular pressure gradient across the aortic valve during balloon inflation, thereby reducing the risk of acute aortic root rupture, annular rupture, or coronary artery occlusion—all of which are heightened risks when maneuvering multiple overlapping metal frames in a restricted space.
