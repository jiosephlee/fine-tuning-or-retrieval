Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the three primary anatomical parameters evaluated using preprocedural multi-slice CT (MSCT) to mitigate the risk of coronary artery obstruction during a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure?

In the management of a complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, preprocedural multi-slice CT (MSCT) is essential for identifying anatomical nuances that could lead to iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction. According to the case report, the three primary parameters evaluated to mitigate this risk are the **landing zone**, **frame interaction**, and **valve-to-coronary distances**.

The clinical reasoning for evaluating these specific parameters is as follows:

*   **Landing Zone (Coronary Ostial Height):** This assessment ensures that there is sufficient anatomical space to anchor the prosthesis without the frame or neo-skirt encroaching upon the coronary ostia.
*   **Frame Interaction:** This is a critical parameter unique to sequential transcatheter valve reinterventions. The clinician must evaluate how multiple overlapping stent frames from prior procedures will interact, as the cumulative mesh burden can complicate flow dynamics and significantly impede future coronary access.
*   **Valve-to-Coronary Distances:** This provides a quantifiable metric to estimate the safety margin between the expanding valve and the coronary arteries, allowing for a predictable assessment of potential obstruction.

In this case, these MSCT findings guided a specific procedural strategy of **high implantation**. By deliberately implanting the third valve at the level of the existing Evolut valve leaflets, the team was able to fix the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame. This approach served two purposes: it prevented the inward folding of leaflets (which would have further reduced the effective orifice area) and minimized the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, thereby preserving coronary perfusion and future access without the need for coronary protection.
