Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] What is the primary therapeutic goal of administering high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) in the management of autoimmune HIT?

The primary therapeutic goal of administering high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) in the management of autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (aHIT) is to **rapidly increase the platelet count** by blocking the interaction between pathogenic autoantibodies and platelet Fc receptors.

In classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), antibodies require the presence of heparin to bind platelet factor 4 (PF4) and activate platelets. However, in aHIT, the antibodies possess heparin-independent platelet-activating properties. This distinction is clinically significant because aHIT often presents with more profound and refractory thrombocytopenia compared to classic HIT. 

While anticoagulation (such as with argatroban or fondaparinux) remains the cornerstone of therapy to manage the associated hypercoagulability and prevent thrombotic events—as seen in this patient who suffered a cerebral infarct—anticoagulation alone may be insufficient to rapidly reverse severe thrombocytopenia in aHIT. IVIg works by interfering with the binding of these autoantibodies to the Fc receptors on the surface of platelets, thereby inhibiting antibody-mediated platelet activation and destruction.

In this case, the patient’s aHIT was characterized by an unusually severe phenotype, including a platelet nadir of 13×10⁹/L and a unique serological profile showing partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade. The modest initial response to IVIg in this patient illustrates that while IVIg is the standard of care for managing the thrombocytopenic component of aHIT, the degree of platelet recovery can be influenced by the specific reactivity and resistance of the patient's unique antibody profile.
