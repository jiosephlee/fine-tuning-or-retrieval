Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the fundamental difference in the mechanism of platelet activation between classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) and autoimmune HIT (aHIT)?

The fundamental difference between classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) and autoimmune HIT (aHIT) lies in the requirement for heparin as a cofactor for antibody-mediated platelet activation.

In **classic HIT**, the pathophysiology involves the formation of immune complexes between IgG antibodies and the cationic protein Platelet Factor 4 (PF4). These antibodies are heparin-dependent, meaning they only recognize and bind to PF4 when it is complexed with polyanionic heparin. This complex subsequently binds to and clusters Fc receptors on the platelet surface, triggering platelet activation and a subsequent prothrombotic state.

In **autoimmune HIT (aHIT)**, the antibodies possess heparin-independent platelet-activating properties. These antibodies can bind to and activate platelets via Fc receptor clustering even in the absence of heparin. This distinction is clinically critical, as aHIT often presents with more severe, refractory thrombocytopenia and a higher risk of complications than classic HIT. 

In this case, the diagnosis of aHIT was confirmed by a serotonin-release assay (SRA) that demonstrated robust platelet activation (96% serotonin release) at 0 U/mL heparin, which is a hallmark of heparin-independent activity. Additionally, the patient’s unique laboratory profile—showing only partial inhibition of this activation by an Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody—suggested an unusually severe and atypical serological phenotype of aHIT.
