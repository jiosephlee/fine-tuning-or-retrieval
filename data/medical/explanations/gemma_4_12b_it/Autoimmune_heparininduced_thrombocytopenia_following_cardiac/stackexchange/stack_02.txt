Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] The patient’s platelet count began to fall before postoperative day 5 (POD5). What specific clinical factor in this patient’s history explains this unusually early onset of HIT-related thrombocytopenia?

The unusually early onset of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) in this patient—occurring before postoperative day 5 (POD5)—is explained by **preoperative heparin exposure**.

In the context of cardiac surgery, the standard temporal window for the development of HIT-related thrombocytopenia is typically between POD5 and POD10, reflecting the time required for the immune system to mount a response to the significant heparin load administered during cardiopulmonary bypass. However, as noted in this case and in clinical literature, approximately 10% of patients who develop HIT post-cardiac surgery exhibit a platelet count decline before POD5. This accelerated timeline occurs when the patient has been "primed" with heparin prior to the major surgical procedure.

In this specific case, the patient underwent a diagnostic heart catheterization one week before his cardiac surgery. During that procedure, he received an intraprocedural dose of 2000 units of unfractionated heparin (UFH). This prior exposure facilitated the early formation of immune complexes and subsequent platelet activation, leading to the rapid decline in his platelet count (from 210×10⁹/L preoperatively to 155×10⁹/L on POD1) before the standard postoperative window had elapsed. Recognizing preoperative anticoagulation is essential for accurately diagnosing HIT in patients who present with early postoperative thrombocytopenia.
