Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] In an adult patient weighing over 50 kg, what is the recommended dose of intramuscular epinephrine for anaphylaxis, and what is the clinical concern regarding the widespread use of 0.3 mg auto-injectors?

For an adult patient weighing over 50 kg, the recommended dose of intramuscular (IM) epinephrine for the treatment of anaphylaxis is 0.5 mg (based on the standard guideline of 0.01 mg/kg, capped at a maximum of 0.5 mg). 

The clinical concern regarding the widespread use of 0.3 mg auto-injectors—which are frequently available in the preclinical setting—is that they can lead to a potentially critical underdosing of epinephrine in adults over 50 kg. While these auto-injectors are valuable for reducing treatment hesitancy and providing rapid access to medication in non-clinical environments, the 0.3 mg dose may be insufficient to achieve a full clinical response in larger patients. Evidence suggests that patients initially receiving 0.3 mg are significantly more likely to require escalation of care, such as additional epinephrine injections, compared to those who receive the recommended 0.5 mg dose.

In this case, the 104 kg patient initially received 0.3 mg via auto-injector. While this provided a prompt response during her first episode, the subsequent multiphasic nature of her reaction highlighted the complexities of managing severe anaphylaxis, where ensuring adequate initial dosing is paramount to preventing the need for repeated interventions.
