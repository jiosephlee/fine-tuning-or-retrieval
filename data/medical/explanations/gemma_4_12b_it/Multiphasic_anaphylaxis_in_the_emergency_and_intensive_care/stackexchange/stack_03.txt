Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] Despite its traditional use in treating anaphylaxis, what does current evidence suggest regarding the efficacy of corticosteroids in preventing biphasic reactions?

Despite the long-standing tradition of administering corticosteroids to prevent biphasic reactions in anaphylaxis, current evidence does not support their efficacy for this purpose. A systematic review of 22 studies found that only one study supported the use of corticosteroids to reduce the risk of biphasic reactions, while 21 studies showed no such benefit.

Furthermore, some evidence suggests that corticosteroids may have adverse effects, including a potential increase in the likelihood of requiring intensive care or hospital admission following prehospital treatment. Consequently, most contemporary guidelines now advise against the routine administration of corticosteroids in the management of anaphylaxis. While they may still be utilized in some clinical settings due to established tradition, they should not be relied upon to prevent recurrence. The cornerstone of management remains the prompt administration of intramuscular epinephrine and appropriate supportive care.
