Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] When evaluating a patient for anaphylaxis, what is the optimal timing for obtaining serial tryptase levels to accurately establish a baseline and identify peak levels during a reaction?

To accurately evaluate anaphylaxis and screen for underlying mast cell disorders, guidelines recommend obtaining at least three serial tryptase samples at specific intervals. The optimal timing is as follows:

1.  **Initial Sample:** This should be collected as soon as feasible without delaying life-saving treatment (such as the administration of epinephrine).
2.  **Peak Sample:** A second sample should be obtained 1–2 hours (but no later than 4 hours) after the onset of symptoms. This timing is critical because serum tryptase levels typically peak during this window following mast cell degranulation.
3.  **Baseline Sample:** A third sample must be taken a minimum of 24 hours after full clinical resolution of the anaphylactic episode to establish the patient's baseline tryptase level.

The clinical reasoning for this serial testing is to differentiate between acute mast cell activation and underlying mast cell pathology. By comparing the peak level to the 24-hour baseline, clinicians can better characterize the severity of the reaction. Furthermore, a significantly elevated baseline tryptase level (as seen in the patient in this case report) is a key indicator of systemic mastocytosis. 

In the presented case, the failure to adhere to these recommended intervals during the initial anaphylactic episodes hindered the early identification of the patient's potential systemic mastocytosis. For clinicians, maintaining this strict sampling schedule is essential for both the diagnostic workup of the acute event and the long-term management of the patient's underlying condition.
