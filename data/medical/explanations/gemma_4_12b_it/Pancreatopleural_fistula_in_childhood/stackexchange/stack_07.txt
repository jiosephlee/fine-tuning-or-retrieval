Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] In a patient with a pancreatopleural fistula who fails to respond to conservative management after 3–4 weeks, what are the primary endoscopic and surgical intervention options?

In cases of pancreatopleural fistula (PPF) that do not respond to conservative management—which typically includes bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, somatostatin analogues (such as Octreotide), and pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy—the management strategy shifts toward endoscopic or surgical intervention.

The primary endoscopic intervention is Endoscopic Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) for trans-papillary stenting. ERCP remains the gold standard for therapeutic planning and visualization of the fistula. In patients with ductal stenosis, endoscopic management may involve balloon dilatation followed by stent insertion. The primary physiological goal of stenting is to reduce intraductal pressure, thereby minimizing the leakage of pancreatic secretions through the fistulous tract and allowing the tract to close.

Surgical intervention is indicated if there is no clinical response to medical management after 3–4 weeks or if the anatomy of the fistula precludes endoscopic access, such as in cases of severe ductal obstruction or a distally located fistula. Surgical options include a distal pancreatectomy, pancreatojejunostomy (the Puestow procedure), or cystojejunostomy. These procedures aim to provide a definitive solution by addressing the source of the pancreatic leakage and redirecting secretions away from the pleural space. While surgical intervention is more invasive, it has demonstrated high success rates in pediatric populations when conservative and endoscopic methods are insufficient.
