Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] A patient presents with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury. What is the most critical laboratory finding used to differentiate Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) from Complement-mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (CM-HUS)?

The most critical laboratory finding used to differentiate Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) from complement-mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (CM-HUS) is the **ADAMTS13 activity level**.

In the clinical evaluation of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), which is characterized by the triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury (AKI), it is essential to distinguish between different underlying pathophysiologies. TTP is specifically defined by a severe deficiency in the ADAMTS13 enzyme, a metalloprotease responsible for cleaving large von Willebrand factor (vWF) multimers. In the absence of sufficient ADAMTS13 activity (typically defined as <10%), these ultra-large vWF multimers persist in the circulation and cause spontaneous platelet aggregation and microthrombi formation.

Conversely, CM-HUS is driven by the dysregulated activation of the alternative complement pathway, leading to endothelial damage and TMA. As demonstrated in this case, a patient may present with identical clinical features of TMA—including schistocytes on peripheral smear, profound thrombocytopenia, and renal failure—making clinical differentiation difficult. In this patient, the ADAMTS13 activity was measured at 43.5%. Because this value is significantly higher than the diagnostic threshold for TTP (<10%), it effectively ruled out TTP as the primary etiology and pointed toward a complement-mediated process.

It is important to note that while complement levels (C3 and C4) are often measured in the workup of CM-HUS, they may remain within normal limits during the acute phase of the disease, as seen in this patient. Therefore, while complement assays are part of the broader diagnostic workup, the quantification of ADAMTS13 activity remains the definitive laboratory standard for excluding TTP in the setting of TMA.
