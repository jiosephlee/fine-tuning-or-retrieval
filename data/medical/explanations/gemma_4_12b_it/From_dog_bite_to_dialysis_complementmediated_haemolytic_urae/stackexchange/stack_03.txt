Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why might a patient with Complement-mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (CM-HUS) present with normal serum C3 and C4 levels during the acute phase of the disease?

In patients with complement-mediated hemolytic uremic syndrome (CM-HUS), serum C3 and C4 levels may remain within normal limits during the acute phase because the primary pathophysiology involves the dysregulated activation of the **alternative complement pathway**, rather than the classical or lectin pathways.

Standard laboratory assays for C3 and C4 are primarily used to screen for the consumption of complement components associated with the classical and lectin pathways (such as those seen in systemic lupus erythematosus or certain types of glomerulonephritis). In contrast, CM-HUS is characterized by an uncontrolled "amplification loop" within the alternative pathway, often triggered by an external stimulus—such as the *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* infection seen in this case—acting upon a host with a genetic predisposition or an acquired regulatory defect (e.g., involving Factor H, Factor I, or Factor B).

Because the alternative pathway can be hyper-activated without significant consumption of the classical pathway components, C3 and C4 levels are often non-diagnostic. As highlighted in this case, the diagnosis of CM-HUS is primarily a clinicopathological one, established by the presence of thrombotic microangiopathy (MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury) and the clinical response to terminal complement blockade with eculizumab. The eventual finding of reduced alternative pathway activity (AH50) in this patient underscores that specialized functional assays are required to evaluate the alternative pathway specifically, as standard C3/C4 testing may provide a false sense of reassurance.
