Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] When evaluating a patient with fever, headache, and lymphocytic pleocytosis in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), what is the systematic approach to excluding infectious versus non-infectious etiologies of aseptic meningitis?

When evaluating a patient with fever, headache, and lymphocytic pleocytosis in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), the primary diagnostic priority is to systematically rule out infectious etiologies before considering non-infectious causes of aseptic meningitis.

The systematic approach begins with comprehensive microbiological screening of the CSF. This includes cultures to rule out bacterial and mycobacterial pathogens (such as *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*), and rapid molecular or antigen testing for common viral pathogens, particularly herpes simplex virus (HSV) and varicella-zoster virus (VZV), as well as screening for fungal pathogens (e.g., cryptococcal antigen). In the reported case, these negative results were foundational in narrowing the differential diagnosis.

Once infectious causes are effectively excluded, the clinician must evaluate for non-infectious triggers of CNS inflammation, namely malignancy and systemic inflammatory disorders. Neuroimaging (brain MRI) and CSF cytology are essential in this step; a normal MRI and negative cytology help to deprioritize primary CNS lymphoma or other malignancies. In this case, the absence of parenchymal lesions or malignant cells shifted the focus toward systemic inflammatory diseases.

The final stage of the systematic approach involves a meticulous synthesis of the patient’s systemic clinical features. The diagnosis of acute neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD) in this patient was established by correlating the aseptic meningitis with hallmark mucocutaneous findings: oral aphthae, folliculitis-like rashes, and genital ulcers. It is important to note that while inflammatory markers (such as serum ACE or sIL-2R) can be measured to screen for conditions like sarcoidosis, they should be interpreted with caution and utilized only when supported by specific clinical features to avoid indiscriminate testing. 

In summary, the diagnostic path follows a "rule-out" hierarchy: first, exclude common pathogens through CSF microbiology; second, rule out malignancy through imaging and cytology; and third, identify specific inflammatory conditions by correlating neurological symptoms with systemic clinical signs. In patients with neuro-BD, the resulting CNS inflammation can also manifest as secondary complications, such as the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), which may further complicate the clinical presentation with symptoms like hyponatraemia and hiccups.
