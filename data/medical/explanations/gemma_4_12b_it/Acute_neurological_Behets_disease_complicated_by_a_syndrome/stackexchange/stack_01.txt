Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] The patient presented with several mucocutaneous findings. Which specific clinical features are most characteristic of Behçet’s disease (BD) and were present in this case?

The hallmark mucocutaneous manifestations of Behçet’s disease (BD) are recurrent oral aphthae, genital ulcers (genital aphthosis), and various cutaneous lesions. In this case, the patient presented with a constellation of these characteristic features: **oral aphthae**, a **folliculitis-like rash** on the face and trunk, and a **painful scrotal ulcer** (genital aphthosis) which appeared on day 5 of admission.

From a diagnostic perspective, these findings are fundamental to the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease. The patient met the requirements for "definite BD" by accumulating a total score of five points: two points for oral aphthae, two points for genital aphthosis, and one point for the folliculitis-like rash (a score of $\geq$4 is required for a definite diagnosis). It is clinically significant to note that genital aphthosis is considered a highly specific finding for BD. 

Recognizing these mucocutaneous lesions is critical for the diagnosis of systemic BD, especially when presenting alongside non-specific neurological symptoms like headache or fever, which can otherwise mimic other inflammatory or infectious processes. In this instance, the identification of these specific features allowed for the concurrent diagnosis of neuro-Behçet's disease.
