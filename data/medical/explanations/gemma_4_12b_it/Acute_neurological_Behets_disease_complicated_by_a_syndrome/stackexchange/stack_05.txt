Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] What are the primary diagnostic criteria for SIADH, and how were they confirmed in this patient’s laboratory results?

The diagnosis of the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) is established based on a specific constellation of clinical and laboratory findings. The primary diagnostic criteria include:

1.  **Hypotonic hyponatremia:** A low serum sodium concentration accompanied by a low serum osmolality.
2.  **Inappropriately concentrated urine:** An elevated urine osmolality (typically >100 mOsm/kg) in the presence of systemic hypotonicity.
3.  **Renal salt wasting:** An elevated urinary sodium concentration (usually >20 mmol/L), which distinguishes SIADH from hypovolemic states where the kidneys would normally conserve sodium.
4.  **Euvolemic status:** The patient must appear clinically euvolemic, without signs of significant volume depletion or excess.
5.  **Exclusion of secondary causes:** Ruling out other conditions that cause hyponatremia, such as renal failure, hypothyroidism, or adrenal insufficiency.

In this case, the laboratory results provided definitive confirmation of SIADH. The patient presented with marked hyponatremia (124 mmol/L) and a low serum osmolality (267 mOsm/kg). His urine osmolality was significantly elevated at 900 mOsm/kg, confirming that his kidneys were inappropriately concentrating urine despite the low systemic osmolality. The urinary sodium concentration was high at 88 mmol/L, supporting the diagnosis of renal salt wasting. Clinical assessment confirmed he was euvolemic, and laboratory evaluations ruled out common mimics: his thyroid-stimulating hormone (1.11 mIU/L), free T4 (1.25 ng/dL), and serum cortisol (42 µg/dL) were all within normal limits. Furthermore, his ADH level was inappropriately elevated (22.4 pg/mL) despite the hypotonic environment, providing biochemical evidence of the syndrome.
