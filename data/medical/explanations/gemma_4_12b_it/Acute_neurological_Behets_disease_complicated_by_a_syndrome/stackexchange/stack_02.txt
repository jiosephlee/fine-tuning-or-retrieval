Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] In the context of acute neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD), what is the hypothesized pathophysiological mechanism linking CNS inflammation to the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH)?

The hypothesized pathophysiological mechanism linking CNS inflammation in acute neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD) to the syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone (SIADH) involves the dysregulation of antidiuretic hormone (ADH) release mediated by pro-inflammatory cytokines—specifically interleukin-6 (IL-6).

In neuro-BD, the primary pathology involves significant CNS inflammation. This inflammatory environment triggers the production of IL-6, which is frequently elevated in various neuroinflammatory disorders and has been shown to stimulate the release of ADH. Within the specific context of Behçet’s disease, aberrant monocyte activation leads to the spontaneous production of IL-6. Clinical evidence has demonstrated that elevated IL-6 levels can be detected in the cerebrospinal fluid of patients with acute neuro-BD, suggesting that IL-6-mediated inflammation serves as a key driver for the non-physiologic secretion of ADH. This persistent secretion leads to impaired water excretion and the subsequent development of hyponatremia characteristic of SIADH. 

In this case, although IL-6 levels were not directly measured, the patient’s markedly elevated serum C-reactive protein (CRP) likely reflects an underlying IL-6-mediated inflammatory response that contributed to the concurrent presentation of acute neuro-BD and SIADH.
