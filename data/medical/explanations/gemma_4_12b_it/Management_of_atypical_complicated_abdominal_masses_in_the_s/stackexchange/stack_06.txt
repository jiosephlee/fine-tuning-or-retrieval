Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Morning Report] The cranial mass was found to be a twisted and leaking pedunculated leiomyoma. What are the immediate clinical risks associated with such a complication, and what surgical principles were employed to manage the mass while preserving the pregnancy?

The identification of a twisted and leaking pedunculated leiomyoma during pregnancy presents several acute clinical risks. Torsion of a pedunculated myoma leads to rapid vascular compromise, resulting in ischemia, infarction, and subsequent necrotic degeneration. In this case, the "leaking" nature of the mass, combined with the patient's low-grade fever and elevated white blood cell count, suggests that necrosis or rupture may have led to local inflammatory changes or early peritonitis. These complications can precipitate acute maternal morbidity, including severe pain, systemic inflammatory response, and potential obstetric complications such as preterm labor or placental abruption.

Management of such a mass requires a balanced surgical approach that prioritizes the preservation of the pregnancy while addressing the acute pathology. The surgical principles employed in this case included:

1.  **Selective Intervention:** The surgical team opted to treat only the symptomatic, complicated cranial mass. By excising only the twisted, leaking myoma, they addressed the immediate clinical emergency while avoiding unnecessary intervention on the caudal mass.
2.  **Minimizing Iatrogenic Trauma:** The mass was excised "gently" using a double clamp technique, and the exposed uterine root was carefully closed with a single absorbable suture to ensure hemostasis and minimize trauma to the myometrium.
3.  **Avoidance of Overtreatment:** A key principle in managing abdominal masses during pregnancy is to avoid over-aggressive surgery. The caudal mass was left *in situ* because it was fixed and lacked acute complications; removing it would have increased the risk of hemorrhage and uterine injury without significant benefit to the immediate clinical outcome.
4.  **Obstetric Safety Measures:** To protect the fetus during the laparotomy, the team employed continuous fetal heart rate monitoring, administered intraoperative progesterone prophylaxis to maintain uterine quiescence, and performed the procedure with a focus on minimizing operative time (completed in approximately 2.5 hours).

By adhering to these principles—treating the complication, preserving the stable anatomy, and utilizing standard obstetric protections—the team successfully managed the acute pathology while ensuring an excellent maternal and neonatal outcome.
