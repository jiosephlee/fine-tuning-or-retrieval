Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] The patient presented with dyspnea and severe upper quadrant pain. Based on the clinical findings, what is the primary mechanism of her respiratory distress, and how did the medical team provide symptomatic relief?

The patient's respiratory distress was primarily caused by **mechanical compression** of the diaphragm and thoracic cavity. This was attributed to the "steric bulk" of the gravid uterus combined with the enormous abdominal masses—specifically the 18 cm upper mass occupying the left subphrenic space—which restricted diaphragmatic excursion. The clinical reasoning for this diagnosis was supported by the fact that the patient maintained a normal oxygen saturation (97%), a normal heart rate, and a normal ECG, which effectively ruled out primary pulmonary or cardiac dysfunction.

To provide symptomatic relief, the medical team utilized a combination of **analgesic therapy** and **positional management**. Specifically, the patient was treated with intravenous paracetamol (1000 mg twice daily) and placed in a **semi-orthostatic position**. This positioning was intended to hypothetically limit the diaphragmatic compression exerted by the large abdominal masses and the pregnant uterus. These interventions were successful in reducing the patient's symptoms (decreasing pain on the Visual Analogue Scale from 5 to 1) without the need for supplemental oxygen or mechanical ventilation.
