Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] Explain the physiological variation of serum CA-125 levels during pregnancy and why these fluctuations limit its utility as a definitive diagnostic marker for ovarian neoplasms in this population.

The serum CA-125 marker undergoes well-documented physiological fluctuations during pregnancy, typically characterized by a biphasic rise. Specifically, CA-125 levels tend to peak during the first trimester (median 23 U/mL) and the third trimester (median 21 U/mL), while remaining lower during the second trimester (median 14.0 U/mL). 

These fluctuations, combined with the fact that CA-125 is a non-specific marker of peritoneal irritation, significantly limit its utility as a definitive diagnostic tool for ovarian neoplasms in pregnant patients. The marker can be elevated by a wide array of benign conditions, including endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, menstruation, and—as seen in this case—uterine leiomyomas. 

In the presented case, the patient’s CA-125 level was 41.8 U/mL. While this exceeded the conventional oncological cut-off of 35 U/mL, it was only a slight elevation above the expected median for the second trimester. Because of the "background noise" created by the physiological state of pregnancy and the potential for elevation by benign pelvic pathology, an isolated CA-125 measurement lacks the specificity required to distinguish between a malignant neoplasm and a benign mass. Consequently, clinical reasoning must rely on serial measurements to identify significant trends rather than single values, complemented by safe imaging modalities (such as non-contrast MRI) and thorough clinical evaluation to guide management.
