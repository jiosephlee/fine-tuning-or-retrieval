Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

Chapter 4: ## Management Strategies and Clinical Outcomes

The management of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) focuses on rapid suppression of the underlying inflammatory process to prevent further muscle necrosis and functional decline. In this case, the severity of the patient’s presentation—characterized by significant proximal muscle weakness and dysphagia—necessitated aggressive initial intervention. The patient was started on pulse methylprednisolone, receiving 1000 mg intravenously daily for five days. High-dose corticosteroid "pulses" are a standard clinical strategy for severe inflammatory myopathies, aimed at achieving a rapid reduction in systemic inflammation and improving muscle strength more quickly than oral steroids alone might allow. Following the pulse therapy, the patient was transitioned to a maintenance dose of oral prednisone (1 mg/kg daily) to sustain the immunosuppressive effect.

Because the patient exhibited persistent weakness and severe clinical features despite initial steroid treatment, adjunctive therapy with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) was initiated. IVIG is frequently employed in the management of severe myopathies and inflammatory disorders to modulate the immune response, potentially by neutralizing pathogenic autoantibodies or interfering with complement activation. In this instance, the patient received 2 g/kg of IVIG over four days. The combination of high-dose corticosteroids and IVIG is a standard approach for patients who do not show an immediate, robust response to steroids alone or those who present with life-threatening complications, such as the dysphagia seen in this case.

A critical component of managing any steroid-dependent myopathy is the gradual tapering of the medication to minimize long-term side effects while ensuring the disease remains in remission. In this report, the patient demonstrated a favorable response to the initial treatment, evidenced by a marked reduction in creatine kinase (CK) levels within two weeks of starting therapy. Because the CK levels remained normal and her functional status improved significantly, she was able to be weaned off prednisone rapidly over a two-month period. The ability to taper steroids quickly is a positive prognostic indicator, suggesting that the inflammatory process was effectively suppressed and that the condition may have been a drug-induced inflammatory event rather than a chronic, progressive idiopathic myopathy.

The ultimate goal of treatment in IMNM is the restoration of baseline function and the normalization of biochemical markers. This patient achieved a successful clinical outcome, regaining her baseline functional status and remaining off all immunosuppressive medications for eight months. Furthermore, the normalization of her CK levels provided biochemical evidence of halted muscle necrosis. The resolution of imaging abnormalities, specifically the normalization of myocardial edema and hypokinesis on cardiac MRI and echocardiogram, further confirms the resolution of the systemic inflammatory process. This case highlights the importance of a multi-modal approach—combining aggressive initial immunosuppression, monitoring of biochemical markers like CK, and longitudinal follow-up to ensure the resolution of both muscular and secondary systemic involvement.
