Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] While GLP-1 receptor agonists are commonly associated with gastrointestinal side effects, what specific muscular adverse effects have been reported, and how does the mechanism of IMNM differ from simple toxic myopathy?

While glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists are predominantly associated with gastrointestinal side effects, they have also been reported to cause a loss of skeletal muscle mass and, in rare instances, rhabdomyolysis. Distinguishing between a simple toxic myopathy and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) is essential, as their clinical courses and management strategies differ significantly.

Simple toxic myopathy, such as drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, is typically characterized by a marked, acute elevation of creatine kinase (CK) that improves rapidly—often within days—following the discontinuation of the offending agent and initiation of intravenous hydration. In such cases, clinical resolution occurs without the need for systemic immunosuppression.

In contrast, IMNM is a distinct autoimmune entity characterized by aggressive proximal muscle weakness and specific histological features, including necrotizing myofibers with a paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates. Histopathology in IMNM often reveals myophagocytosis (macrophage infiltration) and membrane attack complex deposition on necrotic fibers. Clinically, as illustrated in the reported case of semaglutide-associated IMNM, the progression is often more protracted; this patient demonstrated a 12-fold increase in CK over four months rather than a rapid decline. Furthermore, a hallmark of IMNM—whether idiopathic or drug-induced—is the requirement for significant immunosuppressive therapy, such as pulse corticosteroids and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), to achieve clinical improvement and normalization of CK levels.
