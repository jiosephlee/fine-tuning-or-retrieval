Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Based on the clinical course described, how does the progression of this patient's symptoms and creatine kinase (CK) levels differentiate an immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from acute drug-induced rhabdomyolysis?

The differentiation between immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) and acute drug-induced rhabdomyolysis in this case hinges on the temporal progression of the creatine kinase (CK) levels and the clinical kinetics of the patient's weakness.

In a classic case of acute drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, such as the tirzepatide-associated case described in the background of this report, one expects a rapid, massive spike in CK levels (often into the tens of thousands) that begins to resolve within days of discontinuing the offending agent and initiating hydration. In this patient, however, the CK was only modestly elevated (337 U/L) immediately following the first dose of semaglutide. The subsequent 12-fold increase in CK to 3992 U/L over a 4-month period—coupled with the progressive development of severe proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, and dysphonia—is much more characteristic of a subacute to chronic necrotising myopathy.

Furthermore, the clinical response to treatment provides a key diagnostic clue. While rhabdomyolysis typically resolves with supportive care and drug withdrawal, this patient required and responded significantly to aggressive immunosuppression, specifically pulse methylprednisolone and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG). This response suggests an underlying auto-inflammatory or immune-mediated mechanism rather than a direct toxic effect on the muscle fibers. These clinical observations, supported by the histopathology showing necrotising myofibres and macrophage infiltration (CD68 staining), point toward a drug-induced IMNM rather than a simple acute rhabdomyolytic event.
