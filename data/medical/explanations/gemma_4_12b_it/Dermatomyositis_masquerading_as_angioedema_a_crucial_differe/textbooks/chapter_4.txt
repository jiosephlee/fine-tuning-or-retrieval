Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

Chapter 4: ## Management Strategies and Long-term Surveillance

The management of severe inflammatory myopathies, such as dermatomyositis, requires a rapid and aggressive multi-modal approach, particularly when systemic complications like severe soft tissue swelling or dysphagia are present. In this case, the severity of the patient's cervical edema and the threat to his airway necessitated high-dose "pulse" therapy. The patient was initiated on intravenous methylprednisolone (1000 mg daily for three days) to rapidly dampen the systemic inflammatory response. Furthermore, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) was administered. While the exact indications for IVIG in myositis can vary, it is frequently employed in severe presentations, including those with significant edema or refractory symptoms, to provide rapid immunomodulation.

Once the acute inflammatory phase was stabilized, the focus shifted to a long-term maintenance strategy designed to minimize the morbidity associated with chronic corticosteroid use. Steroid-sparing agents are essential in managing dermatomyositis to allow for a gradual taper of prednisone. A critical clinical decision in this case was the selection of mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) over methotrexate. While methotrexate is a common first-line steroid-sparing agent, it is often avoided or used with extreme caution in patients with existing pulmonary involvement or a restrictive pattern on pulmonary function tests, as it can exacerbate interstitial lung disease (ILD) and fibrosis. Given the patient’s restrictive lung pattern and aspiration-related consolidation, MMF provided a safer profile for achieving remission while protecting pulmonary function.

Managing the acute complications of the disease is as vital as the primary pharmacological treatment. The patient suffered from severe pharyngeal dysphagia, which presented a significant risk for aspiration. This was clinically evidenced by the presence of peribronchial consolidation on CT, likely representing aspiration pneumonitis. To ensure adequate nutrition and prevent further pulmonary complications, a nasogastric tube was required for two months. The successful resolution of these complications—evidenced by the eventual removal of the tube and the resolution of pulmonary opacities—serves as a primary indicator of treatment efficacy in severe myositis.

A cornerstone of the long-term management of adult dermatomyositis is the systematic screening for underlying malignancy. Because dermatomyositis is frequently paraneoplastic, clinicians must use standardized protocols like the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines to stratify risk. This patient was classified as high risk due to several factors: the diagnosis of dermatomyositis, the presence of dysphagia, his male sex, and the presence of the SAE1 antibody. Consequently, comprehensive screening including CT imaging, tumor markers, and a PET scan were performed. Ongoing surveillance, including planned gastroscopy and colonoscopy, is mandatory to ensure early detection of any occult malignancy.

The clinical outcome for this patient highlights the importance of early diagnosis and aggressive intervention. The normalization of creatine kinase (CK) and the full recovery of proximal muscle strength within three weeks of treatment demonstrate the effectiveness of the initial pulse therapy and IVIG. Furthermore, the complete resolution of the "pseudoangioedema" and the successful tapering of prednisone over seven months underscore the importance of a structured, step-down treatment plan. For the clinician, this case reinforces that while dermatomyositis can present with atypical features like neck swelling, standard management principles—rapid immunosuppression, careful selection of steroid-sparing agents, and rigorous malignancy screening—remain the pillars of care.
