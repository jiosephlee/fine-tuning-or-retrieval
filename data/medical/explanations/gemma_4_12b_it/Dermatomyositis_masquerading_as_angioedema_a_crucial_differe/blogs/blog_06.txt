Title: Blog about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

# Aggressive Immunosuppression: Navigating Treatment in Severe Inflammatory Myopathies

Imagine a patient presents to your ED with a neck so swollen it looks like a surgical emergency. The clinical team is sweating, fearing Ludwig’s angina or a rapidly progressing deep neck space infection. Then comes the pivot: the patient is afebrile, the nasendoscopy is clear of structural abnormalities, and the "angioedema" doesn't seem to respond to standard treatments. 

This is the "masquerade" of dermatomyositis (DM). While we usually think of DM as a skin and muscle disease, it can manifest as pseudoangioedema—non-histamine, non-bradykinin mediated swelling. In this specific case, a man in his 40s presented with severe neck oedema and dysphagia. The diagnosis was eventually confirmed as SAE1-antibody-positive dermatomyositis, with a staggering creatine kinase (CK) level of over 16,000 U/L. 

When dermatomyositis presents this aggressively—involving the airway muscles and causing significant systemic inflammation—"standard" management isn't enough. We have to move into the realm of aggressive, multi-modal immunosuppression.

## The "Firefighting" Phase: Pulse Steroids and IVIG

When a patient presents with acute, life-threatening involvement (like severe dysphagia or impending airway compromise from muscle inflammation), the goal is to "put out the fire" as quickly as possible. 

In this case, the patient received 3 days of high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone (1000 mg daily). Pulse steroids are the gold standard for inducing rapid remission in severe inflammatory myopathies. However, when steroids alone aren't sufficient or the disease is particularly refractory, we bring in the heavy artillery: Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG). 

IVIG remains a cornerstone for severe DM, especially when there is significant soft tissue oedema or rapid progression. This patient received a massive loading dose (2 g/kg) followed by multiple cycles. The mechanism isn't fully understood, but it likely involves neutralizing pathogenic autoantibodies and modulating the innate immune response. In the setting of "pseudoangioedema" caused by myositis, IVIG can be a game-changer for rapid de-escalation of swelling.

## The Long Game: Steroid-Sparing Agents and the Pulmonary Nuance

Once the acute crisis is stabilized, the focus shifts to maintaining remission while minimizing the long-term toxicity of systemic steroids. This is where the choice of "steroid-sparing" agents becomes a nuanced clinical decision.

The standard workhorses are often Methotrexate or Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF). However, this case highlights a critical clinical pearl regarding pulmonary involvement. This patient had an extrathoracic restrictive pattern on pulmonary function tests and patchy consolidation on CT. 

Because of the risk of exacerbating interstitial lung disease (ILD) or inducing pulmonary fibrosis, **Methotrexate was intentionally avoided.** In patients with concurrent pulmonary concerns, Mycophenolate Mofetil is often the preferred steroid-sparing agent. It offers a robust profile for managing inflammatory myopathy without the same level of pulmonary risk associated with methotrexate.

## Adding Precision: Rituximab

Finally, we have to consider biological modifiers. Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody against CD20, has become increasingly common in the management of refractory or severe dermatomyositis. By depleting B-cells, it hits the underlying autoimmune engine of the disease. In this case, two doses of 1000 mg were added to the regimen of steroids and MMF, providing a multi-pronged attack on the inflammatory process.

## Clinical Pearls

*   **Look beyond the surface:** If a patient presents with "idiopathic" angioedema or neck swelling that doesn't fit the classic clinical picture (no ACE inhibitor use, no C1-esterase deficiency), consider dermatomyositis.
*   **The CK is your friend:** A significantly elevated creatine kinase (CK) in a patient with unexplained oedema should immediately trigger a search for inflammatory myopathy.
*   **Don't ignore the "subtle" signs:** Look for periungual erythema, nailfold microhaemorrhages, and subtle rashes on the thighs or face (Heliotrope eruption).
*   **Tailor the immunosuppression:** When pulmonary issues are present, be extremely cautious with Methotrexate; Mycophenolate Mofetil may be a safer long-term steroid-sparing option.
*   **Malignancy is a must:** In any adult with dermatomyositis (especially those with dysphagia or SAE1 antibodies), a rigorous malignancy screening is mandatory.

## Key Take-Home Messages

*   **Dermatomyositis can masquerade as angioedema.** If the diagnosis is unclear, check the CK and look for skin/nail changes.
*   **Severe presentations require "heavy" initial therapy.** High-dose pulse steroids and IVIG are vital for rapid stabilization of airway-threatening muscle inflammation.
*   **Pulmonary status dictates drug choice.** Avoid methotrexate in patients with existing pulmonary restrictive patterns; consider Mycophenolate Mofetil instead.
*   **Multi-modal therapy is often necessary.** Combining steroids, MMF, and Rituximab can be effective for achieving remission in severe, antibody-positive cases.
