Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone

### [Differential Diagnosis] Which cerebrospinal fluid and clinical findings in this case argue against bacterial meningitis, and why do they favor an aseptic inflammatory process?

The CSF and clinical picture in this patient argue against bacterial meningitis and instead point to an aseptic inflammatory process for two related reasons: the nature of the CSF reaction and the clinical course.

From the CSF: opening pressure was only mildly increased, cell count showed a mild lymphocytic pleocytosis (11 cells/µL) rather than the marked neutrophilic pleocytosis typically seen in acute bacterial meningitis, and protein was only modestly elevated (0.97 g/L). Repeated microbiologic studies—including CSF cultures, acid‑fast staining and nucleic acid testing for common viral and mycobacterial pathogens—were negative. In classic bacterial meningitis you expect a high neutrophil predominance (often hundreds to thousands of cells/µL), markedly elevated protein, low CSF glucose, positive Gram stain/culture or PCR in most cases, and a rapidly progressive, toxic clinical picture. Those findings are absent here.

From the clinical course: the patient had relatively low‑grade fever, no neck stiffness or focal deficits, normal brain MRI, and a subacute course that improved without prolonged broad‑spectrum antibiotics but promptly with anti‑inflammatory therapy (colchicine) directed at Behçet’s. The presence of systemic mucocutaneous features meeting criteria for Behçet’s and the temporal association of CNS symptoms with those manifestations further supports a sterile, immune‑mediated meningeal inflammation (acute neuro‑Behçet’s) rather than bacterial infection.

Putting these together: the sterile CSF with lymphocytic predominance and only mild protein elevation, negative microbiology, lack of neutrophilic dominance and an inflammatory systemic context that responded to anti‑inflammatory therapy favor an aseptic (immune/inflammatory) meningitis—consistent with acute neuro‑Behçet’s—over bacterial meningitis.
