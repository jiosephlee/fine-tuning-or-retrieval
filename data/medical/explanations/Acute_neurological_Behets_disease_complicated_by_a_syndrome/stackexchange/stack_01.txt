Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone

### [Morning Report] In a febrile, euvolemic patient with headache and hyponatraemia, which specific laboratory criteria establish SIADH after excluding hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency, and what thresholds would you expect for serum osmolality, urine osmolality, and urine sodium?

Answer — diagnostic criteria and expected thresholds

SIADH is a diagnosis of hypo‑osmolar, euvolaemic hyponatraemia made after excluding hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, renal failure and recent diuretic use. The key laboratory features are:

1. Hypotonic hyponatraemia
   - Serum sodium <135 mmol/L with low plasma/serum osmolality (classically <275 mOsm/kg; many labs use <270 mOsm/kg).  
   - In the reported case: serum osmolality 267 mOsm/kg with Na 124 mmol/L.

2. Inappropriately concentrated urine for the plasma hypo-osmolality
   - Urine osmolality >100 mOsm/kg indicates inability to maximally dilute; a urine osmolality >300 mOsm/kg is strong evidence of active ADH effect.  
   - In the case: urine osmolality 900 mOsm/kg (markedly concentrated).

3. Elevated urine sodium (reflecting euvolaemia and intact renal salt handling)
   - Urine Na typically >20–30 mmol/L (many sources use >30–40 mmol/L) when the patient is euvolemic and eating a normal salt diet.  
   - In the case: urine Na 88 mmol/L.

4. Clinical euvolaemia on exam (no signs of hypovolaemia or oedema) and normal renal, thyroid and adrenal function
   - Exclude hypothyroidism (normal TSH, free T4 in the case) and adrenal insufficiency (baseline cortisol and/or stimulated cortisol; the patient’s cortisol was clearly not low).  
   - Also exclude recent diuretic use and other causes (renal failure, hyperglycaemia causing translocational hyponatraemia).

Additional points
- Direct ADH/AVP measurement is not required for diagnosis and is variably available; it may be elevated (as in this case: ADH 22.4 pg/mL) but is not necessary to confirm SIADH.
- Practical checklist: hypotonic hyponatraemia + urine osmolality inappropriately high (>100, usually >300) + urine Na >30 mmol/L + euvolaemia + exclusion of thyroid/adrenal/diuretic/renal causes = SIADH.

In short: expect low serum osmolality (<275 mOsm/kg, case 267), concentrated urine (urine osmolality often >300 mOsm/kg, case 900) and elevated urine sodium (>30 mmol/L, case 88) once hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency are excluded.
