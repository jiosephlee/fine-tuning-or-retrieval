### [Morning Report] A man in his 60s presents with fever, headache, oral aphthae, a new painful genital ulcer, and folliculitis-like rashes. Using the 2014 International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD), what score does he achieve and what threshold supports a diagnosis of definite Behçet’s disease?

Using the 2014 International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD), this patient scores 5 points: oral aphthosis = 2 points, genital aphthosis = 2 points, and skin lesions (folliculitis‑like rash) = 1 point. The ICBD threshold for “definite” Behçet’s disease is ≥4 points, so a score of 5 meets the criteria for definite BD.  

Clinically relevant reasoning: the ICBD weights mucocutaneous findings most heavily (oral and genital ulcers each 2 points), so the combination of recurrent oral ulcers plus a painful genital ulcer plus characteristic skin lesions is sufficient for classification without requiring HLA‑B51 or positive pathergy/ocular findings.
