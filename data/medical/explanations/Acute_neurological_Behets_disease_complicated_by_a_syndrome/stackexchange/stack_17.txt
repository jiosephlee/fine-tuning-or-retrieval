### [Clinical Pearls] What is the role of HLA-B51 testing in Behçet’s disease—diagnostic utility and limitations?

HLA-B51 is best thought of as a risk marker, not a diagnostic test. It is strongly associated with Behçet’s disease (BD) in many populations — carriers have an increased odds of developing BD — but the allele is neither necessary nor sufficient for diagnosis. Clinically this means a positive result raises the posterior probability of BD in a patient with compatible features, while a negative result does not exclude the disease.

Why this matters in practice: the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease are entirely clinical and do not require HLA testing because of the allele’s imperfect sensitivity and specificity and marked geographic variation in background prevalence. In populations with high carrier frequency (e.g., parts of Asia, the Middle East), HLA-B51 has lower positive predictive value; in populations with low carrier frequency, a positive test is more informative but still not diagnostic. HLA-B51 may correlate with certain phenotypes (higher risk of ocular and possibly more severe disease, and some reports linking it with more frequent vascular/neuro involvement), so it can have prognostic value in specialist settings, but these associations are probabilistic and do not mandate different management on their own.

Limitations to keep in mind: HLA-B51 testing will not distinguish BD from mimics (infections, inflammatory or neoplastic causes) and should not delay a focused clinical workup or empiric treatment when indicated. A negative test can create false reassurance; a positive test can create anchoring bias if clinicians overweight it relative to the clinical picture. Routine screening with HLA-B51 is not recommended; testing is most useful when the diagnosis is uncertain after a careful clinical assessment or when the result might influence counseling or consideration of prognosis in a specialist clinic.

Applied to this case: the patient met clinical criteria for definite BD based on mucocutaneous findings and neuro involvement, so HLA-B51 added no diagnostic value. Its positivity was supportive but not required, and management decisions (recognising neuro-BD, treating inflammation, and monitoring SIADH) should remain guided by the clinical syndrome rather than the HLA result.
