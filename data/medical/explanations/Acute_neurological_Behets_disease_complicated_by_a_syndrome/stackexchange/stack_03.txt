### [Differential Diagnosis] In suspected acute neuro-Behçet’s with headache and fever, what cerebrospinal fluid profile supports an aseptic meningitis picture, and which infectious studies should be negative to reasonably exclude common viral and bacterial meningoencephalitides?

An aseptic meningitis CSF profile is one that shows inflammation without evidence of a pyogenic bacterial pathogen. In practice that means a modest pleocytosis with lymphocytic predominance (typically in the range of ~10–200 cells/µL, often low‑double digits as in this case), mildly increased protein (often <1.0 g/L or <100 mg/dL), normal or only mildly reduced glucose (CSF glucose usually >40 mg/dL or CSF:serum ratio ≈0.6), and an opening pressure that may be normal or mildly elevated. That pattern (lymphocytic pleocytosis + mild protein elevation + preserved glucose) points away from acute purulent bacterial meningitis and toward viral, drug‑related, or immune/auto‑inflammatory causes such as neuro‑Behçet’s.

To reasonably exclude the common treatable infectious causes of meningoencephalitis you should document negative directed studies on CSF: a negative Gram stain and routine bacterial cultures, and negative PCR assays for herpesviruses (HSV‑1/2 and VZV) are essential because HSV/VZV are the common causes of treatable sporadic viral encephalitis. Also obtain negative enterovirus PCR in appropriate seasons/ages, negative cryptococcal antigen (when risk factors exist), and negative mycobacterial testing (AFB smear and TB PCR/culture) if tuberculosis is a consideration. Blood cultures and relevant serum serologies (HIV, syphilis when indicated) should also be negative or unrevealing.

Clinical reasoning: pyogenic bacterial meningitis usually produces a neutrophil‑predominant pleocytosis, very high protein and low glucose, and positive Gram stain/culture; HSV/VZV encephalitis often causes lymphocytic CSF but is best excluded by sensitive CSF PCR (early empiric acyclovir is warranted until PCR returns). When those infectious tests are repeatedly negative and the CSF pattern remains lymphocytic with only mild protein elevation and preserved glucose, noninfectious inflammatory causes such as neuro‑Behçet’s become far more likely — especially in the presence of compatible systemic findings.
