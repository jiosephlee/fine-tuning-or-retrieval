Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone

### [Clinical Pearls] Using the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease, what score does a patient with recurrent oral aphthae, genital ulcers, and a folliculitis-like rash receive, and does HLA-B51 positivity alter the diagnostic classification?

Answer: Under the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD) this patient scores 5 points — oral aphthae 2, genital aphthosis 2, and a folliculitis‑like skin lesion 1 — and a score ≥4 meets the threshold for “definite” Behçet’s disease. HLA‑B51 positivity does not change the ICBD score or the formal classification; it is a supportive genetic association (increasing disease likelihood in the appropriate clinical context) but is not part of the diagnostic scoring system and cannot substitute for the required clinical criteria.

Clinical reasoning linked to the case:
- The ICBD point system (oral 2, genital 2, ocular 2, skin 1, vascular 1, neurologic 1, pathergy 1) is a clinical, point‑based tool; this patient’s oral ulcers, scrotal ulcer and folliculitis‑type rash give 2+2+1 = 5 points (≥4 = definite BD), as the authors state.
- Although neurological involvement is recognized by ICBD (1 additional point), the patient already met “definite” criteria on mucocutaneous findings alone.
- HLA‑B51 is epidemiologically associated with BD and may raise pretest probability, but it is neither required nor scored by ICBD and should not be used in isolation to make the diagnosis.
