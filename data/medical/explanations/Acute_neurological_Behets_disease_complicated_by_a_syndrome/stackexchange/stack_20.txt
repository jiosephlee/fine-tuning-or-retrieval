### [Management] Outline a monitoring plan for SIADH managed with fluid restriction: specify target intake, frequency of sodium checks, and triggers to escalate therapy to hypertonic saline or vasopressin receptor antagonists.

When you manage SIADH initially with fluid restriction you must do three things simultaneously: set a realistic and physiologically appropriate intake target that will create negative free-water balance, monitor the patient and labs closely so you detect inadequate response or over-rapid correction, and have clear, pre‑specified escalation triggers so that hypertonic fluid or a vasopressin‑receptor antagonist (V2‑antagonist, “vaptan”) are started promptly when indicated.

Aim and rationale for fluid restriction
For most euvolemic SIADH start with a total oral fluid restriction of 800 mL/day (range 500–1000 mL/day individualized to body size and insensible losses). The goal is to make fluid intake less than urine free‑water excretion so serum sodium rises gradually. In the case presented the team used <800 mL/day — a practical starting target in acute, hospitalised patients.

Baseline investigations and continuous monitoring
Before and during restriction obtain a baseline serum sodium, serum osmolality, urine osmolality and urine sodium. Record intake/output and daily weight. While on fluid restriction check serum sodium every 24 hours in clinically stable, mildly symptomatic patients. Also re‑check urine osmolality and urine sodium within 24–48 hours to assess whether restriction is physiologically effective (if urine osmolality remains very high, restriction is unlikely to work). Document mental status and look for new or worsening neurologic symptoms (confusion, lethargy, headache, nausea, vomiting, seizures).

Escalation triggers for hypertonic saline
Use hypertonic (3%) saline when the patient has severe or progressive neurologic symptoms attributable to hyponatraemia (seizure, obtundation, severe confusion, coma) or when serum sodium is very low and there is concern for imminent cerebral oedema. In practice escalate immediately for:
- Any seizure, severe obtundation, or focal neurologic deficit, regardless of the absolute sodium.
- Serum sodium ≤120 mmol/L with moderately severe symptoms (marked nausea, severe headache, agitation, progressive confusion).
When giving hypertonic saline aim for an initial goal increase of 4–6 mmol/L over the first few hours to reverse life‑threatening cerebral oedema, then slow correction to avoid osmotic demyelination. Do not exceed ~8 mmol/L in 24 hours (often safer to target ≤6 mmol/L/24 h in high‑risk patients: chronic hyponatraemia, alcoholism, malnutrition, liver disease).

Escalation triggers for vasopressin receptor antagonists (vaptans)
Consider a vaptan when fluid restriction fails and the patient is stable (no severe neurologic symptoms) but remains hyponatraemic, or when fluid restriction is intolerable/unfeasible. Practical triggers include:
- Failure of serum sodium to rise by ≥2–4 mmol/L after 24–48 hours of strict restriction, or persistent sodium <125–130 mmol/L despite restriction.
- Very high urine osmolality (e.g., >300–500 mOsm/kg) with ongoing urine sodium excretion that makes further restriction unlikely to succeed.
Before starting a vaptan confirm euvolemic SIADH, exclude hypovolaemia and contraindications (concurrent potent CYP3A4 inhibitors, advanced liver disease for some agents). When a vaptan is begun, monitor serum sodium every 6–8 hours for the first 24–48 hours because these agents can produce rapid aquaresis and over‑rapid sodium rise; adjust or stop therapy to avoid >8 mmol/L/24 h correction.

Additional practical safeguards and stopping rules
Weigh daily and maintain strict input/output charting; stop offending medications that cause SIADH. If serum sodium is rising too fast (>0.5 mmol/L/hour or >8 mmol/L/24 h), intervene with free water (D5W) or desmopressin to slow correction. If no response to restriction and you are not yet using a vaptan, consider oral urea (where available) or demeclocycline for chronic outpatient SIADH, but these are not first‑line for the acutely symptomatic patient.

Summary application to this case
Start with total intake of ~800 mL/day, obtain daily serum sodium and clinical neurologic checks, repeat urine osmolality/Na within 24–48 hours to assess response, and escalate to hypertonic saline immediately for seizures/marked neurologic decline or very low Na with symptoms. If the patient is haemodynamically stable, has inadequate sodium rise after 24–48 hours of strict restriction, and no contraindication exists, consider a vaptan with intensive (q6–8 h) sodium monitoring to avoid overcorrection.
