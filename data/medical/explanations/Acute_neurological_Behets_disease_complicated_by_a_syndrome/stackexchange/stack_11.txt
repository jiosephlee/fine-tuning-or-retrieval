### [Clinical Pearls] Name two underrecognized manifestations that can accompany severe hyponatremia due to SIADH in neuroinflammatory states and briefly describe their mechanisms.

Two commonly underrecognized manifestations of profound hyponatremia from SIADH in neuroinflammatory states are persistent hiccups and rhabdomyolysis.

Persistent hiccups often reflect irritation or dysfunction of brainstem centres (the medullary “hiccup reflex” integrating vagal, phrenic and central pathways) and can be triggered by CNS inflammation or by marked hypotonicity causing neuronal swelling and hyperexcitability in those same brainstem circuits. In practice hiccups in a patient with headache/meningitis and hyponatraemia should prompt consideration of central SIADH rather than being dismissed as benign.

Rhabdomyolysis can result directly from severe hyponatraemia: hypotonicity causes myocyte swelling, disruption of membrane ion gradients, intracellular calcium overload and ATP depletion, which lead to myonecrosis and CK release. (Rapid over-rapid correction of sodium is a separate, well-known cause of muscle injury and osmotic demyelination risk, so both the hyponatraemia itself and its mismanagement can produce muscle injury.) Clinically this mandates checking CK and renal function and avoiding too-rapid sodium shifts while treating the underlying cause.

Recognising these associations matters because they broaden the differential for non‑specific symptoms and change monitoring and management — check CK and urine output, monitor sodium frequently, and correct slowly while treating the underlying neuroinflammation.
