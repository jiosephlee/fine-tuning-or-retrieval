### [Clinical Pearls] Which immunosuppressive agent is generally avoided in patients with neuro-Behçet’s disease due to concerns about neurotoxicity or exacerbation of neurologic involvement, and what is the rationale?

Answer: Cyclosporine (cyclosporin A) is generally avoided in patients with neuro‑Behçet’s disease. Clinically, cyclosporine has well‑described neurotoxic effects (seizures, encephalopathy, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome) and there are case reports and series in Behçet’s showing new or worsened CNS disease after cyclosporine exposure. The drug can cause endothelial dysfunction, hypertension, and direct neurotoxicity that may mimic or exacerbate parenchymal neuro‑Behçet’s, so it is avoided when CNS involvement is present or suspected. In practice, CNS disease is managed with high‑dose corticosteroids and steroid‑sparing agents with safer CNS profiles (azathioprine, cyclophosphamide or anti‑TNF therapy) rather than cyclosporine.
