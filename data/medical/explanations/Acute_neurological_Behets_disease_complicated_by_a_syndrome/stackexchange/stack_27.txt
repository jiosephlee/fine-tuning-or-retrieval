### [Clinical Pearls] When present, what are the characteristic MRI findings in parenchymal neuro-Behçet’s disease with respect to lesion location and signal characteristics?

In parenchymal neuro‑Behçet’s disease the MRI pattern is distinctive because it reflects a deep, inflammatory rather than a vascular process. Lesions classically involve the brainstem (most often the mesodiencephalic junction and pontine tegmentum), thalami and basal ganglia, with frequent extension into adjacent deep white matter and internal capsule; supratentorial deep white‑matter lesions may also occur. On conventional sequences these lesions are hyperintense on T2 and FLAIR and hypointense on T1, reflecting edema and inflammatory infiltrate. Contrast enhancement is variable and often patchy or nodular rather than homogenous; acute lesions can show restricted diffusion, whereas subacute/chronic lesions may become gliotic and atrophic over time. Importantly, the distribution is typically non‑territorial (not confined to an arterial vascular territory), multifocal, and centered in deep gray‑white structures — a pattern that helps distinguish neuro‑Behçet’s from ischemia, demyelination, or typical infectious abscesses. Finally, normal MRI early in the disease (as in this case) does not exclude parenchymal neuro‑Behçet’s; repeat imaging or correlation with CSF and clinical findings is often necessary.
