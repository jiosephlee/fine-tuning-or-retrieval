Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] Why are hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis increasingly recognized manifestations of telomere biology disorders, and what mechanisms might link PARN variants to liver disease in addition to pulmonary fibrosis?

Clinical answer (NEJM Morning‑Report style)

Why hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis are now being recognised as part of telomere biology disorders
- Conceptual link: TBDs cause premature telomere shortening → loss of proliferative reserve and increased cellular senescence/apoptosis in tissues with ongoing turnover or regenerative demand. The liver, although quiescent at baseline, requires robust proliferative capacity for regeneration after injury. When hepatocytes, cholangiocytes and liver progenitors have critically short telomeres they cannot replace lost or injured cells effectively, and repair shifts from regeneration toward fibrogenesis.
- Emerging epidemiology: Earlier descriptions of TBD emphasised mucocutaneous findings and bone‑marrow failure; recent cohort and registry data (and the case series cited in this report) show high rates of clinically significant hepatic fibrosis among adults with telomerase complex mutations (eg, TERT/TERC), implying that liver disease is an under‑recognised, but biologically plausible, manifestation.
- Clinical implication: Young adults with unexplained hepatic fibrosis or cirrhosis—especially when accompanied by cytopenias, premature greying, pulmonary fibrosis or other “premature ageing” features—should raise suspicion for a TBD.

Mechanisms linking PARN variants specifically to liver disease (in addition to pulmonary fibrosis)
1. Telomerase insufficiency via defective TERC maturation
   - PARN is required for 3′‑end maturation and stability of TERC RNA. Loss or reduction of PARN function (haploinsufficiency with monoallelic variants or biallelic loss) reduces mature TERC, impairing telomerase activity.
   - Reduced telomerase → accelerated telomere attrition in dividing hepatic cells → senescence/apoptosis, reduced regenerative capacity, and predisposition to fibrosis after injury. This is the primary mechanism linking PARN variants to organ fibrosis, paralleling the well‑established pathway for TERT/TERC mutations.

2. Consequence of impaired regenerative response → profibrotic healing
   - With impaired hepatocyte replication, injured liver more readily activates stellate cells and myofibroblasts and sustains inflammatory/fibrogenic signalling rather than effective parenchymal regeneration, promoting progressive fibrosis and cirrhosis.

3. Senescence‑associated secretory phenotype (SASP) and chronic inflammation
   - Senescent hepatocytes/cholangiocytes release proinflammatory and profibrotic cytokines (SASP), which amplify stellate cell activation and extracellular matrix deposition. This paracrine effect links cellular telomere dysfunction to organ‑level fibrosis.

4. Extra‑telomeric effects of PARN (mRNA regulation)
   - PARN is an exoribonuclease that deadenylates many RNAs. Dysfunction may dysregulate mRNAs involved in DNA repair, cell cycle control, apoptosis and inflammatory pathways, compounding susceptibility to injury and fibrogenesis independent of telomerase/TERC levels. Thus PARN variants can both shorten telomeres and alter expression of genes that influence fibrotic responses.

5. Multihit model (genetic vulnerability + environmental/physiologic stresses)
   - Monoallelic PARN variants may be insufficient alone; additional stressors (metabolic steatosis, inflammation, infections, nutritional deficiencies, alcohol, toxins) accelerate telomere attrition or provoke repetitive injury. In the presented case, grade II steatosis and systemic inflammation likely acted as additional hits on a genetically susceptible liver, explaining early cirrhosis despite a single PARN allele variant.
   - This model also explains phenotypic heterogeneity (why some PARN carriers develop IPF, others HF, marrow failure, or combinations).

How the case supports these mechanisms
- The patient is young with cirrhosis (transient elastography 18.1 kPa), pulmonary fibrosis, pancytopenia and physical signs of premature ageing—exactly the multisystem phenotype expected from telomere dysfunction.
- Genetic finding: monoallelic PARN missense variant in a conserved domain predicted deleterious; biologically plausible because PARN loss impairs TERC maturation.
- Other causes of liver disease were excluded (viral, autoimmune), but she had hepatic steatosis and systemic inflammation—plausible additional hits on a telomere‑compromised liver.
- The report cites registry data showing high prevalence of HF with TERT/TERC mutations, supporting that telomere defects commonly produce clinically significant liver fibrosis; by extension, PARN defects that reduce telomerase activity can produce the same outcome.

Takeaway points for clinical practice
- Consider a telomere biology disorder in young patients with otherwise unexplained hepatic fibrosis, especially when accompanied by marrow abnormalities, pulmonary fibrosis, premature greying or other premature‑ageing features.
- Pathogenesis in PARN carriers is multifactorial: defective TERC maturation → telomerase insufficiency → telomere shortening → impaired regeneration and SASP‑driven fibrogenesis, possibly compounded by PARN’s broader effects on mRNA regulation and environmental “second hits.”
- Recognising the link alters management: genetic testing and telomere measurement, surveillance for multisystem involvement, avoidance of additional liver insults, and early hematology/genetics referral for consideration of transplant or other targeted therapies.
