Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Management] In a young adult with severe vitamin B12 deficiency and a suspected telomere biology disorder, how would you prioritize immediate management and monitoring, and what findings would prompt escalation to consider hematopoietic stem cell transplantation?

Immediate priorities (first 24–72 hours)
- Correct reversible contributors to cytopenia and organ dysfunction. In this case the first and most urgent step was aggressive B12 repletion (IV or IM cyanocobalamin loading then maintenance) plus folate and repletion of vitamin D/electrolytes; this often produces rapid symptomatic and reticulocyte responses and should be done before attributing cytopenias to an inherited marrow failure syndrome. Continue transfusion support for symptomatic anaemia as needed (the authors used whole blood transfusions).
- Supportive care and infection/bleeding precautions: strict neutropenic precautions if neutropenia, platelet support for bleeding or very low counts, treat any active infections promptly, and avoid myelotoxic drugs (chemotherapy, certain immunosuppressants, and excessive alkylators).
- Baseline organ and marrow assessment while deficiencies are corrected: obtain CBC with retic count frequently (eg, twice weekly initially), bone marrow examination (cellularity, dysplasia, cytogenetics), iron studies, hemolysis markers, DAT interpretation in context (as in the case), high-resolution chest CT and pulmonary function tests, liver stiffness assessment (elastography) and hepatic panel, and screen for occult infections. These assessments were performed in the case and helped distinguish ineffective erythropoiesis from immune hemolysis and document pulmonary and hepatic fibrosis.
- Expedite genetic workup and telomere testing: send next‑generation sequencing panel for telomere biology genes and measure telomere length (flow-FISH), because a confirmed pathogenic TBD alters management (HSCT timing, conditioning, donor choice). In the case, clinical exome sequencing identified a monoallelic PARN variant and the clinical picture supported a TBD despite variant classified as VUS—prompting close follow-up.
- Early transplant and specialty referral: involve hematology/transplant, pulmonology and hepatology early. In TBDs, transplant planning and donor selection (avoid related donors who may carry the variant) should begin well before irreversible marrow failure.

Monitoring over weeks–months
- Hematologic surveillance: frequent CBCs with reticulocyte counts to document response after B12 correction. Monitor for progressive cytopenias or transfusion dependence. Repeat bone marrow biopsy if counts worsen, retic remains low, or new cytopenias develop.
- Serial organ surveillance: pulmonary function testing (including DLCO) and HRCT every 6–12 months (or sooner if symptoms worsen) for progression of pulmonary fibrosis; liver synthetic function tests and elastography to track hepatic fibrosis; bone density follow‑up for osteoporosis.
- Telomere length and genetic re-evaluation: if initial genetic result is VUS, consider parental testing, segregation studies, repeat sequencing or functional assays; a short telomere length on flow‑FISH supports a TBD diagnosis and increases urgency of monitoring.
- Risk minimization: vaccination (pneumococcus, influenza), avoid smoking and hepatotoxic agents (alcohol, hepatotoxic drugs), and counsel regarding pregnancy risks and family testing.

When to escalate and consider hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT)
Escalation to HSCT is considered when there is clear, progressive bone marrow failure that is unlikely to be reversed by correcting reversible causes. Specific triggers include:
- Transfusion dependence: need for regular red‑cell or platelet transfusions to maintain acceptable hemoglobin/platelet levels.
- Progressive or severe cytopenias despite correction of deficiencies:
  - ANC persistently <0.5 ×10^9/L with recurrent or severe infections
  - Platelets persistently <20–30 ×10^9/L or clinically significant bleeding
  - Hemoglobin refractory to replacement and supportive measures with poor marrow reserve or symptomatic anemia despite transfusions
- Documentation of bone marrow failure: evolving hypocellularity on marrow biopsy, markedly reduced marrow progenitors, or increasing marrow failure indices (eg, progressive low retic with trilineage decline).
- Clonal evolution / myeloid malignancy: new cytogenetic abnormalities, evolving MDS or transformation to AML—these mandate prompt transplant evaluation.
- Genetic confirmation of a pathogenic telomere gene with progressive haematologic phenotype: a bona fide pathogenic germline variant and short telomeres increase the likelihood that marrow failure will progress and favor earlier transplant referral.
- Failure of medical bridging therapies: lack of durable response to androgens (danazol/nandrolone) or other supportive regimens when used as a bridge.

Practical considerations specific to TBDs
- Timing and candidacy: HSCT in TBD requires individualized timing—early enough to prevent life‑threatening complications but after careful organ assessment because pulmonary or hepatic fibrosis worsens transplant risk. The presence of significant pulmonary fibrosis or advanced cirrhosis may increase transplant morbidity and influence conditioning intensity or make HSCT high risk.
- Conditioning regimens: patients with TBDs tolerate standard myeloablative conditioning poorly; reduced‑intensity or non‑myeloablative regimens and telomere‑sparing approaches are preferred. Discuss these options early with a transplant center experienced in telomere disorders.
- Donor selection: avoid related donors who may harbor the same germline variant; preferentially use an unrelated matched donor or cord blood when appropriate.
- Consider bridging medical therapy: androgens have been used to improve counts in TBDs and can be considered as a temporizing measure while planning HSCT, recognizing limited and variable evidence and potential hepatotoxicity—important in a patient with hepatic fibrosis as in this case.

Bottom line (applied to the presented patient)
- Immediate priority was and remains aggressive correction of severe B12 deficiency and supportive measures; this produced early symptomatic and hematologic improvement in the case.
- Simultaneously, perform baseline telomere testing, marrow evaluation and organ staging (lung and liver), begin genetic counseling, and engage transplant and pulmonology teams early.
- Escalate to HSCT evaluation if the patient develops transfusion dependence, progressive trilineage cytopenias (eg, ANC <0.5, platelets <20–30), marrow hypocellularity or clonal evolution, or if genetic/telomere testing confirms a pathogenic defect with an evolving marrow failure phenotype—balanced against pulmonary/hepatic comorbidity when planning timing and conditioning.
