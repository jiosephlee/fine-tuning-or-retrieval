Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] When using fondaparinux for treatment of HIT, under what circumstances should antithrombin supplementation be considered, and what is the physiological basis for this strategy?

Direct answer
- Consider antithrombin (AT) supplementation when you plan to use fondaparinux for HIT and there is evidence that the patient has low or functionally depleted AT (measured low AT activity or clinical/biochemical evidence of consumptive coagulopathy). In the case report, fondaparinux was started only after an AT activity of 0.70 U/mL (reference 0.80–1.40) was documented, and 1,000 U of AT concentrate was given prior to fondaparinux dosing.

Why — the physiological basis
- Fondaparinux is an indirect, AT‑dependent factor Xa inhibitor: it binds antithrombin and induces a conformational change that markedly increases AT’s ability to inhibit factor Xa. Without sufficient AT, fondaparinux cannot form the active AT–pentasaccharide complex and its anti‑Xa effect is diminished.
- In severe HIT (and especially autoimmune HIT with marked platelet activation and consumptive coagulopathy as in this case — falling fibrinogen, rising D‑dimer, schistocytes), AT can be consumed or low, reducing the efficacy of fondaparinux. Restoring AT restores the drug’s mechanism of action and improves anticoagulant effect.

When to act (practical points)
- Measure AT activity before switching to fondaparinux in patients with severe HIT or signs of consumption. Consider supplementation if AT activity is below the lower limit of normal (the case used 0.70 U/mL < reference 0.80).
- Also consider supplementation when there is clinical evidence of ongoing uncontrolled hypercoagulability (falling fibrinogen, rising d‑dimer, persistent thrombosis) despite anticoagulation.
- Typical approach: give AT concentrate to restore AT activity into the normal range (the case used 1,000 U), then start therapeutic fondaparinux. Dosing and target AT level should follow institutional protocols or haematology advice.

Monitoring and caveats
- Anti‑Xa assays calibrated for fondaparinux are not always available; monitor clinical response (platelet recovery, resolution of thrombosis), and laboratory markers of coagulation (fibrinogen, d‑dimer) and bleeding risk.
- Fondaparinux is renally cleared and is used off‑label for HIT; choose it cautiously in renal impairment and weigh risks vs alternatives (eg, argatroban) as was done in this patient.

Takeaway: because fondaparinux requires antithrombin to work, check AT in patients with severe or consumptive HIT and replenish AT when activity is low to ensure effective anticoagulation — exactly the rationale applied in this case.
