Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 1: ## Case Synopsis and Clinical Trajectory

This case concerns a man in his late 70s with extensive cardiovascular and systemic comorbidity who ultimately required a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV). At baseline he carried multiple high-risk features: three‑vessel coronary artery disease treated with several prior percutaneous coronary interventions, arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and a history of recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding. In 2014 he also developed third‑degree atrioventricular block necessitating permanent pacemaker placement. These comorbid conditions established a high operative risk profile and framed all subsequent therapeutic decisions.

The patient’s aortic valve journey began in 2014 with transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) of a 27 mm Lotus prosthesis for severe native aortic stenosis. Over the following years the valve underwent structural deterioration: by late 2021 his symptoms of exertional dyspnoea progressed and echocardiography showed severe bioprosthetic stenosis with rising transvalvular gradients and a decline in left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) from 54% to 42%. Given his comorbidities and prohibitive surgical risk, a transcatheter redo strategy was pursued.

In May 2022 a valve‑in‑valve procedure was performed with a 23 mm Evolut valve implanted within the degenerate Lotus prosthesis. Hemodynamics improved promptly—the EF recovered to 54%—but the procedure was not without complication: the patient developed a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm that required open vascular surgical repair. The early functional improvement suggested successful relief of obstruction from the degenerated first prosthesis.

Three months after the TAV‑in‑TAV, however, the patient presented with fever and recurrent heart failure. Blood cultures grew Enterococcus faecalis and transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated severe restenosis of the transcatheter prosthesis. A diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) was made. Management followed infectious‑disease guidance: the patient completed a full course of targeted intravenous ampicillin‑based therapy for six weeks, with subsequent oral therapy as advised. Serial clinical assessments, repeated blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography and multislice CT showed resolution of infection with no residual vegetations or paravalvular complications; thus active infection was considered successfully treated.

Despite microbiological and imaging resolution of PVE, valve function again deteriorated over the following 18 months. By January–June 2024 the patient had severe prosthetic valve dysfunction: symptomatic exertional limitation with a mean transvalvular gradient of approximately 49 mm Hg, a calculated aortic valve area of 0.5 cm², and moderate–to‑severe intraprosthetic regurgitation. Notably, left ventricular performance had recovered—EF rose to about 65%—so symptoms reflected critical valve obstruction and regurgitation rather than pump failure.

Faced with this recurrent structural failure in the context of two prior transcatheter frames, prior PVE that had been treated and a markedly elevated surgical risk (EuroSCORE II 27% plus severe comorbidities), the multidisciplinary heart team concluded that another transcatheter approach was the most appropriate option. The decision followed shared decision‑making with the patient and family and relied heavily on detailed preprocedural imaging to assess anatomical feasibility, coronary risk, and the likelihood of achieving an acceptable effective orifice area despite triple stenting. This led to the plan for a CT‑guided, strategy‑driven third TAVI (Acurate neo2) with attention to implantation depth, commissural alignment and post‑dilatation to optimise haemodynamics while preserving coronary access.
