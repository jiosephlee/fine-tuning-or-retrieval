Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] What pathophysiologic mechanisms likely contributed to prosthetic valve failure in this patient (post-infectious leaflet injury/fibrosis, progressive structural degeneration, mechanical leaflet restriction), and how can high implantation in a redo TAVI help pin prior leaflets, limit neo-skirt formation, and improve the effective orifice area and coronary flow?

Direct answer — likely contributors to valve failure
- Multifactorial process in this patient. Three mechanisms plausibly acted together:
  1. Post‑infectious leaflet injury and fibrosis: the documented Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) with subsequent healing is specifically called out in the report as a contributor. Infection can cause leaflet cusp damage, fibrosis, scarring and retraction → loss of leaflet compliance and restricted opening, producing stenosis and/or mixed dysfunction even after microbiologic cure.
  2. Progressive structural degeneration of bioprosthetic tissue: the first Lotus valve (2014) and the subsequent Evolut (2022) had already shown structural deterioration pre‑ and post‑TAV‑in‑TAV with rising gradients over time. Ageing, calcification and leaflet wear produce leaflet stiffening and reduced effective orifice area (EOA).
  3. Mechanical leaflet restriction from layered frames and leaflet crowding: with two nested transcatheter frames in place, previously mobile leaflets can be mechanically pinned, folded inward, or prevented from fully opening. The case documents two overlapping valves, a severely narrowed lumen on CT (minimal diameter 13.7 mm) and very small AVA, consistent with mechanical obstruction from frame–leaflet interactions.

How high implantation of the new valve mitigates these problems (mechanical rationale)
- “Pinning” prior leaflets to the stent frame: deploying the new valve higher — at or above the level of the failing Evolut leaflets — forces the old leaflets to lie fixed against the inner surface of the existing stent frames. That prevents inward folding or billowing of degenerated/fibrotic leaflets into the central flow orifice, restoring a more circular, unobstructed flow lumen.
- Limiting neo‑skirt formation below the coronaries: when a valve is implanted deep, the apposed prior leaflet tissue and valve skirt can form a bulky “neo‑skirt” that projects into the sinuses and may extend beneath coronary ostia, reducing valve‑to‑coronary distance and risking coronary obstruction. A higher (supra‑annular) landing positions the new skirt more cranially so less redundant leaflet tissue accumulates in the sinus region, preserving sinus geometry and the native coronary ostia clearance.
- Improving effective orifice area (EOA) and transvalvular flow:
  - By preventing leaflet prolapse/infolding and reducing skirt bulk in the outflow tract, the central EOA increases compared with a situation where prior leaflets fold inward.
  - Choice of a supra‑annular valve (Acurate neo2) gives leaflets a functional orifice located above the annulus where there is more room, yielding better hemodynamics in a constrained annular/lumen space.
  - High implantation combined with targeted post‑dilation can partially expand the prior self‑expanding frame (the Evolut) and optimise the composite stent geometry, further enlarging the lumen and lowering gradients (the authors attribute improved gradients after the third valve to these effects).
- Preserving coronary flow and future access: by minimising neo‑skirt formation and aligning commissures (as the team did), the ostia are less likely to be covered or obstructed and subsequent coronary cannulation is easier despite frame stacking.

How this relates to the presented case
- The report documents resolved infection without active vegetations, severe restenosis with mixed regurgitation and a minimal Evolut lumen on CT. The team deliberately selected a supra‑annular, low‑frame height valve, used CT modelling to plan a high implant at the level of the Evolut leaflets, performed post‑dilation, and achieved restoration of leaflet mobility, elimination of regurgitation and sustained improvement in gradients (mean gradient fell from 67 mm Hg immediately post‑implant to ~26–27 mm Hg at 1 month and 1 year). The authors attribute the haemodynamic improvement to pinning of prior leaflets, minimised neo‑skirt below the coronaries, commissural alignment and partial mechanical expansion of the prior frame by post‑dilation—exactly the mechanisms outlined above.

Clinical pearl
- In multi‑stented TAV‑in‑TAV scenarios, especially after PVE and leaflet fibrosis, careful preprocedural CT planning, selection of a supra‑annular valve and a deliberate high implant can mechanically mitigate leaflet restriction, limit neo‑skirt formation that threatens coronaries, and optimize the composite EOA — but these strategies require individualized modelling and carry trade‑offs for future coronary access and reintervention.
