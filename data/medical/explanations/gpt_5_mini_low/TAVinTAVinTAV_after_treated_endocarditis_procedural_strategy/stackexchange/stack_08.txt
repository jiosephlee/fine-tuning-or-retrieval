Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Given the patient’s EuroSCORE II of 27% and prior endocarditis now resolved, what elements of multidisciplinary heart-team reasoning favor a third transcatheter intervention over surgery (e.g., prohibitive surgical anatomy with integrated prior frames, comorbidities like COPD/diabetes/GI bleeding, CT-demonstrated feasibility), and how should lifetime management be incorporated at the time of the first TAVI?

Bottom line
- In this patient a third transcatheter intervention was favored because the heart team judged open redo surgery to be prohibitive: very high operative mortality (EuroSCORE II ≈27%), dense integration of two prior transcatheter frames into the aortic root, and major comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, recurrent GI bleeding) that increase perioperative and recovery risk. CT showed that a further TAV-in-TAV was anatomically feasible with acceptable valve-to-coronary distances and the possibility of high implantation/commissural alignment to preserve coronary flow — making a transcatheter strategy the safer, practicable option once infection was proven eradicated.
Why the team chose transcatheter over surgery (clinical reasoning anchored to the case)
- Surgical risk and anatomy: EuroSCORE II ~27% denotes prohibitive predicted operative mortality. The case documents “extensive stent integration into the aortic wall” after two prior TAVIs; explantation would likely require complex root/ascending aortic replacement with long cardiopulmonary bypass, grafts and re-do sternotomy — much higher morbidity and mortality than repeat TAVI.
- Comorbidities that amplify surgical harm: COPD (higher ventilator/respiratory complications), diabetes (wound/infectious risk), and recurrent GI bleeding (limits transfusion options, complicates anticoagulation) all tilt the risk–benefit ratio toward a less invasive option.
- Infection status and guideline nuance: Active prosthetic valve endocarditis is an indication for surgery in many cases, but here infective endocarditis was treated and follow-up TEE, blood cultures and CT showed no active infection. Guidelines call for individualized decisions when infection is controlled; thus absence of active PVE removed a major barrier to a transcatheter reintervention.
- CT-guided anatomical feasibility: Preprocedural MSCT in this patient demonstrated a minimum inner lumen (Evolut) of 13.7 mm and acceptable coronary heights, valve-to-coronary distances and frame relationships. These CT findings allowed planning for high implantation, commissural alignment and avoidance of coronary protection—practical factors that make TAV-in-TAV technically feasible and safer than open surgery.
- Procedural strategy to mitigate specific TAV-in-TAV risks: The team selected a supra‑annular, low‑frame valve (Acurate neo2), planned high implantation to tack prior leaflets outward (reduce neo‑skirt), performed commissural alignment to preserve future coronary access, and used CT modelling to minimize obstruction risk. These targeted maneuvers address the two major concerns of repeat TAVI — coronary obstruction and severe patient‑prosthesis mismatch — and were feasible in this anatomy.
How lifetime management should be incorporated at the time of the first TAVI
- Think beyond the index procedure: recognize that younger or longer‑lived patients will probably require reinterventions. Decisions at first TAVI should be guided by anticipated lifetime trajectory.
- Valve choice with reintervention in mind:
  - Prefer platforms that facilitate future coronary access and re‑intervention: valves with predictable commissural orientation, fluoroscopic markers for orientation, lower frame heights or designs that allow supra‑annular haemodynamics.
  - For small annuli, avoid choices that will create an irreversibly small final orifice when layered (consider supra‑annular valves or root enlarging strategies if surgery is chosen).
- Commissural alignment and implant technique:
  - Plan and execute commissural alignment at the initial TAVI to preserve coronary access and simplify future V-in-V procedures.
  - Use implantation depth and orientation strategies that minimize the creation of a obstructive neo‑skirt.
- Sizing and annular planning:
  - When feasible, avoid undersizing and plan for maximal effective orifice area (EOA) to reduce future PPM risk. In borderline anatomies, consider surgical AVR with annular enlargement if lifetime risk of multiple VIVs would lead to severe PPM.
- Documentation and imaging:
  - Obtain high‑quality baseline MSCT with detailed measurements (annulus, sinuses, coronary heights, valve-to-coronary distance) and record valve orientation fluoroscopic landmarks at implant for future planning.
- Patient selection and shared decision‑making:
  - For patients with long life expectancy, discuss tradeoffs between surgery (potentially better durability and larger EOAs, but higher upfront risk) versus TAVI and downstream complexity of VIVs.
- Anticipate and plan for future techniques:
  - Consider whether initial strategy will permit leaflet modification techniques (BASILICA) or bioprosthetic valve fracture later; choose devices and implant positions that preserve those options if they may be needed.
- Infection prevention and surveillance:
  - Educate about endocarditis prevention, ensure appropriate dental and infection prophylaxis per guidelines, and maintain a low threshold for surveillance imaging and blood cultures after suspected infections.
Case‑specific summary
- In this reported patient the heart team integrated (1) prohibitive computed surgical risk (EuroSCORE II 27%), (2) prior PVE now resolved, (3) extensive frame integration making explant technically hazardous, (4) major comorbidities increasing perioperative risk, and (5) CT-confirmed feasibility of a strategy (high implant, commissural alignment, valve choice) that minimized coronary obstruction and optimized haemodynamics. Those elements justified a third transcatheter solution, with lifetime management lessons emphasizing valve selection, commissural alignment, documentation and patient counseling at the time of the first TAVI to preserve future options.
