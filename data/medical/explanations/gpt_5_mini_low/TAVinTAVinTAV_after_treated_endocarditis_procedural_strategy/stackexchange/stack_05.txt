Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why might a supra-annular, self-expanding valve with relatively low frame height and favorable commissural-alignment capability (e.g., Acurate neo2) be advantageous in a triple-stented anatomy, and how do these design features affect residual effective orifice area, gradients, and the likelihood of maintaining future coronary access?

Direct answer
A supra‑annular, self‑expanding valve with a relatively low frame height and good commissural‑alignment capability (for example, the Acurate neo2 used in this case) is advantageous in triple‑stented anatomy because those design features maximize residual effective orifice area (EOA), minimize transvalvular gradients, and reduce—but do not eliminate—the risk of compromising future coronary access.

Why (clinical reasoning and pathophysiology)
- Supra‑annular leaflet position places the functional valve orifice above the stent frame and above the stacked leaflets of prior prostheses. In a layered (TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV) situation where inner lumen diameter is severely reduced by prior frames and constrained leaflets, a supra‑annular valve creates a larger flow area for the same stent footprint than an intra‑annular design, improving EOA and lowering gradients.
- Self‑expanding nitinol frames exert continuous outward radial force that can partially expand the constrained frame(s) beneath them (as occurred here: post‑implant and post‑dilation improved the Evolut frame expansion). That passive/active radial expansion helps restore leaflet motion of prior valves and increases the final lumen, translating into lower gradients.
- A relatively low frame height limits the vertical extent of a new “neo‑skirt” and the amount of metal/leaflet overlap projecting above the annulus into the sinuses. This reduces coverage of the coronary ostia and lessens obstruction of the sinus, both improving coronary perfusion and reducing the mechanical barrier to future coronary catheter engagement.
- Commissural alignment capability lets the operator orient the new leaflets and posts relative to the existing frames and coronary ostia. Proper alignment minimizes commissure‑to‑ostium overlap and places stent posts away from coronary takeoffs, preserving a clearer path for future coronary access.

How these features affected the reported case
- Valve selection and high implantation strategy (placing the Acurate neo2 at the Evolut leaflet level) were chosen specifically to “fix” the earlier leaflets open against the frame and avoid inward folding that would shrink the orifice. That strategy, combined with the supra‑annular design, produced a sustained improvement in haemodynamics: immediate post‑procedure gradients were high (reflecting early constrained expansion) but after optimisation and partial expansion of the prior frame the mean gradient fell to ~27 mm Hg at 1 month and remained 26 mm Hg at 1 year with AVA ≈1.1 cm² — an acceptable result in this highly constrained anatomy.
- Post‑dilation under high pressure likely augmented the self‑expanding valve’s effect on the underlying Evolut frame, improving lumen diameter and leaflet mobility; this demonstrates how self‑expanding designs can be synergistic with balloon optimisation to restore EOA.
- Commissural alignment was attempted using CT guidance and fluoroscopic landmarks; this helped preserve coronary perfusion during the procedure and reduced the degree of metal/leaflet overlap at the coronaries, supporting preserved coronary flow without prophylactic coronary protection.

Limitations and caveats
- Even with a low‑height, supra‑annular device and careful commissural alignment, cumulative frame mesh and layered leaflets still increase the risk and technical difficulty of future coronary access and interventions. The case explicitly notes the “cumulative mesh burden” may present future challenges.
- Residual prosthesis‑patient mismatch (PPM) remains a real risk in triple‑stented anatomies; supra‑annular and self‑expanding designs mitigate but do not fully eliminate PPM when landing zone diameters are small (the patient’s Evolut minimum lumen diameter was 13.7 mm).
- Commissural alignment is not perfect in all anatomies—CT planning improves odds but cannot guarantee long‑term easy coronary reaccess.

Practical teaching points
- In redo‑TAVI situations with small internal diameters and prior layered frames, prioritize valves whose leaflets sit supra‑annularly and whose frames can impart radial force to expand previous prostheses.
- Choose a device with low frame height and plan commissural alignment when coronary preservation and future reaccess are priorities.
- Combine device selection with CT‑guided implantation strategy and judicious post‑dilation to maximise residual EOA and minimize gradients, accepting that some degree of PPM and future access difficulty may still remain.
