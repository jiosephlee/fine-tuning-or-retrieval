Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Morning Report] For a novel missense HNF1A variant (for example, c.809A>G, p.N270S) absent from ClinVar, what ACMG-aligned evidence can support a likely pathogenic classification (consider segregation with disease, location in a critical domain, in silico scores, and phenotype-match), and why is database absence not definitive against pathogenicity?

Direct answer
The c.809A>G (p.N270S) HNF1A change can reasonably be classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG-style criteria by combining moderate and supporting evidence: PM1 (critical functional domain), PM2_supporting (absent/rare in population databases), PP1 (co-segregation with disease in an autosomal‑dominant family), PP3 (multiple computational predictors support deleterious effect), and PP4 (patient phenotype highly specific for HNF1A‑MODY). Database absence alone (ClinVar/VarSome) is not decisive against pathogenicity because most disease-causing missense variants are rare and many pathogenic variants are novel and not yet curated.

Why these criteria apply in this case (clinical reasoning tied to the report)
- PM1 — Location in a critical domain: The variant lies in exon 4 within the HNF1A DNA‑binding domain, the region where the majority of pathogenic HNF1A missense mutations cluster. Variants in this domain are more likely to disrupt transcriptional activity and thus meet the “critical domain” criterion.
- PM2_supporting — Absent from population and clinical databases: The authors report the variant is not present in ClinVar, VarSome or the literature. For a rare, early‑onset dominant disease, absence from population databases is supportive (particularly if not seen in large population collections), but this is supporting—not stand‑alone—evidence.
- PP1 — Segregation with disease: The pedigree shows multiple affected maternal relatives with early‑onset diabetes (mother, maternal grandmother, younger brother). Co‑segregation of the heterozygous variant with autosomal‑dominant disease across multiple affected family members earns supporting to moderate weight depending on number of meioses; the paper indicates segregation sufficient to support PP1.
- PP3 — In silico prediction: The variant has a very high AlphaMissense score (0.995) and is adjacent to known pathogenic residues, consistent with damaging change by multiple computational tools. Multiple concordant deleterious predictions support PP3.
- PP4 — Phenotype match: The proband’s clinical picture—young onset, negative islet autoantibodies, preserved but insufficient C‑peptide, glycosuria, strong family history, and treatment response typical of HNF1A‑MODY—fits the gene‑disease association specifically and supports PP4.

How these pieces combine to “likely pathogenic”
ACMG is a points/weighting framework. A plausible combination here is: PM1 (moderate) + PM2_supporting + PP1 (supporting) + PP3 (supporting) + PP4 (supporting) → accumulation of one moderate + multiple supporting criteria typically yields a Likely Pathogenic classification. The authors reached this conclusion using similar reasoning.

Why absence from ClinVar/VarSome or literature does not equal benign
- Rarity of pathogenic missense variants: Many pathogenic variants are private or recently discovered and therefore absent from databases that depend on published or submitted annotations.
- Curation lag and incomplete submission: ClinVar/VarSome are incomplete; pathogenic calls require clinical labs or authors to submit them.
- Population frequency context matters: A truly benign interpretation requires demonstration of appreciable allele frequency in appropriate population datasets incompatible with dominant disease penetrance. Simple absence is neutral-to-supporting rather than refuting pathogenicity.
- Functional and segregation evidence can outweigh novelty: Well‑demonstrated segregation, domain localization, concordant in silico prediction, and a highly specific phenotype provide independent lines of evidence that can classify novel variants as likely pathogenic even if not previously reported.

Practical caveats and next steps (teaching points)
- Functional assay: A functional study demonstrating impaired HNF1A transcriptional activity would upgrade confidence (could provide PS3, strong evidence).
- Segregation quantification: The strength of PP1 depends on number of segregations; formal LOD scoring increases rigor.
- Population checks: Confirm absence/very low frequency in large databases (gnomAD) and adjust evidence weight if any low-frequency allele is found.
- Clinical reporting: Label as “likely pathogenic” with recommendation for cascade testing of at‑risk relatives and consideration of phenotype‑driven management (as in this case). Reclassify if new evidence (functional data, additional segregation) emerges.

Bottom line
In this case the combination of location in the DNA‑binding domain (PM1), absence from databases (PM2_supporting), segregation with autosomal dominant early‑onset diabetes (PP1), strong computational deleteriousness (PP3), and a classical HNF1A‑MODY phenotype (PP4) supports a Likely Pathogenic designation per ACMG logic. The fact that the variant was novel in ClinVar does not argue against pathogenicity; it reflects rarity and gaps in curation rather than proof of benignity.
