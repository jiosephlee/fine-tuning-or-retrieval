Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] What is the first-line pharmacotherapy traditionally recommended for HNF1A-MODY, and under what clinical circumstances might a GLP-1 receptor agonist be preferred?

First-line pharmacotherapy historically recommended for HNF1A‑MODY
- Low‑dose sulfonylureas (eg, gliclazide, glimepiride) are the traditional first‑line oral therapy. Rationale: HNF1A mutations produce a progressive β‑cell secretory defect but intact insulin responsiveness to KATP‑channel–mediated stimulation; sulfonylureas augment insulin release directly from residual β‑cells and often achieve excellent glycaemic control at low doses.

When a GLP‑1 receptor agonist may be preferred
- Clinical circumstances favoring a GLP‑1 receptor agonist (GLP‑1 RA) over a sulfonylurea include:
  - Declining or marginal β‑cell function with higher baseline HbA1c (as in this case: HbA1c 10.2% and stimulated C‑peptide 0.83 nmol/L), when insulin‑sparing but more potent secretagogues and β‑cell‑preserving strategies are desirable.
  - High risk of hypoglycaemia or prior hypoglycaemia on sulfonylureas (GLP‑1 RAs have lower hypoglycaemia risk).
  - Need for weight loss or avoidance of weight gain (GLP‑1 RAs promote weight loss; sulfonylureas can cause weight gain).
  - Intolerance or contraindication to sulfonylureas.
  - Patient preference for injectable therapy with cardiovascular or metabolic benefits where applicable.
- Evidence and case support: small crossover trials in HNF1A‑MODY show liraglutide and glimepiride have similar glucose‑lowering effects but GLP‑1 RAs have less mild hypoglycaemia. In the reported patient, genetic confirmation of HNF1A‑MODY, moderately preserved but insufficient β‑cell function, and the goal to discontinue insulin led clinicians to start dulaglutide (1.5 mg weekly) with metformin; HbA1c fell from 10.2% to 6.0% over 6 months, illustrating that a GLP‑1 RA can be an effective and safer alternative to sulfonylurea or ongoing insulin in selected HNF1A‑MODY patients.

Practical point
- Use genotype and β‑cell reserve (C‑peptide), glycaemic severity, hypoglycaemia risk, weight goals and patient preference to individualize therapy. Sulfonylureas remain first‑line historically, but GLP‑1 RAs are an appropriate and increasingly used alternative in patients like this one.
