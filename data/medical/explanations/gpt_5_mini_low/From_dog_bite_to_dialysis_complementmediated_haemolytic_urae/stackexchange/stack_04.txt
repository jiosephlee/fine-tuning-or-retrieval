Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Morning Report] In adults presenting with MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and AKI, what ADAMTS13 activity threshold effectively rules in thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), how can the PLASMIC score guide urgency, and what immediate therapy should begin while awaiting definitive results?

Key points and clinical approach

1) ADAMTS13 threshold that defines TTP
- Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency — classically <10% activity — is the diagnostic threshold for immune-mediated TTP. Values well above that (for example the 43.5% reported in this case) effectively rule out TTP and prompt evaluation for other TMAs such as HUS/CM‑HUS.

2) How the PLASMIC score guides urgency
- The PLASMIC score is a validated bedside pretest tool to estimate the probability of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency and to triage therapy while awaiting the assay.
  - High risk (PLASMIC 6–7): high pretest probability of TTP → treat empirically as TTP without delay.
  - Intermediate risk (PLASMIC 5): consider early treatment and urgent ADAMTS13 testing; clinical judgment required.
  - Low risk (PLASMIC ≤4): low probability of TTP → less likely to need immediate TTP-directed therapy; continue diagnostic workup for other causes of TMA.
- In the presented case the PLASMIC score was 4/7 (low pretest probability), which corresponded with an ADAMTS13 of 43.5% and appropriately shifted the team away from TTP as the cause.

3) Immediate therapy to start while awaiting definitive results
- If clinical suspicion for TTP is high (especially PLASMIC 6–7 or rapidly progressive MAHA with severe thrombocytopenia and end‑organ ischemia), begin urgent plasma exchange (PEX) plus high‑dose corticosteroids immediately and involve hematology. Consider adding caplacizumab (anti‑vWF nanobody) early when available for immune TTP to inhibit platelet‑vWF aggregation while awaiting ADAMTS13 confirmation.
- If suspicion is low (PLASMIC ≤4), focus the workup on alternative TMAs (STEC PCR, complement studies, ADAMTS13) but still monitor closely; PEX may be used as a bridging measure in unstable patients or when diagnosis is uncertain.
- Do not give platelet transfusions for thrombocytopenia unless there is life‑threatening bleeding or need for an invasive procedure; transfusions can worsen microthrombosis in TTP/HUS.
- In this case team prudently initiated PEX while awaiting ADAMTS13 and complement results — a reasonable bridge given ongoing haemolysis and thrombocytopenia — then stopped pursuing TTP when ADAMTS13 was 43.5% and proceeded to treat CM‑HUS with complement blockade (eculizumab) after infection control and vaccination.

Bottom line: ADAMTS13 activity <10% diagnoses TTP. Use the PLASMIC score to decide how aggressively to treat empirically: high scores mandate immediate PEX ± steroids and caplacizumab; low scores make TTP unlikely and prompt evaluation for alternative TMAs. When TTP is possible, begin PEX and steroids immediately while awaiting ADAMTS13 results; reserve platelet transfusion for bleeding.
