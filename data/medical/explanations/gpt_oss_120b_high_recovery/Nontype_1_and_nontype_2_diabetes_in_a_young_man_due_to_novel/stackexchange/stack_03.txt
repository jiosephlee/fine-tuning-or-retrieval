Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In evaluating a young adult with hyperglycaemia, which laboratory investigations help distinguish type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, and HNF1A-MODY, and what result thresholds support each diagnosis?

**Laboratory work‑up that separates type 1 diabetes (T1D), type 2 diabetes (T2D) and HNF‑1α MODY in a young adult**

When a patient under 25 years presents with hyperglycaemia, the first laboratory step is a focused panel that tells us three things simultaneously: (1) whether an autoimmune attack on β‑cells is present, (2) how much endogenous insulin secretion remains, and (3) whether there are biochemical hallmarks of insulin resistance or of the renal‐threshold defect typical of HNF1A‑MODY.

| Test | How it discriminates | Thresholds that point toward each entity |
|------|----------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Islet auto‑antibodies** – GAD65 (GADA), IA‑2A, ZnT8 | Presence = autoimmune β‑cell loss → T1D; Absence = non‑autoimmune → T2D or MODY | **Positive** (any titre ≥ 20 IU/mL for GADA or equivalent) → **T1D**<br>**Negative** (all three < 20 IU/mL) → **T2D/MODY** |
| **C‑peptide** – fasting & stimulated (glucagon or mixed meal) | Low values = little residual secretion → T1D;<br>Normal‑high values = retained β‑cell mass → T2D;<br>Intermediate or modest rise after stimulation suggests a genetically mediated secretory defect → MODY | **Fasting < 0.20 nmol/L** (< 0.5 ng/mL) or stimulated increase < 0.30 nmol/L → **T1D**<br>**Fasting ≥ 0.60 nmol/L** (> 1.5 ng/mL) and stimulated ≥ 1.00 nmol/L → **T2D**<br>**Fasting 0.30–0.60 nmol/L** with stimulated rise to **≈ 0.6–0.9 nmol/L** (as in the reported patient whose glucagon test rose from 0.39→0.83 nmol/L) → **HNF‑1α MODY** |
| **OGTT 2‑h plasma glucose** | All three disorders can exceed 11.1 mmol/L, but a disproportionate post‑prandial spike with relatively lower fasting glucose is classic for MODY | **Fasting ≥7 mmol/L + 2‑h ≥11.1 mmol/L** is required for diabetes in all groups; a pattern of “fasting modestly ↑ / post‑load very high” favours MODY when coupled with other labs |
| **Serum lipids & BMI** | Dyslipidaemia (high TG, low HDL) and obesity are markers of insulin resistance → T2D; normal lipids and lean stature are more common in MODY | **BMI ≥30 kg/m²**, triglycerides > 2·3 mmol/L, HDL < 1·03 mmol/L → supports **T2D**<br>**BMI <27 kg/m²**, normal TG/HDL – does not exclude T2D but leans toward **MODY** |
| **Urine glucose relative to serum glucose** | In HNF1A‐MODY the renal SGLT2 transporter is under‑expressed; glycosuria appears at lower plasma glucose than the usual renal threshold (~10 mmol/L). | Detectable urine glucose when serum glucose ≤9–10 mmol/L → suggestive of **HNF‑1α MODY** |

### How the presented case fits the algorithm

*Autoantibodies* were negative (GADA and IA‑2A < 20 IU/mL), steering away from T1D despite his age.<br>
*Stimulated C‑peptide* rose only to 0.83 nmol/L—below the range typically seen in insulin‑resistant T2D (> 1 nmol/L) yet above the “absent” zone of classic T1D—exactly the pattern described for HNF1A‐MODY.<br>
His *BMI* was 24.6 kg/m² and triglycerides were markedly elevated (4.31 mmol/L); while this could mimic metabolic syndrome of T2D, the absence of acanthosis nigricans and a striking autosomal dominant family history tipped the balance toward MODY.<br>
Finally, *glycosuria* was present despite no ketoacidosis and at a serum glucose that did not far exceed the normal renal threshold—a hallmark of reduced SGLT2 activity caused by HNF‑1α deficiency.<br>
When these laboratory clues converged—negative antibodies + modestly preserved but insufficient C‑peptide + early‐onset disease + family history—the team pursued NGS testing, which identified a pathogenic heterozygous p.N270S variant in HNF1A confirming HNF‑1α MODY.

### Bottom line

- **Negative islet antibodies + any measurable C‑peptide rules out classic T1D**, but you still need to know whether β‑cell reserve is robust (T2D) or only partially intact (MODY).  
- **Fasting C‑peptide ≈ 0.3–0.6 nmol/L with a stimulated rise to ≈ 0.6–0·9 nmol/L is the sweet spot for HNF1A‐MODY**, especially when accompanied by early onset, an autosomal dominant pedigree and glycosuria at relatively low plasma glucose.<br>
- **Elevated C‑peptide (> 0·8 nmol/L fasting; > 1·5 nmol/L after stimulation), obesity/metabolic syndrome features and negative antibodies support T2D**, whereas markedly low values (< 0·20 nmol/L fasting or < 0·3 nmol/L after stimulation) confirm T1D regardless of age.

These targeted investigations allow you to narrow the differential quickly enough to move on to definitive genetic testing when MODY is suspected—as illustrated by Liu et al.’s patient whose biochemical profile guided an accurate molecular diagnosis and an individualized treatment plan.​
