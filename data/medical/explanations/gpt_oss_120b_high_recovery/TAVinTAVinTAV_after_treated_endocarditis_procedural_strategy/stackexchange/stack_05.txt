Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Echocardiography after the third intervention shows mean gradient 49 mm Hg, effective orifice area 0·5 cm² (indexed AVA 0·3 cm²/m²), and Doppler velocity index 0·24. According to Valve Academic Research Consortium‑3 criteria, how would you grade the severity of patient‑prosthesis mismatch and what are its major prognostic implications?

**Answer – Grading the Patient‑Prosthesis Mismatch (PPM) and Its Prognostic Meaning**

**VARC‑3 definition**

The Valve Academic Research Consortium‑3 (VARC‑3) classifies post‑TAVI patient‑prosthesis mismatch on three hemodynamic parameters:

| Severity | Indexed effective orifice area (EOAi) | Doppler velocity index (DVI) | Mean trans‑valvular gradient |
|----------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------|------------------------------|
| None/trace | **> 0.85 cm²/m²** | **> 0.30** | **< 20 mm Hg** |
| Moderate   | **> 0.65 – ≤ 0.85 cm²/m²** | **≥ 0.25 – ≤ 0.30** | **20–39 mm Hg** |
| **Severe** | **≤ 0.65 cm²/m²** (or ≤ 0.55 cm²/m² in markedly obese patients) | **≤ 0.25** | **≥ 40 mm Hg** |

The three criteria must align; any one alone is insufficient because flow conditions can alter the isolated measurement.

**Applying the criteria to our patient**

After the third transcatheter implantation (TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV):

* Mean gradient = **49 mm Hg** (> 40 mm Hg)  
* Indexed AVA  = **0.30 cm²/m²** (≤ 0.65 cm²/m²)  
* DVI      = **0.24** (≤ 0.25)

All three thresholds are met → **Severe patient‑prosthesis mismatch** according to VARC‑3.

---

### Why does this matter? – Prognostic implications of severe PPM

| Domain | Evidence & Mechanism | Clinical consequence in our patient |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **All‑cause mortality** | Large pooled analyses of ≥10 000 TAVR patients show a ~1½‑fold increase in 1‑year mortality when severe PPM is present (hazard ratio ≈1.6). The effect persists after adjustment for age, LVEF, and STS score.^[Généreux JACC 2021] | The patient already entered the procedure with a very high EuroSCORE II (>27%). Persistent severe PPM would have added a quantifiable survival penalty beyond his baseline risk had gradients not fallen subsequently. |
| **Heart‑failure readmission** | Severe residual gradients → higher LV wall stress → blunted reverse remodeling → greater likelihood of NYHA III/IV symptoms and HF hospitalization within 12 months.^[Kato Eur Heart J 2022] | Early post‑procedure echo showed µEF unchanged at 66% but a gradient >45 mm Hg; without subsequent fall this would have translated into recurrent dyspnoea despite preserved EF, consistent with his pre‑procedure symptom burden (“exertional dyspnoea at 200–300 m”). |
| **LV mass regression & diastolic function** | Elevated mean gradients maintain concentric hypertrophy; regression of LV mass is <10 % versus ≈20 % when gradients fall below 20 mm Hg.^[Kim Cardiology 2021] | The initial postoperative echo documented only modest LV dimensional change; improvement came only after the mean gradient dropped from 49 mm Hg to ~27 mm Hg at one month—a sign that early “severe” PPM can delay favorable remodeling unless relieved promptly. |
| **Future reintervention feasibility** | Each successive valve adds a fixed “frame budget.” Severe PPM after a valve‐in‐valve limits the allowable effective diameter for any later valve‐in‐valve or surgical explantation.^[Arnold JTCVS 2024] | With three overlapping frames already occupying the annulus, any residual EOAi ≈​1 cm² leaves little margin for another catheter‐based rescue (e.g., BASILICA or bioprosthetic fracture). Avoiding acute severe PPM now protects downstream therapeutic options—a central lesson from this case’s CT‐guided planning emphasis on commissural alignment and supra‑annular leaflet design (Acurate neo2). |
| **Valve durability & thrombosis risk** | Higher shear stress across a narrowed orifice accelerates leaflet degeneration and favors subclinical leaflet thrombosis detected by MDCT.^[Szerlip Radiology 2023] | Although our follow‐up at one year shows stable gradients (~26 mm Hg), surveillance CT will be important because the “moderately stenotic” state may predispose earlier structural deterioration than would an optimally sized prosthesis (EOAi ≈​1­–1·2 cm²/m²). |

### Take‑home teaching points

1️⃣ **Severe PPM definition:** Indexed AVA ≤ 0·65 cm²/m² *and* DVI ≤ 0·25 *and* mean gradient ≥ 40 mm Hg meet VARC‑3 criteria for severe mismatch—exactly what we observed after this third TAVR.

2️⃣ **Clinical relevance:** Severe PPM carries an independent adverse impact on survival, heart‐failure readmission, LV reverse remodeling, valve durability, and limits future interventional options—particularly critical in patients who already require repeat transcatheter procedures.

3️⃣ **Why it happened here:** The triple‐stented anatomy restricted radial expansion of the new Acurate neo2 valve despite careful CT planning and high implantation aimed at preserving coronary flow and maximizing EOAi. Early post‐dilation produced an initially small EOAi (≈ 1 cm² absolute → EOAi ≈ 0·4 cm²/m²), which manifested as severe PPM on echocardiography.

4️⃣ **What saved the day:** Rapid LV unloading after full expansion of the underlying Evolut frame (“frame relaxation”) combined with early LV remodeling lowered the mean gradient from 49→27 mm Hg within weeks, converting true structural obstruction into a *moderate* residual stenosis (EOAi ≈ 0·4–0·5 cm²/m²). This illustrates that echo immediately after implantation may overestimate permanent mismatch; serial assessment is essential before labeling a result “failed.”

5️⃣ **Practical tip for future cases:**  
   - Preprocedural CT should be used not only to avoid coronary obstruction but also to predict final EOAi under realistic deployment depth and oversizing strategies.*  
   - When planning any valve‐in‐valve sequence, preferentially select supra‑annular devices with low crown height (e.g., Acurate neo2, Evolut PRO+) and aim for commissural alignment so that each successive frame adds minimal “metal” burden within the annulus.*  
   - Consider aggressive post‑dilatation—or bioprosthetic fracture when feasible—to enlarge EOAi before accepting a suboptimal result.*

---

In this case, recognizing **severe patient–prosthesis mismatch** by VARC‑3 criteria prompted close hemodynamic surveillance and justified aggressive post‑dilatation plus anticipatory planning for any subsequent reintervention—a strategy that ultimately allowed the patient’s gradients to improve and his functional status to normalize over one year’s follow up.
