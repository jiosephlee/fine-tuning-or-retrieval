Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the hormonal and molecular mechanisms that drive rapid growth of uterine leiomyomas during early pregnancy, emphasizing the roles of estrogen, progesterone, and leiomyoma‑specific growth factors.

**Answer – Why uterine leiomyomas often explode in size during early pregnancy**

The dramatic enlargement of the pedunculated leiomyoma in our 40‑year‑old patient—an 18 cm vascular mass already producing pain and dyspnoea at 21 weeks’ gestation—is a classic illustration of the hormonally driven surge that occurs in the first two trimesters. The core of this “pregnancy‑associated growth spurt” lies in the coordinated actions of estradiol, progesterone, and a cadre of locally produced growth factors that together convert a quiescent smooth‑muscle nodule into a rapidly expanding tumour.  

---

### 1. Estrogen – the initial accelerator

* **Systemic rise** – In the luteal phase of conception and through the first trimester, ovarian estradiol levels double every 3–4 days, reaching concentrations that are ≈5‑fold higher than pre‑pregnancy values.  
* **Receptor activation** – Leiomyoma cells over‑express **ER‑α** (and to a lesser extent ER‑β). Binding of estradiol triggers both classical genomic pathways (direct transcription of target genes) and non‑genomic signalling through membrane‐associated ERs that rapidly activate **MAPK/ERK** and **PI3K/AKT** cascades.  
* **Growth‑factor amplification** – ER activation up‑regulates transcription of **IGF‑1**, **IGF‑2**, **VEGF**, and **PDGF‑A/B**, creating an autocrine loop that drives cell proliferation and neovascularisation.  
* **Local estrogen synthesis** – Estradiol induces **aromatase (CYP19A1)** within leiomyoma stromal fibroblasts, allowing the nodule itself to manufacture additional estrogen (“autocrine steroidogenesis”), thereby sustaining its own growth even after systemic levels plateau.  

In our patient, the size of the pedunculated lesion at 21 weeks implies that most of its bulk accrued during this early phase when estradiol was climbing most steeply—consistent with published data showing a mean weekly increase of ≈6 % between conception and week 13.

---

### 2. Progesterone – the remodeler and vascular stabiliser

* **Systemic surge** – Corpus luteum–derived progesterone rises from ≈7 ng mL⁻¹ in early gestation to >30 ng mL⁻¹ by week 12, later supplemented by placental production.  
* **PR isoform dynamics** – Leiomyomas display an excess of **PR‑B** relative to normal myometrium; PR‑B is especially potent at inducing genes involved in extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition and angiogenesis.  
* **ECM expansion** – Progesterone stimulates **TGF‑β3**, **CTGF**, and downstream SMAD signalling, prompting prolific collagen and fibronectin synthesis. Because leiomyomas are essentially a fibro‑muscular tumour, this ECM accrual contributes more than cellular proliferation to gross size gain.  
* **Angiogenic drive** – Through PR‐mediated transcription, progesterone markedly raises **VEGF**, **bFGF**, and **angiopoietin‑2**, ensuring an expanded capillary network that supplies nutrients to the growing mass.  

The histopathology from our case showed areas of ischaemic necrosis adjacent to a twisted stalk—precisely the scenario when rapid hormonal‐stimulated expansion outpaces vascular supply despite vigorous VEGF–driven angiogenesis.

---

### 3. Leiomyoma‑specific growth factors – the downstream effectors

| Growth factor | Primary hormone trigger | Main effect on leiomyoma |
|---------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| **IGF‑1 / IGF‑2** | Estrogen & progesterone → ↑IGF‐binding proteins ↓ | Stimulates proliferation via IGF‐1R → PI3K/AKT |
| **PDGF‐A/B** | Estrogen ↑ transcription | Autocrine mitogenic loop; activates Src/FAK |
| **VEGF** | Progesterone ↑ via HIF‑1α & ER | Neoangiogenesis; increases lesion perfusion |
| **bFGF (FGF2)** | Estrogen & mechanical stretch | Promotes endothelial proliferation |
| **TGF‑β3 / CTGF** | Progesterone & mechanical cues | Drives collagen synthesis → bulk increase |
| **HIF‑1α** | Hypoxia secondary to rapid expansion | Further upregulates VEGF & glycolytic enzymes |

These molecules are not merely passive markers; they act synergistically with steroid receptors because their promoters contain estrogen‐ or progesterone‐response elements. Consequently, modest rises in circulating steroids translate into exponential amplification of proliferative signalling within the leiomyoma microenvironment.

---

### 4. Integrated “first‐trimester” model

1️⃣ Early rise in estradiol → ER activation → ↑IGF‐1/VEGF + aromatase → local estrogen feed‑forward  
2️⃣ Concurrent progesterone surge → PR‐B dominant → ↑TGF‑β3 + VEGF + bFGF → ECM deposition & angiogenesis  
3️⃣ Cross‑talk between ER/PR pathways potentiates receptor tyrosine kinases (EGFR, PDGFR) → MAPK/ERK + PI3K/AKT amplification  
4️⃣ Resultant net effect = brisk cellular proliferation + marked ECM accumulation + new vessels = ≈6 % weekly volume increase reported in longitudinal studies—and precisely what we observed as an 18 cm pedunculated myoma causing symptomatic mass effect at 21 weeks.

When delivery occurs, withdrawal of both estrogens and progesterone abruptly halts these pathways; consequently most fibroids regress by ~40 % within six weeks postpartum—a pattern noted by the authors who reported slight reduction of the residual pelvic myoma after birth.

---

### Take‑home pearls for trainees

* **Early pregnancy is a hormonal storm:** The most rapid leiomyoma expansion occurs before placental takeover because estradiol from the corpus luteum peaks first, priming PR expression for subsequent progesterone action.  
* **Estrogen is the proliferative trigger; progesterone is the scaffold builder:** Estrogen drives cell division via IGF‑1/PI3K signalling; progesterone orchestrates ECM thickening and vascular support through TGF‑β3 and VEGF.  
* **Local autocrine loops magnify systemic signals:** Aromatase up‑regulation inside leiomyoma tissue sustains estrogenic stimulation even when systemic levels level off.  
* **Growth factors are downstream executors:** IGF‐1/2, PDGF, VEGF, bFGF, and TGF‐β family members convert steroid receptor activation into tangible mass enlargement—this explains why a seemingly modest hormone rise produces an 18 cm tumour within weeks.  
* **Clinical relevance:** In pregnant patients presenting with new or rapidly enlarging abdominal masses—especially when imaging suggests a pedunculated myoma—recognise that hormonal acceleration may generate torsion or degeneration (as seen histologically). Management decisions must weigh symptom burden against obstetric risk because spontaneous regression after delivery is expected once hormonal drive ceases.

Thus, the massive uterine leiomyoma described in this case is not an incidental finding but rather a textbook demonstration of how early pregnancy hormones—through tightly linked genomic and paracrine pathways—convert quiescent smooth‐muscle nodules into rapidly expanding tumours capable of causing significant maternal morbidity if left unchecked.
