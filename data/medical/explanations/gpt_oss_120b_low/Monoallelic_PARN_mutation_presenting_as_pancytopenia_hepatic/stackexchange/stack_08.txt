Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] Identify the key elements of ACMG/AMP criteria used to classify the PARN c.613T>C (p.Cys205Arg) variant as a VUS, and describe how additional functional data could reclassify it.

**Clinical Pearl – Interpreting the PARN c.613T>C (p.Cys205Arg) Variant**

The missense change **PARN c.613T>C (p.Cys205Arg)** was reported in a young adult with a telomere‑biology disorder (TBD) and was classified by the laboratory as a **Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS)**.  The ACMG/AMP framework (Richards et al., 2015) assigns a variant to one of five categories based on a combination of “pathogenic” and “benign” evidence.  In this case the following criteria were applied:

| ACMG/AMP code | Category | How it was met in this case |
|---------------|----------|-----------------------------|
| **PM1** (moderate) | **Located in a critical functional domain** | p.Cys205 lies in the highly conserved **R3H RNA‑binding domain** of PARN, a region with no reported benign missense variation. |
| **PM2** (moderate) | **Absent/very low frequency in population databases** | The allele is absent from gnomAD, ExAC, and large‐scale control cohorts, satisfying the rarity requirement. |
| **PP2** (supporting) | **Gene is intolerant to benign missense variation and missense is a common disease mechanism** | PARN is among the 20 % most missense‑intolerant genes (high Z‑score), and pathogenicity of missense changes is well documented in PARN‑related TBD. |
| **PP3** (supporting) | **Multiple in‑silico tools predict deleterious effect** | Four of five algorithms (LRT, MutationAssessor, MutationTaster, SIFT) predict the Cys205Arg substitution to be damaging. |
| **PP4** (supporting) – *implied* | **Phenotype highly specific for a disease with a single genetic cause** | The patient’s constellation of pancytopenia, hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis, premature greying, and osteopenia fits the spectrum of PARN‑related telomere disease, reinforcing the gene–phenotype link (though not a formal ACMG criterion without additional segregation data). |

**Why the variant remains a VUS**

- The ACMG system requires **≥1 strong** or **≥2 moderate** criteria (plus supporting evidence) to move a VUS to *Likely Pathogenic*.  Here we have **two moderate (PM1, PM2)** but only **supporting** evidence for pathogenicity; no strong evidence (e.g., functional assay, segregation, or de novo occurrence) is available.  Consequently, the classification stays at VUS.

---

### How Additional Functional Data Could Upgrade the Classification

| Additional Evidence | ACMG/AMP code | Effect on classification |
|---------------------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **In‑vitro enzymatic assay** demonstrating **significant loss of PARN deadenylase activity** (e.g., <10 % of wild‑type) | **PS3** (strong) | Provides a **strong pathogenic** line of evidence. Combined with PM1 + PM2 + PP2 + PP3, the variant would meet ≥1 strong + ≥2 moderate → **Likely Pathogenic**. |
| **Patient‑derived fibroblast or lymphoblast telomere length** showing **marked shortening** compared with age‑matched controls and rescued by wild‑type PARN re‑expression | **PS3** (strong) or **PS3 + BS3** (if rescue observed) | Strong functional evidence of pathogenic impact on the telomere maintenance pathway. |
| **Co‑segregation** of the variant with disease in multiple affected relatives (≥3 meioses) | **PP1** (moderate to strong depending on number) | Adds another moderate/strong line, pushing the total toward **Likely Pathogenic** or even **Pathogenic** if combined with PS3. |
| **De‑novo occurrence** confirmed by parental testing with low‑level mosaicism | **PS2** (strong) | Strong evidence independent of functional data; together with existing criteria would also upgrade to Likely Pathogenic. |
| **Animal model (e.g., knock‑in mouse)** recapitulating the human TBD phenotype | **PS3** (strong) | Same impact as cellular assays. |

**Practical steps for the clinical team**

1. **Obtain patient‑derived cells** (skin fibroblasts or peripheral blood mononuclear cells) and measure:
   - PARN deadenylase activity.
   - TERC maturation and telomerase enzymatic activity.
   - Telomere length by flow‑FISH or qPCR.

2. **Test parental DNA** to determine whether the variant is inherited or de‑novo.

3. **If more affected relatives are identified**, perform segregation analysis.

4. **Report any functional results to the laboratory** so they can re‑apply ACMG criteria (PS3/BS3) and update the classification.

---

**Bottom line:** In the presented case the PARN c.613T>C variant meets two moderate and three supporting ACMG criteria, which is insufficient for a pathogenic label, hence the VUS designation.  Robust functional data—demonstrating a loss of PARN activity or telomere shortening—constitutes a **strong (PS3)** piece of evidence that would reclassify the variant as **Likely Pathogenic** (or Pathogenic if additional strong evidence, such as de‑novo status, is also present).  This re‑classification would have direct implications for counseling, surveillance, and eligibility for family‑donor stem‑cell transplantation.
