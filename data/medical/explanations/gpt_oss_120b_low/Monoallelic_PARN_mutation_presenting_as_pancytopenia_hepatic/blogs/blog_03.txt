Title: Blog about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

# PARN Mutations: From Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis to Multisystem Fibrosis – What Clinicians Need to Know  

---

## Why This Case Stands Out  

When a 28‑year‑old woman walks into your clinic with a five‑year history of fatigue, pancytopenia, premature greying and a dry cough, the first thoughts are usually “nutrient deficiency,” “autoimmune marrow failure,” or “early‑onset cirrhosis.”  In the case report by Soni et al., the answer turned out to be something far less obvious—a **monoallelic PARN missense variant** (c.613T>C; p.Cys205Arg) sitting at the crossroads of **telomere biology disorders (TBDs)**, **pulmonary fibrosis**, and **hepatic fibrosis**.  

What makes this case compelling is that classic dyskeratosis‑congenita (DC) skin/nail changes were essentially absent, yet the patient harboured the same underlying telomere‐centric pathology that drives the most lethal forms of DC.  It underscores an emerging theme: **single‑copy (heterozygous) PARN mutations can present with a multisystem fibrotic phenotype that defies the textbook “DC triad.”**  

---

## TL;DR – The Take‑Home Messages  

- **Monoallelic PARN variants are no longer “just” a pulmonary‑fibrosis gene**; they can drive liver, bone‑marrow, and even neurologic involvement.  
- **Absence of the classic DC mucocutaneous triad does NOT exclude a telomere biology disorder.**  
- **Screening for liver and lung fibrosis should be routine** in any patient with a pathogenic or likely‑pathogenic PARN variant, even when pulmonary disease is the presenting feature.  
- **Genetic counseling and longitudinal surveillance** (telomere length testing, pulmonary function, elastography, bone‑density scans) are essential because disease penetrance is variable and may evolve over years.  
- **Management remains supportive**, but early referral for lung or liver transplantation, and consideration of experimental telomere‑targeted therapies (e.g., danazol), may improve long‑term outcomes.

---

## From Gene to Fibrosis – The Molecular Story  

**PARN (Poly(A)-specific Ribonuclease)** is a deadenylase that trims poly(A) tails from a variety of RNAs, most importantly the *TERC* transcript—the RNA component of telomerase.  Proper deadenylation is required for maturation of TERC; loss of PARN activity leads to **reduced TERC stability**, blunted telomerase activity, and **accelerated telomere shortening**.  

- **Haploinsufficiency**: Monoallelic loss‑of‑function variants (most missense changes in the R3H RNA‑binding domain) are sufficient to tip the telomere balance in high‑turnover tissues.  
- **Telomere attrition** → replicative senescence of **hematopoietic stem cells**, **alveolar epithelial cells**, **hepatic stellate cells**, and **dermal fibroblasts**.  
- The senescent cells adopt a **pro‑fibrotic secretome** (SASP), fostering extracellular matrix deposition and organ fibrosis.  

In contrast to biallelic PARN mutations that cause classic recessive DC (early‑onset bone‑marrow failure, mucocutaneous changes), heterozygous variants tend to manifest later and more variably—most commonly as **idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)**, but increasingly as **multisystem disease**.

## What the Literature Is Telling Us  

| Study | Population | PARN Variant(s) | Main Phenotype(s) |
|------|------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| *Stellacci et al., 2022* | 262 sporadic IPF cases (WES) | 5 monoallelic missense/frameshift | Isolated IPF (median onset 58 y) |
| *Vink et al., 2021* | Registry of TBD (n = 124) | 12 monoallelic, 3 biallelic | IPF ± BM failure; 2 with liver fibrosis |
| *Soni et al., 2024* (case report) | Single 28‑y woman | c.613T>C (p.Cys205Arg) | Pancytopenia, hepatic cirrhosis, IPF, premature greying |
| *Krebs et al., 2023* | 8 families with heterozygous PARN | Various missense | Variable: IPF, early‑onset cirrhosis, anemia, skin hyperpigmentation |

**Key observations**

1. **Penetrance is incomplete** – not everyone with a pathogenic PARN allele develops disease; environmental hits (smoking, viral hepatitis, vitamin deficiencies) likely accelerate tissue injury.  
2. **Liver involvement may be under‑reported** – elastography data from the Aachen Telomere Registry shows >80 % of telomerase‑mutant adults have at least moderate hepatic stiffness; similar trends are now emerging for PARN carriers.  
3. **Bone‑marrow failure can coexist** – even when the primary driver appears to be pulmonary fibrosis, cytopenias may surface under stress (e.g., B12 deficiency, chemotherapy).

## Practical Clinical Approach  

### 1. When to Suspect a PARN‑Related TBD  

- Young‑to‑middle‑aged adult (≤50 y) with **idiopathic interstitial lung disease** (especially UIP pattern) **and** any of the following:
  - Unexplained cytopenias or macrocytic anemia
  - Premature greying, skin hyperpigmentation, nail dystrophy (even subtle)
  - Hepatomegaly, abnormal liver enzymes, or imaging evidence of fibrosis
  - Family history of early‑onset fibrosis, marrow failure, or unexplained death

### 2. Diagnostic Work‑up  

| Test | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| **Targeted NGS panel** (telomere‑related genes) or **whole‑exome sequencing** | Identify PARN or other TBD variants |
| **Telomere length assay** (flow‑FISH on granulocytes) | Confirms functional impact; <1st percentile in age‑matched controls is highly suggestive |
| **High‑resolution CT chest** | Quantify fibrosis pattern; look for UIP, honeycombing |
| **Transient elastography (FibroScan) or MR elastography** | Non‑invasive liver stiffness; >8 kPa warrants hepatology referral |
| **Bone‑marrow aspirate/biopsy** (if cytopenias) | Exclude malignancy; assess cellularity |
| **Baseline labs**: CBC, CMP, vitamin B12, folate, iron studies, autoimmune panel | Rule out reversible contributors (as in the case report) |

### 3. Ongoing Surveillance  

- **Pulmonary** – Full PFTs + DLCO every 6–12 months; repeat HRCT if clinically indicated.  
- **Hepatic** – LFTs quarterly, elastography annually; screen for varices if cirrhosis confirmed.  
- **Hematologic** – CBC with differential every 3–6 months; consider repeat marrow if counts trend down.  
- **Other** – DEXA annually for osteoporosis (common in TBD), ophthalmologic exam for cataracts, and dermatologic assessment for skin changes.  

### 4. Therapeutic Considerations  

| Intervention | Evidence / Comment |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Supportive care** (oxygen, vaccinations, nutrition) | First‑line for symptom control |
| **Androgen therapy (danazol, nandrolone)** | Small series show telomere lengthening & improved marrow output; off‑label, monitor liver enzymes & lipid profile |
| **Lung transplantation** | Accepted indication in IPF; consider earlier referral because systemic disease may limit candidacy later |
| **Liver transplantation** | Rare but reported; requires multidisciplinary evaluation (telomere length may influence post‑transplant complications) |
| **Hemopoietic stem‑cell transplant (HSCT)** | Curative for marrow failure; high transplant‑related mortality in TBD—reserve for severe aplastic anemia or MDS |
| **Clinical trials** (e.g., telomerase activators, gene therapy) | Ongoing; enrollment considered in specialized centers |

## Putting It All Together – A Clinical Vignette  

*Imagine you’re called to evaluate a 35‑year‑old man with progressive dyspnea and a recent diagnosis of “cryptogenic organizing pneumonia.”  His CBC shows mild macrocytosis; he has a subtle streak of gray hair in his early 30s.  A quick review of his chart reveals a mildly elevated ALT and a family history of a cousin who died of liver failure at 45.  You order a CT (UIP pattern) and a FibroScan (stiffness 12 kPa).  The next step?  Send a targeted telomere‑gene panel.  If a monoallelic PARN missense variant pops up, you now have a unifying diagnosis that explains both his lung and liver disease, guides transplant eligibility, and opens the door to genetic counseling for his offspring.*

---

## Bottom Line  

Monoallelic **PARN** mutations are a **bridge** between isolated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and the broader **telomere biology disorder spectrum**.  Clinicians need to broaden their diagnostic net: when a patient presents with *any* combination of unexplained fibrosis (lung, liver, bone), cytopenias, or premature ageing signs, a telomere‑focused genetic work‑up should be high on the differential.  

Early identification enables **targeted surveillance**, **timely transplant referral**, and **family counseling**, potentially altering the trajectory of a disease that would otherwise continue its silent, fibrotic march.
