Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] Explain the physiologic changes in uterine leiomyoma size throughout pregnancy and the postpartum period, including the typical weekly growth rates and the expected regression after delivery.

**Physiologic growth and regression of uterine leiomyomas in pregnancy**

Uterine leiomyomas are hormonally responsive smooth‑muscle tumors that expand markedly when exposed to the high‐estrogen, high‑progesterone milieu of pregnancy. The case of a 40‑year‑old gravida at 21 weeks illustrates the typical kinetic pattern that has been documented in prospective ultrasound series.

| Pregnancy interval | Mean weekly increase in leiomyoma volume* | Comment |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------------|---------|
| 1st trimester → 2nd trimester | **≈ 6 % per week** (6.90 % overall; 6.12 % p < 0.001) | Growth is driven primarily by estrogen‑mediated smooth‑muscle hypertrophy and extracellular matrix deposition. |
| 2nd trimester → 3rd trimester | **≈ 3 % per week** (3.10 % ± 0.39 % p < 0.05) | Progesterone predominates; the slower rate reflects a plateau as the uterus reaches its maximal size and blood flow is redistributed to the placenta. |

*The percentages refer to *volume* change, not linear diameter, but for roughly spherical nodules the linear dimension rises by ~2 % per week in the first half of pregnancy and ~1 % per week thereafter.

#### Mechanisms

1. **Estrogen surge (weeks 4‑14).** Estrogen up‑regulates insulin‑like growth factor‑1 (IGF‑1) and promotes smooth‑muscle cell proliferation, accounting for the brisk early expansion.
2. **Progesterone dominance (mid‑gestation).** Progesterone promotes collagen synthesis and edema within the tumor stroma, leading to the classic “cystic degeneration” seen on ultrasound/MRI (as in the present case). This effect is less proliferative, hence the slower growth rate.
3. **Mechanical stretch.** Uterine enlargement itself stretches the myometrium, adding a component of passive enlargement.

#### Post‑partum involution

After delivery the hormone milieu reverses abruptly:

- **Estrogen and progesterone fall** within days.
- **Oxytocin‑mediated uterine contractions** compress the myoma, enhancing ischemia.
- **Matrix remodeling** (increased metalloproteinase activity) leads to rapid loss of the edema component.

The case report cites a prospective cohort that measured a **mean reduction of 44 % ± 36 % by 6 weeks postpartum** (p < 0.05). In practice:

- **Linear shrinkage** of 10‑15 % per week is common in the first month.
- **Residual size** after 6 weeks often mirrors the pre‑pregnancy baseline, especially for intramural or subserosal lesions that were ≤ 8 cm before conception (as in the residual 7.5‑cm myoma of the patient).

#### Clinical implications

- **Expectant management** is appropriate for most asymptomatic leiomyomas because they will tend to regress after delivery.  
- **Surgical intervention** during pregnancy is reserved for complications (torsion, necrosis, severe pain, obstruction) because the expected physiological growth (≈ 6 %/wk early, ≈ 3 %/wk later) can be anticipated and monitored.  
- **Post‑partum follow‑up** (US at 6 weeks) is essential to document involution and decide whether definitive surgery is needed.

In summary, uterine leiomyomas enlarge rapidly in the first half of pregnancy (≈ 6 % weekly), slow down in the later half (≈ 3 % weekly), and then regress dramatically after delivery, with a mean 44 % volume loss by 6 weeks postpartum. This kinetic profile underlies the conservative approach taken in the presented case, and it guides timing of any necessary surgical intervention.
