Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Morning Report] During intra‑operative management of a pregnant patient undergoing myomectomy, which fetal monitoring and maternal pharmacologic strategies are recommended to minimize the risk of preterm labor and fetal compromise?

**Intra‑operative fetal monitoring and maternal pharmacologic prophylaxis to limit pre‑term labor in a second‑trimester myomectomy**

1. **Fetal monitoring**  
   * **Baseline assessment** – Before the incision the obstetric team must obtain a **baseline fetal heart rate (FHR) tracing** by trans‑vaginal or trans‑abdominal Doppler ultrasound. In the case report the surgeons “checked fetal cardiac activity” before beginning the laparotomy, consistent with current recommendations that a reliable baseline is essential for intra‑operative comparison.  
   * **Continuous or intermittent monitoring** – Because the uterus is still relatively small at 21 weeks, a **continuous external Doppler** probe can be placed and recorded throughout the procedure. If positioning or surgical access makes continuous monitoring unsafe, the standard is **intermittent monitoring every 15‑20 min** (or after any major manipulation of the uterus) with a handheld Doppler. The goal is to detect bradycardia or decelerations promptly; any sustained abnormality should prompt immediate obstetric evaluation and consideration of emergent delivery if gestational age permits.  
   * **Documentation** – All pre‑, intra‑, and post‑operative FHR recordings must be saved in the operative note, as emphasized by the multicenter series cited in the discussion (Zhao et al.).

2. **Maternal pharmacologic strategies**  

   | Goal | Recommended agent (dose & timing) | Rationale in this case |
   |------|-----------------------------------|------------------------|
   | **Uterine quiescence (prevent pre‑term labor)** | **Progesterone** – 100 mg IM immediately after skin incision (as done in the case) and then 100 mg IM daily until discharge; thereafter depot hydroxy‑progesterone caproate 341 mg IM every 3 days until 37 wks. | Progesterone supports the luteal phase, decreases myometrial contractility, and has a well‑established safety profile in the second trimester. The case report used exactly this regimen and reported no episodes of intra‑operative uterine activity. |
   | **Rescue tocolysis (if uterine contractions develop)** | **Nifedipine** 10 mg PO loading, then 20 mg q8h (or IV nicardipine infusion if oral route unavailable). | Calcium‑channel blockers are first‑line for acute uterine contractions; they are safe for the fetus and do not interfere with surgical hemostasis. |
   | **Antibiotic prophylaxis** | **Cefazolin** 2 g IV 30 min before incision (as performed), then 1 g IV q8h for 24‑48 h; continue culture‑directed therapy (e.g., clarithromycin) if infection is present. | Reduces the risk of postoperative infection, which could precipitate uterine irritability and pre‑term labor. |
   | **Anemia correction** (indirectly helps uterine oxygenation) | **IV iron** (e.g., ferric carboxymaltose 1 g) and blood transfusion if Hb < 8 g/dL. | Improves maternal oxygen‑carrying capacity, decreasing fetal hypoxia‑driven uterine activity. |
   | **Avoidance of uterine irritants** | No oxytocin, avoid prostaglandins, NSAIDs, or high‑dose beta‑agonists intra‑operatively. | These agents can trigger contractions; the case report deliberately omitted them. |

3. **Practical intra‑operative workflow (derived from the case)**  

   1. **Pre‑operative** – Review obstetric ultrasound, confirm fetal viability, start prophylactic 100 mg IM progesterone.  
   2. **Induction of anesthesia** – Use agents with minimal uterine effect (e.g., propofol, short‑acting opioids). Maintain normocapnia (PaCO₂ ≈ 35‑45 mm Hg) to avoid fetal acidosis.  
   3. **Positioning** – Semi‑recumbent with left uterine displacement to prevent aortocaval compression.  
   4. **Monitoring** – Baseline FHR, then continuous Doppler if feasible; otherwise intermittent checks every 15 min and after any uterine manipulation.  
   5. **Pharmacology** – Administer the second dose of IM progesterone intra‑operatively (as in the report) and have nifedipine ready for any uterine activity.  
   6. **Post‑operative** – Continue daily IM progesterone until discharge, then switch to depot caproate until 37 weeks; monitor FHR daily with obstetric ultrasound; watch for signs of pre‑term labor (uterine tenderness, cervical change).  

**Bottom line:** In a second‑trimester myomectomy, fetal well‑being is best safeguarded by **baseline and frequent Doppler FHR assessment** throughout the case, while maternal uterine quiescence is achieved with **immediate and ongoing progesterone supplementation** (100 mg IM daily, followed by depot caproate) and **readily available rescue tocolysis** (nifedipine). Antibiotic prophylaxis, correction of anemia, and avoidance of uterine irritants round out a strategy that, as illustrated in the presented case, can preserve pregnancy and yield an excellent neonatal outcome.
