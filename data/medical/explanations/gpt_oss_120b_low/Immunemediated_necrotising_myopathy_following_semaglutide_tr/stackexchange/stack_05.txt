Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the key histopathologic findings on muscle biopsy that confirm a diagnosis of IMNM and differentiate it from other inflammatory myopathies.

**Key Histopathologic Features that Confirm Immune‑Mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM) and Distinguish It From Other Inflammatory Myopathies**

1. **Predominant Myofibre Necrosis with Regeneration**  
   - *What you see*: Scattered, often confluent necrotic fibers admixed with numerous basophilic, newly‑regenerating fibers (central nuclei, enlarged size, basophilic cytoplasm).  
   - *Why it matters*: In IMNM necrosis is the dominant process; the inflammatory infiltrate is minimal. This contrasts with polymyositis (PM) and inclusion‑body myositis (IBM), where end‑omysial or perimysial T‑cell infiltrates are prominent and necrosis is less extensive.

2. **Sparse Lymphocytic Infiltrate**  
   - *What you see*: Occasional CD3⁺ T‑cells, usually perivascular or in the endomysium, but overall low density (often <10 cells per high‑power field).  
   - *Why it matters*: PM and dermatomyositis (DM) show dense CD8⁺ (PM) or CD4⁺/B‑cell (DM) infiltrates that invade non‑necrotic fibers. The paucity of lymphocytes is a hallmark of IMNM.

3. **Prominent Macrophage‑Dominated Phagocytosis (CD68⁺)**  
   - *What you see*: Numerous CD68⁺ macrophages surrounding and engulfing necrotic fibers (myophagocytosis). Acid phosphatase staining highlights these active phagocytes.  
   - *Why it matters*: Macrophage‑mediated clearance is a characteristic response in IMNM. In DM, macrophages are present but usually perivascular; in PM they are fewer and confined to sites of fiber injury.

4. **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC, C5b‑9) Deposition on Sarcolemma**  
   - *What you see*: Immunohistochemistry shows linear or granular C5b‑9 staining on both necrotic and non‑necrotic fibers.  
   - *Why it matters*: Complement‑mediated membrane injury is a pathogenetic driver in IMNM (especially HMGCR‑positive disease). MAC deposition is less extensive in PM/DM and absent in purely toxic rhabdomyolysis.

5. **Absence of Perifascicular Atrophy and Perimysial Inflammation**  
   - *What you see*: No perifascicular fiber atrophy, no perimysial fibrosis or inflammatory cuffs.  
   - *Why it matters*: These findings are typical of DM (perifascicular atrophy) and to a lesser extent PM (perimysial infiltrates). Their absence supports IMNM.

6. **No Rimmed Vacuoles or Intranuclear Inclusions**  
   - *What you see*: No rimmed vacuoles, β‑amyloid–positive inclusions, or p62 aggregates.  
   - *Why it matters*: These are hallmarks of IBM and are not seen in IMNM.

---

### How These Findings Were Illustrated in the Reported Case

- **Biopsy of the right vastus lateralis** showed **dispersed necrotic and regenerating fibers** (Fig 3A‑B).  
- **Acid phosphatase and CD68 immunostains** highlighted **macrophage infiltrates actively phagocytosing necrotic fibers** (Fig 3D, Fig 4B).  
- **C5b‑9 (MAC) staining** was positive on both necrotic and intact fibers, confirming **complement‑mediated injury** (Fig 4C).  
- **CD3⁺ T‑cells** were **rare** and largely perivascular (Fig 4A), demonstrating the **paucity of lymphocytic infiltrate**.  
- **No perifascicular atrophy, perimysial inflammation, rimmed vacuoles, or inclusion bodies** were reported, effectively **ruling out DM, PM, and IBM**.

These combined features satisfy the pathologic criteria for IMNM as delineated in contemporary consensus definitions (European Neuromuscular Centre, 2022). Recognizing this pattern is essential because it directs treatment toward aggressive immunosuppression (high‑dose steroids ± IVIG) rather than supportive care alone, which would be appropriate for a purely toxic rhabdomyolysis picture.
