Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Clinical Pearls] How does the presence of a normal C3/C4 level influence the diagnostic approach to suspected atypical HUS, and why can these levels be misleading?

**Answer**  

A normal C3 and C4 does **not** rule out complement‑mediated (atypical) HUS (CM‑HUS). In the work‑up of a patient with a new‑onset thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) you should still pursue the full complement panel and consider eculizumab even when the routine C3/C4 levels are within laboratory reference ranges.  

**Why a normal C3/C4 can be misleading**

1. **C3/C4 are crude, static measures** – they reflect the bulk plasma concentration of the classical‑pathway components, not the activity of the alternative pathway that drives CM‑HUS.  
2. **Acute phase reactions mask consumption** – during sepsis, inflammation, or after plasma exchange (PEX) the liver synthesises more C3 and C4, often normalising or even elevating the values despite ongoing complement activation.  
3. **Therapeutic interventions alter the assay** – in the presented case the patient received three sessions of PEX and a dose of eculizumab before the extended complement work‑up. Both procedures remove circulating complement fragments and can artificially raise measured C3/C4 while suppressing functional assays (e.g., AH50).  

**How this influences the diagnostic algorithm**

| Step | Typical approach | Modification in light of normal C3/C4 |
|------|------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Initial screen** | Check C3, C4, ADAMTS13, STEC PCR. | If C3/C4 are normal, **do not stop**; still send an **alternative‑pathway functional assay (AH50), factor H/I/B levels, and anti‑factor H antibodies**. |
| **Probability of TTP** | Use PLASMIC score, ADAMTS13. | Normal ADAMTS13 (≈ 43 %) already makes TTP unlikely; focus shifts to complement. |
| **Decision on plasma exchange** | Start PEX if TTP cannot be excluded. | In CM‑HUS, PEX is a temporising measure; continue it only as a bridge while awaiting full complement results. |
| **Trigger assessment** | Look for diarrhoeal prodrome, infection, drugs, pregnancy. | In this case the trigger was **Capnocytophaga canimorsus septicemia**, a recognized, albeit rare, stimulus for uncontrolled alternative‑pathway activation. |
| **Initiation of complement blockade** | Usually after confirming low C3 or genetic abnormality. | Because C3/C4 may be normal, **clinical criteria (persistent MAHA, schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, AKI, biopsy‑proven TMA) and exclusion of TTP/STEC‑HUS** are sufficient to start eculizumab promptly, especially when the patient is stabilising from sepsis and cultures are negative. |

**Teaching point from the case**

The patient’s C3 and C4 were **within normal limits** at the time the complement studies were drawn, yet the functional alternative‑pathway activity (AH50) was profoundly reduced (6 %). This discordance illustrates that reliance on C3/C4 alone would have led to a missed or delayed diagnosis of CM‑HUS. Recognising that normal complement levels are common in CM‑HUS—particularly in the setting of sepsis and after plasma exchange—prevents a false sense of security and ensures that the definitive therapy (eculizumab) is not postponed.

In summary, **normal C3/C4 should not deter you from pursuing a full complement work‑up or from initiating complement inhibition** when the clinical picture is compatible with CM‑HUS. Treat the syndrome, not the laboratory number.
