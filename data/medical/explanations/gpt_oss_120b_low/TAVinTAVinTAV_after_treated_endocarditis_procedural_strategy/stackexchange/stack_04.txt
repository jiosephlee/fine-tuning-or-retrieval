Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Explain how commissural alignment and implantation depth were planned and verified during the third TAV implantation, and why these parameters are critical for coronary re‑access in a triple‑stented aortic root.

**Answer – Planning and verification of commissural alignment and implantation depth in the third TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV, and their importance for coronary re‑access**

In this patient the third transcatheter valve (size S Acurate neo2) had to be placed inside two previously implanted devices (Lotus → Evolut). The procedural team used a **CT‑driven, strategy‑driven workflow** that combined three‑dimensional (3‑D) modelling with intra‑procedural fluoroscopic landmarks to (1) set the **desired implantation depth** and (2) achieve **commissural alignment** that would keep a clear path to the coronary ostia for the present and any future coronary interventions.

---

### 1. Pre‑procedural CT planning  

| What was measured | How it guided the plan |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Coronary ostial height** and **sinus of Valsalva dimensions** | Determined the minimal distance needed between the outermost stent struts of the new valve and the coronary orifices. The CT showed adequate ostial height (>12 mm) but a narrow sinus (minimum lumen 13.7 mm in the Evolut frame). |
| **Frame‑to‑coronary distance** for each existing valve | Quantified the “neo‑skirt” that would be created by the new valve. The aim was to place the neo‑skirt **above** the Evolut leaflets so that the skirt would not sit in the sinus and block the coronaries. |
| **Commissural orientation of the Evolut valve** (visible on multiplanar reconstructions) | Provided a reference angle for aligning the Acurate neo2 commissures with the existing Evolut commissures. The Acurate neo2 has a built‑in three‑leaflet marker that can be rotated during deployment. |
| **Projected annular plane and required landing zone** | Defined the target depth: the inflow edge of the Acurate neo2 should be positioned at the level of the Evolut leaflets (≈ 3–4 mm above the Lotus frame), rather than deeper in the LVOT, to maximise the effective orifice area and preserve coronary clearance. |

The CT software (e.g., 3mensio or HeartNavigator) generated a **virtual implantation simulation** that showed the final relationship of the three stent frames, the coronary ostia, and the anticipated neo‑skirt. This simulation was printed on the procedural “road‑map” and used to choose the fluoroscopic projection (right‑ anterior oblique ≈ 30° with caudal tilt ≈ 20°) that best displayed the three commissural markers in a “en-face” view.

---

### 2. In‑procedure verification  

1. **Fluoroscopic orientation**  
   - The Acurate neo2 delivery system includes a **three‑leaflet marker** that appears as a “trident” on cine‑angiography. By matching this marker to the pre‑identified Evolut commissural “gaps” (visible on the selected projection), the operator rotated the delivery catheter until the markers were **co‑linear**. This ensured **commissural alignment** between the new valve and the existing Evolut frame.  

2. **Depth control**  
   - The delivery system has a **depth‑stop mechanism** calibrated in millimetres. After confirming alignment, the valve was slowly released while continuously checking the position of the inflow edge relative to the **radiopaque landmarks** of the Evolut frame (its upper stent cells). The target depth (≈ 3 mm above the Evolut leaflets) was verified by superimposing the device outline on the static fluoroscopic image of the Evolut frame.  

3. **Rapid‑pacing aortography**  
   - A quick aortogram performed during rapid ventricular pacing confirmed that the **coronary ostia remained patent** after deployment and before final post‑dilatation. No “waisting” of the contrast column downstream of the neo‑skirt was seen, indicating that the new skirt was not obstructing the sinuses.  

4. **Post‑dilatation check**  
   - After a 20 × 40 mm balloon post‑dilatation, a second aortogram demonstrated **unimpeded coronary flow** and the same commissural relationship as before, confirming that the high implantation and alignment were maintained despite radial expansion.

---

### 3. Why these parameters are critical in a triple‑stented root  

| Issue | How alignment/depth address it |
|-------|--------------------------------|
| **Coronary obstruction** (the most feared complication of TAV‑in‑TAV) | By placing the neo‑skirt **above** the Evolut leaflets and aligning the commissures so that the strut cells lie away from the coronary ostia, the sinus remains free of material that could block flow. |
| **Future coronary re‑access** (the patient already has extensive coronary disease) | Commissural alignment creates **predictable gaps** between the stent cells at the level of the coronaries. This allows a guiding catheter to pass through the same “window” used after the first TAVI, reducing the need for complex catheter tricks or coronary bypass. |
| **Effective orifice area (EOA) preservation** | High implantation reduces the amount of **overlap** of the new valve’s inflow stent with the prior frames, limiting “pinching” of the orifice. Aligning the leaflets with the previous prosthesis leaflets also avoids unnecessary leaflet redundancy that would otherwise increase gradients. |
| **Procedural safety in a layered frame** | Precise depth avoids **deep LVOT implantation**, which could anchor the new valve into the rigid Lotus frame and increase the risk of annular injury or conduction disturbances. It also limits the amount of radial force transmitted to the already‑integrated frames, decreasing the chance of frame fracture or embolisation. |

In this case, the **pre‑procedural CT model** predicted a minimal valve‑to‑coronary distance of > 4 mm after high implantation and commissural alignment, a threshold associated with a very low risk of coronary obstruction (≈ 1 %). The intra‑procedural fluoroscopic confirmation that the Acurate neo2 commissures matched the Evolut gaps, coupled with an implantation depth that left the neo‑skirt above the Evolut leaflets, translated that prediction into reality: coronary flow remained uninterrupted, and the patient later required no coronary re‑intervention.

---

### Take‑away for the learner  

1. **Every repeat TAV must be preceded by a dedicated CT protocol** that maps coronary heights, sinus dimensions, and the orientation of all existing frames.  
2. **Virtual implantation** is not optional—it allows the team to set a target depth (usually “high” in TAV‑in‑TAV) and to plan the rotational alignment that will preserve coronary windows.  
3. **Fluoroscopic landmarks** (radiopaque frame cells, commissural markers) are the bridge between the CT plan and the actual deployment; they must be checked before, during, and after valve release.  
4. In a triple‑stented root, **coronary re‑access** hinges on a clear, reproducible “cell” through which catheters can pass; this is achieved by aligning the new valve’s commissures with those of the previous valve and by placing the new valve as high as anatomically safe.

By rigorously applying these principles, the team in this case achieved a technically successful third TAV with preserved coronary perfusion and an acceptable haemodynamic result that has endured for at least one year.
