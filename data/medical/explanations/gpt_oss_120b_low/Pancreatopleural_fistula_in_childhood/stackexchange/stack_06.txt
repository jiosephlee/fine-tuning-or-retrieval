Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] When conservative therapy fails in a child with PPF, what endoscopic intervention is preferred, what is its mechanism of action, and what are the typical success rates reported in pediatric series?

**Answer – Endoscopic trans‑papillary stenting for refractory pediatric pancreatopleural fistula**

When a child with a pancreatopleural fistula (PPF) does not respond to the initial 10‑ to 14‑day course of bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, octreotide and thoracic drainage, the next step that most authors recommend is **endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with placement of a trans‑papillary pancreatic duct stent**.

**Why this technique is preferred**

1. **Pathophysiologic rationale**  
   - The fistula persists because pancreatic secretions continue to leak through a disrupted duct or a pseudocyst into the retro‑peritoneum and up through the diaphragmatic hiatus.  
   - A plastic stent across the major papilla into the main pancreatic duct creates a low‑pressure “drainage conduit” that diverts the bulk of exocrine flow downstream into the duodenum, thereby **reducing intraductal pressure** at the site of disruption.  
   - Lower downstream pressure allows the ruptured ductal wall to approximate and heal, while the existing fistulous tract gradually fibroses and closes.

2. **Technical advantages in children**  
   - ERCP is now routinely performed in pediatric centers with adult‑size scopes and dedicated pediatric accessories.  
   - The procedure is minimally invasive, avoids a laparotomy, and can be repeated if needed.  
   - Stent dwell times of **4–6 weeks** are usually sufficient; the stent is then removed endoscopically.

**Reported success in pediatric series**

| Series (children) | Number of pts | Endoscopic strategy | Primary closure rate |
|-------------------|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Yang et al., 2021 (25 pts) | 25 | ERCP ± pancreatic duct stent | 48 % fistula closure within 2–3 wks; overall success ≈ 100 % when stent placed |
| Uchiyama et al., mixed age (including children) | — | Trans‑papillary stent | Long‑term success > 90 % |
| Surgical series (for comparison) | 15 | Distal pancreatectomy, Puestow, etc. | 93 % immediate success (but higher morbidity) |
| Overall pediatric literature (≤ 47 reported PPF) | — | Endoscopic stent after failed medical therapy | **~48 % rapid closure**, and when a stent could be placed the **overall technical success approaches 100 %** (i.e., the duct is successfully cannulated and stented). |

Thus, in the setting of failed conservative therapy, **ERCP‑guided trans‑papillary pancreatic duct stenting** is the preferred endoscopic intervention. Its mechanism—decompression of the pancreatic ductal system and diversion of secretions—produces fistula closure in roughly half of children within a few weeks, and when the duct can be successfully accessed the procedural success rate is essentially 100 %. If stenting is not feasible (e.g., severe distal duct obstruction or inaccessible fistula), surgical options are considered thereafter.
