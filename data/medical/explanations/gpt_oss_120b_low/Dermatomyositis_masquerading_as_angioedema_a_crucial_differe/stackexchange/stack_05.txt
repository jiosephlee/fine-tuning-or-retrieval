Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] What imaging modalities were employed to assess the extent of muscle involvement and to screen for malignancy, and what specific findings supported the diagnosis of dermatomyositis in this case?

**Imaging that defined the extent of muscle disease and excluded occult cancer**

| Modality | Why it was ordered | Key findings that pointed toward dermatomyositis |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| **CT neck (initial emergency study)** | Performed to rule out a deep‑space infection (Ludwig’s angina) because the patient presented with a rapidly enlarging neck mass. | Diffuse edema of the strap muscles with extension into the retropharyngeal space – a non‑purulent, infiltrative pattern that is atypical for bacterial cellulitis and raised the suspicion that the swelling might be muscular rather than infectious. |
| **MRI of the thighs** | Gold‑standard for delineating active inflammatory myopathy; high‑resolution soft‑tissue contrast can demonstrate edema, necrosis and fatty infiltration. | T2‑weighted/STIR images showed symmetric, high‑signal edema in the bilateral obturator internus/external obturator, hip adductors, gracilis and distal iliopsoas. The distribution (proximal‑pelvic muscles) and the perifascicular pattern are classic for active dermatomyositis. |
| **FDG‑PET/CT** | Recommended in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies both to map the full muscular burden and to screen for an associated malignancy (IMACS cancer‑screening guideline). | Intense, diffuse FDG uptake in multiple muscle groups—most conspicuous in the head/neck, deltoids, forearms, chest wall and pelvis—mirroring the MRI‑demonstrated myositis. No focal hypermetabolic lesions were seen outside the musculature, making a paraneoplastic process unlikely at that time. |
| **CT chest, abdomen & pelvis** (contrast‑enhanced) | Part of the cancer‑screening work‑up (high‑risk phenotype: dermatomyositis + dysphagia + anti‑SAE1). | Scattered non‑cavitating pulmonary nodules (largest 15 mm) with peribronchial consolidation consistent with aspiration rather than metastatic disease; no enlarged mediastinal or abdominal lymph nodes and no solid organ masses. |
| **PET‑CT (whole‑body)** – same study as above | Provides whole‑body metabolic mapping for occult neoplasm. | No metabolically active lymphadenopathy or solid tumor; the only FDG‑avid sites were muscular, supporting the interpretation that the FDG signal was inflammatory rather than malignant. |

**How these studies together confirmed the diagnosis**

1. **Pattern of muscle involvement** – MRI demonstrated symmetric, proximal muscle edema with a perifascicular distribution, while FDG‑PET confirmed that this edema was metabolically active inflammation rather than chronic fatty change. This concordance satisfies the imaging criteria for inflammatory myopathy (e.g., Bohan & Peter, updated IMACS recommendations).

2. **Exclusion of infection or neoplasm** – The CT neck showed non‑purulent edema (no abscess, gas, or fluid collection). Chest/abdomen/pelvis CT and the whole‑body PET failed to reveal any primary or metastatic tumor, meeting the recommended baseline cancer‑screening for dermatomyositis.

3. **Correlation with clinical and laboratory data** – The imaging‑defined muscle inflammation paralleled the markedly elevated CK (16 184 U/L), transaminases, LDH, and the presence of anti‑SAE1 antibodies—together forming a classic sero‑clinical picture of dermatomyositis.

Thus, the **combined use of contrast‑enhanced CT (neck), MRI of the thighs, and whole‑body FDG‑PET/CT** allowed the team to (1) map the full extent of muscular inflammation, (2) distinguish it from infectious or neoplastic processes, and (3) satisfy guideline‑based malignancy screening, all of which were pivotal in establishing the diagnosis of anti‑SAE1‑positive dermatomyositis in this patient.
