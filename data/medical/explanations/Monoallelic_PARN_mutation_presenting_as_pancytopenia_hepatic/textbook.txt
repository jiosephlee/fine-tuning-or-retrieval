# Telomere Biology Disorders Presenting as Pancytopenia with Multiorgan Fibrosis: The PARN Paradigm

## Case Vignette

A woman in her late 20s presented with a 5-year history of progressive fatigue and declining exercise tolerance, culminating in impaired self-care. She noted exertional dyspnea, intermittent chest discomfort, weight loss, anorexia, and long-standing premature graying. Examination showed pallor, icterus, mucosal atrophy (bald tongue), knuckle hyperpigmentation, thoracic scoliosis, bilateral thenar atrophy, hepatosplenomegaly, and reduced basal breath sounds.

Laboratory evaluation revealed pancytopenia with a low reticulocyte count and peripheral smear features of ineffective erythropoiesis, including dimorphic red cells, basophilic stippling, Cabot rings, and hypersegmented neutrophils. Severe vitamin B12 and folate deficiency were confirmed; iron indices were elevated, consistent with ineffective erythropoiesis. Bone marrow showed trilineage hematopoiesis with erythroid hyperplasia and increased iron stores without dysplasia or malignancy; cytogenetics were normal. Lactate dehydrogenase and indirect bilirubin were elevated; a direct antiglobulin test was positive (IgG 2+), but reticulocytopenia and marrow findings argued against immune hemolysis as the dominant process. Duodenal biopsy suggested chronic gastritis with reduced parietal cells. Abdominal ultrasound and elastography demonstrated hepatosplenomegaly, steatosis, and cirrhosis. Autoimmune serologies were negative; infectious testing was unremarkable aside from EBV IgG positivity. PET-CT revealed diffuse marrow uptake without malignancy and bilateral ground-glass and fibrotic subpleural changes in the lungs. Additional findings included hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, vitamin D deficiency, secondary hyperparathyroidism, osteoporosis, and asymmetric sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy.

Given persistent multisystem involvement disproportionate to nutritional deficiency alone—pancytopenia with low reticulocytes, premature graying, hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis, and osteopenia—a telomere biology disorder (TBD) was suspected. Clinical exome sequencing identified a heterozygous missense PARN variant (c.613T>C; p.Cys205Arg) affecting a conserved residue in the R3H domain and predicted to be deleterious by multiple in silico tools. Although classified as a Variant of Uncertain Significance, the gene–phenotype concordance supported a diagnosis on the telomere spectrum. The patient improved symptomatically with vitamin repletion and supportive care and is under surveillance for progression of pulmonary, hepatic, and hematologic disease.

## Clinical Approach: From Cytopenia and Premature Aging to a Unifying Diagnosis

The combination of cytopenias, premature graying, mucosal atrophy, hepatosplenomegaly, neuropathy, and early-onset hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis in a young adult is a red flag for an inherited disorder of cellular maintenance, particularly those affecting highly proliferative tissues. While severe vitamin B12 deficiency accounts for macrocytosis, ineffective erythropoiesis, and neuropathy, several features—multiorgan fibrosis, persistent cytopenias with low reticulocyte response, and stigmata of accelerated aging—suggest an additional mechanism of impaired cellular replicative capacity.

In practice, such a presentation should trigger a dual-track evaluation: (1) address correctable contributors (nutrient deficiency, occult infection, toxins, autoimmune disease) and (2) investigate for inherited marrow failure and telomere biology disorders when organ involvement is disproportionate or unexplained, especially in the absence of alternative causes of cirrhosis or interstitial lung disease. A positive DAT in this setting must be interpreted cautiously; autoimmune hemolysis is typically hyperproliferative with reticulocytosis and splenomegaly, whereas this patient’s reticulocytopenia and marrow hyperplasia point to ineffective hematopoiesis rather than immune destruction.

Key clinical cues to a TBD include:
- Early-onset cryptogenic interstitial lung disease (often fibrotic), cryptogenic cirrhosis or portal hypertension, and/or bone marrow failure in a single patient or family.
- Features of premature aging (premature graying, osteopenia, nail changes, mucocutaneous features of dyskeratosis congenita) that may be incomplete or absent.
- Cytopenias that appear “out of proportion” to nutritional, autoimmune, or toxic causes, or that persist after correction of such causes.

## Pathophysiology: Telomere Homeostasis, PARN Function, and Organ Failure

Telomeres are tandem TTAGGG repeats and shelterin-associated proteins that cap chromosome ends, preventing end-to-end fusion and DNA damage responses. With each somatic division, telomeres shorten. Telomerase—a specialized reverse transcriptase (TERT) using an intrinsic RNA template (TERC)—counters this erosion in stem/progenitor compartments. When telomere maintenance is impaired, critically short telomeres trigger p53-dependent senescence or apoptosis, depleting tissue progenitors in organs with high turnover (bone marrow, skin, mucosa, hair follicles, hepatobiliary epithelium) and in compartments with recurrent injury and repair (alveolar epithelium).

Telomere biology disorders comprise pathogenic variants in telomerase components (TERT, TERC), telomere processing and replication genes (DKC1, NAF1, NOP10, NHP2, RTEL1, PARN, DCLRE1B), and shelterin or replication-coupled factors (TINF2, ACD, POT1, CTC1, STN1). Inheritance may be autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, or X-linked, with incomplete penetrance and age-dependent expressivity. Telomere length can also “anticipate” across generations as inherited short telomeres compound with each meiosis, even when the transmitted allele varies.

PARN biology. Poly(A)-specific ribonuclease (PARN) is a 3′→5′ exoribonuclease that trims poly(A) tails and regulates RNA stability and processing. A critical function relevant to telomere maintenance is the 3′-end maturation of TERC: PARN removes aberrant post-transcriptional oligo(A) tails that otherwise target TERC for decay, thereby sustaining TERC stability and telomerase activity. Haploinsufficiency of PARN reduces TERC levels and telomerase function, causing accelerated telomere attrition. Biallelic PARN loss leads to severe childhood-onset TBD phenotypes; heterozygous variants are enriched among adults with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and, less commonly, bone marrow failure or hepatic disease. PARN is among genes least tolerant to disruptive variation, consistent with dosage sensitivity.

Tissue-specific disease mechanisms. In the lung, repetitive microinjury to type II alveolar epithelial cells demands proliferative repair. Telomere-mediated senescence diminishes epithelial resilience, shifts signaling toward profibrotic pathways (e.g., TGF-β, integrin activation), and promotes fibroblast persistence and matrix deposition, yielding a usual interstitial pneumonia phenotype. In the liver, chronic turnover of hepatocytes and cholangiocytes under metabolic or inflammatory stress—compounded by telomere-mediated replicative limits—promotes fibrosis, nodular regenerative hyperplasia, portal hypertension, and, in some, cirrhosis. In bone marrow, attrition of hematopoietic stem and progenitor pools leads to hypocellularity, cytopenias, and heightened risk of clonal evolution to myelodysplasia or acute leukemia. Mucocutaneous tissues exhibit classic features of dyskeratosis congenita but may be subtle or absent in adult-onset telomeropathies. Environmental factors (smoking, alcohol, viral hepatitis, occupational exposures) and concomitant stressors (severe nutrient deficiency) can unmask or accelerate disease in genetically predisposed individuals.

The case vignette likely reflects two intersecting processes: (1) severe megaloblastic hematopoiesis from vitamin B12/folate deficiency, explaining dimorphic indices, marrow erythroid hyperplasia, hypersegmented neutrophils, elevated LDH, and neurologic manifestations; and (2) an underlying telomere defect from heterozygous PARN variation, accounting for early-onset hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis and possibly lowering marrow regenerative reserve, thereby magnifying the hematologic impact of deficiency and increasing the risk for future marrow failure.

## Building the Differential Diagnosis

In a young adult with pancytopenia, weight loss, premature graying, and organomegaly, the major diagnostic branches include:

- Nutritional and toxin-mediated marrow suppression
  - Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency (macrocytosis, ineffective erythropoiesis, neurologic deficits)
  - Copper deficiency or zinc excess (neuro-hematologic syndrome)
  - Alcohol, medications (e.g., linezolid, chemotherapy), benzene or other toxins
- Primary bone marrow failure and clonal disorders
  - Aplastic anemia, hypocellular myelodysplastic syndromes
  - Inherited bone marrow failure syndromes (Fanconi anemia, GATA2 deficiency, TBDs)
- Immune cytopenias and systemic disease
  - Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, immune thrombocytopenia, Evans syndrome
  - Systemic lupus erythematosus or other connective tissue diseases
  - Hypersplenism of any cause
- Infections and infiltrative processes
  - HIV, hepatitis viruses, parvovirus B19, EBV, tuberculosis, leishmaniasis
  - Lymphoma, leukemia, histiocytosis
- Hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis etiologies
  - Viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease, cholestatic disorders
  - Occupational/environmental interstitial lung disease; autoimmune interstitial pneumonia; sarcoidosis; drug-induced lung fibrosis
  - Telomere-mediated IPF or combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema in those with risk alleles or pathogenic variants (TERT, TERC, RTEL1, PARN, POT1, others)

This patient’s macrocytosis with low reticulocytes, marrow erythroid hyperplasia, and confirmatory B12 deficiency support megaloblastic anemia as a proximate cause of cytopenias; the positive DAT, absence of reticulocytosis, and marrow morphology argue against immune hemolysis as the primary driver. Exclusion of autoimmune hepatitis and common hepatic infections, together with young age and coexistent pulmonary fibrosis, favors a telomeropathy over acquired liver and lung disease. The absence of malignant infiltration and normal marrow cytogenetics decrease the likelihood of clonal myeloid diseases at presentation.

## Diagnostic Workup: From Suspicion to Confirmation

A structured approach to suspected TBD integrates clinical, laboratory, imaging, and genetic data and proceeds in parallel with correction of reversible factors.

- Baseline hematology
  - Complete blood count with indices and reticulocytes; peripheral smear review
  - Hemolysis markers (LDH, indirect bilirubin, haptoglobin); direct antiglobulin test if hemolysis suspected
  - Iron studies (serum iron, ferritin, transferrin saturation), vitamin B12, folate, copper/ceruloplasmin if indicated
  - Bone marrow aspirate/biopsy with cytogenetics when cytopenias persist or marrow failure is suspected
- Organ assessment
  - Liver chemistries, autoimmune serologies, viral hepatitis panel; elastography and ultrasound; consider magnetic resonance elastography or biopsy selectively
  - High-resolution chest CT and pulmonary function testing (spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO) to phenotype interstitial lung disease
  - DEXA for bone mineral density; electrolytes, vitamin D, PTH
  - Neuromuscular evaluation for neuropathy where indicated
- Telomere testing
  - Leukocyte telomere length by flow cytometry with fluorescence in situ hybridization (flow-FISH) is the preferred clinical assay; lengths below the first age-adjusted percentile robustly support a TBD. Southern blot and qPCR-based methods are alternatives but less standardized clinically.
  - Interpretation must be age-adjusted; some pathogenic variants yield lengths within the low-normal range in selected cell subsets, particularly in adults, necessitating integration with genotype and phenotype.
- Genetic analysis
  - Next-generation sequencing panels targeting telomere-related genes or clinical exome/genome sequencing. Variant interpretation follows ACMG/AMP criteria and leverages domain knowledge (e.g., PARN R3H domain), population frequency, computational predictions, segregation, and functional evidence where available.
  - A Variant of Uncertain Significance does not exclude disease; correlation with markedly short telomeres, concordant family history, and classic organ involvement may establish a clinical diagnosis on the telomere spectrum.
- Family evaluation and counseling
  - Three-generation pedigree focused on cytopenias, pulmonary fibrosis, early cirrhosis, premature graying, malignancy, and early deaths.
  - Offer cascade testing and/or telomere length assessment to at-risk relatives when a pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant is identified, or when phenotype is strong despite a VUS.

In the presented case, confirmatory telomere length assessment would strengthen the diagnosis and assist in family counseling. The identified heterozygous PARN p.Cys205Arg variant lies in a critical functional domain and is predicted deleterious, consistent with PARN haploinsufficiency phenotypes, even though definitive pathogenicity awaits further evidence.

## Management Principles and Rationale

Management is individualized to the dominant organ involvement, balances supportive and disease-modifying strategies, and anticipates complications unique to telomere-mediated disease biology.

- Correct reversible contributors
  - Prompt repletion of vitamin B12 and folate to reverse megaloblastosis and prevent progression of neurologic deficits.
  - Address vitamin D deficiency, hypocalcemia, and secondary hyperparathyroidism to improve bone health; institute calcium/vitamin D supplementation and weight-bearing activity; consider antiresorptive therapy for osteoporosis after fracture risk assessment.
  - Optimize nutrition and treat malabsorption (e.g., autoimmune gastritis) to reduce recurrent deficiency.
- Hematologic care
  - Transfuse for symptomatic anemia; monitor iron burden with serial ferritin and consider chelation if transfusions are recurrent.
  - For evolving marrow failure, androgens (e.g., danazol, nandrolone) can improve counts in a subset and have been associated with telomere elongation in early studies; monitor for hepatic toxicity, virilization, lipid derangements, and thrombotic risk.
  - Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor for severe neutropenia with infections can be used judiciously.
  - Immunosuppression for aplastic anemia is less predictably effective in telomeropathies; early referral for transplant evaluation is prudent if counts decline.
  - Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is potentially curative for marrow failure but requires reduced-intensity, non-alkylating, and radiation-sparing regimens to mitigate pulmonary and hepatic toxicity. Donor selection should consider familial telomere status; related donors with short telomeres or pathogenic variants should be avoided.
- Pulmonary fibrosis
  - Antifibrotic therapy (nintedanib or pirfenidone) slows FVC decline in IPF, including patients with short telomeres; assess tolerance and drug interactions.
  - Pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal), smoking cessation, and supplemental oxygen as needed.
  - Early referral to an interstitial lung disease/transplant center; lung transplantation can be life-prolonging in telomeropathies, but cytopenias, renal dysfunction, and wound healing complications are more frequent; multidisciplinary pretransplant evaluation is essential.
- Hepatic disease
  - Standard cirrhosis care: manage portal hypertension (nonselective beta-blockers, variceal screening), treat ascites, prevent encephalopathy, vaccinate for hepatitis A and B.
  - Evaluate for transplant in decompensated disease; telomere biology disorders require careful assessment of extrahepatic organ reserve, and combined organ transplantation may be considered in select cases.
  - Avoid hepatotoxic agents; alcohol abstinence is mandatory.
- Risk mitigation and surveillance
  - Avoid myelotoxic drugs when alternatives exist; use the lowest effective doses of immunosuppression post-transplant.
  - Cancer screening per guidelines with heightened vigilance for head and neck, anogenital, and hematologic malignancies in dyskeratosis congenita-spectrum disease.
  - Schedule surveillance: CBC with differential every 3–6 months; liver enzymes and elastography periodically; annual PFTs and symptom-driven HRCT; bone density every 1–2 years based on risk.
- Genetic counseling and family planning
  - Explain inheritance patterns, variable penetrance, and the concept of inherited short telomeres independent of genotype (“phenocopy” via telomere inheritance).
  - Offer testing to at-risk relatives; discuss reproductive options, including preimplantation or prenatal genetic testing when a pathogenic variant is established.

## Nuances in This Case

- Positive DAT without overt hemolysis. A positive direct antiglobulin test can occur without clinically significant hemolysis, particularly after transfusions or in systemic inflammation. The low reticulocyte count and marrow erythroid hyperplasia support ineffective erythropoiesis as the predominant process here.
- Dimorphic indices with elevated iron parameters. Ineffective erythropoiesis releases iron and elevates ferritin; a dimorphic smear may reflect a mixture of macrocytic megaloblasts and normocytic populations (e.g., post-transfusion or early response to therapy).
- Neuropathy. Severe vitamin B12 deficiency explains the asymmetric sensorimotor axonal neuropathy and thenar wasting; timely repletion is critical to prevent irreversible deficits. Telomere disorders themselves are not classically neuropathic, underscoring the importance of diagnosing coexisting conditions.
- PARN variant interpretation. The p.Cys205Arg change affects the R3H domain important for RNA binding and processing. While currently a VUS, its location, rarity, and deleterious predictions, together with the patient’s phenotype, support clinical attribution within the TBD spectrum. Telomere length testing would further substantiate this inference.

## Prognosis

Natural history varies with genotype, telomere length, and environmental co-stressors. Heterozygous PARN carriers often present in adulthood with pulmonary fibrosis; bone marrow failure and hepatic fibrosis may evolve over time. Early identification permits anticipatory guidance, organ-specific therapies, avoidance of exacerbating exposures, and timely transplantation referral. Survival trajectories are driven by the rate of pulmonary and hepatic progression and by marrow reserve; proactive care can meaningfully alter outcomes.

## Clinical Pearls

- Suspect a telomere biology disorder in any young or middle-aged adult with unexplained interstitial lung disease, cryptogenic cirrhosis or portal hypertension, or persistent cytopenias—especially when two or more organ systems are involved or when there are features of premature aging.
- Telomere length by flow-FISH and targeted next-generation sequencing of telomere-related genes are complementary; a VUS does not exclude disease when the phenotype and telomere length are concordant.
- PARN haploinsufficiency reduces TERC stability and telomerase activity; heterozygous PARN variants most often manifest with pulmonary fibrosis but can include marrow and hepatic involvement.
- In megaloblastic anemia, a positive DAT does not establish immune hemolysis; reticulocyte response and marrow morphology discriminate ineffective erythropoiesis from peripheral destruction.
- Androgen therapy can improve cytopenias and may lengthen telomeres in some patients with TBDs, but careful hepatic monitoring is essential, and long-term efficacy remains under study.
- HSCT can be curative for marrow failure in telomeropathies, but conditioning regimens must minimize alkylators and radiation; avoid related donors with short telomeres or pathogenic variants.
- Antifibrotics slow lung function decline in IPF due to telomeropathies and should be considered alongside transplant evaluation.
- Family counseling and cascade assessment can uncover at-risk relatives who may benefit from early surveillance and risk modification, even when the proband’s variant is initially classified as uncertain.

## Take-Home Points

- Telomere biology disorders are multisystem conditions of impaired tissue renewal; presentations in adults may lack classical mucocutaneous stigmata and mimic common acquired diseases.
- The concurrence of pancytopenia with low reticulocytes, premature graying, hepatic fibrosis, and interstitial lung disease in a young adult should prompt evaluation for a telomeropathy.
- PARN plays a critical role in TERC maturation; monoallelic variants can produce a phenotype of pulmonary fibrosis with variable marrow and hepatic involvement via telomere shortening.
- Management centers on treating reversible contributors, organ-specific therapies, vigilant surveillance, and timely consideration of transplantation with regimens tailored to telomere fragility.
