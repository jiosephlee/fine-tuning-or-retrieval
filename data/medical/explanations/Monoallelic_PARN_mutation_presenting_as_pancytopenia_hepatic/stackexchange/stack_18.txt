Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

### [Clinical Pearls] Which medications and exposures should be avoided or used with caution in patients with telomere biology disorders due to increased risk of myelotoxicity or organ toxicity?

Teachable pearl: patients with telomere biology disorders have limited hematopoietic and organ reserve because short/dysfunctional telomeres impair stem‑cell replication and DNA repair. That biologic vulnerability makes them unusually sensitive to agents or exposures that cause DNA damage, replicative stress, or direct organ toxicity — so several common drugs and environmental insults should be avoided or used only with extreme caution and specialist input.

Which agents/exposures to avoid or use with caution, and why
- Alkylating chemotherapy and high‑dose DNA‑damaging chemotherapy (eg, busulfan, cyclophosphamide, melphalan, chlorambucil): these agents produce profound and often irreversible marrow injury in TBDs. If cytotoxic therapy is unavoidable, consult hematology/genetics and use reduced‑intensity or non‑myeloablative regimens with dose reductions and close monitoring.
- Ionizing radiation and myeloablative radiotherapy: radiation causes DNA breaks and accelerates marrow failure and organ toxicity; avoid therapeutic radiation when possible and minimize exposures.
- Antimetabolites and classic marrow‑suppressing agents (eg, methotrexate, azathioprine, fludarabine, cytarabine): increased risk of prolonged cytopenias and delayed marrow recovery.
- Drugs with known profound idiosyncratic marrow toxicity (eg, chloramphenicol, certain sulfonamides in susceptible patients, prolonged or high‑dose trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole): these can precipitate severe cytopenias; use alternatives or careful monitoring.
- Amiodarone and other pulmonary‑toxic drugs (eg, bleomycin, nitrofurantoin long term): TBD patients already have predisposition to pulmonary fibrosis; avoid drugs that cause or worsen interstitial lung disease.
- Hepatotoxic drugs and alcohol: many TBDs have hepatic fibrosis/cirrhosis at baseline; avoid or dose‑adjust hepatotoxic agents (eg, methotrexate, high‑dose anabolic steroids, certain antifungals, high‑dose acetaminophen) and counsel strict alcohol avoidance.
- Androgens and anabolic steroids: androgens (danazol, nandrolone) can increase telomerase activity and have been used therapeutically, but they carry risks of hepatotoxicity and may worsen liver disease; use only after specialist review and with hepatic monitoring.
- Smoking, occupational toxin exposures (eg, benzene), and other environmental genotoxins: these accelerate telomere attrition and promote marrow and pulmonary injury; strong counselling to avoid.
- Consider vaccine and infection precautions: severe cytopenias or planned immunosuppression may alter timing/type of vaccines; coordinate with specialists.

Clinical approach
Before prescribing any potentially myelotoxic, hepatotoxic, or pulmonary‑toxic therapy, obtain hematology/genetics input. Use lowest effective doses, prefer non‑DNA‑damaging alternatives when possible, institute vigilant laboratory and organ monitoring, and when transplant or chemotherapy is considered choose reduced‑intensity conditioning tailored to TBDs. Counsel patients to avoid alcohol, smoking and occupational/genotoxic exposures and to report new cytopenias, respiratory or hepatic symptoms promptly.

Rationale in one line: defective telomere maintenance means reduced stem‑cell reserve and impaired DNA‑damage responses — agents that damage DNA or target organs already affected by fibrosis result in disproportionate and often irreversible toxicity.
