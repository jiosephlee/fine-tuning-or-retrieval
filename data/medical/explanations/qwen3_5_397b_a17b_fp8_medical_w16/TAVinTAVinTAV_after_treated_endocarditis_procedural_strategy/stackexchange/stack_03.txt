Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why was the Acurate neo2 valve selected over other available transcatheter heart valves for this third implantation, considering the patient's triple-stented anatomy and the need for future coronary access?

The selection of the Acurate neo2 valve for this third transcatheter implantation was driven by its unique architectural features, which specifically addressed the compounded risks of coronary obstruction and prosthesis-patient mismatch inherent in a triple-stented anatomy. Unlike earlier-generation devices or those with taller frames, the Acurate neo2 offers a relatively low profile and supra-annular leaflet positioning. In this case, these characteristics were critical to facilitating a "high implantation" strategy. By deploying the valve at the level of the existing Evolut leaflets, the team could pin the restricted leaflets of the failing prosthesis against the stent frame without creating a dense "neo-skirt" of material below the coronary ostia. This maneuver preserved the sinus space and maintained vital coronary clearance, a paramount concern given the patient's extensive history of coronary artery disease and prior interventions.

Furthermore, the Acurate neo2's design supports precise commissural alignment, which was meticulously planned using preprocedural multislice CT. Aligning the new commissures with those of the underlying valves minimizes the cumulative metal burden overlapping the coronary openings, thereby safeguarding future coronary access—a necessity for a patient with three-vessel disease. Finally, the supra-annular flow dynamics of the Acurate neo2 helped maximize the effective orifice area within the constrained inner diameter of the two previous valves (Lotus and Evolut), mitigating the risk of severe patient-prosthesis mismatch that often plagues valve-in-valve-in-valve procedures. Thus, the device was chosen not merely as another option, but as the only one capable of satisfying the triad of requirements: safe high deployment, preserved coronary accessibility, and optimized hemodynamics in an extremely narrowed landing zone.
