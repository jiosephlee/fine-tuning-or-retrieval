Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] Immediately post-procedure, this patient exhibited a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM), yet follow-up at one month showed significant hemodynamic improvement. What mechanistic explanation accounts for this delayed reduction in transvalvular gradient?

The delayed reduction in the transvalvular gradient observed in this patient—from a severe mean gradient of 67 mm Hg immediately post-procedure to 27 mm Hg at one month—is best explained by the time-dependent viscoelastic expansion of the underlying constrained valve frames and the resolution of acute procedural edema.

In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the newly implanted prosthesis (Acurate neo2) must expand within the rigid confines of two previous stent frames (Lotus and Evolut). Immediately following deployment and even after high-pressure post-dilation, the metallic struts of the innermost constrained valve (the Evolut) may not achieve their maximal theoretical diameter due to elastic recoil and the resistance offered by the outer layers. As described in the case discussion, while intentional frame fracture was not performed, the high-pressure balloon post-dilation likely initiated a gradual remodeling process. Over the ensuing weeks, continued radial force from the self-expanding new valve, combined with the viscoelastic properties of the older stent materials and surrounding tissue, allows for further slow expansion of the nested frames. This incremental increase in the effective orifice area significantly reduces turbulence and pressure gradients over time.

Furthermore, immediate post-procedural gradients are often artificially elevated by transient factors such as local tissue edema at the implantation site and hyperdynamic states induced by procedural stress or anesthesia. As these acute inflammatory changes resolve and the patient's hemodynamics stabilize during recovery, the true steady-state gradient emerges. The case report explicitly attributes this phenomenon to "partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame" alongside optimized leaflet motion, confirming that the initial severe prosthesis-patient mismatch was a transient mechanical constraint rather than a permanent failure of valve sizing. This underscores a critical teaching point in complex valve-in-valve interventions: immediate post-deployment hemodynamics may not predict final functional outcomes, and patience with serial imaging is essential before declaring therapeutic failure.
