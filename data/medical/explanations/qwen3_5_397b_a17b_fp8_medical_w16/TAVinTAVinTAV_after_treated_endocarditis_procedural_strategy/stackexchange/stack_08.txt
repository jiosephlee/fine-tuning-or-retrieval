Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] If this patient were to experience failure of the third prosthesis, why would surgical explantation be considered prohibitively high-risk compared to the initial surgeries, and what emerging catheter-based salvage therapies might theoretically be considered despite their uncertainty in triple-stented configurations?

Surgical explantation in this scenario would be considered prohibitively high-risk due to the cumulative anatomical distortion created by three overlapping transcatheter valve frames deeply integrated into the aortic root. As highlighted in the case discussion, the patient's prior valves have undergone extensive tissue ingrowth and fixation over a decade, effectively fusing the prostheses to the native aortic wall and each other. Attempting surgical removal would not only carry an extreme risk of catastrophic aortic root disruption but would likely necessitate a total root and ascending aorta replacement—a procedure compounded by the patient's prohibitive operative risk profile (EuroSCORE II >27%), significant comorbidities (COPD, diabetes), and hostile mediastinum from prior interventions.

Consequently, if the third prosthesis were to fail, management would almost certainly require a percutaneous approach. Emerging catheter-based salvage therapies that might be theoretically considered include **bioprosthetic valve fracture (BVF)** using high-pressure non-compliant balloons to expand the innermost rigid frame and improve hemodynamics, or **leaflet laceration techniques** such as BASILICA (Bioprosthetic or Native Aortic Scallop Intentional Laceration to Prevent Iatrogenic Coronary Artery Obstruction) to mitigate coronary obstruction risks during a fourth valve-in-valve procedure. However, as the authors note, the efficacy and safety of these techniques remain uncertain in a "triple-stented" configuration, where the cumulative metal burden of three distinct stent frames may resist fracture or create unpredictable tear patterns, making pre-procedural CT modeling even more critical than in previous iterations.
