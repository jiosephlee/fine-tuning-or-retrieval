Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] According to current ESC guidelines, what is the preferred management strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) complicated by heart failure, and what specific factors in this case justified deviating from that standard to pursue a third transcatheter intervention?

According to current European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines, the preferred management strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) complicated by heart failure is early surgical valve replacement. Surgery is mandated in this setting to achieve source control through radical debridement of infected tissue, remove large vegetations, and reconstruct the annulus if abscess formation is present, as medical therapy alone carries a prohibitively high mortality rate in patients with hemodynamic compromise.

However, in this specific case, the clinical team justifiably deviated from the surgical standard to pursue a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) based on three critical factors grounded in the patient's unique trajectory. First, and most fundamentally, the indication for intervention shifted from *active* infection to *structural* valve deterioration following successful eradication of the pathogen. The patient had completed a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy for *Enterococcus faecalis*, and rigorous reassessment—including repeated negative blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT—confirmed the complete resolution of endocarditis with no evidence of active vegetation, abscess, or paravalvular leak. Consequently, the procedural goal was no longer infectious source control but rather the relief of severe mechanical obstruction caused by leaflet fibrosis and restenosis.

Second, the patient presented with prohibitive surgical risk. With a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, significant comorbidities including COPD, diabetes, and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, plus a complex "triple-stented" anatomy where previous valve frames were deeply integrated into the aortic wall, open surgery would have necessitated a hazardous total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement. Third, advanced imaging confirmed anatomical feasibility for a catheter-based approach; preprocedural CT modeling demonstrated that a third valve could be implanted with adequate coronary clearance and commissural alignment to mitigate prosthesis-patient mismatch. Thus, in the absence of active infection and given the extreme operative risk, a tailored transcatheter strategy offered the only viable path to restore hemodynamics and alleviate heart failure.
