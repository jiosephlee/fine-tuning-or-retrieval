Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 4: ## Procedural Execution: Technical Nuances of Triple-Valve Implantation

The execution of a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) demands a departure from standard protocols, requiring precise adaptation to the constraints imposed by two pre-existing stent frames. In this case, the selection of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis was driven by its unique architectural advantages suited for a "triple-stented" anatomy. Unlike self-expanding valves with tall frames that could exacerbate the cumulative height of the construct, the Acurate neo2 features a relatively low profile and supra-annular leaflet positioning. This design is critical in minimizing the vertical footprint of the new device while maximizing the effective orifice area (EOA) within the narrowed lumen defined by the previous Lotus and Evolut valves. The choice of a size Small device reflected the need to fit within the 13.7 mm minimum lumen diameter identified on preprocedural multislice CT (MSCT), balancing the risk of under-expansion against the danger of annular rupture or excessive pressure on the integrated prior frames.

Access and wire manipulation in this scenario presented heightened challenges due to the tortuosity created by multiple overlapping metal struts. The procedure utilized left femoral access for the primary delivery system and left radial access for monitoring, navigating a Safari guidewire into the left ventricle through the constrained central channel of the failing prostheses. Once access was secured, balloon pre-dilation was performed using a 22/40 mm catheter under rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. This step served a dual purpose: it temporarily stabilized cardiac output to prevent embolization during dilation and mechanically expanded the rigid, degenerated leaflets of the underlying Evolut frame. Simultaneous aortography during this phase confirmed that coronary flow remained patent, a vital checkpoint before committing to permanent valve deployment in an anatomy where coronary obstruction carries catastrophic risk.

The core of the procedural strategy lay in the specific implantation depth and commissural alignment. To mitigate the risk of severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) caused by inward folding of the existing bioprosthetic leaflets, the team employed a high implantation technique. By deploying the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets rather than deep within the annulus, the new stent frame effectively pinned the old, restricted leaflets againstthe outer wall of the prior prosthesis. This maneuver prevented them from obstructing central flow and preserved a functional neo-skirt above the coronary ostia. Concurrently, rigorous attention was paid to commissural alignment. Guided by preprocedural CT modeling and fluoroscopic landmarks, the new valve was oriented to align its commissures with those ofthe underlying frames. This alignment is paramount in multi-layer TAVI procedures to maintain clear pathways for future coronary cannulation, preventingthe "mesh effect" where misaligned struts create an impenetrable barrier to catheters.

Final optimization ofthe hemodynamic result relied on controlled post-dilation. Following deployment, a 20/40 mm balloon was inflated under rapid pacing to ensure full expansion ofthe Acurate neo2 withinthe confining rings ofthe prior valves. This step likely facilitated slight radial expansion ofthe intermediate Evolut frame, further improving leaflet mobility and central flow dynamics without inducing frame fracture or embolization. Final aortography confirmedthe success ofthese technical nuances: correct positioning, absenceof paravalvular leak, and crucially, preserved coronary perfusion. While immediate post-procedure echocardiography initially showed elevated gradients consistent with severe PPM—a common phenomenon in triple-valve constructs due to transient edema and incomplete recoil—the strategic execution laidthefoundationforthe significant hemodynamic improvement observed at one-month follow-up, validatingthe approachofhigh implantationandprecise alignmentinmanagingcomplexreinterventions.
