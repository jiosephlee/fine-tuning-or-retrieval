Title: Blog about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

# Don't Lose the Coronaries: Advanced CT Planning for Multi-Layered Valve Interventions

We often talk about "lifetime management" in structural heart disease as a buzzword, but rarely do we see what it actually looks like when the clock runs down on multiple layers of metal. Consider the case of a man in his late 70s who didn't just have a failed TAVI; he had *two* failed TAVIs, separated by a bout of *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis. By 2024, this patient presented with severe stenosis inside a Lotus valve, which was inside an Evolut valve. The solution? A third transcatheter valve—an Acurate neo2—implanted via a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV strategy.

What makes this case terrifyingly fascinating isn't just the sheer number of prostheses stacked in the aortic root; it's that he survived a prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) episode only to require another intervention shortly after. In a world where guidelines still lean heavily toward surgery for PVE, this patient was deemed prohibitively high-risk for open repair due to his comorbidities and the "cemented" nature of his previous valves. The team couldn't cut him open; they had to navigate a tunnel of metal without losing the coronaries or creating a valve so small it would never open. The secret weapon wasn't a new gadget, but rigorous, metric-driven CT planning that turned a potential catastrophe into a one-year success story.

The primary enemy in any redo-TAVI, but especially in a triple-stack scenario, is the "neo-skirt." When you deploy a new valve inside old ones, the leaflets of the innermost valve get pushed outward against the stent struts of the outermost valve. If you aren't careful, these displaced leaflets form a curtain that covers your coronary ostia, leading to instant, fatal obstruction. In this case, the team faced two overlapping frames and infected tissue that had likely altered the geometry of the root. They couldn't rely on standard depths or "feel." They needed hard data from multislice CT (MSCT) to measure the exact distance between the anticipated neo-skirt and the coronary buttons. The scan revealed a minimum lumen diameter of just 13.7 mm within the Evolut frame, confirming severe compromise but also providing the roadmap for survival.

The procedural strategy hinged on two critical concepts derived directly from that CT workup: high implantation and commissural alignment. Instead of seating the new Acurate neo2 deep within the annulus, the operators deliberately implanted it higher, aligning it with the leaflets of the existing Evolut valve. This maneuver pinned the old leaflets against the frame in a way that prevented them from billowing out and obstructing flow, effectively managing the neo-skirt risk without needing bail-out techniques like BASILICA or chimney stenting. Furthermore, by aligning the commissures of the new valve with those of the old ones using fluoroscopic landmarks guided by pre-procedural modeling, they preserved future coronary access—a crucial consideration for a patient with three-vessel CAD who has already undergone multiple PCIs.

The immediate post-procedure echo showed what we often fear in these tight spaces: severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. It looked like a failure. However, thanks to the optimized expansion and alignment strategy, physiology won out over initial numbers. Within one month, remodeling occurred, gradients dropped to 27 mm Hg, and at one year, the patient remains asymptomatic with preserved ejection fraction and no recurrence of infection. This case proves that even in the most hostile anatomical environments—triple-stented roots post-endocarditis—precision imaging can carve out a path where surgery is impossible and standard TAVI would be fatal.

## Key Take-Home Points

*   **CT is Non-Negotiable in Redo-TAVI:** You cannot fly blind in multi-layered valves. Multislice CT must be used to calculate valve-to-coronary distance, analyze sinus dimensions, and model frame interactions to predict neo-skirt formation before touching the patient.
*   **High Implantation Strategy:** In TAV-in-TAV scenarios, deploying the new valve at a higher level (aligning with existing leaflets) can pin old leaflets against stent struts, reducing effective orifice area loss and preventing coronary obstruction compared to deep anchoring.
*   **Commissural Alignment Preserves Future Access:** With every added layer of metal, coronary re-access becomes exponentially harder Deliberate commissural alignment during deployment is essential to maintain windows for future PCI or bailout procedures.
*   **Don't Panic Over Immediate Gradients:** Severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) on immediate post-deployment echo may improve significantly over weeks due to leaflet conditioning and LV remodeling; avoid premature judgment of hemodynamic failure if anatomy was optimized.
*   **PVE Doesn't Always Mean Surgery:** In patients with treated endocarditis who are prohibitive surgical candidates due to complex anatomy (multiple prior valves) and comorbidities, TAVI can be safely performed once infection is confirmed eradicated via TEE and clinical markers.
