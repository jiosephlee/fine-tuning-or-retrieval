Title: Blog about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

# Beyond the Echo: Why MSCT is Non-Negotiable in Complex Valve Reinterventions

We love a good echo. It's portable, dynamic, and gives us real-time hemodynamics that make our brains light up. But if you think transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is going to save you in a "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV" scenario, you are bringing a knife to a nuclear fight.

Consider the case of a man in his late 70s who recently walked out of the cath lab after receiving his *third* transcatheter aortic valve. Yes, you read that right: three valves stacked inside one another. His journey started back in 2014 with a Lotus valve, followed by a TAV-in-TAV with an Evolut in 2022, which was then complicated by *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis. After successfully treating the infection, he developed severe restenosis again. The solution? A third valve—an Acurate neo2—implanted inside the previous two.

This isn't just a trivia question for your next board exam; it's a glimpse into the future of structural heart disease. As our TAVI patients live longer and indications expand, we are going to see more "Russian nesting doll" hearts. The takeaway from this incredible case isn't just that it's possible; it's that it would have been impossible without multislice computed tomography (MSCT) acting as the primary roadmap. In complex reinterventions, echo tells you *that* there is a problem, but only CT tells you *how* to fix it without killing the patient.

When you are dealing with three layers of metal mesh, anatomy becomes a guessing game unless you have high-resolution 3D reconstruction. In this case, the team didn't just look at the valve; they measured the minimum lumen diameter through the existing Evolut stent, finding it constricted to 13.7 mm. That number dictated everything. If they had guessed wrong on the sizing or the implantation depth, they risked catastrophic prosthesis-patient mismatch or immediate coronary obstruction. The CT allowed them to visualize the interaction between the old frames and plan a "high implantation" strategy for the new Acurate neo2. By deploying the new valve specifically at the level of the Evolut leaflets, they pinned the old, restricted leaflets against the stent frame, effectively creating a larger effective orifice area and preventing further narrowing.

Perhaps even more critical was the management of coronary risk. In a triple-stented root, the cumulative thickness of the struts creates a formidable barrier to future coronary access and increases the risk of occlusion during deployment. Standard angiography couldn't predict whether pushing a third valve up would create a "neo-skirt" of tissue and metal that would seal off the coronary ostia forever. The pre-procedural CT modeling allowed the team to calculate virtual valve-to-coronary distances and analyze sinus dimensions with precision. They confirmed that despite the layers of metal, there was enough clearance to proceed without prophylactic coronary protection wires—a bold move that paid off because they knew exactly where every millimeter of steel was sitting relative to the left main and RCA.

The result speaks for itself. Immediately post-procedure, there was some expected gradient due to the tight fit (severe prosthesis-patient mismatch initially), but by one month, remodeling occurred, gradients dropped significantly, and at one year, the patient is asymptomatic with preserved ejection fraction and no regurgitation. This wasn't luck; it was engineering guided by imaging. The case proves that while echo is excellent for screening and follow-up, MSCT is non-negotiable for procedural planning in redo TAVI. You cannot navigate a minefield blindfolded, and in these complex anatomies, anything less than a full CT workup is malpractice waiting to happen.

### Key Take-Home Messages

*   **MSCT is Mandatory for Multi-Layer Redos:** In TAV-in-TAV or TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenarios, echocardiography is insufficient for procedural planning; high-resolution CT is required to assess frame interaction, minimum lumen diameter, and true anatomical constraints.
*   **Strategic Implantation Depth Matters:** Precise CT modeling allows operators to choose specific implantation heights (e.g., high implantation) to pin restricted leaflets against existing frames, maximizing effective orifice area and mitigating prosthesis-patient mismatch.
*   **Coronary Access Must Be Modeled Virtually:** Cumulative metal struts increase obstruction risk; CT-derived measurements of valve-to-coronary distance and neo-skirt formation are essential to determine if coronary protection is needed and to preserve future access.
*   **Infection History Doesn't Preclude Redo TAVI:** In patients with treated prosthetic valve endocarditis who are prohibitive surgical candidates, repeat TAVI is feasible provided active infection is ruled out via TEE/CT and inflammatory markers prior to intervention.
*   **Expect Initial Gradients to Improve:** Severe prosthesis-patient mismatch may be present immediately post-deployment in tight anatomies but often resolves with LV remodeling and optimized leaflet motion over weeks to months.
