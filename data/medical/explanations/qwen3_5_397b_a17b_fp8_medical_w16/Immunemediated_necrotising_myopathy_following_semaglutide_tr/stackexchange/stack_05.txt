Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] Unlike idiopathic IMNM which often requires prolonged maintenance immunosuppression, why did this patient with suspected semaglutide-associated IMNM achieve sustained remission after a relatively short course of corticosteroids and a single cycle of IVIG?

This patient's ability to achieve sustained remission with a short course of immunosuppression, unlike patients with idiopathic immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM), stems from the fundamental difference in disease pathogenesis: the removal of the inciting antigen versus the persistence of a self-sustaining autoimmune loop.

In classic idiopathic IMNM, particularly forms associated with anti-HMGCR or anti-SRP autoantibodies, the immune system generates high-titer, pathogenic autoantibodies that drive continuous muscle fiber necrosis independently of the original trigger (such as statin exposure, which may have ceased years prior). Consequently, these patients typically require prolonged, often multi-agent immunosuppressive therapy to suppress this entrenched humoral response, and relapse is common upon tapering.

In contrast, this case represents a probable drug-induced IMNM triggered by semaglutide. As noted in the discussion, the patient's clinical course—characterized by a 12-fold rise in creatine kinase (CK) over four months following a single dose, rather than immediate resolution as seen in toxic rhabdomyolysis—suggests an immune-mediated mechanism rather than direct toxicity. However, unlike idiopathic disease, the driving force here appears contingent on the presence of the offending agent or the acute inflammatory cascade it initiated. Once semaglutide was discontinued and the acute immune activation was bridged with a finite course of corticosteroids and a single cycle of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), the stimulus for myonecrosis was removed. The patient's muscle biopsy confirmed necrotizing features with macrophage predominance (CD68+) and membrane attack complex deposition but lacked the specific autoantibodies (anti-HMGCR/SRP) that typify refractory idiopathic cases.

Therefore, the successful rapid weaning off prednisone and the absence of further IVIG requirements reflect the natural history of drug-induced autoimmune phenomena: when the trigger is identified and withdrawn early, the immune dysregulation resolves without evolving into a chronic, self-perpetuating autoimmune state. This distinction underscores the critical importance of identifying potential pharmacologic triggers in IMNM, as their removal can fundamentally alter the prognosis and reduce the burden of long-term immunosuppression.
