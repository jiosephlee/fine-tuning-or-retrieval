Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] If conservative management with bowel rest and somatostatin analogues fails after 2–3 weeks, what is the next preferred line of therapy for closing the fistula, and what is its reported success rate in pediatric populations compared to surgical intervention?

If conservative management comprising bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, and somatostatin analogues (such as octreotide) fails to resolve a pancreatopleural fistula after 2 to 3 weeks, the preferred next line of therapy is **endoscopic intervention via Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with transpapillary stenting**.

The clinical rationale for this escalation rests on the pathophysiology of the fistula as a "leaky pipe" driven by high intraductal pressure. While conservative measures aim to reduce pancreatic secretion volume, they do not address underlying ductal disruptions or strictures that perpetuate the leak. ERCP allows for direct visualization of the pancreatic ductal anatomy and therapeutic decompression. By placing a stent across the site of disruption or dilating a stricture, the procedure diverts pancreatic flow away from the fistulous tract and into the duodenum, thereby reducing intraductal pressure and facilitating spontaneous closure of the fistula.

In pediatric populations, this endoscopic approach demonstrates superior efficacy compared to historical surgical outcomes when initial medical management fails. According to the data presented in this case report, ERCP has achieved a **100% success rate** in reported pediatric cases, with long-term success exceeding 90% in mixed-age cohorts. In contrast, while surgical interventions (such as distal pancreatectomy or drainage procedures) are highly effective with a reported **93% success rate**, they carry inherent risks of operative mortality (<5%) and higher morbidity. Furthermore, conservative management alone is noted to have a very low success rate of only **12%** in children once the window for spontaneous closure has passed. Therefore, given its minimally invasive nature and exceptional success profile in the available pediatric literature, ERCP serves as the critical bridge between failed medical therapy and definitive surgical rescue.
