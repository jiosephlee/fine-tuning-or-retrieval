Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 2: ## Diagnostic Imaging Strategies and Interpretation of Biomarkers

The evaluation of complex abdominal masses during pregnancy requires a strategic, stepwise diagnostic approach that balances diagnostic accuracy with fetal safety. In this case, the initial assessment relied heavily on ultrasonography (US), which served as the primary screening modality to characterize the two distinct lesions. The ultrasound revealed large, unilocular cystic masses with regular margins and peripheral vascularization. A critical challenge in this scenario was distinguishing between decidualized endometriomas and cystic degenerating leiomyomas, as both can present with similar sonographic features during pregnancy. Decidualization, a physiological transformation of endometrial stroma induced by high progesterone levels, can cause endometriomas to appear solid or mixed, mimicking malignancy or other neoplasms. Similarly, leiomyomas undergoing red or cystic degeneration lose their classic homogeneous hypoechoic appearance, becoming heterogeneous and cystic. In this patient, the upper mass lacked a visible connective stalk due to the "steric bulk" of the gravid uterus, complicating the determination of its origin, while the caudal mass appeared adherent to the posterior uterine wall, suggesting a myomatous origin. Despite these detailed findings, the sheer size of the masses and their overlapping characteristics with the uterus rendered ultrasound insufficient for a definitive preoperative diagnosis.

When ultrasound findings are equivocal or when precise anatomical mapping is required for surgical planning, non-contrast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) becomes the gold standard adjunctive tool. Unlike CT scans, MRI avoids ionizing radiation, making it safe for fetal evaluation provided specific protocols are followed. Crucially, gadolinium-based contrast agents are generally contraindicated during pregnancy due to potential risks of stillbirth, neonatal death, and rheumatological conditions in the offspring; therefore, diagnostic reliance must rest entirely on native tissue signal characteristics. In this case, the non-contrast MRI utilized T1 and T2 fat-saturated sequences to differentiate tissue composition. The imaging demonstrated thick-walled masses with inhomogeneous fluid content and focal hyperintense solid components on both T1 and T2 weighting. These signal intensities helped characterize the internal architecture, confirming the presence of mixed solid-cystic elements consistent with degenerating leiomyomas rather than simple cysts. Furthermore, MRI provided superior spatial resolution regarding the relationship between the caudal mass and the uterus, revealing continuity with a posterior intramural-subserosal myoma—a detail obscured on ultrasound. This clarification was pivotal in shifting the differential diagnosis away from primary ovarian malignancy toward complicated uterine leiomyomas.

Parallel to imaging interpretation, the analysis of serum tumor markers requires extreme caution due to significant physiological alterations during gestation. CA-125, commonly used as a marker for epithelial ovarian cancer in non-pregnant populations with a standard cutoff of 35 U/mL, exhibits dynamic fluctuations throughout pregnancy. Levels typically peak in the first trimester (median ~23 U/mL) and again in the third trimester, while reaching a nadir in the second trimester (median ~14 U/mL). Consequently, an absolute value of 41.8 U/mL in a second-trimester patient represents a notable deviation from the expected physiological baseline, yet it remains within a range frequently observed in benign conditions. Elevated CA-125 in pregnancy is not specific to malignancy; it can be driven by peritoneal irritation from decidualized endometriosis, adenomyosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, or even large leiomyomas undergoing degeneration or torsion. In this case report, the slight elevation and increasing trend of CA-125 initially favored a diagnosis of endometrioma or raised concern for oncological potential. However., when viewed in isolation, this biomarker lacked specificity. Its utility lay not in confirming cancer but in contributing to a cumulative risk profile when combined with symptomatic worsening and ambiguous imaging.

Synthesizing multimodal data is essential to navigate these diagnostic ambiguities and guide management decisions. The clinical picture here involved massive abdominal distension causing restrictive lung disease and acute pain alongside imaging suggestive of either giant decidualized endometriomas or degenerating pedunculated leiomyomas. While the CA-125 level was mildly elevated, it did not reach thresholds typically associated with advanced ovarian carcinoma, nor were other markers like CA 19-9 or CEA abnormal. The MRI findings of continuity between the pelvic mass and the uterine wall strongly argued against an independent ovarian primary for at least one lesion. Ultimately, the convergence of imaging morphology (cystic degeneration within a uterine context), biomarker trends (mild elevation explainable by benign degeneration), and clinical severity (pain and dyspnea indicative of torsion or necrosis) pointed toward complicated leiomyomas rather than malignancy. This integrated interpretation justified proceeding with conservative surgical intervention aimed at removing only the symptomatic, twisted pedunculated component while preserving the pregnancy and leaving asymptomatic intramural masses in situ.
