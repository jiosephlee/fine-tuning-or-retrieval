Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 4: ## Surgical Technique, Intraoperative Management, and Perioperative Care

The decision to proceed with surgical intervention in a pregnant patient with large abdominal masses requires a careful balance between resolving maternal complications and preserving fetal viability. In this case, the selection of a longitudinal xiphos-pubic laparotomy over a laparoscopic approach was driven by the "steric bulk" of the gravid uterus combined with two massive lesions, one extending to the subphrenic space and the other fixed in the pelvis. While laparoscopy is increasingly utilized for myomectomy in early pregnancy or for smaller, accessible masses, it presents significant technical limitations when dealing with giant, vascularized tumors that obscure anatomical landmarks and restrict working space. The sheer size of the upper mass (18 cm) and the deep pelvic location of the lower mass necessitated the broad exposure provided by an open approach to ensure safe dissection, adequate visualization of potential feeding vessels, and rapid control of hemorrhage should it occur. Furthermore, the uncertainty regarding the origin of the masses and the high vascularity noted on preoperative imaging favored an approach that allowed for immediate conversion to more extensive procedures if malignancy were suspected, although the primary goal remained organ preservation.

The operative strategy employed a selective myomectomy tailored to the specific characteristics of each lesion to minimize uterine trauma. Upon exploration, the cranial mass was identified as a pedunculated leiomyoma exhibiting signs of torsion and leakage, which correlated with the patient's acute pain and low-grade fever. Given the risk of necrosis, infection, and impending rupture, excision was mandatory. The surgical team utilized a gentle double-clamp technique on the pedicle before transection, followed by precise closure of the uterine defect with a single layer of absorbable suture to achieve hemostasis while limiting thermal or mechanical injury to the myometrium. Conversely, the caudal mass, which appeared as a large cystic intramural-subserosal leiomyoma firmly adherent to the posterior uterine torus, was deliberately left *in situ*. Attempting to enucleate a deeply embedded or broadly attached myoma during pregnancy carries a prohibitive risk of catastrophic hemorrhage and uterine irritability that could precipitate abortion or preterm labor. By removing only the symptomatic, compromised pedunculated mass and leaving the stable intramural component untouched, the surgeons effectively mitigated immediate life-threatening risks while preserving the structural integrity of the uterus necessary for continued gestation.

Protecting the fetus throughout the perioperative period involved a multimodal protocol focusing on continuous monitoring and pharmacological prophylaxis. Recognizing that surgical manipulation of the gravid uterus can trigger contractions or compromise placental perfusion, fetal cardiac activity was verified prior to incision and monitored intermittently via ultrasound throughout the procedure. To counteract potential uterine irritability induced by surgical stress or anesthesia, prophylactic progesterone was administered intraoperatively (100 mg intramuscularly) and continued daily post-discharge, transitioning to long-acting hydroxyprogesterone caproate until term. This regimen aims to maintain uterine quiescence by stabilizing myometrial cell membranes. Additionally, given the patient's pre-existing iron deficiency anemia exacerbated by intraoperative blood loss (with hemoglobin dropping to 7.5 g/dL), aggressive correction was instituted immediately. This included intravenous ferric carboxymaltose and packed red blood cell transfusions to restore oxygen-carrying capacity, ensuring adequate fetal oxygenation during the critical recovery phase.

Postoperative care extended beyond hemodynamic stabilization to include targeted infection control based on microbiological findings. Although intraoperative cultures of peritoneal fluid were negative, the patient had a confirmed urinary tract infection with *Klebsiella pneumoniae* identified preoperatively. Consequently, antibiotic prophylaxis was escalated from standard cefazolin alone to a combination regimen including clarithromycin, guided by antibiogram sensitivity results. This tailored approach prevented ascending infections that could lead to chorioamnionitis or sepsis. The successful integration of these measures—judicious surgical access, selective resection, rigorous fetal monitoring, hormonal support, and optimized medical management—allowed for the resolution of acute maternal symptoms without compromisingthe pregnancy. The patient was discharged ten days post-operatively and maintained strict obstetric surveillance, ultimately delivering a healthy infant via elective cesarean section at 37 weeks, dictated by the presence of the residual pelvic mass which would have obstructed vaginal delivery.
