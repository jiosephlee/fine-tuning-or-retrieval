Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] Given the limitations of ionizing radiation and contrast agents in pregnancy, what is the recommended imaging algorithm for characterizing complex abdominal masses when ultrasound findings are inconclusive, and what specific safety constraints apply to MRI in this population?

When ultrasound findings are inconclusive for complex abdominal masses in pregnancy, **non-contrast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** is the recommended second-line modality to characterize tissue composition, vascularity, and anatomical relationships without exposing the fetus to ionizing radiation. As illustrated in the case of the pregnant patient with symptomatic subphrenic and pelvic masses, MRI provided superior diagnostic accuracy over ultrasound by distinguishing between decidualized endometriomas and cystic degenerating leiomyomas, specifically identifying the continuity of the caudal mass with the uterine wall and the pedunculated nature of the cranial mass.

Regarding safety constraints, two critical principles must guide MRI utilization in this population. First, **gadolinium-based contrast agents are contraindicated** unless absolutely vital for immediate maternal survival, as gadolinium crosses the placenta, enters fetal circulation, and is excreted into the amniotic fluid where it may remain for prolonged periods; consequently, the case report explicitly notes that the multidisciplinary team performed a non-enhanced study to avoid these risks. Second, while MRI is generally safe after the first trimester, current recommendations suggest avoiding scanners with field strengths **greater than 1.5 Tesla** when possible, although the primary restriction remains the avoidance of contrast rather than the magnetic field strength itself. This imaging algorithm allows clinicians to differentiate benign entities like twisted or degenerating fibroids from malignancies based on signal characteristics (e.g., T1/T2 weighting) and morphological features, thereby informing the decision between expectant management and surgical intervention while prioritizing fetal safety.
