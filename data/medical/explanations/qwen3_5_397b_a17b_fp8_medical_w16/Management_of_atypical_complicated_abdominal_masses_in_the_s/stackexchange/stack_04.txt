Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the typical non-linear trajectory of uterine leiomyoma size changes throughout the three trimesters of pregnancy and the immediate postpartum period, and how does this influence the timing of potential surgical intervention?

Uterine leiomyomas exhibit a distinct, non-linear trajectory in size throughout gestation and the puerperium, driven primarily by hormonal fluctuations and hemodynamic changes. As highlighted in the discussion of this case, myoma growth is most rapid during the first trimester, increasing by approximately 6.12% per week, likely due to surging estrogen and progesterone levels which stimulate cellular proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition. This growth rate decelerates significantly in the second and third trimesters (approximately 3.10% per week), despite continued absolute uterine expansion.

Crucially, this trend reverses sharply in the late third trimester and immediate postpartum period. Following delivery, the withdrawal of ovarian steroids and the involution of the uterus lead to a substantial reduction in myoma volume, with studies cited in the case reporting a mean decrease of roughly 44% by six weeks postpartum. This physiological regression profoundly influences surgical timing and strategy. In the presented case, although the patient required acute intervention in the second trimester for a twisted, symptomatic pedunculated mass causing respiratory compromise, the surgical team elected to leave a large, asymptomatic caudal myoma *in situ*. This decision was grounded in the expectation of spontaneous postpartum shrinkage. Indeed, follow-up confirmed the residual mass had reduced in size, rendering immediate further surgery unnecessary and allowing the patient to defer definitive management while breastfeeding. Consequently, unless life-threatening complications such as torsion, uncontrollable pain, or malignancy suspicion arise, surgical intervention for incidental or stable myomas is generally deferred until after delivery to leverage this natural involution and avoid the heightened risks of hemorrhage and pregnancy loss associated with antepartum myomectomy.
