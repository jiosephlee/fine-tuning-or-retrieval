Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] During an intraoperative myomectomy in the second trimester, what specific pharmacologic prophylaxis is indicated to mitigate the risk of preterm labor, and what monitoring standards must be maintained regarding fetal viability throughout the procedure?

In the setting of an intraoperative myomectomy during the second trimester, **intramuscular progesterone prophylaxis** is indicated to mitigate the risk of uterine irritability and preterm labor. As illustrated in the reported case of a 21-week gestation undergoing laparotomy for a twisted, symptomatic leiomyoma, the surgical team initiated therapy with **100 mg of intramuscular progesterone intraoperatively**, continued daily until discharge, and transitioned to **hydroxyprogesterone caproate (341 mg)** administered every three days until elective delivery. This regimen addresses the heightened risk of myometrial contraction triggered by surgical manipulation of the gravid uterus.

Regarding fetal surveillance, the standard of care mandates **continuous verification of fetal viability** throughout the procedure. In this case, fetal cardiac activity was confirmed via exploration prior to incision and maintained through **serial and intermittent transvaginal ultrasound monitoring** during the operation. This approach aligns with established recommendations that fetal heart rate must be documented before, during, and after surgery to ensure immediate detection of fetal distress or compromise resulting from hemodynamic shifts or uterine manipulation. The successful outcome in this case—resulting in an elective cesarean delivery at 37 weeks with a healthy neonate—underscores the efficacy of combining pharmacologic uterine quiescence with rigorous intraoperative fetal monitoring when surgical intervention for complicated myomas cannot be deferred.
