Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] While low-dose sulfonylureas are historically the first-line therapy for HNF1A-MODY due to their mechanism on ATP-sensitive potassium channels, what specific safety advantage do GLP-1 receptor agonists offer in this population as beta-cell function progressively declines?

While low-dose sulfonylureas remain the historical first-line therapy for HNF1A-MODY due to their ability to bypass defective ATP-sensitive potassium channels, they carry an inherent risk of hypoglycemia as endogenous insulin secretory capacity wanes. As illustrated in this case, GLP-1 receptor agonists offer a distinct safety advantage by providing potent glucose-dependent insulin secretion, thereby minimizing the risk of hypoglycemia even as beta-cell function progressively declines.

In the reported case of a young man with a novel *HNF1A* mutation (p.N270S), the clinical team initially utilized basal insulin to address severe hyperglycemia (HbA1c 10.2%). However, once genetic confirmation established the diagnosis of HNF1A-MODY and acute glucotoxicity resolved, insulin was discontinued in favor of dulaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist. This transition was strategically chosen because, unlike sulfonylureas which stimulate insulin release regardless of ambient glucose levels, GLP-1 receptor agonists augment insulin secretion only when glucose concentrations are elevated. This mechanism is particularly advantageous in patients with "moderately preserved yet insufficient" beta-cell function, as evidenced by this patient's stimulated C-peptide of 0.83 nmol/L. By avoiding the forced secretion characteristic of sulfonylureas in the setting of diminishing beta-cell mass, GLP-1 receptor agonists mitigate the danger of iatrogenic hypoglycemia while still achieving robust glycemic control, reducing this patient's HbA1c to 6.0% within six months. Consequently, for HNF1A-MODY patients transitioning away from insulin or those susceptible to hypoglycemic events as their disease evolves, GLP-1 receptor agonists represent a safer physiological alternative that aligns with the principles of precision medicine.
