Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

Chapter 4: ## Precision Medicine Management Strategies for HNF1A-MODY

The clinical course of this patient illustrates the critical transition from empirical diabetes management to genotype-guided precision medicine. Upon initial presentation, the patient exhibited severe hyperglycemia (HbA1c 10.2%) alongside classic symptoms of catabolism, including polydipsia, polyphagia, and weight loss. Despite a strong family history suggestive of monogenic diabetes, the immediate clinical priority was rapid glycemic stabilization to resolve glucotoxicity and prevent further metabolic decompensation. Consequently, the initial therapeutic regimen empirically mirrored standard protocols for severe Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) or ketosis-prone presentations: basal insulin degludec was initiated to provide exogenous insulin coverage, while metformin and saxagliptin were added to address potential underlying insulin resistance and augment endogenous secretion. This approach is clinically prudent when genetic confirmation is pending, as it ensures safety and efficacy regardless of the final etiology. However, this phase represents a "diagnostic limbo" where treatment is broad-spectrum rather than targeted, potentially exposing the patient to unnecessary injections if the underlying pathology is purely secretory rather than resistive.

Following the identification of the novel *HNF1A* pathogenic variant (p.N270S), the treatment strategy was fundamentally recalibrated to align with the specific pathophysiology of *HNF1A*-MODY. Unlike T2DM, where insulin resistance is paramount, *HNF1A*-MODY is characterized by a progressive defect in pancreatic beta-cell function and a lowered renal threshold for glucose reabsorption due to reduced SGLT2 expression. Patients with this genotype exhibit exquisite sensitivity to agents that stimulate insulin secretion. While low-dose sulfonylureas have historically been the first-line therapy due to their mechanism of closing ATP-sensitive potassium channels, they carry a significant risk of hypoglycemia, particularly as beta-cell mass declines over time. In this case, the clinical team opted for a Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist (dulaglutide) combined with metformin after discontinuing insulin. This decision leverages the patient's moderately preserved C-peptide response (0.83 nmol/L post-stimulation) while mitigating hypoglycemic risk. Evidence suggests that GLP-1 receptor agonists are highly effective in *HNF1A*-MODY, offering comparable glycemic control to sulfonylureas with a superior safety profile regarding hypoglycemia, making them an ideal choice for long-term maintenance in patients with residual beta-cell function.

Furthermore, precision medicine in *HNF1A*-MODY necessitates not only selecting appropriate therapies but also recognizing specific contraindications inherent to the genetic defect. The *HNF1A* transcription factor regulates the expression of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) in the renal proximal tubule; mutations result in naturally reduced SGLT2 activity and consequent glycosuria even at near-normal blood glucose levels. The use of SGLT2 inhibitors in these patients is theoretically problematic and potentially dangerous. Pharmacologic inhibition of an already compromised transport system can precipitate severe dehydration, volume depletion, and an elevated risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Although this patient was not prescribed an SGLT2 inhibitor, understanding this pathophysiological nuance is vital to avoid iatrogenic harm. The successful reduction of the patient's HbA1c from 10.2% to 6.0% within six months, followed by stabilization on oral agents alone, underscores the transformative impact of genetic diagnosis. It allowed for the deprescribing of insulin and the optimization of oral/injectable non-insulin therapies tailored specifically to the molecular defect, thereby improving adherence, quality of life, and long-term prognostic outcomes.
