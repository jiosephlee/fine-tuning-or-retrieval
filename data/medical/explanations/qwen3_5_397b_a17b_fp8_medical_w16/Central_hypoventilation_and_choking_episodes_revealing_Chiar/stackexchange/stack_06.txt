Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Morning Report] When reviewing historical neuroimaging in a patient with long-standing unexplained neurological symptoms, what specific radiographic sign at the foramen magnum—often overlooked on initial read—is diagnostic for Chiari malformation type I?

The diagnostic radiographic sign for Chiari malformation type I (CMI) is the caudal displacement of the cerebellar tonsils **5 mm or more below the basion-opisthion line** (the plane of the foramen magnum) on a sagittal MRI. However, as illustrated in this case, the critical finding often overlooked on initial reads—particularly on CT scans or quick reviews—is not merely the measurement of descent, but the **crowding of the foramen magnum** characterized by an effacement of the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) spaces at the craniocervical junction.

In this patient's 14-year-old CT scan, the initial interpretation missed the "crowded foramen magnum," where cerebellar tissue extended to the level of the opisthion with a complete loss of the retrotonsillar CSF space. While modern diagnostic criteria rely on MRI to quantify tonsillar ectopia (confirmed in this case as 11 mm), the pathophysiological hallmark is the volumetric mismatch between a small posterior fossa and its neural contents. This crowding compresses the medulla and disrupts CSF flow dynamics. Clinically, this compression explains the patient's triad of symptoms: occipital headaches due to dural traction, lower cranial nerve dysfunction manifesting as neurogenic dysphagia (choking on solids and liquids), and central hypoventilation ("Ondine's curse") resulting from brainstem compression that impairs automatic respiratory drive while sparing voluntary control. Recognizing this obliteration of CSF spaces at the foramen magnum is essential to connecting unexplained respiratory or bulbar symptoms to a structural etiology, preventing the years-long diagnostic delays seen in this case.
