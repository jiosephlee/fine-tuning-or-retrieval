Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] The patient initially received 0.3 mg epinephrine via auto-injector despite weighing 104 kg. According to current dosing guidelines (0.01 mg/kg), what is the recommended adult dose, and why might reliance on standard 0.3 mg auto-injectors lead to suboptimal outcomes in larger adults?

The recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose for an adult is **0.5 mg** (0.01 mg/kg up to a maximum of 0.5 mg), not the 0.3 mg contained in many standard auto-injectors. For a patient weighing 104 kg, as described in this case, the calculated weight-based dose would be roughly 1.0 mg; however, current guidelines cap the single intramuscular dose at 0.5 mg to balance efficacy with safety.

Reliance on fixed-dose 0.3 mg auto-injectors in larger adults can lead to suboptimal outcomes because it results in significant underdosing relative to both weight-based calculations and the established therapeutic ceiling of 0.5 mg. As highlighted in the discussion of this case report, underdosing may fail to adequately reverse vasodilation and bronchospasm, particularly in severe presentations or complex courses like multiphasic anaphylaxis. The authors note that retrospective data suggests patients receiving an initial 0.3 mg dose require escalation of care—including additional epinephrine injections—significantly more often than those receiving the full 0.5 mg dose. In this specific case, the patient required four separate episodes of epinephrine administration; while the initial doses were delivered via 0.3 mg auto-injectors due to institutional preference for speed and ease of use, subsequent doses were manually prepared at 0.5 mg to ensure therapeutic adequacy. Clinicians must recognize that while auto-injectors offer logistical advantages in time-sensitive settings, the fixed 0.3 mg formulation may be insufficient for adults >50 kg, potentially delaying definitive stabilization in life-threatening anaphylaxis.
