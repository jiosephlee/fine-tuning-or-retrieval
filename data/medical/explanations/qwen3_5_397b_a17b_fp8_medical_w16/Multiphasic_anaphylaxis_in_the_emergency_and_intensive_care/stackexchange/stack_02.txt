Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] In this case, the patient experienced four distinct episodes of anaphylaxis after a single exposure to clarithromycin. What is the typical timeframe within which biphasic or multiphasic reactions occur after the initial resolution of symptoms?

In this case, the patient experienced four distinct episodes of anaphylaxis following a single exposure to clarithromycin, illustrating a rare multiphasic course. Based on the clinical data presented, biphasic and multiphasic reactions typically occur within the **first 24 hours** after the initial resolution of symptoms.

While the majority of recurrences happen within this 24-hour window—with approximately half occurring between 6 and 12 hours post-initial event—the literature acknowledges a broader potential timeframe. As noted in the case report, symptom recurrence has been documented as late as **78 hours** after the initial allergen exposure. This variability underscores the challenge in defining optimal observation periods; while traditional monitoring often ranges from 4 to 6 hours, cases like this one, where relapses occurred within the first 12 hours necessitating ICU admission, highlight that high-risk patients may require extended surveillance. Although rare, clinicians must remain aware that the asymptomatic interval can extend up to three days, even in the absence of re-exposure to the trigger.
