Chapter 2: ## Navigating Complex Courses: Biphasic and Multiphasic Anaphylaxis

Understanding the trajectory of anaphylaxis is critical for determining observation periods and anticipating clinical deterioration. While the classic presentation involves a single episode that resolves with treatment, a subset of patients experiences complex courses characterized by symptom recurrence. It is essential to distinguish between four distinct clinical patterns: simple resolution, biphasic anaphylaxis, multiphasic anaphylaxis, and refractory or protracted anaphylaxis. Simple resolution occurs when symptoms abate completely after initial therapy and do not return. Biphasic anaphylaxis is defined as the recurrence of symptoms after a period of complete resolution, typically occurring within 1 to 72 hours of the initial event without re-exposure to the trigger. Multiphasic anaphylaxis, as illustrated in this case report, involves two or more recurrences of symptoms following the initial reaction, creating a roller-coaster clinical course that demands prolonged intensive monitoring. In contrast, refractory anaphylaxis refers to persistent symptoms that fail to respond adequately to repeated doses of epinephrine, while protracted anaphylaxis describes a state where symptoms persist continuously for hours or days without a symptom-free interval. Differentiating these entities is not merely semantic; it dictates whether a clinician should anticipate a new wave of mediator release (biphasic/multiphasic) versus escalating support for ongoing shock (refractory/protracted).

The pathophysiology behind symptom recurrence in the absence of renewed allergen exposure remains incompletely understood but is hypothesized to involve several mechanisms. One theory suggests a "second wave" of inflammatory mediator release from mast cells and basophils that were not fully depleted during the initial degranulation event. Another possibility involves the continued absorption of the allergen from a depot site, such as the gastrointestinal tract following oral ingestion or intramuscular tissue following injection, which sustains antigen presentation and immune activation. In the context of this case, where the patient ingested clarithromycin orally, delayed gastric emptying or variable absorption kinetics could theoretically contribute to prolonged antigen availability. However, the distinct asymptomatic intervals observed between this patient's four episodes strongly favor a mechanism of recurrent mast cell destabilization rather than continuous absorption. Furthermore, underlying mast cell disorders, such as the suspected systemic mastocytosis in this patient, may lower the threshold for degranulation, making the mast cell population hyper-reactive and prone to multiple waves of activation even after the initial trigger has been removed.

Epidemiologically, biphasic reactions are reported in approximately 4% to 6% of anaphylaxis cases, though literature estimates vary widely from 0.4% to 23.3% due to inconsistent definitions and observation protocols. True multiphasic anaphylaxis is significantly rarer and lacks robust incidence data. Identifying patients at risk for these complex courses is challenging because reliable predictors remain elusive. Proposed risk factors include severe initial presentation (e.g., hypotension or respiratory compromise), delayed administration of epinephrine, requirement for multiple epinephrine doses during the initial event, and unknown triggers. In this specific case, the patient exhibited several high-risk features: she presented with hypotensive shock (74/50 mmHg) and hypoxemia shortly after ingesting clarithromycin, necessitating immediate epinephrine. Despite this prompt intervention and an initial favorable response, she experienced three subsequent relapses within 12 hours. This progression underscores a crucial clinical lesson: even when initial stabilization appears successful and vital signs normalize, the potential for multiphasic recurrence mandates extended observation in a monitored setting, particularly for patients with severe index presentations or suspected underlying mast cell pathology.

A notable feature of this patient's multiphasic course was the phenomenon of waning severity with each successive episode. The initial presentation involved profound hypotension, cyanosis, and severe dyspnea indicative of frank anaphylactic shock. The subsequent relapses, while still requiring epinephrine rescue, manifested with progressively milder symptoms, eventually limiting themselves to dyspnea, rash, and pruritus without hemodynamic collapse. This pattern suggests a gradual exhaustion of mediators within the mast cell granules or a diminishing pool of recruitable effector cells with each degranulation event. Recognizing this trend is vital for clinical decision-making; while every episode must be treated aggressively as a potential life-threat until proven otherwise, observing a consistent decrease in severity can provide cautious reassurance that the storm is subsiding. In this case, this downward trajectory influenced the multidisciplinary team to consider an expectant management approach rather than initiating high-risk interventions like continuous intravenous epinephrine infusions preemptively.

Finally，准确 identifying true biphasic or multiphasic reactions requires ruling out diagnostic pitfalls related to incomplete initial treatment or pharmacokinetic variables. A common error is misclassifying refractory anaphylaxis—where symptoms never fully resolved due to inadequate dosing—as a biphasic reaction. For instance, this patient initially received 0.3 mg of epinephrine via auto-injector; while effective enough to stabilize her temporarily, this dose represents potential underdosing for an adult weighing 104 kg (recommended dose ~0.5 mg). If symptoms had persisted immediately after this dose without an asymptomatic window, it would constitute refractory shock due to underdosing rather than a biphasic event. Similarly，持续 symptoms caused by ongoing absorption from slow-release medications or large food boluses can mimic recurrence if not carefully timed against symptom resolution windows. True multiphasic anaphylaxis requires documentation of a clear asymptomatic interval where physiological parameters return to baseline before deteriorating again. In this report，严格 adherence to monitoring allowed clinicians to confirm distinct episodes separated by stability validating the diagnosis of multiphasic anaphylaxis driven by clarithromycin in a patient with probable systemic mastocytosis.
