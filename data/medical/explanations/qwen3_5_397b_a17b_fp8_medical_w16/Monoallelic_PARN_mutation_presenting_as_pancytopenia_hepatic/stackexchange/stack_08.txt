Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Morning Report] Given that the identified PARN variant in this case was classified as a 'Variant of Uncertain Significance' (VUS), what combination of clinical and bioinformatic criteria supported the decision to diagnose the patient with a telomere biology disorder despite the lack of definitive genetic classification?

The decision to diagnose this patient with a telomere biology disorder (TBD) despite the *PARN* variant being classified as a Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS) rests on the principle that clinical diagnosis in genetics often requires synthesizing phenotypic severity with bioinformatic probability, particularly when the alternative explanations have been exhausted.

In this case, the clinical reasoning was driven by a "syndromic convergence" that is highly specific to TBDs. The patient presented with a multisystem constellation involving bone marrow failure (pancytopenia), organ fibrosis (hepatic cirrhosis and pulmonary fibrosis), and features of premature aging (premature greying, osteoporosis, and neuropathy). Crucially, while severe vitamin B12 deficiency explained the hematologic abnormalities, the persistence of disproportionate systemic dysfunction—including fibrotic liver and lung disease—even after nutritional correction, pointed toward an underlying genetic susceptibility rather than an acquired sole etiology. The absence of classical mucocutaneous triads of dyskeratosis congenita did not exclude the diagnosis, as TBDs are increasingly recognized to present in adulthood with isolated organ failure or subtle aging features.

Bioinformatically, although the specific c.613T>C variant lacked definitive population data for pathogenic classification, it satisfied several supportive ACMG/AMP criteria that elevated its likelihood of being disease-causing. The variant affects a highly conserved residue within the critical R3H functional domain of *PARN*, a region intolerant to benign variation. Furthermore, *in silico* predictive tools consistently flagged the substitution as damaging, and *PARN* itself is a gene where missense variants are a known mechanism of disease (haploinsufficiency). The report notes parallels with other *PARN* missense variants in similar domains that are already classified as likely pathogenic.

Therefore, the diagnosis was established not by the genetic result alone, but by the robust correlation between a biologically plausible genetic hit in a telomere-regulating gene and a classic, albeit complex, TBD phenotype. As highlighted in this case, when a patient presents with unexplained multi-organ fibrosis and marrow failure in young adulthood, a VUS in a high-probability candidate gene should be interpreted as causative in the appropriate clinical context to guide essential surveillance and genetic counseling.
