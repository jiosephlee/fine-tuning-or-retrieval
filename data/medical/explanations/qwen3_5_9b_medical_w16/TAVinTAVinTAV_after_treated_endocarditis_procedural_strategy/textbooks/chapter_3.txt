Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 3: ## Preprocedural Imaging and Procedural Planning

In the context of serial transcatheter aortic valve interventions, particularly when addressing a third-generation failure such as TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) serves as the cornerstone of procedural planning. As demonstrated in this case, MDCT is not merely a screening tool but a decision-making engine that dictates valve selection, sizing, and implantation strategy. The complexity of overlapping stent frames from the initial Lotus valve and the subsequent Evolut valve requires a precise evaluation of the annular geometry to mitigate risks of coronary obstruction, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, and paravalvular leak. Unlike single-intervention TAVI, where the annular plane is relatively distinct, sequential valve implantations create a nested anatomy where the landing zone for the third valve is defined by the exterior landing interface of the second-prosthesis frame. In this patient, the preprocedural multislice CT was critical in confirming the resolution of the recent prosthetic valve endocarditis and quantifying the accessible annular perimeter, ensuring that the risk of vegetation had subsided before subjecting the anatomy to further instrumentation.

A critical component of the planning process involves the quantitative assessment of the landing zone dimensions and the minimum lumen diameter of the underlying stent frame. In this case, MDCT analysis revealed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the second-generation Evolut prosthesis. This measurement directly influences the sizing of the third valve and the severity of necessary predilation. A smaller landing zone, such as that approaching 14 mm in a frame that was originally over-stented to preserve the luminal area of the second valve, increases the risk of fracture during passive inner-fracture or high-pressure balloon post-dilation. The technical team utilized these parameters to calculate the cumulative burden of radial expansion required, ensuring that the balancing force applied during predilation would not compromise the structural integrity of the underlying Lotus and Evolut valve frames. This step is pedagogically essential for understanding that the third valve must physically expand the second valve frame, making the structural strength of the pre-existing scaffolding a variable in the differential pressure gradient calculations during the procedure.

Furthermore, the evaluation of coronary artery anatomy is paramount in triple-valve scenarios due to the increased risk of geometric occlusion. Preprocedural MDCT allows for the reconstruction of valve-to-coronary height distances and the interaction between the anticipated neo-skirt and the coronary ostia. In this specific case, the CT planning focused on the relationship between the neo-skirt of the upcoming third valve and the coronary ostia, analyzing the sinus of Valsalva dimensions. The preservation of coronary perfusion relies heavily on avoiding the sealing of the new neo-skirt or frame over the coronary mouths. While coronary protection devices, such as the PAILS strategy or embolic protection systems, are often utilized in high-risk cases, the multidisciplinary team in this instance determined that the CT-guided geometry allowed for a high-implantation strategy without explicit coronary protection. This decision was predicated on the specific measurements of the aortic root and the clearance provided by the high implantation depth, which prevented the new valve frame from encroaching upon the coronary ostia.

Valve selection is another critical outcome of the CT-guided planning process, driven largely by the need to optimize the effective orifice area (EOA) and preserve future access. The decision to implant an Acurate neo2 self-expanding prosthesis rather than a balloon-expandable option was guided by its frame geometry, which is amenable to high implantation relative to native tissue. High implantation of the third valve, positioned to align with the leaflets of the second Evolut valve, was a deliberate strategy to maximize the EOA. By placing the neo-valve at the leaflet level of the deployed Evolut, the team aimed to coat the underlying leaflet structure to prevent leaflet collapse and restriction. This alignment is crucial for maintaining hemodynamic function, preventing prosthesis-patient mismatch, and ensuring that the third valve does not create a concentric restriction within the already narrowed landing zone. The self-expanding nature of the Acurate device facilitates this deployment by conforming to the irregularities of the stented annulus while allowing for post-dilation under rapid pacing to ensure apposition.

Finally, the planning for commissural alignment serves as the final optimization strategy for hemodynamic efficiency and procedural safety. The preprocedural CT and 3D modeling provided the roadmap for identifying the optimal landing height where the commissures would restrict the third valve frame from bulging into the sino-tubular junction. By aligning the three stacked valves, the team sought to minimize the residual transvalvular gradient and patient-prosthesis mismatch. However, this strategy also demands an acknowledgment of future limitations; the cumulative mesh burden of three valves may complicate potential coronary interventions in the future. Therefore, the CT analysis included not just immediate feasibility but the long-term prognosis of coronary access. The patient’s subsequent follow-up confirmed that the prioritization of commissural alignment and high implantation successfully preserved coronary flow and relieved severe stenosis, validating the MDCT-driven approach for complex, serial aortic valve disease. This case underscores that while surgical risk abnormalities (EuroSCORE II) eliminated open surgical options, comprehensive preprocedural imaging enabled a strategic transcatheter solution that extended the functional lifespan of the valve.
