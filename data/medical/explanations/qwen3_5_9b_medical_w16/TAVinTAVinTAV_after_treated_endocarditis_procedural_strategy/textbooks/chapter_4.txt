Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 4: ## Procedural Execution: Technical Nuances of Triple Valve Implantation

The technical execution of a third-generation transcatheter valve implantation necessitates a strategy that prioritizes anatomical precision and hemodynamic safety over speed. In this patient, the procedural team utilized a dual arterial access approach, engaging both the left radial and left femoral arteries to manage the procedural load and optimize positioning of complex guiding systems. A Safari guidewire was advanced from the femoral access into the left ventricle, establishing a stable baseline for device advancement. Balloon pre-dilation was performed using a 22/40 mm catheter under rapid ventricular pacing, which was maintained at 180 beats per minute. This high-rate pacing serves a critical role in controlling hemodynamics during the dilation phase; by limiting cardiac filling diastole and increasing stroke volume, it improves aortic flow and significantly reduces the risk of microvascular ischemia or thromboembolic complications that might arise from transient hemodynamic collapse or back-bleeding during the high-pressure dilation of the calcified or struts of prior prostheses.

Central to the procedural success in a triple-stented anatomy is the precise implantation depth and commissural alignment. The team selected a self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2) but employed a high deployment strategy, positioning the device at the leaflet level of the previously implanted Evolut valve. This supra-annular alignment was deliberate; it prevented the formation of a "neo-skirt" of material extending below the coronary ostia, which could obscure future vascular access or increase the risk of coronary obstruction. By fixing the pre-existing Evolut valve leaflets against the stent frame of the new prosthesis, the team aimed to maximize the effective orifice area and mitigate the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch. This approach minimizes the cumulative burden of overlapping frames, acknowledging that while necessary for relief of severe stenosis, every layer of metal complicates future coronary or re-interventional access.

During the procedure, real-time monitoring was critical to verifying safety before finalizing the deployment. Aortography was performed to track balloon integrity, and simultaneously, coronary flow patency was confirmed immediately following pre-dilation to ensure no stent strut had accidentally impinged on the coronary ostia, a particular concern given the history of endocarditis and valvular calcification. Following successful deployment, the new valve was post-dilated using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid ventricular pacing to ensure maximal radial expansion of the frame and mobility of the leaflets. Final aortography verified correct positioning, which confirmed that the prosthesis was fully deployed without obstructing the coronary ostia or introducing significant paravalvular leaks. This meticulous verification of coronary flow and valve profile ensured the procedural safety of the complex triple-valve configuration, supporting the feasibility of treating structural degeneration even with multiple prior valve stents.
