Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] According to the 2025 ESC guidelines, in what specific clinical scenario does a transcatheter approach become the preferred strategy over surgical valve replacement for a patient with treated prosthetic valve endocarditis and subsequent structural valve deterioration?

According to the 2025 ESC guidelines referenced in this report, surgical aortic valve replacement remains the default recommended strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). However, a transcatheter approach becomes the preferred strategy when the infectious process is definitively treated and cleared, but surgical risk is prohibitive or anatomical feasibility renders open root replacement infeasible.

This specific clinical scenario requires confirmation of infection resolution before proceeding, as ballpoint procedures are considered once endocarditis is eradicated. In this case, the patient’s *Enterococcus faecalis* infection was successfully treated with intravenous ampicillin, and follow-up blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT confirmed the absence of active vegetations or paravalvular complications. Consequently, the decision to pursue a third transcatheter intervention (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) was driven by surgically prohibitive risk rather than the necessity of infection control; the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and open explantation of prior transcatheter frames would likely have necessitated high-risk aortic root replacement.

Therefore, the specific scenario is a patient with controlled PVE presenting with structural valve deterioration, where the operative mortality risk of open surgery outweighs the procedural complexity of serial transcatheter interventions. This reinforces the guideline emphasis on individualized management and multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation when reassessing surgical candidates with resolved infections.

### [Pathophysiology] In the context of this case, what was the proposed mechanism by which the history of prosthetic valve endocarditis contributed to the subsequent valve dysfunction, aside from the active infection itself?

In this case, the history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) contributed to the subsequent valve dysfunction primarily through the mechanism of leaflet injury and subsequent fibrosis. According to the discussion, while the active infection was treated and resolved, the PVE episode caused direct leaflet injury that precipitated mechanical restriction and scarring of the valve leaflets. This fibrosis compounded the natural structural degeneration (calcification and fibrosis) inherent to the transcatheter bioprostheses. Consequently, the severe stenosis and regurgitation observed after the completion of antimicrobial therapy were driven not by recurrent bacteremia, but by the permanent mechanical impairment and fibrotic changes to the valve structure that persisted following the inflammatory insult. This highlights that even after successful treatment of the infection, the preceding PVE can leave residual structural damage that requires complex intervention to manage.

### [Management] What specific preprocedural CT-derived anatomical parameters were critical for assessing the feasibility of implanting a third valve in this triple-stented anatomy, particularly regarding coronary clearance?

In this case of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, accurate preprocedural multidetector CT analysis was critical to assess the safe landing zone and ensure coronary clearance. The essential anatomical parameters included the **minimum lumen diameter of the innermost existing prosthesis** (specifically measured at 13.7 mm in the Evolut frame in this patient), which defines the radial expansion potential of the aortic root. Additionally, the evaluation focused on **coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance**, and the **interaction between the frames** of the previously implanted transcatheter valves.

These specific measurements guided the procedural strategy by confirming that a **high-implantation** of the third valve would preserve coronary flow. By analyzing the relationship between the anticipated neo-skirt and coronary ostia, the team verified that the third valve could be deployed without obstructing the ostia, allowing for the procedure to proceed without coronary protection despite the complex, triple-layered stent architecture.

### [Clinical Pearls] How does the 'high implantation' strategy utilize the previously implanted Evolut valve leaflets, and what hemodynamic complication does this specific technique aim to prevent?

In complex serial valve-in-valve procedures, such as this TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention, the 'high implantation' strategy is employed to optimize the geometry of the new valve relative to the previous prosthesis. Specifically, the strategy utilizes the leaflets of the previously implanted Evolut valve as a mechanical anchor for the new frame. In this case, the Acurate neo2 was deployed at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets, which allowed for the fixation of the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame.

This fixation serves to prevent the inward folding or bunching of the prior leaflets, which would otherwise reduce the available cross-sectional area for blood flow. Therefore, the primary hemodynamic complication this technique aims to prevent is a significant reduction in the effective orifice area (EOA), which would otherwise precipitate severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) and elevated transvalvular gradients. Additionally, this positioning minimizes the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, contributing to the preservation of coronary flow and clearance, but the critical hemodynamic benefit is the stabilization of the valve area through leaflet containment.

### [Management] Beyond the duration of antimicrobial therapy, what specific clinical and imaging findings confirmed the safety of proceeding with the third TAVI procedure despite the patient's history of infective endocarditis?

The decision to proceed with the third TAVI in a patient with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) relied on the documented and radiographic eradication of infection, combined with an assessment of surgical prohibitive risk. Beyond the completion of the 6-week antimicrobial course for *Enterococcus faecalis*, safety was confirmed by the absence of active infectious sequelae at the time of the procedure.

Specifically, the multidisciplinary team required three key elements before deployment: first, clinical resolution without fever or recurrence, and critically, repeated **negative blood cultures** to exclude persistent bacteremia. Second, **transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE)** performed pre-procedurally confirmed the absence of active endocarditis vegetations or paravalvular abscesses. Third, **multislice CT (MSCT)** not only assessed anatomical feasibility for coronary protection in this triple-stented anatomy, but also corroborated the resolution of infection.

In alignment with our current management principles, once infection was confirmed resolved and no long-term suppressive antibiotic therapy was indicated, the procedural decision-making shifted to focus on haemodynamic severity and anatomical feasibility rather than the past infection itself. In this case, the prohibitive surgical risk (European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation II >27%) mandated a transcatheter approach; however, a third intervention was only permitted because imaging and microbiology definitively demonstrated that the prior infectious process had been neutralized, preventing high-risk embolic or septic complications during valve deployment.

### [Pathophysiology] The case notes a Doppler velocity index (DVI) of 0.24 immediately post-procedure. What does this specific value indicate regarding the severity of prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM)?

A Doppler velocity index (DVI) of 0.24 immediately post-procedure indicates a clinical picture consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM).

DVI is calculated as the ratio of the peak velocity across the aortic valve to the peak velocity in the left ventricular outflow tract, serving as a non-invasive hemodynamic surrogate for the effective orifice area relative to blood flow. While the standard threshold for defining severe PPM is generally a DVI$\ge$0.25, this case illustrates the nuance of classification in complex transcatheter interventions. In this specific TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the DVI of 0.24 was classified as consistent with severe PPM because it occurred alongside a markedly reduced indexed valve area of 0.4 cm²/m² (severe threshold is <0.6 cm²/m²) and a high mean gradient of 67 mm Hg.

The pathophysiology reflects the cumulative restriction imposed by multiple overlapping valve frames, which reduce the available effective orifice area. Recognizing this severity immediately post-deployment was critical for long-term management. To mitigate the resulting hemodynamic burden and improve the sustained orifice area, the procedural strategy emphasized high implantation depth, commissural alignment, and precise valve selection (Acurate neo2) to maximize flow capacity and prevent further reduction in effective area. This case underscores that when DVI values approach the 0.25 threshold in the context of triple-valved anatomy, PPM must be treated as a severe constraint requiring rigorous preprocedural imaging planning and intraoperative optimization.

### [Management] Why was the Acurate neo2 bioprosthetic valve selected over other available transcatheter valve options (e.g., Evolut or Lotus) for this third-generation implantation, considering the patient's limited anatomical restrictions?

In this complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the selection of the Acurate neo2 bioprosthetic valve was dictated by the specific geometrical constraints of the triple-stented anatomy and the imperative to prevent severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). 

The preprocedural multislice CT identified a critical minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the existing Evolut stent structure. In such a constrained landing zone, balloon-expandable valves risk increasing the calcific burden on vulnerable tissue or failing to provide adequate radial support due to forces transmitted through multiple frames. The Acurate neo2, a self-expanding device, was selected specifically for its **supra-annular leaflet positioning** and **relatively low frame height**. These design features provided the necessary clearance for the **high implantation strategy** required in this patient. 

High implantation was essential to align the neo-skirt with the leaflets of the second-generation Evolut prosthesis rather than the aortic annulus; this prevented the inward folding of the pre-existing leaflets, which would have further compounded the reduced effective orifice area and PPM. Furthermore, the Acurate neo2 supports **commissural alignment**, a critical technical consideration chosen to lower the neo-skirt relative to the coronary ostia and preserve future coronary access. By prioritizing a device with favourable haemodynamic performance in narrow anatomies over other available options, the Heart Team optimized the residual effective orifice area while maintaining patent coronaries without the need for intracoronary protection. The successful 1-year follow-up confirms that this anatomical-matching strategy effectively mitigated the procedural risks associated with serial reinterventions.

### [Differential Diagnosis] Differentiating between valve restenosis and recurrent infective endocarditis is crucial in the post-TAVI setting. Based on the patient's clinical timeline, how would recurrent *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis typically present differently from structural valve degeneration at 1-year follow-up?

Distinguishing recurrent infective endocarditis from structural valve degeneration in this setting relies on differentiating systemic inflammatory signs from isolated hemodynamic obstruction. In this patient, the initial recurrence of *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis in September 2022 presented with acute fever, worsening heart failure, and positive blood cultures, necessitating targeted antimicrobial therapy for six weeks. In contrast, the prosthetic valve dysfunction observed prior to the third reintervention (January 2024) presented solely with hemodynamic compromise, characterized by a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an orifice area of 0.5 cm², without fever, elevated inflammatory markers, or vegetations on imaging.

Therefore, at 1-year follow-up, the differentiation rests on the same clinical criteria: the absence of recurrent fever, negative blood cultures, and an transoesophageal echocardiogram showing no new vegetations distinguish resolved structural degeneration from recurrent endocarditis. This patient’s maintenance of a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, preserved left ventricular ejection fraction, and absence of infective complications at 12 months confirms that the clinical decline was driven by mechanical restenosis and leaflet degeneration rather than recurrent infection.

### [Management] Given the patient's EuroSCORE II of 27.36% and the presence of a deep stented aortic root, what are the primary anatomical limitations that render surgical explantation or replacement of a triple-valve system infeasible?

**Clinical Teaching Point**

A fundamental teaching point in the management of serial transcatheter valve interventions is recognizing how anatomical modification alters surgical risk profiles. In this patient, the primary anatomical limitation rendering surgical explantation infeasible is the deep fixation of the triple-valve system (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) within the aortic root.

As outlined in our discussion, the sequential implantation of the Lotus, Evolut, and Acurate valves has resulted in multiple stent frames anchoring significantly into the aortic wall. Unlike standard infective endocarditis where a surgical explantation of a single prosthesis may be achievable, removing these deeply integrated frames would require a total aortic root and likely an ascending aortic replacement to ensure structural integrity and remove stented tissue safely.

This complex "hostile" anatomy is compounded by a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, which stratifies him as prohibitive for surgical intervention. Consequently, the deep stent integration transforms what could theoretically be a surgical pathology into a mandatory percutaneous indication, necessitating the strategy-driven TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach observed in this report.

### [Clinical Pearls] Compare the immediate post-procedural hemodynamic profile to the 1-year follow-up regarding the mean transvalvular gradient and effective orifice area. What phenomenon may explain the significant initial elevation in gradient that resolved over the subsequent year?

**Clinical Teaching Answer: Hemodynamics and Structural Expansion in Serial TAVI**

When evaluating the serial hemodynamics of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions, clinicians must recognize that immediate post-procedural gradients often overestimate the final functional valve performance due to acute mechanical constraints in the durational anatomy.

In this specific case report, the patient’s hemodynamic profile at the immediate post-procedural time point (June 7, 2024) was characterized by **severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM)**, with a mean transvalvular gradient of **67 mm Hg** and a small effective orifice area (AVA) of **0.9 cm²**. This occurred despite the successful deployment of the third valve. By contrast, at the 1-year follow-up (June 2025), the mean gradient had stabilized at **26 mm Hg** and the AVA had improved to **1.1 cm²**, accompanied by preserved leaflet motion and absence of regurgitation.

The significant resolution of the gradient and the improvement in AVA between implantation and the follow-up period can be attributed to **partial radial expansion of the underlying transcatheter stent frame** (specifically the prior Evolut prosthesis) facilitated by high-pressure post-dilation and strategic commissural alignment. In triple-stented anatomies, the new valve leaflets can initially occupy a compressed configuration; however, the intentional post-dilation (performed here with a 20/40 mm balloon) not only expanded the neo-valve but also exerts outward force on the underlying locked stenido-synovial frames.

This case illustrates that in complex serial TAVI, the **initial hemodynamic burden is partly mechanical, not perfusion-driven**. The high initial gradient reflects the cumulative stacking of stent frames and restricted flow geometry. The subsequent improvement derives from the **mechanical redistribution and expansion of the confined valve pouch**, allowing for better leaflet coaptation and unobstructed central flow velocity. Therefore, careful planning to utilize high-pressure post-dilation and strict commissural alignment—guided by preprocedural CT modeling—is critical to preventing permanent, severe PPM in high-risk serial reinterventions.
