Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] According to the 2025 ESC guidelines, in what specific clinical scenario does a transcatheter approach become the preferred strategy over surgical valve replacement for a patient with treated prosthetic valve endocarditis and subsequent structural valve deterioration?

According to the 2025 ESC guidelines referenced in this report, surgical aortic valve replacement remains the default recommended strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). However, a transcatheter approach becomes the preferred strategy when the infectious process is definitively treated and cleared, but surgical risk is prohibitive or anatomical feasibility renders open root replacement infeasible.

This specific clinical scenario requires confirmation of infection resolution before proceeding, as ballpoint procedures are considered once endocarditis is eradicated. In this case, the patient’s *Enterococcus faecalis* infection was successfully treated with intravenous ampicillin, and follow-up blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT confirmed the absence of active vegetations or paravalvular complications. Consequently, the decision to pursue a third transcatheter intervention (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) was driven by surgically prohibitive risk rather than the necessity of infection control; the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and open explantation of prior transcatheter frames would likely have necessitated high-risk aortic root replacement.

Therefore, the specific scenario is a patient with controlled PVE presenting with structural valve deterioration, where the operative mortality risk of open surgery outweighs the procedural complexity of serial transcatheter interventions. This reinforces the guideline emphasis on individualized management and multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation when reassessing surgical candidates with resolved infections.
