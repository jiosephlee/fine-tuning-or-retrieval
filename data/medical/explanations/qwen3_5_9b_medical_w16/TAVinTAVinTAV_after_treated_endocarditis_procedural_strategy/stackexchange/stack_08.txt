Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Differentiating between valve restenosis and recurrent infective endocarditis is crucial in the post-TAVI setting. Based on the patient's clinical timeline, how would recurrent *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis typically present differently from structural valve degeneration at 1-year follow-up?

Distinguishing recurrent infective endocarditis from structural valve degeneration in this setting relies on differentiating systemic inflammatory signs from isolated hemodynamic obstruction. In this patient, the initial recurrence of *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis in September 2022 presented with acute fever, worsening heart failure, and positive blood cultures, necessitating targeted antimicrobial therapy for six weeks. In contrast, the prosthetic valve dysfunction observed prior to the third reintervention (January 2024) presented solely with hemodynamic compromise, characterized by a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an orifice area of 0.5 cm², without fever, elevated inflammatory markers, or vegetations on imaging.

Therefore, at 1-year follow-up, the differentiation rests on the same clinical criteria: the absence of recurrent fever, negative blood cultures, and an transoesophageal echocardiogram showing no new vegetations distinguish resolved structural degeneration from recurrent endocarditis. This patient’s maintenance of a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, preserved left ventricular ejection fraction, and absence of infective complications at 12 months confirms that the clinical decline was driven by mechanical restenosis and leaflet degeneration rather than recurrent infection.
