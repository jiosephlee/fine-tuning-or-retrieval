Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] The case notes a Doppler velocity index (DVI) of 0.24 immediately post-procedure. What does this specific value indicate regarding the severity of prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM)?

A Doppler velocity index (DVI) of 0.24 immediately post-procedure indicates a clinical picture consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM).

DVI is calculated as the ratio of the peak velocity across the aortic valve to the peak velocity in the left ventricular outflow tract, serving as a non-invasive hemodynamic surrogate for the effective orifice area relative to blood flow. While the standard threshold for defining severe PPM is generally a DVI$\ge$0.25, this case illustrates the nuance of classification in complex transcatheter interventions. In this specific TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the DVI of 0.24 was classified as consistent with severe PPM because it occurred alongside a markedly reduced indexed valve area of 0.4 cm²/m² (severe threshold is <0.6 cm²/m²) and a high mean gradient of 67 mm Hg.

The pathophysiology reflects the cumulative restriction imposed by multiple overlapping valve frames, which reduce the available effective orifice area. Recognizing this severity immediately post-deployment was critical for long-term management. To mitigate the resulting hemodynamic burden and improve the sustained orifice area, the procedural strategy emphasized high implantation depth, commissural alignment, and precise valve selection (Acurate neo2) to maximize flow capacity and prevent further reduction in effective area. This case underscores that when DVI values approach the 0.25 threshold in the context of triple-valved anatomy, PPM must be treated as a severe constraint requiring rigorous preprocedural imaging planning and intraoperative optimization.
