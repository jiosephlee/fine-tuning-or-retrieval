Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] Beyond the duration of antimicrobial therapy, what specific clinical and imaging findings confirmed the safety of proceeding with the third TAVI procedure despite the patient's history of infective endocarditis?

The decision to proceed with the third TAVI in a patient with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) relied on the documented and radiographic eradication of infection, combined with an assessment of surgical prohibitive risk. Beyond the completion of the 6-week antimicrobial course for *Enterococcus faecalis*, safety was confirmed by the absence of active infectious sequelae at the time of the procedure.

Specifically, the multidisciplinary team required three key elements before deployment: first, clinical resolution without fever or recurrence, and critically, repeated **negative blood cultures** to exclude persistent bacteremia. Second, **transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE)** performed pre-procedurally confirmed the absence of active endocarditis vegetations or paravalvular abscesses. Third, **multislice CT (MSCT)** not only assessed anatomical feasibility for coronary protection in this triple-stented anatomy, but also corroborated the resolution of infection.

In alignment with our current management principles, once infection was confirmed resolved and no long-term suppressive antibiotic therapy was indicated, the procedural decision-making shifted to focus on haemodynamic severity and anatomical feasibility rather than the past infection itself. In this case, the prohibitive surgical risk (European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation II >27%) mandated a transcatheter approach; however, a third intervention was only permitted because imaging and microbiology definitively demonstrated that the prior infectious process had been neutralized, preventing high-risk embolic or septic complications during valve deployment.
