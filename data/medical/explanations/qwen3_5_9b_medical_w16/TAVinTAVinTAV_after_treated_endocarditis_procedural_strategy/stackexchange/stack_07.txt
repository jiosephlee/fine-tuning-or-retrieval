Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] Why was the Acurate neo2 bioprosthetic valve selected over other available transcatheter valve options (e.g., Evolut or Lotus) for this third-generation implantation, considering the patient's limited anatomical restrictions?

In this complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the selection of the Acurate neo2 bioprosthetic valve was dictated by the specific geometrical constraints of the triple-stented anatomy and the imperative to prevent severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). 

The preprocedural multislice CT identified a critical minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the existing Evolut stent structure. In such a constrained landing zone, balloon-expandable valves risk increasing the calcific burden on vulnerable tissue or failing to provide adequate radial support due to forces transmitted through multiple frames. The Acurate neo2, a self-expanding device, was selected specifically for its **supra-annular leaflet positioning** and **relatively low frame height**. These design features provided the necessary clearance for the **high implantation strategy** required in this patient. 

High implantation was essential to align the neo-skirt with the leaflets of the second-generation Evolut prosthesis rather than the aortic annulus; this prevented the inward folding of the pre-existing leaflets, which would have further compounded the reduced effective orifice area and PPM. Furthermore, the Acurate neo2 supports **commissural alignment**, a critical technical consideration chosen to lower the neo-skirt relative to the coronary ostia and preserve future coronary access. By prioritizing a device with favourable haemodynamic performance in narrow anatomies over other available options, the Heart Team optimized the residual effective orifice area while maintaining patent coronaries without the need for intracoronary protection. The successful 1-year follow-up confirms that this anatomical-matching strategy effectively mitigated the procedural risks associated with serial reinterventions.
