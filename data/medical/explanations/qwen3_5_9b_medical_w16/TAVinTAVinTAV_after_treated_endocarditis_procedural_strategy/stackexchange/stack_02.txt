Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] In the context of this case, what was the proposed mechanism by which the history of prosthetic valve endocarditis contributed to the subsequent valve dysfunction, aside from the active infection itself?

In this case, the history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) contributed to the subsequent valve dysfunction primarily through the mechanism of leaflet injury and subsequent fibrosis. According to the discussion, while the active infection was treated and resolved, the PVE episode caused direct leaflet injury that precipitated mechanical restriction and scarring of the valve leaflets. This fibrosis compounded the natural structural degeneration (calcification and fibrosis) inherent to the transcatheter bioprostheses. Consequently, the severe stenosis and regurgitation observed after the completion of antimicrobial therapy were driven not by recurrent bacteremia, but by the permanent mechanical impairment and fibrotic changes to the valve structure that persisted following the inflammatory insult. This highlights that even after successful treatment of the infection, the preceding PVE can leave residual structural damage that requires complex intervention to manage.
