Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] How does the 'high implantation' strategy utilize the previously implanted Evolut valve leaflets, and what hemodynamic complication does this specific technique aim to prevent?

In complex serial valve-in-valve procedures, such as this TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention, the 'high implantation' strategy is employed to optimize the geometry of the new valve relative to the previous prosthesis. Specifically, the strategy utilizes the leaflets of the previously implanted Evolut valve as a mechanical anchor for the new frame. In this case, the Acurate neo2 was deployed at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets, which allowed for the fixation of the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame.

This fixation serves to prevent the inward folding or bunching of the prior leaflets, which would otherwise reduce the available cross-sectional area for blood flow. Therefore, the primary hemodynamic complication this technique aims to prevent is a significant reduction in the effective orifice area (EOA), which would otherwise precipitate severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) and elevated transvalvular gradients. Additionally, this positioning minimizes the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, contributing to the preservation of coronary flow and clearance, but the critical hemodynamic benefit is the stabilization of the valve area through leaflet containment.
