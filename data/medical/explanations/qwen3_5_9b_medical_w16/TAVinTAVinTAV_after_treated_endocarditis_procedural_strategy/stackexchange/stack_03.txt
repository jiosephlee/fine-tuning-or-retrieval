Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] What specific preprocedural CT-derived anatomical parameters were critical for assessing the feasibility of implanting a third valve in this triple-stented anatomy, particularly regarding coronary clearance?

In this case of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, accurate preprocedural multidetector CT analysis was critical to assess the safe landing zone and ensure coronary clearance. The essential anatomical parameters included the **minimum lumen diameter of the innermost existing prosthesis** (specifically measured at 13.7 mm in the Evolut frame in this patient), which defines the radial expansion potential of the aortic root. Additionally, the evaluation focused on **coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance**, and the **interaction between the frames** of the previously implanted transcatheter valves.

These specific measurements guided the procedural strategy by confirming that a **high-implantation** of the third valve would preserve coronary flow. By analyzing the relationship between the anticipated neo-skirt and coronary ostia, the team verified that the third valve could be deployed without obstructing the ostia, allowing for the procedure to proceed without coronary protection despite the complex, triple-layered stent architecture.
