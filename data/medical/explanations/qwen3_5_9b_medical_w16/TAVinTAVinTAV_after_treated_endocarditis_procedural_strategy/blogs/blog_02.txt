Title: Blog about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

# More Than a Scan: Strategic CT Planning for Triple-Stent Anatomy

TAVI is no longer the "new standard"; for the growing population of survivors, it is part of a lifelong strategy. While we are well-versed in the concept of TAV-in-TAV to treat failed bioprostheses, the inverse—performing an intervention on a patient with a history of endocarditis—is rarer. It became more complex when we examine a case requiring a third valve implantation in a stented system that evolved over a decade.

Consider the case of a 77-year-old man requiring a third TAVI following two prior transcatheter procedures and a managed episode of endocarditis. By 2024, this patient needed a third valve. We are dealing with a Lotus from 2014, an Evolut from 2022, and a post-endocarditis deterioration that necessitated an Acurate neo2. The anatomical challenge isn't just fitting a balloon in a small aortic root; it's navigating a forest of metal struts and managing the increased risk of coronary obstruction. This blog post explores how pre-procedural multislice CT (MSCT) guided the decision-making to make a third intervention feasible in a triple-stent anatomy.

## The Triple-Valve Challenge

In high-risk patients, the "one-and-done" era of structural heart disease is ending. With improved durability in patients requiring repeat procedures, technical challenges are escalating. In this specific scenario, the patient presented with dyspnoea, a history of CAD, and paraortic con disease from the outset. The history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) caused by *Enterococcus faecalis* adds a layer of complexity to the structural valve failure.

Critically, the decision to intervene was not made because the infection failed to resolve, but because the valve structure physically collapsed after the infection was cleared. By January 2024, the calculated aortic valve area was down to $0.5 cm^{2}$ with a $49 mm Hg$ mean gradient. The team faced a choice between surgery or medication. Given the prior TAVIs and the patient's comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, prior GI bleeding), surgery carried a prohibitive EuroSCORE II of 27.36%. The question became: could we safely squeeze another valve in without blocking the coronary arteries?

## Pre-Procedure CT Planning

The pivotal moment in this case was the pre-procedural MSCT. This wasn't just a procedural readiness scan; it was a strategic dashboard. The balancing act in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV is one side of the equation: Avoiding coronary obstruction. Because we have three overlapping frames, the risk of the "neo-skirt" of the new valve encroaching on the coronaries is high if we are not meticulous.

The CT analysis revealed a minimum lumen diameter of $13.7 mm$ within the Evolut prosthesis. This specific measurement dictated feasibility, but we went deeper than just size. The MSCT team analyzed the sinus of Valsalva dimensions and the valve-to-coronary distance. Most importantly, they looked at the interaction between the frame of the Lotus, the Evolut, and the landing zone for the Panther Acurate neo2.

A critical decision derived from this planning was the implantation depth. We needed a strategy to maximize the Effective Orifice Area (EOA) while mitigating Patient-Prosthesis Mismatch (PPM). Committal alignment was not an option here; implanting based purely on radiopaque markers (like C-arm) wasn't enough when we were looking for non-violating fit against two prior devices. The CT suggested a high implantation, aligning the new struts with the Evolut leaflets rather than the stent frame. This prevented inward leaflet folding by the neo-stent, which is a known mechanism for PPM in serial TAVI. By preserving the leaflets against the frame of the previous valve, we allowed the Evolut to function as a bed for leaflets rather than a complete constriction. This CT-guided strategy allowed the team to deploy without the need for coronary protection devices—a considerable procedural simplification.

## Procedural Strategy and Implantation

Armed with the CT data, the procedure utilized left radial and left femoral access. Using a Safari guidewire (LM and FM visualized well), they pre-dilated the valve with a 22/40 mm balloon under rapid ventricular pacing. This ensures synchronized release of the heart, a standard but critical step in redo TAVI to prevent interference with restrictions in the valve frame. Aortography confirmed every step of coronary flow unobstruction before the neo-valve joined.

The Acurate neo2 (size S, self-expanding) was chosen for its supra-annular leaflet positioning and low frame height, matching the CT findings of limited landing zone available. Deployment had to be perfect to avoid paravalvular leak or coronary occlusion. Post-dilatation with a 20/40 mm balloon was performed off-pump to optimize expansion, specifically to address the mechanical restriction post-infection. The strategy relied heavily on the CT-assessed frame interaction, ensuring that the Acurate neo2's neo-skirt would not violate the coronary ostia height prescribed in the pre-procedural model.

## Outcomes and Follow-up

The immediate result post-procedure showed successful deployment without regurgitation (Grade 0), but the hemodynamics were initially concerning with a mean gradient of $67 mm Hg$ and an indexed AVA of $0.4 cm^{2}/m^{2}$. This is classic for PPM in serial reviews. However, serial echocardiography tracked a significant improvement over time. One month in, the mean gradient dropped to $27 mm Hg$ with an AVA of $1.1 cm^{2}$. Left ventricular remodeling was observed, and the patient remains asymptomatic at 1-year follow-up in 2025, with an EF of 65%.

This timeline highlights the importance of the 1-year mark as the first benchmark of durability. For the Heart Team, the evidence suggests that with careful CT planning and the right valve profile selection, a third transcatheter valve is a viable option even after endocarditis and multiple prior stents.

## Key Take-Home Messages

*   **TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV is becoming more common** as patient survival outpaces initial valve design durability, particularly in high-risk patient profiles.
*   **Endocarditis history does not contraindicate TAVI**, provided the infection is fully treated and no active vegetations remain on TEE or CT.
*   **Multi-slice CT (MSCT) is mandatory**, not optional, for third-generation indications to accurately assess the minimum lumen diameter ($13.7 mm$ in this case) and plan commissural alignment.
*   **High implantation is a strategic choice** in serial procedures to align the new valve leaflets with the prior frame, preserving Effective Orifice Area (EOA) and preventing PPM.
*   **Coronary preservation must be prioritized**, as overlapping stent frames (Lotus, Evolut, Acurate in this case) significantly reduce the safety margin for coronary ostial access.

Now, you remember that in structural heart disease, a scan is never just a picture; it's the blueprint for the reconstruction. Stay sharp on your volumetric analysis, because as we treat younger and longer-lived populations, "Triple-Stent" is a finite anatomy but an increasingly common reality.
