Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why is allergen-specific skin testing contraindicated during the acute recovery phase of a severe anaphylactic reaction?

Allergen-specific skin testing is contraindicated during the acute recovery phase of severe anaphylaxis because it risks precipitating a recurrence of the systemic reaction through re-exposure. In the case report by Schneider et al., this contraindication was a principal factor in management. Given the patient's multiphasic course with four recurrent episodes of shock, the authors noted that skin testing was avoided "as risking renewed serious anaphylaxis... was not deemed proportional."

The clinical rationale relies on the heightened mast cell reactivity observed in the immediate post-reactive period. In this specific case, the difficulty was compounded by a suspected underlying systemic mastocytosis, which predisposes patients to excessive mediator release upon exposure. The management strategy prioritizes safety over diagnostic certainty during this window, recognizing that strict allergen avoidance is safely achievable during the observation period without the procedural risk of skin testing. Diagnostic confirmation can be deferred until the patient has remained asymptomatic for a sufficient period to confirm resolution of the multiphasic instability.
