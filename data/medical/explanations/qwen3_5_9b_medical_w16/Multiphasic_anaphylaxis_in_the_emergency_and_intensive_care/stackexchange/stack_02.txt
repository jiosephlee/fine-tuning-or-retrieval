Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] Based on current evidence, what is the role of corticosteroids in preventing biphasic or recurrent anaphylaxis?

**Corticosteroids Versus Epinephrine in Biphasic Anaphylaxis**

The cornerstone of anaphylaxis management remains the prompt administration of epinephrine and clinical observation; corticosteroids are not indicated for the prevention of biphasic or recurrent reactions.

While steroid administration is historically ingrained in our practice—our patient in this report received both IV prednisone and dexamethasone following the first recurrence of anaphylaxis based on this traditional belief—the evidence does not support this approach. A 2017 systematic review of 22 studies examining this specific interaction found that 21 studies demonstrated no evidence that corticosteroids reduce the risk of biphasic anaphylaxis, with only one study providing tentative support.

Furthermore, there are potential harms to consider. Recent data suggest a risk of adverse events associated with steroid prophylaxis; specifically, a 2019 study indicated an increased likelihood of ICU or ward admission following prehospital corticosteroid treatment. Consequently, most contemporary guidelines advise against routine corticosteroid administration in this setting.

Despite this, practice remains variable. Our patient was treated per unit protocols that still reflect older practices, yet she ultimately avoided further relapse, likely the result of continued epinephrine availability and monitoring rather than steroid prophylaxis. In summary, do not rely on corticosteroids to prevent biphasic reactions; the risk-benefit ratio favors observation and resuscitative support with epinephrine alone.
