Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] In an adult patient weighing over 50 kg, how should the choice between 0.3 mg and 0.5 mg epinephrine auto-injectors be managed to avoid underdosing?

In adults weighing over 50 kg, clinicians should prioritize the use of 0.5 mg epinephrine auto-injectors over 0.3 mg devices to prevent underdosing and recurrence of anaphylaxis. Guidelines recommend a dose of 0.01 mg/kg (capped at 0.5 mg), making the 0.5 mg dose the standard for patients above this weight threshold. This recommendation is critical, as underdosing with a 0.3 mg device is a common source of treatable therapeutic failure.

The case presented highlights this management principle: a patient weighing 104 kg initially received 0.3 mg auto-injectors, which provided transient symptom relief but was associated with recurrent anaphylactic episodes. While a good initial response was observed, the patient required four total intramuscular doses, with the latter two doses converted to individually prepared 0.5 mg injections to resolve symptoms effectively. The discussion underscores that while 0.3 mg devices remain widely available, they lead to critical underdosing in adults over 50 kg. Notably, a 2025 retrospective study cited in this report demonstrated that patients receiving 0.3 mg epinephrine required significantly more frequent escalation of care compared to those receiving 0.5 mg. Therefore, despite logistical barriers or historical reliance on 0.3 mg devices, adherence to the 0.5 mg standard for patients exceeding 50 kg is essential to maximize first-dose effectiveness and reduce the risk of biphasic or multiphasic relapse.
