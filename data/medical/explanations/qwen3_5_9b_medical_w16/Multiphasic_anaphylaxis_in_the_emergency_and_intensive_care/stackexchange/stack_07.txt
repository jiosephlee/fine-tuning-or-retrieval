Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the clinical guidelines for the duration of observation required after the resolution of anaphylaxis symptoms to anticipate biphasic recurrence?

Current clinical counseling on post-anaphylaxis observation remains a challenge, as there is no universally agreed-upon consensus for the duration of admission required after symptom resolution. Traditional guidelines and common clinical practice recommend a minimum observation period of 4 to 6 hours following complete clinical response to epinephrine. However, the pathophysiology of biphasic recurrence—defined as a recurrence of symptoms within 24 hours of resolution without re-exposure to the allergen—warrants a more nuanced approach, particularly given that approximately half of biphasic reactions can occur between 6 and 12 hours post-event.

In the case presented by Schneider et al., the limited utility of the standard 4- to 6-hour window is illustrated by the patient’s multiphasic course. This patient presented with hypotension and recurrent anaphylactic shock requiring multiple intramuscular epinephrine doses prior to stabilization. Although the initial presentation and response to treatment initially suggested resolution, the administration of four separate epinephrine doses and the development of recurrent shock during the first 12 hours complicated the risk profile. In this scenario, the medical team extended the monitoring period beyond the traditional window; the patient was safely transferred back to a standard care ward only after approximately 24 hours without recurrent symptoms.

Consequently, while a risk-stratified approach formally recommended by emerging guidelines (such as the 2021 UK guideline) is advocated, the practical application must rely on identifying high-risk features. Patients with severe initial presentations, hypotension, or those requiring more than one dose of epinephrine are at significantly higher risk for biphasic or multiphasic recurrence. In these cases, clinical judgment should support prolonged observation periods, often extending up to 24 hours, to ensure patient safety while balancing healthcare resources. Corticosteroid administration remains controversial and is not recommended for preventing recurrence, further placing the burden of monitoring on the duration of observation and the acuity of the clinical sequelae.
