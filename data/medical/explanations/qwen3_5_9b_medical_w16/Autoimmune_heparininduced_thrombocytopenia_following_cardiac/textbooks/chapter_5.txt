Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

Chapter 5: ## Resolution of the Case and Clinical Takeaways

The patient’s clinical trajectory concluded with a favorable outcome, demonstrating the potential for recovery despite the severity of the autoimmune response. By postoperative day 62, the platelet count had normalized to 155×10⁹/L and was maintained at 202×10⁹/L at follow-up, coinciding with the complete resolution of neurological abnormalities associated with the parietal stroke. This recovery was achieved through a prolonged period of anticoagulation with fondaparinux (administered until day 65) before the reintroduction of oral apixaban for long-term management of atrial fibrillation. The durability of this recovery, without evidence of recurrent thrombosis or bleeding complications, highlights that while the initial aHIT presentation was severe, the condition did not mandate indefinite indefinite treatment provided the immune response eventually waned. The normalization of platelet counts also serves as a marker that the immune system has cleared the heparin-antibody interaction, allowing for the reintroduction of long-term oral anticoagulation previously suspended due to stroke risk.

Following clinical recovery, the patient’s serological profile underwent a distinct evolution, providing critical evidence for the dynamic nature of HIT antibodies over time. While earlier SRA results during the nadir phase displayed partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade consistent with severe aHIT, the last blood sample obtained at 124 days post-injury showed a profile characteristic of predominantly heparin-dependent HIT antibodies, with significantly reduced platelet-activating properties in the absence of heparin. This seroconversion underscores a key diagnostic principle: a patient’s initial serology does not necessarily predetermine the long-term serological phenotype, and repeating antibody testing during recovery may be required if the clinical picture shifts or if new surgical exposure to heparin occurs. This evolution confirms that the initial "atypical" profile was likely a transient state of heightened immune activation that normalized over the course of months, rather than a permanent autoimmune defect.

From a management perspective, the switch from argatroban to fondaparinux illustrates the necessity of monitoring indirect markers of hypercoagulability in aHIT cases. Although the patient’s platelet count began to rise modestly, the rising d-dimer levels and the complicated fibrinogen decline indicated ongoing consumptive coagulopathy despite the direct thrombin inhibitor. Consequently, the treating team escalated to fondaparinux, which ultimately provided better control, evidenced by the normalization of coagulation parameters and the cessation of schistocytosis. This clinical decision reinforces that the resolution of thrombocytopenia alone is an inadequate marker of therapeutic success in aHIT; clinicians must monitor for evidence of volthrombosis through fibrinogen and d-dimer trends, particularly in patients with profound thrombocytopenia.

The final lesson from this case centers on the nuances of preoperative heparin exposure and the recognition of atypical HIT phenotypes in high-risk surgical populations. The unusually early onset of thrombocytopenia, preceding day 5 post-cardiac surgery, was directly traced to the intra-procedural heparin exposure during the preoperative catheterization. This demonstrates that the "5-day" window rule for HIT is a guideline rather than an absolute cutoff, and clinicians must account for heparin exposure within days prior to major surgery, including catheterizations. Furthermore, the patient’s phenotype—marked by a platelet nadir of 13×10⁹/L and resistance to rapid platelet recovery with IVIg—aligns with the spectrum of autoimmune HIT (aHIT). For board evaluations or clinical reasoning, this case teaches that severe thrombocytopenia unremittent despite standard IVIg therapy, coupled with stroke or thrombosis, should prompt a re-evaluation for aHIT rather than classic HIT. Finally, the case highlights a need for careful planning in cardiac surgery protocols, advising that preoperative heparin exposure necessitates heightened vigilance for thrombocytopenia in the immediate early postoperative period, potentially influencing the choice of prophylactic agents or the timing of immunosuppressive interventions.
