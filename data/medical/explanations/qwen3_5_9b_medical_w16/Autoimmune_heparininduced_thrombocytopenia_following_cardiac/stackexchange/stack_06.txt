Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] What is the mechanism of action for fondaparinux in the context of HIT, and why was supplemental antithrombin concentrate required before the administration of therapeutic fondaparinux in this case?

Fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide that functions as an indirect Factor Xa inhibitor; it exerts its anticoagulant activity exclusively by binding to and potentiating endogenous antithrombin (AT). Unlike direct thrombin inhibitors, such as argatroban, fondaparinux has no direct activity against thrombin and requires sufficient circulating antithrombin levels to function. In patients with autoimmune HIT, particularly those with severe thromboinflammation and consumptive coagulopathy—as evidenced in this case by the falling fibrinogen and hypercoagulability during preceding argatroban therapy—antithrombin levels can be depleted.

When the patient was switched from argatroban to therapeutic fondaparinux on postoperative day 14, the pre-infusion AT level was only mildly reduced at 0.70 U/mL (reference range 0.80–1.40 U/mL). However, given the critical dependence of fondaparinux on AT for efficacy, supplemental antithrombin concentrate (1000 U) was administered prior to therapy. This ensured adequate circulating levels of antithrombin to support the anticoagulant effect, securing protection against further thrombosis in a patient with severe, refractory antithrombin consumption.
