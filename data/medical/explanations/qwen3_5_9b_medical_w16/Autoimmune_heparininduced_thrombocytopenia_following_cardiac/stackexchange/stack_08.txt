Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] How did the serial monitoring of fibrinogen levels (falling) and D-dimer levels (rising) inform the medical team regarding the efficacy of anticoagulation therapy and the persistence of hypercoagulability?

In this case, serial monitoring of fibrinogen and D-dimer levels provided critical insight into the persistence of a consumptive coagulopathy that was not adequately controlled by initial anticoagulation.

Essentially, these laboratory trends acted as surrogates for thrombotic burden. In the context of this patient’s autoimmune HIT (aHIT), postoperative fibrinogen is typically elevated as an acute-phase reactant. However, the progressive decrease in fibrinogen from Postoperative Day (POD) 6 through POD 17, coupled with peak D-dimer levels around POD 12, indicated that the rate of fibrinogen consumption from occult thrombosis exceeded the rate of acute-phase synthesis. Concurrently, the rising D-dimer suggests ongoing clot formation and breakdown.

Although the platelet count remained profoundly low throughout, the coagulation panel signaled that the thrombotic drive persisted despite therapeutic-dose argatroban. This trajectory, corroborated by the appearance of schistocytes, led the treating team to conclude that argatroban was providing "insufficient control of HIT-associated hypercoagulability." Consequently, the medical team switched the patient to therapeutic-dose fondaparinux on POD 14 to address the ongoing active consumption.

In summary, these serial markers confirmed that despite initial therapeutic intervention, the pro-thrombotic state associated with aHIT had not resolved; the only way to down-trend these aggressive consumptive markers was to escalate anticoagulation strategy.
