Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] In the setting of diagnostic uncertainty between deep neck space infection and angioedema, what single laboratory finding provided the most immediate evidence to reevaluate the clinical diagnosis and initiate immunosuppression?

In this case, the pivotal laboratory finding that reoriented the clinical diagnosis from deep neck space infection or idiopathic angioedema to dermatomyositis was a markedly elevated serum creatine kinase (CK) level. Specifically, the level had risen to 16,184 U/L, providing immediate evidence of significant muscle breakdown.

While angioedema and deep neck space infections are inflammatory processes, they do not typically cause the massive release of muscle enzymes seen in severe myopathy. In contrast, dermatomyositis is an inflammatory myopathy characterized by direct inflammation and necrosis of skeletal muscle fibers. This specific enzyme profile distinguished the subcutaneous "angioedema" from the underlying pathology of myositis causing the tissue swelling. This laboratory data justified the shift to a provisional diagnosis of dermatomyositis, prompting the initiation of aggressive immunosuppression, including intravenous methylprednisolone, to prevent airway compromise and further muscle damage.

In clinical practice, when a patient presents with unexplained neck swelling and atypical physical signs, such as proximal weakness or cutaneous changes, a creatine kinase level should be prioritized. A significantly elevated CK, alongside subtle nailfold changes and rash, is a critical "red flag" that should trigger rheumatologic evaluation for inflammatory myopathy over medical management for primary otolaryngologic pathology.
