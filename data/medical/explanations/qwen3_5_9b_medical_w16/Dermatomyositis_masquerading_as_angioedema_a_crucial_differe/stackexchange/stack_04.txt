Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] How does the pathophysiologic mechanism of dysphagia in dermatomyositis manifest clinically, and why is the barium swallow study critical in confirming the diagnosis and guiding nutritional management?

**Answer**

In dermatomyositis, dysphagia typically manifests as a direct consequence of inflammatory myositis affecting the skeletal muscles involved in swallowing, specifically the pharyngeal and laryngeal musculature. In this case, despite the initial focus on skin changes and neck swelling, the underlying pathophysiology was an autoimmune inflammatory process involving the head and neck muscles, evidenced by the elevated creatine kinase and inflammatory muscle changes on PET and biopsy. This muscle inflammation compromises the coordinated closure of the glottis and propulsion of the bolus, leading to aspiration. Clinically, this patient demonstrated not only proximal muscle weakness but also indirect signs of bulbar involvement, such as increased secretions and coughing during nasendoscopy, which were precursors to the aspiration pneumonitis and subsequent severe pharyngeal dysphagia.

The video fluoroscopic (barium) swallow study is critical in this context for two principal reasons: confirming the functional diagnosis of the swallowing disorder and guiding nutritional management. While the diagnosis of dermatomyositis itself was established via myositis serology (anti-SAE1 antibodies) and biopsy, the barium swallow provided the objective evidence required to confirm **oropharyngeal dysphagia**. In this patient, the study revealed severe pyriform fossae residue and post-swallow penetration of fluid into the vocal cords, confirming that the hydro-dynamics of the swallow were compromised rather than the structural airway alone being obstructed by the neck swelling.

This objective visualization was decisive for nutritional management. Recognizing the aspiration risk identified during the study, the team appropriately initiated enteral feeding via a nasogastric tube at the time of discharge rather than tolerating oral intake, thereby preventing further aspiration injury while the immunosuppressive therapy (corticosteroids, IVIG, and rituximab took effect) allowed for muscle recovery. Ultimately, the barium swallow study validated the need for temporary enteral access, which was safely discontinued once muscle strength and swallowing coordination recovered during the follow-up period.

**Clinical Pearl:** In patients presenting with unexplained neck swelling and dysphagia, always consider inflammatory myopathy, particularly when standard infectious or allergic etiologies are excluded. While serology and biopsy confirm the systemic etiology, the barium swallow is the gold standard for characterizing the swallow mechanism and mitigating aspiration risk through appropriate feeding tube placement.
