Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] According to current International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, why was this patient classified as high-risk for malignancy despite the absence of clinically evident tumors at presentation?

This patient was classified as high-risk for malignancy according to the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) criteria because the guidelines stratify risk based on specific serological and clinical features, not solely on the absence of radiological findings. 

While no malignancy was identified on his initial CT, PET scan, or tumor markers, his case met two of the IMACS-defined high-risk factors: the confirmed diagnosis of dermatomyositis and the presence of clinically significant dysphagia. He also presented with two intermediate risk factors: male sex and anti-SAE1 antibody positivity. 

In the IMACS framework, the accumulation of these risk factors dictates the screening protocol rather than the initial imaging appearance. Therefore, the patient was deemed high-risk, necessitating comprehensive baseline surveillance (including upper and lower GI endoscopy) to exclude occult paraneoplastic disease. This highlights a critical learning point for residents: in dermatomyositis, the risk of associated malignancy is driven by the immunophenotype (antibody profile) and specific clinical phenotypes (like dysphagia) requiring rigorous screening even when constitutional symptoms and imaging are initially reassuring.
