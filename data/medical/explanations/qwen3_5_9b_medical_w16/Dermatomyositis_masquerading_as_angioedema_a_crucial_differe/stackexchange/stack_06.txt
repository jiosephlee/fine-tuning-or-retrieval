Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] Why was methotrexate contraindicated or avoided in the treatment regimen of this patient, specifically considering the patient's baseline pulmonary status as confirmed by imaging and lung function tests?

Methotrexate was avoided in this patient primarily due to the well-documented association with interstitial lung disease (ILD) and pulmonary fibrosis, given the patient's underlying pulmonary compromise. Although the initial chest CT did not show frank pulmonary fibrosis, the patient demonstrated abnormal pulmonary function tests with a restrictive pattern and radiographic evidence of pulmonary nodules and consolidation.

Furthermore, this patient’s specific serology (anti-SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis) is associated with a high prevalence of ILD, reported at approximately 43% in cohort studies. In patients with inflammatory myopathies who already exhibit baseline pulmonary abnormality, the addition of methotrexate poses a significant risk of inducing methotrexate-induced pneumonitis or exacerbating existing lung pathology. Therefore, the treatment regimen prioritized lung-sparing immunosuppressants, specifically mycophenolate mofetil, which allows for effective disease control while minimizing the risk of further respiratory injury. This decision underscores the necessity of evaluating baseline pulmonary function and imaging in inflammatory myopathies before selecting steroid-sparing agents with potential pulmonary toxicity.
