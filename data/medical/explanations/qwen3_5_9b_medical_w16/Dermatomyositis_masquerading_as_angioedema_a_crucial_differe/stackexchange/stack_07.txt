Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] This patient received a multi-modal immunosuppressive regimen including corticosteroids, mycophenolate mofetil, intravenous immunoglobulin, and rituximab. Under what clinical circumstances would such an aggressive combination be justified over standard monotherapy?

This aggressive, multi-modal immunosuppressive regimen is justified in cases of dermatomyositis where there is **organ-threatening involvement, rapid functional decline, or a high-risk serological phenotype that suggests a difficult-to-treat course.**

In the context of this case report, the decision to deploy corticosteroids, mycophenolate mofetil, IVIG, and rituximab was driven by three specific clinical factors:

1.  **Airway and Dysphagia Risk:** The patient presented with cervical myositis mimicking angioedema, carrying a direct threat to the airway. Crucially, he had severe dysphagia and aspiration pneumonia. In severe myositis affecting bulbar function or the airway muscles, standard monotherapy (e.g., steroids alone) may not suppress inflammation rapidly enough or sufficiently to prevent long-term damage. The prompt course of IV methylprednisolone addresses the acute crisis, while IVIG and rituximab provide rapid and broad immunomodulation for the autoimmune storm.
2.  **Antibody Phenotype:** The patient tested positive for anti-SAE1 antibodies. Literature reviews of anti-SAE1 dermatomyositis consistently associate this profile with severe functional impairment, including a high burden of dysphagia (reported in 46% in the review) and interstitial lung disease (43%). This phenotype often requires more than standard therapy to achieve durable remission.
3.  **Safety and Steroid-Sparing Necessity:** The clinicians avoided methotrexate due to the patient's existing ILD risk (restrictive pattern on PFTs) and the known association of methotrexate with pulmonary fibrosis. Conversely, IVIG was favored as it is effective for cutaneous and myopathic disease subtypes with lymphoproliferative tendencies and has a high therapeutic index in the acute setting.

**The Clinical Pearl:**
Do not dismiss dermatomyositis in patients presenting with unexplained neck swelling or dysphagia. When the presentation mimics angioedema but is accompanied by elevated creatine kinase and distinct cutaneous signs (e.g., nailfold hemorrhages), escalate treatment immediately from corticosteroids alone to a multi-modal regimen involving B-cell depletion (rituximab) or cytokine modulation (IVIG), particularly if high-risk myositis antibodies (anti-SAE1, anti-MDA5) or interstitial lung disease are present. This "salami" approach avoids single-agent toxicity (like fibrosis from methotrexate) while maximizing speed of control in life-threatening scenarios.

*Reference: Chan K, et al. Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook. CMAJ. 2024.*
