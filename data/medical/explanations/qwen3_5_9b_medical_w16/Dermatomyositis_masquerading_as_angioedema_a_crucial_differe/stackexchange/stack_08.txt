Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the function of the small ubiquitin-like modifier (SUMO) activating enzyme (SAE1) targeted by the autoantibody in this case, and what clinical phenotype is typically associated with anti-SAE1 positive dermatomyositis?

Anti-SAE1 antibodies target the small ubiquitin-like modifier activating enzyme subunit 1, a protein structurally similar to ubiquitin that is responsible for the post-translational modification of target proteins via sumoylation. While this patient notably presented with the unusual, pseudo-angioedematous swelling of the neck, the clinical phenotype associated with SAE1-positive dermatomyositis is classically characterized by specific cutaneous findings, including heliotrope rash and Gottron’s papules. This antibody profile is further defined by a significant burden of extra-muscular disease and malignancy risk; the literature cited in the case indicates that 46% of SAE1-positive patients develop dysphagia, 43% develop interstitial lung disease, and 18% are diagnosed with malignancy. Recognizing this association in a patient with myopathy or unexplained facial/neck oedema is critical, as it necessitates aggressive immunosuppressive therapy to prevent aspiration and confirms the high need for comprehensive cancer surveillance.
