Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] What specific histopathological and immunohistochemical features on muscle biopsy differentiate dermatomyositis from polymyositis, as seen in this patient's diagnosis?

In this patient, the muscle biopsy distinguished dermatomyositis from polymyositis through two specific pathological features: **perifascicular necrosis** and **membrane attack complex (C5b-9) deposition**.

The histopathology demonstrated foci of necrotic, vacuolated, and regenerating myofibres located specifically in a **perifascicular distribution**. While polymyositis typically presents with endomysial inflammation and T-cell invasion of non-necrotic fibers, the perifascicular pattern is the hallmark of dermatomyositis. The immunohistochemistry further confirmed this diagnosis by revealing **complement component 5b-9 (C5b-9) deposition in the perifascicular endomysial capillaries**. This finding reflects the microangiopathic pathophysiology of dermatomyositis—complement-mediated vascular injury—whereas polymyositis lacks this specific vascular deposition pattern.

These features, combined with the patient's clinical findings of skin changes (Holster sign, periungual erythema) and elevated creatine kinase, confirmed the diagnosis of dermatomyositis in this case.
