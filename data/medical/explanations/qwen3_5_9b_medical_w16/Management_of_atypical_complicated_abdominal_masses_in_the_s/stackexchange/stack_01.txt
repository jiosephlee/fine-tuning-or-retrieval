Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Morning Report] Summarize the primary diagnostic conflict presented by large abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy and the key factors that informed the decision to intervene surgically.

The primary diagnostic conflict in this clinical scenario centers on distinguishing benign uterine leiomyomas with cystic degeneration from complex ovarian neoplasms, a distinction that is often obscured by physiological hormonal fluctuations and overlapping imaging features in the second trimester. In this case, ultrasound and MRI demonstrated large, cystic masses with solid components, while a CA-125 level of 41.8 U/mL—elevated above the physiological median for gestational age while supporting malignancy profiles—created diagnostic ambiguity between endometriosis and degenerating myomas.

The decision to intervene surgically at 21 weeks was driven not by presumptive malignancy, but by acute maternal decompensation and specific exploratory findings. The decision relied on three key factors: the presence of severe, mechanical pain and restrictive dyspnea caused by diaphragmatic compression; signs of infection and fever suggesting possible torsion or necrosis; and the radiological indication of a thick-walled, highly vascularized mass. During the laparotomy, intraoperative assessment confirmed the upper mass was a twisted pedunculated leiomyoma at imminent risk of ischemic damage, validating the procedure as necessary to secure a definitive diagnosis and prevent catastrophic complications. This approach prioritized maternal and fetal safety by removing the compromised tissue while preserving the uterus for a subsequent term delivery.
