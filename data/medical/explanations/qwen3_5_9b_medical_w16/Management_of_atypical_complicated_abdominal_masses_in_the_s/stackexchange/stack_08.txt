Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] How did the size, number, and anatomical localization of the uterine masses influence the final decision regarding the choice of vaginal delivery versus C-section?

In this case, the decision to proceed with an elective cesarean delivery rather than vaginal birth was primarily dictated by the anatomical localization and size of the retained caudal mass. Although the patient presented with two large uterine masses, the clinical team chose to leave the lower lesion in situ to minimize obstetric risk. This caudal mass, measuring approximately 9 to 10 cm, was localized as a fixed, posterior intramural-subserosal myoma occupying the pouch of Douglas.

The final mode of delivery was determined by the mechanical impact of this specific lesion on the birth canal. As noted in the report, this mass "would have represented a huge obstacle for the fetal head progression during the expulsive phase of labour." Its posterior-intramural location and substantial size prevented adequate engagement and descent of the fetus, rendering vaginal delivery unsafe. This outcome underscores a broader management principle: when multiple or large uterine masses are persistently present and fixed in a position that obstructs the pelvic inlet or canal, cesarean delivery is the necessary intervention to prevent dystocia and uterine rupture.
