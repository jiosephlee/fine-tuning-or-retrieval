Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the physiological fluctuation of serum CA-125 levels across the three trimesters of pregnancy and explain why a second-trimester elevation does not confirm malignancy.

Regarding the fluctuation of CA-125 during pregnancy, serum levels generally follow a biphasic physiological curve rather than a static baseline. As detailed in the background and discussion sections of the case, CA-125 typically rises during the first trimester, reaching a median value of approximately 23 U/mL. It subsequently declines to a nadir in the second trimester, where the median level is approximately 14.0 U/mL, before rising again during the third trimester to a median of roughly 21 U/mL.

In the context of our case, the patient presented in the second trimester (21 weeks) with a CA-125 level of 41.8 U/mL. While this value exceeded the physiological second-trimester median of 14.0 U/mL and surpassed the standard ovarian cancer threshold of 35 U/mL, it did not indicate malignancy. The pathology report confirmed benign uterine leiomyomas with cystic degeneration and ischaemic necrosis rather than cancer. This highlights a critical teaching point: while pregnancy itself alters CA-125 dynamics, benign uterine pathology—including leiomyomas, endometriosis, and pelvic inflammation—can cause significant elevation independent of the trimester-specific physiological curve. Therefore, an isolated elevation in the second trimester does not confirm malignancy. Management should prioritize serial evaluation, safe high-accuracy imaging (such as non-contrast MRI), and clinical correlation over reliance on a single tumor marker value, as overtreatment based solely on serology carrying risks to the pregnancy was avoided in this instance.
