Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] Specifically, how does a mutation in the PARN gene impair the processing of telomerase RNA component (TERC) and subsequent telomere maintenance?

The fundamental mechanism by which a *PARN* mutation impairs telomere maintenance lies in the essential role PARN plays in the RNA processing of the telomerase RNA component (TERC). PARN encodes a highly conserved exoribonuclease that regulates gene expression by shortening mRNA poly(A) tail length through deadenylation.

Specifically, as noted in the discussion of this case, TERC undergoes post-transcriptional modification where it acquires oligo(A) tails that target the nuclear RNA for degradation. Functional PARN is required to remove these tails, a process necessary for the proper 3'-prime-end maturation of the TERC. When PARN function is compromised—whether through biallelic mutations causing autosomal recessive dyskeratosis congenita or the monoallelic variant in this patient (c.613T>C; p.Cys205Arg)—TERC cannot be correctly processed. This results in an insufficiency of functional telomerase complex (TERT and TERC), impeding the enzyme’s ability to maintain telomere length.

The consequence of this specific biochemical defect is the progressive attrition of telomeres in dividing cells. As described in the background and case presentation, this premature telomere shortening triggers cellular senescence or apoptosis in tissues with high turnover, such as the bone marrow, lung, and liver. This pathophysiology explains the patient's multisystem presentation of pancytopenia, pulmonary fibrosis, and hepatic fibrosis, distinguishing the genetic mechanism from her acquired vitamin B12 deficiency and highlighting the clinical imperative to identify telomere biology disorders in young adults with multisystem organ failure or premature aging.
