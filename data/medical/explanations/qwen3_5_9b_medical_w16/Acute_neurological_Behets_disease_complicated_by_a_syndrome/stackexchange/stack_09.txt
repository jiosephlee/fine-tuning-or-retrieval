Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] What steps were taken in the initial differential diagnosis to rule out infectious encephalitis and neurosarcoidosis prior to the final diagnosis?

**Teaching Point: Dianosing Acute Neuro-Behçet’s Disease**

The diagnostic challenge in this case centered on distinguishing acute Behçet’s disease from infectious encephalitis and neurosarcoidosis, given the patient’s fever, headache, and aseptic meningitis. Our approach relied on a hierarchy of exclusion based on clinical, radiological, and laboratory data.

First, we prioritized ruling out infectious etiologies common in meningoencephalitis. Bacterial meningitis was excluded by the absence of neutrophilic predominance in the CSF and negative bacterial and acid-fast cultures. Viral meningoencephalitis, the most likely mimic, was further ruled out via negative CSF analysis for HSV, VZV, and cryptococcal antigen.

Second, we evaluated neurosarcoidosis, which can present with normal MRI findings in early stages. To exclude this diagnosis, we assessed serum biomarkers typically associated with granulomatous inflammation. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) and lysozyme levels were within normal limits. Additionally, while raised soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) can suggest malignancy, it is non-specific in inflammation and did not support lymphoproliferative disease in the absence of cytological evidence.

Once these mimics were excluded, we focused on the patient’s mucocutaneous etiology. While the initial presentation lacked genital ulcers, this finding emerged on day 5 of admission. Combined with oral aphthae, folliculitis-like rashes, and fever, the patient met the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease. This confirmed acute neuro-Behçet’s disease, explaining the concurrent syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone (SIADH). This case underscores that while infections are always the priority in a febrile patient with head-of-hospital, a high index of suspicion for autoinflammatory disorders may emerge once pathogen workup is thorough but negative.
