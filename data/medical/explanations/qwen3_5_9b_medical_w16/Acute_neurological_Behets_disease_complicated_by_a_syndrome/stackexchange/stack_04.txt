Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Diagnostic] Before confirming a diagnosis of SIADH as the cause of hyponatremia, which endocrine conditions must be excluded to rule out pseudo-hyponatremia?

Thank you for this important question. First, it is vital to correct a common physiological misconception: **endocrine conditions do not cause pseudo-hyponatremia.**

Pseudo-hyponatremia is an analytical artifact caused by severe hyperlipidemia or hyperproteinemia, which dilutes the plasma water fraction and artificially lowers the measured sodium concentration while maintaining normal serum osmolality. To rule out pseudo-hyponatremia, one must rely on direct measurement of serum osmolality (Kakehi et al. found a low serum osmolality of 267 mOsm/kg, confirming true hypotonic hyponatremia) rather than endocrine testing.

However, to confidently diagnose SIADH, one must exclude endocrine causes of **true** hypotonic hyponatremia, specifically **primary hypothyroidism and secondary adrenal insufficiency**. Both conditions significantly impair free water excretion and can mimic SIADH physiologically. As demonstrated in the Kakehi et al. case, the diagnostic workflow confirmed SIADH by first establishing low serum osmolality and then systematically excluding these endocrine mimics. In this patient, thyroid-stimulating hormone (1.11 mIU/L) and serum cortisol (42 µg/dL) were both within normal limits, effectively ruling out hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency.

**Key Clinical Teaching Point:** Before confirming SIADH, you must (1) verify low serum osmolality to exclude pseudo-hyponatremia via lipids/proteins, and (2) exclude hypothyroidism (via TSH and Free T4) and adrenal insufficiency (via morning cortisol or ACTH stimulation) to ensure the hyponatremia is isolated to dysregulated ADH release. In the setting of CNS inflammation or Behçet’s disease, SIADH often stems from central dysregulation rather than peripheral endocrine failure, as evidenced by the normal endocrine panel in this case.
