Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] How does the proinflammatory cytokine interleukin-6 (IL-6) mediate the release of antidiuretic hormone in the setting of acute neuro-Behçet's disease?

In this case of concurrent Behçet’s disease (BD) and acute neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD), the release of antidiuretic hormone (ADH) leading to the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) is mediated by a cytokine storm originating from CNS inflammation, specifically involving interleukin-6 (IL-6).

As detailed in the discussion, the pathophysiology relies on the concept that aberrant monocyte activation within the central nervous system during acute neuro-BD leads to spontaneous IL-6 production. While serum or CSF levels of IL-6 were not directly measured in this specific patient, the markedly elevated C-reactive protein serves as a surrogate marker for this underlying inflammatory response. Literature cited in the case indicates that IL-6, a proinflammatory cytokine frequently elevated in neuroinflammatory disorders, is associated with increased ADH release. This cytokine-driven dysregulation disrupts the normal osmoregulatory control, resulting in the inappropriate ADH secretion observed in this patient.

The therapeutic response supports this mechanistic pathway. The patient’s hyponatremia and neurological symptoms resolved with colchicine monotherapy. This is consistent with colchicine’s mechanism of inhibiting neutrophil migration and suppressing cytokine production, including IL-1β and IL-6. The simultaneous normalization of serum sodium and ADH levels suggests that suppressing this proinflammatory IL-6 pathway effectively reduced the central drive for inappropriate ADH secretion. Clinicians should recognize that in patients with neuro-BD presenting with hyponatremia, even in the absence of visible brain lesions on MRI, cytokine-mediated SIADH should be suspected and managed judiciously.
