Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In the context of acute neuro-Behçet's disease, how should a normal brain MRI without hypothalamic involvement be weighed against clinical findings to support the diagnosis?

A normal brain MRI, particularly without hypothalamic involvement, should not preclude the diagnosis of acute neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD). While parenchymal lesions, especially involving the brainstem or hypothalamus, are characteristic, acute neuro-BD frequently presents as aseptic meningitis without visible inflammatory lesions on structural imaging, particularly in the early phase. As demonstrated in this case, the patient presented with headache, fever, and hyponatraemia, yet the brain MRI and CT scan revealed no abnormalities involving the hypothalamus or other regions.

In this context, the diagnosis relies on a triad of clinical findings: persistent mucocutaneous manifestations, cerebrospinal fluid evidence of aseptic meningitis, and the exclusion of mimics. This patient met the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (definite status via a score of 5, driven by oral aphthae and genital ulcers) and demonstrated cerebrospinal fluid findings consistent with CNS inflammation, specifically mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein. The resolution of symptoms with colchicine monotherapy further validated the inflammatory etiology.

Furthermore, the presence of the syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone (SIADH) in the absence of a radiological hypothalamic lesion underscores that functional CNS inflammation regarding hypothalamic regulation can precede or exist independently of structural imaging abnormalities. Clinicians must balance the low yield of negative imaging against high-suspicion clinical vignettes—specifically the combination of mucocutaneous findings with CNS symptoms and hyponatraemia—to avoid delaying diagnosis of neuro-BD solely based on normal MRI findings.
