Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Management] Why is the interpretation of mildly elevated soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) levels insufficient to definitively distinguish between CNS malignancy and neuro-inflammatory conditions?

Soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) is a biomarker of T-cell activation, proliferation, or death, and it is frequently employed to screen for primary CNS lymphomas in patients presenting with nonspecific neurological syndromes. However, relying on this marker in isolation is clinically precarious because mild elevations occur in both neoplastic and inflammatory states. 

In this case of concurrent acute neurological Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD) and systemic disease, the patient presented with fever, headache, and aseptic meningitis. Correspondingly, his serum sIL-2R level was mildly elevated at 704 U/mL, exceeding the reference upper limit of 496 U/mL. Although sIL-2R is informative for T-cell lymphoma, as explicitly noted in the discussion of this case report, "mild elevations are non-specific and may reflect inflammatory activity." The distinction arises because both malignant and autoimmune processes involve T-cell activation; for instance, the robust immune response driving Behçet’s disease pathology can release sIL-2R due to cytokine-mediated activation of monocytes and T-cells, independent of malignancy. 

In this specific patient, the diagnostic utility of the elevated sIL-2R was nullified by the definitive exclusion of malignancy through brain MRI and negative CSF cytology, and the confirmation of inflammatory etiology via normal immunological susceptibility patterns and the classic presence of oral aphthae and genital ulcers. Therefore, a mildly elevated sIL-2R must be interpreted as a sign of immune activity rather than neoplasia when contextualized by a full neurological, radiological, and pathological workup. Clinicians should avoid using sIL-2R as a standalone discriminator, as failure to do so may lead to false-positive attributions of malignancy in patients with active neuroinflammatory disorders.
