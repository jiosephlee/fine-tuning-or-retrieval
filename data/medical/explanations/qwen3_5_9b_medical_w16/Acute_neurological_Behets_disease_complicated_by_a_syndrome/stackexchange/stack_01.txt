Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] Typically, neurological involvement in Behçet's disease occurs years after initial diagnosis; what does the concurrent presentation of systemic and acute neurological disease imply for the workup of this patient?

When Behçet’s disease presents acutely with concurrent neurological and systemic involvement, the diagnostic workup shifts from a chronic management model to an acute exclusionary protocol aimed at distinguishing vasculitis from infection, malignancy, or other inflammatory CNS disorders. As demonstrated in this case, while neuro-Behçet’s typically manifests several years after initial diagnosis, the concurrent onset requires a high index of suspicion for primary CNS inflammation but mandates rigorous exclusion of infectious or neoplastic mimics.

First, the presence of fever and acute neurological signs necessitates immediate evaluation for CNS infection, such as HSV, VZV, or bacterial meningitis, and malignancy, including lymphoma. In this case, the team appropriately prioritized CSF analysis to distinguish aseptic meningitis from viral or bacterial etiologies, confirmed by negative viral panels and cultures, and ruled out primary CNS malignancy via normal MRI and cytology. However, clinicians must recognize that acute neuro-Behçet’s disease can present with aseptic meningitis or focal deficits despite a normal initial brain MRI, particularly when the inflammation is limited to meningeal involvement; therefore, a normal neuroimaging study should not preclude the diagnosis.

Second, the workup must address systemic complications that may obscure clinical symptoms, such as the Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (SIADH). This patient’s hyponatraemia and persistent hiccups required specific endocrine evaluation to rule out thyroid, adrenal, or renal causes, confirming the SIADH diagnosis which, in isolation, could suggest a malignancy or lung pathology. The resolution of hyponatraemia alongside neurological symptoms suggests the neuro-inflammatory process disrupted ADH regulation, underscoring the need to evaluate volume status and electrolytes in patients with CNS Behçet’s.

Finally, the diagnosis relies on the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease combined with exclusion of alternative causes. In this concurrent presentation, the appearance of the characteristic genital ulcer progressed from an initial equivocal state to a definitive sign, validating the systemic nature of the disease. Therefore, for patients with acute fever and neurological deficits, the initial mucocutaneous evaluation (oral and genital lesions) is as critical as the neuroimaging to anchor the diagnosis, ensuring that the inflammatory etiology is not misattributed to infection or malignancy.
