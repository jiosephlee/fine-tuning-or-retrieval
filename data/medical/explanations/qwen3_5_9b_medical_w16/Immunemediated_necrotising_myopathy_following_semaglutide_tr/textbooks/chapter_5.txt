Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

Chapter 5: ## Clinical Outcomes and Pharmacovigilance Implications

The patient's clinical trajectory in this case serves as a critical teaching point regarding the natural history of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) and the implications of causality in drug-related adverse events. Following a comprehensive treatment course involving intravenous methylprednisolone, high-dose oral prednisone, and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), the patient experienced marked improvement within two weeks. The definitive outcome was observed ten months after initial evaluation, at which time she had regained her baseline functional status and remained free of significant muscle enzyme elevation. Crucially, she remained off immunosuppressive therapy for eight months without relapse or recurrence of symptoms. This ability to be successfully weaned off corticosteroids rapidly contrasts with idiopathic IMNM, which frequently requires prolonged, often indefinite, maintenance therapy. However, the patient’s severity and the presence of a distinct trigger allowed for initial aggressive intervention with a short course of immunosuppression after the offending agent was removed, paralleling the treatment course of many confirmed drug-induced myopathies. This clinical course reinforces the utility of recognizing a temporal relationship between medication exposure and disease onset to guide immunosuppressive therapy in the acute setting.

Understanding this case requires distinguishing it from other reported adverse events in the class of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, specifically those involving direct muscle toxicity. A previously reported case detailing tirzepatide use demonstrated rhabdomyolysis characterized by massive creatine kinase (CK) elevation and rapid resolution following drug discontinuation and hydration, consistent with a direct toxic mechanism. In contrast, this patient presented with a more insidious progression of proximal weakness and dysphagia over a four-month period following a single dose of semaglutide, necessitating immunosuppression rather than just hydration. This distinction highlights a spectrum of adverse neuromuscular events associated with GLP-1 receptor agonists, ranging from acute rhabdomyolysis to probable immune-mediated necrotising myopathy. These divergent presentations suggest that clinicians must broaden their diagnostic differential beyond simple toxicity, considering a complex immune etiology when symptoms persist or progress over weeks rather than resolving immediately upon cessation.

The emergence of this case expands the known safety profile of GLP-1 agonists, which will likely become increasingly utilized for obesity and metabolic disorders. While gastrointestinal side effects are well-documented, and some evidence suggests a general loss of skeletal muscle mass, evidence of a specific autoimmune mechanism has remained limited. This report provides the first patient-level data supporting an association between semaglutide and IMNM, categorized as probable causality using WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre criteria. Because the specific combination of symptoms, liver history, and muscle biopsy findings points toward a mechanistic trigger, this case underscores the necessity for enhanced pharmacovigilance. The lack of a definitive serological marker (HMGCR or SRP antibodies) in this patient further supports the need to report variation in clinical phenotypes, as not all cases of drug-induced IMNM will present with the typical antibody profile. Accurate reporting of such cases is essential to establish antecedent relationships for a growing population of patients at risk.

For residents and clinicians managing patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists, awareness of this potential adverse effect warrants specific monitoring recommendations. Clinicians should maintain a low threshold for evaluating new onset proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, or muscle pain in this population, even if the medication is well-tolerated regarding gastrointestinal symptoms. Given the temporal lag seen in this case prior to biopsy confirmation, preventing severe disability may rely on early recognition and the temporary discontinuation of the agent while workup proceeds. Although rechallenge is unethical in confirmed cases of severe toxicity, the clinical logic applied here—cessation followed by resolution—is the gold standard for establishing causality. Future monitoring strategies may involve more frequent CK and liver enzyme surveillance in patients initiating or escalating therapy, particularly those with pre-existing conditions such as fatty liver disease, as seen in this patient, which may theoretically modulate susceptibility to immune-mediated injury.
