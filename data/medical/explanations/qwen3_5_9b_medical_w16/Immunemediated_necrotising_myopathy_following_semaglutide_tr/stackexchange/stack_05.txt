Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] The muscle biopsy revealed necrotic fibers with CD68+ macrophage staining and Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) positivity. What specific immunological mechanism does the MAC positivity reflect in the context of muscle necrosis?

The presence of the membrane attack complex (MAC) positivity in this muscle biopsy reflects **terminal complement activation leading to direct sarcolemmal cytolytic injury**.

To elaborate, the membrane attack complex (C5b-9) is the final terminal component of the complement cascade. In this case, its deposition on both necrotising and non-necrotising myofibres indicates that antibody-mediated or immune-triggered complement activation is occurring directly against muscle cell membranes. This results in the formation of transmembrane pores that cause osmotic lysis and necrosis of the fiber.

As described in this report, the patient demonstrated features consistent with immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM). The biopsy revealed dispersed necrotic and regenerating fibers with myophagocytosis (evidenced by CD68+ macrophages) and rare CD3+ T cells. The MAC staining is particularly significant here because it distinguishes this process from permissive myopathies or polymyositis, where necrosis is often driven primarily by T-cell cytotoxicity with less complement deposition. In this context, the MAC positivity confirms that complement-mediated cytotoxicity is a driver of the muscle destruction, corroborating the diagnosis of IMNM and supporting the potential association with semaglutide use identified in the case report. Immunotherapy was subsequently employed, which successfully extinguished this immune-mediated lysis and normalized CK levels.
