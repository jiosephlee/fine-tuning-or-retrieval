Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What are the two primary autoantibodies associated with Immune-mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM), and which one has a stronger historical association with statin therapy?

The two primary autoantibodies associated with Immune-mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM) are anti-signal recognition particle (anti-SRP) and anti–hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (anti-HMGCR). As detailed in the Background section of this case report, the use of statins is strongly associated with anti-HMGCR autoantibody positivity. This serologic marker distinguishes a subset of IMNM patients from those with classic polymyositis or other inflammatory myopathies.

It is clinically relevant to note, as supported by this case report, that approximately one-third of patients with IMNM are seronegative for both anti-SRP and anti-HMGCR. This patient, for example, tested negative for the myositis panel including both HMGCR and SRP, which required reliance on muscle biopsy findings for diagnosis. Therefore, while anti-HMGCR has the stronger historical association with statin therapy, a high index of suspicion should remain even in seronegative patients.
