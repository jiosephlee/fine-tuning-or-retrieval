Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Clinically, how does the trajectory of Creatine Kinase (CK) levels over time in chronic IMNM differ from that seen in acute drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, and why is this distinction important for diagnosis?

**Clinical Teaching Cognition**

It is essential to recognize that the trajectory of creatine kinase (CK) elevation differentiates immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from acute drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, guiding critical management decisions regarding immunosuppression versus supportive care.

In the provided case of semaglutide-associated IMNM, the CK trajectory was subacute and progressive. Following a single dose of semaglutide, the patient initially presented with a modest CK elevation (337 U/L). However, over a 4-month interval—a period during which the drug had effectively ceased—the CK rose to a peak of 3992 U/L (a 12-fold increase), accompanied by progressive proximal weakness. This delayed, chronic escalation indicates an ongoing pathogenic process rather than direct acute toxicity.

By contrast, the discussion notes a comparative case of tirzepatide-induced rhabdomyolysis where the patient presented with a massive acute CK elevation that normalized within days after drug discontinuation and hydration, without the requirement for immunosuppression. While rhabdomyolysis is characterized by a rapid, acute spike in myoglobin and CK leading to renal injury, IMNM is an autoimmune disorder driven by individual myofibrillar necrosis that persists despite withdrawal of the product.

This distinction is diagnostic and therapeuticly vital. Misidentifying IMNM as simple drug toxicity or rhabdomyolysis risks withholding necessary corticosteroids and intravenous immunoglobulin, leading to irreversible muscle damage and persistent weakness. Conversely, over-intensifying immunosuppression in acute rhabdomyolysis carries unnecessary toxicity. A rising CK trend over weeks, particularly in the presence of fatigue or dysphagia, should prompt investigation into an autoimmune etiology and consideration for myositis rather than toxicity alone.
