Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] This patient presented with severe proximal weakness and dysphagia, features often requiring aggressive immunosuppression. Why was Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG) utilized as an adjunctive therapy alongside corticosteroids in this specific case?

In this specific case of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM), the decision to utilize intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) alongside corticosteroids was driven by the severity of the patient's presentation and the critical need for rapid immunomodulation. While high-dose corticosteroids are the standard first-line therapy for IMNM, their onset of action is often delayed, which poses a significant risk to patients with rapid clinical deterioration or life-threatening complications, such as this patient's profound exhaustion and progressive dysphagia. 

The case report explicitly notes that IVIG was administered at 2 g/kg "due to her persistent weakness and severe features." In the setting of severe, rapidly progressive autoimmune myopathy, there is a therapeutic window where the aggressive inflammatory response can outpace the damage-repair capacity of steroid monotherapy. The addition of IVIG aims to rapidly mitigate immune activation and provide a bridge toward remission. In this patient, the combination regimen yielded a marked reduction in creatine kinase levels and functional recovery, allowing for a rapid taper and discontinuation of prednisone within two months. This outcome is clinically significant because idiopathic IMNM typically requires prolonged intensive immunosuppression for remission; by utilizing aggressive combination therapy, the team achieved steroid-sparing efficacy and reduced the long-term toxicity associated with chronic corticosteroid use.
