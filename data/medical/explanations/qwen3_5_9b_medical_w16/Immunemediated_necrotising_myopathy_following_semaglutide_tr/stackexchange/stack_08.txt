Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] The patient achieved complete resolution of CK elevation and functional status off immunosuppression after only 2 months of tapering. How does this recovery profile compare to the typical long-term prognosis of idiopathic IMNM regarding immunosuppressive therapy requirements?

Good morning. Regarding the recovery profile observed in this case, it is crucial to distinguish this presentation from the natural history of idiopathic immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM).

In typical idiopathic IMNM, characterized by chronic antigen-driven inflammation often associated with autoantibodies such as anti–HMGCR or anti–SRP, prognosis regarding immunosuppressive therapy is generally poor in terms of treatment-free intervals. Idiopathic cases often require prolonged, intensive maintenance immunosuppression, sometimes indefinitely, to prevent relapse and manage persistent muscle weakness or CK burden.

In contrast, this patient’s clinical course—complete resolution with a rapid tapering schedule and return to baseline functional status off immunosuppression—closely aligns with a drug-induced etiology. As the authors note, "drug-induced IMNM, which can improve with brief immunosuppression after withdrawal of the offending agent, rather than idiopathic IMNM, which often requires prolonged and intensive immunosuppressive therapy."

Therefore, the short duration of therapy required here serves as a distinguishing clinical feature. While idiopathic IMNM typically necessitates a long-term treatment course to maintain negative serology and normalized muscle function, drug-triggered IMNM suggests that early cessation of the offending agent (semaglutide in this instance) may satisfy the primary driver of inflammation, allowing for resolution with minimal therapy. This distinction highlights the importance of pharmacovigilance and prompting clinicians to consider withdrawal of suspected agents before committing to chronic immunosuppression.
