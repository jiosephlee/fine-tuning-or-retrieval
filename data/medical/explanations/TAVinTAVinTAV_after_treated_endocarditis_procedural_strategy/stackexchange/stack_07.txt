### [Management] What is the recommended first-line antimicrobial regimen and typical duration for Enterococcus faecalis PVE in an older adult, and when might ampicillin–ceftriaxone be preferred over ampicillin–gentamicin?

For Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic‑valve endocarditis the accepted first‑line antimicrobial approach is combination, bactericidal therapy rather than a single beta‑lactam alone. In practice the two recommended regimens are intravenous ampicillin (or penicillin G if susceptible) plus an aminoglycoside (gentamicin) or, alternatively, dual β‑lactam therapy with ampicillin plus ceftriaxone. The total duration for PVE is 6 weeks of intravenous therapy.

Why two options? Enterococci are relatively tolerant to β‑lactams and require synergistic killing. Historically, ampicillin + gentamicin provided reliable synergy and is effective when the isolate is susceptible and the patient can tolerate an aminoglycoside. However, gentamicin causes dose‑dependent nephro‑ and ototoxicity and many elderly patients have baseline renal impairment or other contraindications. Randomized and observational data and guideline recommendations therefore support ampicillin + ceftriaxone as an equally effective, less nephrotoxic alternative for ampicillin‑susceptible E. faecalis infecting prosthetic valves.

Choose ampicillin–ceftriaxone over ampicillin–gentamicin when any of the following apply: the strain has high‑level aminoglycoside resistance (HLAR), the patient is elderly or has reduced renal function or is at high risk for aminoglycoside toxicity (pre‑existing renal disease, hearing impairment), or prolonged aminoglycoside exposure is undesirable. Also prefer dual β‑lactam if therapeutic gentamicin monitoring cannot be reliably performed. When gentamicin is used for PVE it is generally given for the full 6 weeks (unlike some native‑valve regimens that shorten aminoglycoside duration); if aminoglycoside toxicity develops, switch to ampicillin–ceftriaxone.

Practical points: confirm organism susceptibility to ampicillin and ceftriaxone before choosing dual β‑lactam, monitor renal function and drug levels as indicated, obtain repeat blood cultures to document clearance, and coordinate with surgery/infectious‑disease for duration and any need for suppressive therapy.
