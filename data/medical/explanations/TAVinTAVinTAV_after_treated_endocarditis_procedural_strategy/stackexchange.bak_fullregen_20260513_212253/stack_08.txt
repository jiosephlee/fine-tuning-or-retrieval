Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

### [Clinical Pearls] Which echocardiographic indices are most useful for identifying severe patient–prosthesis mismatch after TAVI, and what practical cutoff values are typically used for indexed effective orifice area and Doppler velocity index?

The key echocardiographic tools for detecting severe patient–prosthesis mismatch (PPM) after TAVI are the continuity‑equation–derived effective orifice area (EOA) indexed to body surface area (BSA), the Doppler velocity index (DVI, sometimes called dimensionless index), and corroborating Doppler gradients—interpreted in the context of flow status. These indices are complementary: indexed EOA quantifies the anatomic/functional orifice relative to patient size, DVI avoids errors from LVOT diameter measurement, and mean gradient reflects the haemodynamic consequence but is flow‑dependent.

Practically, the commonly used cutoffs are:
- Indexed EOA (EOA/BSA): severe PPM ≤ 0.65 cm2/m2; moderate PPM 0.65–0.85 cm2/m2. (Values ≤0.65 indicate critically small effective valve area for the patient.)
- Doppler velocity index (DVI = VTI_LVOT / VTI_aortic or Vmax_LVOT / Vmax_aortic): severe PPM ~≤0.25; moderate PPM ~0.25–0.35. A DVI ≤0.25 is strongly suggestive of severe obstruction/PPM independent of LVOT sizing errors.

Use these with additional data: an absolute AVA <1.0 cm2 and mean transvalvular gradient >40 mm Hg support severe obstruction, but gradients can be lower in low‑flow states so rely on indexed EOA/DVI when flow is reduced. Be vigilant for measurement pitfalls—errors in LVOT diameter (especially with supra‑annular or stented valves), misalignment of Doppler beam, and changes in stroke volume after interventions (as in this case where gradients fell as flow and frame expansion improved). When discordant, integrate clinical status, stroke volume index, and imaging (CT to assess frame/neo‑orifice) to decide if the prosthesis is truly mismatched and whether reintervention is indicated.
