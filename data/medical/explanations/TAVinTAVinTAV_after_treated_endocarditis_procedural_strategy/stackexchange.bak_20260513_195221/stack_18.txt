Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

### [Management] What clinical and imaging findings must be documented to declare resolution of PVE before undertaking a transcatheter valve reintervention?

Before reintervention on a prosthetic valve previously infected, you must document microbiologic, clinical and anatomic resolution of infection — not simply clinical improvement — because placing new prosthetic material into an active infection risks relapse and catastrophic prosthetic seeding.

Specifically, document the following. First, microbiologic cure: the patient should have completed a guideline‑directed, pathogen‑specific course of antimicrobial therapy and be afebrile and clinically well off parenteral antibiotics; ideally there are persistently negative blood cultures (serial cultures negative during therapy and after its completion) and no ongoing need for chronic suppressive antibiotics. Second, systemic resolution: inflammatory indices (CRP, ESR, leukocyte count) should have fallen to baseline or show a sustained downward trend consistent with cure and there should be absence of fever, ongoing bacteraemia or embolic phenomena. Third, imaging exclusion of active endocarditis or perivalvular complications: repeat transesophageal echocardiography must show no mobile vegetations, no new or enlarging paravalvular leak, and no abscess, fistula or dehiscence; cross‑sectional imaging (MSCT) and, when ambiguity remains, 18F‑FDG PET/CT should show no focal increased uptake or evolving perivalvular collection. Fourth, temporal stability: findings should be stable on serial exams (clinical and imaging) after completion of therapy — not a single transient negative study — and reviewed by the multidisciplinary heart team (cardiology, cardiac surgery, infectious disease, imaging) who agree the infection is resolved and reintervention is safe.

In practice this means documented completion of appropriate antibiotics, serially negative blood cultures, normalization/decline of inflammatory markers, absence of fever or embolic signs, negative/unchanged TEE and CT (and negative or non‑localizing PET if used), and multidisciplinary consensus — with an observation interval after therapy appropriate to the organism and clinical context — before proceeding with transcatheter valve reintervention.
