Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

### [Management] In a patient with prior PVE and recurrent GI bleeding but no atrial fibrillation after TAVI, what antithrombotic regimen is generally preferred and why?

Short-term single antiplatelet therapy — usually aspirin alone — is generally preferred. 

Reasoning: once infective endocarditis has been treated and there is no other indication for oral anticoagulation (no atrial fibrillation, no mechanical valve, no venous thromboembolism), routine long‑term anticoagulation is not indicated after TAVI. Randomized and guideline data now show that dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin plus clopidogrel) after TAVI increases bleeding without clear ischemic benefit compared with single antiplatelet therapy, and oral anticoagulation solely to prevent valve thrombosis is not routinely recommended in patients at high bleeding risk. In a patient with recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, minimizing antithrombotic intensity reduces the risk of recurrent hemorrhage while providing reasonable thrombotic protection for the bioprosthesis. 

Practical caveats: use aspirin monotherapy if no recent coronary stent or other indication for DAPT/OAC; if there is a compelling need for anticoagulation later (new AF, documented valve thrombosis), weigh stroke risk versus bleeding and involve gastroenterology for bleeding mitigation (eg, PPI, source control).
