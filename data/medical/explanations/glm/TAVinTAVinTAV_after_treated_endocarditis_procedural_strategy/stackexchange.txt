Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] This patient developed prosthetic valve endocarditis caused by Enterococcus faecalis 3 months after his TAV-in-TAV procedure. What is the recommended antimicrobial treatment regimen for enterococcal prosthetic valve endocarditis, and what key differences exist compared to native valve endocarditis in terms of duration and agent selection?

### Answer

Enterococcal prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) demands a more aggressive and prolonged antimicrobial approach than native valve endocarditis (NVE), reflecting both the difficulty of eradicating infection from prosthetic material and the higher risk of relapse.

For **enterococcal PVE** caused by *E. faecalis*, the recommended regimen consists of **6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin (or penicillin G) combined with an aminoglycoside**—typically gentamicin—for synergistic bactericidal activity against the enterococcus. An alternative combination, **ampicillin plus ceftriaxone**, has emerged as an effective regimen that may be preferred when aminoglycoside toxicity is a concern, particularly in patients with renal dysfunction, and it has demonstrated comparable efficacy. In cases of penicillin allergy, **vancomycin** substitutes for ampicillin. When high-level aminoglycoside resistance is present, the ampicillin-ceftriaxone dual-β-lactam regimen becomes particularly valuable.

Several key differences distinguish PVE from NVE management:

**Duration:** PVE requires a **minimum of 6 weeks** of intravenous therapy, as was appropriately delivered in this patient. NVE may sometimes be treated with **4-week courses**, particularly with the ampicillin-ceftriaxone regimen in uncomplicated cases. The extended duration in PVE reflects the persistence of organisms within biofilm on prosthetic surfaces.

**Aminoglycoside synergy:** For PVE, **full-duration aminoglycoside synergistic therapy** is traditionally recommended throughout the 6-week course. For NVE, shorter aminoglycoside exposure (e.g., 2–4 weeks) or the ceftriaxone-based regimen without aminoglycosides may be sufficient, reducing nephrotoxicity risk.

**Post-antibiotic suppressive therapy:** PVE may require consideration of oral suppressive therapy following the IV course, particularly in patients with extensive prosthetic involvement. In this patient's case, following 6 weeks of IV ampicillin-based therapy, an oral course was administered per infectious disease recommendations, and suppressive therapy was ultimately not required—reflecting successful eradication as confirmed by negative blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and CT imaging.

The case well illustrates the principle that **timing of PVE also matters**: this patient developed endocarditis approximately 3 months post-TAV-in-TAV, falling within the **early PVE window** (≤1 year), which is more commonly associated with healthcare-associated or procedural infections. The prompt initiation of targeted, guideline-concordant therapy and confirmation of microbiological cure prior to any further intervention was essential to the feasibility of the subsequent TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, as active infection would have contraindicated transcatheter reintervention.

### [Clinical Pearls] In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, the risk of coronary artery obstruction is a critical concern due to multiple overlapping stent frames and leaflet material. What specific CT-derived measurements are used to assess coronary obstruction risk, and what is the significance of the 'neo-skirt' concept in procedural planning?

In TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures, coronary artery obstruction risk escalates with each successive layer of stent frames and displaced leaflet material. As illustrated in this case, the preprocedural CT assessment centered on several key measurements: **coronary ostial height**, **sinus of Valsalva dimensions**, **valve-to-coronary distance** (the distance from the aortic annular plane or the prosthetic valve frame to each coronary ostium), and the **interaction between the frames of previously implanted transcatheter valves**. The CT also identified the **minimum lumen diameter** within the existing Evolut prosthesis (13.7 mm in this case), confirming severe hemodynamic compromise and defining the anatomical constraints for a third valve. The relationship between the anticipated neo-skirt and the coronary ostia was specifically evaluated, as well as the feasibility of high implantation to preserve coronary flow and maintain future coronary access.

The **neo-skirt** concept is central to procedural planning in valve-in-valve interventions. When a new transcatheter valve is deployed within an existing prosthesis, the leaflets of the prior valve are displaced outward and pressed against the stent frame, creating a circumferential ring of tissue—functionally a "skirt"—that can extend above the annular plane. If this neo-skirt reaches the level of the coronary ostia, it can occlude coronary inflow, particularly when coronary ostial height is low or the sinus of Valsalva is shallow. In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV configuration, the cumulative leaflet and frame material from multiple overlapping valves compounds this risk substantially.

In this case, the procedural strategy deliberately addressed the neo-skirt risk. **High implantation** of the Acurate neo2—positioned at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets—served two purposes: it fixed the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame, preventing inward leaflet folding that would further reduce the effective orifice area, and it minimized neo-skirt formation *below* the coronary ostia, thereby preserving coronary perfusion and future coronary access. This approach, guided entirely by preprocedural CT modeling, allowed the procedure to be performed without active coronary protection (e.g., prophylactic guidewire placement or chimney stents). The case demonstrates that when CT-derived measurements confirm adequate valve-to-coronary distance and a favorable neo-skirt-to-ostium relationship, a strategy of high implantation can mitigate obstruction risk even in triple-stented anatomy.

### [Pathophysiology] Immediately after the third TAVI, this patient had a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg, an indexed aortic valve area of 0.4 cm²/m², and a Doppler velocity index of 0.24—consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM). How is PPM defined and graded using indexed effective orifice area, and what are the major clinical consequences of severe PPM after transcatheter valve implantation?

## Answer

Prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) occurs when the effective orifice area of a normally functioning prosthesis is too small relative to the patient's body size, generating abnormally high transvalvular gradients. PPM is defined and graded by the **indexed effective orifice area (iEOA)**, calculated as the prosthetic effective orifice area divided by body surface area. The standard echocardiographic thresholds, widely endorsed by the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging and others, are:

- **Not significant:** iEOA > 0.85 cm²/m²
- **Moderate PPM:** iEOA ≤ 0.85 but > 0.65 cm²/m²
- **Severe PPM:** iEOA ≤ 0.65 cm²/m² (some definitions use ≤ 0.60 cm²/m², with lower cutoffs applied in obese patients)

A Doppler velocity index (DVI) below 0.25 further supports the diagnosis of severe PPM, as DVI is independent of orifice area measurement assumptions. In this case, immediately after the third TAVI, the indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² and DVI of 0.24 placed the patient well within the severe PPM range, consistent with the markedly elevated mean gradient of 67 mm Hg.

The major clinical consequences of severe PPM after transcatheter valve implantation include:

1. **Persistent or recurrent heart failure symptoms** — less symptomatic relief and poorer functional capacity.
2. **Incomplete left ventricular mass regression** — chronic pressure overload sustains concentric LV hypertrophy.
3. **Higher persistent transvalvular gradients** — as demonstrated by the initial postprocedural gradient in this case.
4. **Increased mortality** — severe PPM is independently associated with worse short- and long-term survival.
5. **Accelerated structural valve deterioration** — elevated mechanical stress on the bioprosthetic leaflets may shorten valve longevity.
6. **Higher rates of heart failure hospitalization.**

Importantly, the hemodynamic compromise in this case was not purely intrinsic valve dysfunction but reflected the geometric constraints of three overlapping valve frames within a fixed aortic annulus — a setting in which even a normally functioning prosthesis can produce severe PPM. The marked improvement seen at 1 month (mean gradient 27 mm Hg, AVA 1.1 cm²) likely resulted from ongoing frame expansion, optimized commissural alignment, and the effects of high-pressure balloon post-dilation, illustrating that early postprocedural gradients in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV may overstate the ultimate hemodynamic result.

### [Morning Report] This patient presented with progressive dyspnea, exertional chest discomfort, dizziness, and fatigue 2 years after his second TAVI, with echocardiography showing a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an aortic valve area of 0.5 cm². What are the main mechanisms of structural valve deterioration in transcatheter bioprostheses, and how did the prior episode of infective endocarditis likely contribute to the degeneration process in this case?

## Structural Valve Deterioration After TAVI and the Role of Prior Endocarditis

Structural valve deterioration (SVD) in transcatheter bioprostheses is driven by several interrelated mechanisms. The primary process is **intrinsic leaflet degeneration**, involving progressive calcification, collagen fiber disruption, and thickening of the bioprosthetic tissue—most commonly bovine or porcine pericardium—which leads to leaflet stiffening, restricted opening, and ultimately a stenotic, regurgitant, or mixed dysfunction pattern. Over time, mechanical wear, repetitive leaflet flexion, and inflammatory processes can also promote **leaflet tear** and **pannus ingrowth** from the host tissue, further compromising valve mechanics. These mechanisms are accelerated by patient-specific factors such as younger age, metabolic disease, and altered hemodynamics across the prosthesis.

In this case, the patient demonstrated classic features of severe SVD two years after his TAV-in-TAV, with echocardiography revealing a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an aortic valve area of only 0.5 cm², accompanied by moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The **mixed stenotic and regurgitant pattern** is highly characteristic of advanced bioprosthetic degeneration, reflecting both leaflet restriction from fibrosis and calcification (stenosis) and impaired leaflet coaptation from structural distortion (regurgitation).

The prior episode of **Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis** likely accelerated this degenerative cascade. As the case report notes, the mechanism of valve failure was "multifactorial," with endocarditis contributing to **leaflet injury and subsequent mechanical restriction** superimposed on progressive structural degeneration of both prostheses. Bacterial endocarditis can damage bioprosthetic leaflets through direct infection of the tissue, leading to vegetations, leaflet perforation, and inflammatory destruction of the collagen matrix. Even after successful antimicrobial eradication—as confirmed in this patient by repeat blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography, and CT—the residual structural damage to the leaflets is irreversible. The healing process itself can promote **fibrotic scarring and retraction** of the leaflet tissue, further stiffening the already degenerating bioprosthetic material and accelerating the transition from functional to severely dysfunctional valve performance.

This is precisely what was observed clinically: significant restenosis was already documented at the time endocarditis was diagnosed (mean gradient 35 mm Hg), and despite complete antimicrobial eradication, valve function continued to worsen over the subsequent 18 months. The MSCT finding of a minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis confirmed that the structural damage was fixed and progressive, not related to ongoing infection. As the authors emphasize, once active infection was excluded, the decision-making for reintervention was driven by anatomical feasibility, hemodynamic severity, and surgical risk—the same framework applied to any patient with bioprosthetic valve failure.

This case underscores an important teaching point: **endocarditis is not only a life-threatening acute event but also a catalyst for accelerated structural valve deterioration**. Patients with prior PVE require vigilant long-term surveillance, as the healed but damaged valve is primed for earlier and more rapid degeneration than would be expected from time alone.

### [Clinical Pearls] The procedural team deliberately employed a high implantation strategy for the third transcatheter valve, positioning it at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets. What is the rationale for this high implantation approach in terms of effective orifice area preservation, coronary flow maintenance, and prevention of leaflet folding, and what are the potential trade-offs of this strategy?

**Rationale for High Implantation of the Third Transcatheter Valve**

In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV configuration, the cumulative stent frame burden and the spatial relationships between multiple overlapping valves create a uniquely constrained anatomy. The procedural team's decision to implant the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets was a deliberate, CT-guided strategy addressing three interrelated mechanical problems.

**Effective Orifice Area Preservation:** By the time of the third intervention, the minimum lumen diameter within the Evolut prosthesis had narrowed to 13.7 mm, with a calculated AVA of 0.5 cm². Implanting the third valve at the leaflet level of the Evolut rather than deeper within the prior frame was intended to maximize the residual effective orifice area. A lower, more deeply nested implant would have positioned the new valve's waist within the narrowest portion of the existing frame, further restricting flow. The supra-annular leaflet design of the Acurate neo2, combined with high positioning, was selected to optimize hemodynamics in this severely compromised anatomy.

**Prevention of Leaflet Folding:** When a new valve is deployed within an existing prosthesis, the leaflets of the prior valve can be pushed inward by the expanding frame, creating a circumferential obstruction that reduces the effective orifice area. By aligning the third valve precisely at the level of the Evolut leaflets—guided by preprocedural CT modeling—the team pinned the pre-existing leaflets against the Evolut stent frame, preventing their inward folding and thereby avoiding additional flow limitation.

**Coronary Flow Maintenance:** High implantation minimized the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia. A neo-skirt forms when the leaflets of prior valves are displaced and create a tissue barrier between the new valve frame and the aortic wall, potentially obstructing coronary ostia. By positioning the third valve higher, the team kept this potential barrier at or above the coronary ostial level, preserving coronary perfusion and maintaining the feasibility of future coronary access. Preprocedural CT assessment of coronary ostial height, valve-to-coronary distance, and the anticipated neo-skirt location confirmed that coronary protection was unnecessary during the procedure.

**Potential Trade-offs:** Despite meticulous planning, this strategy did not fully eliminate prosthesis-patient mismatch. Immediately after implantation, the mean transvalvular gradient was 67 mm Hg with an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m², consistent with severe PPM. Although these parameters improved substantially over the following month—likely due to post-dilatation effects, partial mechanical expansion of the Evolut frame, and ongoing remodeling—the persistent residual stenosis illustrates the inherent limitation of implanting a third valve within two already constrained frames. Additionally, high implantation carries inherent risks of valve instability or migration, and the cumulative mesh burden from three overlapping stent frames may still compromise future coronary access despite commissural alignment. Finally, should this prosthesis fail, further options are extremely limited, as surgical explantation in triple-stented anatomy would likely require aortic root and ascending aorta replacement.

### [Management] According to the 2025 ESC guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis, what is the recommended treatment strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis, and under what circumstances might a transcatheter approach be considered instead of surgery in a patient with prior TAVI who develops PVE?

According to the 2025 ESC guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis, surgical valve replacement remains the recommended treatment strategy for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), particularly when complicated by heart failure, uncontrolled infection, or periannular extension. However, the guidelines explicitly emphasize that management must be individualized, taking into account infection control status, surgical risk, and anatomical feasibility of both surgical and transcatheter options.

A transcatheter approach may be considered when three broad conditions are met. First, the infection must be considered cured — confirmed by clinical assessment, repeated blood cultures, and imaging (transthoracic and transesophageal echocardiography, and where appropriate, CT) demonstrating resolution of vegetations and absence of paravalvular complications. Second, the patient must carry prohibitive or high surgical risk, as assessed by a multidisciplinary heart team. Third, detailed preprocedural imaging — particularly contrast CT — must confirm anatomical feasibility, including adequate coronary clearance, acceptable landing zone dimensions, and a viable strategy to preserve coronary access and minimize prosthesis-patient mismatch.

This case illustrates these principles well. The patient had a history of two prior TAVI procedures and developed PVE caused by *Enterococcus faecalis*, which was treated with a complete course of targeted antimicrobial therapy. Resolution of infection was confirmed by repeated blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography, and CT. When severe prosthetic dysfunction subsequently developed, surgical replacement was deemed exceptionally high-risk: the EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and the deeply integrated frames of two prior transcatheter prostheses would likely have necessitated total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement. In the absence of active infection and with CT-confirmed feasibility — including favorable valve-to-coronary distances and a planned high-implantation strategy to preserve coronary flow — a third transcatheter valve was implanted successfully.

A critical teaching point emerges from this case: once endocarditis is considered cured and no long-term suppressive therapy is required, the decision-making framework for prosthetic valve dysfunction shifts toward the same principles used for structural valve deterioration in patients without a history of infection — namely, anatomical feasibility, hemodynamic severity, and surgical risk. The prior history of treated endocarditis alone should not, in the absence of active infection, mandate surgical management if the patient's risk profile and anatomy favor a transcatheter approach. Multidisciplinary heart team evaluation and CT-guided procedural planning are essential to optimizing outcomes in these complex cases.

### [Pathophysiology] One month after the third TAVI, the mean transvalvular gradient decreased dramatically from 67 mm Hg to 27 mm Hg without any additional intervention. What mechanisms explain this delayed improvement in gradient, including the role of balloon post-dilation on the prior Evolut frame, commissural alignment, and frame remodeling over time?

# Delayed Gradient Improvement After TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV: Mechanisms and Clinical Reasoning

The dramatic reduction in mean transvalvular gradient from 67 mm Hg immediately post-procedure to 27 mm Hg at one month—without any additional intervention—is a striking but explicable phenomenon rooted in the biomechanics of self-expanding valve frames, the effects of balloon post-dilation on nested prostheses, and the importance of commissural alignment in multi-valve configurations.

## 1. Ongoing Frame Expansion and Remodeling

The third prosthesis implanted was an Acurate neo2, a self-expanding nitinol valve. Unlike balloon-expandable valves, which reach their final diameter at the time of deployment, self-expanding nitinol frames continue to exert outward radial force over days to weeks as they adapt to the surrounding anatomy. In this case, the Acurate neo2 was seated within two prior valve frames—a Lotus valve (2014) and an Evolut prosthesis (2022). The triple-stented anatomy created a rigid, concentric nesting environment that initially constrained full expansion of the third valve, producing the high immediate post-procedural gradient of 67 mm Hg with a calculated AVA of only 0.9 cm². Over the subsequent month, the continued outward radial force of the nitinol frame, combined with cyclic hemodynamic loading during systole, permitted gradual frame expansion and remodeling, resulting in the observed increase in AVA to 1.1 cm² and reduction in gradient to 27 mm Hg.

## 2. Balloon Post-Dilation and Partial Mechanical Expansion of the Evolut Frame

During the procedure, balloon post-dilation was performed using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid ventricular pacing. While intentional frame fracture was not attempted, this high-pressure balloon dilation likely produced partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame. The Evolut prosthesis, also a self-expanding nitinol valve, had been constrained within the Lotus frame and may not have achieved its full expanded diameter at the time of its original implantation—or may have been further compressed by the degenerative process and infection. The balloon post-dilation served a dual purpose: it expanded the newly implanted Acurate neo2 and simultaneously applied outward force on the Evolut frame, partially remodeling its architecture. This mechanical intervention initiated a process of frame expansion that continued beyond the procedure itself, contributing to the delayed gradient improvement.

## 3. Commissural Alignment and Leaflet Mechanics

Commissural alignment between the Acurate neo2 and the Evolut was deliberately planned using preprocedural CT modeling. When commissures of nested valves are misaligned, the leaflets of the outer valve can project inward into the orifice of the inner valve, creating a physical barrier to leaflet opening and reducing the effective orifice area—a phenomenon particularly relevant in TAV-in-TAV configurations. By aligning the Acurate neo2 with the Evolut valve leaflets, the procedural team minimized this inward leaflet folding. Additionally, the high implantation strategy—placing the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut leaflets—fixed the pre-existing Evolut leaflets against the stent frame, preventing them from prolapsing inward and obstructing flow. This alignment optimized the central flow dynamics and allowed the combined valve apparatus to function more efficiently as the frames continued to settle over the first month.

## 4. Resolution of Tissue Edema and Adaptive Remodeling

Although not explicitly discussed in the case, it is worth noting that acute procedural trauma, including balloon pre-dilation and post-dilation, can produce transient tissue edema and hematoma in the annular region. Resolution of this edema over the first weeks may also have contributed incrementally to improved valve mechanics and reduced gradient.

## Summary

The delayed improvement in gradient in this case is best explained by the interplay of three mechanisms: **(1)** continued self-expansion and remodeling of the nitinol Acurate neo2 frame within the rigid triple-stented anatomy; **(2)** partial mechanical expansion of the Evolut frame initiated by balloon post-dilation, with ongoing radial remodeling; and **(3)** optimized commissural alignment and high implantation preventing inward leaflet obstruction. Together, these factors allowed the nested valve apparatus to achieve a more favorable hemodynamic configuration over the first month, a phenomenon that underscores the importance of patience when evaluating early gradients after complex self-expanding valve interventions and the value of meticulous CT-guided procedural planning in multi-prosthesis configurations.

### [Differential Diagnosis] If the third transcatheter prosthesis in this patient were to fail prematurely, treatment options would be extremely limited. What is the BASILICA (bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction) technique, and what are the theoretical advantages and limitations of this approach in a triple-stented aortic valve configuration?

**BASILICA and Its Role in Triple-Stented Aortic Valve Configurations**

The BASILICA technique — Bioprosthetic or native Aortic Scallop Intentional Laceration to prevent Iatrogenic Coronary Artery obstruction — is a catheter-based intervention designed to mitigate the risk of coronary artery obstruction during transcatheter aortic valve procedures. Using an electrified guidewire, the operator intentionally lacerates the aortic valve leaflet (scallop) that overlies the coronary ostium at risk. When a subsequent transcatheter valve is deployed, the pre-lacerated leaflet splays outward against the sinus wall rather than being displaced inward to occlude the coronary ostium. The technique was developed to address one of the most feared complications of TAV-in-TAV procedures, where the retained leaflets of a prior bioprosthesis are pushed toward the sinuses during deployment of a new valve, potentially obstructing coronary flow — particularly when coronary ostial height is low or the sinuses are shallow.

In the context of this patient's triple-stented aortic valve configuration, the theoretical advantage of BASILICA would relate to preserving the possibility of a future fourth transcatheter intervention. As the case report emphasizes, should the Acurate neo2 prosthesis fail prematurely, "treatment options would be extremely limited," and surgical explantation would be "technically demanding and high-risk," likely requiring total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement due to deep stent frame integration into the aortic wall. A catheter-based strategy that could prevent coronary obstruction during a hypothetical fourth valve implantation would therefore be conceptually attractive, particularly given this patient's prohibitive surgical risk (EuroSCORE II 27.36%) and extensive comorbidities, including three-vessel coronary disease with prior PCIs, COPD, diabetes, and recurrent GI bleeding.

However, the limitations of BASILICA in this specific anatomy are substantial and must be clearly understood. First, BASILICA was designed and validated for native or single bioprosthetic valve configurations, not for the scenario of three overlapping transcatheter valve frames. The procedure requires precise catheter navigation and electrosurgical laceration of a target leaflet — a task that becomes extraordinarily challenging when three layers of stent mesh, overlapping commissural posts, and densely packed metal struts create a physical labyrinth that must be traversed. The ability to steer an electrified wire through this cumulative mesh burden to reach and cleave the appropriate leaflet tissue is technically daunting, and the margin for error is minimal.

Second, the coronary obstruction risk in multi-layered TAV-in-TAV configurations is not solely a function of leaflet displacement. As the case report notes, the team was particularly concerned about the "neo-skirt" — the cylindrical structure formed by the retained leaflets and stent frames of prior valves that can extend below the coronary ostia. In a triple-stented configuration, this neo-skirt is a composite of multiple layers of prosthetic material, and BASILICA laceration of a single leaflet does not address the broader issue of frame-induced narrowing of the sinus and reduced coronary clearance from cumulative stent bulk.

Third, this patient already exhibits severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m², DVI 0.24 immediately post-procedure), and while gradients improved to a mean of 26 mm Hg at one year, the effective orifice area remains constrained at 1.1 cm². BASILICA does not address the problem of effective orifice area reduction — it is purely a coronary protection strategy. A fourth valve would face an even narrower landing zone, and even with successful coronary protection, the haodynamic result would likely be unacceptable.

Finally, as the authors themselves conclude, while emerging catheter-based solutions such as BASILICA "might be considered," their "feasibility and safety in triple-stented configurations remain uncertain." There is currently no clinical evidence supporting the use of BASILICA in patients with three overlapping transcatheter valve frames, and the technical complexity of such an undertaking cannot be overstated.

The key teaching point is this: BASILICA represents an important tool for coronary protection in selected valve-in-valve scenarios, but its applicability diminishes dramatically as the number of overlapping stent frames increases. In this patient, the most important strategy was the one the team actually employed — meticulous CT-guided preprocedural planning, high implantation of the third valve at the level of the Evolut leaflets to minimize neo-skirt formation, commissural alignment to preserve coronary access, and careful valve selection. These proactive measures at the time of each intervention, rather than reactive salvage strategies, represent the most meaningful approach to extending the longevity of transcatheter valve therapy in these increasingly complex patients.

### [Morning Report] The Acurate neo2 prosthesis was specifically selected for this third TAVI. What design characteristics of the Acurate neo2—such as supra-annular leaflet positioning, frame height, and commissural alignment features—make it suitable for implantation within a narrow anatomy containing two prior transcatheter valve frames, and how do these features compare to those of other self-expanding valves like the Evolut?

# Clinical Teaching Answer: Acurate neo2 Design Advantages in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV

In this case, the Acurate neo2 (size S) was selected for a third transcatheter valve implanted within two existing frames—a 27 mm Lotus (2014) and a 23 mm Evolut (2022)—in an anatomy where MSCT demonstrated a critically narrowed Evolut minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm. The rationale for this selection rests on three interrelated design features that address the unique constraints of triple-frame implantation.

**Supra-annular leaflet positioning** is the first key advantage. By seating the functional leaflets above the plane of the prior valve frames rather than within the narrowest portion of the stacked prostheses, the Acurate neo2 can achieve a larger effective orifice area than would be possible with an intra-annular design. In this patient, who had severe prosthetic stenosis (mean gradient 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm²) and was at high risk for prosthesis-patient mismatch, maximizing the residual orifice area was a central planning objective. The Evolut also features supra-annular leaflets, but the critical difference lies in the frame architecture surrounding those leaflets.

**Relatively low frame height** is the second distinguishing feature. The Acurate neo2 has a shorter stent frame compared to the Evolut, which has a taller frame that extends higher into the aortic root and ascending aorta. In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV configuration, each additional frame adds metal burden to the root, narrows the neo-skirt space, and increases the risk of coronary ostial encroachment. The lower frame height of the Acurate neo2 was particularly relevant here because a high implantation strategy was planned—positioning the third valve at the level of the Evolut leaflets to pin the pre-existing leaflets outward against the stent frame, thereby preventing further inward folding and lumen reduction. A shorter frame made this high positioning feasible while minimizing the neo-skirt height below the coronary ostia, preserving both coronary flow and future coronary access.

**Commissural alignment capability** is the third consideration. The Acurate neo2 design facilitates rotational orientation of its commissures relative to the existing Evolut valve leaflets, as guided by preprocedural CT modeling. In this case, the third valve was deliberately aligned with the Evolut commissures to avoid placing stent struts directly across the coronary ostia. While the Evolut also supports commissural alignment, the combination of the Acurate neo2's lower frame height and alignment features was considered more favorable for maintaining coronary access through the cumulative triple-frame mesh. The case report explicitly notes that commissural alignment was a key consideration for preserving future coronary access, and the procedure was performed without coronary protection based on CT-confirmed feasibility.

In summary, the Acurate neo2 was selected over a second Evolut because its supra-annular leaflets optimize hemodynamics in the narrow residual lumen, its low frame height permits a high implantation strategy that pins prior leaflets open without exacerbating coronary obstruction risk, and its commissural alignment features help orient the new valve's struts away from coronary ostia. These features collectively address the central challenges of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV: maximizing effective orifice area while preserving coronary access through three overlapping stent frames. Despite these optimizations, the case demonstrates that severe prosthesis-patient mismatch may still occur (immediate post-procedure mean gradient 67 mm Hg), though gradual improvement was observed over follow-up, attributed to post-dilation–induced radial expansion of the Evolut frame and optimized commissural alignment.
