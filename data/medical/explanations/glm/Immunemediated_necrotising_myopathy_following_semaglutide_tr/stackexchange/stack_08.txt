Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] According to the WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre causality assessment criteria, what factors in this case support 'probable' (as opposed to 'possible' or 'certain') causality between semaglutide and IMNM, and why was rechallenge not performed?

## Probable Causality per WHO-UMC Criteria: Semaglutide and IMNM

The WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) system defines **probable** causality as an adverse event with a reasonable temporal relationship to drug exposure, unlikely attributable to alternative diseases or medications, and a clinically reasonable response upon drug withdrawal — without requiring rechallenge. This case satisfies each of these criteria while falling short of "certain" on two explicit grounds.

**Reasonable time relationship.** The patient developed progressive proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, and dysphonia with a 12-fold CK rise (from 337 U/L at initial presentation to a peak of 3992 U/L) over the 4 months following her single dose of semaglutide. The temporal sequence — drug exposure followed by insidious but progressive myopathy — is consistent with an immune-mediated process rather than acute direct toxicity, which would be expected to resolve within days of drug cessation, as illustrated by the previously reported tirzepatide rhabdomyolysis case (CK >57,000 with rapid improvement on withdrawal alone).

**Unlikely due to other diseases or drugs.** The patient had no prior statin use, no consumption of supplements associated with IMNM (such as red yeast rice or mushrooms), no family history of myopathy, and no excessive physical activity. Her myositis panel was negative for anti-SRP, anti-HMGCR, and all other known myositis-specific antibodies. She had no clinical features of a connective tissue disease, and her isolated low-titer anti-SSB positivity (with negative ANA and anti-SSA) has no known association with IMNM. The authors acknowledge they cannot definitively exclude a pre-existing smoldering IMNM because no baseline CK was available before semaglutide initiation — a limitation that also contributes to the assessment remaining at "probable" rather than "certain."

**Clinically reasonable response on withdrawal.** Following cessation of semaglutide and treatment with pulse methylprednisolone, oral prednisone, and a single course of IVIG, the patient's CK normalized, her strength improved markedly, and she returned to baseline functional status. She remained well off all immunosuppression for 8 months with continued avoidance of GLP-1 receptor agonists. This sustained remission after a short immunosuppression course is more characteristic of drug-induced IMNM than idiopathic IMNM, which typically requires prolonged, intensive immunosuppression.

**Why not "possible"?** The "possible" category applies when the event could also be reasonably explained by the underlying disease or concomitant drugs. Here, the extensive negative workup effectively excluded alternative etiologies, and the clinical course aligned specifically with a drug-triggered immune mechanism, thereby supporting a higher confidence level.

**Why not "certain" and why no rechallenge?** The "certain" category requires that the event be confirmed by reappearance upon rechallenge. Rechallenge was deemed unethical given the severity of the initial reaction — the patient developed profound quadriparesis with dysphagia requiring hospitalization and aggressive immunosuppression. Deliberately re-exposing her to semaglutide would risk recurrence of a potentially life-threatening myopathy. The absence of rechallenge, combined with the lack of a pre-drug CK measurement, precludes assignment of "certain" causality and appropriately caps the assessment at "probable."
