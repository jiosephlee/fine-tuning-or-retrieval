Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] In PARN-associated telomere biology disorders, biallelic variants classically cause autosomal recessive dyskeratosis congenita (DKCB6) and severe phenotypes such as Hoyeraal-Hreidarsson syndrome, while monoallelic variants are more commonly associated with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and/or bone marrow failure (PFBMFT4). How does this case expand the known clinical spectrum of monoallelic PARN mutations, and what does it suggest about the concept of haploinsufficiency in PARN?

# Clinical Teaching Answer

## Expanding the Phenotypic Spectrum of Monoallelic PARN Mutations

Classically, the genotype-phenotype correlation in *PARN*-associated telomere biology disorders (TBDs) follows a recognizable pattern: biallelic loss-of-function variants produce severe, early-onset phenotypes such as autosomal recessive dyskeratosis congenita (DKCB6) and Hoyeraal-Hreidarsson syndrome, while monoallelic variants are typically linked to a narrower adult-onset spectrum—principally idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and/or bone marrow failure (PFBMFT4). Hepatic fibrosis, when described in TBDs, has been predominantly associated with mutations in *TERT* or *TERC*, not *PARN*.

This case meaningfully expands that spectrum. The patient—a woman in her late 20s—harbored a single monoallelic *PARN* missense variant (c.613T>C; p.Cys205Arg) yet developed a striking multisystem phenotype encompassing pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis with cirrhosis, and pulmonary fibrosis, alongside premature greying, osteoporosis, and neuropathy. Critically, the prior literature documenting hepatic fibrosis in a *PARN* carrier involved a patient who also carried a monoallelic *TERT* variant, leaving open the possibility that the hepatic phenotype was driven by *TERT* rather than *PARN*. This patient had no additional telomere-related gene variant identified, establishing that monoallelic *PARN* dysfunction alone can extend beyond the lung and marrow to include hepatic fibrosis—a manifestation previously considered atypical for this genotype.

## Implications for Haploinsufficiency in PARN

*PARN* ranks among the approximately 20% of human genes least tolerant to rare, disruptive variants, and it functions as a poly(A)-specific ribonuclease essential for the 3′-end maturation of TERC, the RNA component of telomerase. Even a single nonfunctional allele can reduce effective telomerase activity below the threshold needed to maintain telomere length in highly proliferative tissues. This case reinforces the concept that *PARN* haploinsufficiency is not a binary or organ-restricted phenomenon. Rather, a single variant can produce a continuum of telomere-driven organ injury spanning the bone marrow, lung, and liver—the same triad more typically associated with *TERT* or *TERC* mutations.

The case also highlights important modifiers of penetrance. The patient's pancytopenia was initially attributed to severe vitamin B12 deficiency, but the persistence of multisystem dysfunction after nutritional correction suggests that telomere-mediated stem cell exhaustion may have rendered her hematopoietic compartment—and potentially her hepatic parenchyma—unusually vulnerable to superimposed stress. This is consistent with the broader TBD principle: environmental insults, replicative demand, and somatic genetic rescue can differentially influence which organ systems manifest and when.

In sum, this case argues that monoallelic *PARN* mutations should be considered within a broader differential than IPF and bone marrow failure alone. Clinicians evaluating young adults with unexplained hepatic fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, or cytopenia—even in the absence of the classic mucocutaneous triad of dyskeratosis congenita—should consider TBDs and pursue genetic testing and telomere length measurement, recognizing that *PARN* haploinsufficiency may carry a wider phenotypic reach than previously appreciated.
