Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] What are the clinical features that contribute to the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD) scoring system, and what score threshold establishes a diagnosis of definite BD?

The International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), revised in 2014, assign weighted points to key clinical manifestations based on their specificity for BD. The scoring system is as follows:

- **Ocular lesions** (e.g., uveitis, retinal vasculitis): **2 points**
- **Genital aphthosis**: **2 points**
- **Oral aphthosis**: **2 points**
- **Skin lesions** (e.g., folliculitis-like rash, erythema nodosum, acneiform nodules): **1 point**
- **Neurological manifestations**: **1 point**
- **Vascular manifestations** (e.g., superficial phlebitis, deep vein thrombosis, arterial thrombosis/aneurysm): **1 point**
- **Positive pathergy test** (optional): **1 point**

A **total score of ≥4 points** establishes a diagnosis of definite BD.

In the case under discussion, the patient's diagnosis was supported by **oral aphthae (2 points)**, **genital aphthosis (2 points)**, and a **folliculitis-like rash (1 point)**, yielding a total score of **5 points**. Notably, although the patient had documented neurological involvement—aseptic meningitis consistent with acute neuro-BD—the systemic BD diagnosis itself was established purely on mucocutaneous features, which preceded or coincided with the neurological presentation. This underscores an important teaching point: the ICBD relies primarily on **clinical features** and does not require HLA-B51 positivity or radiological confirmation. In this patient, HLA-B51 was positive but contributed nothing additional to the diagnostic determination, consistent with the criteria's design. The concurrent neurological manifestations, while part of the ICBD scoring system (worth 1 point), were not needed to reach the diagnostic threshold here, though they were essential for establishing the separate diagnosis of acute neuro-BD.
