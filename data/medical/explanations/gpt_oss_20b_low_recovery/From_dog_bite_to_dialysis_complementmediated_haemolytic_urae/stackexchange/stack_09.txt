Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] If this patient had shown pathogenic variants in complement factor H or factor I, how might that have changed her long‑term management plan?

**Long‑term management if pathogenic factor H or factor I variants had been present**

Identification of a disease‑causing variant in *CFH* or *CFI* would have shifted the patient’s long‑term strategy from a “watch‑and‑wait” approach to a *predetermined lifelong* complement blockade policy.  
1. **Durability of therapy** – In patients with a confirmed complement‑mediated HUS (CM‑HUS) predisposition, the risk of relapse after drug discontinuation is high (≈ 30 – 40 % in the first year). Therefore, guidelines recommend maintenance eculizumab (1200 mg IV q2 weeks) until the patient’s renal function is stable for at least 12 months and there is no evidence of residual TMA activity.  
2. **Early discontinuation not advised** – Even after renal recovery, the presence of a pathogenic *CFH* or *CFI* allele obliges continuation of therapy, because partial pediatric‑style “taper” or short courses have proven unsafe in this genetic population.  
3. **Peri‑transplant considerations** – If the patient progresses to end‑stage renal disease and requires dialysis or transplant, the complement gene defect mandates that any kidney graft be managed under the same lifelong eculizumab regimen. Pre‑transplant screening (including anti‑factor H antibody testing) and prophylactic vaccination remain mandatory, but the drug is not stopped merely for transplantation.  
4. **Monitoring strategy** – With a known pathogenic variant, the clinician would schedule quarterly laboratory reviews (CBC, LDH, sC5b‑9, complement functional assays) and biannual renal imaging to detect subclinical relapse.  
5. **Patient education** – Patients with inherited complement defects carry a higher burden of infection risk with eculizumab. The clinician would counsel on early signs of infection, maintain strict vaccination schedules (meningococcal and other encapsulated organisms), and consider long‑term antibiotic prophylaxis that may be shorter than the 30‑day course used in our infection‑triggered case.

**In our patient** no pathogenic *CFH* or *CFI* variants were found, so the plan is to continue maintenance eculizumab for at least 4 months and reassess renal function; discontinuation is being considered only if the kidney stabilizes for ≥12 months with no clinical or laboratory evidence of TMA. If a pathogenic variant had been identified, the maintenance phase would have begun immediately after the first dose and would likely become lifelong.
