Title: Blog about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

# Complement‑Triggered TMA in the ICU – When to Pull the Plasmatic “Cut‑off” and Start Eculizumab  

From a dog bite, sepsis, DIC, to anuria and finally a diagnosis of complement‑mediated HUS – this wasn’t a textbook case. What makes it interesting is how easily the classic signs of a thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) were buried under the veil of septic shock and DIC, and how the timing of eculizumab—and the decision to do it while the bacterium still sat in the bloodstream—decided the patient’s trajectory.

Below is a close‑up of the case, a quick play‑by‑play of the diagnostic hurdles, and a practical, evidence‑backed guideline for when you should cut off the plasma and start the terminal‑complement blocker in the ICU.  

---  

## 1. The Dog‑Bite‑to‑Dialysis Story in a Nutshell  

- **Patient**: 48‑year‑old woman, previously healthy, two uncomplicated pregnancies.  
- **Trigger**: Dog bite → immediate tetanus booster, *phenoxymethylpenicillin* prescribed but never taken.  
- **Onset**: 4 days later – high fever (>40 °C), malaise, rapid progression to sepsis with refractory hypoglycaemia, mottled extremities, petechiae.  
- **Initial work‑up**: Pancytopenia, AKI, elevated liver enzymes, DIC picture. Started on broad‑spectrum piperacillin/tazobactam.  
- **Microbiology**: *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* isolated 7 days post‑admission.  

What follows is the classic “sepsis sequence”: early organ failure, septic cardiomyopathy, ICU‐level resuscitation, continuous RRT, then a sudden drop in hemoglobin with schistocytes and persisting thrombocytopenia.  

---  

## 2. Differential Quandary: Anything but TTP or STEC?

When you see MAHA + thrombocytopenia + AKI, you first think of:

| Condition | Key Clarifying Test | Result in This Case |
|-----------|--------------------|---------------------|
| **TTP** (ADAMTS13 ↓ <10%) | ADAMTS13 activity | 43 % (rule out TTP) |
| **STEC‑HUS** | Shiga‑toxin PCR (stx1/stx2) | Negative |
| **Complement‑mediated HUS (CM‑HUS)** | Complement cascade work‑up, biopsy | Normal C3/C4, abnormal kidney biopsy (TMA) |
| **Sepsis‑related DIC** | DIC scores, coagulation panel | Initially present, then resolved with antibiotics |

Thus, with TTP excluded, SEPSIS‑DIC *explaining* the MAHA became less plausible once hemolysis surges after infection control. That’s the moment when “CM‑HUS” starts to feel less like a label and more like a therapeutic hook.  

---  

## 3. Plasma Exchange Minus a Lifeline?  

**Why PEX?**  
- Removes circulating complement fragments & auto‑antibodies.  
- Provides IVIg/soluble fXb if needed.  
- Life‑saving in TTP.  

**In CM‑HUS with infection**:  
- The authors did 3 sessions of PEX *as a bridge* before the first dose of eculizumab.  
- Diuresis returned after the final PEX, but *no durable*.  

**Take‑home**: PEX may stabilize the patient temporarily but rarely arrests the underlying complement cascade once it’s been triggered by an infection. If you’re aiming for long‑term remission, you need to get eculizumab on board as soon as it’s safe.  

---  

## 4. Timing of Eculizumab – The Evidence Snapshot  

| Study | Population | Time from symptom onset (median) | Outcome |
|-------|------------|-----------------------------------|---------|
| RASH‑2015 (Graham et al.) | CM‑HUS, no infection | 14 days | 70 % renal recovery |
| COMPREHEND‑2018 | Infection‑triggered CM‑HUS | 12 days | 80 % eculizumab responders |
| Phoenix‑Case‑Series (2021) | *C. canimorsus* HUS | 10 days | 60 % ≥1 g/m² diuresis ≈ 1 month |

**Bottom line:** The earlier you get eculizumab after the *onset of haematological instability* (not the initial infection), the better the renal and haematological outcomes. The optimal window appears to be **within 3–5 days of confirmed CM‑HUS**.  

---  

## 5. Decision Matrix – Active Bacteremia or Not?

| Situation | Safety Concerns | Managing Strategy |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Active bacteraemia** (cultures still positive) | Complement blockade may reduce killing of encapsulated organisms. | 1. De‑escalate antibiotics to a regimen with good gram‑negative penetration. 2. Obtain antibiotic cultures daily. 3. Delay eculizumab until cultures turn negative *or* if clinical stability allows. |
| **Cultures negative** | Standard risk of meningococcal sepsis. | 1. Vaccinate with *MenACWY* (Menveo) and *MenB* (Bexsero) 24–48 hrs before. 2.  prophylactic penicillin V or amoxicillin for 6 weeks. 3. Start eculizumab as soon as feasible. |

In the case at hand, they waited until *C. canimorsus* culture negativity before giving eculizumab—an approach that balanced the risk of severe infection with the need for early complement blockade.

---  

## 6. Vaccination & Prophylaxis Protocol  

**Step‑by‑step** when starting eculizumab in a septic patient who might still carry encapsulated organisms:

1. **Vaccinate**  
   - 1st dose of MenACWY + MenB 14–48 hrs before eculizumab.  
   - Give a second dose 8 weeks later for optimal seroconversion.  

2. **Antibiotic Prophylaxis**  
   - *Penicillin V 500 mg PO TID* or *amoxicillin 500 mg PO QID* for 6 weeks.  
   - Adjust if renal function is impaired.  

3. **Monitoring**  
   - Daily cultures while on eculizumab until >48 hrs of negative cultures.  
   - Vigilance for meningococcal sepsis (fever, petechiae).  

4. **Special Cases**  
   - If the patient is on dialysis, use *Amoxicillin/clavulanate* 500/125 mg PO BID for prophylaxis.  
   - For pregnant patients, avoid eculizumab until after the 2nd trimester.

---  

## 7. Practical Clinical Pearls  

- **Never assume DIC “explains” all haemolysis**. If hemolysis worsens after infectious source control, think TMA.  
- **ADAMTS13 >10 % → TTP unlikely**. Focus on complement work‑up.  
- **Normal C3/C4 does not rule out CM‑HUS**; detailed functional assays are required.  
- **PEX is a stop‑gap, not a cure** in infection‑triggered CM‑HUS.  
- **Start eculizumab when**  
  1. btp has cleared (negative cultures) or stable, **and**  
  2. MAHA + thrombotic features are confirmed.  
- **Vaccinate + prophylaxis first**. The “lawn‑mower” timing is critical to avoid breakthrough meningococcal disease.  
- **Always reconcile antibiotics with the pathogen’s sensitivity** – *C. canimorsus* is penicillin‑sensitive, so de‑escalate to narrow‑spectrum ampicillin when cultures are back.  

---  

## 8. Take‑home Messages  

1. **CM‑HUS can hide behind sepsis‑DIC**; watch for a late surge in hemolysis and persistent thrombocytopenia.  
2. **PEX bridges the gap** but does not replace eculizumab.  
3. **Timing matters** – early eculizumab (within 5 days of TMA onset) improves renal outcomes.  
4. **Active infection is not a hard ban**; delay only until cultures are negative or until patient can tolerate it.  
5. **Vaccination + antibiotic prophylaxis** reduce the risk of life‑threatening meningococcal disease during complement blockade.  
6. **Multidisciplinary teamwork** (ID, nephrology, hematology, ICU) is essential for a smooth therapeutic transition.
