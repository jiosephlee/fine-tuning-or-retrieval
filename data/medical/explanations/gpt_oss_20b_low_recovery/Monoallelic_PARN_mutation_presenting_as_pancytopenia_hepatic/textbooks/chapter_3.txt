Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

Chapter 3: ## 3. Genetic Investigation and Interpretation of PARN Pathogenicity

Telomere biology disorders (TBDs) arise from inherited defects that interfere with telomere maintenance, leading to premature cellular senescence in highly proliferative tissues. The core molecular machinery comprises telomerase (encoding the protein reverse transcriptase TERT and the RNA template TERC) and accessory proteins such as DKC1 and the RNA‑deadenylating enzyme PARN. Pathogenic variants in any of these genes can shorten telomeres, but the severity and tissue distribution depend on the specific gene, inheritance pattern, and variant type.

### Clinical Exome Sequencing Workflow

The patient’s medical history suggested a syndromic etiology, prompting a clinical exome panel targeting TBD genes. High‑throughput sequencing of ~4,500 genes was performed, with a mean depth exceeding 100× to ensure reliable detection of single‑nucleotide variants (SNVs). Standard bioinformatics pipelines filtered out common polymorphisms (minor allele frequency >1 % in gnomAD) and prioritized loss‑of‑function or missense changes in known TBD loci. Candidate variants were then cross‑referenced against databases such as ClinVar, HGMD, and internal cohorts.

### The PARN c.613 T>C (p.Cys205Arg) Missense Variant

The identified variant is a single‑base transition in exon 8 of PARN, substituting an AAG codon (lysine) to CGG (arginine) at c.613, resulting in the change of cysteine to arginine at amino acid 205. Cys205 lies within the highly conserved R3H RNA‑binding domain (residues 178–245), essential for catalytic activity. Multiple sequence alignments show that cysteine is invariant across vertebrate orthologs, underscoring its functional importance.

In silico prediction tools (SIFT, MutationTaster, LRT, MutationAssessor, and CADD) uniformly predict a deleterious effect, with a CADD score of 28, placing the variant in the top 0.1 % of predicted pathogenic mutations. No benign reports of this allele exist in public databases; the allele frequency in gnomAD is <0.00001, consistent with a rare, potentially disease‑causing variant.

### Application of ACMG/AMP Guidelines

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) provides a framework for variant classification based on population data, computational evidence, functional studies, and segregation. For this PARN variant, the following criteria were satisfied:

| Criterion | ACMG/AMP Code | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **PM1** (moderate) | Substitution within a critical functional domain with no known benign variants | Located in R3H domain, essential for RNA binding |
| **PM2** (moderate) | Variant absent or extremely rare in population databases | gnomAD MAF < 0.00001 |
| **PP3** (supporting) | Multiple computational predictions suggest damaging effect | SIFT, MutationTaster, LRT, MutationAssessor, CADD |
| **PP2** (supporting) | Gene has low rate of benign missense variants; missense is common disease mechanism | PARN pathogenicity largely due to missense variants |

Combining two moderate criteria (PM1, PM2) and two supporting criteria (PP3, PP2) places the variant in the **Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS)** category per ACMG/AMP guidelines (since pathogenicity requires either higher‑strength evidence or functional confirmation).

### Contextualizing with Other PARN Variants

Monoallelic pathogenic variants in PARN have been implicated in a spectrum ranging from isolated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) to bone marrow failure and hepatic fibrosis. For example, the p.Lys59Arg variant (ClinVar VCV000424063.2) and p.Ala383Val (ClinVar VCV000180661.1) are classified as likely pathogenic and are associated with pulmonary fibrosis and early‑onset liver disease. Comparatively, the present c.613 T>C variant affects a different domain but similarly disrupts RNA processing. Functional assays that demonstrate impaired deadenylase activity or defective TERC maturation would strengthen evidence toward pathogenicity.

### Clinical Implications of a VUS

A VUS designation does not exclude a causal role but indicates that the evidence is insufficient for definitive clinical action based solely on genetics. In practice, this means:

- **Surveillance**: Continue monitoring for progression of bone marrow failure, pulmonary fibrosis, and hepatic dysfunction, as the variant may predispose to late‑onset manifestations.
- **Family Testing**: Cascade testing of relatives can clarify segregation; segregation with disease in multiple affected family members would shift the variant toward likely pathogenic.
- **Functional Studies**: Referral to research laboratories for in‑vitro assays (e.g., deadenylase activity, TERC tail length analysis) could provide decisive evidence.
- **Management**: Treatment remains supportive; the VUS status does not preclude consideration of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation or anti‑fibrotic therapy if clinical progression occurs.

In summary, the genetic workup confirms a rare, deleterious‑predicting PARN missense change within a critical domain, yet the absence of definitive functional data and segregation keeps it classified as a VUS. This highlights the importance of integrating genomic findings with phenotypic depth and iterative evidence to guide patient care in telomere biology disorders.
