Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation and Initial Evaluation

### Patient demographics and symptom chronology
The index patient is a woman in her late twenties who presented with a five‑year history of progressive fatigue and generalised weakness. Over the preceding months, her functional capacity deteriorated to the point that she required assistance with activities of daily living. She also reported exertional dyspnoea, intermittent chest discomfort, weight loss, and anorexia, all of which suggest a systemic process rather than isolated organ failure.

### Physical examination
On examination she was haemodynamically stable but appeared pale and icteric. Herald signs of premature ageing were evident: premature greying of the hair, hyperpigmented knuckles, and a bald tongue with atrophy of the lingual papillae. Musculoskeletal assessment revealed thoracic scoliosis and bilateral thenar atrophy, hinting at chronic neuropathic or musculoskeletal involvement. Abdominal palpation uncovered hepatomegaly (approx. 5 cm below the right costal margin) and an extended liver span (18 cm). Respiratory auscultation demonstrated reduced breath sounds over both infra‑scapular regions, consistent with sub‑pleural pathology. Cardiovascular and other systemic examinations were otherwise unremarkable.

### Initial laboratory panel
A complete blood count showed pancytopenia (the absolute counts for haemoglobin, white cells and platelets were all below normal reference ranges) with a markedly low reticulocyte count (0.39 %). A peripheral smear revealed:
- Dimorphic red cells
- Basophilic stippling
- Cabot rings  
- Hypersegmented neutrophils

These features are classic for ineffective erythropoiesis. Serum chemistry confirmed severe vitamin B12 deficiency and folate deficiency. Iron studies were paradoxically elevated (serum iron and ferritin high), a typical finding when marrow is unable to utilize iron efficiently. Inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR) were raised, although autoantibody panels (ANA, anti‑smooth muscle, anti‑LKM‑1) remained negative.

Bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy demonstrated trilineage hematopoiesis with erythroid hyperplasia and abundant iron stores; no dysplasia or malignant infiltration was observed. The absence of cytogenetic abnormalities further argued against an inherited marrow disorder at first glance.

### Imaging studies
An abdominal ultrasound revealed hepatosplenomegaly. Transient elastography quantified hepatic stiffness at 18.1 kPa (classifying as cirrhosis) and a controlled attenuation parameter of 295 dB/m, indicating grade II steatosis. 

A positron emission tomography–computed tomography (PET‑CT) scan showed:
- Diffuse, inhomogeneous fluorodeoxyglucose uptake in the axial and appendicular bone marrow, suggestive of marrow activation or chronic inflammation  
- Multinodular, sub‑pleural fibrotic changes and ground‑glass opacities bilaterally, consistent with pulmonary fibrosis  
- Ascites and splenomegaly without evidence of malignancy

### Assessment of organ involvement
Beyond hematologic and pulmonary abnormalities, ancillary investigations demonstrated:
- Dual‑energy X‑ray absorptiometry: osteoporosis requiring calcium, vitamin D and bisphosphonate therapy  
- Nerve conduction and electromyography: asymmetric sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy with chronic neurogenic changes  
- Laboratory evidence of secondary hyperparathyroidism, hypocalcaemia and hypokalaemia, likely secondary to hepatic dysfunction and impaired vitamin absorption

Taken together, the clinical picture portrays a multisystem disease involving bone marrow, liver, lungs, skeletal system and peripheral nerves. The combination of pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, osteoporosis and neuropathy in a young adult strongly suggests an inherited telomere biology disorder rather than a single‑organ pathology.
