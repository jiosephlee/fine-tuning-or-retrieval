Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

Chapter 4: ## 4. Management Strategy and Follow‑Up

The therapeutic plan for this patient must be individualized, recognising that the underlying telomere biology disorder (TBD) predisposes multiple organ systems to progressive failure. The objectives of care are to (1) correct reversible, non‑genetic contributors, (2) stabilise organ function, (3) mitigate complications, and (4) prepare for eventual curative intervention should disease progression occur. Below is a practical, evidence‑based roadmap that integrates the clinical findings described in the case report.

### Vitamin B12 Replacement Schedule and Monitoring

Severe vitamin B12 deficiency was the most readily correctable cause of her anaemia and neurologic symptoms. The standard regimen in an adult with a reticulocyte recovery lag is:

1. **Intravenous Cyanocobalamin 1 mg**  
   - 1000 µg daily for 5 consecutive days (to rapidly saturate stores).  
   - 1000 µg once weekly for 4 weeks (to build a reliable base).  
   - Transition to **1000 µg monthly** for maintenance if serum levels remain > 200 pg/mL.

2. **Monitoring**  
   - Serum methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine are sensitive functional markers; both should fall within normal limits after 2–4 weeks of therapy.  
   - Hemoglobin and reticulocyte count should rise by > 1 g/dL and > 1 % respectively within 4–6 weeks.  
   - Neurologic follow‑up (clinical exam and, if indicated, repeat nerve conduction studies) at 3 months.

### Transfusion and Supportive Care for Anaemia

While B12 replacement improves endogenous erythropoiesis, the patient’s baseline hemoglobin was below 8 g/dL, warranting red‑cell transfusion:

- **Packed red blood cells** targeting a hemoglobin > 10 g/dL for symptomatic relief; maintenance thresholds may be individualized based on fatigue scores and functional capacity.
- **Iron‑replacement** is not indicated, as iron stores were elevated; high ferritin reflects ineffective erythropoiesis rather than iron overload.
- **Folic acid 1 mg daily** to support erythropoiesis and prevent homocysteine‑mediated vascular complications.

### Osteoporosis Management

Dual‑energy X‑ray absorptiometry (DXA) confirmed osteopenia/osteoporosis. A three‑pronged approach is recommended:

1. **Calcium** – 1000–1200 mg elemental calcium/day, preferably split doses with citrate or phosphate to enhance absorption.  
2. **Vitamin D** – 60 000 IU weekly or 2 000 IU daily, titrated to serum 25‑OH‑vitamin D > 30 ng/mL.  
3. **Bisphosphonate** – Once skeletal retention and safety profile are confirmed, a bisphosphonate (e.g., alendronate 70 mg weekly) may be started, unless contraindicated by renal function or severe hypocalcemia.  
4. **Monitoring** – Repeat DXA at 12 months or sooner if fracture risk escalates; lipid profile and renal function before initiating bisphosphonates.

### Correction of Electrolyte and Endocrine Derangements

The patient exhibited hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, secondary hyperparathyroidism and vitamin D deficiency:

- **Hypocalcemia** – calcium supplementation and adequacy of vitamin D correction; monitor PTH to assess endogenous bone resorption.  
- **Hypokalemia** – oral potassium chloride 20–40 mmol/day, titrated based on serum potassium and ECG.  
- **Secondary hyperparathyroidism** – treat underlying hypocalcemia and vitamin D deficiency; consider calcitriol if PTH remains persistently elevated (> 70 pg/mL) after nutritional optimisation.  
- **Monitoring** – electrolytes, renal function, calcium‑phosphate product every 3–4 weeks initially, then every 3 months.

### Monitoring of Hepatic and Pulmonary Status

The patient has established hepatic fibrosis (cirrhosis on elastography) and pulmonary fibrosis (ground‑glass opacities with subpleural traction).

#### Hepatic Surveillance

| Parameter | Frequency | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Liver Function Tests (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin) | Every 3 months | Detect liver injury, cholestasis, synthetic dysfunction |
| INR, Platelet count | Every 3 months | Evaluate coagulation and portal hypertension |
| FibroScan (liver stiffness) | Every 6–12 months | Track progression or regression of fibrosis |
| Ultrasound ± Doppler | Every 6–12 months | Screen for splenomegaly, portal hypertension, nodules |
| AFP | Every 6–12 months | Baseline for HCC surveillance (if cirrhosis confirmed) |

#### Pulmonary Surveillance

| Test | Frequency | Rationale |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs) – FVC, DLCO | Every 6 months | Quantify restrictive defect and gas exchange |
| High‑resolution CT (HRCT) | Every 12–18 months | Evaluate fibrosis progression, honeycombing, new nodules |
| Six‑minute walk test/NYHA class | Every 3 months | Functional status and exertional tolerance |
| Oxygen saturation (SpO₂) at rest and activity | At each visit | Detect hypoxemia prompting supplemental O₂ |

All imaging and functional studies should be interpreted in the context of the low‑grade cirrhosis and progressive fibrosis; timely intervention (e.g., antifibrotic agents) may be considered when criteria for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis are met.

### Bone Marrow Monitoring for Progression to Failure

Given the TBD predisposition, periodic marrow assessment is critical:

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)** with differential every 4–6 weeks initially, then every 3–6 months.  
- **Peripheral smear** when cytopenias are noted or if rapid changes in CBC occur.  
- **Bone Marrow Aspiration/Biopsy** if cytopenias persist > 1 month, or if there is unexplained drop in any line (absolute neutrophil count < 1 × 10⁹/L, platelets < 50 × 10⁹/L).  
- **Telomere Length Analysis** – Flow‑FISH or qPCR on leukocytes every 12 months or when marrow changes arise; values < 10th percentile for age are suggestive of accelerated telomere attrition.  

Early detection of marrow failure permits pre‑emptive discussion of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT).

### Genetic Counselling and Family Risk Assessment

The identified PARN variant, although classified as a Variant of Uncertain Significance, is biologically plausible and likely contributory. Counselling should include:

- **Inheritance pattern** – Monoallelic (autosomal dominant, though incomplete penetrance) with potential for de novo occurrence.  
- **Maternal and paternal carrier testing** – If feasible, to clarify segregation and reduce uncertainty.  
- **Reproductive options** – Discuss pre‑implantation genetic testing (PGT‑M) for future pregnancies, counseling on the risks of germline versus somatic mosaics, and the limited predictive value of current telomere testing in asymptomatic carriers.  
- **Psychosocial support** – Address anxiety over family transmission and explain that many carriers remain asymptomatic into adulthood.

### Potential Curative Therapies

#### Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)

- **Indication** – Progressive bone marrow failure unresponsive to supportive care, or for hematologic malignancy risk in TBD patients.  
- **Considerations** – Patient age (< 35 y is preferable), organ function (pulmonary and hepatic capacity, cardiac function), and viral serostatus.  
- **Risks** – GVHD, graft failure, infection, and transplant‑related mortality; must be balanced against the high risk of progressive multi‑organ failure.  
- **Monitoring** – Post‑transplant telomere length and chimerism; early detection of graft failure or relapse.

#### Telomerase‑Elongating Agents

- **Androgens** – Danazol or nandrolone have shown promise in elongating telomeres and improving cytopenias in some TBDs.  
- **Clinical trials** – Early‑phase data suggest modest hematologic benefit; long‑term safety remains uncertain, especially in patients with pulmonary fibrosis.  
- **Use** – Consider as a bridge to HSCT or in patients unsuitable for transplantation, after thorough risk‑benefit discussion.

#### Emerging Therapies

- **Gene‑editing** – CRISPR/Cas9–mediated correction of germline PARN mutations is experimental; not yet clinically available.  
- **RNA‑based therapeutics** – Skipping or rescuing defective polyadenylation sites may restore TERC maturation; still in preclinical stage.

### Follow‑Up Schedule Summary

| Timepoint | Evaluation |
|-----------|------------|
| Baseline | CBC, CMP, vitamin levels, telomere analysis, PFTs, HRCT, FibroScan, DXA |
| 4–6 wk | CBC, CMP, transplant/androgen candidacy reassessment |
| 3 mo | CBC, CMP, PFTs, HRCT (optional), liver panel |
| 6 mo | CBC, CMP, PFTs, HRCT, liver panel, DXA |
| 12 mo | CBC, CMP, telomere analysis, PFTs, HRCT, FibroScan, DXA |
| Every 12 mo thereafter | Repeat all above, with tighter intervals if any parameter deteriorates |

Patients with such a multisystem TBD require multidisciplinary coordination (hematology, pulmonology, hepatology, endocrinology, genetics, and physiotherapy). The primary goal is to maintain quality of life, prevent irreversible organ damage, and promptly identify cues for curative interventions.
