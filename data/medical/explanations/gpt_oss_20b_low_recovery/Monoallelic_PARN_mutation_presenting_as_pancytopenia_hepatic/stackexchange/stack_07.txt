Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] Why might a patient with a pathogenic PARN variant develop pulmonary fibrosis before any bone marrow failure, and how does this relate to organ‑specific telomere attrition?

**Why pulmonary fibrosis may precede bone‑marrow failure in a patient with a monoallelic PARN variant**

1. **Telomere length is determined by the rate of attrition in actively dividing cells.**  
   - PARN is a 3’‑exoribonuclease that trims the poly(A) tail of the telomerase RNA component (TERC), enabling proper maturation and steady‑state levels of the telomerase complex.  
   - Haploinsufficiency of PARN reduces TERC stability, leading to modest but cumulative telomere shortening in tissues that divide frequently.

2. **Pulmonary fibroblasts and alveolar epithelial cells have a high proliferative turnover during repair of subclinical injury.**  
   - In the lungs, even mild repetitive micro‑trauma (e.g., from cytokines or oxidative stress) forces epithelial and mesenchymal proliferation; short telomeres in these cells trigger premature senescence or apoptosis, fostering ineffective repair and fibrosis.  
   - Because these cells divide more rapidly than marrow stem cells in a young adult, the telomere deficit manifests earlier as idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).

3. **Bone‑marrow progenitors divide less frequently under homeostatic conditions.**  
   - Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) are relatively quiescent; a modest telomere shortening may not reach the critical threshold for failure until additional stressors (e.g., severe vitamin B12 deficiency) provoke increased turnover.  
   - In this case, the marrow cytopenia was primarily driven by ineffective erythropoiesis from B12 loss. Only with continued genomic attrition or additional insults would the TB‑irritable HSC pool collapse, producing overt bone‑marrow failure.

4. **Organ‑specific telomere attrition is a recognized feature of telomere biology disorders.**  
   - Different tissues have distinct telomerase activity, basal telomere length, and rates of cell loss.  
   - Pulmonary and hepatic fibrosis can be the first clinical signs because their regenerative demands expose telomere insufficiency earlier than the marrow. Multiple reports of monoallelic PARN mutations show a spectrum ranging from isolated IPF to combined pulmonary‑hepatic disease, underscoring this variable organ predilection.

**Bottom line**  
A monoallelic pathogenic variant in PARN diminishes TERC maturation, causing a progressive deficit in telomere maintenance. The lungs—and later the liver—are the first to experience clinically significant shortening because of their high repair turnover, leading to fibrosis before marrow failure becomes apparent. This pattern reflects the organ‑specific telomere attrition that typifies telomere biology disorders.
