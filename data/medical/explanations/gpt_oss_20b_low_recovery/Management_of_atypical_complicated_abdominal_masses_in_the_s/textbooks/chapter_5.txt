Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 5: ## Post‑operative Course, Follow‑up and Delivery Planning

Following the laparotomy, the patient entered a carefully monitored postoperative period that addressed the complications arising from both the surgery and the underlying pregnancy. The principal concerns were anemia from surgical blood loss, potential infection from the intra‑abdominal contamination, and the impact of the residual pelvic myoma on fetal growth and the mode of delivery.

### Post‑operative anemia management and iron supplementation

The intra‑operative removal of the large pedunculated leiomyoma and the associated haemorrhage led to a marked drop in haemoglobin (Hb → 7.5 g/dL) and a haemoglobin–containing blood volume of 24.3 %. In the obstetric context, Hb < 8 g/dL is considered a threshold for transfusion to reduce adverse outcomes such as preterm labour or placental insufficiency. The patient therefore received two units of packed red blood cells, with a postoperative target Hb of approximately 10–11 g/dL. Concurrently, intravenous iron therapy with ferric carboxymaltose (1 g infusion) was administered to replenish iron stores rapidly, given the persistent iron‑deficiency anaemia (serum ferritin = 25 µg/L). The iron infusion commenced immediately after haemodynamic stabilization, and the patient’s Hb rose to 10.0 g/dL, confirming adequate erythropoietic response. This strategy is consistent with current guidelines that recommend early, high‑dose iron replacement in pregnant women with acute haemorrhage to avoid long‑term nutritional deficiency.

### Infection surveillance and culture results

The patient’s low‑grade fever prompted an infection work‑up. Urine culture revealed Klebsiella pneumoniae, sensitive to third‑generation cephalosporins and macrolides, while peritoneal fluid cultures remained sterile. The postoperative antibiotic regimen was thus tailored: cefazolin 1 g IV thrice daily as a continuation of the intra‑operative prophylaxis, supplemented by clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily to cover the urinary pathogen profile. Serial white‑cell counts and C‑reactive protein (CRP) levels were monitored daily. By postoperative day 4, coagulation parameters, WBC, and CRP had returned to baseline, with no evidence of abscess formation or peritonitis. This approach aligns with evidence that combining a β‑lactam with a macrolide can effectively cover common gram‑negative urinary pathogens while avoiding unnecessary broad‑spectrum coverage.

### Serial ultrasound follow‑up for fetal growth and myoma size

The obstetric team scheduled a routine obstetric ultrasound every three weeks, starting immediately after discharge. The scans assessed:

1. **Fetal growth** – the fetus consistently fell in the 86th percentile on the WHO growth chart, indicating appropriate nutrition and placental function despite the large uterine mass.
2. **Amniotic fluid index (AFI)** – remained within normal limits, excluding oligohydramnios that could compromise fetal well‑being.
3. **Residual myoma dimensions** – the pelvic leiomyoma showed a modest reduction from 9.5 cm pre‑operatively to approximately 7.5 cm at 36 weeks, likely due to shrinkage after removal of the pedunculated component and the natural post‑pregnancy regression of fibroids.

These serial studies informed the decision that the residual myoma would not impede vaginal delivery but could pose a mechanical obstruction to cephalic descent if the uterine cavity were markedly distorted. Therefore, the obstetric team opted for a planned caesarean section at 37 weeks, balancing the risk of surgical morbidity against potential obstetric complications.

### Planning for elective caesarean at 37 weeks

The delivery plan hinged on several factors:

- **Anatomical considerations:** The caudal myoma aligned on the posterior uterine wall and occupied a significant proportion of the lower uterine segment. Its presence could compromise the integrity of a vaginal delivery, risking uterine rupture or fetal distress.
- **Previous uterine surgery:** The laparotomy with myomectomy left a midline uterine incision. Performing the caesarean on the same scar reduced the risk of uterine rupture compared to a scar in a different location.
- **Maternal hemodynamics:** The patient had fully recovered from anemia and infection, and her coagulation profile was normal.
- **Fetal readiness:** At 37 weeks, the fetus had reached term with appropriate weight, and the neonatal unit was prepared for a pre‑term NICU admission if needed.

The surgical plan detailed a low transverse incision, careful separation of the myomatous tissue from the uterine cavity, and meticulous haemostasis. Prophylactic intramuscular progesterone (100 mg) was administered peri‑operatively to reduce the risk of miscarriage or preterm labour, as suggested by contemporary obstetric practice in women with surgical uterine interventions.

### Outcome of delivery and neonatal status

The caesarean section proceeded without complications. The uterus was inspected intra‑operatively, confirming that the papillary‑type pedunculated component had been resected and that the residual myoma’s surface was smooth and non‑vulnerable. The neonatal outcome was excellent: a healthy male infant was delivered at 37 weeks, weighing 3,100 g (85th percentile), Apgar scores of 9/10/9 at 1/5/10 minutes, and with no obvious congenital anomalies. Neonatal screening for iron deficiency and maternal infections was negative. The infant was discharged home at day 5 with no further intervention needed.

Post‑partum, the woman reported no recurrence of abdominal pain or dyspnoea. Follow‑up ultrasound at 6 weeks postpartum showed additional regression of the residual myoma to 6.5 cm. Given its asymptomatic nature and the patient’s preference for postpartum regression of fibroids, no further surgical intervention was pursued. The infant’s growth trajectory remained reassuring, and routine pediatric follow‑up continued.

In summary, the postoperative course was characterized by effective anemia correction, controlled infection, diligent fetal surveillance, and strategic timing of delivery, culminating in favorable maternal and neonatal outcomes.
