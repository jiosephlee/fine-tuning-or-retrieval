Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 3: ## Differential Diagnosis and Clinical Reasoning

The patient’s escalation of abdominal pain, dyspnoea and the imaging findings raised concern for a large, symptomatic pelvic mass, prompting a thorough differential. A methodical approach integrated clinical presentation, laboratory trends, and multimodal imaging to narrow the possibilities before definitive surgical intervention.

### Decidualised Endometrioma versus Cystic Leiomyoma

In a pregnant, nulliparous woman of advanced maternal age, ovarian cystic lesions are frequently encountered. The ultrasound revealed two unilocular cystic structures with regular walls and peripheral vascularity—features typical of an ovarian cyst. The upper lesion extended from the left subphrenic space to the uterine fundus, a distribution suggestive of a pedunculated ovarian mass that has been displaced by the enlarging uterus. Decidualisation of endometriomas in pregnancy can render them cystic and vascular, sometimes mimicking malignancy. The lower mass, however, abutted an intramural‑subserosal posterior leiomyoma noted on ultrasound, hinting at a different pathogenesis.

Cystic degeneration is a recognised late change in fibroids, especially those exceeding 5 cm. It produces fluid‑filled cavities within the fibroid, often with irregular internal echoes. The MRI findings of thickened walls (≈1.4 cm) and inhomogeneous fluid content, alongside focal T1‑hyperintense solid areas, are compatible with a fibroid undergoing internal necrosis or degeneration. The continuity of the lower cyst with the uterine wall strengthened the fibroid hypothesis, whereas the upper cyst’s lack of clear attachment made an ovarian origin more plausible.

### Cystic Degeneration of Uterine Fibroids

Fibroids may undergo hyaline, red or cystic degeneration. Cystic changes lead to the formation of a unilocular or multilocular cavity filled with fluid of variable protein content. These lesions can become large enough to compress adjacent organs. In this case, the size of the lower lesion (≈9 cm) and its association with a 6 cm posterior myoma suggested a primary uterine origin. T1‑hyperintense sequences are common when cystic contents contain blood or proteinaceous material, further supporting the fibroid degeneration theory.

### Mechanical Syndromes (Dyspnoea from Mass Effect)

The patient’s dyspnoea was attributable to mechanical compression rather than pulmonary pathology. The enlarged uterus, coupled with two large cystic masses, precipitated diaphragmatic elevation and restricted lung expansion. The clinical picture was consistent with pressure‑induced dyspnoea, a known sequela in cases of large abdominal masses during the second trimester. Recognising this mechanism informed the urgency of surgical relief, as continued respiratory compromise could jeopardise both maternal and fetal oxygenation.

### Role of CA‑125 Trend in Decision Making

CA‑125 is a non‑specific marker that rises physiologically during the first and third trimesters. In the second trimester, average values hover around 14 U/mL, making the patient’s 41.8 U/mL reading markedly elevated. Although a single abnormal value cannot confirm malignancy, the rising trend from 36 U/mL two weeks prior suggested an active process. This biochemical signal, combined with the imaging features of a vascular, thick‑walled cyst, heightened suspicion of an ovarian neoplasm, thereby tipping the balance toward surgical exploration.

### Exclusion of Malignant Processes and Infection

The work‑up explicitly sought to rule out primary malignancy or infectious suppuration:

1. **Tumor Markers** – CA 19–9, CA 15–3, CEA, β‑2‑microglobulin, and LDH were normal. While not definitive, their absence of elevation lowered the pre‑test probability of a high‑grade carcinoma.

2. **Imaging** – Both ultrasound and MRI failed to reveal irregular solid nodules, nodular capsular thickening, or peritoneal dissemination. The thick walls were uniform, and no visualised feeding vessel or oblique stalk raised the suspicion for a malignant mass.

3. **Cytology and Culture** – Peritoneal fluid obtained intraoperatively was negative for malignant cells and bacterial growth, arguing against a primary peritoneal neoplasm or an advanced infection.

4. **Clinical Context** – No fever spikes, no elevated white count beyond mild leukocytosis, and the absence of systemic symptoms made an infectious etiology unlikely. The isolated Klebsiella infection in the urine was appropriately managed with antibiotics and did not affect the abdominal masses.

By systematically excluding malignancy and infection, the multidisciplinary team justified proceeding with myomectomy for the upper pedunculated lesion, while leaving the lower mass in situ to preserve the pregnancy and minimise operative risk. This reasoned, step‑wise differential culminated in a focused surgical intervention that addressed the most threatening pathology while respecting obstetric safety.
