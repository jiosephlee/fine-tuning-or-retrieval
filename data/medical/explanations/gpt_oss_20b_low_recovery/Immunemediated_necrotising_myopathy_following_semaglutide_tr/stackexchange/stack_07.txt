Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Describe how you would apply the WHO‑Uppsala Monitoring Centre causality assessment to this case and interpret the findings.

**Applying the WHO‑Uppsala Monitoring Centre (WHO‑UMC) Causality Assessment to the Semaglutide‑Associated IMNM Case**

The WHO‑UMC system classifies drug‑adverse event relationships into six categories—*Certain, Probable/Likely, Possible, Unlikely, Conditional/Unclassified,* and *Unassessable/Unclassifiable*—based on 10 criteria grouped into four sets (see Table 1).  

| Criterion Set | What it asks | How it was met (case) |
|---------------|--------------|-----------------------|
| **A. Timing** | How closely the event onset follows drug exposure? | IMNM symptoms began 4 months after a *single* semaglutide injection. The rise in CK (from 337 U/L to >3 900 U/L) occurred progressively over that period. The latency is longer than typical for a direct toxic effect (e.g., acute rhabdomyolysis), but within the plausible window for an immune‑mediated reaction. |
| **B. Course on cessation** | Does the reaction improve after drug withdrawal? | Semaglutide was never re‑administered. The patient stopped using semaglutide after the initial dose and experienced *gradual* improvement of symptoms and CK with immunosuppression, without recurrence. |
| **C. Re‑challenge/Re‑exposure** | Does the reaction recur upon drug re‑introduction? | Not applicable (unethical; patient declined). |
| **D. Alternatives** | Are other causes (disease, other drugs, or chance) excluded? | No statin use, no other known IMNM‑triggering supplements, no other autoimmune disease or infection explaining the necrotising myopathy, and the only plausible trigger was semaglutide. Other drugs (e.g., antihypertensives, thyroid meds) are unlikely triggers. |
| **E. Objective evidence** | Histology, lab, imaging supporting the event? | Muscle biopsy showed necrotic/regenerative fibres, CD68+ macrophage infiltration, and membrane‑attack complex deposition—classic for IMNM. CK elevation was marked and sustained. Autoantibody panel was negative (seronegative IMNM), but seronegativity does not preclude drug‑induced disease. |
| **F. Biological plausibility** | Is there a plausible mechanism? | GLP‑1 receptor agonists have been linked to muscle mass loss and rare rhabdomyolysis in other reports (tirzepatide). Immune‑mediated muscle injury is plausible via drug‑induced autoimmunity or direct immune activation. |
| **G. Prior information** | Is there prior evidence of the drug causing the event? | No prior reports of semaglutide‑induced IMNM, but similar events with other GLP‑1 RA (tirzepatide) have been described, suggesting class effect. |

**Interpretation**

Applying the WHO‑UMC algorithm, all *Positive* criteria (A, B, D, E, F) are fulfilled, and no *Negative* criteria (C, G) are present. The absence of a rechallenge precludes a *Certain* rating, and the relatively long latency and lack of prior reports argue against a *Probable/Likely* assignment. Consequently, the authors’ conclusion of **“probable causality”** is justified under WHO‑UMC: *the event is more likely than not attributable to semaglutide, given the temporal relationship, exclusion of alternatives, and objective evidence*. 

**Clinical Take‑away**

- Use the WHO‑UMC matrix to systematically weigh timing, de‑challenge, exclusion of alternatives, objective findings, and prior data.
- In this case, “probable” is the appropriate rating; “certain” would require a rechallenge or more definitive pre‑existing data.
- Clinicians should remain vigilant for muscle‑related adverse events when initiating GLP‑1 RA therapy, especially in patients with pre‑existing hepatic or metabolic disease.
