Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] Explain the rationale for using a short course of high‑dose methylprednisolone followed by oral prednisone and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) in treating IMNM.

**Short‑course high‑dose steroids + oral prednisone + IVIG: the rational strategy for treating IMNM**

Immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) is a severe, rapidly progressive myopathy in which necrotic fibres predominate, lymphocytic infiltration is sparse, and serum creatine kinase (CK) is markedly elevated.  Two well‑established therapeutic principles underlie the regimen used in the case:

1. **Rapid control of the acute inflammatory‑immune response**  
   • Pulsed methylprednisolone (1 g IV daily for 3–5 days) delivers a high steroid concentration that quickly suppresses cytokine production, blocks macrophage recruitment, and limits complement‑mediated myofibre injury.  In severe IMNM, patients often have life‑threatening respiratory or bulbar weakness; the pulse dose can reverse these deficits within hours to days, as seen clinically in this patient (improved dysphagia and proximal strength after 5 days).  
   • The high dose also dampens the activity of the complement membrane‑attack complex, which immunohistochemistry in the biopsy showed staining for C5b‑9 on necrotic fibres.

2. **Maintaining immunosuppression until the autoimmune process is silenced**  
   • Reinitiation of oral prednisone at 1 mg/kg/day keeps the immune system suppressed while the patient’s muscles recover.  A gradual taper over 4–6 weeks avoids rebound inflammation and permits endogenous regulatory T‑cell re‑establishment.  In this report the patient was weaned off prednisone over ~2 months, consistent with the expected time needed for muscle regeneration and normalization of CK.

3. **Targeted modulation of pathogenic antibodies and immune complexes**  
   • Because many IMNM patients are seropositive for anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP antibodies (the so‑called “autoantibody‑positive” subsets), or have otherwise pathogenic immune complexes, IVIG at 2 g/kg infused over 2–4 days provides several mechanisms:  
     ‑ competition for Fc receptors, thereby reducing macrophage‑mediated myofibre phagocytosis (the acid‑phosphatase‑positive, CD68‑positive macrophages seen on the biopsy).  
     ‑ neutralization of autoantibodies and harmful cytokines.  
     ‑ blockade of the complement cascade, important given the membrane‑attack complex deposition on necrotic fibres.  
   • In drug‑induced cases like the semaglutide‑associated IMNM reported here, the inflammatory trigger may be removed (cessation of the drug), but the immune system can remain “primed.”  IVIG accelerates resolution of this primed state, which explains the rapid drop in CK over 2 weeks noted in the figure.

4. **Practical advantages of a short course**  
   • The combination achieves a swift deceleration of muscle destruction while limiting long‑term steroid exposure, thereby reducing infection risk (the patient later developed a septic shock from a pseudomonal UTI).  
   • IVIG, although costly, is most effective within the early acute phase; continued therapy is unnecessary once CK normalizes and strength improves, as observed.

**Bottom line:** In acute, severe IMNM—especially when a potential drug trigger has been identified—a brief, high‑dose methylprednisolone pulse followed by oral prednisone and a single cycle of IVIG delivers rapid, multi‑pronged immunosuppression, leading to swift CK reduction and functional recovery while permitting quick steroid tapering and avoiding prolonged immunosuppression.
