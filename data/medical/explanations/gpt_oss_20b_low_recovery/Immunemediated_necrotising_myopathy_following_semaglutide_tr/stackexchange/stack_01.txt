Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the key clinical features that differentiate immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from statin‑induced myopathy and rhabdomyolysis?

**Clinical Pearl – Distinguishing Immune‑mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM) from Statin‑Induced Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis**

| Feature | IMNM | Statin‑Induced “toxic” Myopathy | Drug‑induced Rhabdomyolysis |
|---------|------|---------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Onset & Course** | Gradual, progressive proximal weakness over weeks–months; often after a drug trigger but can be insidious. | Rapid, usually within days of statin initiation or dose escalation; usually resolves when drug is stopped or dose reduced. | Sudden, often within hours–days of exposure; dramatic spike in CK followed by *rapid* improvement after drug cessation. |
| **Creatine Kinase (CK)** | Moderate–severe elevation (1000–10 000 IU/L) that **persists or slowly falls** despite stopping the inciting agent; often > 5× ULN. | Mild–moderate rise (few hundred IU/L); often < 5× ULN; usually normalizes within a week of withdrawal. | Extremely high peak (10 000–100 000 IU/L) that **declines within 48–72 hrs** after withdrawal and adequate hydration. |
| **Muscle Biopsy** | Extensive fibre necrosis + macrophage (“muscle‑phagocytosis”) with *scarce* lymphocytic infiltrate; membranous attack complex deposition on necrotic fibres. | No specific histology; often shows mild myophospholipidosis or interstitial oedema; no necrosis. | No biopsy usually performed; if done, shows necrosis but without inflammatory infiltrate. |
| **Autoantibodies** | Anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP (≈ 60 % of cases); seronegative subset exists. | Typically *negative* for these autoantibodies; the condition is considered a toxic myopathy. | No autoantibodies. |
| **Response to Immunosuppression** | **Improvement** with corticosteroids ± IVIG or other immunosuppressants; may require prolonged therapy. | No benefit from steroids; improvement with drug withdrawal alone. | No role for immunosuppression; recovery is driven by drug discontinuation and supportive care. |
| **Other Systemic Signs** | Often mild systemic inflammation (elevated ESR/CRP); may have dysphagia, dysphonia, cardiac involvement (rare). | Usually isolated muscle, no systemic inflammation. | May have myoglobinuria leading to renal failure, but no systemic immune features. |
| **Temporal Relation to Drug** | Can have a delayed/subclinical trigger (e.g., semaglutide in the case report) but CK rises gradually over weeks. | Immediate or within days after initiation or change. | Immediate, often dramatic rise within hours of exposure. |

**Key Take‑home Points from the Semaglutide Case**

1. **Slow Muscle‑CK Trajectory** – CK rose from ~300 U/L at initial hospitalisation to > 4000 U/L over 4 months, whereas a drug‑induced rhabdomyolysis would have peaked and fallen within days.  
2. **Persistent Weakness** – Progressive proximal weakness with dysphagia/dysphonia is classic for IMNM; statin toxicity usually spares distal muscles and does not produce swallowing difficulty.  
3. **Biopsy Confirmation** – The muscle biopsy showed classic necrotising myopathy with macrophage‑mediated myophagocytosis and minimal lymphocytic infiltrates—hallmarks of IMNM not seen in toxic myopathies.  
4. **Immunosuppression Response** – Rapid clinical improvement and CK decline after a short course of methylprednisolone and IVIG is consistent with an autoimmune process; statin toxicity would not benefit from steroids.  

**Bottom Line**

When faced with proximal muscle weakness and a markedly elevated CK, ask:
- Is the CK trajectory **rapid and resolving** (rhabdomyolysis) or **persistent and progressive** (IMNM)?
- Are there **autoantibodies** (HMGCR/SRP) or a **biopsy** showing necrosis with macrophage phagocytosis?
- Does the patient **improve** with steroids/IVIG, or does recovery depend solely on drug withdrawal?

The pattern in the semaglutide‑associated case—gradual CK rise, disabling weakness, biopsy‑proven necrosis, and steroid/IVIG response—points squarely to immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy rather than statin toxicity or classic rhabdomyolysis.
