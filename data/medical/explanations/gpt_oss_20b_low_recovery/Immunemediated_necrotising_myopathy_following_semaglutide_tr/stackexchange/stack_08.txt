Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Given the patient's isolated SS‑B positivity without ANA or SS‑A, how might this serology influence your evaluation for overlap connective‑tissue disease?

**Clinical Pearls – Serology and Overlap Connective‑Tissue Disease**

**Question:** *Given the patient’s isolated SS‑B positivity without ANA or SS‑A, how might this serology influence your evaluation for overlap connective‑tissue disease?*

**Answer:**

1. **Interpretation of isolated SS‑B positivity**  
   - Anti‑SS‑B (Ro‑52) can be found in isolation and is not specific for Sjögren’s syndrome.  
   - In patients without anti‑SSA (Ro‑60) or a positive ANA, it usually reflects low‑titres of a nonspecific autoantibody (e.g., in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, mixed connective‑tissue disease, or even in healthy controls).  
   - The titre in this case was low (2.4, vs < 1.0 normal). Low‑titre, isolated SS‑B is less likely to signify a clinically significant overlap syndrome.

2. **Guiding the evaluation**  
   - **Routine work‑up remains unchanged:** ANA, anti‑SSA/SS‑B, anti‑Ro52, anti‑Jo‑1, anti‑SRP, anti‑HMGCR, and other myositis‑specific antibodies should all be obtained.  
   - An isolated SS‑B does **not** mandate an exhaustive search for a systemic autoimmune disease, but it should lower the threshold for pursuing a detailed musculoskeletal and systemic assessment if clinical features evolve.  
   - In this patient, the lack of sicca symptoms, normal salivary gland ultrasound, and absence of other connective‑tissue disease markers argue against Sjögren’s or mixed connective‑tissue disease.

3. **Overlap disease considerations**  
   - Overlap syndromes (e.g., Polymyositis + systemic sclerosis, or polymyositis + systemic lupus) typically present with multiple autoantibodies, a positive ANA, and clinical clues (e.g., skin changes, Raynaud phenomenon).  
   - Isolated anti‑SS‑B, especially at low titre, is not a diagnostic criterion for overlap and rarely predicts progression to a systemic entity.  
   - Therefore, the serology should not tip the clinician toward classifying this case as “overlap connective‑tissue disease” but rather keep the possibility of a drug‑induced myopathy in mind.

4. **Clinical application in the presented case**  
   - The patient’s IMNM was confirmed on biopsy, autoantibody panel was negative for myositis‑specific antibodies, and no other systemic autoimmune features were present.  
   - The isolated SS‑B positivity was noted but did not correlate with clinical disease activity or overlap syndrome.  
   - The temporal relationship with semaglutide and improvement after drug withdrawal and brief immunosuppression strongly favor a drug‑induced IMNM rather than a primary autoimmune connective‑tissue disease.

5. **Take‑home point**  
   - An isolated, low‑titre SS‑B autoantibody in the absence of ANA or SS‑A should be interpreted as a nonspecific finding. It does not, by itself, push the diagnosis toward overlap connective‑tissue disease. Focus should remain on the clinical picture, histology, and response to therapy.
