Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Pathophysiology] Explain the pathophysiological rationale for using epinephrine as the first-line treatment in anaphylaxis, and describe why a 0.3 mg dose may represent under‑dosing in adults with a body weight of 104 kg.

**Why epinephrine is the first‑line drug in anaphylaxis**

Anaphylaxis is a systemic, IgE‑mediated reaction that triggers massive mediator release from mast cells and basophils.  The key pathophysiologic sequelae are:

| Pathway | Effect | Epinephrine action |
|---------|--------|--------------------|
| **Cardiovascular** | Reflex vasodilation, systemic vasodilation, histamine‑induced capillary leak → hypotension | ↑α₁‑adrenergic vasoconstriction restores SVR and reduces capillary leak |
| **Pulmonary** | Bronchospasm, mucus hypersecretion, airway edema → bronchoconstriction, hypoxaemia | ↑β₂‑adrenergic bronchodilation and ↓α₁‑mediated edema |
| **Dermatologic** | Urticaria, angioedema from mast‑cell degranulation | Limited effect, but rapid hemodynamic support prevents progression |

Because epinephrine simultaneously activates **α₁** (vasoconstriction), **β₁** (↑cardiac output) and **β₂** (bronchodilation) pathways, it counteracts the three most life‑threatening components of anaphylaxis in a single agent.  Prompt administration restores perfusion, prevents airway compromise, and stops the cascade of mediator release.

**Guideline‑based dosing**

The American College of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology (ACAAI) and the World Allergy Organization recommend

- **0.01 mg /kg** of body weight (maximum 0.5 mg per dose) administered intramuscularly.
- Repeat every 5–15 min until symptoms resolve.

For an adult ≥50 kg this simplifies to a **0.5 mg** IM dose.  In the case described, the patient weighed **104 kg**, so the ideal first‑dose would be

> 0.01 mg × 104 kg = 1.04 mg → 0.5 mg (capped by the guideline maximum).

**Why a 0.3 mg auto‑injector may under‑dose an obese patient**

– The auto‑injector used in the ED delivered **0.3 mg** epinephrine.  
– For a 104 kg adult this is only **≈0.003 mg /kg**, **≈30 %** of the target 0.01 mg /kg.  
– The case report documents that the patient required **three additional IM doses** (two 0.5 mg) after the initial 0.3 mg injection to reverse recurrent anaphylaxis, highlighting that the first dose was insufficient to halt mediator release and restore hemodynamics.  

In a patient with systemic mastocytosis, underlying mast‑cell hyper‑responsiveness further amplifies mediator spill‑over, making adequate epinephrine dosing even more critical.  A 2025 retrospective analysis cited in the report showed that patients who first received 0.3 mg were **significantly more likely** to need escalated care (additional epinephrine, ICU admission) compared with those who received the guideline‑recommended 0.5 mg.

**Clinical take‑away**

1. **Use epinephrine immediately** when anaphylaxis is suspected; time to drug is the single most important determinant of outcome.  
2. **Dose according to weight** (0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg) – standard 0.5 mg auto‑injectors or 0.5 mg IM preparations avoid under‑dosing in adults >50 kg.  
3. **Avoid reliance on 0.3 mg auto‑injectors** in clinical settings where patient weight is ≥50 kg; the risk of under‑dosing can lead to repeated episodes, as seen in the reported patient.  

These principles apply to all clinical contexts, whether pre‑hospital or in the ED, and are essential for preventing the severe complications illustrated by the multiphasic anaphylaxis case.
