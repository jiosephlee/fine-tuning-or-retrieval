Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

Chapter 4: ## 4. Follow‑up, Outcome, and Long‑Term Surveillance

The clinical course of the patient demonstrates a textbook evolution from an acute, life‑threatening presentation of dermatomyositis to sustained remission after aggressive immunosuppression. Serial objective measures allow precise assessment of treatment response and guide subsequent therapy adjustments.

### Short‑term recovery

Within three weeks of high‑dose intravenous methylprednisolone and a single course of intensive IVIG, the patient’s serum creatine kinase (CK) fell from 16 184 U/L to 37 U/L, reflecting complete biochemical remission of myofibre necrosis. Muscle strength, evaluated by the Medical Research Council (MRC) grading system, improved from 4/5 in the hip flexors to full strength (5/5) over the same period. Cervical soft‑tissue swelling, the most striking clinical feature, decreased markedly on physical examination and was corroborated by repeat magnetic‑resonance imaging showing near‑resolution of edema in the strap muscles and retropharyngeal space. These changes occurred in parallel with a substantial drop in acute‑phase reactants (CRP < 5 mg/L).

### Long‑term remission milestones

After four weeks of oral prednisone (starting 60 mg/day) and concurrent mycophenolate mofetil (1 g BID), the patient achieved corticosteroid tapering to 5 mg/day over seven months, at which point prednisone was discontinued. Dysphagia, initially necessitating a nasogastric tube, had resolved sufficiently by three months to allow a return to a soft‑solid diet and eventual tube removal. This functional recovery aligns with the normalization of swallowing biomechanics seen on video‑fluoroscopic studies and comprehensive PET‑CT that revealed no persistent muscular hypermetabolism.

### Pulmonary function and imaging follow‑up

The initial restrictive pattern on spirometry (FEV₁/FVC < 0.8) and CT‑identified ground‑glass opacities were attributed to aspiration pneumonitis secondary to dysphagia. Repeat pulmonary function tests at 12 months showed only a mild residual restrictive defect (FEV₁ 80 % predicted), compatible with a baseline of reduced thoracic compliance in the patient’s age group. Serial chest CT at 11 months demonstrated near‑complete resolution of ground‑glass infiltrates and atelectasis, confirming recovery of the airway‑air‑way interface. No new nodules or fibrotic changes emerged, mitigating concern for interstitial lung disease progression.

### Risk assessment for occult malignancy

Anti‑SAE1 dermatomyositis confers a moderate to high risk for malignancy, particularly in male patients with dysphagia. Applying the IMACS consensus criteria, the patient accrued two high‑risk factors (dermatomyositis diagnosis, dysphagia) and two intermediate‑risk factors (anti‑SAE1 positivity, male sex), yielding a high‑risk classification. Initial malignancy work‑up—including CT scans of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, comprehensive tumor‑marker panel (CEA, CA19‑9, CA‑125, PSA), and a PET‑CT scan—identified no tumours. Consequently, a vigilant surveillance strategy has been instituted, premised on early detection of malignancy in a disease that typically manifests within 5 years of myositis onset.

### Surveillance plan

1. **Imaging**: Annual low‑dose CT of the chest, with repeat scans every 18 months or sooner if symptoms arise. Given the absence of lymphadenopathy and the patient’s age, abdominal imaging is performed every 24 months.  
2. **Tumor markers**: Quarterly assessment of CEA, CA19‑9, CA‑125, and PSA to detect organ‑specific neoplasia.  
3. **Endoscopy**: Colonoscopy and esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) within 12 months of diagnosis, repeated annually thereafter given persistent dysphagia and the association of dermatomyositis with upper‑GI tract malignancies.  
4. **Physical examination**: Bi‑annual dermatologic review focusing on cutaneous manifestations (heliotrope rash, Gottron’s papules) that may herald relapse or new tumours.  
5. **Functional assessments**: Quarterly MRC strength testing and swallowing evaluations for early relapse detection.

### Role of maintenance IVIG and tapering mycophenolate

Maintenance IVIG (2 g/kg every 4 weeks) was continued for the first 12 months, a regimen supported by retrospective data indicating reduced relapse rates in refractory dermatomyositis. Mycophenolate mofetil was gradually tapered over 6 months, contingent on sustained remission of CK, muscle strength, and absence of active disease on imaging. The decision to phase out this agent was guided by the patient’s favorable tolerability profile and lack of corticosteroid‑related adverse effects.

### Patient education and self‑monitoring

The patient was counselled on recognizing early signs of relapse: new or worsening proximal weakness, facial or neck swelling, unexplained fevers, or increased fatigue. A home symptom diary was instituted, with prompts to record daily muscle strength (self‑rated) and any cutaneous changes. Education included dietary modifications for dysphagia and safe swallowing techniques. Emphasis was placed on the importance of adhering to scheduled follow‑ups, promptly reporting any new symptoms, and maintaining a healthy lifestyle to reduce overall malignancy risk.

In summary, the patient’s trajectory exemplifies a favorable outcome in anti‑SAE1 dermatomyositis when prompt recognition, aggressive early therapy, and rigorous long‑term surveillance are employed. The multidisciplinary approach—encompassing rheumatology, immunology, ENT, pulmonology, and oncology—proved essential in achieving remission and mitigating both systemic disease progression and malignancy risk.
