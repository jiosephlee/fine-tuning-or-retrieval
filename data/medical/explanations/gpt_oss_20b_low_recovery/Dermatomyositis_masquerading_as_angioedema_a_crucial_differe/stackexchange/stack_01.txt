Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why was a creatine kinase measurement immediately ordered after the neck CT, and what would a markedly elevated CK suggest in this clinical context?

**Answer**

After the neck CT showed diffuse inflammatory edema of the strap muscles but no source of infection, the treating team suspected an atypical “pseudo‑angioedema.” In that setting the first laboratory bedside test that can distinguish an inflammatory myopathy from a histamine‑ or bradykinin‑mediated process is serum creatine kinase (CK).  

- **Why order CK immediately?**  
  • CK is the most sensitive marker of skeletal‑muscle injury.  
  • Dermatomyositis (DM) is an autoimmune myopathy that frequently presents with markedly elevated CK.  
  • In contrast, classic angioedema causes swelling without muscle necrosis, so CK remains normal.  
  • Detecting a high CK level directs the differential away from ENT‑oriented infectious or allergic etiologies and toward rheumatologic evaluation, allowing prompt immunosuppression.

- **What would a markedly elevated CK imply in this patient?**  
  A CK >10 000 U/L, as seen (16 184 U/L), strongly suggests active inflammatory muscle disease. In the context of skin changes, proximal weakness, dysphagia, and the absence of drug or infection triggers, it confirms dermatomyositis (here anti‑SAE1 positive). The elevated CK also correlates with the degree of muscle inflammation (FDG‑PET, MRI) and predicts tissue edema from myositis rather than vascular leakage. Thus, an acutely raised CK shifts management from airway‑monitoring for angioedema to urgent high‑dose steroids, IVIG, and steroid‑sparing therapy for DM.

In short, an immediate CK measurement was a rapid, disease‑specific triage tool that revealed the true etiology—dermatomyositis—allowing targeted therapy and avoiding unnecessary ENT interventions.
