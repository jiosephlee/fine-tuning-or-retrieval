Title: Blog about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

# Multidisciplinary Management of Aggressive Dermatomyositis:
## From IVIG to Rituximab

---

## Why this case should keep your phone screen on little white‑coat‑color mice

A previously healthy man in his 40s walked into the ED with a 3‑week history of neck swelling that looked like Ludwig’s angina. A CT scan showed diffuse subcutaneous edema of the strap muscles. He had a modest CRP, no fever, no obvious source of infection, and no tachypnoea. ENT cleared him of a deep‑neck space infection, and immunologists were called in for *idiopathic angioedema*. When the creatine kinase came back at 16,000 U/L, the puzzle pieces fell into place: anti‑SAE1–positive dermatomyositis presenting as pseudo‑angioedema.

It’s a classic narrative for “here’s a thick – we spell on steroids” but the fire‑walls of modern practice are more layered. How do we juggle high‑dose steroids, IVIG, rituximab, mycophenolate, infection prophylaxis, and a looming malignant risk without turning a good patient into a toxic‑waste‑pump? Below, I distill the algorithm that guided this gentleman to near‑complete recovery.

---

## The triad of failure: airway, dysphagia, and inflammation

1. **Airway compromise** – cervical subcutaneous edema from myositis, not histamine or bradykinin.  
2. **Dysphagia** – evidenced by video fluoroscopy and aspiration‑related consolidation on CT.  
3. **Marked muscle inflammation** – CK 16 184 U/L, PET avidity in multiple muscle groups, perifascicular necrosis on biopsy.

With all three present, the patient crossed the “organ‑involving” threshold for aggressive therapy.

---

## Step‑by‑step treatment algorithm

| Step | Intervention | Rationale & Timing | Key Points |
|------|--------------|--------------------|------------|
| **1️⃣ High‑dose IV methylprednisolone** | 1 g IV daily × 3 days | Rapid anti‑inflammatory effect, work‑up for neuromuscular involvement, bridge to other agents | Taper 5 mg off prednisone every 2–3 weeks; avoid a “steroid burst” without coverage |
| **2️⃣ First‑line immunosuppressant – Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)** | 1 g BID (oral) | MTX avoided due to baseline restrictive PFTs (lung risk). MMF has shown benefit in DM and is orally convenient | Monitor CBC/renal function every 2–4 weeks |
| **3️⃣ Bypass the globin‑centred “busy” calcineurin drugs – avoid azathioprine, tacrolimus** | Not used | Both have high infection & GI risk; azathioprine can worsen ILD | Choose agents with better toxicity profile |
| **4️⃣ Adjunctive IVIG** | 2 g/kg over 5 days (first cycle) + 5 more cycles 4 weeks apart | Beneficial in refractory DM, can reduce steroid burden, addresses immune dysregulation | Watch for volume overload in heart‑failed patients; concomitant infection risk |
| **5️⃣ Second‑line biologic – Rituximab** | 1 000 mg IV × 2, 2 weeks apart | B‑cell depletion targets circulating autoantibody‑producing cells; evidence in DM & PM | Pre‑medicate with acetaminophen & antihistamine; monitor for infusion reactions |
| **6️⃣ Infection prophylaxis** | • PJP prophylaxis: start TMP‑SMX, but if hemolytic anemia → nebulised pentamidine 300 mg q4 wks. • ACE‑I or ARB‑avoid? Not relevant to DM but baseline blood pressure can worsen. | ΔT: suppression of macrophage function with steroids & MMF. | Check haptoglobin, LDH, and CBC before each TMP‑SMX cycle |
| **7️⃣ Monitoring** | • CK and transaminases bi‑weekly until <200 U/L, then monthly. • PFTs every 3 months. • Repeat PET or CT if worsening symptoms. | Capture fluctuations earlier; avoid overtreatment or delaying relapse | Document a V4 “flare score” (CK, strength, PFT, imaging) before taper |
| **8️⃣ Steroid taper** | Start at 60 mg PO daily → reduce 5 mg every 2–3 weeks if no relapse. | Target cumulative dose <2 g over 6 months; avoid re‑exposure to 10 mg/day for >4 weeks | If relapse, pause taper, re‑intensify IV steroids, and resume after control |
| **9️⃣ Malignancy surveillance** | Baseline chest CT, tumour markers, PET (already done). Follow with upper/lower GI endoscopy in 6 months, then biennial. | SAE1 positivity confers ~18 % malignancy risk. | Keep a high index for new rash, weight loss, night sweats. |

## Pearls & pitfalls

- **“Pseudo‑angioedema” can be myositis** – Always check CK in any unexplained, non‑radiologically documented neck or facial swelling.  
- **Don’t forget the nailfold capillaroscopy** – periungual erythema or microhaemorrhages can steer you toward dermatomyositis.  
- **Inflammatory armamentarium is not consensual** – The literature shows heterogeneity; select based on organ‑involvement, tolerability, and baseline comorbidity.  
- **TMP‑SMX → hemolytic anemia even without G6PD** – Monitor CBC, haptoglobin, LDH; switch to nebulised pentamidine if anaemia develops.  
- **PJP prophylaxis is mandatory until you’re below steroid 10 mg/day** – Even if you stop MMF or rituximab.  
- **Steroid taper is a negotiation** – Use a “taper‑with‑hit‑back” protocol if CK rises >2× ULN or strength wanes.  
- **Avoid methotrexate in interstitial lung disease** – It’s a known culprit for exacerbating ILD and fibrosis.  
- **EBV reactivation risk with rituximab** – Screen baseline EBV IGG/IgM; consider prophylactic ganciclovir if high‑risk.  

---

## Take‑home points

- **Treat aggressively but exactly** – Use high‑dose steroids to reverse airway compromise, pair with MMF for disease stabilization, add IVIG for rapid control, and rituximab for refractory cases.  
- **Watch the side‑effect profile** – Hemolytic anaemia from TMP‑SMX, PJP risk from steroids/MMF, infusion reactions from rituximab, and nephrotoxicity from MMF.  
- **Institutional protocols should be multidisciplinary** – SN, ENT, rheumatology, and infectious disease all need to agree on prophylaxis, monitoring schedules, and taper timelines.  
- **Early malignancy screening** – Immunogenic disease + dysphagia + SAE1 positivity → high‑risk. Adopt a structured IMACS‐style program.  
- **Measurable outcomes** – CK, strength, PFTs, imaging. Use these to guide tapering and to pre‑empt relapse.  

---

### Bottom line

The cascade from IV methylprednisolone to rituximab, coupled with vigilant infection prophylaxis and scheduled tapering, leads to an impressive recovery trajectory—even in the face of pseudo‑angioedema, dysphagia, and a high CK load. The muscle‑driven “white space” in the neck stayed healthy and the nerve‑gated swallowing apparatus finally normalised. This case underscores how fluid multidisciplinary collaboration and evidence‑guided escalation are vital for steering a severe dermatomyositis needle through the messy periphery of the immune system.
