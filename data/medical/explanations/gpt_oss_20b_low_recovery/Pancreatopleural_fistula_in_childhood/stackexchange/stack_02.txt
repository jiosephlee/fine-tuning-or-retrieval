Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a child with a history of blunt abdominal trauma presenting with recurrent right‑sided massive pleural effusions, what is the appropriate imaging sequence to confirm a pancreatopleural fistula and why?

**Imaging sequence to confirm a pancreatopleural fistula (PPF) in a child with recurrent right‑sided massive pleural effusions**

1. **Contrast‑enhanced CT of the chest (± abdomen)**  
   *Why first:* CT is the fastest, widely available modality that can simultaneously assess the pleura, lungs, diaphragm, and retroperitoneal space. In this case, the CT already revealed a multiloculated hypodense collection tracking from the right paraesophageal region to the tail of the pancreas, and a hydropneumothorax. The CT is also excellent for identifying vascular injuries or diaphragmatic defects that might accompany blunt abdominal trauma.  
   *What to look for:* a hypodense fluid collection extending into the thorax, continuity with the pancreatic tail, and any evidence of pancreatic ductal disruption or pseudocyst.

2. **Magnetic‑resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)**  
   *Why second:* MRCP provides superior soft‑tissue contrast and high‑resolution ductal imaging without ionising radiation. It can delineate the pancreatic duct anatomy, confirm the presence of a fistulous tract, and identify the exact point of communication with the pleura (e.g., through the oesophageal hiatus in this child). In the described patient, MRCP confirmed a tubular cystic collection from the distal pancreas extending into the right pleural cavity—definitively proving the PPF.  
   *What to look for:* a continuous, tubular connection between the pancreatic duct (or pseudocyst) and the pleural space; any ductal strictures or dilatations proximal to the fistula that may guide subsequent endoscopic therapy.

**Rationale for this sequence**

- **Speed and safety:** In a child with respiratory distress, rapid imaging is essential. CT delivers a quick, comprehensive survey of both thoracic and abdominal pathology.
- **Diagnostic yield:** CT reliably shows the external tracking of a fluid collection and any associated diaphragmatic or vascular injury, but its sensitivity for explicitly visualising a ductal fistula is only ~33–47 %. MRCP, with its excellent ductal visualization, increases diagnostic certainty (70–80 % sensitivity for PPF) and offers critical anatomical detail for therapy planning.
- **Avoidance of unnecessary procedures:** By confirming the pancreatic origin early, the sequelae—unnecessary antitubercular therapy, repeated thoracocenteses, and delayed drainage—are avoided, as demonstrated in the case report.

**Bottom line**  
In a child with a history of blunt abdominal trauma and recurrent massive pleural effusions, obtain a contrast‑enhanced CT of the chest first, followed immediately by MRCP. This two‑step approach provides rapid detection of the pleural–pancreatic connection and detailed ductal anatomy, enabling timely, targeted management of a pancreatopleural fistula.
