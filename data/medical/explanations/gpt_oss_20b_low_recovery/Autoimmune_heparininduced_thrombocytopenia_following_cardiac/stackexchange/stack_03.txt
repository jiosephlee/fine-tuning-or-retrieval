Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] In the serotonin‑release assay, why does an antibody that can trigger serotonin release in the absence of heparin (0 U/mL) indicate an autoimmune (heparin‑independent) HIT, and how does this differ from classic HIT antibodies?

**Answer**

In the serotonin‑release assay (SRA), platelets are incubated with patient serum and graded heparin concentrations.  Classic HIT antibodies are IgG molecules that recognize platelet factor 4 (PF4) only when it is complexed with the polyanion heparin.  Because of this requirement, platelet activation is seen only in the low‑ to intermediate‑range heparin concentrations (usually 0.1–0.3 U/mL).  At very low heparin levels (0 U/mL) or at high, therapeutic heparin concentrations the PF4–heparin complexes that trigger IgG cross‑linking of FcγRIIa cannot form, so serotonin release falls to background levels (<20 %).

Autoimmune HIT (aHIT) differs immunologically: the pathogenic IgG antibodies recognize PF4 that is “pre‑complexed” or self‑activated, independent of externally added heparin.  These antibodies can bind and activate platelets even in the **absence of heparin**.  Consequently, when the SRA is run at 0 U/mL heparin, a strong serotonin release (>95 % relative to the maximal release with 0.1 U/mL) is observed—an unmistakable hallmark of aHIT.

The case reported by St Bernard et al. illustrates this principle.  Their patient’s serum caused 96 % serotonin release at 0 U/mL heparin, a feature that is **absent** in classic HIT but **present** in aHIT.  Moreover, the release pattern (strong at 0, 0.1 and 0.3 U/mL, but no release at 100 U/mL) is characteristic of the pathogenic antibodies that favor low heparin concentrations.  The partial resistance to FcγRIIa blockade (monoclonal antibody IV.3) further supports a high‑affinity, heparin‑independent interaction—again typical of aHIT.

**Bottom line:**  
An SRA that shows robust platelet activation at 0 U/mL heparin identifies antibodies that can trigger platelet activation without the ligand that drives classic HIT.  This pattern, absent in classical HIT but central to autoimmune HIT, mandates a distinct clinical management (direct thrombin or factor Xa inhibitors, avoidance of heparin) and underscores the importance of repeating functional testing when suspicion for aHIT remains high.
