Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Morning Report] What explains the unusually early onset of thrombocytopenia (before POD 5) in a patient who underwent cardiac surgery, and how does pre‑operative heparin exposure alter the typical HIT timeline?

**Answer:**

Early‑onset thrombocytopenia (before POD 5) in cardiac‑surgery patients usually signals *pre‑operative* heparin exposure. In the index case, the patient received 2 000 U of UFH during a transradial coronary angiogram a week prior to bypass. Because HIT antibodies develop within ~5–10 days after the first heparin exposure, the pre‑operative dose primed the immune system. Once the patient was re‑exposed to UFH during CPB, the antibody‑mediated platelet activation occurred almost immediately, producing a rapid decline in platelets that began on POD 1 and reached the nadir by POD 5.  

In classic HIT, the first documented platelet fall is usually 5–10 days after heparin exposure. Pre‑operative heparin “re‑synchronizes” the immune response, so the window narrows to the first few postoperative days. The severe drop seen here is further amplified by the presence of *autoimmune HIT* (aHIT), whereby the antibodies are heparin‑independent and still activate platelets in the absence of exogenous heparin. Thus, pre‑operative heparin shortens the temporal lag and, when coupled with aHIT, can produce unusually early and profound thrombocytopenia.
