Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Management] Discuss the management strategy for hyponatraemia due to SIADH in the setting of neuro‑Behçet’s disease, including fluid restriction targets and monitoring parameters.

**Management of SIADH‑related hyponatraemia in acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease**

1. **Initial stabilization**  
   * Treat any symptomatic hyponatraemia (seizures, severe confusion, or coma) with a rapid‑acting 3 % saline bolus (100 cc in 10 min); do **not** administer 0.9 % saline because it can worsen sodium levels.  
   * Avoid hypertonic saline unless the patient is critically symptomatic; if used, limit the rate to ≤ 8 mEq Na⁺ L⁻¹ h⁻¹ (≈ 12 mmol/L daily).

2. **Fluid restriction**  
   * Target **< 800 mL of total fluid intake per day** in the setting of neuro‑BD, as in the case report.  
   * Include all oral fluids, drinks, and enteral tube feeds.  
   * In patients with mild to moderate hyponatraemia without overt symptoms, a 12–18 h intermittent restriction (no fluids for 6 h, then 1 L) can be initially tried before moving to a strict 800 mL/d schedule.

3. **Monitoring parameters**  
   * **Serum sodium**: check every 6–12 h for the first 48 h, then at least daily until ≥ 135 mmol/L.  
   * **Serum osmolality** and **urine osmolality**: assess daily or more frequently if sodium worsens.  
   * **Urine sodium**: monitor for response to fluid restriction; a drop < 20 mmol/L suggests adequate restriction.  
   * **Vital signs** and **mental status**: assess every 4–6 h during the acute phase.  
   * **Renal function and electrolytes**: daily; hyperchloremia or rising creatinine may signal over‑restriction.  
   * **Duration of restriction**: continue until serum sodium stabilizes ≥ 135 mmol/L and the underlying inflammatory trigger (neuro‑BD activity) has resolved.

4. **Adjunctive measures**  
   * **Fludrocortisone** (0.1 mg PO daily) can be added if free water intake is > 1 L/d and the patient remains euvolemic, but its use is limited by mineralocorticoid side‑effects and is rarely required in isolated SIADH.  
   * **Vasopressin receptor antagonists** (tolvaptan) are generally avoided in acute neuro‑BD because of cost, lack of evidence in this specific population, and the pre‑existing inflammatory milieu; they are reserved for refractory cases where fluid restriction fails.

5. **Red flags for rapid correction**  
   * Do **not** allow serum sodium to rise > 6 mmol/L in 24 h or > 10 mmol/L in 48 h to prevent osmotic demyelination.  
   * If sodium rises too quickly, consider administering free water or discontinuing fluid restriction temporarily.

6. **Integration with neuro‑BD therapy**  
   * Treat the underlying CNS inflammation (colchicine, corticosteroids, or other immunosuppressants as appropriate).  
   * As the inflammatory activity declines, the SIADH often resolves concurrently, as observed in the case report (serum Na⁺ 124 → 136 mmol/L, ADH 22.4 → 1.8 pg/mL after colchicine alone).  
   * Continue fluid restriction only as long as serum sodium remains < 135 mmol/L and ADH remains inappropriately high.

**Take‑home point**: In neuro‑Behçet’s disease, SIADH is managed with a conservative fluid restriction of < 800 mL/day, vigilant serial sodium monitoring, and avoidance of rapid correction. The underlying inflammation is the key driver; thus, treating neuro‑BD often leads to SIADH resolution.
