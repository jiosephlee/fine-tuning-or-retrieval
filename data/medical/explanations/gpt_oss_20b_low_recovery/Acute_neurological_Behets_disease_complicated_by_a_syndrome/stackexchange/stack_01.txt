Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the key diagnostic criteria for Behçet’s disease in adults, and how do mucocutaneous findings contribute to a definite diagnosis?

**Key diagnostic criteria for Behçet’s disease in adults**

| Criterion | Points (ICBD 2014) | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrent oral aphthosis (≥3/month or ≥2 in previous year) | 2 | Most common and earliest feature; high sensitivity |
| Recurrent genital aphthosis (≥2 in previous year) | 2 | Highly specific; strong indicator of definite BD |
| Ocular lesions (uveitis, retinal vasculitis, etc.) | 2 |  |
| Skin lesions (erythema nodosum, papulopustular lesions, folliculitis‑like rash, thrombophlebitis) | 1 |  |
| Vascular involvement (vein or artery thrombosis) | 1 |  |
| Neurologic involvement (meningoencephalitis, parenchymal lesions) | 1 |  |
| Positive HLA‑B51 | 0.5 | Supports diagnosis but not required |

**Definite adult Behçet’s disease** is diagnosed when the total ICBD score is **≥4**.  
In the presented case, the patient achieved:

* Oral aphthae – 2 pts  
* Genital ulcers – 2 pts  
* Folliculitis‑like rash – 1 pt  

Total = 5 pts → **definite BD** (≥ 4). HLA‑B51 positivity further corroborated the clinical picture, but it is not part of the scoring system.

---

### How mucocutaneous findings contribute to a definitive diagnosis

1. **Specificity of genital ulcers.**  
   The International Criteria assign 2 pts to genital aphthosis, the highest single contribution. In the case, genital ulcers appeared on day 5, tipping the balance toward certainty. Genital lesions are seldom seen in other inflammatory or infectious conditions, so their presence is a powerful discriminator.

2. **Sensitivity of oral aphthous ulcers.**  
   Oral ulcers are very common in the general population, but when they recur ≥3 times/month over a year, they strongly suggest BD. The patient had persistent oral aphthae at admission, already providing a baseline of 2 pts.

3. **Supplementary skin manifestations.**  
   The folliculitis‑like rash (1 pt) extended the diagnostic confidence. Skin lesions are often the first extra‑oral symptoms and, while not specific, add to the cumulative score.

4. **Temporal relationship with systemic symptoms.**  
   The mucocutaneous lesions helped link the fever, headache, and hyponatraemia to an underlying inflammatory process rather than a primary infection or malignancy. Their appearance and evolution paralleled the neurological findings (aseptic meningitis) and resolution with colchicine, reinforcing the BD diagnosis.

Thus, in adults the combination of recurrent oral and genital ulceration plus supportive cutaneous lesions yields a high ICBD score and a robust, clinically actionable diagnosis, as illustrated by this patient’s concurrent neuro‑Behçet’s and SIADH.
