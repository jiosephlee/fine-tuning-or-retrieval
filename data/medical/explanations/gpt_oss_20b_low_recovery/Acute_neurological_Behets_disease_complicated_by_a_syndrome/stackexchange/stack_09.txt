Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Which laboratory markers can help rule out malignant lymphoma and sarcoidosis in a patient with suspected neuro‑Behçet’s disease, and what are the limitations of these tests?

**Answer**

To rule out malignant lymphoma and sarcoidosis in a patient with suspected neuro‑Behçet’s disease, the work‑up should focus on the most sensitive and specific laboratory markers while being aware of their limitations.

| Condition | Key laboratory marker | What it tells us | Limitations in this setting |
|------------|----------------------|------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Malignant lymphoma (especially CNS lymphoma)** | Soluble interleukin‑2 receptor (sIL‑2R) | Elevated sIL‑2R reflects T‑cell activation and is often markedly increased in aggressive lymphomas (often > 1000 U/mL or > 2–3× upper limit). | In our case the sIL‑2R was only 704 U/mL (upper normal 496 U/mL). Mild elevations can occur with any inflammatory state—including acute neuro‑BD—hence the result is non‑specific and cannot confirm lymphoma. |
|  | Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) | A high LDH supports high tumour burden. | LDH was not reported; normal LDH would argue against lymphoma, but its absence does not rule out early CNS lymphoma. |
| **Sarcoidosis** | Angiotensin‑converting enzyme (ACE) | Serum ACE is elevated in ~50 % of sarcoidosis patients. In this patient, ACE 11.4 U/L (normal 7–25 U/L) was within the normal range, making sarcoidosis unlikely. | ACE has poor sensitivity; normal levels do not exclude sarcoidosis, especially when extrapulmonary or early disease. Inflammatory cytokines can transiently raise ACE, but a normal reading reduces its weight. |
|  | Serum lysozyme | Elevated in sarcoidosis; normal lysozyme (5.1 µg/mL; ref 5–10) argues against sarcoidosis. | Same as ACE: normal lysozyme lowers the likelihood but is not definitive. |
|  | Serum calcium, hypercalciuria | Hypercalcemia may accompany sarcoidosis. | Not assessed; would provide additional information if abnormal. |

**Practical take‑away**

- **sIL‑2R** is useful for screening lymphoma, but a mild rise (as seen in this patient) is non‑diagnostic and can be seen with any systemic inflammation.
- **ACE and lysozyme** are inexpensive and quick; normal values make sarcoidosis less likely, but negative results do not exclude it. More definitive tests (e.g., chest CT, tissue biopsy) are needed only if clinical suspicion remains high.
- In neuro‑BD, normal ACE/lysozyme plus only a mild sIL‑2R elevation strongly support the conclusion that lymphoma and sarcoidosis are unlikely, as the case report demonstrated when these markers were normal or minimally elevated and the patient’s CSF cytology was negative.
