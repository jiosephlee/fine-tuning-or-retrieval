Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient presenting with fever, headache, mucocutaneous lesions and severe hyponatraemia, list the differential diagnosis that should be prioritized and justify the exclusion of viral meningitis, bacterial meningitis, tuberculous meningitis, CNS malignancy and neurosarcoidosis.

**Differential diagnosis that must be prioritized in this patient**

1. **Acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease (neuro‑BD) with CNS inflammation**  
   *Rationale:* The patient harbors classic mucocutaneous features (oral and genital aphthae, folliculitis‑like rash, HLA‑B51 positivity) that satisfy the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (≥ 4 points). His CSF shows a mild lymphocytic pleocytosis with elevated protein but no neutrophilia or organisms—a pattern typical of aseptic meningitis secondary to neuro‑BD. Brain MRI is normal early in neuro‑BD; focal lesions develop later. Moreover, the temporal resolution of fever, headache, and mucocutaneous lesions with colchicine supports an inflammatory, not infectious or malignant, process.  
2. **SiADH secondary to CNS inflammation**  
   *Rationale:* The hyponatraemia (124 mmol/L) is euvolemic, with low serum osmolality, high urine osmolality and sodium, and an elevated ADH level. The CSF findings and clinical picture are compatible with a central‑cause SIADH, most likely driven by cytokine‑mediated ADH release (IL‑6) in neuro‑BD.  
3. **Other infectious meningitides** (viral, bacterial, tuberculous) – initially high on the list but progressively excluded.  
4. **Primary CNS malignancy or neurosarcoidosis** – considered but ruled out by imaging, CSF cytology, and laboratory screening.  

**Justification for exclusion of the listed conditions**

| Condition | Clinical / laboratory clues that argue against it | Key evidence from the case |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Viral meningitis (HSV/VZV)** | • Mild CSF pleocytosis, not the prominent lymphocytic elevation typical of HSV/VZV encephalitis. <br>• No focal neurological deficits or temporal‑lobe changes. <br>• CSF PCR for HSV, VZV, and serologies all negative. | CSF HSV/VZV PCR and serology negative; no antiviral treatment needed. |
| **Bacterial meningitis** | • Absence of neutrophilic pleocytosis; CSF neutrophil count normal. <br>• Polymorphonuclear cells not predominant. <br>• Negative cultures (blood and CSF). | CSF shows lymphocyte‑predominant pleocytosis (11 cells/µL), protein 0.97 g/L; cultures sterile. |
| **Tuberculous meningitis** | • No CSF acid‑fast bacilli or GeneXpert positivity. <br>• No chronic constitutional symptoms, night sweats, or pulmonary infiltrates. <br>• Normal chest imaging. | CSF Ziehl‑Neelsen stain and TB PCR negative; chest CT normal. |
| **Primary CNS malignancy (e.g., lymphoma)** | • Brain MRI no mass or enhancing lesions. <br>• CSF cytology negative for malignant cells. <br>• Mild sIL‑2R elevation is nonspecific and could reflect inflammation. | Brain MRI unremarkable; CSF cytology clean. |
| **Neurosarcoidosis** | • Serum ACE and lysozyme within normal limits. <br>• Brain MRI normal – early lesions can be missed but CSF does not show lymphocytic pleocytosis typical of sarcoid. <br>• No systemic pulmonary or intrathoracic findings. | ACE 11.4 U/L (normal 7–25); lysozyme 5.1 µg/mL (normal 5–10); chest imaging unchanged. |

**Conclusion**  
In the setting of fever, headache, mucocutaneous lesions consistent with Behçet’s disease, and euvolemic hyponatraemia fulfilling SIADH criteria, the most parsimonious explanation is acute neuro‑BD with secondary SIADH.  Infectious causes were excluded by CSF microbiology and serologies; malignant or granulomatous CNS disease was ruled out by imaging and cytology.  The temporal resolution of all symptoms with colchicine monotherapy further supports a self‑limited inflammatory etiology rather than a persistent infectious or neoplastic process.
