# Commissural Alignment and Effective Orifice Area: The Fine Art of Valve Placement

## Why This Case Is a Game‑Changer

When a patient lands at the cath lab with a third transcatheter aortic valve—after two prior TAV‑in‑TAVs and a bout of endocarditis—you're not just treating a single valve. You're untangling a lattice of metal, tissue, and scar tissue that sits inside the aortic root. The 2024 case from Merkulov et al. demonstrates that a carefully choreographed, CT‑guided implantation can turn a seemingly hopeless anatomy into a functional solution, preserving coronary flow and avoiding the dreaded patient‑prosthesis mismatch (PPM). It shows that, even after multiple valve layers, a supra‑annular, self‑expanding valve (Acurate Neo2) can still pack a decent orifice area if you get the rotation and depth just right. 

## The Anatomical Chaos of Multiple Stent Frames

- **Three bundles of metal**: a 27 mm Lotus, a 23 mm Evolut, and a 22 mm Acurate Neo2 (S). 
- **Coronary protection**: The Evolut’s frame sits high, the Lotus’s skirt sits low—chaotic distances.
- **Coronary ostia and sinus dimensions**: Pre‑procedure MSCT is essential to confirm a ≥4 mm clearance even between stacked frames.
- **Effective orifice area (EOA) threats**: Each new valve raises the risk of foreshortening the effective lumen, especially when the next valve has to lay atop a stiffer, partially stiffened supra‑annular plane.

## The Principle: Commissural Alignment

### What It Means

Align a new valve’s commissures with the closest preserved commissural posts of the previous frame. By doing so you:

1. **Preserve a true, supra‑annular opening**—anti‑folding the leaflets.
2. **Avoid the “neo‑skirt”** crowding the coronary ostia.
3. **Create a predictable path** for future coronary interventions, should the need arise.

### The Macro‑Studio Plan

1. **Pre‑procedural CT**:   
   - Pick the coronaries’ height relative to the valve frame.  
   - Measure the angle of the existing Evolut commissures relative to the aortic annulus (use the annular western and eastern commissures).
   - Simulate a 3‑D model of the triple stack—look for intersecting lattices that might choke flow.

2. **Fluoroscopic Landmarks**:  
   - Use the aortic sinuses and the “sharp” edges of the Evolut stent as a reference.  
   - The “C‐loop” of the Evolut frame clearly visible under fluoroscopy serves as a rotational anchor.  
   - Establish a “zero‑degree” orientation where the left main and right coronary cusps line up with the fluoroscopic beam.

3. **Real‑Time Guidance**:  
   - Pull the delivery sheath toward the aortic root, stop on the “C‑loop”, and then rotate the valve to match the predetermined angles.  
   - Keep the valve’s body above the Y‑shaped part of the Evolut frame to avoid deep sub‑annular insertion that would reduce EOA.  

### Hands-On Example from the Case

- **Landing Zone**: The Evolut frame crests were about 10 mm above the annular plane.  
- **Target Depth**: The Acurate Neo2 was implanted at the *level of the Evolut leaflets*, not the annulus—allowing the Evolut leaflets to “float” into the Acurate frame.  
- **Result**: Early post‑deployment TTE showed a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg (acute physiologic PPM), but 1 month later the gradient dropped to 27 mm Hg, EOA improved to 1.1 cm², and no residual regurgitation. The key: by aligning commissures and ensuring the Acurate strut did not intrude into the Neo‑skirt, the residual area was salvaged.

## Practical Tips for the Cath Lab

- **Start with a “Co-Surgeon” approach**: Have an imaging fellow or a cardiac CT analyst review the scaffold geometry before you cross the sheath.
- **Triple‑Frame Mapping**: Use a software like Vivacass or an image overlay if available—superimpose the 3D model on live fluoroscopy to confirm orientation.
- **High‑Pressure Post‑Dilation**: If you see residual gradients, consider a balloon that can simultaneously dilate the Acurate frame and gently re‑expand the underlying Evolut stent.  
- **Avoid “Deep Implantation”**: A depth >12 mm relative to the annulus increases PPM risk; in a triple stack aim for >4 mm to the Evolut leaflets.  
- **Monitor Coronary Blood Flow**: If you have coronary arteries still accessible, an intra‑aortic wire can confirm any obstruction during rapid pacing withdrawal.

## Clinical Pearls

- **Coronary clearance >4 mm** is non‑negotiable in layered valve anatomy—pre‑procedural CT is your god‑document.  
- **Commissural alignment** is more than aesthetic—it preserves EOA and future coronary access.  
- **Post‑deployment Doppler** can reveal “acute” PPM; time (1–3 months) allows remodeling and valve slippage—watch the trends.  
- **Infection control**: Treat PVE completely before any valve‑in‑valve; no “preventive” antibiotics needed once sterilized.  
- **Lab‑to‑LHS**: With each new valve, create a “Valve‑Stent Scale”—document annulus, previous device size, and projected landing zone depth.

## Take‑Home Messages

- *Story*: A late‑70s gentleman with two prior TAV and a resolved endocarditis obituary met a third transcatheter valve, and he has not had a single pleuritic escape in 1 yr.  
- *Process*: CT‑driven, commissure‑aligned, supra‑annular placement can rescue the effective orifice in complex over‑tuned hearts.  
- *Future*: With more patients surviving initial TAVIs, the landmark of “third‑time TAVI” will become less exotic; effort on imaging, registry, and refined forums will be paramount.  
- *Epilogue*: In the world of structural heart disease, prudence, planning, and the artistry of valve orientation can mean the difference between a patient in a treadmill and a patient in a bicycle.
