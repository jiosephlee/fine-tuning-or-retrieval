Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 2: ## Pathophysiology of Prosthetic Valve Deterioration and Endocarditis

Understanding why this patient developed progressive prosthetic valve dysfunction requires familiarity with two intersecting processes: structural valve deterioration (SVD) and prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In this case, the patient's Lotus valve, implanted in 2014, demonstrated SVD after approximately 7 years—a timeline consistent with the expected durability of bioprosthetic valves. SVD encompasses the intrinsic degeneration of valve leaflets over time, characterized by collagen fiber disruption, calcification, and progressive leaflet thickening or tearing. These changes lead to either stenosis, from reduced leaflet mobility, or regurgitation, from incomplete coaptation. By late 2021, echocardiography revealed severe bioprosthetic stenosis with a mean gradient of 83 mm Hg, reflecting classic SVD pathophysiology.

The TAV-in-TAV procedure performed in May 2022, implanting a 23 mm Evolut valve within the failed Lotus prosthesis, introduced new anatomical constraints. Valve-in-valve procedures inherently reduce the effective orifice area (EOA) because the new valve must fit within the existing frame. The Evolut valve's skirting and leaflets occupy space within the Lotus frame, potentially creating prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM). In this patient, the rapid development of symptoms and the subsequent diagnosis of PVE only 3 months after the TAV-in-TAV procedure suggests that the hemodynamic burden of the valve-in-valve configuration may have contributed to clinical decompensation, even before infection occurred.

PVE represents a distinct and serious complication of prosthetic valves. Enterococcus faecalis, identified in this patient's blood cultures, is a gram-positive organism that accounts for approximately 5–10% of PVE cases. Enterococcal endocarditis is notable for its indolent presentation, often developing over weeks to months, and its association with healthcare exposures, including gastrointestinal procedures. The pathogenesis of PVE involves bacterial adherence to the prosthetic surface, where biofilm formation protects organisms from both host immunity and antimicrobial penetration. On bioprosthetic valves, infection typically involves the leaflets themselves, causing vegetations, leaflet perforation, or destructive lesions that accelerate structural failure.

In this patient, the interaction between prior infection and mechanical failure exemplifies how PVE compounds SVD. Even after successful antimicrobial therapy—6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment followed by oral therapy—residual damage to the Evolut leaflets likely contributed to the severe restenosis observed by January 2024. The infection may have caused leaflet injury, fibrosis, or mechanical restriction that persisted beyond bacteriological cure. By the time of the third intervention, the minimum lumen diameter within the Evolut prosthesis had narrowed to 13.7 mm, resulting in a severely reduced aortic valve area of 0.5 cm² and moderate-to-severe regurgitation.

The choice of valve types in this case carries important implications for durability and reintervention. The Lotus valve, discontinued from the market, featured a mechanically adaptive frame designed to minimize paravalvular regurgitation, but its bulky frame created challenges for future interventions. The Evolut valve, a self-expanding supra-annular device, offers favorable hemodynamics but requires careful positioning to avoid coronary obstruction. The Acurate neo2, selected for the third procedure, features supra-annular leaflets and a relatively low frame height—characteristics that influenced the decision-making process for managing triple-stented anatomy.

The hemodynamic consequences of progressive prosthetic stenosis in this patient followed a predictable physiological sequence. As the effective orifice area decreased, left ventricular afterload increased, initially causing a decline in ejection fraction to 42%. The mean gradient of 83 mm Hg before the second TAVI indicated severe stenosis. After successful TAV-in-TAV, the ejection fraction improved to 54%, demonstrating reversible afterload-mediated dysfunction. However, the subsequent episode of endocarditis and the development of combined stenosis and regurgitation necessitated the third intervention. Post-procedural echocardiography revealed severe PPM with an indexed aortic valve area of 0.4 cm²/m²—highlighting the unavoidable hemodynamic compromises inherent in sequential valve-in-valve procedures.
