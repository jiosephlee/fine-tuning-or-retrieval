Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 4: ## Procedural Strategy and Technical Execution

The decision to proceed with a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation in this patient required careful consideration of multiple anatomical and clinical factors. With two previously implanted transcatheter valves—a Lotus valve from 2014 and an Evolut valve from 2022—the patient presented with what is often termed "triple-stented anatomy." This configuration presents unique technical challenges that do not exist in first-time TAVI procedures or even standard TAV-in-TAV interventions. The heart team approach was essential in this case, bringing together interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging specialists, and anesthesiologists to evaluate the risks and benefits of each potential strategy.

The primary decision point was whether to pursue surgical valve replacement or a third transcatheter intervention. Surgical aortic valve replacement after two prior TAVI procedures is technically demanding because the stent frames of transcatheter valves become incorporated into the aortic wall and surrounding tissues over time. In this patient, the EuroSCORE II of 27.36% reflected prohibitive surgical risk, compounded by his comorbidities including COPD, diabetes, and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding. The heart team determined that surgical explantation would likely require total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement, a procedure associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Given these considerations, a transcatheter approach was selected as the most viable option.

Valve selection represents a critical step in complex TAV-in-TAV procedures. The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen for several specific design characteristics that addressed the patient's anatomical constraints. First, the supra-annular leaflet positioning of this valve allows the functional leaflets to sit above the plane of the native annulus, which can optimize hemodynamic performance when implanted within a narrow anatomy. Second, the relatively low frame height reduces the amount of additional stent material projecting into the ascending aorta, which is particularly important when multiple overlapping valve frames are already present. Third, the Acurate neo2 design facilitates commissural alignment—a technique where the commissural posts of the new valve are oriented to align with those of the existing valve(s), thereby preserving future coronary access.

The implantation strategy was deliberately planned to optimize the effective orifice area in this triple-valve configuration. The third valve was implanted at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets rather than at a lower position within the Evolut frame. This "high implantation" strategy serves several purposes. When a new valve is deployed, the existing bioprosthetic leaflets are pushed outward against the stent frame of the prior valve. If the new valve is implanted too low, the pinned leaflets of the previous valve can create a "neo-skirt"—an additional layer of tissue that further narrows the effective orifice area. By implanting at the level of the Evolut leaflets, the procedure minimized this neo-skirt formation and reduced the risk of severe prosthesis-patient mismatch.

Preprocedural CT imaging was essential for procedural planning. The CT allowed measurement of the minimum lumen diameter within the Evolut prosthesis (13.7 mm), which confirmed severe hemodynamic compromise and helped guide valve sizing. Equally important was the assessment of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, and valve-to-coronary distance. These measurements informed whether coronary protection would be necessary during valve deployment. The risk of coronary artery obstruction during TAV-in-TAV procedures is significantly elevated because the leaflets of the existing valve can cover the coronary ostia when pushed aside by the new prosthesis. In this case, CT modeling demonstrated adequate clearance, and the procedure was performed without coronary protection devices—a decision that proved successful.

The procedure was performed under local anesthesia with both left radial and left femoral arterial access. The radial access provided a route for aortography and coronary monitoring, while the femoral access was used for valve delivery. After positioning a Safari guidewire in the left ventricle, balloon pre-dilation was performed using a 22/40 mm catheter under rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. This pre-dilation step serves to crack any calcification or fibrosis within the existing valve leaflets and create a more receptive landing zone for the new prosthesis. Simultaneous aortography during pre-dilation confirmed that coronary flow remained unobstructed.

The Acurate neo2 valve was then deployed with careful attention to commissural alignment, using preprocedural CT imaging and fluoroscopic landmarks as guides. After deployment, post-dilation was performed using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid pacing to optimize valve expansion. This post-dilation step likely contributed to improved hemodynamics by causing further radial expansion of the Evolut frame and enhancing leaflet motion. Final aortography confirmed correct valve positioning, preserved coronary perfusion, and absence of both perivalvular and transvalvular regurgitation.

The immediate post-procedural echocardiogram revealed a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg with an AVA of 0.9 cm², consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch. However, this initial result improved substantially over the following month, with the mean gradient decreasing to 27 mm Hg and AVA increasing to 1.1 cm². This phenomenon of gradient improvement over time has been observed in other TAV-in-TAV cases and may relate to continued frame expansion, resolution of edema, and adaptive remodeling of the stacked valve configuration. The case demonstrates that even in the most complex anatomical circumstances, careful CT-guided planning, strategic valve selection, and meticulous procedural technique can achieve acceptable hemodynamic results in patients who would otherwise have limited treatment options.
