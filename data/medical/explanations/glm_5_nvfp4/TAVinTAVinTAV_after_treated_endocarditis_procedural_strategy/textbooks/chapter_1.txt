Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation and Medical History

The patient, a man in his late 70s, presented to a tertiary referral center with symptoms of progressive exertional dyspnea, a spastic sensation in the left chest during physical activity (limiting walking to 200–300 meters), dizziness, and fatigue. These symptoms are classic manifestations of aortic stenosis, representing the clinical consequence of fixed left ventricular outflow tract obstruction. In a patient with known prosthetic aortic valve history, such symptom onset should immediately prompt evaluation for prosthetic valve dysfunction. The combination of dyspnea and chest discomfort reflects elevated left ventricular filling pressures and increased myocardial oxygen demand, while dizziness suggests reduced cardiac output during exertion. The symptom cluster in this context indicates hemodynamically significant valve obstruction and warrants urgent investigation.

His medical history exemplifies the multi-system comorbidity burden commonly encountered in patients selected for transcatheter aortic valve implantation. Extensive three-vessel coronary artery disease had been managed with multiple percutaneous coronary interventions spanning 2009 to 2014, involving the right coronary artery, left anterior descending artery, and circumflex artery. Third-degree atrioventricular block necessitated permanent pacemaker implantation in 2014, a concurrent finding often seen in severe aortic stenosis due to calcific infiltration of the conduction system. Additional comorbidities included arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding beginning in 2022. This comorbidity profile underscores the fundamental rationale for transcatheter intervention: surgical aortic valve replacement would carry prohibitive risk in such a patient.

The timeline of aortic valve interventions reveals a decade-long evolution of structural valve disease. In 2014, the patient underwent his initial transcatheter aortic valve implantation with a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) for severe native aortic stenosis. By December 2021, he had developed symptomatic bioprosthetic valve stenosis, with echocardiography demonstrating severe prosthetic stenosis. By early 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg with left ventricular ejection fraction declining from 54% to 42%—a critical degree of obstruction with secondary myocardial depression. In May 2022, a valve-in-valve procedure was performed using a 23 mm Evolut prosthesis (Medtronic), successfully reducing the gradient and improving ejection fraction to 54%. However, the procedure was complicated by a pulsatile femoral hematoma with pseudoaneurysm formation requiring open vascular surgical repair.

Three months after the second TAVI, in September 2022, the clinical course was further complicated by prosthetic valve endocarditis. The patient presented with persistent fever and worsening heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed severe restenosis with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg, and blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis as the causative organism. Prosthetic valve endocarditis carries substantial morbidity and mortality, and management requires prolonged antimicrobial therapy with careful monitoring for complications including abscess formation, paravalvular extension, and septic embolization. The patient completed a full treatment course consisting of six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based therapy followed by oral antimicrobials. Resolution of infection was confirmed through clinical assessment, repeated blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography, and computed tomography, all demonstrating no evidence of active endocarditis prior to the third intervention.

Following successful treatment of endocarditis, the patient experienced partial symptom resolution. However, routine follow-up echocardiography in January 2024 revealed deteriorating prosthetic valve function with severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, with left ventricular ejection fraction recovering to 66%—indicating that myocardial function had normalized but valve dysfunction persisted. This pattern of symptom recurrence after a period of stability following treated endocarditis suggests that infection-related leaflet injury, combined with structural degeneration of multiple overlapping bioprosthetic valves, had resulted in progressive hemodynamic compromise. At the time of admission for the third intervention in June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography confirmed grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a severely reduced aortic valve area of 0.5 cm², establishing the indication for reintervention.
