Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

Chapter 4: ## Treatment Strategy and Clinical Management

The management of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) requires prompt recognition and aggressive immunosuppressive therapy to prevent irreversible muscle damage and life-threatening complications such as dysphagia and respiratory weakness. In this case, the severity of the patient's presentation—marked by profound proximal muscle weakness rendering her unable to stand or sit without assistance, dysphagia, and a creatine kinase (CK) level peaking at 3992 U/L—necessitated an intensive treatment approach. The therapeutic strategy centered on two pillars: rapid induction of immunosuppression to halt ongoing muscle injury, and complete avoidance of the presumed triggering agent.

The treatment began with intravenous pulse methylprednisolone at 1000 mg daily for 5 days. Pulse corticosteroid therapy achieves rapid immunosuppression by delivering high plasma concentrations that more effectively suppress inflammatory cascades and reduce autoantibody-mediated damage than oral corticosteroids alone. This approach is particularly important in severe myositis with dysphagia, as the risk of aspiration and respiratory compromise is substantial. The 5-day duration of pulse therapy in this case reflects standard practice for inducing remission in severe inflammatory myopathies before transitioning to maintenance immunosuppression.

Following pulse therapy, the patient was transitioned to oral prednisone at 1 mg/kg daily. This high-dose oral regimen serves as a bridge to maintain immunosuppression while longer-acting steroid-sparing agents take effect, though in this case, the patient's rapid improvement allowed for an abbreviated course of corticosteroid therapy. Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) was administered at 2 g/kg divided over 4 days, a standard dosing protocol for inflammatory myopathies. IVIG is particularly valuable in IMNM because it modulates the immune response through multiple mechanisms, including neutralization of autoantibodies, inhibition of complement activation, and suppression of Fc receptor-mediated processes. In this patient, IVIG was selected due to persistent weakness and the severity of her clinical features despite pulse corticosteroid therapy. The combination of corticosteroids and IVIG represents a multi-pronged immunomodulatory approach that addresses both the cellular and humoral components of the autoimmune process.

Monitoring treatment response in IMNM relies on both biochemical markers and functional assessment. CK levels serve as a useful surrogate for ongoing muscle injury, though the correlation between CK and clinical improvement is imperfect. In this patient, CK levels demonstrated a marked decline over 2 weeks following initiation of therapy, falling from 1237 U/L at presentation to normal levels. More importantly, her functional status improved progressively, allowing her to regain independence in activities of daily living. The normalization of CK levels and functional recovery guided the decision to wean prednisone rapidly over approximately 2 months—a shorter course than typically required for idiopathic IMNM, which often necessitates prolonged immunosuppression. This abbreviated taper was appropriate given the suspected drug-induced etiology and the patient's sustained improvement after discontinuation of the triggering agent.

The importance of drug withdrawal cannot be overstated in cases of drug-associated IMNM. In this patient, complete avoidance of semaglutide and other glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists was a critical component of the management strategy. Unlike idiopathic IMNM, which requires ongoing immunosuppression, drug-induced immune myopathies may resolve with removal of the offending agent and a limited course of immunosuppression. The patient's sustained remission off all immunosuppression for 8 months, with continued avoidance of GLP-1 receptor agonists, supports the classification of this as a drug-induced phenomenon rather than a de novo autoimmune condition.

Immunosuppressive therapy carries inherent risks that require vigilance. This patient developed septic shock from a pseudomonal urinary tract infection approximately 12 days after discharge, highlighting the infectious complications associated with high-dose corticosteroids and IVIG. Despite this serious adverse event, her CK remained normal during the readmission, indicating that the myopathy remained controlled. The septic episode did not prompt escalation of immunosuppression, and she ultimately achieved full recovery without recurrence of her myopathy. This outcome reinforces the importance of balancing aggressive immunosuppression in severe disease with appropriate infection prophylaxis and close monitoring for complications.
