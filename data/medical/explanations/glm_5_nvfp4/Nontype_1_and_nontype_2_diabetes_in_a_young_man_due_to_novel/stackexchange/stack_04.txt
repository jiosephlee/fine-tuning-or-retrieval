Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] What is the first-line pharmacologic treatment for HNF1A-MODY, and what alternative agents have demonstrated efficacy in this population?

**First-line therapy for HNF1A-MODY is low-dose sulfonylureas.**

Sulfonylureas are the pharmacologic mainstay because they directly address the underlying pathophysiology—HNF1A mutations cause progressive β-cell dysfunction with impaired insulin secretion, but the β-cells remain responsive to sulfonylureas, which close ATP-sensitive potassium channels and stimulate insulin release independent of glucose sensing. Patients with HNF1A-MODY often exhibit remarkable sensitivity to sulfonylureas, sometimes allowing transition off insulin entirely.

**Alternative agents with demonstrated efficacy include GLP-1 receptor agonists.** As noted in the discussion, a small randomized crossover trial showed that liraglutide and glimepiride achieved comparable reductions in fasting and postprandial glucose, but GLP-1 receptor agonists carried a lower risk of hypoglycemia. In this case, after genetic confirmation, insulin was discontinued and replaced with dulaglutide, which—combined with metformin—reduced the patient's HbA1c from 10.2% to 6.0% within months. GLP-1 receptor agonists are particularly valuable when β-cell function has declined or when patients are prone to sulfonylurea-induced hypoglycemia.

**SGLT-2 inhibitors should generally be avoided** in HNF1A-MODY. Because HNF1A regulates SGLT2 expression in the renal tubules, these patients already have a reduced renal threshold for glucose reabsorption. Further pharmacologic inhibition of SGLT2 increases the risk of severe dehydration, ketoacidosis, and vascular collapse.

This case exemplifies precision medicine: genetic diagnosis directly informed the decision to discontinue insulin and transition to oral and injectable non-insulin therapies, achieving sustained glycemic control.
