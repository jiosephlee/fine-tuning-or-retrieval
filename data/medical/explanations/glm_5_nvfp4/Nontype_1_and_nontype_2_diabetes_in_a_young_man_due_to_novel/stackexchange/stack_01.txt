Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Pathophysiology] How does a mutation in the HNF1A gene lead to both impaired insulin secretion and glycosuria in patients with HNF1A-MODY?

**Clinical Teaching Answer:**

The HNF1A gene encodes a transcription factor expressed in pancreatic β-cells, hepatocytes, and renal proximal tubules. Understanding its role in these tissues explains the dual presentation of impaired insulin secretion and glycosuria seen in this patient.

**In the pancreas**, HNF1A regulates the expression of genes critical for glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, including GLUT2 (the glucose transporter) and pyruvate kinase. GLUT2 allows glucose entry into β-cells, and pyruvate kinase facilitates glucose metabolism through glycolysis, generating ATP that triggers insulin release via closure of ATP-sensitive potassium channels. As noted in the case discussion, mutations in HNF1A lead to "impaired expression of insulin and GLUT2 genes, as well as reduced pyruvate kinase activity," resulting in defective glucose sensing and progressive β-cell dysfunction. This explains why our patient had an insufficient C-peptide response on glucagon stimulation testing (stimulated C-peptide of 0.83 nmol/L) despite having no evidence of autoimmune destruction.

**In the kidney**, HNF1A regulates the expression of SGLT2 (sodium-glucose cotransporter 2), the primary transporter responsible for glucose reabsorption in the proximal tubule. When HNF1A function is impaired, SGLT2 expression is reduced, lowering the renal threshold for glucose reabsorption. This produces glycosuria at lower blood glucose concentrations than in typical diabetes—a hallmark clinical feature demonstrated in our patient, who presented with "hyperglycaemia and glycosuria" on routine examination.

This dual mechanism is pathognomonic for HNF1A-MODY and explains why patients may present with relatively modest hyperglycemia yet significant glycosuria, an important clinical clue that should prompt consideration of monogenic diabetes.
