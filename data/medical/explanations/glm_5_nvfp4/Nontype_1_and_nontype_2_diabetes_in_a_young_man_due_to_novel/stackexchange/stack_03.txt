Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What laboratory findings help distinguish HNF1A-MODY from type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes during the diagnostic evaluation?

### Clinical Teaching Answer

Distinguishing HNF1A-MODY from type 1 and type 2 diabetes requires integrating several key laboratory findings, as illustrated in this case.

**First, islet autoantibodies are essential.** In HNF1A-MODY, autoantibodies—including glutamic acid decarboxylase antibodies (GADA), islet antigen-2 antibodies (IA-2A), and zinc transporter 8 antibodies (ZnT8)—are typically **negative**, whereas they are positive in approximately 85–90% of patients with type 1 diabetes. In this case, both GADA and IA-2A were negative, effectively ruling out autoimmune type 1 diabetes and prompting consideration of a monogenic form.

**Second, C-peptide levels help differentiate HNF1A-MODY from type 1 diabetes.** Patients with HNF1A-MODY have preserved or moderately reduced β-cell function, with measurable C-peptide levels. A stimulated C-peptide level >0.60 nmol/L generally indicates preserved endogenous insulin secretion. In this patient, the glucagon stimulation test demonstrated a stimulated C-peptide of 0.83 nmol/L, confirming residual β-cell function inconsistent with classic type 1 diabetes, where C-peptide is typically low or undetectable. However, as noted in the case, C-peptide can be preserved early in type 1 diabetes, so this finding should be interpreted alongside autoantibody status.

**Third, glycosuria at relatively lower glucose thresholds is a distinctive feature of HNF1A-MODY.** HNF1A regulates SGLT2 expression in the renal tubules; mutations reduce the renal threshold for glucose reabsorption, leading to glycosuria even at modest hyperglycemia. This patient presented with glycosuria detected incidentally during a health examination—an important clue.

**Fourth, insulin resistance markers help distinguish HNF1A-MODY from type 2 diabetes.** Patients with HNF1A-MODY typically lack features of insulin resistance. This patient had a BMI of 24.6 kg/m², no acanthosis nigricans, and no clinical evidence of insulin resistance—unlike typical type 2 diabetes presentations, where obesity, acanthosis nigricans, and metabolic syndrome features are common.

**Ultimately, genetic testing is the definitive diagnostic tool.** When clinical and laboratory features suggest HNF1A-MODY—early-onset diabetes, negative autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide, glycosuria, and a strong family history in an autosomal dominant pattern—next-generation sequencing confirms the diagnosis, as it did in this case with identification of a novel pathogenic HNF1A variant.

**In summary:** Negative islet autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide secretion, glycosuria at lower glucose thresholds, and absence of insulin resistance markers together support HNF1A-MODY over type 1 or type 2 diabetes, with genetic testing providing definitive confirmation.
