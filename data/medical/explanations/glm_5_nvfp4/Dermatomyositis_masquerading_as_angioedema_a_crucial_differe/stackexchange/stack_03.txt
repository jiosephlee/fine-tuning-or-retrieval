Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] What are the characteristic histopathological findings on muscle biopsy that distinguish dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies such as polymyositis and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy?

### Clinical Teaching Answer

The muscle biopsy in this case demonstrates several classic histopathological features that distinguish dermatomyositis from polymyositis and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM).

**Perifascicular distribution of pathology** is the hallmark of dermatomyositis. As seen in this patient, necrotic, vacuolated, and regenerative myofibers were distributed in a perifascicular pattern—meaning the abnormalities concentrate at the edges of muscle fascicles rather than diffusely throughout. This contrasts with polymyositis, where inflammation and fiber invasion occur in an **endomysial** pattern, with CD8+ T cells attacking individual myofibers throughout the fascicle.

**Perimysial inflammation** rather than endomysial inflammation is characteristic of dermatomyositis. This patient showed scattered perimysial inflammation with dispersed histiocytes, reflecting the humorally-mediated pathology of dermatomyositis, as opposed to the direct T-cell mediated cytotoxicity seen in polymyositis.

**C5b-9 (membrane attack complex) deposition** in perifascicular capillaries is virtually pathognomonic for dermatomyositis. This patient's immunohistochemistry demonstrated exactly this finding, reflecting the underlying complement-mediated microangiopathy that characterizes dermatomyositis. This capillary deposition is absent in polymyositis and IMNM.

In contrast, **immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy** shows prominent fiber necrosis and regeneration but with **minimal or absent inflammatory infiltrates**—lacking both the perimysial inflammation of dermatomyositis and the endomysial CD8+ infiltrates of polymyositis. IMNM also lacks the perifascicular capillary C5b-9 deposition seen here.

In summary, the triad of perifascicular pathology, perimysial inflammation, and C5b-9 capillary deposition—as demonstrated in this patient's biopsy—reliably distinguishes dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies.
