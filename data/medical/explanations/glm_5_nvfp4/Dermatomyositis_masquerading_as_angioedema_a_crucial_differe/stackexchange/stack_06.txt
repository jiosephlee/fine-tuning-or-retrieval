Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] What factors should guide the selection of immunosuppressive agents in dermatomyositis, particularly when choosing between methotrexate and mycophenolate mofetil in patients with dysphagia or potential pulmonary involvement?

**Clinical Teaching Answer**

The selection between methotrexate and mycophenolate mofetil in dermatomyositis should be guided primarily by the presence or risk of interstitial lung disease (ILD), as well as dysphagia severity and patient-specific factors.

**Pulmonary involvement favors mycophenolate mofetil.** Methotrexate carries a well-established risk of drug-induced pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis, which can be difficult to distinguish from underlying ILD—a common and serious manifestation of dermatomyositis. In this case, the patient had abnormal pulmonary function tests with a restrictive pattern and CT evidence of peribronchial consolidation from aspiration, necessitating an agent with a favorable pulmonary safety profile. Mycophenolate mofetil has demonstrated efficacy in treating dermatomyositis-associated ILD and lacks the pulmonary toxicity associated with methotrexate. For this reason, the treating team appropriately avoided methotrexate, explicitly citing its association with interstitial lung disease and pulmonary fibrosis.

**Dysphagia influences route and tolerability considerations.** While both agents are available orally, methotrexate can cause mucositis and gastrointestinal upset, which may compound the nutritional challenges in patients with significant dysphagia. This patient had severe pharyngeal dysphagia requiring nasogastric tube feeding, making gastrointestinal tolerability particularly relevant. Mycophenolate was selected in part due to its tolerability profile.

**Anti-SAE1 antibody status adds prognostic context.** In SAE1-positive dermatomyositis, dysphagia and ILD occur in approximately 46% and 43% of patients, respectively. This high prevalence of pulmonary involvement should prompt clinicians to favor mycophenolate as a first-line steroid-sparing agent in this antibody subtype.

**Practical bottom line:** In dermatomyositis patients with any evidence of ILD, abnormal pulmonary function tests, or antibodies associated with pulmonary involvement (including anti-SAE1), mycophenolate mofetil is preferred over methotrexate. Methotrexate remains a reasonable option in patients without pulmonary concerns, particularly when cutaneous disease predominates.
