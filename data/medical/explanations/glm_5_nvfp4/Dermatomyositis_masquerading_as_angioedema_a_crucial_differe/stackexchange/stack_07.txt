Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] How should malignancy screening be approached in a patient newly diagnosed with dermatomyositis, and what clinical and serological factors are associated with higher malignancy risk?

**Clinical Teaching Answer**

In any adult patient newly diagnosed with dermatomyositis, malignancy screening is mandatory because the association between dermatomyositis and malignancy is well established, with an estimated 15–25% of adult patients having an underlying malignancy at diagnosis or developing one within 3–5 years. Screening should be comprehensive and guided by the patient's individual risk profile.

**Approach to Malignancy Screening:**
The International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) recommends age-appropriate screening combined with risk-stratified additional investigations. At minimum, this includes:
- Thorough history and physical examination
- Basic laboratory investigations (complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel)
- Age-appropriate cancer screening (colonoscopy for patients ≥45 years, prostate-specific antigen for males, mammography and cervical screening for females)
- Cross-sectional imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis (CT or MRI)
- Consideration of positron emission tomography (PET)-CT, particularly in high-risk patients, as it can detect occult malignancies not visualized on conventional imaging

In this case, the patient underwent appropriately comprehensive screening, including CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, a PET scan, and tumor markers, with plans for gastroscopy and colonoscopy. The PET scan was particularly valuable in excluding metabolically active disease elsewhere, given his high-risk status.

**Clinical Factors Associated with Higher Malignancy Risk:**
Several clinical features portend increased malignancy risk:
- **Older age at onset** (particularly >45 years)
- **Male sex** (as demonstrated in this case, where male sex was considered an intermediate risk factor)
- **Dysphagia** (designated a high-risk factor in this case)
- **Rapid disease onset** or severe cutaneous manifestations

**Serological Factors Associated with Higher Malignancy Risk:**
Myositis-specific autoantibodies significantly stratify malignancy risk:
- **Anti-TIF1-γ (anti-p155/140)**: Strongly associated with malignancy, particularly in adults; the risk can exceed 50%
- **Anti-NXP2 (anti-p140)**: Associated with malignancy in adults
- **Anti-SAE1**: Associated with intermediate malignancy risk; the case notes an 18% malignancy rate in literature review of SAE1-positive patients

Conversely, **anti-Mi-2** and **anti-Jo-1** antibodies are associated with lower malignancy risk.

**Risk Stratification in This Case:**
This patient was appropriately classified as high risk for malignancy based on two high-risk factors (dermatomyositis diagnosis itself and dysphagia) and two intermediate-risk factors (SAE1 antibody positivity and male sex). This risk stratification appropriately guided the intensity of his malignancy screening and the plan for ongoing surveillance.

**Ongoing Surveillance:**
Malignancy risk persists for 3–5 years following dermatomyositis diagnosis, necessitating continued vigilance. As noted in the case, the patient will continue to be monitored based on symptoms, clinical examination, regular blood tests, and relevant imaging. Repeat screening at regular intervals is warranted even if initial evaluation is negative, particularly in high-risk patients.
