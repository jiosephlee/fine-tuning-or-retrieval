Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] What infection prevention measures must be implemented before initiating eculizumab therapy, and what is the rationale for these precautions?

### Infection Prevention Before Eculizumab Therapy

Before initiating eculizumab, two critical infection prevention measures are required: **meningococcal vaccination** and **antimicrobial prophylaxis** when adequate immunization cannot be achieved prior to therapy.

**Meningococcal Vaccination**

Patients must receive vaccines covering all major meningococcal serogroups. In this case, the patient received Menveo (covering serogroups A, C, Y, and W) and Bexsero (covering serogroup B) before eculizumab initiation. Ideally, vaccines should be administered at least **2 weeks before** starting complement blockade to allow for adequate immune response.

**Antimicrobial Prophylaxis**

When the 2-week window cannot be met, antibiotic prophylaxis is mandatory. This patient received prophylactic phenoxymethylpenicillin (Apocillin) because vaccination occurred immediately before eculizumab initiation, precluding full immunologic protection.

**Rationale**

Eculizumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits cleavage of complement component C5, thereby preventing formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9). While highly effective for complement-mediated HUS, this blockade creates a functional deficiency of terminal complement activity, which is essential for defense against encapsulated bacteria—particularly *Neisseria meningitidis*. Patients on eculizumab carry a **1000- to 2000-fold increased risk** of invasive meningococcal disease compared to the general population.

As highlighted in this case, terminal complement components are also important for host defense against *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*. This underscores the broader principle: complement blockade during active bacteremia requires careful risk-benefit assessment. Here, eculizumab was appropriately withheld until blood cultures were negative, minimizing infection risk while still enabling timely treatment of the underlying complement-mediated TMA.

**Key Teaching Point**

Vaccination and prophylaxis remain essential even after complement blockade is discontinued, as vaccine responses may be suboptimal and infection risk persists.
