Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] What imaging modality offers the highest sensitivity for visualizing the fistula tract and pancreatic duct anatomy in suspected pancreatopleural fistula, and what is its approximate sensitivity?

### Answer

**Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)** offers the highest sensitivity among imaging modalities for visualizing the fistula tract and pancreatic duct anatomy in suspected pancreatopleural fistula, with an approximate sensitivity of **70%–80%**.

### Clinical Reasoning

The diagnosis of pancreatopleural fistula (PPF) requires demonstration of a communication between the pancreatic ductal system and the pleural cavity. While CT scanning is often the initial imaging obtained—demonstrating pancreatic calcifications, pseudocysts, or ductal dilation—it depicts the actual fistula tract in only **33%–47%** of cases. In this case, the CT showed a multiloculated hypodense collection tracking from the right paraesophageal region to the pancreatic tail, which was suggestive but not definitive.

MRCP provides superior soft tissue contrast and can visualize the entire pancreatic ductal anatomy and fistula tract in a noninvasive manner without requiring contrast administration or endoscopic instrumentation. In this patient, MRCP was performed after the CT raised suspicion, and it definitively confirmed the diagnosis by demonstrating a tubular cystic collection communicating with the distal pancreatic duct, extending posteriorly through the retroperitoneum and superiorly through the esophageal hiatus into the right pleural cavity.

It is important to note that **endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)** remains the gold standard for therapeutic planning and intervention, with fistula visualization rates in children ranging from **38%–78%**. However, given its invasive nature and potential complications, MRCP is the preferred initial diagnostic imaging modality for delineating fistula anatomy, reserving ERCP for cases requiring therapeutic intervention such as transpapillary stenting.
