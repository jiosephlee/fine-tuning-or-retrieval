Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] In a patient with pancreatopleural fistula who fails conservative management after 2-3 weeks, what endoscopic intervention is typically attempted, and what is its mechanism of action?

**Endoscopic Intervention for Pancreatopleural Fistula After Failed Conservative Management**

When conservative management fails after 2–3 weeks, the endoscopic intervention of choice is **endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with transpapillary pancreatic duct stent placement**, often accompanied by balloon dilatation if ductal stenosis is present.

**Mechanism of Action:**

The pancreatic duct stent works by **reducing intraductal pressure** within the pancreatic duct. By providing a low-resistance pathway for pancreatic secretions to flow physiologically into the duodenum, the stent bypasses the site of ductal disruption and decreases hydrostatic pressure at the fistula. This pressure reduction allows the fistulous tract to seal and close spontaneously. Additionally, in cases where ductal stenosis contributes to upstream hypertension, balloon dilatation of the stricture further facilitates drainage and healing.

**Clinical Context from the Case:**

As noted in the discussion, endoscopic intervention typically closes the fistula within 2–3 weeks in approximately 48% of patients, with a stent duration of 4 weeks to 6 months. Importantly, ERCP has demonstrated a 100% success rate in pediatric cases and a long-term success rate exceeding 90% in mixed-age cohorts. In this particular patient, conservative management with bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, octreotide infusion, and thoracic drainage succeeded within 10 days—making endoscopic intervention unnecessary. However, the case illustrates the established treatment algorithm: conservative measures are attempted first, and if drainage persists beyond 2–3 weeks, ERCP with stenting is the next step before considering surgical options such as distal pancreatectomy or Puestow procedure.
