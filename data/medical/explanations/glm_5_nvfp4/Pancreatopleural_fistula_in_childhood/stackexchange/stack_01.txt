Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] A child presents with recurrent hemorrhagic pleural effusion. What pleural fluid amylase level is considered pathognomonic for pancreatopleural fistula, and how does this help differentiate it from other causes of amylase-rich pleural effusions?

**Clinical Teaching Answer**

A pleural fluid amylase level **≥1000 IU/L is considered pathognomonic for pancreatopleural fistula (PPF)**. In this case, the pleural fluid amylase was markedly elevated at 6118 U/L, which strongly supported the diagnosis.

This threshold helps differentiate PPF from other causes of amylase-rich pleural effusions through both the magnitude of elevation and the isoenzyme type. While esophageal perforation (Boerhaave's syndrome), malignancy, and tuberculosis can all cause elevated pleural fluid amylase, these conditions typically produce more modest elevations and originate from **salivary-type amylase**. In contrast, PPF produces **pancreatic-type amylase**, reflecting direct leakage of pancreatic secretions through a fistulous tract.

The diagnostic delay in this case illustrates an important clinical pearl: PPF wears a "pulmonary mask." The child presented with recurrent hemorrhagic right-sided pleural effusion and respiratory distress, leading to initial empirical treatment for tuberculosis. The diagnosis became apparent only after repeated history-taking revealed blunt abdominal trauma from a bicycle handlebar, and imaging demonstrated a collection tracking from the pancreatic tail through the esophageal hiatus into the pleural space. In any patient with recurrent, bloody pleural effusion—particularly with a history of pancreatitis or abdominal trauma—a pleural fluid amylase level should be obtained. A level ≥1000 IU/L with pancreatic-type isoenzyme pattern should prompt immediate consideration of PPF and appropriate cross-sectional imaging such as MRCP to visualize the fistulous tract.
