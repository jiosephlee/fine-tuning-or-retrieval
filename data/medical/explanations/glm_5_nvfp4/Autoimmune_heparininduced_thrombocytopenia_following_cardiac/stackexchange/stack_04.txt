Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Morning Report] How should clinicians interpret a serotonin release assay (SRA) that shows strong serotonin release in the absence of heparin and only partial inhibition by the Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody IV.3?

**Interpretation of Atypical SRA in Autoimmune HIT**

This pattern strongly suggests **autoimmune HIT (aHIT)**, a distinct subtype characterized by HIT antibodies with heparin-independent platelet-activating properties.

**Understanding the Classic SRA Pattern:**
In typical HIT, the serotonin release assay shows:
- Minimal release at 0 U/mL heparin (no heparin present)
- Strong release at therapeutic heparin concentrations (0.1–0.3 U/mL)
- Inhibition at very high heparin (100 U/mL) due to disruption of PF4–heparin complexes
- Near-complete inhibition by IV.3, which blocks the platelet FcγRIIa receptor

**What the Atypical Pattern Signifies:**

**Strong release at 0 U/mL heparin** indicates heparin-independent platelet activation—the hallmark of aHIT. In this patient, serum caused 96% serotonin release even without heparin, demonstrating that the HIT antibodies can directly activate platelets without requiring exogenous heparin to form immune complexes.

**Partial inhibition by IV.3** is a novel and clinically important finding. In classic HIT, IV.3 should block FcγRIIa-mediated platelet activation, reducing release to <20%. In this case, despite IV.3, 76% serotonin release persisted at 0.1 U/mL heparin. This partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade suggests:
1. The antibodies are unusually potent, capable of overcoming partial Fc receptor blockade
2. Some platelet activation may occur through mechanisms beyond FcγRIIa clustering
3. This represents a severe serological phenotype of aHIT

**Clinical Implications:**
As demonstrated in this case, aHIT often presents with:
- Earlier onset than typical HIT (this patient's platelet count fell before POD5, attributed to preoperative heparin exposure during cardiac catheterization)
- More profound thrombocytopenia (nadir 13×10⁹/L)
- Greater risk of thrombosis (this patient suffered stroke)
- Potential for delayed or suboptimal response to high-dose IVIG

**Key Teaching Point:**
An SRA showing strong serotonin release without heparin plus incomplete IV.3 inhibition should **not** be dismissed as "indeterminate"—this profile is diagnostic for aHIT and indicates a more severe disease course requiring aggressive management with alternative anticoagulation and often high-dose IVIG.
