Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] What are the key pathophysiologic differences between classic heparin-dependent HIT and autoimmune HIT (aHIT) in terms of antibody behavior and platelet activation?

### Pathophysiologic Differences: Classic HIT vs. Autoimmune HIT

In classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), IgG antibodies form against platelet factor 4 (PF4) when complexed with heparin. These antibodies are strictly **heparin-dependent**—they only bind and activate platelets when heparin is present at therapeutic concentrations. The resulting immune complexes cluster platelet FcγRIIa receptors, triggering activation and serotonin release. Importantly, this activation is fully inhibitable by Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibodies (such as IV.3) and is suppressed at very high heparin concentrations (100 U/mL), where PF4-heparin complexes cannot form.

In contrast, **autoimmune HIT (aHIT)** features antibodies with **heparin-independent platelet-activating properties**. These pathogenic antibodies can recognize PF4 or PF4 complexes without requiring heparin. The key laboratory hallmark is strong platelet activation even in the *absence* of heparin—a finding clearly demonstrated in our patient, whose serum caused 96% serotonin release at 0 U/mL heparin on the serotonin-release assay (SRA).

A second critical difference, uniquely illustrated in this case, is **partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade**. Normally, adding IV.3 antibody inhibits HIT antibody-mediated platelet activation to less than 20% serotonin release. Our patient's serum showed only partial inhibition (76% release persisted), indicating antibodies of such high affinity and pathogenicity that they partially override Fc receptor blockade. This novel serological profile correlates with the unusually severe clinical phenotype—profound thrombocytopenia (nadir 13×10⁹/L), consumptive coagulopathy, and thrombotic stroke.

Clinically, these pathophysiologic distinctions matter: aHIT often presents with more severe thrombocytopenia, higher thrombosis risk, and may require adjunctive therapies like high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin alongside alternative anticoagulation.
