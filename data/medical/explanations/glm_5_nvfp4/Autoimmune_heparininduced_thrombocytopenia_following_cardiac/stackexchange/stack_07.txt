Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] Why might fondaparinux be an effective treatment option for autoimmune HIT, and what consideration should be given to antithrombin levels when initiating this therapy?

**Clinical Teaching Answer**

Fondaparinux is an effective treatment option for autoimmune HIT (aHIT) primarily because of its very low cross-reactivity with HIT antibodies. Unlike unfractionated heparin and low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH), which are large polyanions that readily form immunogenic complexes with platelet factor 4 (PF4), fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide that is too short to support the formation of large PF4/polyanion immune complexes. Consequently, it does not propagate the platelet-activating effects of HIT antibodies, even in aHIT where antibodies demonstrate heparin-independent platelet activation. In this case, the patient was transitioned from argatroban to therapeutic-dose fondaparinux on postoperative day 14 due to persisting hypercoagulability—manifested by falling fibrinogen, rising D-dimer, and schistocytosis—and ultimately achieved gradual platelet count recovery and complete neurological resolution.

A critical consideration when initiating fondaparinux is the patient's antithrombin (AT) level. Fondaparinux exerts its anticoagulant effect indirectly by binding to and potentiating antithrombin-mediated inhibition of factor Xa. In severe HIT, particularly aHIT with consumptive coagulopathy, antithrombin levels may be depleted due to ongoing thrombin generation and consumption. In this patient, the pre-infusion AT level was mildly reduced at 0.70 U/mL (reference range 0.80–1.40 U/mL). Therefore, supplemental antithrombin concentrate (1000 U) was administered prior to starting fondaparinux to ensure adequate anticoagulant efficacy. Clinicians should assess AT levels in patients with severe HIT or consumptive coagulopathy and consider AT supplementation if levels are subtherapeutic before initiating fondaparinux therapy.
