Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] What observation period is generally recommended after resolution of anaphylaxis symptoms, and what patient factors might warrant extended monitoring?

### Clinical Teaching Answer

The standard observation period after anaphylaxis resolution is typically **4 to 6 hours**, though this should be individualized based on risk stratification. The rationale for observation stems from the phenomenon of biphasic anaphylaxis—a recurrence of symptoms after apparent resolution without re-exposure to the trigger—which occurs in approximately 4–6% of cases, with approximately half of these recurrences manifesting within 6 to 12 hours of the initial event.

**Patient factors warranting extended observation (12–24 hours or admission) include:**

1. **Severe initial presentation** – Patients presenting with hypotension, hypoxia, or airway compromise are at higher risk for biphasic reactions. Our patient demonstrated both hypotension (74/50 mm Hg) and hypoxemia (SpO₂ 88%) at presentation.

2. **Multiple epinephrine doses required** – Need for more than one dose of epinephrine to control initial symptoms is a well-established risk factor. This patient required four separate epinephrine injections across four distinct anaphylactic episodes.

3. **Delayed epinephrine administration** – Delayed treatment of the initial reaction increases biphasic risk.

4. **Prior history of biphasic reaction** – A previous biphasic episode predicts recurrence.

5. **Underlying mast cell disorders** – The patient's elevated baseline tryptase and probable systemic mastocytosis likely contributed to her multiphasic course, as mast cell disorders predispose patients to severe and recurrent anaphylaxis.

This case illustrates the challenge vividly: despite initial symptom resolution, our patient developed recurrent anaphylactic shock within the first 12 hours, requiring ICU admission and multiple epinephrine doses. She was transferred from the ICU only after approximately 24 hours without symptom relapse.

The UK 2021 guideline offers a practical three-tier risk-stratified approach. For high-risk patients—such as those with severe presentations, multiple epinephrine doses, or underlying mast cell disease—extended observation of 24 hours or admission is prudent. The absence of reliable predictors makes clinical judgment essential, but the consequences of premature discharge can be life-threatening.
