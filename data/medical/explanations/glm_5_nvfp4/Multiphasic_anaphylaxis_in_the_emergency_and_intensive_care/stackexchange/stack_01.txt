Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose for anaphylaxis in adults, and why might the commonly available 0.3 mg auto-injector lead to suboptimal treatment?

**Clinical Answer:**

The recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose for anaphylaxis in adults is **0.01 mg/kg, up to a maximum of 0.5 mg per dose**. This weight-based approach is often simplified to a fixed 0.5 mg dose for most adults, given that the majority weigh more than 50 kg.

The commonly available 0.3 mg auto-injector can lead to **suboptimal treatment due to systematic underdosing**. This case illustrates the problem clearly: the patient weighed 104 kg, meaning her weight-based dose would have been approximately 1 mg, capped at the 0.5 mg maximum. The 0.3 mg auto-injector delivered only 60% of the recommended maximum dose. As noted in the discussion, patients receiving 0.3 mg rather than 0.5 mg epinephrine require escalation of care—including additional epinephrine injections—significantly more often.

The case demonstrates this principle: the first two episodes were treated with 0.3 mg via auto-injector, while the subsequent two episodes required individually prepared 0.5 mg doses. While auto-injectors offer practical advantages in time-sensitive settings and reduce treatment hesitancy among staff unfamiliar with vasoactive medications, clinicians must recognize that the 0.3 mg dose represents potential underdosing for adults over 50 kg. In severe anaphylaxis, particularly with hypotension and hypoxemia as seen in this case, adequate initial epinephrine dosing is critical to achieving timely resolution and may reduce the risk of refractory or biphasic courses.
