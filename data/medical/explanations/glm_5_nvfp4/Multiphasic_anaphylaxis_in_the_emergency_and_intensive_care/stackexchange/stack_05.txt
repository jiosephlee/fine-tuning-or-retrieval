Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What underlying condition should be suspected in patients with severe, protracted, or multiphasic anaphylaxis, and what clinical and laboratory findings support this diagnosis?

**Systemic mastocytosis should be suspected in patients presenting with severe, protracted, or multiphasic anaphylaxis.**

This underlying condition is characterized by an abnormal clonal proliferation of mast cells, leading to excessive mediator release and predisposition to severe, recurrent anaphylactic reactions. As illustrated in the present case, the patient experienced four distinct anaphylactic episodes over approximately 12 hours despite appropriate epinephrine administration and clinical resolution between events—a classic multiphasic course that prompted evaluation for an underlying mast cell disorder.

**Clinical findings suggestive of systemic mastocytosis include:**
- Severe or refractory anaphylaxis, particularly with hypotension as a prominent feature
- Multiphasic or biphasic anaphylaxis course
- Anaphylaxis triggered by insect stings (the patient had a prior strong local reaction to a wasp sting)
- Hepatomegaly (found on abdominal sonography in this case)
- Neuropsychiatric symptoms such as depression and cognitive difficulties ("brain fog"), which were also noted in this patient

**Laboratory findings supporting the diagnosis:**
- **Elevated baseline serum tryptase**—this is the cornerstone screening test. In this case, tryptase levels were elevated during the anaphylactic episode and remained elevated at follow-up, indicating an abnormally high baseline rather than just acute mast cell activation.
- The World Health Organization (WHO) diagnostic criteria for systemic mastocytosis require either the **major criterion** (dense mast cell aggregates on bone marrow biopsy) plus one minor criterion, or three **minor criteria** (including elevated baseline tryptase >20 ng/mL, abnormal mast cell morphology, presence of KIT D816V mutation, or abnormal CD25/CD2 expression on mast cells).

In this patient, bone marrow biopsy material was limited, precluding assessment of the major criterion; however, the combination of elevated baseline tryptase and clinical findings yielded a "high probability" of systemic mastocytosis even without meeting formal diagnostic criteria.

**The key teaching point:** when you encounter anaphylaxis that is unusually severe, requires multiple epinephrine doses, follows a biphasic or multiphasic course, or occurs without an obvious trigger, send a serum tryptase level—ideally at presentation, 1–2 hours post-event, and at baseline after resolution. An elevated baseline tryptase should prompt referral to allergology or hematology for further evaluation of systemic mastocytosis.
