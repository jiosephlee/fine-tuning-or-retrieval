Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 3: ## Procedural Execution: The TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV Technique

The execution of a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with two prior devices and a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis represents one of the most complex challenges in structural heart medicine. In this case, the procedural strategy was dictated by the need to navigate a "triple-stented" anatomy while mitigating the risks of coronary obstruction, severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM), and residual regurgitation. The intervention was performed under local anesthesia utilizing a combined access strategy of the left radial and left femoral arteries. This hybrid approach facilitated simultaneous diagnostic angiography and therapeutic device delivery while minimizing vascular trauma in a patient with a history of femoral pseudoaneurysm requiring surgical repair. The entire procedure relied heavily on preprocedural multislice computed tomography (MSCT) modeling to visualize the spatial relationship between the existing Lotus and Evolut frames, ensuring that the new device could be deployed safely without compromising coronary perfusion.

Valve selection was pivotal to overcoming the anatomical constraints imposed by two overlapping stent structures. The team chose the Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthesis over other available platforms due to its specific design advantages for narrow, multi-layered landing zones. Unlike some balloon-expandable or lower-profile self-expanding valves, the Acurate neo2 features a supra-annular leaflet configuration, meaning the functional leaflets sit above the outer frame skirt. This design allows for a larger effective orifice area (EOA) relative to the external footprint of the device, which is critical in preventing severe PPM when deploying into an already restricted lumen measuring only 13.7 mm. Furthermore, the device's upper stabilizing arches allow for precise positioning without excessive radial force that could damage the fragile tissue surrounding the previous prostheses or cause embolization. The selection was further justified by the valve's capacity for commissural alignment, a feature essential for preserving future coronary access through layers of metal struts that would otherwise obscure catheter navigation.

The core technical maneuver distinguishing this procedure from standard TAV-in-TAV interventions was the deliberate strategy of high implantation. Preprocedural CT analysis revealed that placing the third valve too deeply would result in the inward folding of the leaflets from both underlying prostheses into the new valve's inflow tract, drastically reducing hemodynamic performance. To counteract this, the Acurate neo2 was deployed at a higher vertical position, aligning specifically with the leaflet plane of the middle Evolut prosthesis rather than sitting flush against its inflow edge. This "high placement" technique served two distinct physiological goals: first, it prevented the mechanical restriction caused by folded leaflets collapsing into the new valve's opening; second, it minimized the formation of a "neo-skirt"—the composite barrier created by overlapping stent frames below the coronary ostia—which poses a significant risk for acute coronary obstruction and hinders future catheter engagement. By anchoring above these critical structures, clinicians ensured that coronary flow remained unimpeded despite three concentric layers of stenting.

Optimization maneuvers were executed with precision to ensure maximal expansion and stability within this rigid environment. Prior to deployment, balloon predilation was performed using a 22/40 mm catheter under rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute to temporarily halt cardiac output and stabilize catheter position during inflation. Crucially, simultaneous aortography confirmed that coronary flow remained patent throughout this aggressive dilation phase. Following device release under fluoroscopic guidance with attention to rotational orientation for commissural alignment, post-dilation was conducted using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid pacing again to optimize radial expansion against both native calcification and stiff metallic struts from prior valves. This step was vital not only for sealing paravalvular leaks but also for mechanically fracturing or expanding any restrictive elements of the inner Evolut frame that might impede leaflet motion in subsequent cycles. Final confirmation via angiography verified correct positioning, preserved coronary perfusion pressures, and an absence of both transvalvular and paravalvular regurgitation before concluding the procedure under local anesthesia without sedation-induced hemodynamic instability.
