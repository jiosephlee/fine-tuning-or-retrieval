Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation and Complex Patient History

The patient, a man in his late seventies, presented to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center exhibiting classic symptoms of severe hemodynamic compromise secondary to aortic valve dysfunction. His primary complaints included exertional dyspnea, a spastic sensation in the left chest wall during physical activity (specifically walking distances of 200–300 meters), dizziness, and profound fatigue. These symptoms reflect the physiological consequences of fixed left ventricular outflow obstruction, where the failing heart cannot adequately increase cardiac output to meet metabolic demands during stress. While these presenting features are typical of severe aortic stenosis, they occur here in the context of a uniquely complex medical trajectory involving multiple prior transcatheter interventions and a recent bout of life-threatening infection.

Underlying this acute presentation is a heavy burden of comorbidities that significantly elevates the patient's perioperative risk profile and complicates therapeutic decision-making. Beyond arterial hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus, the patient suffers from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and a history of recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding since 2022, which limits anticoagulation strategies often required for structural heart procedures. Perhaps most critically, he has extensive coronary artery disease characterized by three-vessel involvement, having undergone four separate percutaneous coronary interventions (PCIs) between 2009 and 2014 targeting the right coronary artery, left anterior descending (LAD) artery, and circumflex artery. Additionally, his conduction system disease manifested as a third-degree atrioventricular block in 2014, necessitating permanent pacemaker implantation shortly before his first valve intervention. This constellation of conditions places him at prohibitive risk for conventional surgical valve replacement or complex redo sternotomy.

The evolution of this patient's structural heart disease began in 2014 with progressive degeneration leading to severe aortic stenosis. Due to his high surgical risk profile at that time, he underwent transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) with a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific). For several years, this intervention stabilized his condition; however, by December 2021, clinical deterioration recurred with worsening exertional dyspnea. Echocardiographic evaluation revealed severe bioprosthetic valve stenosis with a dramatic rise in mean transvalvular gradient to 83 mm Hg and a concurrent decline in left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) from 54% to 42%. Recognizing the failure of the initial prosthesis and the continued unsuitability for surgery due to his comorbidities and prior sternotomy avoidance goals, the clinical team opted for a "valve-in-valve" strategy. In May 2022, he underwent his second TAVI procedure (TAV-in-TAV), receiving a 23 mm Evolut prosthesis (Medtronic) inside the failed Lotus valve. While this successfully restored hemodynamics initially—improving EF back to 54%—the procedure was marred by immediate vascular complications, specifically a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm that required open surgical repair.

The clinical course took another precarious turn just three months after the second intervention when the patient was hospitalized with persistent fever and worsening heart failure symptoms in September 2022. Diagnostic workup identified severe restenosis of the newly implanted Evolut prosthesis alongside bacteremia positive for *Enterococcus faecalis*, confirming a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). This represents one of the most formidable challenges in structural heart medicine: managing valve dysfunction superimposed on active infection within an already compromised anatomical space containing two overlapping metal frames. The patient was managed with aggressive antimicrobial therapy consisting of six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment followed by oral suppression as directed by infectious disease specialists. Crucially, all subsequent management relied on rigorous confirmation that the infection had been eradicated; repeated blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography (TEE), and multislice computed tomography (MSCT) eventually confirmed resolution with no evidence of active vegetation or paravalvular abscess. Once the infection was cleared but as symptoms recurred due to mechanical degeneration exacerbated by the inflammatory insult, the stage was set for what would become an unprecedented third transcatheter intervention: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV.
