Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Despite immediate post-procedure echocardiography showing a mean gradient of 67 mmHg and severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM), the patient's symptoms resolved and gradients normalized by one month. What physiological mechanisms explain this delayed hemodynamic improvement in a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario?

**Clinical Pearl:** In complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures, an acute post-procedural echocardiogram demonstrating severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM) and elevated gradients does not necessarily predict permanent hemodynamic failure. Delayed normalization of gradients over weeks to months is often attributable to **continued radial force-mediated expansion of the nested stent frames** and **remodeling of the leaflet geometry**.

In this specific case, the immediate post-procedure mean gradient of 67 mmHg reflected a "locked" state where the newly implanted Acurate neo2 valve had not yet fully expanded the underlying Evolut and Lotus frames. The acute finding of severe PPM (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²) was likely exacerbated by the restricted mobility of the prior valve leaflets being pinned against the new frame. However, several mechanisms facilitated recovery by one month:

1.  **Radial Expansion and Frame Remodeling:** The self-expanding nature of the Acurate neo2, combined with aggressive post-dilation (using a 20/40 mm balloon), exerts continuous outward radial force. Over days to weeks, this force gradually expands the rigid metal struts of the two underlying prostheses (the Evolut and Lotus), effectively increasing the true internal diameter of the triple-layered conduit beyond what was achieved acutely.
2.  **Leaflet Optimization:** The procedural strategy utilized high implantation to fixate the prior Evolut leaflets against its own stent frame. As the outer frame expanded, it likely prevented inward folding of these older leaflets ("neo-skirt" formation), thereby unmasking a larger effective orifice area that was mechanically constrained immediately post-deployment.
3.  **Hemodynamic Adaptation:** The patient's left ventricle, which had shown recovery in ejection fraction (from 42% to 66%), underwent favorable reverse remodeling with reduced chamber volumes. This decreased stroke volume demand relative to valve area may have contributed to lower measured gradients as flow dynamics stabilized.

Therefore, clinicians should interpret acute high gradients in multi-valve TAVR scenarios with caution, recognizing that full hemodynamic optimization may lag behind procedural success due to delayed mechanical expansion of nested devices. Serial imaging is essential before labeling such cases as failures or considering re-intervention for PPM.
