Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] When selecting a third-generation transcatheter heart valve for a 'TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV' procedure involving two prior devices (Lotus and Evolut), why was the Acurate neo2 chosen over other available platforms, specifically regarding its impact on coronary access and effective orifice area?

In this complex "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV" scenario involving a Lotus valve, followed by an Evolut, and now a third intervention, the selection of the **Acurate neo2** was driven principally by its unique supra-annular leaflet architecture and lower frame profile, which directly addressed the twin threats of coronary obstruction and prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM).

When stacking multiple devices, the primary hemodynamic penalty arises from the accumulation of frame struts and the potential for prior valve leaflets to fold inward into the new orifice, creating a "neo-skirt" that restricts flow. The Acurate neo2 features a **supra-annular design**, meaning its leaflets sit above the sealing skirt. In this case, we leveraged this geometry to deploy the device at a higher implantation depth relative to the existing Evolut frame. By aligning the Acurate neo2 with the Evolut leaflets rather than burying them deep within the annulus, we effectively pinned these redundant leaflets against the outer stent struts. This maneuver prevented inward folding and maximized the residual effective orifice area (EOA), mitigating severe PPM despite three layers of metal scaffolding.

Furthermore, regarding coronary access—the most feared complication in multi-valve interventions—the Acurate neo2's **low-profile upper crown** and lack of an external skirt extending significantly above the valve plane offered superior clearance compared to other self-expanding or balloon-expandable alternatives. The pre-procedural CT modeling confirmed that this specific platform minimized interference with the coronary ostia. Additionally, its design facilitates better opportunities for **commissural alignment**. Aligning the new device's commissures with those of the underlying Evolut reduces strut overlap at key angles, theoretically preserving a clearer pathway for future coronary cannulation if needed. Thus, in a patient with prohibitive surgical risk and triple-layered anatomy, the Acurate neo2 represented the optimal balance between maximizing flow through a narrowed channel and maintaining accessibility to vital coronary arteries.
