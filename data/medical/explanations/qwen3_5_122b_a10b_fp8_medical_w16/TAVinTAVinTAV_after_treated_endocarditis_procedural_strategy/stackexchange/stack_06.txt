Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] According to current ESC guidelines, surgical replacement is generally preferred for prosthetic valve endocarditis. Under what specific anatomical and clinical conditions did the Heart Team justify proceeding with a third TAVI in this patient despite these general recommendations?

In this complex case, the Heart Team justified proceeding with a third TAVI (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) over the standard recommendation for surgical replacement by identifying a convergence of **prohibitive surgical risk**, **strict exclusion of active infection**, and **anatomical feasibility** confirmed by advanced imaging.

First, regarding the clinical condition of infection, the decision hinged on the fact that the patient's prosthetic valve endocarditis (caused by *Enterococcus faecalis*) was fully treated. The patient had completed a six-week course of targeted antibiotics, and crucially, comprehensive workup—including transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT)—confirmed the complete resolution of infection with no evidence of active vegetations, abscesses, or paravalvular extension. Current ESC guidelines permit transcatheter intervention in PVE only when surgery is contraindicated and infection is controlled; this patient met both criteria.

Second, the surgical risk was deemed prohibitive. The patient possessed an EuroSCORE II of 27.36% and significant comorbidities including COPD, diabetes, prior GI bleeding, and a history of vascular complications requiring open repair after his second TAVI. Anatomically, the presence of two deeply integrated prior valve frames (Lotus and Evolut) meant that surgical explantation would likely require total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement, carrying an unacceptably high mortality risk compared to a percutaneous approach.

Finally, anatomical feasibility was rigorously established through CT-guided planning. The team identified that high implantation of a self-expanding Acurate neo2 valve could avoid coronary obstruction by navigating the "neo-skirt" above the coronary ostia while maximizing the effective orifice area. By aligning commissures and utilizing post-dilation to expand the intermediate frame without fracturing it, they achieved a viable hemodynamic solution where surgery offered little chance of success. Thus, the deviation from standard surgical preference was driven by a life-saving balance between extreme operative danger and a meticulously planned percutaneous alternative in a sterile field.
