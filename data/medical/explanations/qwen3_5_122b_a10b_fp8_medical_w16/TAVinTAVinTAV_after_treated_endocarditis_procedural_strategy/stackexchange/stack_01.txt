Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] In a patient with a history of treated prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) presenting with new-onset severe valve dysfunction, what are the critical preprocedural requirements before considering a repeat transcatheter intervention instead of surgical explantation?

In a patient with a history of treated prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) presenting with severe valve dysfunction, the critical preprocedural requirement before considering a repeat transcatheter intervention over surgical explantation is **definitive confirmation of infection resolution** coupled with **comprehensive multidisciplinary anatomical assessment**.

As illustrated in our case report, the decision to pursue a "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV" procedure was predicated on rigorous proof that the *Enterococcus faecalis* infection was fully eradicated. The Heart Team relied on a convergence of data: completion of a full six-week antimicrobial course, negative repeat blood cultures, and crucially, the absence of vegetations or paravalvular abscesses on both transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT). Without this absolute certainty that the infection is quiescent, attempting another percutaneous intervention would carry an unacceptably high risk of seeding the new prosthesis or failing to treat an occult perivalvular extension.

Furthermore, given the prohibitive surgical risk inherent in this patient's profile—including prior open vascular repairs, severe comorbidities, and the technical difficulty of explanting deeply integrated stent frames—the second pillar of preprocedural planning is **high-resolution CT angiography for anatomical feasibility**. In this specific scenario, MSCT was indispensable for evaluating the "triple-stented" anatomy. It allowed us to measure the minimum lumen diameter of the existing Evolut frame (13.7 mm), calculate valve-to-coronary distances to rule out obstruction by a potential neo-skirt, and plan for high implantation depths to preserve coronary flow.

Therefore, the threshold for proceeding with transcatheter salvage in post-PVE patients is not merely hemodynamic severity but the dual verification of sterile status and a CT-validated roadmap that ensures coronary patency and adequate landing zones despite complex frame layering. Only when these conditions are met does the transcatheter approach offer a viable alternative to high-risk surgical root replacement.
