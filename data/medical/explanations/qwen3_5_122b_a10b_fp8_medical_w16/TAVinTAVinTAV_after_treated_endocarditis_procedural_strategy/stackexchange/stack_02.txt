Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] How does the 'neo-skirt' phenomenon contribute to coronary obstruction risk in TAV-in-TAV procedures, and what specific implantation strategy was utilized in this case to mitigate this risk while maintaining hemodynamic efficacy?

**Answer:**

The "neo-skirt" phenomenon represents a critical mechanical hazard in TAV-in-TAV (and increasingly TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) procedures. It occurs when the leaflets of the innermost, failing valve are pushed outward by the new valve's frame but then fold back inward or are compressed against the outer valve's stent struts. This creates a dense, multilayered fabric barrier—the "neo-skirt"—that extends toward the sinuses of Valsalva. If this structure forms below the coronary ostia, it physically obstructs coronary blood flow and renders future coronary cannulation impossible due to the inability to traverse the thickened, rigid mesh.

In this specific case, mitigating this risk required a departure from standard deployment depths. The team employed a **high-implantation strategy**, guided by meticulous preprocedural multislice CT (MSCT) modeling. Rather than seating the third-generation Acurate neo2 valve deeply within the existing Evolut frame—which would have trapped the previous leaflets and formed a neo-skirt occluding the coronaries—the device was intentionally deployed higher. The goal was to align the new valve's inflow with the *leaflets* of the intermediate Evolut prosthesis. By doing so, the old leaflets were pinned firmly against the outer Lotus frame, preventing them from prolapsing inward to create an obstructive skirt below the coronary ostia.

This strategic placement preserved a patent channel for coronary flow and maintained potential access for future interventions, despite the extreme complexity of three concentric stent layers. Crucially, this was balanced against hemodynamic needs: aligning with the Evolut leaflets maximized the effective orifice area by preventing inward leaflet collapse, thereby reducing patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). While immediate post-procedure gradients were elevated due to severe PPM in this triple-stented anatomy, subsequent remodeling and balloon optimization led to sustained hemodynamic improvement at one year without compromising coronary patency. This case underscores that in complex redo scenarios, anatomical precision regarding implantation depth is paramount to balancing acute hemodynamics with long-term coronary accessibility.
