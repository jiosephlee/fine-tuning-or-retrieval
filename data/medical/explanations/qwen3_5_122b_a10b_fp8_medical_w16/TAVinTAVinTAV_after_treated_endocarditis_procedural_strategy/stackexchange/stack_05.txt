Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Why is computed tomography (CT) angiography considered superior to transthoracic echocardiography for procedural planning in complex multi-layered valve interventions, particularly concerning the assessment of coronary ostial height and frame interaction?

Computed tomography (CT) angiography is superior to transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) for procedural planning in complex multi-layered valve interventions because it provides the necessary three-dimensional spatial resolution to visualize the intricate geometry of overlapping stent frames and their relationship to the aortic root structures. While TTE offers valuable hemodynamic data, as seen in our case where it identified severe restenosis and regurgitation, it lacks the ability to precisely delineate the "neo-skirt" formed by multiple valve layers or measure critical distances such as coronary ostial height relative to the innermost stent struts.

In this specific case of a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, the patient's anatomy involved three distinct prosthetic generations: a Lotus valve, an Evolut prosthesis, and finally an Acurate neo2. The decision to proceed relied entirely on multislice CT (MSCT) findings, which revealed a minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the Evolut frame and allowed us to map the exact interaction between these frames. Crucially, CT enabled us to assess the risk of coronary obstruction by measuring the valve-to-coronary distance and analyzing how the anticipated new frame would sit relative to the existing ones. This visualization was pivotal in selecting a high-implantation strategy for the Acurate neo2; we deliberately aligned it with the Evolut leaflets to prevent inward folding that could further occlude flow or create a barrier to future coronary access. Without CT's capacity to model these complex frame interactions and verify adequate sinus dimensions, attempting this third intervention would have carried an unacceptable risk of acute coronary occlusion or catastrophic prosthesis-patient mismatch. Thus, CT serves as the indispensable roadmap for navigating the "lifetime management" challenges inherent in serial transcatheter interventions.
