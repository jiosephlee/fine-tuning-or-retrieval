Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] Compare the mechanisms of action of Plasma Exchange (PLEX) versus Eculizumab in the treatment of CM-HUS. Why was PLEX utilized as a bridge therapy rather than definitive monotherapy in this case?

The fundamental distinction between Plasma Exchange (PLEX) and Eculizumab in Complement-Mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (CM-HUS) lies in their ability to address the underlying pathophysiology. PLEX acts as a temporizing measure by mechanically removing activated complement fragments, inflammatory mediators, and potential autoantibodies from circulation while replenishing missing regulatory factors like Factor H. However, it does not correct the inherent dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway; once the infusion stops, the uncontrolled cascade resumes. In contrast, Eculizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to terminal complement component C5, preventing its cleavage into C5a and C5b. By blocking the formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9), Eculizumab halts the downstream endothelial injury and thrombotic microangiopathy at its source, providing definitive disease control.

In this specific case of *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*-triggered CM-HUS, PLEX was appropriately utilized as a "bridge" therapy rather than definitive monotherapy for two critical reasons derived from our clinical course. First, while PLEX provided transient stabilization—evidenced by the return of spontaneous diuresis after the final sessions—it failed to sustain hematological remission or prevent persistent thrombocytopenia and hemolysis on its own. Second, and perhaps more importantly in this scenario involving active bacterial sepsis, there was a significant theoretical risk that PLEX alone might remove protective complement factors necessary for clearing the encapsulated *C. canimorsus*, potentially exacerbating the infection without stopping the TMA. Therefore, we initiated PLEX to stabilize the patient's microvascular thrombosis while awaiting confirmatory ADAMTS13 results and arranging for Eculizumab. Once blood cultures cleared and meningococcal vaccination was administered to mitigate infectious risk, we transitioned to Eculizumab to achieve sustained blockade of the terminal complement pathway. As seen in this patient's trajectory, durable hematological remission and partial renal recovery were only realized after the introduction of Eculizumab, confirming that while PLEX buys time in acute crises, it cannot replace targeted complement inhibition as the cornerstone of long-term management for CM-HUS.
