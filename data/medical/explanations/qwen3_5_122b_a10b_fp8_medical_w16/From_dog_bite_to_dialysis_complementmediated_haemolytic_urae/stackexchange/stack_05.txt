Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] When evaluating an adult with MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury, what is the critical role of ADAMTS13 activity levels in differentiating Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) from Complement-Mediated HUS (CM-HUS), and what threshold defines TTP?

In the evaluation of an adult presenting with the classic triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury (AKI), determining the etiology of the thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) is paramount because the treatments differ radically. The measurement of ADAMTS13 activity is the definitive discriminator between Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) and Complement-Mediated Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (CM-HUS).

The critical threshold defining TTP is an ADAMTS13 activity level **below 10%**. This severe deficiency reflects an acquired or congenital lack of the von Willebrand factor-cleaving protease, leading to the accumulation of ultra-large vWF multimers and subsequent platelet-rich microthrombi. In contrast, CM-HUS is characterized by dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway, where ADAMTS13 activity is typically preserved or only mildly reduced.

Our case illustrates this distinction clearly. The patient initially presented with *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* sepsis and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), which complicated the clinical picture. As her infection improved, she developed a distinct phase of isolated MAHA and persistent thrombocytopenia with dialysis-dependent AKI. While plasma exchange was initiated empirically as a bridge, our ADAMTS13 assay returned at **43.5%**. This result definitively excluded TTP, as values above 10% render that diagnosis highly unlikely regardless of clinical suspicion. Consequently, we shifted focus to CM-HUS, confirmed by the exclusion of Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*, normal C3/C4 levels (which do not rule out CM-HUS), and renal biopsy findings consistent with TMA. The preservation of ADAMTS13 activity guided us away from prolonged reliance on plasma exchange—which offers only transient benefit in CM-HUS—and toward the targeted therapy of eculizumab, resulting in hematologic remission and partial renal recovery. Thus, obtaining ADAMTS13 levels is not merely academic; it is a decisive step that prevents misdiagnosis and directs life-saving therapy toward complement inhibition rather than continued plasmapheresis alone.
