Title: Blog about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

# Colchicine Monotherapy in Acute Neuro-Behçet's: A Paradigm Shift or a Fluke?

Let's talk about a case that just might make your standard treatment algorithms twitch. We are taught, almost as gospel, that acute neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD) is a beast requiring aggressive immunosuppression—think high-dose IV methylprednisolone followed by azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, or TNF inhibitors. The logic is sound: it's a fulminant CNS inflammation driven by Th1/Th17 pathways, and you need heavy artillery to put it out.

But what happens when a patient walks into the ED with fever, headache, and severe hyponatremia, gets diagnosed with *both* systemic and acute neuro-BD simultaneously (a rarity in itself), and then recovers completely on **colchicine monotherapy**? No steroids. No cyclophosphamide. Just colchicine. And not only did the oral ulcers and genital sores vanish, but the neurological symptoms and the Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone Secretion (SIADH) resolved right along with them.

This isn't just a "nice story"; it's a case report by Kakehi et al. that forces us to question whether we are over-treating certain phenotypes of neuro-BD or if we've missed a nuance in how colchicine works in meningoencephalitic presentations versus parenchymal disease. Let's dive into the details of this fascinating outlier.

## The Patient Who Had Everything (Except Parenchymal Lesions)

The protagonist of this story is an Asian man in his 60s who rolled into the ER with three days of fever, headache, malaise, and some very specific complaints: persistent hiccups and arthralgia. On top of that, he had oral aphthae. Vital signs showed he was febrile (38.1°C) and tachycardic (119 bpm). Physical exam revealed the classic triad for Behçet's suspicion: oral ulcers, folliculitis-like rashes on the face/trunk, and later in his course, a painful scrotal ulcer.

Here is where things get tricky for the emergency physician or hospitalist: he also had **severe hyponatremia** (Na+ 124 mmol/L). He was euvolemic on exam, but labs told a different story regarding water handling: low serum osmolality (267 mOsm/kg), high urine osmolality (900 mOsm/kg), high urine sodium (>80 mmol/L), and elevated ADH levels. This was textbook SIADH.

The initial workup was a whirlwind of exclusion. They ruled out bacterial meningitis (negative cultures), viral encephalitis (negative HSV/VZV PCR), TB meningitis, malignancy (normal MRI head/chest/abdomen), and sarcoidosis. The CSF showed aseptic meningitis with mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein, but crucially, **the brain MRI was normal**. No brainstem lesions, no hypothalamic enhancement—the usual suspects for parenchymal neuro-BD were absent.

By Day 5, the appearance of the genital ulcer sealed the deal for Systemic Behçet's Disease based on International Criteria scoring ≥4 points (oral + genital ulcers + rash). With CNS symptoms in this context and infectious/malignant causes excluded, they landed on **Acute Neuro-BD**.

## The Therapeutic Twist

Standard protocol would dictate starting pulse dose steroids immediately to prevent permanent neurological sequelae or death from cerebral edema/inflammation. But here is where this case diverges from the script. Once the diagnosis was secured on Day 8, they started **colchicine monotherapy**. That's it. Topical steroids for the mouth were allowed locally, but no systemic corticosteroids were touched.

The result? Within 24 hours of starting colchicine, his fever broke and appetite returned. Over two weeks, his vertigo cleared up, his hiccups stopped (a huge relief for anyone who has suffered through them), his mucocutaneous lesions healed, and critically, his sodium normalized back to 136 mmol/L as ADH levels plummeted to near zero. He went home after one month on colchicine alone and stayed relapse-free for four years while maintaining good glycemic control for his previously undiagnosed Type 2 Diabetes.

## Why This Matters: Mechanisms vs. Dogma

So why did this work? For decades, systematic reviews have labeled colchicine as ineffective for *parenchymal* neuro-BD because it doesn't cross into deep brain tissue inflammation well enough compared to biologics or cytotoxic agents. However, this patient didn't have parenchymal disease; he had an aseptic meningoencephalitis picture with SIADH likely driven by hypothalamic irritation without frank structural damage visible on MRI.

Colchicine is primarily known for inhibiting microtubule polymerization in neutrophils and suppressing chemotaxis via NF-kB inhibition downregulation of IL-1β production. Recent evidence also suggests it suppresses IL-6 release from activated monocytes—a key player here given that IL-6 is known to stimulate ADH release directly or indirectly via CNS inflammation pathways. If you view SIADH in this context as a downstream effect of IL-6 mediated CNS inflammation rather than direct structural destruction of the posterior pituitary pathway or hypothalamus target neurons being destroyed by necrosis ("parenchymal" disease), then blocking that cytokine cascade becomes highly logical even without massive steroid doses.

It raises an uncomfortable question: Are we treating every case of acute neuro-BD like it's impending herniation when many might actually be self-limiting meningoencephalitides responsive to less toxic agents? Or was this simply spontaneous remission dressed up nicely? The authors argue against pure coincidence given the temporal relationship between drug initiation and rapid resolution across three distinct systems (skin/neuro/endocrine). Still，we must acknowledge that single-case reports are hypothesis-generating at best; they don't rewrite guidelines overnight without replication cohorts showing similar efficacy rates compared to steroid-sparing regimens containing biologics like infliximab or adalimumab which currently dominate modern management strategies especially severe forms involving brainstem structures specifically targeted heavily by those drugs themselves!
