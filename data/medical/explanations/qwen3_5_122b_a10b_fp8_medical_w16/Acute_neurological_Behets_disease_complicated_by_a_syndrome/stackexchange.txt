The scoring breakdown relies strictly on the clinical findings documented in this case:
*   **Oral aphthae:** Awarded **2 points**. The patient presented with recurrent oral ulcers, which serves as the mandatory entry criterion for ICBD classification.
*   **Genital aphthosis:** Awarded **2 points**. Although the painful scrotal ulcer developed later in the course (on day 5), its presence alongside oral lesions solidified the mucocutaneous phenotype.
*   **Folliculitis-like rash:** Awarded **1 point**. The patient exhibited characteristic skin lesions on the face and trunk, distinct from acne or erythema nodosum.

With a cumulative score of 5, well above the cutoff of 4, and after excluding infectious, neoplastic, and other autoimmune etiologies, the diagnosis of definite Behçet's disease was confirmed concurrently with acute neuro-Behçet's disease.

### [Morning Report] In the context of acute neuro-Behçet's disease presenting with meningoencephalitis-like symptoms, what is the expected frequency of parenchymal brain lesions visible on MRI compared to the frequency of aseptic meningitis findings in the cerebrospinal fluid?

In the classic presentation of acute neuro-Behçet's disease, one typically anticipates meningoencephalitis accompanied by parenchymal brain lesions, most frequently involving the brainstem. However, as illustrated in this case report, the radiological and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) findings can diverge significantly from this textbook expectation. Specifically, **parenchymal brain lesions are absent in a substantial subset of patients**, whereas **aseptic meningitis findings in the CSF are consistently present** even when imaging is unrevealing.

In this patient, who presented with fever, headache, and hyponatremia due to SIADH, the brain MRI was entirely normal, showing no evidence of the characteristic parenchymal inflammation or brainstem involvement often seen in neuro-Behçet's. Conversely, the CSF analysis definitively demonstrated aseptic meningitis, characterized by mild lymphocytic pleocytosis (11 cells/µL) and elevated protein (0.97 g/L), after infectious and neoplastic etiologies were rigorously excluded. This discrepancy underscores a critical diagnostic principle: **a normal brain MRI does not rule out acute neuro-Behçet's disease.** The discussion within the case report explicitly notes that acute neuro-Behçet's may manifest as "aseptic meningitis without parenchymal brain lesions," particularly in the early phases or specific phenotypes where meningeal inflammation predominates over parenchymal necrosis. Therefore, while parenchymal lesions are a hallmark of many cases, their absence should not deter the diagnosis if clinical features (such as concurrent mucocutaneous ulcers) and CSF findings support active CNS inflammation. In this specific instance, the diagnosis relied on the convergence of systemic Behçet's criteria and CSF evidence of sterile inflammation rather than radiological confirmation of parenchymal disease.

### [Pathophysiology] What specific physiological mechanism linking CNS inflammation in neuro-Behçet's disease to the development of SIADH involves the cytokine IL-6?

Based on the case report by Kakehi et al., the specific physiological mechanism linking CNS inflammation in neuro-Behçet's disease to SIADH via Interleukin-6 (IL-6) involves the **direct stimulation of arginine vasopressin (AVP, or ADH) release from the posterior pituitary** driven by local neuroinflammation.

While the case report notes that serum and CSF IL-6 levels were not explicitly measured in this patient, the authors infer this pathway based on established pathophysiology in neuroinflammatory disorders. Behçet's disease is characterized by aberrant monocyte activation leading to spontaneous overproduction of proinflammatory cytokines, specifically IL-6. In acute neuro-Behçet's, these cytokines infiltrate the central nervous system, where IL-6 acts as a potent secretagogue for AVP.

The clinical evidence supporting this inference in our patient includes:
1.  **Marked Systemic Inflammation:** The patient presented with significantly elevated CRP (180 mg/L) and ESR, serving as a surrogate marker for an intense IL-6-mediated inflammatory cascade.
2.  **Temporal Correlation:** The patient exhibited classic SIADH physiology (hyponatremia, hypotonicity, inappropriately concentrated urine, and elevated serum ADH at 22.4 pg/mL) concurrent with acute meningoencephalitis symptoms (fever, headache, hiccups).
3.  **Resolution with Anti-Cytokine Therapy:** Crucially, the administration of colchicine—which suppresses cytokine production including IL-6—led to the normalization of serum sodium and a dramatic drop in ADH levels (from 22.4 to 1.8 pg/mL) alongside the resolution of neurological symptoms.

Therefore, the mechanism is not necessarily structural damage to the hypothalamus (as MRI was normal), but rather a functional dysregulation where circulating and intrathecal IL-6 overrides normal osmotic feedback loops, driving inappropriate ADH secretion until the inflammatory drive is suppressed.

In a patient presenting with fever, headache, and hyponatremia where infectious etiologies like HSV encephalitis and tuberculous meningitis have been definitively excluded, the clinical finding that most strongly pivots the differential diagnosis toward Behçet's disease (BD) over neurosarcoidosis or primary CNS lymphoma is the **development of painful genital ulcers** (genital aphthosis) in conjunction with existing oral aphthae and folliculitis-like rashes.

While neurosarcoidosis and CNS lymphoma can mimic the meningoencephalitic presentation and even cause SIADH through hypothalamic infiltration or inflammation, they lack the specific mucocutaneous signature of Behçet's. As illustrated in this case report, the patient initially presented with nonspecific systemic symptoms and hyponatremia, leading to a broad workup that included ruling out malignancy (via negative CSF cytology and imaging) and sarcoidosis (via normal ACE and lysozyme levels). The critical turning point occurred on day 5 with the appearance of a painful scrotal ulcer.

According to the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease, the presence of recurrent oral ulcers combined with genital ulcers constitutes a highly specific diagnostic feature. Furthermore, the concurrent resolution of neurological symptoms, SIADH-related hyponatremia, and mucocutaneous lesions upon initiation of colchicine monotherapy supports that the neurological dysfunction was driven by acute neuro-Behçet's disease rather than a neoplastic or granulomatous process requiring different therapeutic modalities. Thus, in the setting of aseptic meningitis and SIADH, new-onset genital ulceration serves as the pathognomonic sign anchoring the diagnosis to Behçet's disease.

### [Management] Given that standard guidelines typically recommend high-dose corticosteroids or immunosuppressants for acute neuro-Behçet's disease, what unique aspect of this case challenges conventional management paradigms regarding the efficacy of colchicine monotherapy?

The unique aspect of this case that challenges conventional management paradigms is the successful resolution of **acute parenchymal neuro-Behçet's disease and associated SIADH using colchicine monotherapy**, whereas current guidelines universally recommend high-dose corticosteroids combined with immunosuppressants (such as azathioprine or cyclophosphamide) for active neurological involvement.

Standard teaching posits that colchicine is effective only for mucocutaneous and articular manifestations of Behçet's disease and is explicitly ineffective for controlling acute neurological flares. Systematic reviews typically cite colchicine as inadequate for neuro-BD, necessitating aggressive immunosuppression to prevent permanent disability. However, this case demonstrates that in a patient presenting with concurrent systemic Behçet's, acute meningoencephalitis (manifesting as headache, vertigo, and aseptic meningitis), and SIADH (evidenced by hyponatremia and persistent hiccups), colchicine alone induced complete remission of all three domains within two weeks.

The clinical reasoning here rests on the shared inflammatory pathway: colchicine inhibits neutrophil chemotaxis and suppresses the production of key pro-inflammatory cytokines, specifically IL-1β and IL-6. Since IL-6 is implicated in both the CNS inflammation driving neuro-BD and the dysregulated ADH secretion causing SIADH, colchicine likely addressed the root pathophysiology of both conditions simultaneously. While one must acknowledge that spontaneous resolution of meningoencephalitis-like presentations can occur, the rapid temporal correlation between initiating colchicine and the normalization of sodium levels alongside neurological improvement suggests a therapeutic effect beyond chance. This case serves as a critical reminder that while corticosteroids remain the first-line standard, colchicine monotherapy may be a viable alternative in select patients where steroid toxicity is a concern or if the clinical phenotype overlaps significantly with mucocutaneous-driven inflammation.

### [Clinical Pearls] Beyond persistent hiccups, which laboratory abnormality was identified in this case as a recognized but often overlooked complication of severe hyponatremia secondary to SIADH?

Beyond the classic presentation of persistent hiccups, the laboratory abnormality identified in this case as a recognized but often overlooked complication of severe hyponatremia secondary to SIADH is **rhabdomyolysis**, evidenced by a significantly elevated creatine kinase (CK) level of 1349 U/L.

In this patient with acute neurological Behçet's disease and concurrent SIADH, the profound hyponatremia (initially 124 mmol/L) precipitated skeletal muscle injury. While hiccups are a well-documented neuromuscular irritability sign of severe hyponatremia, rhabdomyolysis represents a more insidious metabolic consequence that can easily be attributed to alternative etiologies such as sepsis, trauma, or direct inflammatory myopathy in the setting of systemic illness. As noted in our discussion, CK elevation is not routinely investigated in every febrile patient with arthralgia and fatigue; however, in the context of neuroinflammation and SIADH, one must recognize that apparently unrelated musculoskeletal symptoms may actually signal electrolyte-driven muscle necrosis. Fortunately, in this case, the rhabdomyolysis resolved spontaneously alongside the normalization of serum sodium and ADH levels following colchicine monotherapy and fluid restriction, underscoring that treating the underlying hyponatremia is paramount to resolving these downstream muscular complications.

### [Morning Report] Why is HLA-B51 positivity considered supportive but not mandatory for diagnosing Behçet's disease according to current international criteria, and how does its utility compare to the scoring system based on mucocutaneous lesions?

Based on the case presented, HLA-B51 positivity is considered supportive but not mandatory for diagnosing Behçet's disease (BD) because the **International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD)** prioritize a weighted scoring system based on clinical phenotypes over genetic markers. As illustrated in this patient, a definitive diagnosis was established solely through clinical findings: recurrent oral aphthae (2 points), genital ulcers (2 points), and folliculitis-like skin lesions (1 point), yielding a total score of 5. Since the threshold for "definite" BD is ≥4 points, the presence of HLA-B51 was diagnostically redundant in this instance.

The ICBD framework reflects the understanding that while HLA-B51 has a strong association with BD—particularly in populations along the ancient Silk Road—it lacks sufficient sensitivity and specificity to serve as a standalone diagnostic criterion. Many patients with classic clinical presentations are HLA-B51 negative, and conversely, many healthy individuals carry the allele without developing disease. Relying on genetics could lead to false positives or delay diagnosis in seronegative patients who nonetheless exhibit the hallmark mucocutaneous triad.

In contrast, the clinical scoring system offers superior diagnostic precision by quantifying the burden of specific organ involvement. In our case, the appearance of genital aphthosis—a highly specific feature—was pivotal in shifting the diagnosis from a broad differential of infection or malignancy to definite BD, even before HLA-B51 results were available or considered essential. Furthermore, this case underscores that neurological involvement (acute neuro-BD) can present concurrently with mucocutaneous symptoms rather than years later, and its diagnosis relies on integrating these clinical scores with supportive evidence like aseptic meningitis and exclusion of mimics. Thus, while HLA-B51 serves as a helpful epidemiological clue in ambiguous cases, the ICBD scoring system remains the gold standard for establishing diagnosis based on observable pathology rather than genetic susceptibility.

### [Diagnostic Reasoning] In a patient with new-onset neurological symptoms and hyponatremia, why might serum soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) elevation be a misleading marker for hematologic malignancy in this specific inflammatory context?

In patients presenting with new-onset neurological symptoms and hyponatremia, an elevated serum soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) level can be a misleading marker for hematologic malignancy because it lacks specificity in the setting of intense systemic inflammation. While sIL-2R is a valuable biomarker for T-cell activation and is frequently elevated in lymphomas—particularly CNS lymphoma—it is also released by activated T-cells and macrophages during robust inflammatory responses unrelated to neoplasia.

As illustrated in this case of acute neurological Behçet's disease (neuro-BD), the patient presented with fever, aseptic meningitis, and SIADH alongside a mild elevation of sIL-2R (704 U/mL). Despite this finding, the clinical picture lacked evidence of malignancy: brain MRI was unremarkable, CSF cytology was negative for malignant cells, and imaging excluded extracranial tumors. Instead, the sIL-2R elevation reflected the underlying polygenic inflammatory storm characteristic of Behçet's disease. The discussion notes that such mild elevations are non-specific and likely represent immune activation driven by cytokines like IL-6, which is known to be elevated in acute neuro-BD. Consequently, relying solely on sIL-2R levels without corroborating clinical, radiological, or pathological evidence risks misdiagnosing a benign inflammatory disorder as a malignancy. In this specific context, the marker served as a red herring; the definitive diagnosis relied on the constellation of mucocutaneous findings (oral/genital ulcers) and the exclusion of infectious or neoplastic etiologies, ultimately confirming that the "malignant" signal was actually a manifestation of active vasculitis.
