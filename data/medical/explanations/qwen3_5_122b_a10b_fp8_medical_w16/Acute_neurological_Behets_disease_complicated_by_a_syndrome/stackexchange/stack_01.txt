Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Based on the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), how many points did this patient accumulate from his clinical findings to establish a diagnosis of 'definite' Behçet's disease?

Based on the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), this patient accumulated a total of **five points**, thereby satisfying the threshold of ≥4 points required to establish a diagnosis of "definite" Behçet's disease.

The scoring breakdown relies strictly on the clinical findings documented in this case:
*   **Oral aphthae:** Awarded **2 points**. The patient presented with recurrent oral ulcers, which serves as the mandatory entry criterion for ICBD classification.
*   **Genital aphthosis:** Awarded **2 points**. Although the painful scrotal ulcer developed later in the course (on day 5), its presence alongside oral lesions solidified the mucocutaneous phenotype.
*   **Folliculitis-like rash:** Awarded **1 point**. The patient exhibited characteristic skin lesions on the face and trunk, distinct from acne or erythema nodosum.

It is important to note that while the patient presented with acute neurological manifestations (headache, vertigo, aseptic meningitis) and tested positive for *HLA-B51*, neither neurological involvement nor genetic markers contributed to the ICBD score. Under current criteria, neurological and vascular involvement are not assigned specific point values within the primary scoring algorithm; rather, they represent severe organ involvement once the diagnosis is secured via mucocutaneous criteria. Similarly, *HLA-B51* positivity was noted but carries zero weight in the ICBD calculation. With a cumulative score of 5, well above the cutoff of 4, and after excluding infectious, neoplastic, and other autoimmune etiologies, the diagnosis of definite Behçet's disease was confirmed concurrently with acute neuro-Behçet's disease.
