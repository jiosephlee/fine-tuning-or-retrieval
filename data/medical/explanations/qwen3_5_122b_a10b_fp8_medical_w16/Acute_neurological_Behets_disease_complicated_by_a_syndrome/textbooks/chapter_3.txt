Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

Chapter 3: ## Diagnostic Reasoning: Exclusionary Workup and Confirmatory Criteria

The diagnostic journey in this case began with a critical challenge: distinguishing between a primary infectious etiology, a neoplastic process, and an emerging autoimmune disorder in a patient presenting with fever, headache, and profound hyponatraemia. Initial clinical suspicion naturally leaned toward viral meningoencephalitis, specifically Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) or Varicella Zoster Virus (VZV), given the acute onset of neurological symptoms and fever. Consequently, empirical antiviral therapy was initiated while awaiting confirmatory data. However, the definitive exclusion of infectious causes relied heavily on comprehensive cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis. The CSF profile demonstrated mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein but lacked the neutrophilic predominance typical of bacterial meningitis. Crucially, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays and antigen testing for HSV, VZV, and other common pathogens were negative, as were cultures for bacteria and mycobacteria. This effectively ruled out tuberculous meningitis and viral encephalitis, shifting the diagnostic focus away from an infectious agent toward a sterile inflammatory or neoplastic process.

With infection excluded, the differential diagnosis expanded to include primary central nervous system (CNS) malignancy and other systemic inflammatory diseases such as neurosarcoidosis. The presence of elevated soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) levels raised concern for hematologic malignancy or lymphoma; however, this marker must always be interpreted within the clinical context. While sIL-2R is sensitive for lymphoproliferative disorders, mild elevations are non-specific and can occur in various active inflammatory states. In this case, the absence of mass lesions on brain MRI and the lack of malignant cells on CSF cytology rendered CNS lymphoma highly unlikely despite the biochemical anomaly. Similarly, neurosarcoidosis was considered due to its ability to mimic CNS inflammation with normal imaging in early stages. Yet, the normal serum angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) and lysozyme levels provided evidence against granulomatous disease, reinforcing that these systemic markers were likely reflecting non-specific immune activation rather than a specific sarcoidotic process.

The pivotal moment in the diagnostic algorithm occurred on day 5 with the emergence of a painful scrotal ulcer. Prior to this event, the diagnosis remained equivocal despite the presence of oral aphthae and folliculitis-like rashes; however, genital ulceration is a hallmark feature that significantly increases diagnostic specificity for Behçet's disease (BD). This new finding allowed clinicians to apply the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), which assigns points based on clinical manifestations: two points each for oral aphthosis and genital aphthosis, one point for folliculitis-like rash or papulopustular lesions. With a cumulative score of five—exceeding the threshold of four required for "definite" BD—the systemic nature of the disease was confirmed. Notably, while HLA-B51 positivity was noted later in the workup, it served merely as supportive evidence rather than a diagnostic requirement under current ICBD guidelines.

The final synthesis of the diagnosis integrated these systemic findings with the neurological presentation to establish acute neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD). The patient exhibited classic signs of CNS inflammation—headache, vertigo, fever, and aseptic meningitis on lumbar puncture—in the setting of confirmed systemic BD. Importantly, this diagnosis was reached despite a normal brain MRI showing no parenchymal lesions in the brainstem or hypothalamus. This case illustrates that acute neuro-BD can manifest primarily as aseptic meningitis without visible structural abnormalities on imaging during early phases. Therefore, once infectious and neoplastic mimics were rigorously excluded through CSF analysis and advanced imaging, the presence of definitive mucocutaneous criteria combined with sterile meningeal inflammation provided sufficient grounds to diagnose acute neuro-BD complicated by SIADH. This logical progression underscored that in patients with characteristic mucocutaneous lesions presenting with non-specific neurological symptoms and hyponatraemia，the exclusion of alternative causes followed by strict adherence to ICBD scoring is essential for timely identification of this rare but treatable condition.
