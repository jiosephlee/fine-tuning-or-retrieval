Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Based on the histological findings described in this case, what specific cellular markers and patterns distinguish Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM) from classic Polymyositis?

The critical histological distinction between Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM) and classic Polymyositis lies in the dominant cell type mediating myofiber destruction and the density of the inflammatory infiltrate. As illustrated in this case, IMNM is characterized by prominent **myophagocytosis** and necrosis with a **paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates**.

In classic Polymyositis, the pathology is driven by endomysial infiltration of **CD8+ T-cells**, which directly invade non-necrotic muscle fibers expressing MHC Class I antigens. Conversely, in IMNM, the primary effector cells are **macrophages**. In our patient's biopsy of the vastus lateralis, we observed abundant macrophages identified by **CD68** staining actively engulfing necrotic debris (myophagocytosis), alongside scattered **rare CD3+ T-cells**. Furthermore, IMNM frequently demonstrates deposition of the **membrane attack complex (MAC/C5b-9)** on both necrotic and viable non-necrotic myofibers, indicating complement-mediated injury—a feature less central to the T-cell mediated cytotoxicity seen in Polymyositis.

While Polymyositis typically presents with a dense lymphocytic infiltrate surrounding and invading fibers, this case highlights the "necrotizing" phenotype where inflammation is sparse relative to the extent of fiber necrosis. This histological pattern, combined with the clinical absence of other connective tissue disease features and the presence of severe weakness with markedly elevated CK, supports the diagnosis of IMNM even in this seronegative patient (negative for anti-HMGCR and anti-SRP). Recognizing this macrophage-dominant, T-cell-poor pattern is essential to differentiate IMNM from Polymyositis, as it dictates a therapeutic approach focused on B-cell modulation and complement inhibition (e.g., IVIG) rather than solely targeting T-cell pathways.
