Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Reasoning] In a patient presenting with thrombocytopenia and new-onset thrombosis shortly after cardiac surgery, what specific element of the preoperative history can explain an 'early-onset' (<5 days) presentation of Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT), contrary to the typical post-operative timeline?

In a patient presenting with thrombocytopenia and new-onset thrombosis within five days of cardiac surgery, the critical historical element that explains this "early-onset" presentation of Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT) is **recent preoperative exposure to heparin**.

Classically, HIT manifests 5 to 10 days after heparin initiation because it requires time for the immune system to generate IgG antibodies against the platelet factor 4 (PF4)/heparin complex. However, if a patient received heparin within the preceding 100 days—typically defined as less than 30 days for rapid recall—the immune system retains memory B-cells capable of producing anamnestic antibody responses. Upon re-exposure during surgery, these pre-existing antibodies bind immediately to circulating PF4/heparin complexes, triggering rapid platelet activation and consumption.

As illustrated in the case report by St Bernard et al., this mechanism was pivotal in diagnosing autoimmune HIT (aHIT) in an elderly man who presented with severe thrombocytopenia and a parietal stroke by postoperative day 5. While the massive intraoperative unfractionated heparin dose during cardiopulmonary bypass provided the antigenic stimulus, the timing of the reaction was dictated by a small dose of unfractionated heparin administered just one week earlier during a diagnostic heart catheterization. Without recognizing this preoperative exposure, clinicians might erroneously attribute early postoperative thrombocytopenia to surgical factors or dismiss HIT as impossible due to the short interval. Therefore, in any case of suspected early-onset HIT post-cardiac surgery, one must specifically inquire about invasive procedures involving heparin performed weeks prior to admission.
