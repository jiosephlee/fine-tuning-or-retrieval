Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] How does the pathophysiology of Autoimmune HIT (aHIT) differ mechanistically from classic heparin-dependent HIT regarding the requirement for exogenous heparin to trigger platelet activation via FcγRIIa receptors?

In classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), platelet activation is strictly dependent on the presence of exogenous heparin. Mechanistically, IgG antibodies bind to complexes formed between platelet factor 4 (PF4) and heparin; these immune complexes then cross-link FcγRIIa receptors on the platelet surface, triggering potent activation and thrombosis. Consequently, in a standard serotonin-release assay (SRA), platelet activation requires low-to-moderate concentrations of heparin to facilitate complex formation and is inhibited at supratherapeutic concentrations where excess heparin prevents complex assembly.

Autoimmune HIT (aHIT) diverges fundamentally because the pathogenic IgG antibodies possess intrinsic heparin-independent activity. As illustrated in this case report, aHIT antibodies can spontaneously activate platelets even in the complete absence of added heparin (0 U/mL). While these antibodies often retain some ability to bind PF4-heparin complexes, their defining feature is the capacity to induce massive serotonin release without any exogenous cofactor. Furthermore, unlike classic HIT where FcγRIIa blockade effectively abrogates activation, aHIT antibodies may demonstrate partial resistance to such blockade, suggesting that the mechanism of receptor clustering or downstream signaling involves pathways less susceptible to simple competitive inhibition by anti-FcγRIIa monoclonal antibodies like IV.3. In summary, while classic HIT is an "on-off" switch requiring heparin to flip the immune complex lever, aHIT represents a constitutively active state where the antibody itself drives platelet destruction and thrombosis regardless of circulating heparin levels.
