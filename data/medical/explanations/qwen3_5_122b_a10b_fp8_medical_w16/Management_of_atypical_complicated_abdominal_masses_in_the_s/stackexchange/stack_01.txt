Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] How do the physiological fluctuations of serum CA-125 during pregnancy complicate the differentiation between benign uterine pathology (such as leiomyomas or endometriosis) and malignant ovarian neoplasms?

**Clinical Pearl:** The utility of serum CA-125 in differentiating benign uterine pathology from malignant ovarian neoplasms during pregnancy is severely compromised by physiological fluctuations and the inherent cross-reactivity of the marker with benign conditions. As illustrated in this case of a patient in her fourth decade presenting with massive abdominal masses in the second trimester, relying on a single CA-125 measurement above the standard cutoff (≥35 U/mL) can lead to diagnostic confusion and potentially unnecessary anxiety regarding malignancy.

Physiologically, CA-125 levels exhibit a biphasic pattern during gestation, typically peaking in the first trimester (median ~23 U/mL) and again in the third trimester (~21 U/mL), with a nadir occurring in the second trimester (median ~14 U/mL). While our patient's level of 41.8 U/mL exceeded both the standard reference range and the expected median for her gestational age, this elevation did not confirm ovarian carcinoma. Instead, it reflected the complex interplay of benign etiologies known to elevate CA-125, including endometriosis, adenomyosis, pelvic inflammation, and notably, uterine leiomyomas undergoing degeneration or torsion.

In this specific case, imaging suggested two distinct entities: an upper mass mimicking a decidualized endometrioma and a lower mass appearing continuous with a posterior intramural-subserosal leiomyoma. The "slight" elevation of CA-125 initially favored an endometriotic origin for the upper lesion; however, surgical exploration revealed that *both* masses were actually giant, pedunculated leiomyomas—one of which was twisted and ischemic. This underscores a critical pathophysiological principle: serous mesothelial irritation caused by hemorrhage, necrosis, or inflammation from degenerating fibroids can stimulate peritoneal production of CA-125 independent of malignancy. Consequently, an isolated elevated value cannot reliably distinguish between a benign degenerating myoma and an epithelial ovarian tumor.

Therefore, clinical reasoning must prioritize morphological characterization via high-resolution ultrasound and non-contrast MRI over biomarker interpretation alone. As demonstrated here, despite the elevated CA-125 suggesting potential malignancy or endometriosis, the definitive diagnosis relied on imaging features—specifically the continuity of the caudal mass with uterine tissue—and ultimately histopathology confirming benign leiomyomas with ischemic necrosis. In pregnant patients with suspected adnexal or uterine masses, serial monitoring of trends may offer some insight into progression versus stability, but decisions regarding surgical intervention should be driven by symptomatology (pain, obstruction), risk of acute complications (torsion/rupture), and anatomical constraints rather than biomarker thresholds alone.
