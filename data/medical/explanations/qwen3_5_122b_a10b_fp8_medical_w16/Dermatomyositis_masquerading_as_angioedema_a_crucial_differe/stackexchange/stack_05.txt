Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] Based on the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines mentioned in the case, what factors contributed to classifying this patient as 'high risk' for underlying malignancy, and what constitutes an appropriate screening strategy for such patients?

Based on the IMACS guidelines referenced in this case, the classification of "high risk" for underlying malignancy in this patient was driven by a confluence of specific demographic, clinical, and serologic factors. While dermatomyositis itself carries an inherent increased risk of paraneoplastic syndromes compared to other inflammatory myopathies, this patient possessed two distinct **high-risk** criteria: the diagnosis of dermatomyositis (specifically with significant cutaneous and muscular involvement) and the presence of **dysphagia**. Furthermore, he exhibited two **intermediate-risk** factors: male sex and positivity for **anti-SAE1 antibodies**. Although anti-SAE1 is often associated with cutaneous-dominant disease and a lower malignancy rate than anti-TIF1-γ or anti-NXP2, the IMACS framework aggregates these variables; the combination of adult-onset dermatomyositis with dysphagia elevates the pre-test probability sufficiently to mandate aggressive screening.

Consequently, the appropriate screening strategy for such high-risk patients must be comprehensive and multimodal, as illustrated by our workup. Initial evaluation should include a thorough history and physical examination focused on constitutional symptoms and lymphadenopathy. Imaging is paramount; in this case, we utilized a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis alongside an FDG-PET scan to identify occult metabolic activity or structural masses not visible on standard cross-sectional imaging. Laboratory assessment included age-appropriate tumor markers (such as CEA, CA-19-9, CA-125, and PSA), acknowledging their limited sensitivity but utility as adjuncts. Crucially, because solid organ imaging can miss mucosal lesions, endoscopic evaluation is mandatory. As outlined in our follow-up plan for this patient, upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy (gastroscopy and colonoscopy) are essential components of the initial screen to rule out colorectal or gastric malignancies that might be asymptomatic at presentation. Given that malignancy can precede or coincide with myositis diagnosis, IMACS recommends repeating this extensive screening annually for at least four years in high-risk individuals who remain negative on initial evaluation.
