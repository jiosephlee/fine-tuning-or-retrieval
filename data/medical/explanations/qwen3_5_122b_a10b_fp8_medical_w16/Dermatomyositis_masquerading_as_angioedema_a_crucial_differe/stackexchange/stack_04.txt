Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] When evaluating a new-onset inflammatory myopathy like dermatomyositis, why is it critical to assess for interstitial lung disease prior to initiating certain immunosuppressive therapies such as methotrexate, and how did this influence the treatment selection in this specific case?

In the evaluation of new-onset inflammatory myopathies such as dermatomyositis, assessing for interstitial lung disease (ILD) before initiating specific immunosuppressants is a critical safety step because certain agents, particularly methotrexate, carry a well-documented risk of inducing or exacerbating pulmonary fibrosis. While methotrexate is often a first-line steroid-sparing agent for myositis, its use is relatively contraindicated in patients with pre-existing ILD or those with antibody profiles strongly associated with rapid-onset lung injury. Failure to screen could precipitate life-threatening acute respiratory failure in an already vulnerable patient.

In this specific case, the decision to avoid methotrexate was directly driven by the patient's pulmonary status and antibody profile. Although the patient was a lifelong non-smoker and lacked classic radiographic fibrosis on initial CT, his pulmonary function tests revealed an extrathoracic restrictive pattern, and he exhibited significant dysphagia leading to aspiration pneumonitis—a known complication that complicates the pulmonary picture. Furthermore, while anti-SAE1 antibodies are classically associated with cutaneous prominence and dysphagia, they are also linked to ILD in approximately 43% of cases. Given the presence of restrictive physiology and the inherent risk of methotrexate-induced pneumonitis superimposed on active muscle inflammation affecting the respiratory musculature, the treating team opted against methotrexate. Instead, they selected a regimen comprising high-dose corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), rituximab, and mycophenolate mofetil. This approach allowed for aggressive control of the severe myositis and pseudo-angioedema while minimizing iatrogenic pulmonary toxicity, ultimately resulting in full recovery of muscle strength and resolution of swallowing dysfunction without worsening respiratory compromise.
