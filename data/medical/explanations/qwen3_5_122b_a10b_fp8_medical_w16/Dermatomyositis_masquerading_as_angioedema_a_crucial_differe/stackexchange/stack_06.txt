Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] If a muscle biopsy is performed on a patient with suspected dermatomyositis, what is the hallmark histological pattern of inflammation and what specific immunohistochemical finding regarding the complement system confirms the diagnosis?

Based on the case of anti-SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis presented here, the hallmark histological pattern observed on muscle biopsy is **perifascicular atrophy**, characterized by foci of necrotic, vacuolated, and regenerating myofibers specifically located at the periphery of the muscle fascicles. Unlike polymyositis, where inflammation is predominantly endomysial and involves individual fibers throughout the fascicle, dermatomyositis exhibits a distinct topographic distribution of pathology centered around the fascicular border.

The specific immunohistochemical finding that confirms this diagnosis is the deposition of the **membrane attack complex (complement component C5b-9)** within the **capillaries of the perifascicular endomysium**. As illustrated in Figure 4 of our case report, this microvascular injury is driven by complement activation, leading to ischemia and subsequent atrophy of the adjacent outermost muscle fibers. While our patient's clinical presentation was dominated by profound neck swelling mimicking angioedema—a manifestation arising from muscle inflammation rather than histaminergic or bradykinin pathways—the underlying pathophysiology remains rooted in this complement-mediated microangiopathy. Identifying C5b-9 deposition in perifascicular capillaries alongside perifascicular atrophy provides definitive pathological confirmation of dermatomyositis, distinguishing it from other inflammatory myopathies even when classic cutaneous signs like Gottron's papules are absent or subtle.
