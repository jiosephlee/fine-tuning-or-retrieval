Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy

### [Management] During exploratory laparotomy at 22 weeks, a large pedunculated cystic mass is found to be a twisted, leaking, highly vascular fibroid. What surgical steps minimise uterine manipulation and haemorrhage while safely resecting the pedicle?

When you open the abdomen at 22 weeks and find a large pedunculated, torsed, leaking and highly vascular leiomyoma, the priorities are to remove the source of sepsis/bleeding quickly while disturbing the gravid uterus as little as possible and securing haemostasis. The operative plan should therefore focus on isolating and controlling the pedicle, not on extensive uterine dissection.

First, avoid routine manipulation or exteriorisation of the uterus — leave the uterus in situ and handle only the pedunculated mass with atraumatic forceps. Identify the pedicle at its uterine implantation and expose a length of stalk sufficient to apply control. Inject dilute vasopressin into the base of the stalk (if not contraindicated) to produce local vasoconstriction and reduce bleeding. Place two atraumatic vascular clamps across the pedicle (one proximal, one distal) and then ligate the base with a secure absorbable transfixing suture (figure‑of‑eight or transfixing ligature) close to the uterine wall; divide the stalk between clamps. If the pedicle is broad-based, apply a purse‑string or interrupted figure‑of‑eight suture to include myometrium and serosa and reinforce the uterine side. Use bipolar cautery for any small bleeding points; avoid extensive myometrial resection.

Keep the procedure short. Maintain cell‑saving and blood products available and monitor fetal heart rate before, during and after. Minimise uterine contractions by avoiding uterotonics intraoperatively (unless indicated for haemorrhage postpartum) and by gentle tissue handling. If the lesion is very vascular or the pedicle cannot be readily controlled, consider temporary uterine artery occlusion (clips or vessel loops) or a local tourniquet placed at the lower uterine segment to reduce inflow while you ligate the stalk. Close any myometrial defect with an absorbable single‑layer, inverted or imbricating suture to reapproximate serosa and prevent bleeding.

In short: expose and control the pedicle with local vasoconstriction, double clamp and transfixing ligature placed close to the uterus, divide between clamps, secure uterine side with absorbable sutures, use bipolar coagulation for oozing, avoid uterine exteriorisation and prolonged manipulation, and keep operative time and blood loss to a minimum while having blood products and fetal monitoring ready.
