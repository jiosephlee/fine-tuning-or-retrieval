### [Differential Diagnosis] List benign and malignant etiologies for two large, unilocular cystic abdominal masses detected in the second trimester, and provide one or two imaging features for each that help in differentiation.

When confronted with two large, unilocular cystic abdominal masses in the second trimester, the differential must explicitly include pathologies of ovarian/adnexal origin, cystic uterine lesions (including degenerating myomas), inflammatory collections, and malignant cystic neoplasms. Imaging features on ultrasound (grayscale + Doppler) and non‑contrast MRI that point toward one diagnosis over another are crucial because management in pregnancy balances maternal/fetal risk with the low but real possibility of malignancy.

Benign etiologies

1) Ovarian serous cystadenoma (simple adnexal cyst)
Reasoning and key imaging features: usually unilocular, thin-walled and anechoic on US with no solid components and minimal or absent internal vascularity. On MRI it is homogeneously fluid signal (low T1, high T2) without mural nodules or thick septa.

2) Endometrioma (including decidualised endometrioma in pregnancy)
Reasoning and key imaging features: classic T1 hyperintense “blood‑product” signal with T2 shading on MRI; on US often homogeneous low‑level internal echoes. Decidualisation can produce vascularized mural nodules during pregnancy — look for vascularized papillary solid components that may mimic malignancy but usually lack irregular invasive borders and diffuse peritoneal disease.

3) Cystic degeneration of uterine leiomyoma (including pedunculated cystic leiomyoma)
Reasoning and key imaging features: continuity with the uterine myometrium or a visible pedicle/stalk (bridging vessel sign on Doppler) and location abutting the uterus point to myoma origin. MRI shows a lesion contiguous with uterine tissue, often heterogeneous T2 signal with areas of cystic change; lack of true enhancing papillary nodules favors benign degeneration.

4) Paraovarian/paratubal cyst
Reasoning and key imaging features: thin‑walled unilocular cyst separate from the ovary on careful transvaginal US (ovary visualized separately) and simple fluid signal on MRI without solid components.

5) Tubo‑ovarian abscess or infected cyst
Reasoning and key imaging features: clinically febrile/raised inflammatory markers; imaging shows thick irregular walls, internal debris or septations, surrounding inflammatory fat stranding on MRI and restricted diffusion on DWI sequences. Peripheral hyperemia on Doppler supports inflammation.

Malignant (or borderline) etiologies

1) Ovarian epithelial malignancy / cystadenocarcinoma
Reasoning and key imaging features: multiloculated or unilocular cysts with mural nodules or papillary projections, thick/irregular septations, solid components with internal vascularity on Doppler; MRI demonstrates solid T2‑intermediate nodules within cyst, heterogeneous signal, and often associated ascites or peritoneal implants.

2) Borderline ovarian tumor
Reasoning and key imaging features: papillary excrescences or nodules on the inner cyst wall but often less invasive appearance than frank carcinoma; nodules are vascularized but peritoneal invasion is absent or limited.

3) Cystic ovarian metastasis (Krukenberg‑type or other)
Reasoning and key imaging features: often bilateral, solid nodular components or multiple nodules, associated peritoneal disease and lymphadenopathy; MRI/US show mixed cystic–solid masses with marked vascularity.

4) Cystic degeneration in a uterine sarcoma (e.g., leiomyosarcoma with necrosis)
Reasoning and key imaging features: rapid interval growth, irregular margins, heterogeneous signal with hemorrhagic/necrotic areas (high T1 signal foci), and irregular solid components that on Doppler show chaotic internal vascularity. True sarcoma is rare in pregnancy but suspect when rapid growth plus irregular enhancing solid tissue is present.

How to apply this to the presented case
The imaging that matters here is (A) continuity with the uterus or a visible pedicle — strongly favors leiomyoma/cystic degeneration or pedunculated myoma rather than primary ovarian tumor, and (B) presence of vascularized solid mural nodules or papillary projections — raises concern for decidualised endometrioma or neoplasm. The case’s features — large unilocular cysts, one contiguous with a posterior myoma, peripheral hypertrophied vessels, inhomogeneous fluid and focal T1/T2 hyperintense solid components — narrow the differential to cystic degenerated leiomyoma (or pedunculated cystic myoma) versus decidualised endometrioma; the absence of ascites, nodes, and diffuse peritoneal disease and normal other tumor markers argued against ovarian carcinoma, while intraoperative identification of uterine stalks confirmed benign myomata.
