### [Management] In the second trimester, which imaging modalities are preferred and considered safe for characterising complex adnexal or uterine masses, and which should be avoided unless benefits clearly outweigh fetal risks?

In the second trimester the cornerstone for evaluating adnexal or uterine masses is transabdominal and transvaginal ultrasound with colour Doppler; it gives real‑time anatomic detail, vascularity and dynamic relationships to the gravid uterus without ionising radiation and is the first‑line test. When ultrasound is inconclusive for complex, large, posterior or multiple lesions, non‑contrast MRI is the preferred second‑line study because it provides superior soft‑tissue characterisation and multiplanar anatomy without ionising radiation. Current practice is to use non‑contrast MRI at 1.5 T (avoid routine use of higher field strengths) and to avoid gadolinium unless a life‑threatening maternal diagnosis cannot be established otherwise and the information would change management.

Studies and guidelines therefore recommend avoiding imaging modalities that expose the fetus to ionising radiation or transplacental contrast unless the maternal benefit clearly outweighs fetal risk. CT scans (especially of the abdomen/pelvis), PET/CT and diagnostic studies requiring iodinated or gadolinium contrast should not be used routinely in pregnancy. Gadolinium crosses the placenta and has been associated with rare fetal/neonatal adverse effects; iodinated contrast can affect fetal thyroid function. Plain radiographs or limited CT for critical, time‑sensitive indications may be acceptable with dose minimisation and shielding, but they should be an exception after multidisciplinary discussion and informed consent.

Clinical principle: use the least risky test that will answer the clinical question. Start with ultrasound, proceed to non‑contrast 1.5‑T MRI when needed for anatomical clarification, and reserve contrast‑enhanced MRI, CT or PET only for situations where diagnostic uncertainty poses a greater threat to mother or fetus than the imaging risks.
