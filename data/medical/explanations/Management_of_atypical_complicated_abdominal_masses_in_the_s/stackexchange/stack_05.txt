### [Clinical Pearls] How should a CA-125 level of 42 U/mL in the second trimester be interpreted in a patient with large adnexal/uterine masses, and what is the value of serial trends versus single measurements during pregnancy?

A CA‑125 of 42 U/mL in the second trimester must be interpreted in context, not as a standalone “cancer” signal. CA‑125 normally varies with pregnancy (peaks in the first and again in the third trimester) and is influenced by many benign processes—including leiomyomas, endometriosis, pelvic inflammation, necrosis, torsion and even peritoneal irritation. The usual oncologic cut‑off of 35 U/mL loses positive predictive value in pregnancy and in the presence of large pelvic masses.

The clinical reasoning therefore is: a single, modest elevation (eg, 36 → 42 U/mL) in the second trimester is non‑specific and by itself is insufficient to diagnose malignancy or mandate radical intervention. What matters more is the trend and concordance with the rest of the picture. A steadily rising CA‑125 over days–weeks, especially when accompanied by new pain, fever, inflammatory markers or imaging showing complex, solid components, thick walls, increased vascularity or loss of cleavage planes, increases concern for complication (degeneration, torsion, infection) or, less commonly, neoplasm. Conversely, a stable, mildly elevated value with reassuring ultrasound/MRI features and benign clinical course argues for expectant management.

Practically, use CA‑125 as an adjunctive, serial test rather than a single diagnostic threshold in pregnancy. Repeat measurements at short interval (eg, 1–2 weeks) to define a trend, and always interpret them with careful clinical assessment and high‑quality imaging. In the presented case the modest but rising CA‑125 combined with worsening pain, fever and complex, highly vascularised masses on imaging appropriately raised concern and supported the decision to operate; the final pathology (degenerating, torsed pedunculated leiomyoma) is a classic benign explanation for the CA‑125 change.
