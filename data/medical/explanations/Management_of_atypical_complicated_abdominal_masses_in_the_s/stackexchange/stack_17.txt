### [Management] What analgesic strategies are preferred for fibroid-related pain in pregnancy, and which medications should be avoided or limited by gestational age due to fetal risks?

Acetaminophen (paracetamol) is the first‑line analgesic for fibroid‑related pain in pregnancy because it is effective for moderate pain, has a favorable safety profile for the fetus at usual doses, and was the drug used successfully in this case. Nonpharmacologic measures — pelvic rest, positional changes (semi‑upright to reduce diaphragmatic compression), abdominal support, heat, and iron repletion if anaemia is present — should be applied concurrently because they reduce analgesic needs and maternal physiologic stress.

If acetaminophen alone is inadequate, short, judicious use of other agents may be considered with attention to gestational age and fetal risks. Nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) can provide superior analgesia for myoma pain or degeneration but must be used with caution: avoid routine or prolonged NSAID use in pregnancy and especially avoid NSAIDs after mid‑pregnancy because of well‑documented fetal risks (oligohydramnios from decreased fetal renal perfusion and premature closure of the ductus arteriosus). Current practice is to avoid NSAIDs after about 20 weeks’ gestation (and certainly in the third trimester); if used earlier, keep duration and dose as short and low as possible and counsel about potential risks. Selective COX‑2 inhibitors carry similar concerns and should be avoided.

Opioids are acceptable for severe, refractory pain for short courses, using the lowest effective dose and monitoring the mother and fetus; prolonged or high‑dose opioid exposure in late pregnancy risks neonatal respiratory depression at delivery and neonatal withdrawal. Avoid chronic opioid therapy during pregnancy whenever possible.

Practical approach: begin with acetaminophen and supportive measures; if breakthrough pain before mid‑pregnancy, a short NSAID course may be used sparingly with informed consent; after ~20 weeks, avoid NSAIDs and, if needed, use short opioid courses with obstetric and neonatal planning. Always weigh maternal benefit against fetal risk and coordinate management with obstetrics, anaesthesia and (if needed) neonatology.
