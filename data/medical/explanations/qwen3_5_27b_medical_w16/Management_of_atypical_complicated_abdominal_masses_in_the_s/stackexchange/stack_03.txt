Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] What clinical criteria necessitate surgical intervention for uterine leiomyomas during pregnancy, as opposed to expectant management?

Expectant management is the standard of care for uterine leiomyomas during pregnancy, as surgical intervention carries significant risks of hemorrhage, pregnancy loss, and preterm labor. However, surgical intervention becomes necessary when specific clinical criteria indicate that the risks of the masses outweigh the risks of the procedure. Based on this case, the criteria necessitating surgery include persistent or worsening symptoms unresponsive to conservative measures, acute complications such as torsion or degeneration, and oncological suspicion.

In this patient, the decision for laparotomy at 21 weeks was driven by the worsening clinical condition, specifically severe abdominal pain and restrictive dyspnea caused by the mechanical compression of the large masses. Conservative measures, including analgesia and positioning, provided only temporary relief. Furthermore, the imaging and clinical picture suggested complicated masses at high risk for rupture, torsion, or infection; indeed, intraoperative findings confirmed the upper mass was a pedunculated leiomyoma that was twisted and leaking, consistent with ischemic necrosis.

Additionally, surgical management was indicated here to address diagnostic uncertainty. The masses presented with atypical features, including mixed solid and cystic components and a rising CA-125 trend, which raised concern for malignancy despite the likelihood of benign degeneration. The discussion emphasizes that myomectomy should be considered for complicated masses with torsion or persistent symptoms. In this instance, the surgical team prioritized removing the pedunculated, symptomatic upper mass while leaving the fixed lower mass in situ to minimize obstetric risk, illustrating that the goal is to preserve the pregnancy while mitigating immediate life-threatening complications.
