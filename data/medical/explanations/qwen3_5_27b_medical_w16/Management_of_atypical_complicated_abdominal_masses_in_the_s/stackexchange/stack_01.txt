Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a pregnant patient with an abdominal mass, how does the interpretation of CA-125 differ from the non-pregnant population, and what are the physiologic peaks during gestation?

In the pregnant population, the interpretation of CA-125 differs significantly from the non-pregnant standard due to physiologic fluctuations during gestation. While the standard oncological cutoff for ovarian cancer is generally ≥35 U/mL, CA-125 levels vary by trimester. As noted in the discussion of this case, serum CA-125 typically reaches two physiologic peaks: one in the first trimester (median 23 U/mL) and another in the third trimester (median 21 U/mL), with lower levels observed during the second trimester (median 14.0 U/mL).

In our patient, the CA-125 was 41.8 U/mL at 21 weeks gestation. Although this exceeded both the standard cutoff and the second-trimester median, the final pathology revealed benign uterine leiomyomas. This underscores that CA-125 is not specific for malignancy in pregnancy, as elevations can occur with benign conditions such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, and myomas, as well as during menstruation and near-term pregnancy. Therefore, isolated measurements are not diagnostic. Clinical management should prioritize serial evaluations to establish trends and rely on safe imaging modalities, such as ultrasound and non-contrast MRI, rather than relying solely on tumor markers to avoid overtreatment.
