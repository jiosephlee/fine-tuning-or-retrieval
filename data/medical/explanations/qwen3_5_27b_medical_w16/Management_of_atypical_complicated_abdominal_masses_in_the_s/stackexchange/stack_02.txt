Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] Why is gadolinium-enhanced MRI generally contraindicated during pregnancy, and what are the safety guidelines for non-contrast MRI in the second trimester?

Gadolinium-enhanced MRI is generally contraindicated during pregnancy due to the risk of adverse fetal outcomes. As noted in the management of this patient, current recommendations advise against contrast administration unless strictly necessary because of the potential for rheumatological, inflammatory, or infiltrative skin conditions in the neonate, as well as an increased risk of stillbirth or neonatal death.

For non-contrast MRI, which was utilized in this case to characterize the abdominal masses after ultrasound, safety guidelines are more permissive. The case highlights that non-contrast MRI is considered reasonably safe after the first trimester. Specifically, the multidisciplinary team avoided using magnetic field strengths greater than 1.5-T. In the second trimester, as seen with our 21-week patient, non-contrast MRI provides superior diagnostic accuracy for complex masses, such as large intramural or posterior leiomyomas, helping to determine the relationship between the mass and the uterine cavity without the teratogenic risks associated with gadolinium.
