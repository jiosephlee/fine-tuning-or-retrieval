Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] What pathophysiologic mechanisms lead to red degeneration or torsion of pedunculated leiomyomas during pregnancy?

Based on the findings in this case, the pathophysiologic mechanisms leading to torsion and subsequent degenerative changes in uterine leiomyomas during pregnancy are driven primarily by anatomical predisposition and rapid hormonal growth.

In this patient, the upper abdominal mass was identified intraoperatively as a **huge pedunculated leiomyoma** with a connective stalk. This pedunculated anatomy is the critical structural factor predisposing the mass to **torsion**, which was confirmed by the finding that the mass was "initially twisted." The discussion notes that the presence of a pedicle presents a "significant risk for an acute, typical complication," specifically torsion.

The mechanism of **degeneration and necrosis** is closely linked to this vascular compromise and the physiological growth of the tumor during gestation. As detailed in the discussion, uterine leiomyomas follow a non-linear growth trend, increasing by approximately **6.90% every week**, with a significantly higher growth rate between the first and second trimesters. This rapid enlargement, combined with the "steric bulk" of the gravid uterus, increases the mechanical leverage on the pedicle, facilitating torsion.

Once torsion occurs, the vascular supply is compromised. The pathological examination in this case revealed **"areas of ischaemic necrosis near the twisted pedicle,"** confirming that the twisting of the stalk led to hypoxia and tissue death. The discussion further supports this, noting that mass growth carries a "higher chance to go through hypoxia, necrosis and degeneration." While the lower mass showed cystic degeneration, the acute presentation of the upper mass was driven by the torsion-induced ischemia.

Therefore, the key mechanisms are the **pedunculated anatomy** allowing for rotation and the **pregnancy-induced rapid growth** that increases mass weight and vascular demand, ultimately precipitating torsion and ischemic necrosis.
