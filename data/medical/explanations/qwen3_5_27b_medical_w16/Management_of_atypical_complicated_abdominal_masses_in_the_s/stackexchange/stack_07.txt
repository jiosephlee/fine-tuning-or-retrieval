Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] On MRI, what specific features help differentiate a degenerating leiomyoma from a decidualized endometrioma in a pregnant patient?

In this case, the most critical MRI feature for differentiating a degenerating leiomyoma from a decidualized endometrioma is the **anatomical continuity or adherence of the mass to the uterine tissue**, specifically a known leiomyoma.

In this patient, the caudal mass was identified as a degenerating leiomyoma because the MRI demonstrated that the cleavage of the mass was not clear and it appeared **in continuity with a posterior intramural-subserosal myoma**. In contrast, decidualized endometriomas typically present as separate adnexal cysts without such uterine continuity.

Regarding signal characteristics, the degenerating leiomyomas in this case exhibited specific MRI findings including:
*   **Thick walls** (measuring 1.4 cm).
*   **Inhomogeneous fluid content** on both T1 and T2 fat-sat weighted images.
*   **Focal hyperintense T1-T2 weighted solid components.**

However, imaging interpretation requires caution. The upper mass in this patient lacked an identifiable connective stalk or tumour-feeding vessel on MRI, which initially oriented the diagnosis toward an ovarian endometrioma. Surgical exploration revealed it was actually a **pedunculated leiomyoma**. Therefore, while thick walls and solid components are suggestive of degenerating fibroids in this context, the absence of a visible stalk does not rule out a myomatous origin. The anatomical relationship to the uterus remains the primary radiologic clue, though multidisciplinary assessment is essential when imaging is equivocal.
