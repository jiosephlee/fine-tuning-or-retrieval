Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the rationale for administering progesterone prophylaxis following myomectomy during pregnancy?

**Clinical Pearl:** The rationale for administering progesterone prophylaxis following myomectomy during pregnancy is to maintain uterine quiescence and mitigate the risk of preterm labor induced by surgical manipulation of the gravid uterus.

In this case, a patient at 21 weeks gestation underwent laparotomy for a symptomatic, pedunculated leiomyoma. As noted in the discussion, surgical management of uterine masses during pregnancy is typically reserved for complicated cases due to inherent risks of pregnancy loss, preterm labor, and uterine rupture. To counteract the uterine irritation associated with the myomectomy, the multidisciplinary team initiated intramuscular progesterone prophylaxis intraoperatively. This regimen continued daily until discharge and was maintained with hydroxyprogesterone caproate until the elective delivery at 37 weeks. The case illustrates that when surgical intervention is necessitated by complications such as torsion or severe symptoms, adjunctive progesterone therapy serves as a critical supportive measure to preserve fetal viability and extend gestation. The patient’s excellent maternal and neonatal outcome underscores the value of combining careful surgical technique with hormonal support to manage the obstetric risks of myomectomy.
