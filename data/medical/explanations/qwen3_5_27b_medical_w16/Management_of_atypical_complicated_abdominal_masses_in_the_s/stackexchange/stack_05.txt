Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] How does the location of a uterine leiomyoma (e.g., lower uterine segment vs. fundus) influence the mode of delivery?

The anatomical location of a uterine leiomyoma is a critical determinant in selecting the mode of delivery, primarily due to the risk of mechanical obstruction during labor. While fundal masses may not impede vaginal delivery, leiomyomas located in the lower uterine segment, isthmus, or pelvis can physically block the birth canal.

In this case, the decision for an elective caesarean delivery was driven specifically by the location of the caudal mass. Although the upper fundal mass was resected during the second trimester, the lower mass was left in situ to minimize obstetric risk during pregnancy. This caudal mass was located in the lesser pelvis and pouch of Douglas, adherent to the posterior intramural-subserosal myoma of the uterine torus and isthmic site. The clinical team determined that this mass would represent a "huge obstacle for the fetal head progression during the expulsive phase of labour." Consequently, despite the preservation of the pregnancy, an elective caesarean was performed at 37 weeks to avoid obstructed labor.

This case illustrates the management principle that the mode of delivery must depend on the size, number, and anatomical location of uterine masses. Specifically, large myomas adherent to the posterior wall of the isthmic site or occupying the pelvis often necessitate caesarean section, whereas masses in other locations may allow for vaginal birth if they do not obstruct the fetal descent.
