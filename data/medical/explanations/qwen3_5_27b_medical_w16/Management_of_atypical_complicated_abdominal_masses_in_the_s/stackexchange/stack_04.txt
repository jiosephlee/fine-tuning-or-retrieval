Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why is the second trimester considered the optimal window for non-obstetric abdominal surgery during pregnancy?

The second trimester is considered the optimal window for non-obstetric abdominal surgery during pregnancy because it balances fetal safety with surgical feasibility, minimizing the risks associated with both earlier and later gestational ages. In this case, the decision to operate at 21 weeks was guided by the need to address acute symptoms while avoiding the specific hazards of the first and third trimesters.

First, the second trimester avoids the period of most rapid pathological growth. The discussion notes that uterine leiomyomas increase in size by 6.12% between the first and second trimesters, compared to only 3.10% between the second and third. By intervening in the second trimester, we avoid the accelerated expansion phase of the first trimester, which increases the risk of acute complications such as torsion, necrosis, or hemorrhage, as seen with the twisted pedunculated mass in this patient.

Second, diagnostic and surgical safety is optimized during this period. The case highlights that non-contrast MRI is considered reasonably safe after the first trimester, allowing for precise localization of masses without the risks associated with contrast agents. Additionally, the "steric bulk" of the gravid uterus, which complicates surgical access and visualization, is less pronounced at 21 weeks than in the third trimester. This facilitates a shorter operative time, which is critical for preserving the pregnancy.

Finally, delaying surgery to the third trimester significantly increases obstetric morbidity. In this case, the residual pelvic mass would have obstructed vaginal delivery, necessitating an elective caesarean section. The discussion cites evidence that combining myomectomy with caesarean section increases hospitalization and surgery duration. Furthermore, the multidisciplinary team noted that elective surgery in the second trimester could be associated with a decreased risk of preterm delivery compared to managing complicated masses later in gestation. Therefore, when surgery is unavoidable, the second trimester offers the safest balance between maternal pathology control and fetal well-being.
