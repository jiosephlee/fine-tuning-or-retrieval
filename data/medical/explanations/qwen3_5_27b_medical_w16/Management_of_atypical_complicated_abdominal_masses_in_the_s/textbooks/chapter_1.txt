Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 1: ## Pathophysiology and Clinical Context of Abdominal Masses in Pregnancy

The management of abdominal masses during gestation requires an understanding of the epidemiology and natural history of uterine pathology, as well as the physiological adaptations that alter standard diagnostic benchmarks. Uterine leiomyomas are the most common pelvic tumors in women, with an incidence during pregnancy ranging from 0.3% to 10.1%. These masses are hormonally sensitive, and their size typically follows a non-linear trend throughout gestation. In the first and second trimesters, leiomyomas may increase in size by approximately 6.90% per week due to high estrogen and progesterone levels, which can exacerbate mass effect and symptoms. However, following delivery, significant regression often occurs, as seen in this case where the residual pelvic myoma reduced to 7.5 cm postpartum. Recognizing this growth pattern is crucial for anticipating complications, such as the worsening pain and restrictive lung disease observed in the patient at 21 weeks, as the enlarging uterus and masses compete for intra-abdominal space.

Diagnostic interpretation must also account for physiological fluctuations in serum markers that differ from non-pregnant populations. Serum CA-125, often utilized as a marker for ovarian malignancy, exhibits a biphasic pattern during pregnancy, with median peaks in the first and third trimesters and a nadir in the second trimester. While the standard cut-off for malignancy is generally ≥35 U/mL, benign conditions such as endometriosis, myomas, and pregnancy itself can elevate levels. In this case, the patient presented with a CA-125 of 41.8 U/mL during the second trimester, where the median is typically 14.0 U/mL. Although this elevation raised suspicion for endometrioma or malignancy, it ultimately proved consistent with benign leiomyomatous disease. Therefore, isolated CA-125 measurements should not be considered diagnostic for cancer in pregnancy; rather, serial evaluations are necessary to assess trends, as the clinical team noted an increasing trend that contributed to the decision for further imaging and surgical evaluation.

Imaging modalities are the cornerstone of diagnosis, but their selection is governed by fetal safety profiles. Ultrasound with colour Doppler is the first-line investigation, providing initial characterization of vascularity and morphology, as demonstrated by the identification of the two large cystic masses in this patient. When ultrasound findings are indeterminate or require precise localization relative to the uterine cavity, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the preferred advanced modality. However, contrast agents must be used with extreme caution; gadolinium-enhanced MRI is generally contraindicated due to potential risks of stillbirth or neonatal death. Consequently, the patient underwent a non-contrast MRI, which offered superior accuracy in defining the thick walls, solid components, and continuity of the caudal mass with the uterine tissue. This imaging strategy allowed the team to differentiate between decidualised endometriomas and cystic degenerating leiomyomas without exposing the fetus to unnecessary contrast risks.

Finally, the clinical presentation of abdominal masses in pregnancy is driven by specific pathophysiological mechanisms, primarily mechanical compression and tissue degeneration. Large masses can create a significant steric bulk, displacing abdominal organs and elevating the diaphragm, which directly compromises respiratory mechanics. In this case, the patient’s dyspnoea was attributed to mechanical compression from the massive abdominal lesions rather than primary pulmonary or cardiac dysfunction. Additionally, rapid growth or torsion of a pedunculated mass can compromise blood supply, leading to ischaemic necrosis and red degeneration. This pathophysiology explains the patient’s severe abdominal pain and low-grade fever, which were later confirmed by pathology to result from the twisted, leaking pedunculated leiomyoma. Understanding these mechanisms helps clinicians distinguish between obstetric emergencies and mass-related complications, guiding the decision to proceed with surgical intervention to relieve symptoms and prevent further degeneration.
