Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] Based on the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, what specific risk factors in this case mandated a comprehensive malignancy screening protocol?

In accordance with the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, this patient was classified as high risk for malignancy due to a specific constellation of clinical and serological features. As detailed in the discussion section of the report, the patient possessed two high-risk factors: the diagnosis of dermatomyositis itself and the presence of dysphagia. Additionally, he exhibited two intermediate-risk factors: positive anti-small ubiquitin-like modifier activating enzyme (SAE1) antibodies and male sex.

The aggregation of these factors mandated the comprehensive malignancy screening protocol he underwent, which included CT imaging of the chest and abdomen, tumor marker testing, a PET scan, and plans for upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy. This case underscores the necessity of applying IMACS stratification tools in all patients with idiopathic inflammatory myopathy, as the presence of specific features like dysphagia and certain autoantibodies significantly elevates the pre-test probability of an underlying occult malignancy, necessitating rigorous baseline and interval surveillance to exclude paraneoplastic associations.
