Title: Blog about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

# Semaglutide Myopathy: The Next Safety Signal in the GLP-1 Boom?

## The GLP-1 Revolution and a New Safety Signal

We are living in the age of the GLP-1 receptor agonist. From semaglutide to tirzepatide, these drugs have transformed the landscape of obesity and type 2 diabetes management. We know the common side effects by heart: nausea, vomiting, gastroparesis, and the occasional gallbladder issue. We are also becoming increasingly aware of "sarcopenia" or muscle loss as a metabolic consequence of rapid weight loss. However, a recent case report challenges us to look beyond simple toxicity and consider a rare, aggressive autoimmune phenomenon: immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM).

This is the first reported case of IMNM probably associated with semaglutide use. It serves as a critical reminder that as we prescribe these medications more frequently, we must remain vigilant for novel adverse events that mimic common conditions but require vastly different management.

## The Case: A Slow Burn After a Single Dose

The patient was a female in her 60s with a history of obesity and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). She had been evaluated for fatty liver for years and was finally initiated on semaglutide. Here is where the timeline gets interesting. Within hours of her *single* 0.25 mg subcutaneous dose, she developed severe nausea and vomiting, leading to a hospitalization for acute renal failure and metabolic acidosis. At that initial admission, her creatine kinase (CK) was mildly elevated at 337 U/L. She was treated with hydration, recovered, and was discharged.

However, the story didn't end there. Over the subsequent four months, she developed progressive proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, and dysphonia. She deteriorated to the point where she could not stand or sit upright without assistance. When she presented to a tertiary center, her CK had skyrocketed to 3992 U/L. There was no rash, no skin changes, and no history of statin use. She had not taken any other doses of semaglutide.

## Distinguishing IMNM from Rhabdomyolysis

The core clinical challenge in this case was differentiating between direct drug toxicity (rhabdomyolysis) and an immune-mediated process. We recently saw a case report of tirzepatide-associated rhabdomyolysis where the patient had a massive CK elevation that resolved within days of stopping the drug and hydration. That is the classic toxic myopathy picture.

This patient presented differently. Her CK elevation was progressive over months, not acute and self-limiting. More importantly, she required immunosuppression to recover. The muscle biopsy was the clincher. It revealed dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibers with myophagocytosis (macrophages eating muscle fibers) and membrane attack complex (MAC) positivity. There was a paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates, which is the hallmark of IMNM.

While she tested negative for the classic IMNM antibodies (anti-HMGCR and anti-SRP), she had a low-titre positive SS-B antibody. It is worth noting that roughly one-third of patients with IMNM are seronegative, making the biopsy and clinical course paramount for diagnosis.

## Management and Outcome

The treatment approach mirrored that of idiopathic IMNM but with a crucial difference in the long-term outlook. She received pulse methylprednisolone (1000 mg IV daily for 5 days), followed by oral prednisone (1 mg/kg) and a course of IVIG (2 g/kg). The response was dramatic. Her CK levels normalized within two weeks, and her functional status improved steadily.

Because this was likely drug-induced, she did not require the prolonged, heavy immunosuppression often needed for idiopathic IMNM. She was successfully weaned off prednisone within two months and was off all immunosuppression for eight months by her last follow-up. She regained her baseline functional status, and her cardiac findings (which initially suggested possible myocarditis) also normalized.

## Clinical Reasoning and Pharmacovigilance

Why does this matter for the practicing clinician? As GLP-1 usage explodes, we are likely to see more rare adverse events. The temporal relationship here was strong: one dose, followed by a delayed but progressive decline. The causality was deemed "probable" based on WHO-Uppsala criteria due to the time relationship, the exclusion of other causes (like statins or connective tissue disease), and the response to withdrawal and treatment.

However, we must remain cautious. The patient had a pre-existing positive SS-B antibody and a history of liver disease, which complicates the picture. We cannot definitively rule out a smouldering autoimmune process that was unmasked by the drug. Nevertheless, the 12-fold rise in CK and the specific histology support a medication-related etiology. If you see a patient on a GLP-1 agonist presenting with progressive weakness and rising CK, do not dismiss it as simple rhabdomyolysis. Consider the autoimmune spectrum.

## Key Take-Home Points

*   **Differentiate Toxicity from Autoimmunity:** Direct drug-induced rhabdomyolysis typically resolves with hydration and drug cessation. Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) progresses over weeks to months and requires immunosuppression.
*   **Know the Biopsy:** IMNM is characterized by necrotizing myofibers with macrophage infiltration and membrane attack complex deposition, often with a paucity of lymphocytes.
*   **Serology Can Be Negative:** Approximately one-third of IMNM cases are seronegative for anti-HMGCR and anti-SRP antibodies; do not rule out the diagnosis based on negative serology alone.
*   **Monitor for Delayed Onset:** Adverse reactions to GLP-1 agonists may not be immediate. Progressive weakness appearing weeks to months after initiation warrants investigation.
*   **Drug-Induced IMNM May Be Treatable:** Unlike idiopathic IMNM, cases triggered by a medication may respond to a short course of immunosuppression and allow for rapid weaning once the offending agent is avoided.
