Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] How does the clinical course of creatine kinase (CK) elevation and muscle weakness differentiate immune-mediated necrotising myopathy from direct drug-induced rhabdomyolysis?

The critical distinction lies in the temporal trajectory of muscle injury relative to drug exposure and the response to withdrawal. In direct drug-induced rhabdomyolysis, muscle necrosis is driven by active toxicity; consequently, CK elevation and weakness typically peak during active treatment and resolve rapidly—often within days—upon discontinuation and supportive care. In contrast, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) involves a self-sustaining autoimmune process that persists and progresses even after the offending agent is removed.

In this case, the patient received only a single dose of semaglutide yet developed progressive proximal weakness and a 12-fold increase in CK over four months, rising from 337 U/L to a peak of 3992 U/L despite cessation of the drug. This delayed, progressive course contrasts sharply with the tirzepatide-associated rhabdomyolysis described in the background, where CK levels normalized within days of stopping the medication without immunosuppression. Furthermore, our patient’s clinical improvement required corticosteroids and IVIG, whereas direct toxicity typically resolves with hydration alone. Thus, a progressive clinical course with rising CK levels after drug discontinuation, coupled with a response to immunosuppression, strongly favors IMNM over direct toxic rhabdomyolysis.
