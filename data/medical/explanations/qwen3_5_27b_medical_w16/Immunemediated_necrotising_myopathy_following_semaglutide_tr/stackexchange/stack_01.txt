Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What specific histological features on muscle biopsy distinguish immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) from polymyositis, and how do these findings support the diagnosis in this case?

The critical histological distinction lies in the predominance of muscle fiber necrosis with macrophage-mediated clearance, rather than the endomysial T-cell invasion characteristic of polymyositis. As noted in the background of this report, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) is defined by necrotising myofibres and a paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates.

In this patient, the muscle biopsy confirmed this phenotype. The specimen demonstrated dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibres with prominent CD68 staining, indicating active myophagocytosis by macrophages. Crucially, immunohistochemistry revealed only rare CD3-positive T-cells, consistent with the required paucity of lymphocytes. Additionally, the presence of membrane attack complex (MAC) deposition on necrotising and non-necrotising myofibres further supported an immune-mediated mechanism.

These findings were essential for diagnosis in this case because the patient was seronegative for the classic IMNM autoantibodies (anti-HMGCR and anti-SRP). The biopsy phenotype of necrosis with macrophage dominance and minimal T-cell infiltration distinguished this from polymyositis, which typically presents with significant endomysial inflammation. This histological confirmation justified the initiation of immunosuppression with corticosteroids and IVIG, to which the patient responded, rather than treating it as a purely toxic myopathy or inflammatory myopathy with different histological drivers.
