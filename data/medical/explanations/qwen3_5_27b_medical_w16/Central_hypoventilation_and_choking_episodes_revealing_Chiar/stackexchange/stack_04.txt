Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Management] What is the definitive treatment for symptomatic Chiari malformation type I with evidence of brainstem compression, and how does it address the respiratory symptoms?

The definitive treatment for symptomatic Chiari malformation type I with evidence of brainstem compression is posterior fossa decompression. In this case, the patient presented with 11 mm of cerebellar tonsillar descent causing recurrent choking, nocturnal oxygen desaturation to 70%–80%, and predominant central sleep apnea. While the patient initially opted for conservative management, posterior fossa decompression was recommended as the intervention capable of reversing these symptoms.

The procedure addresses the respiratory pathology by relieving brainstem compression and restoring normal cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) flow dynamics. This decompression alleviates the disruption of automatic respiratory control, which is often compromised while voluntary breathing remains intact, thereby potentially resolving the acquired central hypoventilation syndrome. Clinicians must recognize that persistent unexplained respiratory dysfunction in Chiari malformation warrants consideration of decompression to prevent life-threatening hypoxemia, distinguishing this structural brainstem pathology from primary pulmonary or neuromuscular causes.
