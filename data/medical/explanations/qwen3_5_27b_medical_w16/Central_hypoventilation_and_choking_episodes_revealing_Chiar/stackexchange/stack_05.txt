Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Morning Report] What is the primary methodological limitation of a Type III sleep study that may lead to underestimation of the Apnea–Hypopnea Index in patients with suspected central hypoventilation?

The primary methodological limitation is the absence of electroencephalogram (EEG) monitoring, which prevents the accurate determination of total sleep time. In a Type III sleep study, the Apnea–Hypopnea Index (AHI) is calculated using total recording time rather than true sleep time. Because recording time includes periods of wakefulness, the denominator is artificially inflated, leading to an underestimation of the respiratory event frequency.

In this case, the patient exhibited severe clinical symptoms, including recurrent nocturnal choking and documented oxygen desaturations to 70%–80%, yet the Type III study reported a mild AHI of 6.0. The discussion explicitly attributes this discrepancy to the lack of EEG data, noting that without the ability to distinguish wakefulness from sleep, the reported AHI likely underestimates the true severity of sleep-disordered breathing. This finding underscores that in patients with suspected central hypoventilation and significant desaturation, portable monitoring may yield false reassurance; confirmation with Type I polysomnography, which includes full EEG monitoring to accurately quantify sleep time, is necessary to avoid underdiagnosis.
