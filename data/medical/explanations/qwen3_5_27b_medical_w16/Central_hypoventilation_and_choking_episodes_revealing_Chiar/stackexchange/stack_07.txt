Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Pathophysiology] What pathophysiological mechanism accounts for the lack of conscious sensation of breathlessness during severe hypoxemia in patients with brainstem compression?

The lack of conscious breathlessness during severe hypoxemia in this setting is attributed to the disruption of afferent neural pathways ascending from the brainstem to the cortex.

In this patient, the Chiari malformation type I caused significant brainstem compression (11 mm tonsillar descent). As described in the discussion, this pathology likely interrupts the critical afferent signals required to produce the conscious sensation of dyspnea. Breathlessness is a form of interoception that depends on the processing of these signals by key cortical regions, specifically the anterior insular cortex. When brainstem compression disrupts these signals during their transit, the cortex fails to receive the necessary input to generate the feeling of air hunger.

This mechanism explains the alarming clinical finding in this case: the patient experienced oxygen desaturations to 70%–80% during episodes at home and at the airport without any associated respiratory distress or awareness. While voluntary breathing pathways remained intact, the failure of automatic respiratory control combined with the interruption of sensory afferent pathways resulted in a dangerous lack of awareness regarding her physiological distress. This underscores the importance of recognizing brainstem compression in patients with unexplained hypoxemia, even in the absence of subjective dyspnea.
