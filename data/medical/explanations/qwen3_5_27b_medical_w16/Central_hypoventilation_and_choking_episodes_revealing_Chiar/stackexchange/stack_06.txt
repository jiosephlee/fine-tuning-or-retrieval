Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Clinical Pearls] What diagnostic lesson is illustrated by the retrospective review of historical neuroimaging in patients with chronic, unexplained neurological symptoms?

The diagnostic lesson illustrated by this case is that retrospective review of historical neuroimaging is a critical, high-yield strategy for patients with chronic, unexplained neurological or respiratory symptoms, as initial interpretations may overlook subtle structural abnormalities. In this patient, a non-contrast CT scan of the head obtained 14 years prior to presentation was originally reported as normal; however, a detailed re-review revealed a crowded foramen magnum and loss of cerebrospinal fluid space at the craniocervical junction. This previously missed finding prompted targeted MRI, which confirmed Chiari malformation type I (CMI) with 11 mm cerebellar tonsillar descent. This diagnosis unified her complex clinical picture, including 14 years of occipital headaches, bulbar dysfunction manifesting as recurrent choking, and acquired central hypoventilation syndrome mimicking Ondine’s curse.

The case underscores that structural brainstem pathology can present with atypical respiratory or swallowing symptoms that often lead to extensive, negative workups in gastroenterology and pulmonology. Clinicians must recognize that posterior fossa anomalies may be present long before symptom onset or may be misinterpreted in the context of unrelated trauma. Therefore, when evaluating patients with chronic neurological dysfunction or unexplained respiratory compromise, actively retrieving and re-evaluating prior imaging studies is essential to identify reversible structural causes that may have been initially overlooked.
