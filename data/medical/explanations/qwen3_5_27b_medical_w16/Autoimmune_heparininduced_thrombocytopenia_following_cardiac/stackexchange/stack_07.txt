Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] What is the role of high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) in the management of autoimmune HIT, and how did this patient's platelet response compare to the typical expectation?

High-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is utilized in autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (aHIT) as an adjunct to anticoagulation to neutralize pathogenic antibodies and accelerate platelet recovery. In typical cases of aHIT, we expect a brisk correction of thrombocytopenia following IVIG administration. In this patient, however, the response was markedly attenuated. Despite receiving 100 g of IVIG on three consecutive days, his platelet count increased only modestly from a nadir of 13×10⁹/L to 54×10⁹/L over seven days, a rise significantly lower than the usual expectation for aHIT.

This blunted response aligned with his unique serological profile, which demonstrated partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade in vitro, indicating an unusually severe form of the disease. Consequently, despite IVIG therapy, persisting hypercoagulability necessitated a switch from argatroban to fondaparinux. This case illustrates that while IVIG is standard in aHIT, clinical response varies, and severe phenotypes may require intensified anticoagulant management despite immunomodulatory therapy.
