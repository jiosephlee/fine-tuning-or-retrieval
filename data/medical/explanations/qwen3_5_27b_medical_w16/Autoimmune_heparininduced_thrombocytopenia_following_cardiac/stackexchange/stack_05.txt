Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the clinical significance of a strongly positive PF4/polyanion ELISA paired with an 'indeterminate' or atypical serotonin-release assay result in a patient with suspected HIT?

This laboratory profile is highly suggestive of autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (aHIT), a severe variant of HIT that requires aggressive management. In this case, the "indeterminate" serotonin-release assay (SRA) result did not rule out HIT; rather, it revealed the specific pathophysiology of aHIT.

While the strongly positive PF4/polyanion ELISA indicated the presence of antibodies, the SRA demonstrated heparin-independent platelet activation. Specifically, the patient’s serum caused strong serotonin release in the absence of heparin (0 U/mL) and showed partial resistance to inhibition by Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibodies. This atypical serological profile distinguishes aHIT from classic heparin-dependent HIT and correlates with a more severe clinical course, as evidenced by this patient’s profound thrombocytopenia (nadir 13×10⁹/L) and thrombotic stroke.

Therefore, when a strongly positive ELISA is paired with an indeterminate SRA in a patient with clinical features of HIT, do not dismiss the diagnosis. Instead, consider aHIT, which may present with heparin-independent activation and requires prompt treatment with non-heparin anticoagulants, such as argatroban or fondaparinux, as demonstrated in this patient's successful recovery.
