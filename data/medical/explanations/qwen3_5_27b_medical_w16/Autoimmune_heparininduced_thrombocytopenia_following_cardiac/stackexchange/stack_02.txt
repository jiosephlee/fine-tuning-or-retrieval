Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] How does the serotonin-release assay (SRA) profile of autoimmune HIT (aHIT) differ from classic HIT regarding platelet activation in the absence of added heparin?

The critical distinction lies in the requirement of heparin to trigger platelet activation. In classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), the serotonin-release assay (SRA) demonstrates platelet activation only when heparin is present to facilitate the formation of immune complexes between PF4, heparin, and IgG antibodies. Conversely, in autoimmune HIT (aHIT), the antibodies possess heparin-independent platelet-activating properties.

In this case, the patient’s SRA profile revealed strong serotonin release (96%) in the complete absence of unfractionated heparin (0 U/mL), which is significantly above the reference range of less than 20%. The report explicitly notes that this finding is a characteristic feature of aHIT but is not observed in classic heparin-dependent HIT. While both conditions typically show a lack of activation at supratherapeutic heparin concentrations (100 U/mL) due to the inability to form immune complexes, the presence of significant serotonin release without added heparin confirms the diagnosis of aHIT. This heparin-independent activation explains the patient’s persistent thrombocytopenia and thrombotic risk despite the cessation of heparin exposure.
