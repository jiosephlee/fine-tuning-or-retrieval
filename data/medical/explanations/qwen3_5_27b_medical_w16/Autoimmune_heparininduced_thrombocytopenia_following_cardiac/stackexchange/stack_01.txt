Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] In the context of cardiac surgery, what specific exposure history explains the development of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) symptoms prior to the typical postoperative day 5–10 window?

In the context of cardiac surgery, the development of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) symptoms prior to the typical postoperative day 5–10 window is explained by **recent preoperative heparin exposure**.

While classic HIT typically manifests between postoperative days 5 and 10, approximately 10% of patients with post-cardiac surgery HIT present with a platelet count fall before day 5. As illustrated in this case, this early onset is linked to prior sensitization from heparin administered during a preoperative procedure. Specifically, this patient underwent diagnostic heart catheterization one week prior to his surgery, during which he received 2,000 units of unfractionated heparin (UFH). This initial exposure primed the immune system to form antibodies against the heparin–platelet factor 4 complex. Consequently, upon re-exposure to heparin during cardiopulmonary bypass or subsequent postoperative thromboprophylaxis, the patient mounted a rapid anamnestic antibody response, precipitating thrombocytopenia and thrombosis earlier than the conventional timeline.

Clinicians must maintain a high index of suspicion for HIT even in the early postoperative period if there is a history of recent heparin exposure, such as a catheterization or prior surgery within the preceding month. In this case, recognizing the preoperative catheterization as the sensitizing event was critical to diagnosing autoimmune HIT (aHIT) and initiating appropriate non-heparin anticoagulation.
