Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Morning Report] How does the serological profile of HIT antibodies typically evolve during the recovery phase of autoimmune HIT, as evidenced by the patient's follow-up testing?

In autoimmune HIT (aHIT), the serological profile typically evolves from heparin-independent platelet activation toward a heparin-dependent pattern as the disease resolves. In this patient, the initial serotonin-release assay (SRA) demonstrated strong platelet activation in the absence of heparin (96% release at 0 U/mL), confirming the autoimmune nature of the antibodies. However, follow-up testing at postoperative day 124, after platelet count recovery, revealed a distinct shift: serotonin release was minimal without heparin (15%) but robust with heparin (100% at 0.3 U/mL). 

This transition reflects the waning of the specific heparin-independent aHIT antibodies, leaving a residual profile characteristic of classic heparin-dependent HIT. Therefore, persistent antibody positivity months after recovery does not necessarily indicate active disease; rather, the evolution from heparin-independent to heparin-dependent reactivity signifies the resolution of the autoimmune phenotype. Clinicians must interpret follow-up serology in the context of this potential phenotypic shift to avoid misdiagnosing residual antibodies as active thrombocytopenia.
