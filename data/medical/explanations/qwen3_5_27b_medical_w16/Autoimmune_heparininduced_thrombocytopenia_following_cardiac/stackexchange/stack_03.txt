Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Which initial laboratory findings in this case helped exclude thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) as the primary cause of thrombocytopenia despite the later appearance of schistocytes?

The key initial laboratory findings that helped exclude thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) were the elevated haptoglobin level and the absence of schistocytes on the initial peripheral blood film. TTP is a thrombotic microangiopathy characterized by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia; therefore, one would typically expect low haptoglobin due to hemoglobin binding and the presence of circulating red cell fragments early in the clinical course.

In this case, the patient’s haptoglobin was elevated at 3.19 g/L (reference range 0.32–1.97 g/L), consistent with the absence of overt hemolysis, and the review of the postoperative day 7 peripheral blood film confirmed thrombocytopenia without red cell fragments. Although schistocytes did appear later (postoperative days 9 to 16), the case report attributes this to severe, progressive HIT-associated consumptive coagulopathy rather than primary TTP. Recognizing the initial lack of hemolytic markers was essential to prioritize heparin-induced thrombocytopenia over TTP in the differential diagnosis.
