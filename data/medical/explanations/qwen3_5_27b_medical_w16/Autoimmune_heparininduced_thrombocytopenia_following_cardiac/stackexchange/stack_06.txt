Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] What unique serological feature regarding Fc receptor blockade was observed in the serotonin-release assay of this patient, and what does it suggest about the reactivity of their HIT antibodies?

In this case, the unique serological feature observed in the serotonin-release assay (SRA) was the **partial resistance of platelet activation to Fc receptor blockade**.

Typically, in classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), the addition of the Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody (IV.3) completely inhibits antibody-mediated platelet activation, reducing serotonin release to less than 20%. However, in this patient, the addition of IV.3 failed to fully inhibit the reaction; **76% serotonin release persisted** at 0.1 U/mL UFH in the presence of the blocking antibody.

This atypical profile suggests that the patient’s HIT antibodies were **unusually reactive** and possessed heparin-independent platelet-activating properties, consistent with the diagnosis of autoimmune HIT (aHIT). While classic HIT relies on immune complex formation requiring heparin and subsequent Fc receptor clustering, this patient’s antibodies activated platelets even in the absence of heparin (96% release at 0 U/mL) and demonstrated a serological phenotype where the Fc receptor pathway could not be fully blocked by standard monoclonal intervention. Recognizing this partial resistance is critical, as it distinguishes this severe form of aHIT from classic HIT and may explain the profound thrombocytopenia and resistance to standard therapies observed in this patient.
