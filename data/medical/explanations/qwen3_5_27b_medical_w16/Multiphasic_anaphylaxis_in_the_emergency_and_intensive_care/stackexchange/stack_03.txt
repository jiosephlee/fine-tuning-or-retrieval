Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] Based on current systematic reviews and guideline updates, what is the evidence regarding the efficacy of corticosteroids in preventing biphasic or multiphasic anaphylactic reactions?

Based on the evidence summarized in this case report, current data do not support the efficacy of corticosteroids in preventing biphasic or multiphasic anaphylactic reactions. While our patient received intravenous corticosteroids following her first recurrence of anaphylaxis due to the traditional role of steroids in preventing biphasic courses, the discussion highlights a critical divergence between historical practice and contemporary evidence.

A 2017 systematic review cited in the report examined 22 studies, of which 21 showed no evidence that corticosteroids reduce the risk of biphasic anaphylaxis. Furthermore, recent studies emphasize potential harm; a 2019 study noted an increased likelihood of ICU or hospital ward admission following prehospital corticosteroid treatment. Consequently, most contemporary guidelines now advise against routine corticosteroid administration for anaphylaxis, despite the persistence of this practice in clinical settings as illustrated by our patient’s management. The focus of prevention should remain on prompt epinephrine administration and appropriate observation periods rather than adjunctive steroid therapy.
