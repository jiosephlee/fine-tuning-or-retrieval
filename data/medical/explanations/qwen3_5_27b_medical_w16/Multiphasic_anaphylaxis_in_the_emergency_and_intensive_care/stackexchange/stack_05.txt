Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient with recurrent anaphylaxis and elevated baseline tryptase, what are the WHO diagnostic criteria for systemic mastocytosis, and which findings in this case suggested the diagnosis despite not meeting formal requirements?

Formal diagnosis of systemic mastocytosis requires meeting specific World Health Organization (WHO) criteria, which are categorized into major and minor findings. As noted in this case report, a formal diagnosis was not established because the major criteria were not assessable due to limited material obtained during the bone marrow biopsy, and the patient met only two of the WHO minor criteria.

Despite failing to meet the strict diagnostic threshold, several findings in this patient strongly suggested the diagnosis. Clinically, the patient presented with multiphasic anaphylaxis, characterized by four distinct episodes of anaphylactic shock requiring repeated epinephrine administration despite initial stabilization. Biochemically, tryptase levels were elevated during the third anaphylactic episode and remained elevated at baseline during follow-up. Additional supportive findings included hepatomegaly identified on abdominal sonography and possible neuropsychiatric symptoms, including depression and memory problems.

The underlying pathophysiology of systemic mastocytosis involves an abnormal increase in mast cells and excessive mediator release, which predisposes patients to severe and recurrent anaphylaxis. In this case, the combination of multiphasic anaphylaxis, elevated baseline tryptase, and organ involvement created a high probability of systemic mastocytosis, underscoring that clinical suspicion should remain high even when formal WHO criteria are not fully met due to sampling limitations.
