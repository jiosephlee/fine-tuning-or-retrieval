Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] What is the generally recommended minimum observation period following an episode of anaphylaxis to monitor for biphasic reactions, and how should risk stratification influence this duration?

The traditional recommendation for observation following anaphylaxis is 4 to 6 hours after symptom resolution; however, this window may be insufficient for high-risk patients, as biphasic reactions can occur up to 78 hours later, with approximately half occurring within 6 to 12 hours. While the 2021 UK guideline proposes a three-tier risk-stratified approach to monitoring, the case report highlights that reliable predictors for targeted monitoring are not yet available.

In this case, the patient presented with severe initial anaphylactic shock requiring multiple epinephrine doses—a known risk factor for biphasic or multiphasic courses. Despite initial stabilization, she experienced recurrent episodes within the first 12 hours of admission, necessitating ICU transfer and extended monitoring. She was only transferred to a standard ward after 24 hours without renewed symptoms. This underscores that while standard observation periods range from 4 to 6 hours, patients with severe presentations, delayed epinephrine administration, or multiple epinephrine doses require prolonged observation, potentially up to 24 hours, to mitigate the risk of relapse. Current evidence does not support the use of corticosteroids to prevent biphasic reactions, making adequate observation the primary safety strategy. Ultimately, clinicians must balance healthcare resources with patient safety, recognizing that complex courses like multiphasic anaphylaxis defy standard protocols and demand individualized, extended monitoring.
