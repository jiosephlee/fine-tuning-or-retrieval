Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the recommended protocol for serum tryptase sampling to confirm anaphylaxis and assess for underlying mast cell disorders, including the specific timing relative to symptom onset and resolution?

To confirm anaphylaxis and assess for underlying mast cell disorders, current guidelines recommend obtaining a minimum of two, ideally three, serum tryptase samples at specific intervals. An initial sample should be drawn as soon as feasible during the acute reaction without delaying treatment. This is followed by a second sample 1 to 2 hours, but within 4 hours, of symptom onset, which corresponds to when tryptase levels usually peak. Finally, a third sample must be obtained at least 24 hours after full clinical resolution to establish the patient's baseline level.

In the case presented, this recommended protocol was not adhered to during the initial workup. Tryptase levels were not determined during the first anaphylactic episodes; rather, the first sample was taken one hour after the third anaphylactic episode, and the second was drawn 27 hours after the last recurrence. Despite this suboptimal timing, the results revealed elevated tryptase levels during the episode and increased levels that remained elevated during follow-up. These findings supported a high likelihood of systemic mastocytosis, although the patient did not meet formal diagnostic criteria. This case highlights that while delayed sampling can still yield diagnostic clues, strict adherence to the 1- to 2-hour peak window and the 24-hour post-resolution baseline is critical for accurately confirming anaphylaxis and distinguishing it from underlying mast cell pathology.
