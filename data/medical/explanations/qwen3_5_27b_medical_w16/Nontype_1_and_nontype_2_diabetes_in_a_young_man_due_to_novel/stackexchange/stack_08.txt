Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] What are the limitations of using the MODY probability calculator developed by the Exeter group when evaluating patients from non-White European populations?

The primary limitation of the Exeter MODY probability calculator is its lack of generalizability across diverse ethnicities. As explicitly highlighted in the discussion of this case, the algorithm was derived from data specific to White European populations. When applied to patients from non-White groups, the tool may overestimate the probability of MODY.

In this specific case, the patient’s clinical data yielded a calculated probability of 62.4%; however, the authors caution that this score must be interpreted with care outside the original demographic. Consequently, reliance on the calculator alone is insufficient for diverse populations. Clinical judgment remains paramount. In patients presenting with early-onset diabetes, a strong family history of early-onset disease, and negative islet autoantibodies—as seen in this patient—genetic testing should be pursued regardless of the calculator’s output. This ensures accurate diagnosis and facilitates precision medicine, such as the appropriate transition from insulin to GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy demonstrated in this case.
