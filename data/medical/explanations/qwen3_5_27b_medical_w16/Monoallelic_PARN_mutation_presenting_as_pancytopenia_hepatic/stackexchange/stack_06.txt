Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Management] What is the primary approach to treatment for telomere biology disorders, and under what specific clinical circumstances is haematopoietic stem cell transplantation considered?

The primary approach to treatment for telomere biology disorders (TBDs) is supportive care, tailored to symptom severity and specific organ involvement. In this case, management focused on correcting severe vitamin B12 and folate deficiencies, providing transfusion support for symptomatic anaemia, and implementing surveillance for hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis.

Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is considered a potentially curative intervention, but it is specifically indicated for patients with progressive haematologic manifestations, such as bone marrow failure. Although our patient presented with pancytopenia, her condition was managed supportively because the cytopenia was attributed to nutritional deficiency rather than primary marrow failure at that stage. However, the discussion highlights that HSCT should be evaluated in progressive cases with haematologic involvement, as telomere shortening compromises the replicative potential of haematopoietic stem cells. Thus, while supportive care remains the mainstay, early recognition of TBD is crucial to guide genetic counselling and timely consideration of transplantation in those with evolving marrow failure.
