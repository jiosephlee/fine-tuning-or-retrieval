Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Management] Given the diagnosis of a telomere biology disorder, which specific malignancies and organ systems require lifelong surveillance due to increased risk?

The diagnosis of a telomere biology disorder (TBD) necessitates lifelong surveillance for haematological and solid malignancies, as well as close monitoring of the bone marrow, liver, and pulmonary systems.

This management strategy is grounded in the pathophysiology of TBDs, which are caused by Mendelian defects leading to abnormally short or dysfunctional telomeres. As described in the background of this case, actively dividing cells are predominantly affected, including bone marrow, skin, mucosa, hair, and hepatocytes. Premature telomere attrition in these tissues triggers cellular senescence or apoptosis, predisposing patients to organ failure and neoplastic transformation.

In our case, the patient presented with a constellation of findings reflecting this multisystem vulnerability: pancytopenia indicating bone marrow involvement, hepatic fibrosis progressing to cirrhosis, and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. The discussion section explicitly notes that recognised manifestations of TBD include an increased risk of haematological and solid malignancies. Consequently, the patient’s follow-up plan was tailored to monitor the progression of bone marrow or systemic involvement, alongside regular surveillance of hepatic and pulmonary function.

For the clinician, the takeaway is that early recognition of TBD—particularly in young adults with unexplained multisystem disease—is crucial not only for genetic counselling but for instituting this specific, lifelong surveillance protocol to detect malignancy and organ failure early.
