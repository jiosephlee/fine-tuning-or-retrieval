Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] Beyond next-generation sequencing, what is the gold standard functional assay used to quantify telomere length and support the diagnosis of a telomere biology disorder?

The gold standard functional assay used to quantify telomere length, complementing next-generation sequencing, is flow cytometry-based fluorescence in situ hybridisation (Flow-FISH).

As detailed in the discussion of this case, telomere biology disorders (TBDs) arise from Mendelian defects in telomere-related genes that lead to abnormally short or dysfunctional telomeres. Under physiological conditions, telomerase maintains telomere length; however, pathogenic variants impair this function, causing premature telomere attrition. This shortening triggers cellular senescence or apoptosis, predominantly affecting actively dividing cells such as those in the bone marrow, skin, mucosa, hair, and hepatocytes.

In this patient, the clinical presentation of pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis, and pulmonary fibrosis in a young adult raised suspicion for a TBD despite the absence of the classical mucocutaneous triad of dyskeratosis congenita. While clinical exome sequencing identified a monoallelic *PARN* variant classified as a Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS), the discussion explicitly notes that the gold standard for diagnosing suspected TBD includes both genetic testing and Flow-FISH assays to measure telomere length. This functional assay is critical for quantifying telomere attrition and supporting the diagnosis, particularly when genetic findings are ambiguous or when the phenotype involves multisystem fibrosis and marrow failure consistent with the spectrum of TBD.
