Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the fundamental cellular mechanism linking pathogenic variants in telomere-related genes to the premature ageing and organ failure observed in telomere biology disorders?

The fundamental mechanism is **premature telomere attrition leading to replicative senescence in high-turnover tissues.**

Telomeres preserve genomic integrity by shielding chromosome ends from degradation. Under physiological conditions, the enzyme telomerase maintains telomere length through elongation. However, pathogenic variants in telomere-related genes impair this function. In this case, the *PARN* gene encodes an exoribonuclease required for the 3-prime-end maturation of the telomerase RNA component (TERC). The patient’s monoallelic *PARN* variant disrupts this maturation, compromising telomerase activity and leading to abnormally short or dysfunctional telomeres.

As cells divide, telomeres shorten until they reach a critical length that triggers cellular senescence or apoptosis. This loss of replicative capacity predominantly affects actively dividing cell groups, such as bone marrow, skin, mucosa, hair, and hepatocytes. Consequently, the patient presented with pancytopenia, hepatic and pulmonary fibrosis, and premature greying. The multisystem organ failure observed in telomere biology disorders, therefore, results from the inability of high-turnover tissues to sustain replication due to genetically driven telomere shortening.
