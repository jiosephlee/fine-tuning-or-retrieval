Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Pathophysiology] How does the clinical phenotype and inheritance pattern of monoallelic PARN variants typically differ from biallelic PARN variants in the spectrum of telomere biology disorders?

In the spectrum of telomere biology disorders (TBDs), the clinical phenotype and inheritance pattern associated with *PARN* variants are critically dependent on zygosity. As outlined in our case report, biallelic *PARN* variants typically follow an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern and are associated with Dyskeratosis Congenita, autosomal recessive 6 (DKCB6), or severe childhood-onset syndromes such as Hoyeraal-Hreidarsson (HH) syndrome. In contrast, monoallelic *PARN* variants are more often implicated in telomere-related pulmonary fibrosis and/or bone marrow failure (PFBMFT4), frequently presenting in adults with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) rather than primary hematological manifestations.

Pathophysiologically, *PARN* encodes an exoribonuclease required for the 3-prime-end maturation of the telomerase RNA component (TERC) by removing post-transcriptionally acquired oligo(A) tails. Loss of function leads to premature telomere attrition, affecting actively dividing cells such as those in the bone marrow, liver, and lungs. While the typical monoallelic phenotype is dominated by pulmonary fibrosis, our patient illustrates the expanding spectrum of *PARN*-associated TBDs. Despite harboring only a monoallelic missense variant, she presented with a multisystem phenotype including pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis, and pulmonary fibrosis. This underscores that while zygosity generally dictates the clinical syndrome, variable penetrance and environmental factors can result in overlapping features, necessitating a high index of suspicion for TBDs in young adults with unexplained multisystem fibrosis and cytopenia.
