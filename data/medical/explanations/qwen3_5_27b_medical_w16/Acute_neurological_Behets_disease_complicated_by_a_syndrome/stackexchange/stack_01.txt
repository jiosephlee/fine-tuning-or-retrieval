Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Based on the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), which specific clinical features contributed to this patient's score of five points, and what is the threshold for a definite diagnosis?

The International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD) utilize a weighted scoring system to standardize diagnosis, where a total score of 4 points or greater indicates definite disease. In this case, the patient achieved a score of 5 points based on three specific clinical features: oral aphthae (2 points), genital aphthosis (2 points), and a folliculitis-like rash (1 point).

As highlighted in the case report, genital aphthosis is considered a particularly specific finding for Behçet’s disease, which strongly supported the diagnosis in this patient. It is important to note that while the patient tested positive for HLA-B51, this genetic marker did not contribute to the ICBD score, as the criteria rely primarily on clinical manifestations. The accumulation of these points allowed for a definitive diagnosis of Behçet’s disease, which was crucial for identifying the concurrent acute neuro-Behçet’s disease and the associated syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion.
