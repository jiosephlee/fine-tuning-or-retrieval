Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 3: ## Strategic Decision-Making and Risk Stratification

The decision to proceed with a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) rather than surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) was driven by a rigorous risk stratification process conducted by the multidisciplinary Heart Team. This patient presented with a European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation (EuroSCORE II) of 27.36%, indicating prohibitive surgical risk due to significant comorbidities including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, type 2 diabetes, and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding. Anatomical considerations further complicated the surgical option; the two previously implanted transcatheter valves had extensively integrated into the aortic wall, meaning that open surgical explantation would likely have necessitated a total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement. Consequently, the Heart Team consensus prioritized a transcatheter strategy to avoid the physiological stress of sternotomy and cardiopulmonary bypass, accepting the technical complexity of a triple-stented configuration in exchange for a significantly reduced procedural mortality risk.

A critical component of this decision-making process involved determining the appropriate timing for reintervention following the patient’s episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). While the 2025 European Society of Cardiology guidelines generally recommend surgical valve replacement for PVE, particularly when complicated by heart failure or uncontrolled infection, management in this case required individualisation based on infection control status. The team deferred the third TAVI until after the patient had completed a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy for *Enterococcus faecalis*, which included six weeks of intravenous ampicillin followed by oral treatment. Preprocedural evaluation confirmed the resolution of infection through negative blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT, ensuring no active vegetations or paravalvular complications were present. This clearance was essential to prevent seeding the new prosthesis and to align the procedural risk profile with that of a patient without active infection, allowing the team to focus on anatomical feasibility rather than infectious control.

Once the decision for transcatheter intervention was made, the focus shifted to mitigating specific technical risks associated with a triple-stented anatomy, particularly coronary obstruction and patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). Multislice CT was utilised to assess the minimum lumen diameter of the innermost prosthesis, which measured 13.7 mm, and to evaluate the distance between the valve frames and the coronary ostia. To mitigate the risk of coronary obstruction, the procedural strategy emphasised high implantation of the third valve, positioning the device at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets to preserve coronary flow and prevent the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia. Additionally, to address the inevitable reduction in effective orifice area caused by layering three prostheses, the team selected the Acurate neo2 valve. This self-expanding bioprosthesis was chosen for its supra-annular leaflet positioning and relatively low frame height, features designed to maximise the residual effective orifice area in narrow anatomies. Commissural alignment was further employed during deployment to optimise haemodynamics and preserve future coronary access, acknowledging that while the initial post-procedural gradients were high, optimised expansion and alignment could improve long-term functional outcomes through partial mechanical expansion of the prior frames.
