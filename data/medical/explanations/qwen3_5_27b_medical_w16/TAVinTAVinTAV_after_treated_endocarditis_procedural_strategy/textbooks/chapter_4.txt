Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 4: ## Procedural Technique and Technical Nuances

The successful execution of a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure requires meticulous technical planning and intra-procedural adaptability to navigate the complex anatomy created by multiple overlapping stent frames. In this case, the intervention was performed under local anaesthesia to minimize physiological stress on a patient with significant comorbidities, utilizing a hybrid vascular access strategy. Access was obtained via the left femoral and left radial arteries, providing the stability necessary for precise valve manipulation and the ability to manage potential complications from multiple entry points. A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the femoral access, establishing a stable rail for device delivery. This dual-access approach allows for simultaneous aortography and device deployment, which is critical for maintaining hemodynamic monitoring and ensuring real-time visualization of coronary perfusion throughout the procedure.

Before valve deployment, balloon pre-dilation was performed to facilitate the passage of the third prosthesis through the calcified and layered structures of the prior valves. A 22/40 mm balloon was inflated under rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute to reduce cardiac output and minimize the risk of embolization. Crucially, simultaneous aortography was performed during this phase to verify that coronary flow remained unobstructed. In sequential TAVI procedures, the risk of coronary artery obstruction is amplified by the cumulative height of the stent frames and the potential for leaflet prolapse. Confirming patent coronary flow prior to permanent valve deployment serves as a vital safety checkpoint, ensuring that the pre-dilation maneuver has not inadvertently compromised coronary ostia before the final prosthesis is committed to the position.

The selection and deployment of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis were guided by preprocedural CT modelling, with a specific emphasis on commissural alignment. In a triple-stented anatomy, the risk of future coronary access being blocked by the mesh of overlapping valves is significant. By aligning the commissures of the new valve with those of the existing Evolut prosthesis, the interventional team aimed to maximize the effective orifice area and reduce patient-prosthesis mismatch. More importantly, this alignment strategy was designed to preserve the pathways for future coronary interventions. The CT-guided planning allowed the operator to identify fluoroscopic landmarks that corresponded to the anatomical commissures, enabling precise rotational positioning of the self-expanding frame to maintain coronary accessibility.

A defining technical nuance in this case was the deliberate strategy of high implantation. The device was deployed at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets rather than lower in the annulus. This positioning was intended to fix the pre-existing leaflets of the Evolut valve against the stent frame of the new Acurate neo2 prosthesis. By anchoring the old leaflets outward, the team prevented inward leaflet folding, which would otherwise reduce the effective orifice area. Furthermore, high implantation minimized the formation of a "neo-skirt" below the coronary ostia. In complex redo-TAVI cases, the skirt of a new valve can extend below the coronary openings and obstruct flow or access; implanting the device higher within the aortic root mitigates this risk, ensuring that the coronary ostia remain clear of the prosthetic fabric.

Following valve deployment, post-dilation was performed using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid pacing to optimize frame expansion and leaflet mobility. This step is often necessary in TAV-in-TAV procedures to ensure the new valve fully expands within the constrained space of the prior prostheses. In this patient, the immediate post-procedural echocardiogram showed a high mean gradient of 67 mm Hg, which was attributed to the initial constraints of the triple-stented anatomy. However, the post-dilation maneuver, combined with the mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame, contributed to improved hemodynamics over time. Follow-up imaging demonstrated a reduction in the mean gradient to 27 mm Hg at one month and 26 mm Hg at one year, indicating that the procedural optimization of frame expansion and leaflet positioning successfully restored durable valve function despite the anatomical complexity.
