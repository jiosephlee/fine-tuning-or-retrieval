Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 2: ## Clinical Presentation and Comprehensive Diagnostic Workup

The clinical management of patients requiring serial transcatheter valve interventions requires a meticulous assessment of the patient's baseline risk profile and current physiological status. In this case, the patient was a man in his late 70s with a complex medical history that significantly influenced the decision-making process for the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI). His background included arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and a history of recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding. Cardiovascular history was extensive, featuring three-vessel coronary artery disease managed with multiple percutaneous coronary interventions between 2009 and 2014, and a third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating a pacemaker in 2014. Crucially, he had undergone two prior TAVI procedures: a 27 mm Lotus valve in 2014 and a 23 mm Evolut valve in 2022. Given these comorbidities and the technical complexity of operating on a triple-stented aortic root, the patient was deemed to have a prohibitive surgical risk, with a calculated EuroSCORE II of 27.36%. This high-risk profile necessitated a percutaneous strategy, but only after confirming that the patient could tolerate the procedure and that the anatomical constraints allowed for a safe reintervention.

Symptom assessment provided the clinical indication for reintervention, reflecting the progression of prosthetic valve dysfunction. Upon admission in June 2024, the patient presented with dyspnea, fatigue, dizziness, and a spastic sensation in the left side of the chest during physical exertion. His functional capacity was notably limited, with walking distances restricted to 200–300 meters before symptom onset. These symptoms are consistent with severe aortic stenosis and heart failure, but in the context of a failed bioprosthesis, they signal structural valve deterioration (SVD) or prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The clinical team correlated these symptoms with objective hemodynamic data to confirm that the patient’s decline was valve-related rather than solely due to his underlying comorbidities. The presence of worsening heart failure symptoms despite guideline-directed medical therapy further supported the need for mechanical correction of the valve obstruction.

Echocardiographic evaluation served as the primary modality for assessing the hemodynamic severity of the valve dysfunction. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed severe prosthetic valve dysfunction characterized by a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg and a markedly reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm². The AVA was calculated using the continuity equation, based on left ventricular outflow tract measurements and Doppler-derived velocity-time integrals, which is the standard method for assessing stenosis severity in prosthetic valves. Additionally, TTE identified persistent grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation and visualized two overlapping bioprosthetic valves. While TTE confirmed the hemodynamic burden, it also highlighted the limitations of ultrasound in complex multi-prosthesis anatomy, where acoustic shadowing from multiple stent frames can obscure detailed structural assessment. Consequently, echocardiography was used to track functional decline and rule out gross paravalvular leaks, but it was insufficient for procedural planning.

Multislice CT (MSCT) imaging was the cornerstone of the procedural planning strategy, providing critical anatomical details that echocardiography could not resolve. The MSCT revealed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the previously implanted Evolut prosthesis, confirming severe haemodynamic compromise and defining the landing zone for the new valve. Beyond lumen sizing, the CT assessment focused on the interaction between the frames of the previously implanted transcatheter valves and the coronary anatomy. Specific measurements included coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, and the valve-to-coronary distance. These metrics were essential to evaluate the risk of coronary artery obstruction, a known complication in TAV-in-TAV procedures that is amplified in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenarios. The imaging also guided the strategy for implantation depth; the team planned a high implantation to preserve coronary flow and minimize the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia. This detailed anatomical mapping allowed for the selection of an Acurate neo2 prosthesis and the determination of a deployment strategy that maximized the effective orifice area while maintaining coronary access.

Finally, confirming the resolution of prior infective endocarditis was a prerequisite for safe reintervention. The patient had a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis caused by *Enterococcus faecalis* in September 2022, which was treated with a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy. Before proceeding with the third TAVI in 2024, the clinical team had to establish that no active infection remained, as implanting a new prosthetic device into an infected field carries a high risk of recurrent endocarditis and treatment failure. Resolution was confirmed through a multimodal approach: clinical assessment showed no fever or signs of sepsis, repeated blood cultures were negative, and transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and MSCT demonstrated no evidence of vegetations or paravalvular complications. With the infection considered resolved and no long-term suppressive antibiotic therapy required, the management strategy aligned with that of patients without active endocarditis, allowing the team to prioritize anatomical feasibility and hemodynamic severity in their decision to proceed.
