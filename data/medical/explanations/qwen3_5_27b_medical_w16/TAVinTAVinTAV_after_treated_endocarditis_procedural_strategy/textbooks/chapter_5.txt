Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 5: ## Clinical Outcomes, Follow-up, and Lifetime Management Lessons

The immediate procedural success of the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was confirmed by the absence of paravalvular or transvalvular regurgitation and correct device positioning. However, the initial hemodynamic assessment revealed significant patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM), a critical consideration in serial valve-in-valve procedures. Immediately following the implantation of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis, transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated a mean transvalvular gradient of 67 mm Hg and an aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²). The Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, consistent with severe PPM. This initial hemodynamic burden highlights a common challenge in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions, where the cumulative stent frames reduce the effective orifice area, potentially compromising flow dynamics despite the technical success of valve deployment.

Long-term follow-up data demonstrated a marked improvement in hemodynamic parameters, suggesting that initial gradients may not reflect the final functional state of the prosthesis. At one month post-implantation, the mean gradient decreased significantly to 27 mm Hg, and the AVA increased to 1.1 cm². By the one-year mark, the patient remained clinically stable and asymptomatic, with a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg and a preserved left ventricular ejection fraction of 65%. This resolution of severe PPM over time is attributed to the combination of optimized commissural alignment, effective post-dilatation, and the partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame during the procedure. The sustained improvement in valve function and the absence of recurrent endocarditis or regurgitation at one year confirm the durability of the intervention in this high-risk anatomical setting.

Surveillance strategies for patients with serial transcatheter prostheses must be rigorous, focusing on both hemodynamic performance and structural integrity. In this case, monitoring was conducted using transthoracic echocardiography at one month, 3.5 months, and one year post-procedure. Key metrics included mean gradients, AVA, and the presence of regurgitation or vegetations. Given the history of prosthetic valve endocarditis, the surveillance protocol also prioritized the detection of recurrent infection, confirmed through clinical assessment and repeat blood cultures. The stability of the prosthesis function over the first year suggests that, once infection is resolved, the management of prosthetic dysfunction aligns with standard structural valve deterioration protocols, provided anatomical feasibility is maintained.

Future considerations for lifetime management in patients with multiple prior TAVI procedures are complex and often limit options for subsequent failure. In this patient, the European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation (EuroSCORE II) was 27.36%, and the deep fixation of prior valves into the aortic wall would likely necessitate total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement for surgical explantation, rendering surgery prohibitively high-risk. Consequently, if the current prosthesis were to fail prematurely, emerging catheter-based solutions such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration techniques (e.g., BASILICA) might be considered. However, the feasibility and safety of these interventions in a triple-stented configuration remain uncertain. This case underscores the necessity of early, multidisciplinary planning regarding procedural trajectories, emphasizing that even at the time of the first intervention, clinicians must anticipate the potential need for serial reinterventions and the associated anatomical constraints.
