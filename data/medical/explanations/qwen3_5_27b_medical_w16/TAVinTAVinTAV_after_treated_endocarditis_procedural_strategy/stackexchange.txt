Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] According to current ESC guidelines, surgical replacement is the standard of care for prosthetic valve endocarditis complicated by heart failure. Based on this case, what specific patient factors justified the deviation from this guideline to pursue a third TAVI?

Although the 2025 ESC guidelines recommend surgical replacement for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) complicated by heart failure, this case demonstrates that management must be individualized based on infection status, surgical risk, and anatomical feasibility. Three specific factors in this patient justified the deviation to a third TAVI.

First, the infection was resolved. Unlike the guideline indication for active or uncontrolled infection, this patient had completed a full course of antimicrobial therapy. Clinical assessment, repeated blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT all confirmed the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular complications at the time of the decision.

Second, the surgical risk was prohibitive. The patient presented with a EuroSCORE II of 27.36% and significant comorbidities, including COPD, diabetes, recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, and complex coronary artery disease requiring multiple prior interventions. The heart team determined that open surgery posed an unacceptable mortality risk.

Third, the anatomical constraints made surgery technically hazardous. With two prior overlapping transcatheter prostheses deeply fixed into the aortic wall, surgical explantation would likely have required total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement. Conversely, preprocedural MSCT confirmed that a third TAVI was anatomically feasible, allowing for coronary protection and optimized hemodynamics without the morbidity of sternotomy.

Ultimately, the decision relied on the multidisciplinary heart team confirming that the structural dysfunction could be managed transcatheterally once the infection was cleared and surgical risk was deemed prohibitive.

### [Morning Report] In the context of a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, what specific anatomical measurements on preprocedural multislice CT are critical to assess to mitigate the risk of coronary artery obstruction?

In the context of a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure, preprocedural multislice CT (MSCT) must specifically evaluate the coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, and the valve-to-coronary distance, with particular attention paid to the interaction between the frames of previously implanted transcatheter valves. As illustrated in this case, the critical assessment extends beyond simple distances to include the relationship between the anticipated neo-skirt of the new prosthesis and the coronary ostia.

The pathophysiological risk in serial valve-in-valve interventions stems from the cumulative layering of stent frames, which can reduce the effective orifice area and physically obstruct coronary flow if the new valve is implanted too deeply or if the neo-skirt extends below the coronary ostia. In this patient, MSCT was essential to guide a strategy of high implantation. By analyzing the frame interaction and estimating valve-to-coronary distances, the team could determine that implanting the third valve at the level of the prior Evolut valve leaflets would fix the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame. This approach minimized the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, thereby preserving coronary flow and future access without the need for coronary protection devices during the procedure. Consequently, meticulous CT planning of these specific anatomical relationships is the primary determinant for mitigating coronary obstruction risk in complex, multi-prosthesis anatomies.

### [Pathophysiology] Beyond mechanical wear, what pathophysiological mechanisms related to the prior episode of Enterococcus faecalis endocarditis likely contributed to the structural valve deterioration observed in this patient?

Beyond mechanical wear, the prior episode of *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis likely contributed to structural valve deterioration through direct leaflet injury resulting in fibrosis and mechanical restriction.

In this case, the mechanism of prosthetic valve failure was multifactorial. While progressive structural degeneration of the transcatheter prostheses played a significant role, the discussion section explicitly attributes additional pathology to the prior infection. Specifically, the authors note that the episode of infective endocarditis "may have contributed to leaflet injury and subsequent mechanical restriction." Furthermore, the observed structural degeneration involved "leaflet fibrosis from prior degeneration and probable mechanical restriction following infection."

Clinically, this manifests as the severe restenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation observed in January 2024, approximately 16 months after the initial endocarditis diagnosis and following the completion of antimicrobial therapy. This highlights that even after successful eradication of the organism, the inflammatory sequelae of prosthetic valve endocarditis can permanently alter valve architecture, accelerating dysfunction through scarring and reduced leaflet mobility independent of active infection.

### [Clinical Pearls] Why does the cumulative burden of multiple overlapping transcatheter valve frames significantly increase the risk of coronary artery obstruction compared to a primary TAVI procedure?

In serial transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedures, the cumulative burden of multiple overlapping frames significantly increases the risk of coronary artery obstruction primarily by reducing the vertical distance between the prosthetic structure and the coronary ostia. Unlike a primary TAVI, where the coronary height is measured against the native annulus, reinterventions must account for the thickness of every prior stent frame and the potential displacement of previous valve leaflets.

As illustrated in this case, each additional valve layer consumes anatomical space within the aortic root. The report highlights that in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenarios, "multiple overlapping valve frames must be carefully considered" because they alter the interaction between the valve-to-coronary distance and the anticipated neo-skirt. Specifically, the implantation of a new valve can push the leaflets of the underlying prosthesis outward, creating a "neo-skirt" that may cover the coronary ostia if the new valve is implanted too low. In this patient, preprocedural multislice CT (MSCT) was essential to evaluate the "interaction between the frames of the previously implanted transcatheter valves" and to confirm that high implantation could preserve coronary flow.

The clinical principle derived from this case is that procedural planning must prioritize the preservation of coronary clearance as layers accumulate. The team utilized CT-guided modeling to ensure the third valve (Acurate neo2) was implanted at the level of the second valve's leaflets, thereby minimizing the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia. Consequently, in patients with serial TAVI, the risk of obstruction is not merely additive but geometric; meticulous assessment of frame interaction and coronary height is mandatory to prevent iatrogenic ischemia.

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient with a history of treated prosthetic valve endocarditis who presents with worsening heart failure and valve dysfunction, what clinical and imaging findings would help distinguish recurrent infection from aseptic structural valve deterioration?

In a patient with a history of treated prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) presenting with worsening heart failure and valve dysfunction, distinguishing recurrent infection from aseptic structural valve deterioration relies on the absence of systemic inflammatory signs and specific imaging findings of active infection.

As illustrated in this case, the initial episode of PVE in 2022 was characterized by persistent fever and positive blood cultures (*Enterococcus faecalis*). In contrast, the subsequent presentation in 2024 involved worsening hemodynamics—severe aortic stenosis with a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and moderate-to-severe regurgitation—without evidence of active infection. To differentiate aseptic deterioration from recurrent endocarditis prior to the third transcatheter intervention, the clinical team employed a multimodal assessment.

Specifically, the exclusion of active infection required negative repeated blood cultures and a clinical assessment devoid of fever or systemic inflammatory signs. Crucially, imaging played a definitive role: transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice CT (MSCT) confirmed the absence of vegetations, paravalvular complications, or abscesses. The MSCT further delineated the structural etiology, revealing a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm consistent with severe restenosis rather than infectious destruction.

The management principle derived from this case is that reintervention in patients with prior PVE is feasible only after rigorous confirmation of infection resolution. Once active endocarditis is excluded via cultures and advanced imaging, the management of valve dysfunction aligns with that of aseptic structural valve deterioration, focusing on anatomical feasibility and hemodynamic severity rather than infectious control.

### [Management] The Acurate neo2 valve was selected for the third intervention. What specific design characteristics of this prosthesis make it advantageous for patients with narrow anatomies and a need for commissural alignment?

In this complex TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the selection of the Acurate neo2 was driven by specific anatomical constraints and procedural goals inherent to triple-stented aortic root anatomy. As noted in the case discussion, the prosthesis was chosen for three primary design characteristics: supra-annular leaflet positioning, a relatively low frame height, and favorable hemodynamic performance specifically suited for narrow anatomies.

In this patient, multislice CT revealed a minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the prior Evolut prosthesis, confirming a severely narrowed landing zone. The Acurate neo2’s low frame height and supra-annular design help maximize the effective orifice area within such constrained spaces, thereby mitigating the risk of severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). Furthermore, the device’s design supports commissural alignment, which was a critical technical consideration in this case. By aligning the new valve’s commissures with the leaflets of the underlying Evolut valve, the team aimed to optimize leaflet motion and preserve coronary access. This alignment strategy, guided by preprocedural CT modeling, was essential to minimize the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia and to maintain future reaccessibility in a patient with significant coronary artery disease history. Thus, the valve’s specific structural features directly addressed the dual challenges of maintaining hemodynamic efficiency in a narrow lumen and ensuring long-term procedural safety regarding coronary flow.

### [Pathophysiology] This patient presented with severe patient-prosthesis mismatch and high transvalvular gradients immediately after the third TAVI, which improved significantly at one month. What physiological or mechanical processes likely account for this hemodynamic evolution?

This is an excellent observation regarding the hemodynamic evolution following this complex triple-valve intervention. The dramatic reduction in mean transvalvular gradient from 67 mm Hg immediately post-procedure to 27 mm Hg at one month, accompanied by an increase in aortic valve area (AVA) from 0.9 cm² to 1.1 cm², is primarily driven by mechanical frame expansion and subsequent ventricular remodeling, rather than acute thrombotic resolution.

Based on the case report, three key mechanisms account for this improvement:

First, **mechanical expansion of the nested valve frames** played the central role. The procedure utilized a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis implanted within a prior self-expanding Evolut valve. Immediately post-deployment, the interaction between the frames likely resulted in under-expansion, contributing to severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). The case discussion explicitly attributes the gradient disappearance to "effective post-dilatation and partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame." The use of a 20/40 mm balloon for post-dilation likely exerted radial force that further expanded the inner Evolut frame, thereby increasing the effective orifice area.

Second, the **settling of the self-expanding prosthesis** contributed to the hemodynamic gain. Unlike balloon-expandable valves, the Acurate neo2 continues to exert radial force over time. This gradual expansion against the rigid inner stent frame optimizes the geometry of the neo-orifice, reducing resistance to flow as evidenced by the AVA increase at one month.

Third, **left ventricular (LV) remodeling** supported the hemodynamic stabilization. The report notes that at one month, "Left ventricular remodelling was observed, with a reduction in chamber volumes." As the severe afterload was relieved, the LV reduced its chamber size and wall stress, which improves the efficiency of ejection and contributes to the sustained reduction in transvalvular gradients.

Finally, the procedural strategy of **high implantation** was critical. By aligning the third valve at the level of the Evolut leaflets, the team prevented inward leaflet folding that would have further reduced the orifice area. This technique, guided by preprocedural CT, preserved the effective orifice area and allowed for the observed improvement in flow dynamics.

In summary, this case illustrates that in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenarios, immediate severe PPM may be transient. A combination of aggressive post-dilation to expand nested frames and the inherent properties of self-expanding devices, coupled with physiological LV remodeling, can yield significant hemodynamic recovery within the first month.

### [Clinical Pearls] Given the prohibitive surgical risk and the complexity of triple-stented anatomy, what emerging catheter-based strategies might be considered if this third prosthesis fails, and what are their limitations?

In this high-risk scenario with triple-stented anatomy, treatment options for future prosthesis failure are extremely limited. As noted in the case discussion, surgical explantation remains technically demanding and high-risk. Emerging catheter-based strategies that might be considered include bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration techniques, such as the BASILICA procedure. However, the case report emphasizes that the feasibility and safety of these interventions in triple-stented configurations remain uncertain. The primary limitation arises from the cumulative mesh burden of multiple overlapping frames, which complicates coronary access and increases the risk of coronary obstruction, making procedural planning for subsequent interventions significantly more complex.
