Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] Before initiating eculizumab therapy for CM-HUS, what specific prophylactic measures are mandatory to mitigate the risk of life-threatening opportunistic infections associated with terminal complement blockade?

Before initiating eculizumab therapy for complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome (CM-HUS), mandatory prophylactic measures include vaccination against *Neisseria meningitidis* and antimicrobial prophylaxis.

Eculizumab blocks the terminal complement component C5, which is critical for host defense against encapsulated bacteria. Consequently, blockade significantly increases the risk of life-threatening invasive meningococcal disease. In this case, the patient received first doses of meningococcal vaccines—Menveo (serogroups A, C, Y, and W) and Bexsero (serogroup B)—prior to eculizumab induction. However, because protective immunity typically requires two weeks to develop, and the vaccines were not administered at least two weeks before starting complement blockade, the patient was concurrently given prophylactic penicillin (Apocillin) to bridge this gap.

This case underscores that vaccination and antimicrobial prophylaxis are essential prerequisites for safe complement inhibition. While eculizumab is the standard of care for CM-HUS and markedly improves renal outcomes, it must be balanced against the risk of opportunistic infections. In this patient, eculizumab was initiated only after blood cultures became negative, further mitigating the risk of uncontrolled infection during the period of complement blockade.
