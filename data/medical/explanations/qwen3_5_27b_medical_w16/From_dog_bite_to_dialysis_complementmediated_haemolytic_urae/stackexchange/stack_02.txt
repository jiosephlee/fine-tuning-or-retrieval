Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a patient presenting with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) and thrombocytopenia, what specific ADAMTS13 activity level is required to effectively rule out thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and guide further workup for other thrombotic microangiopathies?

In a patient presenting with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) and thrombocytopenia, an ADAMTS13 activity level greater than 10% is required to effectively rule out thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP). As highlighted in this case, TTP is defined by severe ADAMTS13 deficiency, typically presenting with activity below 10%. Our patient’s ADAMTS13 activity was 43.5%, which effectively excluded TTP and guided the diagnostic workup toward other thrombotic microangiopathies (TMAs), specifically complement-mediated hemolytic uremic syndrome (CM-HUS).

The diagnostic priority in adults with MAHA, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury is to exclude TTP, given its high mortality and requirement for urgent plasma exchange. In this case, despite the confounding presence of *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* sepsis and disseminated intravascular coagulation, the persistence of hemolysis and thrombocytopenia necessitated TMA evaluation. The ADAMTS13 result of 43.5% was the critical discriminator; it confirmed the absence of severe deficiency, allowing the team to rule out TTP and investigate alternative causes such as Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (STEC) or complement dysregulation. Following the exclusion of TTP and STEC-HUS, the diagnosis of CM-HUS was established based on renal biopsy findings and clinical response to eculizumab. Thus, an ADAMTS13 activity above 10% serves as the threshold to redirect management from TTP protocols to other TMA etiologies.
