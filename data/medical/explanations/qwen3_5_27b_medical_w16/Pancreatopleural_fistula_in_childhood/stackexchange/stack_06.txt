Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Morning Report] In the diagnostic imaging workup, why is Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) often preferred over Contrast-Enhanced CT for defining the fistulous tract in suspected pancreatopleural fistula?

**Answer:**

MRCP is preferred over contrast-enhanced CT in this setting because it provides superior visualization of the pancreatic ductal anatomy and the specific fistulous tract. While CT is excellent for identifying parenchymal changes such as calcifications, pseudocysts, or ductal dilation, it frequently fails to depict the actual fistula.

In this case, the contrast-enhanced CT was instrumental in raising suspicion, revealing a multiloculated hypodense collection tracking from the pleural space to the pancreatic tail. However, it was the MRCP that definitively confirmed the diagnosis by demonstrating the tubular cystic collection communicating directly with the distal pancreatic duct, along with proximal ductal narrowing. The discussion in the report highlights this distinction quantitatively: MRCP offers a 70% to 80% sensitivity for visualizing the fistula and duct anatomy, whereas CT depicts the actual fistula in only 33% to 47% of cases.

Defining the precise anatomy of the tract and the pancreatic duct is critical for management planning. As noted in the discussion, if conservative management fails, endoscopic therapy (ERCP) or surgery may be required, both of which rely on accurate mapping of the ductal disruption. Therefore, while CT may suggest the diagnosis, MRCP is the diagnostic standard for defining the tract to guide therapeutic intervention.
