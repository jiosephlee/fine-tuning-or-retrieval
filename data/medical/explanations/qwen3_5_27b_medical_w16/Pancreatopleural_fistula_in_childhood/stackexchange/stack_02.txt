Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Pathophysiology] Describe the anatomical pathway by which pancreatic fluid typically tracks from the retroperitoneum to the pleural cavity in cases of pancreatopleural fistula.

The anatomical pathway for pancreatic fluid to reach the pleural cavity involves tracking from the retroperitoneum through natural diaphragmatic hiatuses. As described in the case discussion, when a pancreatic duct or pseudocyst ruptures posteriorly, fluid leaks into the retroperitoneal space and follows the path of least resistance superiorly into the mediastinum. It then traverses the diaphragm, typically through the aortic or esophageal hiatus, to breach the pleura. In this specific patient, MRCP confirmed a tubular cystic collection in the distal body and tail of the pancreas extending posteriorly in the retroperitoneum and tracking superiorly through the esophageal hiatus into the right pleural cavity. This route accounts for the recurrent, dark red, high-amylase pleural effusion observed in the absence of primary thoracic pathology. Identifying this trajectory is essential to recognize the abdominal source of the respiratory symptoms and guide appropriate management.
