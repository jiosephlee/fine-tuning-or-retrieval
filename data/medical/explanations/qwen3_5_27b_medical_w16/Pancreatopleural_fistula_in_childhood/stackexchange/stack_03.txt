Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Differential Diagnosis] This patient initially presented with elevated pleural fluid adenosine deaminase (ADA) and a contact history for tuberculosis. Which specific molecular test result was crucial in excluding tuberculosis as the primary diagnosis?

The specific molecular test result crucial in excluding tuberculosis as the primary diagnosis was a **negative GeneXpert for MTB**.

In this case, the clinical presentation initially favored tuberculous pleurisy due to the patient’s documented contact history with pulmonary Koch’s and the finding of mildly elevated pleural fluid adenosine deaminase (ADA). These factors are classic indicators for tuberculosis and led to the initiation of empiric antitubercular therapy. However, as detailed in the differential diagnosis section of the report, the medical team recognized that elevated ADA alone is not definitive. The negative GeneXpert result provided the necessary molecular confirmation to rule out Mycobacterium tuberculosis, allowing the team to discontinue antitubercular drugs.

This diagnostic pivot was essential because the patient’s underlying pathology was a pancreatopleural fistula (PPF), a rare entity that presents with a "pulmonary mask" of recurrent pleural effusions. By relying on molecular testing rather than clinical suspicion alone, the team avoided unnecessary prolonged chemotherapy and correctly identified the pancreatic origin of the effusion, confirmed subsequently by markedly elevated pleural fluid amylase and MRCP imaging. This case illustrates that in complex pleural effusions with overlapping features, molecular exclusion of common infectious etiologies is vital to uncovering rare systemic causes.
