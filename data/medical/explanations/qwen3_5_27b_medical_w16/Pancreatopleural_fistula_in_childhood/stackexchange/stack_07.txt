Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] If conservative medical management fails to close a pancreatopleural fistula after an adequate trial period, what is the preferred next-line intervention and how does it facilitate healing?

While our patient successfully closed his pancreatopleural fistula with conservative management, it is crucial to understand the escalation algorithm should medical therapy fail. According to the management principles outlined in the discussion of this report, if conservative measures (bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, and somatostatin analogues) do not resolve the fistula after an adequate trial, the preferred next-line intervention is **Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with trans-papillary stenting**.

This endoscopic approach facilitates healing by placing a stent across the pancreatic duct disruption, which effectively **reduces intraductal pressure and leakage**, allowing the fistulous tract to close. The report notes that endoscopic intervention is favored due to minimal morbidity and mortality, with a success rate of 100% in pediatric cases and long-term success exceeding 90% in mixed cohorts. Surgical options, such as distal pancreatectomy or pancreatojejunostomy, are reserved for cases where stent placement is not possible due to severe obstruction or if there is no response to endoscopic management. Thus, while we prioritized medical management in this case, ERCP remains the definitive therapeutic step for refractory pancreatopleural fistulas.
