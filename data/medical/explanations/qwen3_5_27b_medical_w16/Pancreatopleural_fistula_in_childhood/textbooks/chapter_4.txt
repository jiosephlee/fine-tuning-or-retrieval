Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

Chapter 4: ## Therapeutic Management and Decision Making

The management of pancreatopleural fistula (PPF) in the pediatric population presents a significant clinical challenge due to the rarity of the condition and the absence of standardized treatment guidelines. In this case, the clinical team adopted a stepwise therapeutic approach, prioritizing conservative medical management to promote spontaneous fistula closure before considering invasive interventions. The primary physiological goal of this strategy is to minimize pancreatic exocrine secretion, thereby reducing the volume of fluid leaking through the fistula tract and allowing the disrupted ductal system to heal. Upon confirming the diagnosis via MRCP and elevated pleural fluid amylase, the patient was immediately placed on nil per oral (NPO) status and initiated on total parenteral nutrition (TPN). This bowel rest is critical as it reduces the stimulation of pancreatic enzyme production, which is essential for decreasing the pressure within the pancreatic duct and facilitating tract closure.

Pharmacological intervention played a central role in suppressing pancreatic activity alongside nutritional support. The patient received a continuous infusion of Octreotide, a somatostatin analogue, at a dose of 30 µg/kg/day, which falls within the therapeutic range of 10–40 µg/kg/day described in pediatric case literature. Octreotide functions by inhibiting the release of gastrointestinal hormones and directly suppressing exocrine pancreatic secretion, effectively acting to reduce the flow of pancreatic fluid through the fistula. Additionally, pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT) was administered to modulate feedback mechanisms. Concurrently, the medical team made the decision to discontinue empiric antitubercular drugs once the diagnosis was confirmed, preventing unnecessary medication exposure and potential toxicity.

Control of the pleural effusion was managed through a progression of drainage techniques tailored to the patient's clinical course. Initially, bedside lung ultrasound and thoracocentesis were utilized during the first two admissions, providing temporary symptomatic relief by aspirating approximately one liter of dark red fluid. However, due to the recurrent nature of the effusion and the need for continuous monitoring, an intercostal drain (ICD) was inserted during the third admission. This intervention was vital not only for relieving respiratory distress and preventing lung compression but also for monitoring the therapeutic response. The ICD allowed the team to track the volume of fluid output daily, serving as a direct and quantitative marker of fistula activity.

Decision-making regarding the escalation of care is guided by the response to conservative measures and the anatomical findings of the pancreatic duct. While this patient responded well to medical management, the clinical framework dictates that if conservative therapy fails after two to three weeks, or if there is significant ductal stenosis or obstruction, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) with trans-papillary stenting is the preferred next step. Stenting reduces intraductal pressure and diverts flow, with reported success rates exceeding 90% in mixed age cohorts. Surgical interventions, such as distal pancreatectomy or pancreatojejunostomy, are reserved for cases where endoscopic options are not feasible or have failed, typically involving higher morbidity and are considered only when less invasive measures are exhausted.

Monitoring therapeutic response in this case relied heavily on the reduction of fluid drainage from the ICD. Over a period of 10 days, the output gradually decreased and eventually ceased, indicating successful fistula closure. This clinical improvement allowed for the removal of the chest tube and the transition back to oral intake. The patient was discharged symptom-free and maintained stability during monthly follow-ups. This outcome underscores the importance of a dedicated conservative trial in pediatric PPF, provided there are no contraindications such as severe ductal anomalies, and highlights the necessity of close monitoring to determine the appropriate timing for invasive escalation.
