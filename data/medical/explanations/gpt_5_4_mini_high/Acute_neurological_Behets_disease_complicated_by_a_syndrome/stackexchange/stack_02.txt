Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] In a patient with newly recognized Behçet disease who has fever, headache, vertigo, and cerebrospinal fluid showing mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein, how would you diagnose acute neuro-Behçet disease when brain MRI is normal, and what neurologic phenotype does this presentation represent?

Acute neuro-Behçet disease is a **clinical diagnosis** in this setting. In a patient who already meets criteria for Behçet disease, the combination of **new fever, headache, vertigo, and inflammatory CSF**—here, **mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein with negative bacterial, mycobacterial, and viral studies**—supports acute neuro-Behçet **even if brain MRI is normal**. A normal MRI does **not** exclude the diagnosis, particularly early in the illness; the key is to recognize the neurologic syndrome in the context of definite Behçet disease and to **exclude mimics** such as infection, malignancy, and other inflammatory disorders.

This presentation represents the **acute meningeal phenotype of neuro-Behçet—essentially aseptic meningitis/meningoencephalitis-like disease without parenchymal MRI lesions**. In other words, it is a **nonfocal inflammatory CNS presentation**, rather than the classic brainstem parenchymal lesion pattern often seen in neuro-Behçet.
