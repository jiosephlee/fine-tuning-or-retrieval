Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Management] What is the usual first-line treatment for acute neuro-Behçet disease, and how would you justify or question colchicine monotherapy in a patient with meningoencephalitis-like symptoms but no focal deficits or MRI lesion?

The usual first-line treatment for **acute neuro-Behçet disease** is **systemic corticosteroid therapy**, typically high-dose glucocorticoids, after infection has been reasonably excluded. This is especially true for the classic **parenchymal/meningoencephalitic** form of neuro-Behçet, in which most patients are treated with steroids and, if disease is severe, relapsing, or steroid-dependent, a steroid-sparing immunosuppressant such as **azathioprine** is often added.

In this case, **colchicine monotherapy** is best viewed as an **exceptional, nonstandard observation rather than established therapy**. Colchicine is well supported for the **mucocutaneous** manifestations of Behçet disease, but the case report itself notes that systematic reviews generally find it **ineffective for acute neuro-Behçet** and that it is usually used as an adjunct, not as sole treatment. The patient’s improvement is intriguing, but it does not prove colchicine was the active treatment; this episode may have been a **mild, self-limited nonparenchymal meningoencephalitis**, and spontaneous recovery cannot be excluded.

So, how do you justify or question it in a patient like this?

- **Justify it only cautiously** if the presentation is mild, infection has been excluded, MRI is normal, there are **no focal deficits**, and there is a strong concurrent systemic Behçet picture, as in this report.
- **Question it strongly** because a **normal MRI does not exclude acute neuro-Behçet**, especially early, and the absence of focal deficits does not make CNS inflammation benign. If CSF shows aseptic meningitis and the clinical syndrome fits, the standard approach is still **corticosteroids**, not colchicine alone.

In short: **steroids are the usual first-line therapy; colchicine monotherapy is not standard and should be interpreted as anecdotal in this case, not practice-changing evidence.**
