Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] In a child with recurrent, massive, blood-stained unilateral pleural effusions and minimal abdominal symptoms, what pleural fluid laboratory finding should immediately raise suspicion for pancreatopleural fistula, and what threshold is classically considered highly suggestive?

The key pleural fluid clue is a **markedly elevated amylase level** in the effusion—an **amylase-rich pleural effusion** should immediately raise suspicion for a **pancreatopleural fistula**. Classically, a **pleural fluid amylase ≥ 1,000 IU/L** is considered **highly suggestive** and is often cited as essentially diagnostic in the appropriate clinical setting.

In the reported child, the pleural fluid amylase was **6,118 U/L**, which strongly supported the diagnosis. If available, **pancreatic isoenzyme predominance** further supports pancreatic origin.
