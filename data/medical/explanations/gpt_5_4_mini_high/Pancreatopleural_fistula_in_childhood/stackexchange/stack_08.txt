Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Management] When should pancreatopleural fistula be escalated from conservative therapy to endoscopic intervention, and what pancreatic duct findings make transpapillary stenting or balloon dilatation the preferred next step?

In pancreatopleural fistula, conservative therapy is the initial step **only if there is no major pancreatic duct abnormality**: bowel rest, total parenteral nutrition, octreotide, pancreatic enzyme replacement, and adequate pleural drainage. In the case report, this approach was used first and the drainage decreased over 10 days, which is consistent with the recommended trial of medical therapy.

You should **escalate to endoscopic intervention when conservative treatment fails to control the fistula after about 2–3 weeks, and certainly by 3–4 weeks if there is persistent high-output drainage or recurrent pleural effusion**. The rationale is that ongoing ductal leakage will not close if pancreatic juice continues to be driven through a disrupted or obstructed duct.

The **preferred next step is ERCP with transpapillary stenting, or balloon dilatation followed by stenting, when imaging shows a ductal leak that communicates with the pancreatic duct and there is ductal narrowing/stenosis proximal to the fistula**. That is exactly the kind of anatomy described in the report: the collection communicated with the distal pancreatic duct, with **narrowing of the duct proximal to the communication**. Such a finding suggests an obstructed, pressurized duct system; endoscopic decompression diverts flow internally, lowers intraductal pressure, and promotes fistula closure. Balloon dilatation is particularly useful when a focal stricture must be traversed before a stent can be placed.

If ERCP cannot bridge the leak because of **severe duct obstruction, a distal fistula that is not stentable, or failure of endoscopic therapy**, surgery becomes the next option.
