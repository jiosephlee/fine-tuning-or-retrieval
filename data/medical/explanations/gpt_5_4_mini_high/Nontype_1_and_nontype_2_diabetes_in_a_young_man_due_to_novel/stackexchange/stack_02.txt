Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Morning Report] Which clinical and laboratory features in this case make HNF1A-MODY more likely than classic type 1 diabetes and more likely than typical type 2 diabetes?

This case has the classic pattern that should prompt consideration of **HNF1A-MODY**: **young onset diabetes in several successive generations, no ketosis, negative islet autoantibodies, and preserved but insufficient endogenous insulin secretion**.

More specifically, it is **less consistent with type 1 diabetes** because he had:

- **No diabetic ketoacidosis**
- **Negative GAD and IA-2 antibodies**
- **Detectable glucagon-stimulated C-peptide** (0.83 nmol/L), showing retained β-cell function rather than the near-absolute insulin deficiency typical of classic type 1 diabetes

It is **less consistent with typical type 2 diabetes** because he had:

- **Early-onset diabetes** in a **clear autosomal dominant family pattern**: grandmother in her 30s, mother in her late 20s, brother at 20
- **No obesity** (BMI 24.6 kg/m²)
- **No acanthosis nigricans** or obvious insulin-resistance phenotype
- **Glycosuria and marked postprandial hyperglycemia**, which are particularly suggestive of HNF1A-MODY because HNF1A variants lower the renal threshold for glucose

So the key teaching point is: **early-onset familial diabetes, autoantibody negativity, preserved C-peptide, absence of ketosis, and lack of a strong insulin-resistance phenotype** should move HNF1A-MODY ahead of both type 1 and typical type 2 diabetes in the differential.
