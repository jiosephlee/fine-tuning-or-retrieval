Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] Once HNF1A-MODY is diagnosed in one patient, what is the rationale for cascade testing and counselling of family members, and which relatives should be prioritised?

Once HNF1A-MODY is diagnosed in one patient, cascade testing is recommended because this is an **autosomal dominant** disorder: each first-degree relative has a **50% chance** of carrying the familial variant. The point is not only to label relatives, but to **find affected family members early**, before or at the onset of hyperglycemia, so they can receive the correct diagnosis, appropriate glucose monitoring, and MODY-directed therapy rather than being treated as having type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Counseling also matters because it clarifies **inheritance risk, reproductive risk, and long-term surveillance** for microvascular complications.

The relatives to **prioritize first** are the **first-degree relatives** of the proband: **parents, siblings, and children**. In this case, the pedigree already suggests **vertical transmission on the maternal side**, so the **mother, younger brother, and other maternal relatives with early-onset diabetes** would be the highest-yield family members to test first. If the familial mutation is confirmed in them, testing can then be extended to additional at-risk relatives in that branch of the family.
