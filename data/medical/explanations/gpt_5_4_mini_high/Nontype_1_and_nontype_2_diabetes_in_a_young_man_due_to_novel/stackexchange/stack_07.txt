Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Management] After genetic confirmation of HNF1A-MODY in a patient initially treated with basal insulin, metformin, and a DPP-4 inhibitor, how should therapy be reconsidered, and why can insulin often be discontinued once glycaemic control is achieved?

Once **HNF1A-MODY** is genetically confirmed, the treatment plan should be **reframed from “type 1 or type 2 diabetes” to monogenic, β-cell–dysfunction diabetes**. In practice, that means insulin should be **reassessed and often tapered off**, provided there is **no diabetic ketoacidosis, no absolute insulin deficiency, and glycaemic control can be maintained with noninsulin therapy**.  

In this case, the patient had **negative islet autoantibodies** and **preserved stimulated C-peptide**, indicating **residual endogenous insulin secretion**. That is the key reason insulin can often be discontinued in HNF1A-MODY: the disorder is **not autoimmune β-cell destruction**, as in type 1 diabetes, but a **transcription-factor defect causing progressive, partial impairment of insulin secretion**. As long as some β-cell function remains, patients may do well on agents that enhance endogenous insulin release or incretin effect.  

For HNF1A-MODY, **low-dose sulfonylureas are classically first-line**, because these patients are highly sulfonylurea sensitive. In the reported case, insulin was stopped after genetic confirmation and glycaemic improvement, and therapy was shifted to **dulaglutide**, with later simplification to **metformin plus a DPP-4 inhibitor**. That is reasonable when sulfonylureas are not preferred or when a GLP-1 receptor agonist is better tolerated or considered safer.  

So the teaching point is: **after genetic diagnosis, do not keep insulin by default.** If the patient has **residual C-peptide and stable glycaemia**, insulin can usually be withdrawn and replaced with **MODY-directed therapy**, because the underlying problem is **insufficient but not absent insulin secretion**.
