Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] A previously unreported HNF1A missense variant was identified in this patient. What types of evidence can support classifying a novel variant as pathogenic or likely pathogenic under ACMG principles, even if it is absent from ClinVar?

Absence from ClinVar does **not** mean a variant is benign; it usually means only that the variant is **novel or not yet curated**. Under ACMG/AMP principles, a previously unreported **HNF1A missense** variant can still be classified as **likely pathogenic** or **pathogenic** if multiple independent lines of evidence converge.

In this case, the strongest supporting evidence would be the following: **rarity or absence from population databases** (PM2); **segregation with diabetes in the family** in a pattern consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance (PP1); a **phenotype highly specific for HNF1A-MODY**—early-onset diabetes, negative islet autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide, glycosuria, and a strong multigenerational family history (PP4); placement in a **critical functional domain or mutational hot spot**, or near other known pathogenic residues, such as the **DNA-binding domain** in this report (PM1/PM5); and **computational evidence** suggesting deleterious impact, such as conservation and high pathogenicity scores like the reported AlphaMissense score (PP3). If available, **functional studies** showing impaired HNF1A transcriptional activity would add strong evidence (PS3), and a true **de novo** event would also be powerful evidence in the right context (PS2).

So, the key teaching point is that **ACMG classification is evidence-based, not database-based**. In this patient, the combination of classic MODY phenotype, family segregation, and the variant’s location in a biologically important region supported a **likely pathogenic** interpretation even though ClinVar did not yet contain the variant.
