Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In a post-cardiac surgery patient with severe thrombocytopenia, what alternative diagnoses should be considered besides HIT, and which features in this case argued against sepsis, post-transfusion purpura, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, and drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia?

In a post-cardiac surgery patient, severe thrombocytopenia should prompt a broad differential, not just HIT. Important alternatives include **sepsis/critical illness with consumptive thrombocytopenia, post-transfusion purpura, thrombotic microangiopathy such as TTP, DIC, and drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia**.

In this case, several features argued against those mimics:

- **Sepsis/critical illness:** he was **hemodynamically stable**, clinically doing well aside from delirium, had a **normal white-cell count and differential**, **negative blood and urine cultures**, and no major abnormality on chest radiography.
- **Post-transfusion purpura:** he had **no blood transfusions**, making this diagnosis implausible.
- **TTP:** the initial evaluation showed **normal haptoglobin**, **no schistocytes** on the peripheral smear, and no clear evidence of microangiopathic hemolysis; moreover, the coagulation profile was not suggestive of a thrombotic microangiopathy.
- **Drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia:** the platelet fall occurred **too early** for most newly administered postoperative drugs to be responsible, and it was also earlier than expected for drugs given on the day of surgery or during the postoperative course, including **vancomycin, cefazolin, tranexamic acid, furosemide, metoprolol, pantoprazole, and midodrine**.

So, while HIT remained the leading diagnosis, the case illustrates the key teaching point: **postoperative thrombocytopenia must be interpreted in light of timing, smear findings, hemolysis markers, transfusion history, and evidence of infection or shock.**
