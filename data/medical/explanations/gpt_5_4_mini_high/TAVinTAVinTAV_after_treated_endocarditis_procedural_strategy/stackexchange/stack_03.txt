Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] Before offering a third transcatheter aortic valve procedure to a patient with prior prosthetic valve endocarditis, what clinical, microbiologic, and imaging evidence should be present to support that the infection has been adequately treated and is no longer active?

Before a third transcatheter valve procedure is offered after prosthetic valve endocarditis, you want evidence that the infection has been **clinically quiescent, microbiologically cleared, and anatomically inactive**.

In practical terms, that means:

- **Clinical evidence:** the patient should be **afebrile and without ongoing signs of infection**—no persistent sepsis, no new embolic phenomena, and no other features suggesting uncontrolled endocarditis. Residual heart-failure symptoms can still be present if they are explained by **structural prosthetic failure**, not infection.
- **Microbiologic evidence:** **targeted antimicrobial therapy should be completed**, and **repeat blood cultures should be negative**, showing clearance of the prior pathogen. In the case report, the patient had completed a full course for *Enterococcus faecalis* PVE and was **not on chronic suppressive antibiotics** at the time of reintervention.
- **Imaging evidence:** **TEE is essential**, and CT is helpful, to show **no vegetations, no periannular abscess, no prosthetic dehiscence, and no paravalvular complications**. In this case, both **TEE and MSCT confirmed resolution of infection** and no evidence of active endocarditis.

So the decision to proceed should rest on a **concordant picture**: the patient is clinically well from an infectious standpoint, cultures have cleared, and TEE/CT show **no active endocarditis**. Only then should the remaining valve problem be treated as **degenerative prosthetic dysfunction**, as was done here.
