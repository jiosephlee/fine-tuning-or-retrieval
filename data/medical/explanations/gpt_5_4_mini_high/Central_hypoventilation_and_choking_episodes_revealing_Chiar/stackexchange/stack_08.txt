Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Clinical Pearls] In suspected central sleep-disordered breathing, what is the major limitation of a type III sleep study compared with full polysomnography with EEG and capnography, and how can this lead to underestimation of disease severity?

The key limitation of a type III study is that it does not measure EEG sleep staging, and often does not measure CO₂. In suspected central sleep-disordered breathing, that matters because respiratory event indices are then calculated using total recording time rather than true total sleep time, so periods of wakefulness dilute the apnea-hypopnea index and make the disorder look milder than it is.  

In addition, without capnography, type III testing can miss sustained nocturnal hypoventilation and hypercapnia, which are especially important in central hypoventilation syndromes. Thus, a patient may appear to have only “mild” sleep apnea by AHI, as in this case, while still having clinically significant central respiratory failure with profound desaturations and brainstem-related dysregulation. Full polysomnography with EEG and capnography is therefore the preferred study when central hypoventilation is suspected.
