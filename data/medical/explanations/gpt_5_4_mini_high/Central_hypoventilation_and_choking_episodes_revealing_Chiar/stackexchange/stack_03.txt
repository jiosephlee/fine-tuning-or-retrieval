Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Clinical Pearls] What radiographic criterion defines Chiari malformation type I on sagittal MRI, and what historical CT finding at the craniocervical junction should raise suspicion even if the original report was interpreted as normal?

On sagittal MRI, Chiari malformation type I is defined by **caudal descent of the cerebellar tonsils at least 5 mm below the foramen magnum**. In this case, MRI showed **11 mm of tonsillar descent**.

On historical CT of the craniocervical junction, the clue that should prompt suspicion is a **crowded foramen magnum with effacement or loss of the normal CSF space at the cervicomedullary junction**—in this patient, the re-reviewed CT also showed **cerebellar tissue extending to the level of the opisthion**. Even if the original CT report was read as normal, that pattern should raise concern for Chiari I and prompt MRI confirmation.
