Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Clinical Pearls] A previously healthy adult develops fulminant sepsis with disseminated intravascular coagulation 4 days after a dog bite. Which zoonotic pathogen should be strongly suspected, and what microbiologic characteristic commonly delays culture-based diagnosis?

The pathogen to strongly suspect is **Capnocytophaga canimorsus**. In a previously healthy adult who develops **fulminant sepsis, DIC, and multiorgan failure within days of a dog bite**, this organism should be high on the differential, even without classic immunocompromising risk factors.

The microbiologic feature that commonly delays diagnosis is that **C. canimorsus is fastidious and slow-growing**—a **slow-growing, encapsulated, gram-negative rod** that may require **prolonged incubation** and can be difficult to recover on routine culture. As in the case report, blood cultures may not turn positive for several days, which is why clinical suspicion must prompt early empiric therapy.
