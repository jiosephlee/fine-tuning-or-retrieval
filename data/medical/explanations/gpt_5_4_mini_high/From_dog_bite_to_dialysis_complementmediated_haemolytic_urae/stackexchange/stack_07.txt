Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Morning Report] What renal biopsy findings are most consistent with thrombotic microangiopathy in this case, and how can biopsy help when the diagnosis remains uncertain after laboratory evaluation?

The renal biopsy findings most consistent with thrombotic microangiopathy in this case were **fibrin thrombi within ischemic glomeruli**, accompanied by **severe acute tubular necrosis** and **scattered crescent formation**. On the report, **immunofluorescence showed no immune-complex deposition** and electron microscopy did not show a primary glomerulopathy, which supports a **microvascular thrombotic/endothelial injury pattern** rather than an immune-complex nephritis or another primary glomerular disease.

Biopsy is especially valuable when the laboratory evaluation is not definitive, as in this patient, whose course was initially confounded by **sepsis, DIC, AKI, thrombocytopenia, and hemolysis**. A kidney biopsy can **confirm that renal injury is due to TMA**, and it can also **exclude mimics** such as immune-complex glomerulonephritis, vasculitis, or another intrinsic renal process. In this case, the biopsy helped anchor the diagnosis of **TMA/CM-HUS** after **ADAMTS13 was not severely reduced** and routine complement testing was nondiagnostic, showing that tissue diagnosis can be decisive when the clinical and laboratory picture remains ambiguous.
