Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] Why is plasma exchange often started empirically in adults presenting with thrombotic microangiopathy while ADAMTS13 results are pending, and why is its role different in complement-mediated HUS than in TTP?

Adults who present with the triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury should be treated as having **possible TTP until proven otherwise**, because **TTP is a hematologic emergency** and treatment delay can be fatal. The diagnosis is ultimately laboratory-based—**severe ADAMTS13 deficiency, typically <10%, confirms TTP**—but that result is often not immediately available. Therefore, if the clinical suspicion is meaningful, plasma exchange is started empirically as a **bridge while ADAMTS13 is pending**, rather than waiting and risking irreversible neurologic or organ injury.

The reason plasma exchange is so effective in **TTP** is mechanistic: it **removes circulating anti-ADAMTS13 autoantibodies and ultra-large von Willebrand factor multimers** and **replaces deficient ADAMTS13** with donor plasma. In other words, it treats the core pathophysiology.

Its role is quite different in **complement-mediated HUS (CM-HUS)**. CM-HUS is driven by **dysregulated alternative complement activation**, not ADAMTS13 deficiency. Plasma exchange may provide only **transient benefit** by removing circulating complement fragments or, in some cases, autoantibodies, but it does **not correct the underlying complement abnormality**. That is why the definitive therapy in CM-HUS is **terminal complement blockade with eculizumab**, which in this case was associated with sustained hematologic recovery and partial renal recovery, whereas plasma exchange was only a temporary bridge. The case illustrates this well: ADAMTS13 later returned at **43.5%**, making TTP unlikely, and the patient’s durable improvement occurred only after complement inhibition, not plasma exchange alone.
