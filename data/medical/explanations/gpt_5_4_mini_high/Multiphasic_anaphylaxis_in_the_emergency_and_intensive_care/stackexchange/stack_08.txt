Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Clinical Pearls] How should serum tryptase be measured after suspected anaphylaxis in terms of number and timing of samples, and what is the clinical significance of a persistently elevated baseline tryptase level after recovery?

After suspected anaphylaxis, serum tryptase should be drawn serially: obtain an initial sample as soon as feasible without delaying treatment, a second sample 1–2 hours after symptom onset (and no later than 4 hours, when tryptase typically peaks), and a third sample at least 24 hours after complete clinical resolution to establish the baseline. In the case report, the authors note that guidelines recommend a minimum of two, ideally three, timed samples because interpretation depends on comparing the acute rise with the recovery baseline.

A persistently elevated baseline tryptase after recovery is clinically important because it suggests an underlying mast-cell disorder rather than just transient mediator release from the anaphylactic event. In this case, the elevated baseline tryptase, which remained increased on follow-up, raised a high likelihood of systemic mastocytosis, even though formal diagnostic criteria were not fully met. Practically, a high baseline tryptase should prompt further allergologic evaluation and consideration of mast-cell disease, especially in patients with unusually severe, recurrent, or multiphasic anaphylaxis.
