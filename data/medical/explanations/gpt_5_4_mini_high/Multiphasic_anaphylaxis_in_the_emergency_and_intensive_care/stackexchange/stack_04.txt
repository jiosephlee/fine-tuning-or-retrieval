Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] For a 104-kg adult with anaphylaxis, what is the recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose, how is it derived from weight-based dosing, and why might repeated use of a 0.3 mg auto-injector represent clinically important underdosing?

For a 104-kg adult with anaphylaxis, the recommended intramuscular epinephrine dose is **0.5 mg**.

The usual guideline dose is **0.01 mg/kg IM**, but this is **capped at a maximum of 0.5 mg** in adults. In this patient, the weight-based calculation would be 0.01 × 104 = **1.04 mg**, but because of the adult maximum, the correct initial IM dose is **0.5 mg**, not 1 mg.

Repeated use of a **0.3 mg auto-injector** is clinically important underdosing because it delivers only **60% of the recommended adult dose** for a patient of this size. In other words, it is **40% below the guideline dose** of 0.5 mg. As the case report emphasizes, auto-injectors containing 0.3 mg are common but may be **potentially critical underdosing in adults over 50 kg**, and insufficient initial dosing can contribute to **incomplete reversal of vasodilation, bronchospasm, and hypotension**, increasing the likelihood that additional epinephrine doses or escalation of care will be needed—as occurred in this patient’s multiphasic course.
