Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What muscle biopsy findings are most characteristic of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, and how do necrotic and regenerating fibers with macrophage-predominant myophagocytosis and sparse lymphocytic infiltrates help distinguish it from other inflammatory myopathies?

The muscle biopsy most characteristic of immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) shows **dispersed necrotic and regenerating myofibers with prominent myophagocytosis by macrophages and only sparse lymphocytic inflammation**. In the case you provided, the biopsy of the vastus lateralis demonstrated exactly this pattern: **necrotic and regenerating fibers, CD68-positive macrophages engulfing damaged fibers, membrane attack complex staining on necrotic and non-necrotic fibers, and only rare CD3-positive T cells**.

This histology is important because IMNM is defined by **myofiber injury out of proportion to inflammation**. The dominant macrophage infiltrate reflects cleanup of necrotic fibers rather than a dense T-cell–mediated attack. That helps distinguish IMNM from other inflammatory myopathies:

- **Polymyositis** typically shows **endomyosial CD8-positive T cells invading non-necrotic fibers**.
- **Dermatomyositis** more often shows **perivascular/perimysial inflammation, perifascicular atrophy, and capillary injury**, rather than widespread necrosis with minimal lymphocytes.
- **Inclusion body myositis** has **endomysial inflammation plus rimmed vacuoles and chronic degenerative changes**.

So, in practice, a biopsy with **necrosis and regeneration, macrophage-predominant myophagocytosis, and scant lymphocytes** strongly points to IMNM, especially when paired with proximal weakness and marked CK elevation, as in this case.
