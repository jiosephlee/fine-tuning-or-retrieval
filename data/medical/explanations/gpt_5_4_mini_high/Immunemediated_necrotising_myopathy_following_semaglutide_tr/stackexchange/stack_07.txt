Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] What initial treatment strategy is appropriate for severe immune-mediated necrotising myopathy presenting with marked proximal weakness and bulbar symptoms, and what is the rationale for using pulse methylprednisolone followed by high-dose prednisone and IVIG in this case?

In **severe immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM)** with **profound proximal weakness and bulbar involvement** such as dysphagia and dysphonia, the appropriate initial strategy is **prompt, aggressive immunosuppression** rather than watchful waiting. In practice, that means:

1. **Pulse IV methylprednisolone** for rapid disease control,  
2. Transition to **high-dose oral prednisone** (about **1 mg/kg/day**) to maintain suppression, and  
3. **Early IVIG** (typically **2 g/kg over 2–5 days**) when weakness is severe, bulbar function is impaired, or a rapid response is needed.

The rationale is that IMNM is an **immune-driven necrotizing myopathy**, and in severe presentations the priority is to **halt ongoing muscle injury quickly** and prevent aspiration, respiratory decline, and further functional loss. **Pulse steroids** provide immediate anti-inflammatory effect; **oral prednisone** sustains that effect after the pulse. **IVIG** is often added early because severe IMNM may respond incompletely or too slowly to glucocorticoids alone, and IVIG can produce a **faster clinical and CK improvement**, especially when there is marked weakness or swallowing difficulty.

That is exactly the logic in this case: the patient had **rapidly progressive, disabling proximal weakness with dysphagia/dysphonia and CK in the thousands**, so she was treated with **IV methylprednisolone followed by high-dose prednisone plus IVIG**, with subsequent improvement in strength and normalization of CK.
