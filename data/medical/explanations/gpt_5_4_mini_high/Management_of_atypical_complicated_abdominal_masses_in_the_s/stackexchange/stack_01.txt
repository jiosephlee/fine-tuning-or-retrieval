Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Clinical Pearls] In a 21-week pregnancy with severe abdominal pain and dyspnoea from large abdominopelvic masses, what diagnostic modalities are considered safe and most useful, and what additional information can bimanual examination and non-contrast MRI provide beyond ultrasound?

In a symptomatic 21-week pregnancy, the safest and most useful diagnostic workup is a careful pelvic examination followed by ultrasonography with color Doppler, and, if the anatomy remains unclear, non-contrast MRI. These modalities are preferred because they avoid ionizing radiation; gadolinium should be avoided in pregnancy unless there is a compelling indication. In the case report, ultrasound was the key first test, and MRI without contrast was used to refine the diagnosis when the masses were large and their origin remained uncertain.

Bimanual examination adds bedside anatomic information that ultrasound may not fully capture in a very large gravid abdomen: it can show whether a mass is fixed or mobile, its consistency, and whether it is bulging into the posterior fornix or filling the lesser pelvis, as in this patient. Non-contrast MRI then adds the most useful detail for operative planning: it better defines whether the mass is ovarian versus uterine in origin, whether it is continuous with a leiomyoma or pedicle, the thickness of the wall, internal cystic degeneration versus solid components, the relationship to the uterine cavity and adjacent organs, and whether there is associated adenopathy. In this case, MRI helped show that the caudal lesion was in continuity with a posterior myoma and characterized the complex features of the larger mass, thereby guiding management and delivery planning.
