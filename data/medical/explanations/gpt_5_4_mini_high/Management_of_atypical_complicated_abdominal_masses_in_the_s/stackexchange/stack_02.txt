Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] When ultrasound in pregnancy shows two large unilocular cystic masses with regular margins and peripheral vascularisation, what differential diagnoses should be prioritized, and which imaging or anatomic clues would favor cystic-degenerating leiomyoma over decidualised endometrioma or primary ovarian neoplasm?

In pregnancy, a large, unilocular cystic mass with smooth margins and peripheral vascularity should first raise concern for a benign pregnancy-related mimic of malignancy, especially **decidualized endometrioma** and **cystic-degenerating leiomyoma**, while still keeping a **primary ovarian neoplasm** in the differential.

The key distinction is **anatomic origin**. A **cystic-degenerating leiomyoma** is favored when imaging shows:
- **Continuity with the uterus** or with a known intramural/subserosal myoma
- A **pedicle, stalk, or broad uterine attachment**
- **Bridging or tumor-feeding vessels** arising from the uterus
- A mass that is **fixed to the posterior uterine wall/torus** rather than clearly ovarian
- On MRI, **thick walls**, **heterogeneous T1/T2 signal**, and **solid components** within a lesion that tracks with a uterine myoma

By contrast, a **decidualized endometrioma** is more likely when the lesion is clearly **ovarian in origin** and shows the classic endometrioma background, with **mural nodules** that may enhance in pregnancy. A **primary ovarian neoplasm** becomes more concerning if there is **no uterine continuity**, more complex solid-papillary architecture, ascites, adenopathy, or progressive malignant features.

In the case report, the strongest clue against an ovarian tumor was the **caudal mass’s continuity with a posterior intramural-subserosal leiomyoma**, and the upper lesion was ultimately found at surgery to be a **pedunculated, highly vascularized uterine leiomyoma**. Mild CA-125 elevation did not distinguish the entities, because CA-125 is nonspecific in pregnancy.

So, in practice: **prioritize leiomyoma versus decidualized endometrioma first, and use uterine continuity/pedicle/feeding vessels and MRI correlation to favor cystic-degenerating leiomyoma over ovarian disease.**
