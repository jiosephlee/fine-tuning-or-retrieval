Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] What factors would make an open longitudinal laparotomy preferable to laparoscopy for management of a symptomatic mass in the second trimester of pregnancy?

An open longitudinal laparotomy is preferable to laparoscopy when the mass is **too large, too complex, or too poorly accessible for safe minimally invasive surgery** in the setting of a gravid uterus. In the case report, the decision for a xipho-pubic incision was driven by several factors that favor open exposure:

- **Giant size and mass effect**: the upper lesion was 18 cm and the pregnancy itself had already reduced intra-abdominal working space, making trocar placement and safe dissection difficult.
- **Multiple lesions**: there were two large masses, increasing operative complexity.
- **Critical location**: one mass extended into the left subphrenic space and the other was fixed in the pelvis/posterior uterus, where laparoscopic access and specimen control are harder.
- **Intramural or poorly separable origin / unclear anatomy**: imaging could not clearly define the cleavage plane, and the caudal mass appeared contiguous with the posterior uterine wall.
- **High vascularity and mixed solid-cystic features**: these raise concern for bleeding and make laparoscopic hemostasis more challenging.
- **Acute symptoms or complications**: worsening pain, dyspnea, and concern for torsion, rupture, infection, or necrosis justify a more definitive approach.
- **Need for diagnostic certainty**: when the origin is uncertain and there is any oncologic concern, open surgery allows better inspection and control.

By contrast, laparoscopy is generally better reserved for **smaller, more accessible, non-intramural masses**, especially earlier in the second trimester. In this case, the combination of **size, location, vascularity, symptoms, and uncertain anatomy** made open longitudinal laparotomy the safer and more controlled option.
