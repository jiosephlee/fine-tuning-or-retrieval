Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] During non-obstetric abdominal surgery in pregnancy, what intraoperative and perioperative measures should be taken to reduce maternal and fetal risk, including fetal assessment and obstetric precautions?

In non-obstetric abdominal surgery during pregnancy, the fetus is best protected by protecting the mother. That means careful preoperative multidisciplinary planning with obstetrics, anesthesia, and surgery; confirmation of gestational age, placental location, and fetal viability by ultrasound; and optimization of maternal problems such as anemia or infection before the operation. In the reported case, the team used ultrasound and non-contrast MRI to define anatomy, then proceeded only because the masses were symptomatic and potentially complicated.

Intraoperatively, the goals are to maintain uteroplacental perfusion and minimize uterine stimulation: avoid maternal hypoxia, hypotension, and aortocaval compression; use positioning that reduces diaphragmatic and vena caval compression; keep the procedure as short and blood-sparing as possible; and handle the uterus and adnexa as gently as possible. Fetal cardiac activity should be documented before incision and again after surgery, and if the fetus is viable, intermittent or continuous intraoperative monitoring may be used when technically feasible and when an emergency obstetric response is possible. Obstetric backup should be immediately available for preterm labor, fetal distress, or emergent delivery. Postoperatively, the patient needs close surveillance for contractions, bleeding, rupture of membranes, and fetal growth; tocolytic or progestational support may be considered when uterine irritability is a concern. In this case, the surgeons confirmed fetal cardiac activity during laparotomy, limited the procedure to the symptomatic pedunculated myoma to preserve the pregnancy, and then followed the pregnancy closely to a planned cesarean delivery because the residual pelvic mass would have obstructed labor.
