Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] How would you acutely manage dermatomyositis presenting with marked cervical swelling, severe pharyngeal dysphagia, and concern for airway muscle involvement?

Acute management is to treat this as **severe dermatomyositis with potential airway threat**, not as histaminergic or bradykinin-mediated angioedema.

First, the patient should be **admitted to a monitored setting** with **urgent ENT/anesthesia involvement**. Flexible nasendoscopy is appropriate to assess the upper airway, but if there is **stridor, drooling, hypoxemia, rapidly progressive swelling, or inability to handle secretions**, the airway should be secured early rather than waiting for further evolution. In this case, the endoscopy showed no fixed obstruction, but there was substantial secretion burden and coughing, consistent with bulbar involvement.

Second, because the dysphagia is severe, the patient should be made **NPO** and protected from aspiration. If swallowing is unsafe, provide **enteral nutrition via nasogastric tube**; that was necessary in this case, and it is often the safest bridge while inflammation improves.

Third, the key disease-directed therapy is **prompt high-dose glucocorticoids**. For severe myositis with cervical and pharyngeal involvement, this case appropriately used **intravenous methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 3 days**, then a **high-dose oral prednisone taper**.

Fourth, in marked bulbar disease or when a rapid response is needed, I would add **early IVIG**; in this case, **2 g/kg** was given over 5 days, with subsequent monthly cycles. That is a reasonable choice when dysphagia and airway muscle involvement are prominent.

Finally, once the patient is stabilized, add a **steroid-sparing immunosuppressant** such as **mycophenolate mofetil**; rituximab is reasonable for severe or refractory disease, as was done here. Supportive measures should include aspiration precautions, PJP prophylaxis if prolonged high-dose immunosuppression is planned, and malignancy screening.

The important pearl from this case is that **marked neck swelling in dermatomyositis can be inflammatory pseudoangioedema from myositis**, and the treatment is **rapid immunosuppression plus airway and swallowing support**, not antihistamines alone.
