Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] What clinical phenotype is associated with anti-SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis, and which complications or associated conditions are especially important to anticipate in these patients?

Anti-SAE1 antibody–positive dermatomyositis most often has a **cutaneous-predominant phenotype**: patients commonly develop the typical dermatomyositis rash, including **heliotrope eruption, Gottron’s papules, V-neck/shawl sign, facial erythema, and periungual/nailfold changes**. In this case, the disease presented atypically with **marked inflammatory neck swelling (“pseudoangioedema”)** and only subtle early weakness, but the underlying process was still classic dermatomyositis with severe myositis and dysphagia.

The key complications and associated conditions to anticipate are **bulbar dysfunction and aspiration**, **interstitial lung disease**, and **malignancy**. In the review cited in the report, **dysphagia occurred in about 46%**, **interstitial lung disease in 43%**, and **malignancy in 18%** of SAE1-positive cases. Thus, these patients warrant close attention to swallowing/airway safety, pulmonary evaluation, and **systematic cancer screening with ongoing surveillance**.
