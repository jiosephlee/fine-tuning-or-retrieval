Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the proposed pathophysiologic basis of 'pseudoangioedema' in dermatomyositis, and how does it differ mechanistically from histaminergic and bradykinin-mediated angioedema?

In dermatomyositis, “pseudoangioedema” is thought to be **inflammatory soft-tissue edema from active myositis and dermatomyositis-associated microangiopathy**, not true angioedema. The swelling reflects **capillary/endothelial injury with leakage into affected muscle and surrounding tissues**, likely driven by the same complement-mediated vascular injury seen in dermatomyositis; in this case, the markedly elevated CK, MRI/CT evidence of strap-muscle and retropharyngeal edema, and the biopsy showing **perifascicular C5b-9 deposition** all support that mechanism.

This differs from:
- **Histaminergic angioedema**: mast-cell degranulation with histamine release, causing rapid vasodilation and increased permeability, often with urticaria/pruritus and response to antihistamines, epinephrine, and steroids.
- **Bradykinin-mediated angioedema**: excess bradykinin from C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency or ACE-inhibitor effect, producing vascular permeability without urticaria, and typically not responsive to antihistamines or epinephrine.

So, the key distinction is that dermatomyositis-related “pseudoangioedema” is **myositis-driven inflammatory edema**, whereas true angioedema is **mediator-driven vascular leak**.
