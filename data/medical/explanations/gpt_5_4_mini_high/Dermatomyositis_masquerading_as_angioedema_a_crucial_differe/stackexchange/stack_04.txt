Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] Which combination of laboratory, imaging, and pathology findings in this case most strongly supports the diagnosis of dermatomyositis, and which muscle-biopsy feature helps distinguish dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies?

The combination that most strongly supports dermatomyositis in this case is the **markedly elevated creatine kinase** with accompanying muscle enzyme elevation, the **MRI and FDG-PET evidence of active, multifocal skeletal muscle inflammation**, and the **myositis-serology positivity for anti-SAE1 antibody** in the setting of compatible clinical findings. In other words, the very high CK of 16,184 U/L, MRI showing active myositis in the pelvic and proximal thigh muscles, PET showing diffuse FDG uptake in multiple muscle groups, and a muscle biopsy with a classic dermatomyositis pattern together make the diagnosis highly convincing.

The biopsy feature that best distinguishes dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies is **perifascicular pathology with complement-mediated microangiopathy**—here, **perifascicular myofiber degeneration/atrophy with C5b-9 (membrane attack complex) deposition in perifascicular endomysial capillaries**. That capillary complement deposition is a classic hallmark of dermatomyositis and helps separate it from immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy, which is more diffusely necrotizing, and from inclusion body myositis, which has a different pattern of endomysial inflammation and rimmed vacuoles.
