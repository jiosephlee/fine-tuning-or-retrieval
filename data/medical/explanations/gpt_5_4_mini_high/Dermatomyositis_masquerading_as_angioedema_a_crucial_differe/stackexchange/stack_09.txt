Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] This patient was considered high risk for occult malignancy. Which features placed him in a high-risk cancer-screening group, and what baseline and interval malignancy evaluation is appropriate in dermatomyositis of this type?

He was considered **high risk for occult cancer because his dermatomyositis was accompanied by dysphagia**, which the IMACS guideline treats as a **high-risk feature**. In addition, he had **anti-SAE1 positivity** and **male sex**, both of which are **intermediate-risk features**; taken together with dermatomyositis itself, this moved him into the high-risk screening group. In the case report, this is exactly how the team framed his risk.

For **baseline malignancy evaluation** in this form of dermatomyositis, the appropriate approach is **enhanced screening at diagnosis**: careful history and physical examination, routine laboratory studies, and **age- and sex-appropriate cancer screening**, with **cross-sectional imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis**; in higher-risk patients, **FDG-PET/CT** is reasonable if conventional evaluation is unrevealing or suspicion remains high. The workup in the case—CT imaging, tumor markers, PET scan, and planned gastroscopy/colonoscopy—fits this strategy.

If the baseline evaluation is negative, the patient should undergo **interval surveillance for at least 3 years**, typically **annual reassessment** with symptom review, examination, routine blood tests, and repeat imaging or endoscopy **guided by symptoms, prior abnormalities, and age-appropriate screening needs**.
