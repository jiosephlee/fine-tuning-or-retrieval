Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 1: ## Clinical Presentation and the Evolution of Prosthetic Valve Failure

The patient in this case is a man in his late 70s with a complex medical profile that significantly influenced the selection of transcatheter interventions over surgical options. His history is marked by extensive cardiovascular and systemic comorbidities, including three-vessel coronary artery disease—requiring multiple percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI) between 2009 and 2014—arterial hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Additionally, he had developed a third-degree atrioventricular block, which necessitated the implantation of a pacemaker in 2014. For a clinician, these factors are critical because they contribute to a high surgical risk profile, making the patient a candidate for transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) rather than conventional open-heart surgery.

The patient's history of prosthetic valve failure began in 2014, when progressive degeneration of the native aortic valve led to severe stenosis. Due to the aforementioned comorbidities, he underwent his first TAVI with the implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve. While the procedure initially restored valve function, the patient eventually experienced structural valve deterioration (SVD). SVD is a common long-term complication of bioprosthetic valves, characterized by mechanical wear, calcification, or leaflet fibrosis, which gradually narrows the valve orifice and impairs hemodynamic performance.

By late 2021 and early 2022, the clinical manifestation of this deterioration became evident. The patient presented with classic symptoms of severe aortic stenosis: exertional dyspnea, dizziness, and fatigue. In elderly patients, these symptoms often reflect the heart's inability to increase cardiac output during physical activity. The hemodynamic markers of this failure were profound; echocardiography revealed a mean transvalvular gradient that had risen to 83 mmHg, indicating severe obstruction to left ventricular outflow. This chronic pressure overload led to a decline in the left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) from 54% to 42%, signaling the onset of heart failure secondary to the failing prosthesis.

To address this failure, a TAV-in-TAV procedure was performed in May 2022, where a 23 mm Evolut valve was implanted inside the existing Lotus valve. This strategy is designed to treat a failed transcatheter prosthesis by deploying a new valve frame within the old one, thereby restoring a functional orifice without the need for surgical explantation. Following this second intervention, the patient's hemodynamic status improved, as evidenced by the recovery of the EF to 54%. This sequence of events illustrates the iterative nature of managing structural valve disease in high-risk patients, where each subsequent failure requires a careful balance of anatomical feasibility and hemodynamic optimization.
