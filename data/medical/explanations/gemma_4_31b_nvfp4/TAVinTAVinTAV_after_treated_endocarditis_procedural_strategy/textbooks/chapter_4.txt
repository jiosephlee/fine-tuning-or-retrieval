Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

Chapter 4: ## Technical Execution of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV Procedure

Performing a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) presents significant technical hurdles, primarily due to the "triple-stented" anatomy. With each successive valve, the cumulative thickness of the prosthetic frames increases, which can severely restrict the effective orifice area (EOA) and obstruct access to the coronary arteries. In this case, the procedural strategy was meticulously planned using multislice CT (MSCT) to optimize hemodynamic performance and ensure patient safety.

The selection of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis was a critical technical decision. In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV scenario, the available lumen is markedly reduced—in this patient, the minimum lumen diameter within the existing Evolut prosthesis was only 13.7 mm. The Acurate neo2 was chosen for its supra-annular leaflet positioning and relatively low frame height, which are advantageous in narrow anatomies to maintain a more favorable hemodynamic profile and reduce the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A central component of the procedural strategy was the "high implantation" technique. Rather than seating the third valve deep within the previous frames, the team positioned the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets. The goal of this approach is twofold: first, by pressing the pre-existing leaflets outward against the stent frame, the team prevented the leaflets from folding inward, which would have further decreased the EOA. Second, high implantation minimizes the creation of a "neo-skirt"—the accumulation of displaced tissue and prosthetic material at the base of the valve—below the coronary ostia, thereby reducing the risk of coronary obstruction.

To preserve future coronary access, the team focused on commissural alignment. With three overlapping layers of metallic mesh, future percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI) become exponentially more difficult. By aligning the commissures of the Acurate neo2 with those of the previous Evolut valve—guided by pre-procedural CT modeling and fluoroscopic landmarks—the operators attempted to maintain "windows" of lower metal density to facilitate future catheter access to the coronary arteries.

The intraprocedural execution involved several key maneuvers to ensure stability and function. After establishing access, balloon pre-dilation of the existing prosthetic complex was performed using a 22/40 mm catheter under rapid ventricular pacing (180 bpm). This prepared the site for the third valve. Following the deployment of the Acurate neo2, high-pressure post-dilation with a 20/40 mm balloon was utilized. This step was essential to optimize the expansion of the new valve and likely contributed to the radial expansion of the underlying Evolut frame, improving overall leaflet motion and central flow dynamics.

Notably, the procedure was performed without the use of coronary protection (such as stents or guidewires in the coronary ostia). This decision was based on the pre-procedural MSCT, which provided precise measurements of the coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, and the valve-to-coronary distance. The CT modelling confirmed that the planned high implantation strategy provided sufficient clearance of the coronary ostia, rendering the risks of coronary obstruction low enough to forgo protection.
