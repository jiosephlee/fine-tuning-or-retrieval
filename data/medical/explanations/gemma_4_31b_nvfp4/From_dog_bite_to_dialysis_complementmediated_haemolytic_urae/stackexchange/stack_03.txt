Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] When evaluating a patient with suspected thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), how is the measurement of ADAMTS13 activity used to differentiate Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) from other forms of TMA, such as complement-mediated HUS (CM-HUS)?

In the evaluation of a patient presenting with thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA)—characterized by the triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and organ dysfunction—the measurement of ADAMTS13 activity is the critical diagnostic step to differentiate thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) from other forms of TMA, such as complement-mediated hemolytic uremic syndrome (CM-HUS).

**Clinical Reasoning and Pathophysiology**
TTP is caused by a severe deficiency of the enzyme ADAMTS13, a von Willebrand factor (vWF)-cleaving protease. Without sufficient ADAMTS13 activity, ultra-large vWF multimers persist in the circulation, leading to spontaneous platelet aggregation and the formation of microthrombi. A deficiency of ADAMTS13 activity to **less than 10%** is the diagnostic hallmark of TTP.

In contrast, CM-HUS is driven by dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway. Endothelial damage is caused by the uncontrolled formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9) rather than vWF-mediated platelet aggregation. Consequently, patients with CM-HUS typically have normal or near-normal ADAMTS13 activity.

**Application to the Case**
In this case, the patient presented with a complex clinical course starting with *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* sepsis and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), which later evolved into a distinct TMA (schistocytosis, persistent thrombocytopenia, and anuria). 

The measurement of ADAMTS13 activity was pivotal: the result was **43.5%**. Because this value is well above the <10% threshold, TTP was effectively ruled out. This result allowed the clinical team to shift the differential diagnosis toward other TMAs. By further ruling out Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (via negative stool PCR) and confirming fibrin thrombi on renal biopsy, the diagnosis of CM-HUS was established.

**Management Implications**
The differentiation is not merely academic; it dictates urgent therapeutic choices. Plasma exchange (PEX) is the life-saving standard of care for TTP as it replaces the missing ADAMTS13 enzyme. While PEX was used as bridging therapy in this patient, it provided only transient stabilization. The definitive treatment for CM-HUS is complement blockade with eculizumab (a monoclonal antibody targeting C5). An accurate ADAMTS13 measurement ensures that the patient is transitioned from the general supportive measures of PEX to the targeted therapy required for complement-mediated disease.
