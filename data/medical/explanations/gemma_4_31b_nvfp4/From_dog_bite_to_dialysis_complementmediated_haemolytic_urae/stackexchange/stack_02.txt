Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] In the early phase of sepsis, the patient exhibited disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). How does the subsequent development of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) and persistent thrombocytopenia after the resolution of the initial septic phase shift the differential diagnosis?

In this case, the transition from a clinical picture of disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) to one of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) and persistent thrombocytopenia—occurring *after* the resolution of the systemic inflammatory response—represents a critical diagnostic pivot. It shifts the differential diagnosis from a **consumptive coagulopathy secondary to sepsis** to a **primary thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA).**

During the early phase of sepsis, the patient exhibited the hallmarks of DIC: pancytopenia, acute kidney injury (AKI), and coagulopathy. In DIC, the activation of the coagulation cascade is systemic and driven by the inflammatory milieu of sepsis; typically, once the triggering infection is controlled and the inflammatory profile improves, the coagulopathy should resolve. 

The "red flag" in this case occurred when the patient’s coagulation parameters normalized and the inflammatory markers improved, yet she developed a sudden drop in hemoglobin, undetectable haptoglobin, rising LDH, and 15%–20% schistocytes on the peripheral smear. The persistence of thrombocytopenia and the emergence of MAHA in the absence of active sepsis indicate that the pathology is no longer a global consumption of clotting factors, but rather a localized process of endothelial damage and microvascular thrombosis.

This clinical shift narrows the differential to the three primary TMAs:
1. **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP):** Ruled out in this patient by an ADAMTS13 activity of 43.5% (typically $<10\%$ in TTP).
2. **Shiga Toxin-Producing E. coli HUS (STEC-HUS):** Ruled out via negative stool PCR and the absence of a prodromal diarrheal illness.
3. **Complement-Mediated HUS (CM-HUS):** This became the most likely diagnosis, supported by the kidney biopsy (fibrin thrombi in ischemic glomeruli) and the dramatic response to eculizumab.

This case illustrates a sophisticated diagnostic challenge: the early "noise" of sepsis-induced DIC can obscure the onset of a TMA. When MAHA and thrombocytopenia persist or emerge after the septic phase has resolved, clinicians must look beyond the initial diagnosis of DIC and investigate for an underlying complement dysregulation or ADAMTS13 deficiency, especially when triggered by fastidious pathogens like *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*.
