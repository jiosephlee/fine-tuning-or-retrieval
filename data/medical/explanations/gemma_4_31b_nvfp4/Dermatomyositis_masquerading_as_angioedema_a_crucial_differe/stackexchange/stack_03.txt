Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] In the setting of suspected inflammatory myopathy, the muscle biopsy for this patient showed C5b-9 deposition in perifascicular endomysial capillaries. What is the pathophysiologic significance of this finding, and how does it help differentiate dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies?

The deposition of **C5b-9** (the membrane attack complex) in the perifascicular endomysial capillaries is a hallmark of the pathophysiology of **dermatomyositis (DM)**. 

From a pathophysiologic standpoint, this finding signifies that dermatomyositis is primarily a **complement-mediated microangiopathy** rather than a primary muscle disease. The activation of the complement cascade leads to the formation of the membrane attack complex on the walls of the endomysial capillaries, resulting in capillary destruction, microvascular ischemia, and subsequent myofiber necrosis. Because the blood supply to a muscle fascicle is most precarious at its periphery, the resulting ischemia and necrosis occur in a characteristic **perifascicular distribution**, as seen in this patient's biopsy.

This finding is critical for differentiating dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies:

*   **Dermatomyositis:** The pathology is characterized by a humoral-mediated attack on the vasculature. The presence of C5b-9 deposition and perifascicular atrophy/necrosis distinguishes it as a vasculopathy.
*   **Polymyositis:** In contrast, polymyositis is primarily a cell-mediated process. It is characterized by endomysial inflammatory infiltrates (predominantly CD8+ T cells) that directly invade non-necrotic muscle fibers, without the primary microvascular complement deposition seen here.
*   **Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM):** While IMNM presents with prominent myofiber necrosis and regeneration (similar to the necrotic fibers seen in this patient), it typically lacks the specific perifascicular distribution and the characteristic complement-mediated capillary damage.

In this case, the combination of significantly elevated creatine kinase, anti-SAE1 antibodies, and the biopsy finding of C5b-9 deposition in perifascicular capillaries confirms the diagnosis of dermatomyositis and explains the systemic nature of the patient's presentation, including the atypical "pseudoangioedema" caused by profound muscle inflammation and edema.
