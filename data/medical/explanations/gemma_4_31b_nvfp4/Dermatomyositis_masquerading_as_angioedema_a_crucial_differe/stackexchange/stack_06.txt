Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] The medical team chose to use mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab as steroid-sparing agents but specifically avoided methotrexate. Based on the patient's pulmonary function tests and CT chest findings, what was the clinical reasoning for avoiding methotrexate in this case?

The decision to avoid methotrexate in this patient was based on the presence of baseline pulmonary dysfunction, which increases the risk of drug-induced pulmonary toxicity.

Methotrexate is a cornerstone steroid-sparing agent in the management of dermatomyositis; however, it is associated with a well-documented risk of methotrexate-induced pneumonitis, a hypersensitivity reaction that can lead to interstitial lung disease (ILD) and permanent pulmonary fibrosis. In clinical practice, the presence of pre-existing lung disease or abnormal pulmonary function tests (PFTs) is often considered a relative contraindication or a factor necessitating extreme caution, as these patients may be more susceptible to pulmonary toxicity or may have their existing respiratory compromise exacerbated.

In this specific case, the patient's pulmonary function tests demonstrated an extrathoracic restrictive pattern. Furthermore, his CT chest revealed multiple non-cavitating pulmonary nodules and patchy peribronchial consolidation. While the consolidation was attributed to aspiration pneumonitis secondary to severe pharyngeal dysphagia, the combination of a restrictive pattern on PFTs and parenchymal abnormalities on imaging indicated that the patient already had compromised pulmonary reserve. 

To minimize the risk of precipitating a severe drug-induced pneumonitis or worsening his restrictive lung disease, the medical team opted for alternative immunosuppressants—mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab—which provide effective steroid-sparing activity without the same specific risk profile for methotrexate-induced pulmonary fibrosis.
