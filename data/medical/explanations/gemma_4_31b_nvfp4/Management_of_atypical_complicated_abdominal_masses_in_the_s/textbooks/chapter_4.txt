Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 4: ## Postoperative Care and Obstetric Follow-up

Following the surgical excision of the upper pedunculated leiomyoma, postoperative management focused on stabilizing the patient's hematologic and infectious status while prioritizing the maintenance of the pregnancy. The patient experienced a postoperative drop in hemoglobin (Hb 7.5 g/L) and hematocrit (24.3%), compounding a pre-existing iron deficiency. To restore maternal oxygen-carrying capacity and minimize fetal risk, the clinical team utilized a combination of intravenous iron supplementation (ferric carboxymaltose 1 g) and two blood transfusions, which successfully stabilized her hemoglobin at 10.0 g/L. Simultaneously, the *Klebsiella pneumoniae* urinary tract infection identified preoperatively was treated with a targeted regimen of intravenous cefazolin and clarithromycin, guided by the culture's antibiogram.

To mitigate the risk of preterm labor or uterine irritability following major uterine surgery during the second trimester, the patient received prophylactic progesterone. This began intraoperatively with 100 mg intramuscular (IM) doses, continuing daily until discharge. Long-term pregnancy maintenance was then managed with intramuscular caproate hydroxyprogesterone (341 mg every three days) until delivery. Such pharmacological support is critical in high-risk scenarios where surgical manipulation of the uterus may trigger premature uterine contractions.

Obstetric follow-up was intensified, with evaluations occurring every three weeks to monitor fetal viability, the amniotic fluid index, and fetal growth. Despite the presence of a large residual caudal mass, the fetus showed healthy development, reaching the 86th percentile on the WHO fetal growth chart. This rigorous monitoring was essential to ensure that the residual uterine mass did not compromise placental perfusion or fetal development as the pregnancy progressed into the third trimester.

The final stage of management involved determining the safest mode of delivery. Although vaginal delivery is generally preferred, the residual caudal leiomyoma—located in the pelvis and originating from the uterine torus—presented a significant mechanical obstruction. The multidisciplinary team concluded that this mass would likely impede the progression of the fetal head during the expulsive phase of labor, increasing the risk of obstructed labor and fetal distress. Consequently, an elective caesarean section was performed at 37 weeks of gestation. By utilizing the existing surgical scar from the previous laparotomy, the surgical team minimized further uterine trauma, resulting in an excellent maternal and neonatal outcome.
