Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] The patient in this case initially received 0.3 mg of epinephrine via an auto-injector. For an adult weighing over 50 kg, what is the standard recommended dose, and what clinical evidence suggests that the 0.3 mg dose may be suboptimal?

For an adult weighing over 50 kg, the standard recommended dose of epinephrine for the treatment of anaphylaxis is **0.5 mg administered intramuscularly**. While guidelines specify a weight-based dose of 0.01 mg/kg, this is typically simplified to 0.5 mg for the majority of the adult population.

In the presented case, the patient weighed 104 kg, yet she initially received only 0.3 mg of epinephrine via an auto-injector. This represents a significant underdose based on current guidelines. The clinical evidence suggesting that a 0.3 mg dose may be suboptimal is highlighted by a retrospective study (referenced as 2025 in the report) which found that patients receiving an initial dose of 0.3 mg required escalation of care—such as additional epinephrine injections—significantly more often than those who received the recommended 0.5 mg dose.

This discrepancy often arises because many commercially available epinephrine auto-injectors are standardized at 0.3 mg, creating a gap between readily available delivery devices and evidence-based dosing guidelines. In this patient’s multiphasic course, it is noteworthy that the first two episodes were treated with 0.3 mg doses, while the subsequent two episodes were treated with the full 0.5 mg dose. This underscores the importance of adhering to the 0.5 mg standard in adults to maximize the initial therapeutic response and potentially reduce the need for subsequent dosing.
