Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] When transitioning this patient to fondaparinux, the team administered supplemental antithrombin (AT) concentrate. What is the mechanistic reason for providing antithrombin when using fondaparinux in the setting of HIT?

Fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide that acts as an **indirect inhibitor of factor Xa**. Unlike direct thrombin inhibitors (such as argatroban) or direct factor Xa inhibitors (such as apixaban), fondaparinux does not inhibit its target directly; instead, it binds selectively to **antithrombin (AT)**, inducing a conformational change that dramatically accelerates the rate at which antithrombin neutralizes factor Xa.

Because antithrombin is a mandatory cofactor for the drug's activity, the efficacy of fondaparinux is directly dependent on the patient's available AT levels. In the setting of severe heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), and particularly **autoimmune HIT (aHIT)**, patients can develop a profound consumptive coagulopathy. This is characterized by the massive activation of the coagulation cascade, which can lead to the depletion of endogenous antithrombin.

In this specific case, the patient demonstrated clear signs of severe hypercoagulability and consumptive coagulopathy, including falling fibrinogen levels, rising d-dimer levels, and the presence of schistocytes. His pre-infusion antithrombin level was mildly reduced (0.70 U/mL). If fondaparinux were administered in the presence of deficient antithrombin, the drug would be unable to exert its anticoagulant effect, leaving the patient unprotected against further thrombotic events—a critical concern given his recent cerebral infarct. 

Therefore, supplemental antithrombin concentrate was administered to ensure sufficient cofactor availability, thereby guaranteeing that fondaparinux could effectively inhibit factor Xa and control the patient's hypercoagulable state.
