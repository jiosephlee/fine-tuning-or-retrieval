Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] The patient developed indicators of HIT-associated consumptive coagulopathy despite receiving argatroban. Which specific laboratory trends in this case suggested a failure of anticoagulant control and the need for a change in therapy?

In this case, the transition from argatroban to fondaparinux was prompted by laboratory evidence of a **consumptive coagulopathy**, indicating that the hypercoagulable state associated with autoimmune HIT (aHIT) was not adequately controlled.

The specific laboratory trends that signaled this failure of anticoagulant control were:

*   **Falling Fibrinogen Levels:** A progressive decrease in fibrinogen from postoperative day (POD) 6 to POD 17 (reaching a nadir of 2.49 g/L). While fibrinogen is typically elevated postoperatively as an acute-phase reactant, a downward trend in the setting of HIT suggests massive consumption due to widespread thrombin generation.
*   **Rising D-dimer Levels:** D-dimer levels increased, peaking around POD 12. This indicates active, ongoing fibrin formation and subsequent degradation, characteristic of a prothrombotic state.
*   **Appearance of Schistocytes:** The peripheral blood film showed red cell fragments (schistocytes) from POD 9 to POD 16. This is a critical finding signifying microangiopathic hemolytic anemia secondary to the deposition of fibrin thrombi in the microvasculature.

These three markers—falling fibrinogen, rising D-dimer, and schistocytosis—collectively represent a "consumptive" pattern. In a patient with aHIT, this suggests that thrombin generation is so profound that it overcomes the pharmacological blockade of a direct thrombin inhibitor like argatroban. This clinical picture, combined with a poor platelet response to high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg), indicated a severe phenotype of aHIT requiring a change in the anticoagulation strategy to a more potent or different mechanism of action (in this case, the factor Xa inhibitor fondaparinux) to arrest the coagulopathy.
