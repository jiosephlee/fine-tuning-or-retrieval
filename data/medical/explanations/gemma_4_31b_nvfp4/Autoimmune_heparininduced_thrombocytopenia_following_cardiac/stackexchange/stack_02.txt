Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Morning Report] The typical temporal window for the onset of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) is postoperative day 5 to 10. In this case, the platelet count began falling on day 1. What clinical factor explains this accelerated onset?

The accelerated onset of thrombocytopenia in this patient is explained by **prior preoperative exposure to unfractionated heparin (UFH)**.

In classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), the typical temporal window of onset (postoperative day 5 to 10) reflects the time required for the immune system to develop IgG antibodies against the platelet factor 4 (PF4)-heparin complex. However, if a patient has been exposed to heparin within the preceding few weeks or months, preformed antibodies may already be present in the circulation. When the patient is re-exposed to heparin—in this case, the high-dose UFH administered during cardiopulmonary bypass—these antibodies trigger immediate platelet activation and consumption.

In this specific case, the patient underwent a diagnostic heart catheterization one week prior to his cardiac surgery, during which he received 2,000 U of UFH. This prior exposure primed the patient's immune system, allowing the HIT-related platelet count decline to begin as early as postoperative day 1, rather than the typical window. This phenomenon occurs in approximately 10% of cardiac surgery patients who develop HIT.
