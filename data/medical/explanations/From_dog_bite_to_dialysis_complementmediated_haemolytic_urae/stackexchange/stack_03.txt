Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Morning Report] In an adult with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury, what laboratory test must be sent urgently to evaluate for thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, and what ADAMTS13 activity threshold essentially rules out TTP?

Send an ADAMTS13 activity assay (functional activity) urgently — ideally with concurrent testing for an ADAMTS13 inhibitor/autoantibody — and draw the sample before plasma exchange or major transfusion if possible. Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10% activity) is the hallmark of immune-mediated TTP; an activity well above that threshold (for practical purposes >10%) essentially rules out TTP and should prompt evaluation for other causes of TMA (eg, HUS/CM‑HUS).

Why: ADAMTS13 is the metalloprotease that cleaves ultra‑large vWF multimers; severe deficiency leads to platelet-rich microthrombi, MAHA and profound thrombocytopenia. The diagnosis is laboratory‑based, so an urgent functional assay is required even when you begin empiric plasma exchange for high clinical suspicion (use PLASMIC score to triage). In the presented case the ADAMTS13 activity was 43.5%, which effectively excluded TTP and helped redirect the workup toward complement‑mediated HUS.
