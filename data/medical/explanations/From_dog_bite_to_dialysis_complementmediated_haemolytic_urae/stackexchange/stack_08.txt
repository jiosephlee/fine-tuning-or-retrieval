Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Morning Report] How do plasma exchange and eculizumab therapy alter the interpretation of complement studies (for example, AH50 and C3/C4), and how should the timing of sampling relative to these therapies influence diagnostic conclusions?

Bottom line
- Both plasma exchange (PEX) and eculizumab profoundly and predictably alter complement assays. If you sample after either therapy, functional assays (CH50/AH50) and antigenic factor levels (C3, C4, factor H/I/B) may reflect treatment effects rather than the patient’s intrinsic complement state. For correct diagnostic interpretation, obtain key complement studies before PEX and before starting eculizumab whenever feasible and always document the timing and replacement fluid used.

Why this happens — pathophysiology and assay mechanics
- PEX: removes circulating complement proteins, split products and autoantibodies. The effect on assays depends on the replacement fluid:
  - Plasma replacement (FFP/Octaplas) will tend to replete complement proteins and can transiently normalize antigenic levels and functional activity, and can dilute or replace patient autoantibodies.
  - Albumin replacement will dilute and lower complement proteins and functional activity (false low).
  - Repeated PEX can therefore either mask a deficiency/autoantibody (if plasma replacement) or produce artifactual low complement activity (if albumin).
- Eculizumab: is an anti‑C5 monoclonal antibody that blocks terminal pathway cleavage of C5. It abolishes terminal pathway–dependent haemolytic assays (CH50 and AH50) and renders them markedly reduced or undetectable while the drug is present. Antigenic levels of upstream proteins (C3, C4, factor B, factor H/I) are not directly destroyed by eculizumab, but their measured function may be misleading: ongoing consumption upstream may still be present even if terminal activity is suppressed.
  - Practically: a very low/absent AH50 or CH50 after eculizumab is expected and reflects drug effect, not intrinsic terminal pathway failure.

Practical implications and timing (clinical pearls)
- Best practice: draw baseline diagnostic tests before any PEX and before the first dose of eculizumab:
  - Functional assays: CH50 and AH50 (or functional AP/CP assays)
  - Antigenic complement levels: C3, C4, factor H, factor I, factor B
  - Anti–factor H autoantibodies
  - ADAMTS13 (for TTP exclusion) — also before PEX if possible
  - Genetic testing (complement gene sequencing) — not affected by PEX or eculizumab; can be sent any time
- If PEX has already been given:
  - Record number of sessions, timing of last session and the replacement fluid (plasma vs albumin).
  - Be cautious: a normal C3/C4 or normal AH50 after plasma replacement does not exclude a prior deficiency or autoantibody (may be donor-derived). A low AH50 after albumin PEX suggests depletion from the procedure.
  - Anti‑complement autoantibodies (eg, anti–factor H) may be removed by PEX and produce false‑negative results; ideally sample before PEX or repeat testing several days to weeks after the last exchange to allow reappearance.
- If eculizumab has been given:
  - Expect CH50/AH50 to be suppressed for the duration of C5 blockade (measurable for days–weeks after dosing). Do not use CH50/AH50 to assess underlying alternative pathway dysregulation while on eculizumab.
  - A low AH50 shortly after eculizumab (as in this case — AH50 6% measured 4 days after the first eculizumab dose) is most consistent with drug effect.
  - Antigenic assays and genetic testing remain interpretable, but autoantibody testing can still be affected if prior PEX was performed.
- If you must interpret tests obtained after therapy:
  - Low AH50/CH50 + recent eculizumab → interpret as treatment effect unless no drug was given.
  - Low AH50/CH50 + recent PEX with albumin → likely depletion from exchange.
  - Normal complement levels after PEX with plasma replacement → may be donor-driven; pathology can still exist.
  - Autoantibody assays performed after PEX are unreliable for ruling out antibody‑mediated CM‑HUS.

Applying this to the case
- In the reported patient, basic C3/C4 and pathway tests were obtained before the first PEX and were normal. Extended testing (AH50 markedly reduced to 6%) was performed 8 days after the final PEX and 4 days after the first eculizumab dose. That timing explains the finding: the suppressed AH50 is most consistent with eculizumab’s terminal‑pathway blockade (and to a lesser degree the recent PEX), rather than proving intrinsic alternative‑pathway consumption or deficiency. Genetic testing (unaffected) later showed no pathogenic variants; anti–factor H antibodies had been negative, but interpretation of antibody tests performed after PEX must be cautious because PEX can remove such antibodies.

Summary checklist when you suspect complement‑mediated TMA
- Before therapy (ideal): draw CH50/AH50, C3/C4, factor H/I/B, anti‑factor H antibodies, ADAMTS13, and send genetic testing.
- If therapy already started: document timing of last PEX and eculizumab and the replacement fluid; interpret CH50/AH50 as likely drug/procedure effects; repeat critical antibody assays some time after last PEX if initial sampling was post‑exchange.
- Remember: normal C3/C4 does not exclude CM‑HUS; functional assays are most informative when obtained before PEX/eculizumab.
