Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Pathophysiology] What are the characteristic kidney biopsy findings of thrombotic microangiopathy on light microscopy, and how do immunofluorescence and electron microscopy help distinguish TMA from immune complex–mediated glomerulonephritides?

Direct answer
- On light microscopy (LM) acute thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) characteristically shows platelet‑ and fibrin‑rich thrombi occluding glomerular capillaries and small arterioles, swollen and detached endothelial cells, mesangiolysis, ischemic collapse or wrinkling of glomerular tufts and often acute tubular necrosis from ischemia. Segmental necrosis and crescents may be present. Chronic TMA changes include intimal mucoid/“onion‑skin” thickening of arterioles, recanalization, and double contours (GBM duplication) from new basement membrane formation.
- Immunofluorescence (IF) in TMA is typically negative or shows only nonspecific/trapped IgM or C3 in areas of injury; it does not show the granular or linear immune‑complex staining (IgG, IgA, C3) that defines immune complex–mediated glomerulonephritides.
- Electron microscopy (EM) in TMA demonstrates endothelial cell swelling and detachment, subendothelial widening with electron‑lucent (“fluffy”) fibrinous material or fibrin tactoids and GBM duplication in chronic lesions, but it lacks the electron‑dense immune complex deposits that are the hallmark of immune complex GN (eg, subepithelial “humps” in post‑infectious GN, subendothelial deposits in lupus, mesangial deposits in IgA nephropathy).

Clinical reasoning tied to the case
- The patient’s biopsy in the report showed severe ATN, ischaemic glomeruli with fibrin thrombi and scattered crescents on LM—classic acute TMA findings explaining dialysis‑dependent AKI.  
- IF showed no immune complex deposition and EM had “no evidence of primary glomerulopathy,” which argues against immune complex–mediated GN and supports a diagnosis of complement‑mediated HUS/TMA.  
- Practically, presence of occlusive fibrin thrombi on LM plus absent immune complexes on IF/EM shifts the pathology away from immune complex GN and toward a thrombotic (complement‑driven or other) microangiopathic process, consistent with the clinical picture and response to complement blockade in this case.
