### [Clinical Pearls] How does the PLASMIC score inform initial management when TTP is suspected, and how should it be used while awaiting ADAMTS13 results?

The PLASMIC score is a practical bedside tool for estimating the pretest probability that an adult with MAHA and thrombocytopenia has severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (TTP). Its value lies not in making the diagnosis but in guiding the urgency and aggressiveness of empiric therapy while you wait for the definitive ADAMTS13 assay.

How it should change initial management
Use the PLASMIC score to stratify risk and decide whether to start immediate therapeutic plasma exchange (PEX) and empiric corticosteroids. A high PLASMIC score (typically 6–7) confers a high probability of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency and should prompt immediate initiation of PEX without waiting for the assay result, because untreated TTP has very high mortality and PEX is lifesaving. A low score (0–4) indicates a low probability of TTP and should prompt careful pursuit of alternative causes of TMA (sepsis-associated DIC, STEC-HUS, complement-mediated HUS, malignant hypertension, drugs, pregnancy-related TMAs) and generally argues against routine empiric PEX unless the clinical picture is otherwise compelling.

How to use it while awaiting ADAMTS13 results
Obtain ADAMTS13 samples immediately (draw before PEX if possible) and send them stat. While awaiting results, apply PLASMIC in the context of the whole clinical picture: a high score plus compatible clinical features → start PEX and steroids promptly; a low or intermediate score → prioritize targeted evaluation for other TMAs, supportive care, and limited or no empiric PEX unless the patient is deteriorating or has features strongly suggestive of TTP (rapid neurologic decline, refractory shock, or otherwise unexplained severe thrombocytopenia/hemolysis). Reassess frequently and be prepared to start or stop PEX based on dynamic clinical course and the ADAMTS13 level when it returns.

Limitations and practical caveats
Remember that PLASMIC is a pretest probability tool, not a rule-out test. It was derived in cohorts where TTP predominated; its performance can be altered by confounders common in sepsis and DIC (abnormal INR, renal dysfunction, transfusions). Clinical judgment must override a low score when the patient is rapidly deteriorating. Also, try to obtain the ADAMTS13 sample before PEX because plasma therapy can alter levels, but do not delay life‑saving PEX to do so.
