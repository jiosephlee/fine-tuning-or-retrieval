Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome

### [Management] What is the mechanism of action of eculizumab in CM-HUS, and what hematologic and renal responses should clinicians expect in the first weeks after initiation?

Eculizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds complement component C5 and prevents its cleavage into C5a and C5b. By blocking C5 activation it prevents formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b‑9) and the downstream inflammatory effects of C5a. In CM‑HUS this stops ongoing complement‑mediated endothelial injury of the microvasculature, halts thrombus formation in glomerular and arteriolar capillaries, and therefore interrupts the pathophysiologic cascade that produces microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopaenia and acute kidney injury.

What to expect clinically and in the laboratory in the first days–weeks after starting eculizumab
- Haematologic response is usually rapid. Platelet counts typically begin to rise within 24–72 hours and often reach a sustained improvement within the first week; transfusion requirements usually fall quickly. Markers of haemolysis (LDH falls, haptoglobin normalizes, bilirubin declines) commonly improve over 48–96 hours, and schistocyte burden decreases over days to weeks. In practice you will see cessation of active haemolysis and stabilization of haemoglobin within the first week in most responders.
- Renal response is slower and more variable. Urine output, if any recoverable kidney function exists, may return within days to a couple of weeks; serum creatinine typically improves more gradually over weeks to months. Some patients who are dialysis‑dependent recover enough function to stop dialysis within days–weeks, but many have only partial recovery and persistent chronic kidney disease despite early haematologic remission. Time to eculizumab initiation and the degree/duration of renal ischemic injury before treatment are the main determinants of renal outcome.

Practical implications
Start eculizumab promptly when CM‑HUS is diagnosed or strongly suspected because haematologic remission is rapid and earlier therapy correlates with better renal recovery. Remember the infectious risk from terminal complement blockade: ensure appropriate meningococcal vaccination and give antimicrobial prophylaxis until vaccine immunity is established, and monitor cultures—ideally start eculizumab after blood cultures are negative if feasible. In this case the clinical course is typical: rapid haematologic remission after eculizumab and only partial renal recovery (creatinine 175 µmol/L, dialysis discontinued) reflecting both the delay to therapy and the severity of initial kidney injury.
