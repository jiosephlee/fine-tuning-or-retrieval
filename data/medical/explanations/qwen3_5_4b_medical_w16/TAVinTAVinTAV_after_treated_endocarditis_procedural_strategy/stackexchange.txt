Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Management] In a patient with a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, multiple comorbidities, and a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis with resolved infection, what clinical factors led to the decision to perform a third transcatheter valve implantation instead of surgical aortic valve replacement?

### Clinical Pearls: Management of Complex Transcatheter Reintervention After Treated Endocarditis

In this high-risk patient, the decision to perform a third transcatheter valve implantation (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) instead of surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) was driven by the convergence of prohibitive surgical risk, confirmed infection resolution, and validated anatomical feasibility on multidetector computed tomography (MSCT).

Although 2025 ESC guidelines generally recommend surgical replacement for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) with heart failure, management must be individualized based on risk and anatomy. In this case, the decision favored a transcatheter strategy for three specific reasons:

1.  **Prohibitive Surgical Risk:** The patient presented with a EuroSCORE II of 27.36% and significant comorbidities including COPD, type 2 diabetes, and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding. Critically, the deep fixation of two prior valves (Lotus in 2014 and Evolut in 2022) required surgical stagedExpl: "Out of curiosity, what is the minimum lumen diameter in the Evolut frame?" The presence of two prior transcatheter valves integrated into the aortic wall makes open surgical retrieval or replacement technically hazardous, likely necessitating total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement.
2.  **Resolution of Infective Endocarditis:** The patient had active PVE caused by *Enterococcus faecalis*, but the decision was supported by confirmed infection clearance. Following a course of ampicillin-based intravenous therapy and oral treatment, TTE, TEE, and blood cultures confirmed the absence of vegetations, active infection, or paravalvular complications. Once infection was controlled, the structural valve dysfunction was managed consistent with native/structural disease protocols rather than active endocarditis.
3.  **Validated Anatomical Feasibility:** Preprocedural MSCT screening demonstrated a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm and assessed the relationship between the valve frames and coronary ostia. This precise planning allowed for the implantation of a self-expanding Acurate neo2 valve at a height that maximized flow through the Evolut frame without occluding coronary access, a safety concern specific to sequential reinterventions.

**Clinical Takeaway:** In complex multi-valve anatomic scenarios where surgical risk is prohibitive, infection must be deemed clinically resolved via antimicrobial therapy and robust imaging surveillance before pursuing a third or subsequent TAVI.

### [Differential Diagnosis] How does the distinction between active prosthetic valve endocarditis and structural valve deterioration influence the decision-making process for a third valve intervention in a patient with a history of *Enterococcus faecalis* infection?

In the context of a patient with prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) and structural valve deterioration necessitating a third transcatheter aortic valve intervention (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), the stability of the active infection status is the primary determinant for procedural modality. The case report of Merkulov et al. illustrates that while guidelines recommend surgical valve replacement for active PVE, particularly complicated by heart failure, the decision shifts to a catheter-based strategy when infection is confirmed resolved and surgical risk is prohibitive.

In this patient, the evolution from a 2022 diagnosis of *Enterococcus faecalis* endocarditis to a 2024 decision for TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV hinged on confirming resolved infection. Diagnostic standards required a comprehensive assessment including negative blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography (TEE), and multislice CT (MSCT) to rule out vegetations or paravalvular abscess complications at the time of re-intervention. Because antimicrobial therapy was completed with no long-term suppressive antibiotics and clinical assessment showed resolution, the management aligned more closely with standard structural valve deterioration (SVD) protocols rather than acute infectious protocols.

Given the prohibitive surgical risk—including a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, complex prior valve frames, and prohibitive technical hazard of aortic root replacement—catheter-based reintervention became the preferred pathway. The distinction between active infection and resolved degeneration is thus procedural: active endocarditis precludes valve replacement strategies without source control, but resolved degeneration in a high-surgical-risk patient permits TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV planning. Furthermore, the choice of specific device, the Acurate neo2, was driven by anatomical considerations of a triple-nested valve configuration. Preprocedural CT-guided planning was essential to optimize commissural alignment and ensure coronary flow, as high implantation depth could compromise coronary arteries. Consequently, the decision-making process rests on imaging-confirmed infection resolution and the Framing distance assessment, allowing for a strategy-driven transcatheter intervention when surgical risk outweighs procedural complexity.

### [Management] Which specific pre-procedural multidetector CT (MSCT) measurements were prioritized during the planning phase to ensure the third valve frame would not obstruct coronary blood flow in a previously stented aortic root?

**Key Pre-Interventional MSCT Metrics for Multi-Stage TAVI**

To ensure the third valve frame does not obstruct coronary blood flow in a patient with prior transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI), pre-procedural multidetector CT (MSCT) must prioritize four specific measurements: **coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance, and frame interaction (overlap).**

In this case, accurate assessment of these parameters was critical for determining the feasibility of **high implantation**. High implantation allows the device to sit above the stent residue of the prior valve, minimizing the formation of a **neo-skirt** that compresses the coronary ostia or distorts flow dynamics. By aligning the new valve (Acurate neo2) with the Evolut frame leaflets, we preserved future coronary access and ensured that the cumulative mesh burden did not obstruct perfusion. These CT-guided planning metrics, verified intra-procedurally by aortography demonstrating unobstructed flow without the need for coronary protection, represent the standard of care for managing serial valve-in-valve interventions to prevent catastrophic coronary obstruction.

### [Management] What was the rationale behind implanting the third valve in a high position (supra-annular) relative to the previous Evolut valve leaflets to manage the effective orifice area and complex prostheses geometry?

In complex sequential transcatheter valve interventions, specifically TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, the height of implantation relative to the existing prosthesis is a critical determinant of subsequent haemodynamic performance and coronary safety. In this case report described by Merkulov et al., the rationale for implanting the third Acurate neo2 valve in a high, supra-annular position relative to the Evolut leaflets was twofold. First, this positioning served to "fix" the pre-existing leaflets of the Evolut prosthesis against the new stent frame. This mechanical stabilization prevented inward folding of the outer leaflets, which would otherwise reduce the effective orifice area and lead to patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM). Second, the high implantation strategy was essential to minimize the formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, thereby preserving coronary access and flow despite the overlapping stent frames.

Clinical decision-making relied heavily on pre-procedural multislice CT to model the interaction between the three valve frames, ensuring the new Acurate neo2 valve had sufficient clearance from the coronary ostia and minimal frame height. This strategic placement was validated at one-year follow-up, demonstrating sustained valve function with no residual regurgitation and preserved coronary perfusion. Consequently, in patients with prohibitive surgical risk and complex anatomy, a CT-guided high-implantation strategy using supra-annular devices is an essential strategy to optimize haemodynamics and avoid iatrogenic coronary obstruction.

### [Pathophysiology] What distinct pathological mechanisms approximately contributed to the severe restenosis and prosthetic dysfunction observed between the second TAVI procedure in 2022 and the third intervention in 2024?

The severe restenosis and prosthetic dysfunction observed between the 2022 Evolut valve implantation and the 2024 third-intervention was driven by a **multifactorial pathological mechanism** involving both infective injury and progressive structural degeneration.

First, the development of *Enterococcus faecalis* prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE) contributed directly to the complication. As detailed in the discussion, the infective episode likely caused **leaflet injury** that resulted in **mechanical restriction**, compromising the mobility of the bioprosthetic tissue. While infection was successfully treated with antibiotics, the text notes that "structural degeneration observed... involved leaflet fibrosis from prior degeneration and probable mechanical restriction following infection," indicating that the inflammatory insult accelerated tissue damage and narrowing.

Second, these events occurred in a setting of **progressive structural degeneration** of the Evolut prosthesis. The patient experienced severe stenosis requiring aggressive intervention, characterized by a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an AVA of 0.5 cm². This progression suggests a combination of the valve's inherent bioprosthetic fatigue and compounding fibrosis over the two-year history. Thus, the deterioration was not solely infectious; it represented a convergence of infection-related mechanical restriction and the natural degradation of the transcatheter prosthesis that has become increasingly challenging in "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV" scenarios, particularly regarding valve frame layering and haemodynamic output.

### [Clinical Pearls] At the one-year follow-up, which specific hemodynamic parameters and clinical signs demonstrated the long-term durability and functional success of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention in this high-risk patient?

At the one-year follow-up, the long-term durability and functional success of this TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention were characterized by persistent clinical stability and favorable hemodynamics. The patient remained asymptomatic and clinically active without dyspnea, demonstrating sustained freedom from heart failure symptoms.

Regarding hemodynamic parameters, transthoracic echocardiography confirmed persistent efficacy:
*   **Mean Transvalvular Gradient:** Remained low at 26 mm Hg.
*   **Aortic Valve Area (AVA):** Normalized to 1.1 cm².
*   **Ejection Fraction (EF):** The left ventricular ejection fraction stabilized at 65%, reflecting successful hemodynamic unloading and left ventricular remodeling.

Notably, the absence of mechanical complications over this period further supports the procedural success. There were no signs of prosthetic valve dysfunction, residual regurgitation, or recurrent endocarditis at the one-year audiology. This sustained hemodynamic performance in the setting of a triple-valve configuration underscores the utility of CT-guided planning to mitigate risks of patient-prosthesis mismatch, while confirming that complex multi-prosthetic interventions can achieve durable outcomes without requiring surgical revision.

### [Management] If the third valve frame were to fail and lock into the adjacent stents, limiting the ability to perform further repairs, what are the most significant limitations regarding surgical or catheter-based salvage options described in this scenario?

In the context of a triple-valve TAVI (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) procedure, as illustrated in this case, should the third valve frame fail and lock onto adjacent stents, the available intervention options become exceptionally restricted. According to the discussion section of this report, the authors explicitly state that "should the current prosthesis fail prematurely, **treatment options would be extremely limited**." They characterize surgical reconciliation as **technically demanding and high-risk**, specifically noting that the deep fixation of the prior valves within the aortic wall would likely necessitate a **total aortic root and ascending aorta replacement** rather than a standard valve explantation. This is particularly hazardous given the patient's prohibitive surgical risk profile, an elevated EuroSCORE II of 27.36% in the context of prior endocarditis.

Regarding catheter-based salvage, the discussion remains cautious, labeling that while catheter-based solutions such as valve fracture or leaflet laceration *might* be considered, **their feasibility and safety in triple-stented configurations remain uncertain**. The cumulative mesh burden from the multiple overlapping stent frames significantly impedes coronary access, creating a structural hurdle for future interventions that aim to disengage or reshape the valve. Thus, the locking of the third valve frame represents a critical procedural checkpoint where the technical complexity of the anatomy ultimately limits both surgical escalation and percutaneous salvage strategies, emphasizing the necessity of lifetime planning and multidisciplinary review before the initial implantation.
