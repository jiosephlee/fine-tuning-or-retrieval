# Passive Surveillance: Long-Term Management of Triple-Valve Anatomy

## The Extreme Case of Triple Transcortic Valve Repair

We are seeing a lot of translational heart failure, yes, and a lot of repeated TAVIs. But getting to TAV-In-TAV-In-TAV after a history of Prosthetic Valve Endocarditis (PVE) is like finding a needle in a haystack. As超声 echo clinicians know, each valve adds up-layering geometry. The patient in this case is a man in his late 70s who saw three distinct aortic prostheses implanted between 2014 and 2024. It is a profile that screams "high risk surgery," which ironically is a case for *catheter* with CT-guided planning.

The first TAV was a Lotus in 2014. By 2022, the bioprosthetic got stiff, so his team tried to add an Evolut manually, but then came the bleeding complications (pseudoaneurysm). But this was the patient's work, not the fail. After infection was handled, the rotation was plain in 2024: An Acurate neo2 in the aortic position. The key here is not just the procedure; it is the timeline. The PVE was treated, the infection resolved clinically and radiologically, and then the actual valve dysfunction started. It turns out that the prior infection and subsequent leaflet fibrosis make the next valve even more fragile.

## What Is The Goal of Surveillance Here?

You might think, "Just do TAV and done." But with three stents stacked up in the ascending aorta frame, the anatomy is a maze. The surveillance needs to be aggressive but not just TTE every three months. You need to visualize the interaction of the frames without opening the cage.

The key is the CT planning and the "survive" to the shadows. The CT was used to measure the minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm in the Evolut prosthesis. In this specific case, the team did high implantation with the 2024 Acurate neo2 to keep the coronary flow safe. This was a proactive decision to avoid a neo-skirt that could obstruct the coronary ostia.

So, where does the next 1-year come? It comes from tracking the gradient and the ventricular remodeling. This surveillance isn't just about valve leaks. It's about knowing when to stop the progression of the overrides.

## Key Points to Remember

*   **Long-term surveillance is critical in triple-valve anatomy** to detect calcification or stent corrosion that could jeopardize coronary access.
*   **Pre-procedural MSCT is non-negotiable** prior to TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV to evaluate valve-to-coronary distances and planned high-implantation strategies.
*   **Infection clearance** must be validated via TEE and blood cultures before proceeding with a reintervention post-TAV.
*   **1-year follow-up was asymptomatic** in this case, suggesting the durability of the super strategy can be preserved if managed carefully.
*   **Backup options are limited** should the frame structure fail; surgical explantation is very high risk for this anatomy.

## Managing the Future of Valve Management

This case highlights that the valve landscape is different. Every new valve adds a new dimension. The team's decision to use a self-expanding Acurate neo2 in this specific context was to minimize the drastic drop in leaflet function. They aligned the 3D frame so it didn't fold over the 2D Evolut leaflets.

Surveillance now must expand to include *three-dimensional* functional assessment. A TTE showing low gradients might be misleading if there is a neo-skirt that affects coronary flow. The patient's EF is now 65%, but we must ensure we don't interpret that as success if the coronary access is compromised.

In summary, managing the triple-valve heart is about knowing when to watch and when to act.
