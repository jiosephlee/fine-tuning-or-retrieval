Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] In this case report, how were the principles of the ACMG guidelines applied to classify a novel genetic variant (p.N270S) that was not present in established databases like ClinVar?

**Clinical Teaching Point: ACMG Guidelines for Novel Variants in HNF1A-MODY**

In this case, the classification of the novel HNF1A variant **p.N270S** demonstrates how the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines are applied when a variant is absent from established databases like ClinVar. While the absence of the variant in truth tables and population databases does not automatically prove benignity, it necessitates the accumulation of additional positive evidence to justify a *Likely Pathogenic (LP)* classification. In this patient, this challenge was overcome by integrating three specific lines of evidence:

1.  **Computational Prediction:** In silico analysis showed a high AlphaMissense score of 0.995, indicating a high probability of functional impact.
2.  **Structural Context:** The residue was located in the DNA-binding domain and was adjacent to known pathogenic residues, suggesting a similar mechanism of dysfunction.
3.  **Family Segregation:** The variant segregated with the disease phenotype in the family, confirmed by the presence of diabetes in the maternal grandmother, mother, and younger brother.

The combination of these factors—despite the absence of the variant in ClinVar—provided sufficient confidence for an LP classification. This accurate genetic resolution allowed for a definitive shift in management: insulin therapy was discontinued and replaced with a GLP-1 receptor agonist and metformin, successfully lowering HbA1c to 6.0%. This underscores the critical role of rigorous variant classification using ACMG principles in unmasking MODY cases and guiding precisely matched medical therapy.
