Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] In the absence of classic Heliotrope rash or Gottron's papules on initial presentation, which subtle cutaneous findings and nailfold capillaroscopy signs observed in this patient contributed significantly to the suspicion of dermatomyositis?

**Clinical Teaching Point: Atypical Cutaneous Clues in Subtle Dermatomyositis**

In the absence of classic heliotrope rash or Gottron’s papules, the suspicion of dermatomyositis (DM) in this case was driven by three key data points that distinguished inflammatory musculoskeletal pathology from idiopathic angioedema. First, **nailfold capillaroscopy** revealed **proximal nailfold microhaemorrhages and avascular areas**, which are highly suggestive of an inflammatory connective tissue process. Second, physical examination identified **generalised erythema of the face including the nasolabial folds**, **periungual erythema**, and a confirmed **positive Holster sign** (persistent erythema) on the lateral thigh. Finally, these subtle skin findings were supported by disproportionately elevated serum creatine kinase (CK 16,184 U/L) and the presence of **anti-SAE1 antibodies**.

This constellation of findings was critical because it pointed towards a non-histaminic origin. As the report notes, the neck swelling in this patient represents **inflammatory soft tissue swelling from myositis**, rather than the vascular leakage mechanisms of classic angioedema (C1 esterase deficiency or ACE inhibitor use). Therefore, before settling on a diagnosis of idiopathic angioedema, it is imperative in patients with unexplained neck or facial swelling to carefully assess for subtle nailfold changes, erythema of the nasolabial folds, and a high index of suspicion for an underlying myositis, recognizing that classic lesions may be absent in antibody-specific phenotypes like SAE1-positive DM.
