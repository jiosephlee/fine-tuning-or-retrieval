### [Management] According to the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, what specific clinical risk factors and lab findings in this patient elevated the risk for occult malignancy to high risk, necessitating the initiation of GI endoscopy screening?

Based on the Discussion section provided, the patient's risk for occult malignancy was classified as "high" per International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines. This classification, which necessitated GI endoscopy screening, was driven by a specific combination of **high-risk factors** and **intermediate-risk factors**.

The patient presented with two **high-risk factors**: the confirmed diagnosis of **dermatomyositis** and the presence of **dysphagia**. These factors were compounded by two **intermediate-risk factors**: positivity for **SAE1 antibodies** and the patient being of **male sex**. The literature review within the case report notes that dermatomyositis is associated with malignancy in approximately 18% of cases, and dysphagia serves as a significant clinical marker that elevates this risk further due to potential gastrointestinal or oropharyngeal involvement. Consequently, despite initial negative tumor markers and imaging, the convergence of these stratification parameters mandated comprehensive GI endoscopy (gastroscopy and colonoscopy) according to IMACS protocols.
