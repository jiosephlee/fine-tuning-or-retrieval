Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Pathophysiology] This patient tested positive for anti-SAE1 antibodies. How do the clinical manifestations associated with anti-SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis, such as interstitial lung disease and dysphagia, compare statistically to those of patients with anti-Mi2 or anti-TIF1-gamma antibodies?

**Clinical Teaching Point**

In response to your teaching question regarding the statistical comparison of interstitial lung disease (ILD) and dysphagia between antibody subtypes, the provided case report quantifies these manifestations explicitly for the **anti‑SAE1** phenotype but notes that specific comparative prevalence data for anti‑Mi2 or anti‑TIF1‑gamma regarding these specific organ involvement are not provided in the discussion section.

The discussion cites a literature review of 215 patients in which dysphagia and interstitial lung disease were reported in **46% and 43%** of anti‑SAE1 dermatomyositis cases, respectively. This highlights a strong association between the anti‑SAE1 marker and classic myositis-related organ damage.

Regarding the comparison with anti-Mi2 or anti-TIF1-gamma, the case report explicitly states that antibodies such as **anti-TIF1-gamma are more commonly associated with angioedema** than other antibodies (which typically refer to SAE1). While the text does not provide statistical rates for ILD or dysphagia in the TIF1-gamma cohort, this distinction is clinically vital. As demonstrated in this patient, neck swelling (pseudoangioedema) was present, necessitating a differential that included angioedema (linked to TIF1-gamma in the authors' narrative) versus the myositis-mediated EDophagia seen in SAE1.

Therefore, for educational purposes: the case data supports identifying **anti‑SAE1 as the serologic marker most strongly linked to prevalent dysphagia and ILD** in this series. For patients presenting with primary angioedema without significant dysphagia or classic myositis, the report implies anti‑TIF1-gamma (or MDA-5) should be prioritized in the differential, whereas anti‑SAE1 should heighten suspicion for systemic myositis organ involvement.

**Teaching Takeaway:**
In dermatomyositis, patients with **anti-SAE1 antibodies have a high risk of organ manifestation (46% dysphagia, 43% ILD in cited data)**. While the report notes anti-TIF1-gamma is specifically associated with angioedema, do not assume all other myositis antibodies follow SAE1's ILD/dysphagia prevalence; recognize the SAE1 profile as the primary statistic for these myositis complications within this case context.
