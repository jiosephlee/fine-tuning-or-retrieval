Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Clinical Pearls] Despite the resolution of the initial neck swelling with immunosuppression, why did the patient require long-term nutritional support via nasogastric tube initially, and what changes over the 11-month follow-up demonstrated the efficacy of the treatment protocol?

The patient required nasogastric (NG) tube feeding initially due to active inflammatory myositis affecting the pharyngeal muscles, which caused severe dysphagia and aspiration risk rather than a mechanical or structural airway obstruction. Although the dermal and subcutaneous edema of the neck resolved rapidly, the myopathic process in the swallowing complex persisted. Videofluoroscopic swallow study confirmed severe pharyngeal dysphagia with shovel avoidance and penetration, necessitating NG access to prevent recurrent aspiration pneumonia.

The efficacy of the treatment protocol (prednisone, IVIG, mycophenolate, and rituximab) over the 11-month follow-up was demonstrated by the resolution of both the primary myopathy and its complications. Clinical improvement included the normalization of creatine kinase (from 16,184 U/L to 37 U/L) and the ability to wean the patient from the NG tube within two months of discharge. Crucially, the 11-month follow-up CT chest showed near-complete resolution of the basal ground-glass opacities and airspace consolidation initially attributed to aspiration pneumonitis. This radiological improvement confirms that sustained immunosuppression was necessary to clear secondary lung injury, validating the protocol despite the rapid resolution of the initial neck swelling.
