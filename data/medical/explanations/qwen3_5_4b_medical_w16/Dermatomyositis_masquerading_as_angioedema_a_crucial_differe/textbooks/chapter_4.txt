Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

Chapter 4: ## Therapeutic Management: Immunosuppression Strategies in Myositis

Effective management of autoimmune inflammatory myopathies, particularly in the acute setting where multiorgan involvement is present, relies on aggressive immunosuppression to halt disease progression and preserve respiratory muscle function. In this case, the patient presented with severe cervical soft tissue swelling and dysphagia suggestive of airway compromise driven by myositis. Consequently, the initial therapeutic strategy prioritized rapid reduction of inflammation. The first line of intervention involved high-dose intravenous corticosteroids. An initial protocol of intravenous methylprednisolone at 1000 mg daily for 3 days was utilized to acutely suppress the inflammatory response within the neck muscles and associated soft tissues. This "pulse" therapy serves as a standard induction approach to stabilize airway patency and reduce systemic cytokine storms, transitioning the patient to oral corticosteroids once adequate inflammatory control was achieved. The patient was then stepped down to hydrocortisone (prednisone) tapering regimen starting at 60 mg daily to maintain remission whilst minimizing long-term systemic toxicity.

Following the induction phase, the treatment plan evolved into a maintenance and combination regimen designed to sustain disease control and spare long-term steroid toxicity. Given the patient's condition—specifically the presence of anti-SAE1 antibodies and features consistent with inflammatory lung involvement—monotherapy with corticosteroids was deemed insufficient for durable remission. A combination approach was adopted, incorporating mycophenolate mofetil (1 g twice daily), rituximab (1000 mg two doses, administered 2 weeks apart), and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) at 2 g/kg. This tri-phasic regimen targets the underlying autoimmune process through distinct mechanisms: mycophenolate mofetil inhibits purine synthesis to halt lymphocyte proliferation, rituximab depletes B-cells implicated in autoantibody production, and IVIG modulates immune function and neutralization of circulating factors. While treatment outcomes for dermatomyositis-associated oedema are variable, the combination of these agents allowed for significant clinical improvement, resolving the inflammatory neck swelling and normalizing creatine kinase levels.

A critical decision-making point in the chemoprophylaxis was the avoidance of methotrexate. Although widely utilized as a steroid-sparing agent in dermatomyositis, methotrexate was contraindicated in this specific patient due to the risk of exacerbating pulmonary complications. Upon presentation, the patient exhibited abnormal lung function tests (restrictive pattern) and CT findings suggesting peribronchial consolidation and pulmonary nodules, which were indicative of an ILD-prone inflammatory state. Clinical guidelines suggest that in patients with anti-SAE1 dermatomyositis risk factors for interstitial lung disease (ILD) and those who already demonstrate respiratory compromise, methotrexate-associated fibrotic changes pose a significant safety hazard. Therefore, the decision to avoid methotrexate and rely on the combination of mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab was a strategic choice to prioritize lung preservation while managing the inflammation.

Finally, the management required careful consideration of prophylaxis for opportunistic infections, specifically Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP), alongside vigilant monitoring for hematologic toxicity. Standard prophylaxis involves sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim; however, in this patient, that regimen was discontinued due to the development of haemolytic anaemia. This occurred despite the absence of documented glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, highlighting the importance of monitoring red blood cell indices during steroid and immunosuppressant therapy. To manage the PJP risk without compromising red cell health, the patient was subsequently switched to nebulised pentamidine 300 mg every 4 weeks. This modification allowed for the continuation of necessary infection prophylaxis while maintaining hematologic stability throughout the treatment course. Ultimately, this comprehensive regimen facilitated a successful transition to partial steroid taper and mucocutaneous resolution, allowing for the eventual removal of thoracic assistance and removal of the nasogastric tube.
