Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

Chapter 5: ## Malignancy Risk Stratification and Long-Term Follow-up

The management of initial inflammatory myopathies, including dermatomyositis (DM), requires rigorous assessment for an underlying neoplastic etiology, as malignancy occurs more frequently in anti-SAE1–positive patients compared to other antibody profiles. In the context of this case, the patient presented with a high risk of occult malignancy despite a negative malignancy screen at diagnosis. This risk is stratified using the International guidelines for Malignancy Screening (IMACS) by the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group. Under the IMACS framework, dermatomyositis itself constitutes a high-risk factor. Furthermore, the presence of dysphagia, SAE1抗体 positivity, and the patient's male sex in this demographic contribute additional high-risk and intermediate-risk points. Because anti-SAE1 antibody-positive dermatomyositis is associated with dysphagia and interstitial lung disease, which are warning signs for malignancy, the low threshold for cancer screening is critical. In this instance, the IMACS criteria identified two high-risk factors and two intermediate-risk factors, classifying the patient as having a very high cancer risk profile. Consequently, the initial clinical management prioritized a comprehensive malignancy workup to rule out paraneoplastic triggers or metastatic burden, ensuring that immunosuppressive therapy was not initiated prior to the exclusion of a malignancy, especially given the patient's significant airway and muscular involvement.

Malignancy surveillance in patients with anti-SAE1 dermatomyositis was guided by these IMACS risk criteria, necessitating multimodal imaging and laboratory testing immediately at the diagnosis. The patient underwent detailed Chest CT imaging to assess for interstitial lung disease (ILD) and potential metastases, with subsequent findings showing patchy consolidation likely due to aspiration pneumonitis rather than malignancy. While CT alone has limitations, the addition of a fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)–positron emission tomography (PET) scan provided a functional metabolic map, revealing no metabolically active malignancy elsewhere in the body. This finding is pedagogically significant as it demonstrates that functional imaging can be superior to structural imaging in seronegative or paucisymptomatic periods, though it is not a standalone substitute for tissue diagnosis. Additionally, tumor markers—including CEA, CA 19-9, CA-125, and PSA—were part of the baseline screening panel to establish a reference point for future comparison, despite normal results in this patient. Gastroscopy and colonoscopy were identified as essential screenings to exclude colorectal and upper gastrointestinal malignancies, a plan that remains a cornerstone of long-term monitoring to detect occult cancers that may not be immediately symptomatic. These procedures were integrated into the multidisciplinary plan to ensure that the profound immunosuppression required for the dermatomyositis does not occur in the setting of an undiagnosed cancer.

Recovery trajectories in anti-SAE1 positive dermatomyositis differ significantly depending on the severity of the initial inflammatory burden and the promptness of immunosuppressive therapy. In this case, the aggressive initial dosing of intravenous methylprednisolone and the subsequent infusion of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) correlated with a rapid resolution of the proximal muscle weakness. Muscle biopsy findings and clinical strength assessments showed that the creatine kinase level normalized to baseline within three weeks, and proximal muscle strength fully recovered by discharge. However, extramuscular manifestations such as cervical soft tissue swelling, while initially dramatic, demonstrated a slower resolution timeline. By three months post-discharge, the cervical swelling had completely resolved, coinciding with the tapering of corticosteroids, whereas the facial erythema persisting longer responded more gradually to the combination of immunosuppressants. This discrepancy highlights that inflammatory edema in the subcutaneous tissues, while a hallmark of SAE1 dermatomyositis (as seen here), may persist longer than muscle inflammation requires for remission. Furthermore, the preservation of pulmonary function and the resolution of aspiration-related consolidations indicate that addressing the pharyngeal dysphagia via enteral feeding and immunosuppression was required to mitigate secondary intrapulmonary complications, demonstrating that the neuromuscular component of the disease often requires the most active management during the acute phase.

Ongoing monitoring for DM involves a balanced strategy of tailoring steroid-sparing agents while maintaining long-term efficacy for sustained remission. The patient on discharge demonstrated the need for cessation of oral corticosteroids in light of side effects, adhering to a standard taper schedule from 60 mg/day down to a discontinuation at seven months post-discharge. To prevent relapse, the patient was maintained on mycophenolate mofetil and monthly IVIG, with methotrexate withheld due to the pre-existing restrictive lung pattern identified on pulmonary function tests. Long-term follow-up protocols focus on maintaining efficacy while minimizing the cost of hospitalization and the risk of secondary malignancies from chronic immunosuppression. In this specific case, the patient remained on mycophenolate and IVIG, with compliance depending on clinical progress. The continuation of total IVIG and mycophenolate tapering plans, alongside ongoing respiratory function testing and complex imaging surveillance, supports the management paradigm where aggressive anti-inflammatory control must proceed without the pressure of indefinite immunosuppression on tumor screening or organ health. Ultimately, this case underscores that while dermatomyositis is often manageable with sustained remission, the initial risk of malignancy and the varying timelines of cutaneous and muscular recovery dictate a multidisciplinary, longitudinal surveillance approach.
