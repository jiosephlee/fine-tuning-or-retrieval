Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

Chapter 5: ## Postoperative Course and Long-term Clinical Implications

### Maternal Recovery and Clinical Resolution
Following the laparotomy, the first priority for the patient was hemodynamic stabilization and resolution of perioperative complications. Despite the initial nebulized anemia presenting as hemoglobin 7.5 g/L and hematocrit 24.3% post-surgery, definitive management required intravenous iron supplementation (ferric carboxymaltose 1 g) and two units of allogenic blood to achieve a hemoglobin level of 10.0 g/L. Uncomplicated infection control was achieved through targeted antibiotic therapy based on the urine bacterial load of *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, utilizing cefazolin in conjunction with clarithromycin from the time of hospitalization. This response allowed the patient to be discharged without complications on day 10 postoperatively, at which point the severe localized pain had been resolved, and vital signs stabilized. The use of intraoperative progesterone prophylaxis continued post-discharge to support placental stability, illustrating a protocol where maternal physiological recovery was prioritized alongside the immediate postoperative course. In this instance, the admission of a C-reactive protein of 1.39 mg/dL prior to discharge indicated that the inflammatory response was successfully contained without leading to sepsis, nor did it necessitate prolonged hospitalization.

### Fetal Well-being and Delivery Planning
The management of a gravid uterus with atypical masses required rigorous monitoring of fetal growth and fetal status throughout the recovery period. During the strict obstetric follow-up, fetal growth was calculated consistent with healthy development, achieving the 86th percentile with a strong amniotic fluid index, confirming that the uterine mass at the time of surgery did not compromise oxygenation or nutrient transport. Although the patient underwent an elective Caesarean delivery at 37 weeks gestation, the timing and mode of delivery align with the critical planning mentioned in the case report: the residual pelvic mass, while left in situ, grew to a size that would impede the vertex presentation in the expulsive phase of spontaneous labor. Consequently, performing the Caesarean section minimizes the risk of uterine scar rupture, a critical consideration given the previous lower uterine segment incision for hysterotomy or potential for expansion. The neonatal outcome was described as excellent, reinforcing that surgical intervention of the maternal masses did not yield iatrogenic complications such as intrauterine growth restriction or preterm labor in this specific presentation.

### Pathophysiology of Uterine Fibroid Behavior
The immediate postpartum course included observation of the residual uterine tissue to understand the natural history of leiomyomas. The pathology revealed benign uterine leiomyomas that underwent ischaemic necrosis due to the twisted pedicle of the excised upper mass, explaining the low-grade fever and leukocytosis during the hospital stay. Pathologically and clinically, the remaining lower mass—which persisted at the uterus fundus—followed a predictable trajectory for fibroids in response to hormonal withdrawal and resumption of atrophic hormonal status. Uterine leiomyomas generally follow a non-linear trend of size changes; they increase in size by up to 4.1 cm or 69% every week during the first trimester and 3.1% in the third trimester due to estrogen and progesterone-mediated proliferation. However, regardless of the aggressive size changes during gestation, the third trimester marks a turning point followed by significant decrease after delivery due to the cessation of hormonal stimulation. In this patient, the residual mass measured 7.5 cm by postpartum follow-up, a reduction from the 9 cm instability seen during the pregnancy episode, demonstrating the role of post-delivery hormonal regression in size management.

### Future Management Decisions: Observation versus Reintervention
The decision-making process regarding future gynecological management underscores the balance between symptomatology and elective intervention. While large fibroids may prompt immediate diagnosis and potential myomectomy postpartum, the patient in the present case opted for a deferred approach to measurement and time-sensitive myomectomy. This decision was driven by the evidence showing that the residual mass had slightly reduced to 7.5 cm following the second trimester, and crucially, the patient remained asymptomatic regarding pain, dyspnoea, or dysmenorrhea. Surgical preference is often weighed against the potential morbidity of repeat dissection versus the utility of natural regression. The patient’s high functional profile and decision to reschedule the next surgical step—either elective waiting or partial hysteresis—reflects a cautious strategy where maternal quality of life takes precedence unless symptoms reemerge. Furthermore, the patient’s breastfeeding well-being and desire to fulfill motherhood without planned hysterectomy indicates that the uterine preservation strategy was successfully validated in this case, minimizing the need for further aggressive surgical management.
