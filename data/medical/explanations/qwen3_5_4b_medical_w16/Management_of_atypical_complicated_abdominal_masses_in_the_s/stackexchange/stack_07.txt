Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Pathophysiology] Why was a non-contrast MRI preferred over a gadolinium-enhanced study for this patient during the second trimester, considering the safety profile of contrast agents regarding fetal development?

In the management of pregnant patients with complex pelvic or abdominal pathology, the primary objective is diagnostic clarity without compromising fetal safety. As illustrated in this case, the decision to utilize non-contrast MRI over a gadolinium-enhanced study during the second trimester is driven by established safety guidelines regarding contrast agents in pregnancy.

The case report explicitly states that "up-to-date recommendations" advise that "gadolinium-enhanced MRI should not be performed during pregnancy, unless strictly necessary, due to rheumatological, inflammatory or infiltrative skin conditions, risk of stillbirth or neonatal death." While gadolinium crosses the placenta and its association with fetal harm remains a concern, the multidisciplinary team prioritized the non-enhanced modality because non-contrast MRI is reasonably safe after the first trimester and avoids direct exposure to contrast agents. In this specific clinical scenario, the diagnostic yield of MRI—distinguishing between cystic endometriomas, solid leiomyomas with degeneration, and adnexal masses—remains the standard of care when ultrasound is inconclusive.

Therefore, when advanced imaging is required for atypical masses in pregnancy, non-contrast MRI is the preferred standard. It provides the necessary soft-tissue characterization to guide surgical intervention while preserving fetal tissue integrity, reserving contrast administration exclusively for scenarios where non-contrast imaging fails to resolve the diagnosis or where maternal/fetal stability is immediately threatened by diagnostic uncertainty.
