Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Which specific ultrasonographic or MRI characteristics were pivotal in distinguishing the two masses in this case, particularly the upper pedunculated lesion, from a differential diagnosis of large decidualized endometriomas?

In this case, the differentiation between a pedunculated uterus leiomyoma and decidualized endometriomas was challenging given that both modalities initially mimicked cystic adnexal lesions. While color Doppler ultrasound identified peripheral vascularization, it was insufficient for definitive distinction.

The pivotal imaging characteristics distinguishing the massive upper lesion from an endometrioma came from the **non-contrast MRI**. Specifically, the presence of **focal hyperintense T1-T2 weighted solid components** within the otherwise cystic mass was the key discriminator.

Clinically, this finding suggested **cystic degeneration within a myomatous matrix** (leiomyoma) rather than a purely cystic pathologic structure like an endometrioma. Additionally, the **thick walls (1.4 cm)** identified on MRI supported the diagnosis of a symptomatic myoma, whereas endometriomas typically present with thin, hemosiderin-laden walls. The **richtung of the caudal mass adhering to an intrinsic intramural-subserosal myoma** further corroborated a myomatous origin via a urogenital connection despite the initial “steric bulk” obscuring the stalk on imaging.

Ultimately, while the upper mass appeared to lack a visible connective stalk on preoperative imaging (initially favoring an ovarian origin), the combination of **solid components on MRI, high vascularity, and proximity to uterine fundal tissue** facilitated the surgical identification of a large pedunculated uterine leiomyoma. This highlights that in the second trimester, structural tissue characteristics on MRI are crucial to prevent underestimation of the uterine origin of adnexal masses mimicking decidualized endometriosis.
