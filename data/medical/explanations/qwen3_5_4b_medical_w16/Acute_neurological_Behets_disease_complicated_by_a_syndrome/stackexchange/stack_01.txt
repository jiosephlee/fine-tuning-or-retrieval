Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What infectious and neoplastic etiologies were effectively excluded from the differential diagnosis based on cerebrospinal fluid analysis and neuroimaging?

In this case, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis and neuroimaging were critical in systematically excluding infectious and neoplastic etiologies to confirm the diagnosis of acute neurological Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD).

**Infectious etiologies** were ruled out based on specific CSF findings. Viral meningoencephalitis, specifically caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV) or varicella zoster virus (VZV), was excluded when CSF testing yielded negative results for both viral antigen and antibodies. Bacterial and tuberculous meningitis were also effectively excluded: bacterial infection was negated by the absence of neutrophilic predominance and negative CSF cultures, while tuberculous meningitis was ruled out by negative acid-fast staining and tuberculosis DNA testing.

**Neoplastic etiologies**, particularly primary CNS malignancy (such as lymphoma), were excluded using neuroimaging and cytological review. Although the patient exhibited symptomatic neurological deficits and a mildly elevated serum interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) level, the brain MRI showed no abnormalities or paraneoplastic lesions. Furthermore, CSF cytology did not reveal malignant cells.

This case demonstrates that the diagnosis of acute neuro-BD relies heavily on the exclusion of these common mimics. The combination of mucocutaneous findings, aseptic meningitis on CSF analysis, and a normal brain MRI provided the definitive basis for confirming the vascular inflammatory process of Behçet’s disease in the CNS.
