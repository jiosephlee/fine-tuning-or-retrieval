Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Upon initial evaluation, what metabolic complication related to severe hyponatremia was identified that is often overlooked in acute neurological presentations?

Clinical Pearl: **Rhabdomyolysis**

Upon initial evaluation of a patient with acute neurological Behçet’s disease presenting with severe hyponatremia, **rhabdomyolysis** was identified as a metabolic complication often overlooked in pure neurological presentations.

In this case, the patient presented with a serum sodium of 124 mmol/L and creatine kinase (CK) of 1349 U/L; the latter finding revealed rhabdomyolysis, a complication frequently recognized yet underappreciated in the context of severe hyponatremia. While the diagnosis of syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) accounted for the water balance disturbance, the metabolic sequelae of profound sodium depletion extended beyond the central nervous system to include skeletal muscle integrity.

**Educational Point:**
Clinicians must broaden their differential diagnosis beyond central inflammatory markers when asymptomatic patients present with fever, headache, and hyponatremia. While the workup of acute neuro-BD often prioritizes MRI, CSF analysis, and autoimmune screens to rule out CNS infection and malignancy, severe hyponatremia can induce osmotic injury and cellular swelling that predisposes to myocyte damage independent of direct neurological localization. This case underscores that in patients with acute neuro-BD and SIADH, initial evaluation must include CK to identify hidden metabolic injury, such as rhabdomyolysis, which can otherwise be mistaken for unrelated myositis or unexplained fatigue.
