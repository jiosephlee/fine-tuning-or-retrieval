Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] According to the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease, what mucocutaneous features allowed for the diagnosis of definite systemic disease in conjunction with acute neuro-BD diagnosis?

In this case report, the diagnosis of **definite systemic Behçet’s disease (BD)** concurrent with acute neurological involvement (acute neuro-BD) was established based on the cumulative scoring system of the International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease. To be classified as definite BD, a patient requires a total score of **≥4 points**.

Based on the patient’s clinical findings described in the report, the mucocutaneous features contributing to this_score were:

1.  **Oral Aphthae:** 2 points
2.  **Genital Aphthosis:** 2 points (presented as a painful scrotal ulcer in this case)
3.  **Folliculitis-like Rash:** 1 point

The total score calculated was **5 points**, fulfilling the threshold for definite systemic disease. While HLA-B51 was positive, the authors note that international criteria rely primarily on clinical features, and HLA genotyping does not solely determine the definitive diagnosis.

A key teaching point derived from this case is that **neurological symptoms and an asymptomatic or normal MRI brain scan** do not rule out BD. This patient demonstrated acute neuro-BD marked by aseptic meningitis (elevated CSF protein and lymphocytes without bacterial pathogens) but with no parenchymal MRI abnormalities. Because neuro-BD typically presents several years after initial mucocutaneous onset, a concurrent diagnosis of acute neuro-BD requires strong end-organ variation scoring in the mucocutaneous domain to satisfy the systemic diagnostic definition. This underscores the necessity of confirming mucocutaneous criteria alongside neurological suspicion to avoid misdiagnosing SD with non-neuroBD etiologies.
