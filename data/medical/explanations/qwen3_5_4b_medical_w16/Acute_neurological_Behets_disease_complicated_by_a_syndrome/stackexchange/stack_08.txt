Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] In this case, brain MRI was initially normal despite clinical signs of CNS inflammation. How does this imaging finding alter the differential diagnosis compared to typical neuro-Behçet's disease?

In this context, a normal brain MRI does not exclude the diagnosis of acute neurological Behçet's disease (neuro-BD) and necessitates a shift in diagnostic weighting from structural imaging to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis and clinical exclusion.

While typical neuro-BD often presents with delayed onset (years after mucocutaneous diagnosis) and parenchymal lesions, frequently involving the brainstem, this case represented a rare concurrent onset of acute neuro-BD and systemic disease. Consequently, the absence of MRI abnormalities suggests an early inflammatory phase where axillary and parenchymal changes may not yet be radiologically evident, or that the inflammation is meningeal rather than parenchymal at presentation.

This imaging finding alters the differential diagnosis by compelling clinicians to rely on CSF analysis and systematic exclusion of mimics. As described in the case report, while the MRI was normal, the CSF demonstrated aseptic meningitis (mild lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, increased opening pressure). Without radiation findings, the diagnosis becomes contingent upon ruling out infectious causes (such as HSV/VZV encephalitis or bacterial meningitis) and malignancies. The presence of a normal MRI specifically in the context of severe hyponatraemia (SIADH) in this patient highlights that endocrine complications can arise from CNS inflammation even when structural lesions are not yet visualized. Thus, the diagnostic pathway pivots to satisfying clinical inflammatory criteria supported by CSF evidence and the absence of mimics, ensuring that a normal MRI does not hinder the recognition of early neuro-Behçet's disease.
