Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Management] What pharmacological monotherapy resulted in the resolution of neurological symptoms and hyponatremia in this case without the use of corticosteroids, contrasting with standard management?

In this case of acute neurological Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD) complicated by syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), **oral colchicine monotherapy** was the agent that led to the resolution of neurological symptoms and hyponatremia without the use of corticosteroids. While corticosteroids are considered the mainstay of treatment for acute neuro-BD, colchicine resolved the patient’s fever, mucocutaneous lesions, ventricular symptoms, and severe hyponatremia (124 mmol/L) over two weeks, normalizing serum sodium to 136 mmol/L and reducing ADH levels.

The clinical reasoning relies on the understanding that neuro-BD is driven by CNS inflammation mediated by pro-inflammatory cytokines, specifically IL-6. The case report suggests that colchicine, which inhibits neutrophil migration and suppresses cytokine production (including IL-1β and IL-6), effectively modulated this inflammatory cascade in this specific context. Despite the fact that literature reviews generally classify colchicine as ineffective for acute neuro-BD, this case underscores the importance of individualized management and the association between neuro-BD and SIADH. Clinicians should consider colchicine as a viable monotherapy option for acute neuro-BD, particularly in patients where corticosteroid toxicity is a concern or when rapid recovery of neurological function and fluid balance is observed.
