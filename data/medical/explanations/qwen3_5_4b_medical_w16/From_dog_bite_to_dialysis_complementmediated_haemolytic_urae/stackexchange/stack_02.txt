Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What specific laboratory finding definitively excludes Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) as a diagnosis of exclusion in a patient presenting with Thrombotic Microangiopathy?

In a patient presenting with the multisystemic triad of microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA), thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury, the specific laboratory finding that definitively excludes Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) is a normal ADAMTS13 activity level.

TTP is pathophysiologically distinct from other forms of Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA) due to the acquired severe deficiency of the ADAMTS13 metalloprotease. According to the guideline cited in the Evansen et al. case report, TTP is defined by an ADAMTS13 activity level below 10%. Therefore, in the presence of significant TMA features, any ADAMTS13 activity level above 10% (typically reported as >10% or specifically in the clinical range of 30–100% for this case context) effectively rules out TTP.

This distinction is critical in our case report by Fredriksen et al. The patient presented with features consistent with sepsis-related DIC, which evolved into a TMA phenotype following *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* infection. Characterization of her condition relied on laboratory confirmation. Although standard complement studies (C3/C4) were normal, the specific diagnostic discriminator was ADAMTS13 activity, which was reported at 43.5%, effectively ruling out TTP. This corroborated a diagnosis of Complement-Mediated HUS (CM-HUS).

Clinically, this lab finding dictates management strategy. TTP is a medical emergency requiring immediate plasma exchange (PEX). However, as seen in the case report, misdiagnosing CM-HUS as TTP and relying solely on PEX may lead to suboptimal outcomes and prolonged transfusion requirements. Conversely, initiating complement blockade (eculizumab) without excluding TTP first carries risks if TTP is active. Thus, the ADAMTS13 assay remains the indispensable laboratory test to triage TMA patients correctly, ensuring the initiation of targeted therapy over broad empirical supportive care.
