Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Management] Why was Plasma Exchange (PEX) used as a bridging therapy rather than a definitive treatment, and what is the therapeutic limitation of PEX in this specific context compared to eculizumab?

In this case, plasma exchange (PEX) was employed as bridging therapy rather than definitive treatment because it provides only transient stabilization, whereas eculizumab offers the necessary pathophysiological blockade required for sustained remission. The patient had complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome (CM-HUS) triggered by *Capnocytophaga canimorsus* sepsis, a diagnosis confirmed after excluding thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) by a normal ADAMTS13 activity (43.5%). While PEX clears circulating complement activation products and inflammatory mediators, the case report explicitly states it "provides only temporary benefit in CM-HUS, mainly by removing circulating complement fragments, inflammatory mediators or autoantibodies, without correcting the underlying complement system dysfunction." In contrast, eculizumab provides definitive therapy by targeting the terminal complement component C5, preventing the formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC) that drives endothelial injury. The patient’s clinical course highlighted this limitation: while "spontaneous diuresis returned... following the second and third PEX sessions," she required "sustained haematological and renal recovery" only after the initiation of eculizumab to suppress complement activation at its source. Thus, PEX was a critical temporary measure to stabilize the patient while the definitive complement blockade of eculizumab took effect and the infection was cleared.
