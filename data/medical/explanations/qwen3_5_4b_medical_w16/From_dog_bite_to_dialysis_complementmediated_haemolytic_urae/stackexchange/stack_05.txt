Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Pathophysiology] Why do conventional serum complement levels (C3/C4) fail to rule out complement-mediated HUS during the acute active phase, and how does the functional assay (alternative pathway) clarify this?

**Clinical Teaching Answer**

In the acute phase of complement-mediated TMA (CM-TMA), conventional serum complement levels (C3/C4) frequently remain within normal limits, rendering them unreliable for exclusion of the diagnosis. The serum concentrations of C3 and C4 are static markers of protein synthesis and consumption; however, in the setting of infection-triggered TMA like that seen in this patient with *Capnocytophaga canimorsus*, the complement activation driven by dysregulated regulation of the alternative pathway does not always result in sufficient consumption to drop levels below the laboratory reference range. As the case report notes, "Complement levels (C3/C4) may be normal during the disease course... normal complement levels (C3/C4) do not exclude CM-HUS."

The functional assay, specifically quantitative alternative pathway activity (AH50), clarifies the mechanism and management strategy by assessing the actual regulatory capacity of the pathway rather than relying on factor concentrations. In this case, while initial C3/C4 and all standard functional assays were normal, follow-up testing six weeks later showed AH50 was markedly reduced to 6% following the initiation of eculizumab therapy. This reduction confirmed target-mediated suppression of the terminal complement, validating that the alternative pathway was the dominant driver of the microangiopathy even when C3/C4 levels appeared benign. This distinction is clinically critical as it redirects diagnostic focus from protein conservation (levels) to pathway regulation (function), guiding the immediate initiation of eculizumab despite normal conventional complement workup.
