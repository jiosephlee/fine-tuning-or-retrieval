Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Which imaging modality provides the highest diagnostic yield for visualizing the actual pancreatic ductal communication with the pleural space, given that standard CT often fails to depict the fistula itself?

Based on the case report provided, **Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)** provides the highest diagnostic yield for visualizing the actual pancreatic ductal communication with the pleural space, paralleling ductal patency when contrast-enhanced CT is insufficient.

Although the patient in the report presented with a clear history of abdominal trauma and recurrent hemorrhagic pleural effusions amply supported by fluid analysis (amylase 6118 U/L), the right intercostal drain placed initially did not solve the problem due to the diagnostic uncertainty of the origin. While the contrast-enhanced CT identified a hypodense collection tracking from the pancreatic tail, the discussion explicitly states that such modality "depicts the actual fistula in only 33%–47%" of cases. Consequently, the subsequent MRCP was critical for confirmation.

MRCP was superior in this instance because it visualized the "tubular cystic collection... extending posteriorly in the retroperitoneum and then tracking superiorly... into the right pleural cavity" and identified the "narrow communication with the distal pancreatic duct." The case highlights that MRCP offers 70%–80% sensitivity for the visualization of the fistula and duct anatomy, significantly outperforming CT in this specific anatomical context. Recognizing this "pulmonary mask" effect allows clinicians to pivot from presumption of thoracic pathology to abdominal etiology, guiding definitive management—such as the initiation of total parenteral nutrition and somatostatin analogues—based on accurate localization of the fistula tract.
