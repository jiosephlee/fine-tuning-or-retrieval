Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

Chapter 4: ## Conservative Management and Drainage Interventions

The management of Pancreatopleural Fistula (PPF) presents a unique clinical challenge due to the rarity of the condition and the lack of standardized treatment guidelines, particularly within the pediatric population. In this case, the therapeutic approach prioritized conservative measures over immediate surgical intervention, relying on the physiological principle that the fistula tract requires a period of ductal rest to close spontaneously. The primary objective of this phase was to minimize the volume and pressure of pancreatic fluid leakage while ensuring the patient remained hemodynamically stable and nutritionally adequate. In the case presented, the patient was immediately placed on a nil per oral (NPO) status upon admission to the Pediatric Intensive Care Unit. Strict bowel rest is a foundational component of PPF management because dietary intake, particularly oral food, stimulates pancreatic exocrine function via vagovagal reflexes. This stimulation exacerbates the enzyme output and increases intraductal pressure, effectively widening the fistula tract. Consequently, the patient was placed on a total parenteral nutrition (TPN) protocol to provide essential hydration and calories without engaging the gastrointestinal tract. This nutritional strategy allowed the pancreatic system to divert energy toward ductal closure rather than exocrine secretion, facilitating the reduction of leakage into the pleural cavity.

To further suppress the exocrine drive and reduce ductal pressure, specific pharmacological intervention was initiated. Octreotide, a somatostatin analogue, was started at a dose of 30 µg/kg/day (within the recommended range of 10–40 µg/kg/day). Octreotide inhibits the release of various hormones, including insulin and glucagon, and has the unique ability to decrease splanchnic blood flow and pancreatic secretions. By inhibiting these enzymatic outputs, the pharmacological regimen complemented the bowel rest strategy, aiming to create a conducive environment for the fistulized tract to seal without pressure. Percutaneous or surgical drainage was deemed necessary given the recurrence of pleural effusions despite initial attempts at thoracocentesis. The patient received multiple thoracocentesis performed under aseptic precautions, which temporarily relieved respiratory distress, but fluid accumulation recurred rapidly. This necessitated the insertion of an intercostal drain (ICD) during the patient’s third admission. The ICD served a dual purpose: sustained decompression of the pleural space to prevent respiratory compromise and ongoing monitoring of fluid output. Daily monitoring of the drain allowed the clinical team to correlate fluid output with clinical improvement or disease progression, guiding the decision to proceed with ICD removal once the flow ceased.

The diagnostic confirmation of PPF guided the discontinuation of concurrent empiric therapies and the confirmation of the management plan. Following the insertion of the ICD and the observation of dark red pleural fluid, imaging and laboratory markers provided the definitive etiology. Pleural fluid analysis revealed significantly elevated amylase (6118 U/L) and lipase (7211 U/L), alongside a contrast-enhanced CT and Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) that visualized the pancreatic duct tracking to the pleural space. Although the patient had a family history of tuberculosis and slightly elevated pleural fluid Adenosine Deaminase (ADA), the absence of fever, negative GeneXpert results, and the high enzymatic profile ruled out active infection. Consequently, the empiric antitubercular drugs were halted immediately. Additionally, Pancreatic Enzyme Replacement Therapy (PERT) was started as an adjunctive measure. While the primary rationale in this context was the suppression of enzyme output, the addition of PERT supported patients who may have mild insufficiency or who require enzyme modulation during ductal healing. Over a period of ten days, concordant with the reduction in drain output, the fluid output gradually decreased. The successful plug of the fistula tract is evident when these drainages stop, at which point the intercostal drain and monitoring can be safely terminated. In this instance, the successful resolution led to the removal of the ICD and discharge of the child with no further episodes, highlighting a protocol of conservative management that effectively controlled the pathology without surgical morbidity.
