Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] Based on the successful course off semaglutide and immunosuppression, what guidance is advised for patients regarding the use of potentially causative medications in future clinical encounters?

Based on this case, the primary guidance for patients is to avoid future re-exposure to GLP-1 receptor agonists, as the cessation of these agents, rather than immunosuppression alone, was essential for sustaining remission. The authors emphasize that while the patient responded well to corticosteroids and IVIG, she was successfully weaned off immunosuppression only after her semaglutide was discontinued for several months. Sustained functional improvement over 10 months was achieved through the long-term avoidance of the suspected trigger.

Because the clinical course aligns with an immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) that can mimic autoimmunity in this context, the experience necessitates caution in future clinical encounters for metabolic management. Dr. Lam et al. explicitly noted that a rechallenge would be unethical given the severity of the initial reaction and the probable causality. Therefore, patients who develop proximal muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase, or dysphagia associated with weight-loss or diabetes medication initiation should be counseled to discontinue or avoid these specific medications to prevent recurrence.
