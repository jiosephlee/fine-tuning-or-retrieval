Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] In a patient with a history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and elevated CK, what was the clinical interpretation of simultaneously elevated transaminases that did not normalize upon CK normalization?

The clinical interpretation was that the simultaneous elevation in transaminases reflected the patient’s underlying Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD), rather than muscle necrosis or drug-induced hepatotoxicity. This determination relies on the kinetic dissociation between muscle and liver markers. While muscle breakdown can release both AST and ALT, these levels should normalize concurrently with creatine kinase (CK) if the source is myopathy. In this specific case, the patient’s transaminases (AST 191 U/L and ALT 165 U/L) persisted even after CK levels normalized following immunosuppression and semaglutide discontinuation. Given her biopsy-proven hepatosteatosis three years prior, the authors concluded that the transaminases indicated chronic hepatic pathology, effectively ruling out muscle-derived enzyme elevation.

**Clinical Pearl:**
In the setting of concurrent myopathy and elevated liver enzymes, transaminases that persist despite normalization of CK levels should be attributed to intrinsic hepatic disease (e.g., NAFLD) rather than muscle breakdown.
