Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Which autoantibodies are most strongly associated with immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, and what additional diagnostic challenge is presented by a patient who tested seronegative for these markers?

**Teaching Point: Anti-HMGCR and Anti-SRP in IMNM**

Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) is primarily defined by the presence of autoantibodies targeting the hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A reductase (HMGCR) or the signal recognition particle (SRP). However, a critical diagnostic challenge arises for approximately one-third of patients who are seronegative for these classic markers.

In this case report, the patient was seronegative for both SRP and HMGCR antibodies. Her diagnosis relied not on serology but on muscle biopsy findings of necrotizing myofibres with inflammatory infiltrates and macrophages, followed by clinical response to immunosuppression. This highlights that in seronegative cases, clinicians must maintain a high index of suspicion based on clinical features and histopathology rather than antibody panels alone. Furthermore, as noted in the background section, the absence of antibodies in this subset suggests that unidentified drugs or triggers—such as the GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide described here—may induce IMNM without the typical serological profile.
