Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Clinical Pearls] Why were the presence of dysphagia and dysphonia considered critical findings supporting a diagnosis of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy with bulbar involvement?

Dysphagia and dysphonia were critical findings because they signaled a severe extension of the inflammatory myopathy into the cranial musculature, specifically confirming the presence of **bulbar involvement**. In the context of Immune-Mediated Necrotising Myopathy (IMNM), this extension distinguishes the pathology as a systemic feature requiring aggressive management, rather than a localized toxic reaction.

As detailed in the case report, the patient progressed to a state where she was "no longer able to stand or sit upright" and "noticed difficulty swallowing liquids and solids." These severe clinical features were instrumental in managing the patient as a "severe" IMNM case, warranting the prompt initiation of high-dose IV corticosteroids and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG). This intervention was crucial to confirm the diagnosis through clinical response; unlike the direct toxic effect seen in other drug-induced rhabdomyolysis (e.g., tirzepatide cases where CK normalizes within days after discontinuation), this patient's marked improvement correlated with immunosuppression and withdrawal from the offending agent. Therefore, the persistence of these bulbar symptoms alongside a 12-fold rise in CK supported the classification of an aggressive autoimmune necrotising myopathy requiring life-saving immunotherapy.
