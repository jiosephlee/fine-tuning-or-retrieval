Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] What was the recommended pharmacological intervention for this patient with severe immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, and what specific clinical indicators influenced the decision to include intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) in the treatment regimen?

For this patient with immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM), the recommended pharmacological intervention consisted of high-dose intravenous corticosteroids—specifically methylprednisolone (1000 mg daily for 5 days) followed by oral prednisone (1 mg/kg daily)—supplemented by a course of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) at 2 g/kg split over 4 days.

The inclusion of IVIG in the treatment regimen was driven by two specific clinical indicators: **persistent weakness** and the presence of **severe features**. Despite the initiation of pulse steroids for acute stabilization, the patient exhibited profound disabling weakness (unable to stand or sit upright without assistance) and bulbar dysfunction (dysphagia and dysphonia). These severe attributes influenced the decision to escalate immunosuppression with IVIG to mitigate potential respiratory compromise and accelerate functional recovery. The patient demonstrated a marked response to this combination therapy, allowing for a rapid steroid taper and eventual return to baseline functional status while remaining GLP-1 agonist-averse.
