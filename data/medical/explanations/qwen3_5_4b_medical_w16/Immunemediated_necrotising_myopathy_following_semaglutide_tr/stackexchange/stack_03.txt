Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] The muscle biopsy in this case revealed specific staining patterns; what immunohistochemical markers (e.g., CD68, acid phosphatase) were noted and how do they correlate with the diagnosis of necrotising myopathy despite the lack of classic autoantibodies?

In this case, the immunohistochemical evaluation provided definitive evidence for immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) via **acid phosphatase** and **CD68** staining, which were critical given the patient was seronegative for the classic anti-HMGCR and anti-SRP autoantibodies.

The presence of **CD68 staining** identified a macrophage infiltrate engaged in **myophagocytosis**, while **acid phosphatase** highlighted the necrotic and regenerating myofibers. This pattern is characteristic of IMNM, where necrotic myofibres are cleared primarily by macrophage activity rather than T-cell invasion. This histologic profile supports the diagnosis and distinguishes it from polymyositis, particularly in the context of the "paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates" typically described in IMNM.

This case reinforces that approximately one-third of patients with IMNM are seronegative. Therefore, when classic autoantibodies are absent, clinical suspicion, temporal association with an offending agent, and confirmatory muscle biopsy are essential. The immunohistochemical markers validate the necrotising pathology, justifying immunosuppressive treatment despite the absence of classic serology.
