Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

Chapter 3: ## Diagnostic Reasoning: Integrating Histology, Serology, and Differential Diagnosis

In this case of suspected immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM), the primary diagnostic challenge involved distinguishing between a direct toxic reaction to the GLP-1 receptor agonist, a latent autoimmune etiology, and a secondary stress response. The clinical team effectively utilized muscle histology as the definitive diagnostic standard, which revealed dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibres within the right vastus lateralis. In the context of necrotising myopathy, pathognomonic histological findings are the presence of necrotic myofibres surrounded by macrophages, evidenced here by the presence of CD68 staining indicative of myophagocytosis. A critical teaching point for future learners is that IMNM is characterised by profound necrosis driven by complement-dependent mechanisms rather than T-cell mediated inflammation; hence, the biopsy in this patient showed a notable paucity of CD3-positive T-cells, which typically distinguishes necrotising myopathy from polymyositis. This low inflammatory infiltrate, combined with the membrane attack complex positivity on necrotising and non-necrotising myofibres, confirmed a necrotising pattern over a hyperinflammatory myopathy, although it highlighted the immunological complexity of the patient's presentation.

Serological evaluation often serves as the second pillar of diagnosis, yet it presented a significant discordancy here despite being consistent with the biomarkers present in other forms of IMNM. The patient’s autoantibody panel was negative for anti-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMGCR) and anti-signal recognition particle (SRP), the antibodies most strongly associated with classic IMNM and statin toxicity respectively. Furthermore, her myositis panel was negative for Jo-1, MDA-5, and other primary inflammatory myopathy markers. This negative serological profile underscores the acknowledged fact that an estimated third of patients with IMNM are seronegative; therefore, the absence of autoantibodies should not disqualify a diagnosis of necrotising myopathy when clinical and pathologic features are aligned. The patient did exhibit a low titre positive SS-B autoantibody, which is unusual in the setting of isolated negative ANA and SS-A, suggesting either a coincidental finding or a complex autoantibody profile not yet fully characterised in the literature regarding myopathy. In the absence of a compatible autoantibody profile, the team relied on the histopathology and the clinical timeline to track causality.

To differentiate drug-induced toxicity from overt autoimmune myopathy, the team analysed the temporal relationship between the offending agent and the pathological process, alongside ruling out stress-induced causes. In typical cases of drug-induced toxicity or rhabdomyolysis associated with other GLP-1 agonists like tirzepatide, one expects a sharp increase in creatine kinase resolved within days of drug withdrawal without immunosuppression. Conversely, this patient demonstrated a progressive rise in CK that peaked at 3992 U/L and persisted for four months, an escalation in duration inconsistent with acute toxic rhabdomyolysis and more consistent with a chronic autoimmune process. A crucial aspect of the differential reasoning was excluding stress myopathy induced by immobilization or the acute illness, as stress myopathy often resolves with rehabilitation and session control rather than requires systemic immunosuppression. Additionally, the exclusion of other red flags, such as interstitial lung disease on high resolution CT chest and lymphadenopathy on MRI, helped narrow the focus away from systemic autoimmune connective tissue disease and确立了 the localized muscle pathology.

Ultimately, the integration of these diagnostic modalities allowed for a misleadingly negative serology to be reconciled with a robust clinical diagnosis. The decision to proceed with immunosuppression based on pulse methylprednisolone and IVIG rather than solely on temporal deduction was supported by the need to distinguish the current myopathy from the chronic NAFLD-related transaminitisation (which is hepatic in origin rather than myopathic). The resolution of the patient's weakness and normalisation of CK following cessation of semaglutide and course of immunosuppression provided the necessary evidence to support probable causality per the WHO-Uppsala Causality Criteria. The final diagnostic reasoning taught from this report emphasizes that even with negative autoantibodies and serology, a biopsy demonstrating necrotising myofibres with myophagocytosis in a patient with temporal association to a pharmacological agent warrants a trial of immunomodulation to exclude idiopathic pathology and identify the specific adverse reaction.
