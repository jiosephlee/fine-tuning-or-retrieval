Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

Chapter 2: ## Clinical Presentation and Timeline: Unmasking the Revascularization Defect

The clinical evolution of immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) following semaglutide treatment requires a meticulous analysis of the temporal relationship between drug exposure and symptom onset, as this distinction is vital for differentiating drug-induced adverse events from coincidental or pre-existing autoimmune conditions.

### Baseline Medical History: Obesity, NAFLD, and Hypothyroidism Context
To understand the significance of the symptom progression, one must first contextualize the patient's baseline health. The female patient presented a complex history often seen in metabolic syndrome, including obesity, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), hypertension, hypothyroidism, and a remote history of deep venous thrombosis. Her liver function tests had shown mild transaminase elevations (AST and ALT approx. twice normal range) three years prior, consistent with a diagnosis of NAFLD confirmed by hepatic biopsy. This baseline was stable, and she was a functional independent adult prior to the clinical deterioration. Recognizing this "pre-adaptive" state helps the clinician rule out underlying hepatic failure or pre-existing connective tissue disease features that might complicate the diagnosis, ensuring the new symptoms are viewed as distinct from her chronic metabolic profile.

### Acute Event: Initial Semaglutide Intolerance (GI, Renal, Metabolic Acidosis)
The trigger for the subsequent diagnosis was the administration of the glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, semaglutide. The temporal relationship is the defining feature of drug-related toxicity. Hours after her first dose, the patient experienced an acute gastrointestinal reaction characterized by nausea and vomiting. This progressed to a hospitalization for nausea, vomiting, poor oral intake, dyspnea, acute renal failure, and severe metabolic acidosis. While this initial presentation mirrored acute toxicity, it did not involve skeletal muscle symptoms initially. The serum creatine kinase (CK) was modestly elevated at 337 U/L, which is insufficient to classify as rhabdomyolysis. Notably, her symptoms resolved gradually after discontinuation of the drug agent, but without muscle-specific data in her discharge status, this period serves as the critical window to track the emergence of neurological and muscular sequelae against the background of the initial pharmacological insult.

### Subacute Course: The Emergence of Proximal Weakness and Dysphagia
Following the initial recovery from acute gastro-nephrotoxity, the patient entered a subacute phase distinct in clinical presentation. Approximately four months after receiving the single dose of semaglutide, she began experiencing progressive proximal muscle weakness. The onset of symptoms was subtle, peaking in severity over the second and third months. This weakness was accompanied by bulbar symptoms, specifically dysphagia (difficulty swallowing liquids and solids) and dysphonia. The patient reported an inability to stand or sit upright without assistance. This clinical trajectory—weakness progressing from the chest/shoulders to involve the jaw and pharynx—is highly characteristic of autoimmune myopathies. The timeline (4 months post-exposure) suggests an immune-mediated process that required time to develop rather than an immediate toxic effect or rhabdomyolysis.

### Symptom Distribution: Proximal vs. Distal, Bulbar Symptoms
The distribution of weakness was strictly proximal, sparing the distal extremities. The patient faced challenges primarily climbing stairs, rising from a chair, and arm abduction. Unlike polymyositis which can also affect distal muscles, the myopathic distribution of weakness due to IMNM is typically rapid and severe in proximal muscles. Furthermore, the presence of dysphagia indicates involvement of the mouth floor and intoleraniy of the throat, which can be a precursor to respiratory failure. The absence of rashes, skin changes, or signs of photosensitivity helps distinguish this from dermatomyositis. The distribution highlights the involvement of the limb girdles and bulbar muscles, consistent with the histological finding of widespread myofiber necrosis.

### Laboratory Trajectory: The 12-fold Rise in Creatine Kinase (CK)
Laboratory monitoring provided the crucial evidence distinguishing the etiology. On admission for the weakness, CK was elevated to 3992 U/L, a 12-fold increase from admission value and a significant rise from the baseline elevation during acute intolerance. This rapid and substantial increase in CK activity is suggestive of muscle breakdown, specifically necrosis. While rhabdomyolysis follows a similar pattern, it typically occurs acutely (within hours). A gradual, sustained rise in CK over four months points toward an evolving inflammatory process. The lack of corollary features like severe dehydration at the time of the peak CK, nor significant hematuria, further points away from urine-facing rhabdomyolysis. The persistence of elevated CK despite hydration and improvement post-immunosuppression reinforces the autoimmune nature of the myopathy.

### Distinguishing Early CK Elevation from Rhabdomyolysis Risk
A key teaching point is differentiating the initial admission (acute heart-failure-like picture with mild CK elevation) from the subacute phase (marked muscle weakness with severe CK elevation). In the case of semaglutide-associated rhabdomyolysis cases, typically, the CK elevation resolves rapidly (within days) upon drug discontinuation without the need for immunosuppression. In contrast, this patient had a 12-fold elevation sustained over four months, suggesting an underlying autoimmune process triggered by the drug. Furthermore, liver function tests (AST and ALT) remained elevated relative to CK normalisation, indicating a chronic hepatic component (NAFLD) rather than muscle breakdown. This distinction is critical for management, as it shifts the treatment paradigm from aggressive hydration and supportive care towards immunosuppression.
