Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] What specific pathophysiological distinction exists between the platelet-activating antibodies in classic HIT (membrane Fc receptor clustering via PF4-heparin complexes) and the antibody profile described in this case of autoimmune HIT (aHIT)?

The fundamental pathophysiological distinction lies in the requirement for heparin-PF4 complex formation versus heparin-independent platelet activation. In classic HIT, IgG antibodies target the platelet factor 4 (PF4)-heparin complex; these immune complexes deposit on the platelet membrane, binding to Fc receptors (FcγRII/III) to initiate signal transduction clustering. This mechanism is strictly dependent on the polyanionic properties of heparin to stabilize the immune complex.

In contrast, the antibodies in autoimmune HIT (aHIT) induce platelet activation independently of heparin binding to PF4. As demonstrated in this case, the patient’s serum elicited a serotonin-release assay (SRA) result of 96% release at 0 U/mL unfractionated heparin. Classic HIT antibodies exhibit a baseline release of less than 20% in the absence of heparin because no immune complex can form. Furthermore, this patient’s antibodies displayed partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade (76% serotonin release remained unblocked despite monoclonal antibody IV.3), distinguishing this profile from the near-complete inhibition seen in classic HIT where the IgG-mediated clustering is the primary trigger.

The presence of heparin-independent activation and partial Fc receptor blockade, confirmed by the unique SRA profile in this case, defines the autoimmune phenotype, necessitating specialized management strategies that exceed standard HIT protocols.
