Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Pathophysiology] Which specific findings on the serotonin-release assay (SRA) – specifically regarding heparin concentration and monoclonal antibody inhibition – distinguish this patient's serum as having heparin-independent platelet-activating properties inherent to aHIT?

**Teaching Point:**
While classic heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) is characterized by heparin-dependent platelet-activating IgG antibodies, the serotonin-release assay (SRA) identifies autoimmune HIT (aHIT) by demonstrating heparin-independent activation and partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade. In this patient, two specific SRA findings distinguished aHIT:

1.  **Heparin-Independent Reactivity:** Classic HIT antibodies require the heparin-PF4 complex to trigger platelet activation. Therefore, SRA reactivity at 0 U/mL of heparin should be undetected. In this case, the patient’s serum elicited **96% serotonin release in the absence of UFH (0 U/mL)**, confirming the antibodies can trigger platelet activation independently of heparin.
2.  **Partial Resistance to Fc Receptor Blockade:** Classic HIT antibodies are typically neutralized by monobasic Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody (IV.3), resulting in serotonin release below 20%. This patient exhibited **significant residual activation (76% release) in the presence of the monoclonal antibody**, indicating incomplete blockade. This suggests the aHIT antibodies bind to Fc receptors with sufficient avidity to drive activation despite the presence of the blocking antibody.

These atypical serological profiles, combined with the clinical context of early postoperative thrombocytopenia and cerebral thrombosis, confirm the diagnosis of severe aHIT rather than classic HIT.
