Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] SIADH has been previously reported in Behçet's disease patients receiving cyclophosphamide. How does the SIADH in this case differ from prior reports, and why is the temporal resolution of hyponatremia following colchicine therapy significant for understanding the relationship between neuro-BD and SIADH?

**How SIADH in This Case Differs from Prior Reports**

In previously reported cases of SIADH occurring in patients with Behçet's disease, the syndrome was attributed to cyclophosphamide therapy — a well-recognized pharmacologic trigger of inappropriate ADH release. In those cases, SIADH was fundamentally a drug-induced adverse effect, temporally and causally linked to medication exposure rather than to the underlying disease process itself.

This case is fundamentally different. The patient developed SIADH in the complete absence of cyclophosphamide or any other known pharmacologic trigger. No offending drug could be implicated. Instead, SIADH appeared concurrently with acute neuro-Behçet's disease, presenting as aseptic meningitis with lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated CSF protein, but without parenchymal brain lesions on MRI. The SIADH was therefore most plausibly linked to the CNS inflammatory process itself. The authors propose an IL-6–mediated mechanism: aberrant monocyte activation in BD drives spontaneous IL-6 production, IL-6 is elevated in the CSF of patients with acute neuro-BD, and IL-6 is known to stimulate ADH release. While IL-6 levels were not directly measured in this patient, the markedly elevated CRP (180 mg/L) is consistent with an IL-6–driven inflammatory response. Thus, this case represents SIADH arising from the disease's neuroinflammatory activity rather than from an iatrogenic trigger — a distinction that shifts the conceptual framework from a medication side effect to a potential direct complication of neuro-BD.

**Why the Temporal Resolution After Colchicine Is Significant**

The significance lies in what happened — and what did not happen — during treatment. The patient received colchicine monotherapy alone, without corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants. Colchicine inhibits neutrophil migration and suppresses cytokine production, including IL-1β and IL-6. Over the following two weeks, the patient's mucocutaneous lesions, neurological symptoms (headache, vertigo, hiccups), serum sodium (124 → 136 mmol/L), and ADH levels (22.4 → 1.8 pg/mL) all normalized in parallel.

This parallel resolution is important for two reasons. First, it provides temporal evidence that SIADH was mechanistically tied to the neuro-BD inflammatory process: as colchicine suppressed the inflammation driving the neurological disease, the stimulus for inappropriate ADH secretion abated, and water homeostasis normalized. If SIADH had been an independent or coincidental process, one would not necessarily expect it to resolve in lockstep with the neurological and mucocutaneous manifestations following a single anti-inflammatory intervention. Second, it demonstrates that treating the underlying inflammation — rather than fluid restriction alone, which was employed but is a supportive measure — addressed the root cause. While spontaneous resolution cannot be entirely excluded, the close temporal coupling between anti-inflammatory treatment and the simultaneous resolution of both neuro-BD and SIADH strongly supports a causal or at least mechanistically linked relationship between CNS inflammation in neuro-BD and dysregulated ADH secretion.

**Clinical Takeaway**

When you encounter a patient with Behçet's disease who presents with headache, fever, and hyponatremia, SIADH should be considered not merely as a drug effect but as a potential direct complication of neuro-BD–associated CNS inflammation. Recognizing this association expands the differential diagnosis for hyponatremia in BD beyond medication side effects and may guide treatment decisions, as controlling the neuroinflammatory process can address both the neurological and endocrine manifestations simultaneously.
