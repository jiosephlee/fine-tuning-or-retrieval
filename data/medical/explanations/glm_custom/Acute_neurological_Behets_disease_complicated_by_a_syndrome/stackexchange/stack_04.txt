Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] This patient had a normal brain MRI at presentation. Does a normal brain MRI exclude the diagnosis of acute neuro-BD, and what CSF findings supported the diagnosis of aseptic meningitis in this case?

A normal brain MRI does not exclude acute neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD). As illustrated by this case, acute neuro-BD may present as aseptic meningitis without parenchymal brain lesions, and radiological findings can be entirely normal, particularly early in the disease course. The absence of MRI abnormalities — even at typical sites such as the brainstem or hypothalamus — should therefore not dissuade clinicians from the diagnosis when clinical and CSF findings are suggestive.

The CSF findings supporting aseptic meningitis in this patient included a mildly elevated opening pressure (17 cmH₂O), increased protein (0.97 g/L), and a mild lymphocytic pleocytosis (11 cells/µL). Crucially, extensive microbiological evaluation was negative, including CSF cultures, acid-fast staining and tuberculosis DNA testing, and assays for HSV, VZV, and cryptococcal antigen. CSF cytology revealed no malignant cells. This pattern — inflammatory CSF profile with lymphocytic predominance and exclusion of infectious and neoplastic causes by negative cultures, molecular testing, and cytology — established the diagnosis of aseptic meningitis.

The key teaching point is that in neuro-BD, the diagnosis is fundamentally clinical and relies on the combination of characteristic systemic features (oral aphthae, genital ulcers, folliculitis-like rash, as seen here), compatible neurological symptoms, and supportive but non-specific CSF findings, after rigorous exclusion of infection and malignancy. MRI may corroborate the diagnosis when parenchymal lesions are present, but a normal scan does not exclude it.
