Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Pathophysiology] HNF1A encodes a transcription factor expressed in the liver, kidneys, and pancreas. How do loss-of-function mutations in HNF1A lead to progressive beta-cell dysfunction, and which specific downstream target genes and enzymatic activities are impaired?

## Answer

HNF1A encodes hepatocyte nuclear factor 1-alpha, a transcription factor expressed in the liver, kidneys, and pancreas that regulates a network of genes essential for pancreatic β-cell function. As described in this case, loss-of-function mutations in HNF1A produce progressive β-cell dysfunction through impairment of several critical downstream targets:

**Impaired insulin gene expression.** HNF1A directly regulates transcription of the insulin gene. Reduced HNF1A activity diminishes insulin synthesis, leaving β-cells unable to meet secretory demand, particularly as glucose excursions worsen over time.

**Reduced GLUT2 expression.** GLUT2 is the glucose transporter responsible for glucose entry into β-cells and serves as the proximal sensor for glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. When HNF1A function is impaired, GLUT2 expression falls, blunting the β-cell's ability to sense and respond to ambient glucose. This explains the marked postprandial hyperglycemia observed on oral glucose tolerance testing in this patient (2-hour glucose of 19.8 mmol/L).

**Decreased pyruvate kinase activity.** Pyruvate kinase is a key glycolytic enzyme whose activity is regulated downstream of HNF1A. Reduced activity impairs glycolytic flux, limiting ATP generation from glucose metabolism. Since glucose-stimulated insulin secretion depends on ATP-mediated closure of ATP-sensitive potassium channels, depolarization, calcium influx, and insulin granule exocytosis, diminished pyruvate kinase activity directly attenuates this secretory cascade. This is consistent with the patient's glucagon stimulation test demonstrating insufficient C-peptide response (stimulated C-peptide 0.83 nmol/L), confirming impaired β-cell reserve.

**Reduced SGLT2 expression in renal tubules.** Beyond the pancreas, HNF1A transcriptionally controls SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubule. Reduced SGLT2 expression lowers the renal threshold for glucose reabsorption, producing glycosuria even at modestly elevated glucose levels—a feature noted at presentation in this patient and characteristic of HNF1A-MODY.

In this case, the novel heterozygous p.N270S variant resides in the DNA-binding domain of HNF1A. Mutations in this domain impair the protein's ability to bind promoter regions of its target genes, thereby reducing transcription of insulin, GLUT2, and other metabolic genes while diminishing pyruvate kinase activity. The cumulative effect is a progressive decline in glucose-stimulated insulin secretion—initially manifesting as postprandial hyperglycemia and glycosuria, and eventually progressing to overt fasting hyperglycemia and symptomatic diabetes, as observed across three generations in this family.

This molecular framework also explains the treatment rationale: GLP-1 receptor agonists amplify the remaining β-cell secretory capacity through cAMP-mediated potentiation of insulin exocytosis, bypassing the impaired glycolytic sensing pathway—a strategy that proved effective in this patient after genetic confirmation.
