Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] The Acurate neo2 prosthesis was specifically selected for this third TAVI. What design characteristics of the Acurate neo2—such as supra-annular leaflet positioning, frame height, and commissural alignment features—make it suitable for implantation within a narrow anatomy containing two prior transcatheter valve frames, and how do these features compare to those of other self-expanding valves like the Evolut?

# Clinical Teaching Answer: Acurate neo2 Design Advantages in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV

In this case, the Acurate neo2 (size S) was selected for a third transcatheter valve implanted within two existing frames—a 27 mm Lotus (2014) and a 23 mm Evolut (2022)—in an anatomy where MSCT demonstrated a critically narrowed Evolut minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm. The rationale for this selection rests on three interrelated design features that address the unique constraints of triple-frame implantation.

**Supra-annular leaflet positioning** is the first key advantage. By seating the functional leaflets above the plane of the prior valve frames rather than within the narrowest portion of the stacked prostheses, the Acurate neo2 can achieve a larger effective orifice area than would be possible with an intra-annular design. In this patient, who had severe prosthetic stenosis (mean gradient 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm²) and was at high risk for prosthesis-patient mismatch, maximizing the residual orifice area was a central planning objective. The Evolut also features supra-annular leaflets, but the critical difference lies in the frame architecture surrounding those leaflets.

**Relatively low frame height** is the second distinguishing feature. The Acurate neo2 has a shorter stent frame compared to the Evolut, which has a taller frame that extends higher into the aortic root and ascending aorta. In a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV configuration, each additional frame adds metal burden to the root, narrows the neo-skirt space, and increases the risk of coronary ostial encroachment. The lower frame height of the Acurate neo2 was particularly relevant here because a high implantation strategy was planned—positioning the third valve at the level of the Evolut leaflets to pin the pre-existing leaflets outward against the stent frame, thereby preventing further inward folding and lumen reduction. A shorter frame made this high positioning feasible while minimizing the neo-skirt height below the coronary ostia, preserving both coronary flow and future coronary access.

**Commissural alignment capability** is the third consideration. The Acurate neo2 design facilitates rotational orientation of its commissures relative to the existing Evolut valve leaflets, as guided by preprocedural CT modeling. In this case, the third valve was deliberately aligned with the Evolut commissures to avoid placing stent struts directly across the coronary ostia. While the Evolut also supports commissural alignment, the combination of the Acurate neo2's lower frame height and alignment features was considered more favorable for maintaining coronary access through the cumulative triple-frame mesh. The case report explicitly notes that commissural alignment was a key consideration for preserving future coronary access, and the procedure was performed without coronary protection based on CT-confirmed feasibility.

In summary, the Acurate neo2 was selected over a second Evolut because its supra-annular leaflets optimize hemodynamics in the narrow residual lumen, its low frame height permits a high implantation strategy that pins prior leaflets open without exacerbating coronary obstruction risk, and its commissural alignment features help orient the new valve's struts away from coronary ostia. These features collectively address the central challenges of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV: maximizing effective orifice area while preserving coronary access through three overlapping stent frames. Despite these optimizations, the case demonstrates that severe prosthesis-patient mismatch may still occur (immediate post-procedure mean gradient 67 mm Hg), though gradual improvement was observed over follow-up, attributed to post-dilation–induced radial expansion of the Evolut frame and optimized commissural alignment.
