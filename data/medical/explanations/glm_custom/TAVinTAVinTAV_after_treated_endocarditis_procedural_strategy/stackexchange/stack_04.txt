Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] This patient presented with progressive dyspnea, exertional chest discomfort, dizziness, and fatigue 2 years after his second TAVI, with echocardiography showing a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an aortic valve area of 0.5 cm². What are the main mechanisms of structural valve deterioration in transcatheter bioprostheses, and how did the prior episode of infective endocarditis likely contribute to the degeneration process in this case?

## Structural Valve Deterioration After TAVI and the Role of Prior Endocarditis

Structural valve deterioration (SVD) in transcatheter bioprostheses is driven by several interrelated mechanisms. The primary process is **intrinsic leaflet degeneration**, involving progressive calcification, collagen fiber disruption, and thickening of the bioprosthetic tissue—most commonly bovine or porcine pericardium—which leads to leaflet stiffening, restricted opening, and ultimately a stenotic, regurgitant, or mixed dysfunction pattern. Over time, mechanical wear, repetitive leaflet flexion, and inflammatory processes can also promote **leaflet tear** and **pannus ingrowth** from the host tissue, further compromising valve mechanics. These mechanisms are accelerated by patient-specific factors such as younger age, metabolic disease, and altered hemodynamics across the prosthesis.

In this case, the patient demonstrated classic features of severe SVD two years after his TAV-in-TAV, with echocardiography revealing a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and an aortic valve area of only 0.5 cm², accompanied by moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The **mixed stenotic and regurgitant pattern** is highly characteristic of advanced bioprosthetic degeneration, reflecting both leaflet restriction from fibrosis and calcification (stenosis) and impaired leaflet coaptation from structural distortion (regurgitation).

The prior episode of **Enterococcus faecalis prosthetic valve endocarditis** likely accelerated this degenerative cascade. As the case report notes, the mechanism of valve failure was "multifactorial," with endocarditis contributing to **leaflet injury and subsequent mechanical restriction** superimposed on progressive structural degeneration of both prostheses. Bacterial endocarditis can damage bioprosthetic leaflets through direct infection of the tissue, leading to vegetations, leaflet perforation, and inflammatory destruction of the collagen matrix. Even after successful antimicrobial eradication—as confirmed in this patient by repeat blood cultures, transesophageal echocardiography, and CT—the residual structural damage to the leaflets is irreversible. The healing process itself can promote **fibrotic scarring and retraction** of the leaflet tissue, further stiffening the already degenerating bioprosthetic material and accelerating the transition from functional to severely dysfunctional valve performance.

This is precisely what was observed clinically: significant restenosis was already documented at the time endocarditis was diagnosed (mean gradient 35 mm Hg), and despite complete antimicrobial eradication, valve function continued to worsen over the subsequent 18 months. The MSCT finding of a minimum lumen diameter of only 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis confirmed that the structural damage was fixed and progressive, not related to ongoing infection. As the authors emphasize, once active infection was excluded, the decision-making for reintervention was driven by anatomical feasibility, hemodynamic severity, and surgical risk—the same framework applied to any patient with bioprosthetic valve failure.

This case underscores an important teaching point: **endocarditis is not only a life-threatening acute event but also a catalyst for accelerated structural valve deterioration**. Patients with prior PVE require vigilant long-term surveillance, as the healed but damaged valve is primed for earlier and more rapid degeneration than would be expected from time alone.
