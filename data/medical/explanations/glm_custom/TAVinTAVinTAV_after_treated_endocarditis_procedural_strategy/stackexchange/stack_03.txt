Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] Immediately after the third TAVI, this patient had a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg, an indexed aortic valve area of 0.4 cm²/m², and a Doppler velocity index of 0.24—consistent with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM). How is PPM defined and graded using indexed effective orifice area, and what are the major clinical consequences of severe PPM after transcatheter valve implantation?

## Answer

Prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM) occurs when the effective orifice area of a normally functioning prosthesis is too small relative to the patient's body size, generating abnormally high transvalvular gradients. PPM is defined and graded by the **indexed effective orifice area (iEOA)**, calculated as the prosthetic effective orifice area divided by body surface area. The standard echocardiographic thresholds, widely endorsed by the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging and others, are:

- **Not significant:** iEOA > 0.85 cm²/m²
- **Moderate PPM:** iEOA ≤ 0.85 but > 0.65 cm²/m²
- **Severe PPM:** iEOA ≤ 0.65 cm²/m² (some definitions use ≤ 0.60 cm²/m², with lower cutoffs applied in obese patients)

A Doppler velocity index (DVI) below 0.25 further supports the diagnosis of severe PPM, as DVI is independent of orifice area measurement assumptions. In this case, immediately after the third TAVI, the indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² and DVI of 0.24 placed the patient well within the severe PPM range, consistent with the markedly elevated mean gradient of 67 mm Hg.

The major clinical consequences of severe PPM after transcatheter valve implantation include:

1. **Persistent or recurrent heart failure symptoms** — less symptomatic relief and poorer functional capacity.
2. **Incomplete left ventricular mass regression** — chronic pressure overload sustains concentric LV hypertrophy.
3. **Higher persistent transvalvular gradients** — as demonstrated by the initial postprocedural gradient in this case.
4. **Increased mortality** — severe PPM is independently associated with worse short- and long-term survival.
5. **Accelerated structural valve deterioration** — elevated mechanical stress on the bioprosthetic leaflets may shorten valve longevity.
6. **Higher rates of heart failure hospitalization.**

Importantly, the hemodynamic compromise in this case was not purely intrinsic valve dysfunction but reflected the geometric constraints of three overlapping valve frames within a fixed aortic annulus — a setting in which even a normally functioning prosthesis can produce severe PPM. The marked improvement seen at 1 month (mean gradient 27 mm Hg, AVA 1.1 cm²) likely resulted from ongoing frame expansion, optimized commissural alignment, and the effects of high-pressure balloon post-dilation, illustrating that early postprocedural gradients in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV may overstate the ultimate hemodynamic result.
