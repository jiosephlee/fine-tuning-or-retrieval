Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] One month after the third TAVI, the mean transvalvular gradient decreased dramatically from 67 mm Hg to 27 mm Hg without any additional intervention. What mechanisms explain this delayed improvement in gradient, including the role of balloon post-dilation on the prior Evolut frame, commissural alignment, and frame remodeling over time?

# Delayed Gradient Improvement After TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV: Mechanisms and Clinical Reasoning

The dramatic reduction in mean transvalvular gradient from 67 mm Hg immediately post-procedure to 27 mm Hg at one month—without any additional intervention—is a striking but explicable phenomenon rooted in the biomechanics of self-expanding valve frames, the effects of balloon post-dilation on nested prostheses, and the importance of commissural alignment in multi-valve configurations.

## 1. Ongoing Frame Expansion and Remodeling

The third prosthesis implanted was an Acurate neo2, a self-expanding nitinol valve. Unlike balloon-expandable valves, which reach their final diameter at the time of deployment, self-expanding nitinol frames continue to exert outward radial force over days to weeks as they adapt to the surrounding anatomy. In this case, the Acurate neo2 was seated within two prior valve frames—a Lotus valve (2014) and an Evolut prosthesis (2022). The triple-stented anatomy created a rigid, concentric nesting environment that initially constrained full expansion of the third valve, producing the high immediate post-procedural gradient of 67 mm Hg with a calculated AVA of only 0.9 cm². Over the subsequent month, the continued outward radial force of the nitinol frame, combined with cyclic hemodynamic loading during systole, permitted gradual frame expansion and remodeling, resulting in the observed increase in AVA to 1.1 cm² and reduction in gradient to 27 mm Hg.

## 2. Balloon Post-Dilation and Partial Mechanical Expansion of the Evolut Frame

During the procedure, balloon post-dilation was performed using a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid ventricular pacing. While intentional frame fracture was not attempted, this high-pressure balloon dilation likely produced partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame. The Evolut prosthesis, also a self-expanding nitinol valve, had been constrained within the Lotus frame and may not have achieved its full expanded diameter at the time of its original implantation—or may have been further compressed by the degenerative process and infection. The balloon post-dilation served a dual purpose: it expanded the newly implanted Acurate neo2 and simultaneously applied outward force on the Evolut frame, partially remodeling its architecture. This mechanical intervention initiated a process of frame expansion that continued beyond the procedure itself, contributing to the delayed gradient improvement.

## 3. Commissural Alignment and Leaflet Mechanics

Commissural alignment between the Acurate neo2 and the Evolut was deliberately planned using preprocedural CT modeling. When commissures of nested valves are misaligned, the leaflets of the outer valve can project inward into the orifice of the inner valve, creating a physical barrier to leaflet opening and reducing the effective orifice area—a phenomenon particularly relevant in TAV-in-TAV configurations. By aligning the Acurate neo2 with the Evolut valve leaflets, the procedural team minimized this inward leaflet folding. Additionally, the high implantation strategy—placing the Acurate neo2 at the level of the Evolut leaflets—fixed the pre-existing Evolut leaflets against the stent frame, preventing them from prolapsing inward and obstructing flow. This alignment optimized the central flow dynamics and allowed the combined valve apparatus to function more efficiently as the frames continued to settle over the first month.

## 4. Resolution of Tissue Edema and Adaptive Remodeling

Although not explicitly discussed in the case, it is worth noting that acute procedural trauma, including balloon pre-dilation and post-dilation, can produce transient tissue edema and hematoma in the annular region. Resolution of this edema over the first weeks may also have contributed incrementally to improved valve mechanics and reduced gradient.

## Summary

The delayed improvement in gradient in this case is best explained by the interplay of three mechanisms: **(1)** continued self-expansion and remodeling of the nitinol Acurate neo2 frame within the rigid triple-stented anatomy; **(2)** partial mechanical expansion of the Evolut frame initiated by balloon post-dilation, with ongoing radial remodeling; and **(3)** optimized commissural alignment and high implantation preventing inward leaflet obstruction. Together, these factors allowed the nested valve apparatus to achieve a more favorable hemodynamic configuration over the first month, a phenomenon that underscores the importance of patience when evaluating early gradients after complex self-expanding valve interventions and the value of meticulous CT-guided procedural planning in multi-prosthesis configurations.
