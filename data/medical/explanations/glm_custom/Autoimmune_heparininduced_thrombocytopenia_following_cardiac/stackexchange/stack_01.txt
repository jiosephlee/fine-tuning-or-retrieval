Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the typical temporal window for the onset of HIT-related thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery, and what preoperative event in this patient explained the unusually early platelet count decline that began before postoperative day 5?

The typical temporal window for the onset of HIT-related thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery is between postoperative days 5 and 10 (inclusive). This timing reflects the ~5-day minimum required for generation of pathogenic anti-PF4/heparin IgG antibodies following a *first* heparin exposure. When thrombocytopenia begins before POD5 in a post-cardiac surgery patient, a preoperative heparin exposure within the prior ~1–2 weeks should be suspected, as this prior exposure "primes" the immune system such that re-exposure to heparin during surgery triggers an anamnestic antibody response and accelerated platelet activation.

In this patient, the unusually early platelet count decline—beginning on POD1 and reaching 50 × 10⁹/L by POD5—was explained by intraprocedural unfractionated heparin (2000 U) administered during transradial cardiac catheterization performed one week before surgery. This preoperative UFH exposure initiated anti-PF4/heparin antibody formation, so that the large intraoperative heparin dose (45,000 U for cardiopulmonary bypass) served as a rapid secondary immunizing stimulus, producing a "rapid-onset HIT" phenotype rather than the more typical 5- to 10-day postoperative onset. Only about 10% of cardiac surgery patients who develop HIT exhibit a platelet count fall before POD5, and prior preoperative heparin exposure is the key explanatory factor, as was documented here.
