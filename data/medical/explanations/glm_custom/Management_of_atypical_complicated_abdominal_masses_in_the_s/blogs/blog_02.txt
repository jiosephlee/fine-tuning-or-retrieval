Title: Blog about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

# CA-125 in Pregnancy: A Tale of False Positives and Physiological Peaks

## The Setup

A 40-year-old nulliparous woman, BMI 33, presents at 21 weeks gestation with severe abdominal pain and mild dyspnoea. Ultrasound reveals two large cystic abdominal masses — one 18 cm, the other 9.5 cm. Tumour markers? All normal except CA-125, which sits at 41.8 U/mL, up from 36 U/mL two weeks prior. The team anchors on that mildly elevated CA-125 and the imaging appearance, leaning toward a diagnosis of decidualised endometriomas. At laparotomy, the surprise: both masses are benign cystic leiomyomas — one pedunculated, twisted, and leaking, the other an intramural-subserosal myoma with cystic degeneration. No endometrioma. No malignancy. The CA-125 elevation? A physiological red herring amplified by the presence of myomas themselves.

## CA-125 in Pregnancy: What's Normal?

Here's where it gets interesting. CA-125 doesn't hold steady during pregnancy — it has a bimodal distribution. The median value peaks in the first trimester at approximately 23 U/mL, dips to a median of 14 U/mL in the second trimester, and rises again in the third trimester to a median of 21 U/mL. This patient's value of 41.8 U/mL at 21 weeks was clearly above the second-trimester median, which understandably raised concern. But "above median" and "above the oncologic cut-off of 35 U/mL" are two very different clinical thresholds, and in a pregnant patient, the standard cut-off has limited discriminatory value.

## The Benign Differential for an Elevated CA-125 Is Vast

The problem with CA-125 is that it is not an ovarian cancer marker — it is a peritoneal mesothelial reactivity marker. Anything that irritates the peritoneum can elevate it. The list of benign conditions associated with elevated CA-125 is long: endometriosis and adenomyosis (>19 U/mL), pelvic inflammatory disease, menstruation, early pregnancy, near-term pregnancy, and yes — even uterine leiomyomas. In this case, the patient had not one but two large degenerating myomas, one of which was torsed and leaking. That alone is more than enough to explain a CA-125 of 41.8 U/mL. The team's initial diagnostic lean toward endometrioma was understandable given the imaging appearance and the marker, but it illustrates a classic cognitive trap: anchoring on a single non-specific data point in a patient with low pretest probability of malignancy.

## Serial Measurements: Trend Over Threshold

The authors make an important point that deserves emphasis: isolated CA-125 measurements during pregnancy are essentially uninterpretable. Serial measurements, on the other hand, can provide useful trend information for differential diagnosis and follow-up. In this case, the trend from 36 to 41.8 U/mL over two weeks was interpreted as concerning, but this is a narrow window and the magnitude of change is small. A steeply rising serial CA-125 with escalating clinical symptoms might warrant more aggressive investigation, but a mildly elevated value with a slow upward drift in a pregnant patient with known large pelvic masses is well within the range of expected physiological and benign-pathology variation.

## Imaging Should Lead, Not Tumour Markers

The diagnostic backbone in this case was ultrasound followed by non-contrast MRI. Ultrasound identified the two cystic masses, their vascularisation, and the caudal mass's continuity with a posterior intramural-subserosal myoma. MRI without gadolinium — appropriately avoided in pregnancy — provided superior soft tissue characterisation, showing thick walls, inhomogeneous fluid content, and the relationship of the caudal mass to the uterine torus. Notably, MRI could not definitively identify a connecting stalk or feeding vessel for the cranial mass due to the sheer bulk of the gravid uterus, which is why the diagnosis of pedunculated leiomyoma was only confirmed intraoperatively. The CA-125 value added noise, not signal, to the diagnostic process.

## When to Operate During Pregnancy

The decision to proceed with laparotomy at 21+6 weeks was driven by clinical deterioration — worsening pain, dyspnoea from mass effect, and the risk of complications including torsion, rupture, and infection — not by the CA-125 value. The surgical team appropriately limited the intervention to the cranial mass, which had a twisted pedicle and was actively leaking, while leaving the caudal myoma in situ to minimise obstetric risk. Intraoperative fetal heart rate monitoring and prophylactic progesterone were used. The caudal mass, fixed in the pelvis, ultimately dictated the mode of delivery — elective caesarean at 37 weeks through the same surgical scar. The lesson: in pregnancy, the indication for surgery should be complications or oncologic suspicion, not an isolated mildly elevated marker.

## Key Take-Home Messages

- **CA-125 has physiological peaks in the first and third trimesters** (medians ~23 and ~21 U/mL respectively) and a nadir in the second trimester (~14 U/mL). A value of 41.8 U/mL at 21 weeks is elevated relative to the second-trimester median but remains in benign-disease territory.
- **The standard oncologic cut-off of ≥35 U/mL has poor discriminatory value in pregnancy.** Benign conditions including myomas, endometriosis, adenomyosis, and pregnancy itself can elevate CA-125 above this threshold.
- **Serial measurements over time are more informative than isolated values**, but trends must be interpreted in clinical context — a slow drift upward in a patient with large degenerating myomas is expected, not alarming.
- **Anchoring on a single tumour marker in a pregnant patient with low pretest probability of malignancy can bias diagnostic reasoning.** In this case, it steered the team toward endometrioma when the reality was cystic degenerating leiomyomas.
- **Imaging — ultrasound first, non-contrast MRI when needed — should drive the diagnostic process**, not tumour markers. MRI without gadolinium is safe after the first trimester and provides critical anatomical detail.
- **Surgery during pregnancy should be reserved for complications or genuine oncologic concern**, not for diagnostic certainty driven by marker anxiety. When operating, limit the intervention to what is necessary and preserve the pregnancy.

## The Bottom Line

This case is a textbook reminder that CA-125 is a mesothelial reactivity marker, not an ovarian cancer marker — and in pregnancy, the peritoneum is reactive by default. Two large degenerating myomas can easily produce a CA-125 of 41.8 U/mL, and the decision to operate should rest on clinical deterioration and imaging findings, not on a number that was never designed to carry the diagnostic weight we sometimes assign it.
