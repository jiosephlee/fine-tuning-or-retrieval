Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

Chapter 4: ## Confirmatory Investigations and Disease Characterization

Once the clinical immunology team recognized that this patient's neck swelling was atypical for angioedema—supported by the absence of triggering medications, no personal or family history of angioedema, and the presence of subtle cutaneous and muscular signs—a structured diagnostic pathway was pursued. The goals of investigation were threefold: to definitively exclude hereditary and acquired angioedema, to confirm the suspected diagnosis of dermatomyositis through autoantibody profiling and tissue pathology, and to characterize the full extent of organ involvement to guide treatment intensity and anticipate complications.

### Excluding Angioedema: Complement Studies

Because the patient's initial presentation centered on neck swelling, complement studies were performed to rule out bradykinin-mediated forms of angioedema. Hereditary angioedema, caused by deficiency or dysfunction of C1 esterase inhibitor, is characterized by low C4 levels and reduced C1 esterase inhibitor function or quantity. Acquired angioedema, seen in association with B-cell lymphoproliferative disorders or autoimmune C1 esterase inhibitor consumption, similarly produces low C4 and low C1 esterase inhibitor levels, often with reduced C1q. In this patient, C3 and C4 were normal, and both C1 esterase inhibitor function and quantification were within the normal range. These results effectively excluded hereditary and acquired angioedema and redirected the diagnostic focus toward a myositis-associated process. The normal complement levels also argued against hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis, another condition that can produce angioedema-like swelling.

### Autoantibody Profiling

The patient's antinuclear antibody (ANA) was positive at a titre of 1:1280 with a speckled pattern, indicating a systemic autoimmune process. ANCA was negative, reducing the likelihood of ANCA-associated vasculitis as a contributor to the clinical picture. A comprehensive myositis antibody panel of 16 antigens was performed using the Euroimmun Euroline immunoassay, a line immunoassay that simultaneously tests for multiple myositis-specific and myositis-associated autoantibodies. Each myositis-specific antibody tends to correlate with distinct clinical phenotypes: for example, anti-Jo-1 and other anti-synthetase antibodies are associated with interstitial lung disease and antisynthetase syndrome, anti-TIF1-γ and anti-NXP2 antibodies are linked to malignancy-associated dermatomyositis, and anti-MDA-5 antibodies are associated with rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease and amyopathic disease. In this case, the panel returned strongly positive for anti-SAE1 (small ubiquitin-like modifier activating enzyme subunit 1) antibodies, with a signal intensity of 173 (normal range <35). All other myositis-specific antibodies, including anti-Mi-2α, anti-TIF1-γ, anti-Jo-1, and anti-OJ, were negative. This serological profile effectively narrowed the subtype of dermatomyositis and had direct implications for prognosis and associated risks.

### Significance of Anti-SAE1 Antibodies

Anti-SAE1 antibodies target the SUMO activating enzyme, a protein structurally homologous to ubiquitin that mediates post-translational modification of target proteins through a process called sumoylation. SAE1-positive dermatomyositis is a rare subtype that, based on a literature review of 215 patients, is typically characterized by classic cutaneous features such as heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules, V-neck sign, and shawl sign. Dysphagia is reported in approximately 46% of cases and interstitial lung disease in approximately 43%, while malignancy occurs in roughly 18%. This patient's presentation was somewhat atypical in that the dominant cutaneous signs were subtle periungual erythema and a positive Holster sign, while the classic heliotrope eruption and Gottron's papules were absent. The prominence of neck swelling as the chief complaint further distinguished this case from the usual SAE1 phenotype, underscoring the variability with which dermatomyositis can present and the value of antibody testing in establishing the diagnosis when classical skin findings are incomplete.

### Muscle Imaging: MRI and FDG-PET

MRI of the thighs demonstrated changes consistent with active myositis, with the most prominent inflammation in the bilateral obturator internus and externus muscles, and milder involvement of the hip adductors, bilateral gracilis muscles, and distal iliopsoas muscles. MRI is the preferred imaging modality for assessing muscle inflammation in inflammatory myopathy, as it detects edema within inflamed muscle through fluid-sensitive sequences such as short tau inversion recovery (STIR) or T2 fat-suppressed images. The pattern of muscle involvement on MRI helps distinguish dermatomyositis from other inflammatory myopathies: dermatomyositis classically shows perifascicular predominance on histology, but imaging may reveal more generalized muscle edema. In this case, the MRI findings corroborated the clinical observation of proximal hip flexor weakness and provided objective evidence of disease activity to justify aggressive immunosuppression.

A whole-body FDG-PET scan was also performed and demonstrated diffuse abnormal FDG accumulation across multiple muscle groups, most prominently in the head and neck, bilateral deltoids, forearms, chest wall, abdomen, and pelvis. FDG-PET served a dual purpose. First, it confirmed the widespread extent of muscle inflammation, which was particularly important given that the neck swelling itself resulted from inflamed strap muscles and adjacent soft tissue rather than from a vascular or allergic process. Second, it functioned as a malignancy screening tool. The association between dermatomyositis and underlying malignancy is well established, and FDG-PET is valuable in this setting because it can detect metabolically active tumors that may not yet be structurally apparent on conventional CT. In this patient, no metabolically active disease was identified outside the muscular inflammation, providing reassurance that occult malignancy was not driving the myositis at the time of evaluation.

### Muscle Biopsy Histopathology

A muscle biopsy of the thigh provided the definitive pathological confirmation of dermatomyositis. Histological examination revealed foci of necrotic, vacuolated degenerate, myophagocytosed, and regenerative myofibers in a perifascicular distribution, along with scattered perimysial inflammation consisting predominantly of histiocytes. The perifascicular distribution of myofiber damage is the pathological hallmark of dermatomyositis and reflects the disease's primary pathophysiology: a complement-mediated microangiopathy targeting endomysial capillaries. This mechanism was directly demonstrated in this biopsy by immunohistochemical staining for the membrane attack complex, which showed deposition of complement component C5b-9 in perifascicular endomysial capillaries. The deposition of C5b-9 in capillaries is among the earliest observable abnormalities in dermatomyositis and represents the downstream consequence of immune complex deposition and complement activation. This capillary injury leads to ischemic injury of perifascicular myofibers, which are located at the periphery of the fascicle and are most vulnerable to reduced perfusion. The biopsy findings thus confirmed the diagnosis at a tissue level and distinguished dermatomyositis from immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy, which characteristically shows necrosis and regeneration without prominent perifascicular patterning or complement deposition, and from inclusion body myositis, which features rimmed vacuoles and endomysial inflammation.

### Assessment of Dysphagia

A video fluoroscopic (barium) swallow study was performed to formally characterize the dysphagia that had been suggested by increased secretions and coughing during flexible nasendoscopy. The study demonstrated severe pharyngeal dysphagia with significant pyriform fossae residue and post-swallow penetration of thin and mildly thick fluids to the level of the vocal cords. This finding has important clinical implications. Pharyngeal dysphagia in dermatomyositis results from involvement of the striated muscles of the pharynx and upper esophageal sphincter, which share embryological and structural characteristics with skeletal muscle. The severity of dysphagia in this patient was a major determinant of treatment intensity, as it indicated that myositis was affecting muscles critical for airway protection. The documented penetration of fluids to the vocal cords also explained the peribronchial consolidation seen on chest CT, which was attributed to micro-aspiration. This finding necessitated nasogastric tube feeding to maintain nutrition and prevent further aspiration while immunosuppressive therapy was initiated.

### Pulmonary Evaluation

Pulmonary function tests revealed an extrathoracic restrictive pattern with no significant bronchodilator response. This pattern was consistent with restriction originating from upper airway and pharyngeal muscle dysfunction rather than intrinsic lung disease. CT of the chest showed multiple non-cavitating pulmonary nodules with a mid to lower zone distribution, the largest measuring 15 mm in the right lower lobe, as well as patchy areas of peribronchial consolidation attributed to aspiration pneumonitis. Importantly, there was no radiological evidence of pulmonary fibrosis. The absence of interstitial lung disease was significant, as it influenced the choice of immunosuppressive agents and prognostic expectations. The pulmonary nodules, while requiring ongoing surveillance, were assessed in the context of the overall malignancy screening strategy.

### Malignancy Risk Stratification and Screening

The association between dermatomyositis and malignancy is a central concern in the management of affected patients. According to the International Guideline for Idiopathic Inflammatory Myopathy-Associated Cancer Screening by the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS), patients are stratified into risk categories based on specific clinical and serological factors. This patient carried two high-risk factors (dermatomyositis itself and dysphagia) and two intermediate-risk factors (SAE1 antibody positivity and male sex). This combination placed him in a high-risk category for malignancy, justifying a comprehensive and proactive screening approach.

The malignancy screening strategy incorporated CT imaging of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which showed no evidence of primary or metastatic malignancy and no lymphadenopathy. A whole-body FDG-PET scan, as discussed above, detected no metabolically active disease outside the inflamed muscles. Tumor markers, including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cancer antigen 19-9 (CA 19-9), cancer antigen 125 (CA-125), and prostate-specific antigen (PSA), were all within normal limits. Based on the high-risk stratification, plans were made for upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy to exclude occult gastrointestinal or colorectal malignancy. This multi-modal screening strategy reflects the principle that the type and intensity of malignancy surveillance in dermatomyositis should be tailored to individual risk, and that screening should continue longitudinally rather than being a one-time assessment, as malignancies can manifest months to years after the initial diagnosis.

### Pre-Immunosuppression Infection Screening

Before initiating intensive immunosuppressive therapy, patients must be screened for latent or chronic infections that could reactivate under immunosuppression. This patient's screening included serology for hepatitis B (HBsAg, HBcAb, HBeAb), hepatitis C, and HIV, all of which were negative. Varicella zoster virus IgG was negative, indicating no prior immunity and a potential need for vaccination before immunosuppression. Cytomegalovirus IgG and IgM were negative. An indeterminate QuantiFERON-TB Gold result prompted further evaluation with three sputum samples for acid-fast bacilli, all of which were negative for Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Immunoglobulin G levels and subclasses were normal, providing reassurance that the patient was not immunodeficient at baseline and could safely tolerate the planned immunosuppressive regimen. This infection screening is an essential component of the pre-treatment evaluation in any patient anticipated to receive high-dose corticosteroids, rituximab, mycophenolate mofetil, and intravenous immunoglobulin, as these agents carry variable risks of viral reactivation, opportunistic infection, and impaired humoral defense.

In summary, the confirmatory investigations in this patient served to close the diagnostic loop opened by the atypical presentation of neck swelling. Complement studies excluded angioedema, the anti-SAE1 antibody and muscle biopsy established dermatomyositis at a serological and pathological level, imaging and functional studies mapped the extent of muscular and pharyngeal involvement, and malignancy risk stratification guided a surveillance strategy that reflected the high-risk profile of this patient. Together, these investigations provided the evidence base for the aggressive, multi-agent immunosuppressive treatment that followed.
