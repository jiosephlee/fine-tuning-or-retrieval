Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Management] What is the standard treatment approach for IMNM, and how might the treatment course differ between drug-induced IMNM (after withdrawal of the offending agent) and idiopathic IMNM in terms of intensity and duration of immunosuppression?

# Treatment of IMNM: Drug-Induced vs. Idiopathic

The standard treatment approach for immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) centers on immunosuppression, typically beginning with high-dose corticosteroids — oral prednisone at 0.5–1 mg/kg/day, or pulse intravenous methylprednisolone in severe presentations — often in combination with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), particularly when weakness is profound, dysphagia is present, or CK elevation is marked. In this case, the patient received both pulse methylprednisolone (1000 mg daily for 5 days) and IVIG at 2 g/kg over 4 days, reflecting the severity of her presentation with progressive proximal weakness, dysphagia, dysphonia, and a peak CK of nearly 4000 U/L. Additional steroid-sparing agents — such as methotrexate, azathioprine, or mycophenolate mofetil, and in refractory cases rituximab — are frequently required, particularly for idiopathic IMNM.

The critical distinction lies in the anticipated duration of therapy. Idiopathic IMNM, particularly when associated with anti-SRP or anti-HMGCR antibodies, typically follows a relapsing or chronic course and often demands prolonged, intensive immunosuppression with sequential or combination agents to achieve and sustain remission. In contrast, drug-induced IMNM — as illustrated by this case, where the putative trigger was a single dose of semaglutide — may resolve with a comparatively short course of immunosuppression provided the offending agent is withdrawn and avoided. After her initial treatment, this patient was rapidly weaned off prednisone over approximately two months, received no additional IVIG courses, and remained off all immunosuppression for eight months with sustained normal CK levels and full return to baseline function. The persistent rise in CK over four months despite only a single drug exposure — rather than rapid normalization expected with direct toxic rhabdomyolysis — supported an immune-mediated mechanism triggered by the drug, and the durable response to brief immunosuppression after drug cessation is characteristic of this drug-induced phenotype. This clinical trajectory contrasts sharply with the prolonged treatment requirements expected in idiopathic IMNM, where ongoing immunosuppression is usually necessary to prevent relapse.
