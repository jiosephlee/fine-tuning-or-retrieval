Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the pathophysiological mechanism by which GLP‑1 receptor agonists could theoretically precipitate a necrotising myopathy?

**Answer**

GLP‑1 receptor agonists (GLP‑1 RAs) can theoretically trigger an immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) by inducing a loss of skeletal‑muscle tissue that becomes a target for a dysregulated immune response.  The following pathophysiologic sequence is plausible and consistent with the case of the 60‑year‑old woman who developed IMNM after a single dose of semaglutide:

1. **Direct myotoxic effect** – GLP‑1 RAs are known to reduce lean body mass and, in rare instances, cause acute rhabdomyolysis (e.g., tirzepatide).  Even a subclinical insult may create fragmentary myofiber necrosis that serves as an antigenic source.

2. **Release of intracellular “danger” signals** – Necrotic myofibers expose intracellular proteins (e.g., myosin heavy chain, enolase) and danger‑associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) such as HMGB1 and ATP.  These molecules activate innate immune pathways (TLR, NLRP3 inflammasome), promoting local macrophage and complement deposition, as seen in the muscle biopsy (CD68⁺ macrophages, membrane‑attack‑complex staining).

3. **Activation of autoreactive lymphocytes** – The inflammatory milieu can lead to epitope spreading and presentation of myogenic peptides by antigen‑processing cells.  T‑cell help and autoantibody production may ensue, although the patient’s serology was negative for classic IMNM antibodies (HMGCR, SRP).  The weak SS‑B positivity suggests a broader autoimmune predisposition that may become manifest once the muscle membrane is breached.

4. **Complement‑mediated myofiber lysis** – Complement activation, evidenced by C5b‑9 (membrane‑attack‑complex) deposition on necrotic and viable fibers, can directly damage muscle cells, perpetuating the cycle of necrosis and inflammation.

5. **Clinical progression** – The gradual rise in creatine kinase (from 337 U/L to >4000 U/L over months) and progressive proximal weakness aligns with a slowly evolving autoimmune process rather than acute, purely toxic myopathy.

Thus, semaglutide may initiate a cascade in which an initial drug‑related myogenic insult unlocks an immune response against muscle antigens, culminating in an immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy that responds to immunosuppression and withdrawal of the GLP‑1 RA.
