Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Which autoantibodies are most frequently associated with IMNM, and how would a seronegative profile influence your diagnostic approach?

**Answer**

The two autoantibodies most strongly linked to immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) are:

| Autoantibody | Typical association in IMNM |
|---------------|----------------------------|
| **Anti‑HMGCR** | Statin‑related IMNM; often presents after a long course of cholesterol‑lowering therapy; high CK, proximal weakness, necrotising histology. |
| **Anti‑SRP** | Idiopathic IMNM; occurs without statins and may coexist with other autoimmune features; also high CK, proximal weakness, necrosis with few lymphocytes. |

**Diagnostic implications of a seronegative profile**

1. **IMNM cannot be ruled out** – Approximately one‑third of patients with classic IMNM have *no* detectable anti‑HMGCR or anti‑SRP antibodies. If the clinical picture (rapidly progressive proximal weakness, markedly elevated CK, muscle biopsy with necrosis and macrophage‑mediated phagocytosis, and low lymphocytic infiltrate) is classic, seronegativity does not exclude the diagnosis.

2. **Broaden the myositis panel** – In a seronegative patient, continue testing for the full spectrum of myositis‑specific antibodies (Jo‑1, Pl‑7, PL‑12, Mi‑2, etc.) because other autoantibodies may be present or may emerge over time. In the case report, the patient’s panel was negative for all tested antibodies, yet the biopsy and clinical course fulfilled the criteria for IMNM.

3. **Focus on tissue morphology** – Muscle biopsy is the gold standard in seronegative IMNM. The study demonstrates classic necrotising fibres with macrophage phagocytosis and occasional T‑cell infiltration, confirming the diagnosis despite negative serology.

4. **Consider drug triggers** – When antibodies are absent, a thorough medication history is critical. The patient’s abrupt and dramatic rise in CK after a single dose of semaglutide, followed by gradual improvement with immunosuppression and drug withdrawal, suggests a drug‑induced IMNM rather than an autoimmune disease driven by unknown antibodies.

5. **Tailor therapy** – Seronegative IMNM may respond rapidly to short courses of high‑dose steroids and IVIG, as in the reported case, because the underlying trigger (here, a GLP‑1 receptor agonist) is removed. Long‑term immunosuppression may be unnecessary when the inciting agent is discontinued and the patient’s weakness resolves.

**Bottom line:** Anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP antibodies are the hallmark serologic markers of IMNM. However, a negative antibody panel does not exclude the disease; confirmatory muscle biopsy and a meticulous review of drug exposures become pivotal in the diagnostic algorithm. In seronegative patients, clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for drug‑induced IMNM and prioritize prompt withdrawal of potential culprits alongside immunosuppressive therapy.
