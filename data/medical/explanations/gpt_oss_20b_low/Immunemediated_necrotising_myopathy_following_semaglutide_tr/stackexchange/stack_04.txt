Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Morning Report] In this case, why might the initial creatine kinase (CK) elevation of 337 U/L be considered incongruent with a classic rhabdomyolysis picture?

**Answer**

A CK of 337 U/L is only modestly above the upper limit of normal and does not meet the classic laboratory threshold for rhabdomyolysis (typically >5,000 U/L). In classical drug‑induced rhabdomyolysis, a single exposure to a myotoxic agent produces an abrupt, dramatic surge in CK (often 10‑ to 40‑fold above normal) accompanied by myalgias, dark‑urine pigmenturia, and sometimes acute kidney injury that improves rapidly with hydration and drug discontinuation. In this patient:

* **Magnitude** – The initial CK was only ~1.8 × ULN, far below the >5‑fold rise characteristic of rhabdomyolysis.
* **Symptomatology** – She had no muscle pain, myoglobinuria, or other signs of myofiber necrosis at that time; the only complaints were gastrointestinal and renal in‑patient symptoms.
* **Time course** – Creatinine and metabolic acidosis returned with hydration, but CK rose slowly over months, eventually reaching 3,992 U/L after 4 months—an indolent, progressive pattern more typical of an autoimmune myopathy than an acute, toxin‑driven muscle injury.

Thus, the initial CK elevation is incongruent with a classic rhabdomyolysis picture; it fits better with the early, low‑grade muscle damage that precedes the full‑blown immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy observed later in this case.
