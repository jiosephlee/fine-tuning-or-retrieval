Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] What histopathological findings on muscle biopsy are characteristic of IMNM, and why is the paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates significant?

**Histopathology of IMNM**

A muscle biopsy in immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy shows:

| Feature | What you see | Why it matters |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Necrotic fibers** | Numerous acutely necrotic myofibers, frequently with a “ghost” nuclear outline or characteristic cytoplasmic fragmentation | Demonstrates the primary muscle‑cell injury that defines the disease |
| **Regenerating fibers** | Capsulated glycogen‑rich or “bamboo‑shaped” nuclei | Confirms ongoing repair of the necrotic injury |
| **Macrophage‑mediated phagocytosis** | Acid phosphatase‑positive, CD68‑positive macrophages engulfing or “spearing” necrotic cores (myophagocytosis) | The dominant effector cells in IMNM are macrophages rather than lymphocytes |
| **Membrane‑attack‑complex (MAC) deposition** | C5b-9 immunostain on necrotic and surrounding fibers | Indicates complement‑mediated cytotoxicity, a central pathogenetic mechanism |
| **Sparse lymphocytic infiltrate** | Rare CD3⁺ T‑cells and minimal perivascular inflammation | The hallmark of IMNM; the paucity differentiates it from polymyositis/dermatomyositis, which show dense interstitial or perivascular lymphocytic infiltrates and sometimes fibrin strands |

**What the paucity of lymphocytes tells us**

1. **Pathophysiologic distinction** – In IMNM the primary injury is not driven by T‑cell mediated attack on myofibers but by antibody‑dependent complement activation and macrophage‑mediated clearance. Thus, immune cells are largely absent from the interstitium.

2. **Diagnostic clarity** – The absence of a prominent lymphocytic infiltrate helps rule out overlap myositis or dermatomyositis and supports the diagnosis when the clinical picture and CK elevation are consistent.

3. **Therapeutic implications** – Because the main effector arm involves complement and macrophages, treatments that blunt antibody‑mediated injury (corticosteroids, IVIG, rituximab) can be effective even when standard anti‑inflammatory regimens might be less so.

In the case presented, the biopsy of the right vastus lateralis showed “dispersed necrotic and regenerating muscle fibres,” acid phosphatase and CD68 staining confirming macrophage phagocytosis, MAC deposition on fibers, and only *rare* CD3⁺ T‑cells and perivascular inflammation. This classic histologic triad—necrosis, macrophage phagocytosis, and minimal lymphocytic infiltrate—confirms an immune‑mediated necrotising myopathy, supporting the diagnosis that led to prompt immunosuppressive therapy and recovery.
