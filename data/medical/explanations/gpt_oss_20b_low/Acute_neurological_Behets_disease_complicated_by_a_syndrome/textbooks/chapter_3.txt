Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

Chapter 3: ## 3. Diagnostic Confirmation of Behçet’s Disease and Neuro‑Behçet’s Disease

The diagnostic process for Behçet’s disease (BD) relies on a combination of clinical manifestations rather than a single laboratory test. The 2018 International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD) provide a quantitative framework: oral aphthous ulcers, genital ulcers, skin lesions, ocular involvement, and a positive pathergy test are each assigned specific points. A total score of ≥4 establishes a definite diagnosis. In this case, the patient accrued five points, thereby satisfying the ICBD for definite BD.

### ICBD scoring system and patient’s points

| ICBD Criterion | Points | Patient’s Findings |
|----------------|--------|--------------------|
| Oral aphthae (≥2) | 2 | Multiple erythematous ulcers in the oral mucosa |
| Genital ulcers (≥2) | 2 | Painful ulceration of the scrotal skin |
| Skin lesions (moderate) | 1 | Folliculitis‑like papulopustules on face and trunk |
| Ocular involvement | 0 | None (slit‑lamp exam normal) |
| Pathergy test | 0 | Not performed (not required for definite disease) |
| **Total** | **5** | **≥4 → definite BD** |

The presence of fever contributed to the overall clinical picture but is not a formal ICBD component; nevertheless, it supports systemic inflammation typical of BD.

### Role of HLA‑B51 and its limitations

HLA‑B51 is a genetic marker strongly associated with BD, particularly in Asian populations. Its presence increases the pre‑test probability of disease but is neither necessary nor sufficient for diagnosis. In this patient, HLA‑B51 positivity reinforced the clinical suspicion but did not influence the ICBD score. Consequently, the final diagnosis remained grounded in the clinical criteria rather than genetic testing.

### CSF profile consistent with aseptic meningitis and its significance for neuro‑BD

On day 3, the lumbar puncture revealed:

- **Opening pressure**: mildly elevated (17 cm H₂O)
- **Protein**: 0.97 g/L (slightly high)
- **Cell count**: 11 cells/µL, predominantly lymphocytes

These findings constitute an aseptic meningitis profile: mild leukocytosis with a lymphocytic predominance, intact glucose, and modest protein elevation. Extensive microbiological evaluation (bacterial cultures, acid‑fast bacilli, PCR for HSV, VZV, and cryptococcal antigen) returned negative. The absence of a conclusive infectious etiology, combined with the clinical context of mucocutaneous BD, pointed toward an immune‑mediated process affecting the central nervous system.

In BD, neuro‑involvement can present as a self‑limited aseptic meningitis without overt parenchymal lesions. The CSF picture in this case is characteristic of an inflammatory, non‑infectious meningitis and is highly suggestive of neuro‑BD, particularly when other etiologies have been excluded.

### Normal MRI in early neuro‑BD – pathophysiological explanation and clinical implications

Brain MRI was performed on day 3 and returned without focal lesions, vascular abnormalities, or brain‑stem involvement—the typical sites of neuro‑BD pathology. However, a normal MRI does **not** rule out neuro‑BD. Several mechanisms explain an initially normal scan:

1. **Early inflammatory phase**: In the acute meningoencephalitic stage, cytokine‑mediated edema may be diffuse and below the resolution of conventional imaging.
2. **Transient blood‑brain barrier dysfunction**: The inflammatory process can increase permeability without forming distinct focal lesions.
3. **CSF‑mediated inflammation**: Aseptic meningitis can result from cytokine spillover into CSF without causing parenchymal damage detectable on MRI.

Clinical implications are profound. Physicians must recognize that a normal neuroimaging study does **not** exclude neuro‑BD, particularly when CSF indicates inflammation and systemic BD is evident. Therefore, the diagnostic criteria for neuro‑BD include CSF findings in addition to imaging.

### Combined diagnosis of definite BD and acute neuro‑BD

The diagnostic pathway unfolded as follows:

1. **Definitive BD**: ICBD score of 5 (oral aphthae + genital ulcers + folliculitis‑like rash) confirmed systemic BD.
2. **Neuro‑BD**: Aseptic meningitis on CSF, absence of infectious, autoimmune, and neoplastic causes, and a normal MRI in the early disease stage satisfied the definition of **acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease**.
3. **Concurrent onset**: The temporal coincidence of systemic mucocutaneous lesions and CNS inflammation justified a simultaneous diagnosis, even though neuro‑BD typically occurs years after initial BD.

Thus, the patient met the diagnostic criteria for definite BD and was subsequently classified as having acute neuro‑BD, despite a normal MRI, due to the supportive CSF profile and the exclusion of alternative causes. This dual diagnosis formed the basis for targeted management and subsequent therapeutic decisions.
