Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Morning Report] During the workup of this case, tuberculous pleural effusion was initially suspected. What clinical or laboratory findings were inconsistent with tuberculosis in this patient?

**Answer**

The pleural fluid in this child was overwhelmingly **blood‑stained** (dark red), not a straw‑yellow or cloudy exudate typical of tuberculous pleural effusion. Tube thoracentesis repeatedly yielded about **one litre of bloody fluid** rather than a purulent or serosanguinous exudate. Moreover, the pleural fluid showed **markedly elevated amylase (≈6,100 U/L) and lipase (≈7,200 U/L)**—levels that are far above the usual range for tuberculosis (generally <200 U/L) and are characteristic of a pancreatic leak. In addition, the patient lacked the constitutional symptoms of TB (fever, night sweats, weight loss) and had **no leukocytosis or neutrophilic predominance** in the pleural fluid; the sample was not an exudate with the typical lymphocyte‑rich profile seen in TB. Finally, GeneXpert for Mycobacterium TB was negative. These clinical and laboratory findings are inconsistent with tuberculous pleural effusion and point toward a pancreatopleural fistula.
