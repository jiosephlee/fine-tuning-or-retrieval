Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Differential Diagnosis] Which genetic mutations are most commonly implicated in hereditary forms of pediatric pancreatitis that predispose to fistula formation?

**Answer**

In children, hereditary pancreatitis most often results from defects in genes that regulate the activation or inhibition of pancreatic enzymes or the epithelial transport of chloride. The four genes most frequently implicated—and therefore the most likely culprits when a child develops recurrent pancreatitis that progresses to complications such as a pancreatopleural fistula—are:

| Gene | Main mutation(s) highlighted in the literature | Pathophysiologic effect |
|------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| **PRSS1** (cationic trypsinogen) | R122H (exon 3), c.194A > G (N199D) | Gain‑of‑function; premature trypsin activation → autodigestion |
| **SPINK1** (serine protease inhibitor Kazal type 1) | N34S (exon 5), c.476G > A (P199L) | Loss of trypsin inhibitor → unchecked trypsin activity |
| **CFTR** (cystic fibrosis transmembrane regulator) | ΔF508, G551D, R117H | Impaired chloride transport → thickened pancreatic ducts and ductal stasis |
| **CTRC** (chymotrypsin C) | p.Arg89Trp (exon 2), p.Glu61Lys | Loss of trypsin degradation → sustained trypsin activity |

These mutations are responsible for roughly 30 % of pediatric chronic pancreatitis cases, the same cohort that most frequently develops severe complications like ductal disruption and, eventually, pancreatopleural fistulas, as illustrated in the case report. In contrast to adults, alcohol is rarely a precipitant; instead, the genetic “handicap” in ductal architecture and enzyme regulation predisposes children to duct rupture and extravasation of pancreatic fluid that can track superiorly through the oesophageal or aortic hiatus into the pleural space.
