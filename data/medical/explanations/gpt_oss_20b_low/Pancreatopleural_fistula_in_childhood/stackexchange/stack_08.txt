Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] Besides pancreatic disorders, what other conditions can produce a high pleural fluid amylase level and what distinguishing laboratory test can differentiate them?

**Answer**

A pleural fluid amylase that exceeds 1 000 IU/L is not exclusive to a pancreatopleural fistula (PPF). Two other clinical entities can also produce markedly elevated pleural fluid amylase:

| Condition | Typical source of amylase | Distinguishing laboratory test |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Oesophageal (Boerhaave) perforation** | Salivary type amylase leaks into the pleural space | **Amylase isoenzyme typing** (salivary/amylase in the fluid >80 % of the total) |
| **Pancreatic pseudocyst rupture into the pleural cavity** | Pancreatic type amylase | **Amylase isoenzyme typing** (pancreatic amylase >80 % of the total) |

In practice, the most reliable way to tell them apart is to send the pleural fluid for **amylase isoenzyme analysis**. Pancreatic amylase (encoded by the AMY2 gene) predominates in pancreatic leakage, whereas salivary amylase (AMY1) predominates when the source is the oesophagus. In the reported case, the pleural fluid contained both raised amylase and lipase with imaging confirming a pancreatic duct‑to‑pleural fistula; isoenzyme typing would yield a pancreatic‑type profile, thereby distinguishing PPF from oesophageal perforation or other causes of high pleural amylase.
