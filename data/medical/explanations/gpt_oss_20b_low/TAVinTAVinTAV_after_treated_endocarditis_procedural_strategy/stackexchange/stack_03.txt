Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Morning Report] Explain how commissural alignment of a self‑expanding valve can help preserve future coronary access after a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV implantation.

**Answer**

Commissural alignment of a self‑expanding valve in a TAV‑in‑TAV‑in‑TAV implantation preserves future coronary access by ensuring that the new valve’s struts and skirt lie directly over the preceding valve’s commissures rather than between them. This positioning has two key consequences:

1. **Minimises “neo‑skirt” encroachment on the coronary ostia.**  
   When a self‑expanding valve (e.g., Acurate neo2) is deployed, its lower stent frame becomes the new “neo‑skirt.” If the implant is mis‑aligned, the skirt can extend laterally across the sinus of Valsalva and obstruct the coronary ostium, or create a channeled path that makes subsequent coronary wiring difficult. Aligning the new valve’s commissures with those of the underlying Evolut frame confines the skirt to the native commissural planes, preserving a clear channel to the left and right coronary arteries (Fig. 5, 7 in the report).  

2. **Maintains a vertical “tube” of the aortic root.**  
   The third valve’s supra‑annular leaflets are positioned above the Evolut leaflets, and the commissures line up such that the radial expansion of the new frame does not squeeze the sinus walls inward. This keeps the aortic annulus generously open and prevents the pinch‑off of the coronary arteries that can occur when successive frames overlap off‑center.  

In the reported case, pre‑procedural CT modeling identified the best depth and orientation to avoid coronary obstruction. During deployment, fluoroscopic landmarks were used to align the Acurate neo2’s commissures with those of the Evolut prosthesis. Post‑dilation further expanded the Evolut stent, improving leaflet excursion and reducing transvalvular gradients while the aligned commissures ensured that the final neo‑skirt did not overhang the coronary ostia. Consequently, aortography after the third implantation showed preserved coronary flow and no need for coronary protection.

Thus, meticulous commissural alignment in serial TAV‑in‑TAV procedures is a critical strategy to safeguard future coronary interventions, a consideration that becomes increasingly important as patients require multiple transcatheter re‑interventions.
