Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

Chapter 1: ## 1. Clinical Presentation and Initial Diagnostic Work‑up

A young woman in her late twenties presented with a **5‑year history of progressive fatigue, unintentional weight loss, and dyspnea on exertion**. The functional decline began insidiously: she reported a gradual reduction in her ability to perform routine tasks, which magnified over the last three months to the point of needing assistance with self‑care. Associated respiratory symptoms included intermittent chest discomfort and exertional shortness of breath, suggestive of early pulmonary compromise.

### Physical examination
On examination the patient was hemodynamically stable but visibly **pallor and icterus** were noted. Dermatologic examination revealed **premature greying of hair** and **knuckle hyperpigmentation**, while the tongue was **atrophic with a loss of papillary ridges** – a classic sign of macroglossia that can accompany vitamin B12 deficiency or systemic disease. Musculoskeletal inspection uncovered **thoracic scoliosis** and **bilateral thenar atrophy**, hinting at chronic muscle wasting. Abdominal palpation demonstrated **hepatomegaly** (≈5 cm below the right costal margin) and a palpable spleen, confirming **hepatosplenomegaly**. Respiratory auscultation identified **reduced breath sounds** in the infra‑scapular areas, raising suspicion for interstitial involvement. Cardiovascular and neurologic examinations were otherwise unremarkable.

### Baseline laboratory data
A **complete blood count** revealed **pancytopenia** with a markedly low **reticulocyte count (0.39 %)**, indicating a blunted marrow response. Peripheral smear morphology showed **dimorphic erythrocytes**, **basophilic stippling**, presence of **Cabot rings**, and **hypersegmented neutrophils**. These features collectively point toward **ineffective erythropoiesis**, often seen in megaloblastic anemia or marrow failure syndromes.

Serum studies confirmed **severe vitamin B12 deficiency** and low folate levels. Iron studies were paradoxically **elevated serum iron and ferritin**, consistent with iron retention secondary to ineffective erythropoiesis rather than true iron overload. Inflammatory markers were elevated, but **autoimmune serologies** (ANA, hepatitis antibodies) were negative, narrowing the differential to nutritional or marrow‑centric causes.

### Bone marrow evaluation
An aspirate and core biopsy showed **trilineage hematopoiesis** with **erythroid hyperplasia** and **increased iron stores**. No cytogenetic abnormalities or dysplastic features were detected. The absence of malignancy or infiltrative disease, coupled with the marrow’s attempt to compensate for peripheral cytopenias, reinforced the diagnosis of **ineffective erythropoiesis** rather than aplastic or neoplastic processes.

### Hepatic assessment
Abdominal ultrasound identified **hepatosplenomegaly**. **Transient elastography** quantified **grade II steatosis** (controlled attenuation parameter 295 dB/m) and **cirrhosis** (liver stiffness 18.1 kPa). An infectious work‑up (HBV, HCV, HIV, CMV) and autoimmune work‑up (ANA, ASMA, AMA, LKM‑1) were negative. These findings suggested a non‑etiologic source of hepatic fibrosis, with nutritional or genetic etiologies being foremost considerations.

### Pulmonary imaging
Whole‑body **18F‑FDG PET‑CT** revealed diffuse **ground‑glass haziness** with **fibrotic and subpleural atelectatic changes** bilaterally, consistent with **idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)**. No focal uptake suggesting malignancy or granulomatous disease was seen, thereby focusing the differential toward interstitial lung disease rather than metastatic or infectious etiologies.

### Additional organ involvement
A **dual‑energy X‑ray absorptiometry (DEXA)** scan documented **osteoporosis**, aligning with the patient’s history of recurrent bone pain and fractures. Laboratory derangements included **secondary hyperparathyroidism**, **hypocalcaemia**, **hypokalaemia**, and **vitamin D deficiency**, all contributing to bone demineralization. **Electromyography and nerve conduction studies** uncovered an **asymmetric sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy** with chronic neurogenic changes, corroborating the clinical signs of peripheral neuropathy.

### Summary of the initial differential
The constellation of **pancytopenia with ineffective erythropoiesis**, **hepatic fibrosis**, **pulmonary fibrosis**, **premature greying**, and **multisystem involvement** steered the diagnostic process toward a **telomere biology disorder** rather than isolated nutritional deficiency or acquired marrow failure. These findings justified proceeding to a targeted genetic evaluation, which ultimately revealed a monoallelic variant in **PARN**, a key player in telomere maintenance.
