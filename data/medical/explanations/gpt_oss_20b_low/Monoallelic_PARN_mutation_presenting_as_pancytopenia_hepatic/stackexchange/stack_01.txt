Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Clinical Pearls] What laboratory finding best distinguishes ineffective erythropoiesis from a hemolytic process in a patient with pancytopenia and a positive DAT?

**Answer**

The laboratory hallmark that separates ineffective erythropoiesis from a peripheral hemolytic process in a pancytopenic patient with a positive DAT is a **low reticulocyte count (or reticulocyte index < 1.5)** despite anemia.  

In true hemolysis the marrow compensates by markedly increasing reticulocyte production, often yielding a high reticulocyte count and reticulocyte index. In contrast, ineffective erythropoiesis, as illustrated in this case, is characterized by a blunted reticulocyte response (0.39 % in the report), accompanied by marrow erythroid hyperplasia, iron‑laden reticulo‑endothelial cells, and normal or high ferritin/serum iron—all signifying that red cells are produced but fail to survive to circulation.  

Thus, the reticulocyte count (and derived index) is the key discriminator.
