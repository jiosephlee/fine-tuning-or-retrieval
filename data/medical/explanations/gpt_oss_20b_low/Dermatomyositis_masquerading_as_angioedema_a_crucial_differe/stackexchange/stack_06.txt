Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] Which malignancy‑screening strategy was employed for this patient, and how does the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guideline classify his risk profile?

**Malignancy‑screening strategy employed**

The patient underwent a *comprehensive, multimodal baseline work‑up* at the time of diagnosis, in line with the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guideline for idiopathic inflammatory myopathy‑associated cancer screening:

| Modality | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Whole‑body contrast‑enhanced CT** (chest, abdomen, pelvis) | Detect occult tumours in common sites (lung, breast, prostate, ovary, GI tract). |
| **18F‑FDG PET/CT** | Assess for metabolic activity suggestive of malignancy in any region, including lymph nodes or unusual sites. |
| **Serum tumour markers** (CEA, CA‑19‑9, CA‑125, PSA) | Provide adjunctive evidence of potential malignancy, especially GI and genitourinary cancers. |
| **Upper and lower gastrointestinal endoscopy** | Direct visual inspection and biopsy of oesophageal, gastric, colonic mucosa; important in dermatomyositis where colorectal cancer risk is elevated. |
| **Clinical examination and symptom monitoring** | Ongoing surveillance for weight loss, night sweats, fevers, or other paraneoplastic clues. |

All imaging was negative for neoplasia; tumour markers were within normal limits, and endoscopic evaluations were planned but not yet completed at the time of the case report.

---

**IMACS risk‑category classification**

According to the IMACS guideline (2018) the risk of malignancy in dermatomyositis is stratified by the presence of high‑ and intermediate‑risk factors:

| High‑risk factors | Intermediate‑risk factors |
|-------------------|---------------------------|
| Dermatomyositis (vs. polymyositis) | Antibody subtype (e.g., anti‑TIF1‑γ, anti‑MDA‑5, anti‑SAE1) |
| Age ≥ 50 years | Male sex |
| Dysphagia | Specific cutaneous manifestations (shawl sign, heliotrope rash) |
| Onset ≤ 3 days post‑vaccination (rare) | Elevated CK >10× ULN |
| … | … |

**Application to this patient**

*High‑risk*  
1. **Dermatomyositis** – confirmed diagnosis.  
2. **Dysphagia** – documented on video‑fluoroscopy and clinically significant.

*Intermediate‑risk*  
1. **SAE1 antibody positivity** – identified on myositis panel.  
2. **Male sex** – patient is male.

The patient therefore meets **two high‑risk** and **two intermediate‑risk** criteria, placing him in the **high‑risk** category for associated malignancy. This dictates the aggressive baseline imaging and endoscopic surveillance described above, with ongoing yearly reassessment.

---

**Key teaching points**

1. **Screening is most intensive in high‑risk patients** (≥2 high‑risk or 1 high‑risk + ≥2 intermediate‑risk).  
2. **Whole‑body CT + PET/CT** remains the core of initial imaging; tumour markers add information but are not definitive.  
3. **Endoscopy** is mandatory because GI cancers (especially colorectal) are a common occult malignancy in dermatomyositis.  
4. **IMACS categorisation** guides clinician‐level decision‑making; in this case the patient was appropriately classified as high risk, warranting the screening strategy undertaken.
