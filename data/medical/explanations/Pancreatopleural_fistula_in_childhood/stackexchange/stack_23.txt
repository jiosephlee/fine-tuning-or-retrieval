Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Management] Provide the typical pediatric dosing range for octreotide in PPF and one monitoring parameter or side effect to watch for during therapy.

Clinical pearl: In pediatric pancreatopleural fistula the somatostatin analogue most commonly used is octreotide given as a continuous infusion alongside NPO/TPN and drainage. Typical pediatric dosing reported in case series and in this report is 10–40 µg/kg/day (the authors used 30 µg/kg/day) — which corresponds roughly to a continuous infusion of about 0.4–1.7 µg/kg/hr. (Protocols vary; some centres use doses up to ~1–3 µg/kg/hr for short periods, so always check your institution’s protocol.)

Why this range: octreotide reduces pancreatic exocrine secretion and intraductal pressure to promote fistula closure; dosing is guided by clinical response (drain output) and tolerance rather than a single universal dose.

Key monitoring / side effect to watch for: monitor blood glucose closely. Octreotide suppresses insulin and glucagon and can cause dysglycemia (usually hyperglycaemia, but hypoglycaemia can occur especially in infants or with concurrent fasting/TPN changes). Check bedside glucose several times daily after starting and with dose changes. Also be mindful of other adverse effects (bradycardia, gallbladder stasis/sludge with prolonged use, GI upset, and electrolyte changes) and stop or adjust therapy if significant toxicity develops.
