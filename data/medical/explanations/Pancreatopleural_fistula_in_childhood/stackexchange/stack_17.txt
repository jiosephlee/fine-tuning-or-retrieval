### [Morning Report] A child with recurrent pleural effusions after bicycle-handle abdominal trauma has pleural fluid amylase of 6000 U/L and lipase of 7000 U/L. What is the most likely diagnosis, and what immediate management priorities should be addressed before definitive ductal therapy?

Most likely diagnosis: a pancreatopleural fistula from traumatic disruption of the pancreatic duct with pancreatic fluid tracking through the oesophageal hiatus into the pleural space. The very high pleural amylase and lipase (many-fold above 1,000 U/L), the history of bicycle-handle abdominal trauma, and the MRCP showing communication between a pancreatic collection/duct and the right pleural cavity make this the correct diagnosis.

Immediate management priorities before definitive ductal therapy are to stabilise the child and control pancreatic secretion and the pleural collection so you can plan definitive ductal intervention (ERCP stent or surgery) electively. Practically, that means first treating the respiratory problem: supplemental oxygen and prompt pleural evacuation — ultrasound‑guided thoracentesis for large symptomatic effusions and a chest tube for recurrent or high‑volume drainage and hydropneumothorax, with careful pleural‑space care. Simultaneously, you must rest the pancreas and reduce fistula output: keep the patient nil per os, provide adequate fluid and electrolyte support, and institute bowel rest with nutritional support (early TPN if enteral feeding cannot be safely used). Give a somatostatin analogue (eg, octreotide) to suppress exocrine secretion, and start pancreatic enzyme replacement as guided by nutrition plans. Obtain cross‑sectional imaging (MRCP preferred) and amylase/lipase measurements to define duct anatomy, and arrange ERCP for diagnostic/therapeutic planning once the patient is stable. Monitor chest‑tube output, hemoglobin, electrolytes and signs of infection; give antibiotics only if there is evidence of infection. Indications to proceed urgently to endoscopic or surgical therapy rather than continued conservative care include persistent high‑output fistula despite 2–3 weeks of conservative measures, ongoing sepsis or hemodynamic instability, or anatomic ductal obstruction/pseudocyst not amenable to endoscopic drainage.

In short: stabilise the airway/breathing with drainage, rest the pancreas (NPO + nutrition) and suppress secretion (octreotide), define anatomy with MRCP/ERCP, then proceed to definitive ductal therapy when optimised or sooner if the child fails conservative care.
