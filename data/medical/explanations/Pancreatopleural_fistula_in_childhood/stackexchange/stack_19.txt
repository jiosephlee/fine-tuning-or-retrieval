Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Pathophysiology] How does pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT) support conservative management of PPF beyond nutritional support?

Think of the fistula as a leak in a pressurised secretory system. Conservative therapy succeeds when you both reduce the drive for pancreatic juice production and minimise the volume and enzymatic activity bathing the fistulous tract so it can seal.

Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT) helps in that second way, independent of its obvious nutritional benefit. When given orally with enteral feeding, exogenous enzymes digest luminal nutrients and reconstitute duodenal proteolytic activity. That accomplishes two physiologic effects that lower endogenous pancreatic secretion and fistula output. First, by degrading CCK‑releasing peptides in the proximal gut, PERT reduces cholecystokinin-mediated stimulation of pancreatic acinar secretion. Second, improved digestion limits prolonged intraluminal stimuli that would otherwise sustain pancreatic secretion. The net result is less pancreatic juice produced, lower intraductal pressure and a reduction in the volume and enzymatic aggression at the fistula site — all of which favour closure.

Clinically this complements bowel rest/TPN and somatostatin analogues: TPN removes enteral stimulation, octreotide suppresses secretion neurohormonally, and PERT prevents a rebound or persistent stimulus from undigested luminal contents when enteral feeds are resumed. Practically, enzymes must be given with meals (not between meals) and in adequate doses to have the feedback effect; improper timing can fail to suppress — and theoretical stimulation if given alone has been discussed but is avoided by coadministration with feeding. If conservative measures including PERT do not reduce drainage in 2–3 weeks, proceed to ERCP ± stenting or surgery.

Bottom line: PERT is not just nutritional support in PPF — it reduces pancreatic exocrine drive and enzymatic damage to the fistula tract, helping lower output and promote healing when combined with somatostatin and drainage.
