Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Clinical Pearls] What is the typical laterality of pleural effusion in pancreatopleural fistula, and by what pathway can a right-sided effusion occur?

Pancreatopleural fistulas classically produce large, usually left‑sided pleural effusions. The pancreas (especially body and tail) lies on the left and disrupted ductal fluid or a pseudocyst most readily tracks upward into the left hemithorax through the aortic or oesophageal hiatus or by direct transdiaphragmatic erosion, so left predominance is the rule.

A right‑sided effusion can still occur when pancreatic fluid follows retroperitoneal planes and mediastinal routes to the right pleural space — for example, a collection that ascends through the oesophageal or aortic hiatus and then crosses the posterior mediastinum to the right, or a pseudocyst that tracks along the para‑esophageal/para‑aortic region into the right hemithorax. In short, anatomic continuity through the diaphragmatic hiatuses and posterior mediastinum — rather than a primary pancreatic location on the right — explains right‑sided or bilateral effusions, as illustrated by this case.
