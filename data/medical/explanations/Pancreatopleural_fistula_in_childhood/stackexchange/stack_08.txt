### [Management] In a child with suspected PPF and recurrent massive pleural effusions, which initial conservative management steps are recommended, and what is the typical duration to assess for response?

In a child with suspected pancreatopleural fistula and recurrent, large pleural effusions the safe initial strategy is conservative and directed at three goals: reduce pancreatic secretion and intraductal pressure, provide adequate pleural drainage, and maintain nutrition while the fistula seals.

Begin with bowel rest (nil per os) and institution of nutritional support—preferably total parenteral nutrition if enteral feeding cannot be safely continued—so the pancreas is “rested.” Provide effective pleural drainage with thoracentesis or a chest tube/ICD for large or recurrent effusions and monitor daily output and pleural fluid amylase. Start a somatostatin analogue (eg, octreotide, weight‑based infusion) to suppress exocrine pancreatic secretion, and give pancreatic enzyme replacement if indicated. Continue supportive care (oxygen, analgesia) and treat or exclude superimposed infection; stop empiric therapies (eg, anti‑TB) once PPF is confirmed. Repeat cross‑sectional imaging (MRCP/CT) and consider ERCP for ductal definition if intervention may be needed.

Assess response over days but plan a formal trial of conservative care for about 2–3 weeks. In practice a meaningful response is falling chest‑tube output and improving imaging/clinical status within that window (the reported pediatric experience often shows improvement within 7–14 days, as in this case where drainage ceased by day 10). If drainage and symptoms do not meaningfully improve after roughly 2–3 weeks (and certainly by 3–4 weeks), escalate to endoscopic therapy (ERCP with transpapillary stent or sphincterotomy) to lower ductal pressure, and if endoscopic approaches fail or are not feasible, proceed to surgery.
