### [Morning Report] What are the reported sensitivities of MRCP and CT for visualizing a pancreatopleural fistula tract, and why is ERCP considered the gold standard for therapeutic planning?

Reported sensitivities in the literature are that MRCP visualizes the pancreatopleural fistulous tract in roughly 70–80% of cases, whereas contrast CT demonstrates the actual fistula much less often — only about 33–47% of the time. Those numbers reflect the different strengths of the modalities: MRCP is noninvasive and excels at delineating ductal anatomy and fluid collections, so it detects most but not all fistulous communications; CT is excellent for detecting complications (pseudocysts, calcifications, mediastinal or pleural collections) but often only shows the tract indirectly.

ERCP is regarded as the gold standard for therapeutic planning because it provides direct, high‑resolution visualization of the pancreatic ductal system and any point of disruption, and — critically — it is simultaneously therapeutic. Through ERCP you can confirm the site and extent of ductal disruption, define ductal anatomy more precisely than imaging alone, and intervene immediately (transpapillary stent placement, sphincterotomy, balloon dilatation or selective drainage) to lower intraductal pressure and promote fistula closure. In short, MRCP is the best noninvasive test to suggest and map a PPF; CT helps identify associated collections and complications; ERCP both definitively delineates ductal pathology and allows endoscopic therapy that changes management — which is why it is the procedural gold standard.
