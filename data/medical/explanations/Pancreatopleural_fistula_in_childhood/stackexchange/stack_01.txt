Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Clinical Pearls] In a child with recurrent, large, unilateral pleural effusions and minimal abdominal symptoms, what pleural fluid test most strongly suggests a pancreatopleural fistula, and what threshold value is considered pathognomonic?

The single pleural‑fluid test that most strongly suggests a pancreatopleural fistula is an elevated pleural‑fluid amylase, specifically the pancreatic (rather than salivary) isoform. A pleural‑fluid amylase ≥1,000 U/L is widely cited as essentially pathognomonic for a pancreatic source in the right clinical context. 

Clinical reasoning: pancreatic duct disruption or a leaking pseudocyst pours enzyme‑rich fluid into the retroperitoneum and mediastinum, producing massive, recurrent, typically unilateral effusions with very high amylase (and often lipase). High pleural amylase is not entirely specific — oesophageal rupture, some malignancies and other processes can raise pleural amylase (often the salivary isoform) — so isoenzyme typing (pancreatic vs salivary) and cross‑sectional imaging (MRCP/CT) or ERCP to define the ductal anatomy are the next steps to confirm a pancreatopleural fistula.
