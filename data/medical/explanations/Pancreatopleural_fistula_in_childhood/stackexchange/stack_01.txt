Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood

### [Clinical Pearls] In a child with recurrent large unilateral pleural effusions and dyspnea, which pleural fluid laboratory measurement is most diagnostic of a pancreatopleural fistula, what threshold value supports the diagnosis, and what additional assay can confirm pancreatic (rather than salivary) origin?

Answer in one line
- Measure pleural fluid amylase; a value ≥1,000 U/L (or markedly greater than serum) is strongly suggestive—often cited as pathognomonic—of a pancreatopleural fistula. To prove pancreatic (not salivary) origin, perform amylase isoenzyme typing (or a pancreatic‑specific amylase assay); markedly elevated pleural fluid lipase also supports a pancreatic source.

Clinical reasoning and teaching points
- Pathophysiology: a disrupted pancreatic duct or pseudocyst leaks enzyme‑rich pancreatic fluid into the retroperitoneum and through the mediastinum/hiatus into the pleural space, producing a large, often blood‑stained, exudative effusion.
- Best initial pleural test: pleural fluid amylase — very high concentrations (classically ≥1,000 U/L) should prompt consideration of pancreatopleural fistula (PPF). Other causes of high pleural amylase (e.g., oesophageal rupture, some malignancies) more commonly show the salivary (S) isoenzyme.
- Confirmatory laboratory test: distinguish amylase isoenzymes (electrophoresis or pancreatic‑specific immunoassays) demonstrating pancreatic (P)‑type amylase confirms a pancreatic leak. Alternatively, very high pleural fluid lipase (as in this case: pleural amylase 6,118 U/L and lipase 7,211 U/L) is strong supportive evidence of pancreatic origin.
- Next diagnostic steps: when labs suggest PPF, obtain cross‑sectional imaging (MRCP is noninvasive and sensitive; ERCP remains the gold standard for ductal anatomy and therapeutic planning).

Case correlation
- In the reported child the pleural fluid amylase was 6,118 U/L with pleural lipase 7,211 U/L; those striking elevations (well above the 1,000 U/L threshold) plus MRCP visualization of a fistulous tract confirmed PPF and guided conservative management.
