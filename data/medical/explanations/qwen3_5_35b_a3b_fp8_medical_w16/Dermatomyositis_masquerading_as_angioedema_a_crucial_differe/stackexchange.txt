Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] In a patient presenting with acute neck swelling where deep neck space infection and angioedema have been ruled out, what specific laboratory value and physical examination finding should immediately raise suspicion for dermatomyositis?

When evaluating a patient with acute neck swelling where life-threatening etiologies like deep neck space infection and classic angioedema have been excluded, the two critical indicators that should immediately trigger suspicion for dermatomyositis are **significantly elevated creatine kinase (CK)** levels and **subtle cutaneous or nailfold abnormalities**.

As illustrated in this case, the patient's neck swelling was actually "pseudoangioedema" resulting from inflammatory edema within the strap muscles rather than true histamine- or bradykinin-mediated vascular leakage. While the initial presentation mimicked an otolaryngologic emergency, the key to diagnosis lay in recognizing that the swelling was accompanied by proximal muscle weakness and specific skin changes. The definitive laboratory red flag was a massively elevated CK level of 16,184 U/L, reflecting severe muscle necrosis and inflammation. Concurrently, a meticulous physical examination revealed subtle but pathognomonic signs often missed in acute settings: **periungual erythema** and **nailfold microhemorrhages** (avascular areas seen on capillaroscopy). Although classic findings like Gottron's papules or a heliotrope rash were absent in this instance, the presence of generalized facial erythema and localized nail changes alongside unexplained neck swelling and dysphagia should prompt immediate measurement of muscle enzymes. Failure to recognize these clues leads to delays in initiating necessary immunosuppressive therapy, whereas early recognition allows for rapid intervention to prevent airway compromise from laryngeal muscle edema.

### [Pathophysiology] How does the pathophysiological mechanism of 'pseudoangioedema' in dermatomyositis fundamentally differ from that of histaminergic or bradykinin-mediated angioedema?

The fundamental difference lies in the underlying mechanism driving the tissue expansion: **histaminergic or bradykinin-mediated angioedema results from plasma extravasation driven by vascular permeability**, whereas the "pseudoangioedema" seen in dermatomyositis represents **actual inflammatory infiltration and edema within skeletal muscle tissue**.

In classic angioedema, mast cell degranulation releases histamine, or complement activation generates bradykinin, both of which act directly on post-capillary venules to increase endothelial permeability. This allows fluid to leak into the *interstitium* without cellular infiltration. Consequently, this process is typically rapid-onset, transient (hours), and responds to antihistamines, corticosteroids, or specific antagonists like icatibant. As illustrated in our case, the patient's C3/C4 levels and C1 esterase inhibitor function were normal, effectively ruling out hereditary or acquired angioedema pathways.

Conversely, the swelling in this patient with anti-SAE1 dermatomyositis is a manifestation of active myositis. The MRI confirmed diffuse inflammation specifically localized to the strap muscles and retropharyngeal space. The pathophysiology involves T-cell mediated cytotoxicity targeting muscle fibers and deposition of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9) on perifascicular capillaries, leading to ischemia, necrosis, and a robust inflammatory infiltrate. This process creates true tissue mass from muscle fiber destruction and perimysial inflammation rather than simple fluid leakage. Therefore, unlike true angioedema which resolves quickly once the mediator is blocked, this "swelling" persists until the underlying autoimmune myositis is suppressed with intensive immunosuppression—such as the high-dose steroids and IVIG utilized in this patient—to halt muscle injury and resolve the inflammatory infiltrate. Recognizing this distinction prevents futile attempts at treating with epinephrine or antihistamines and directs immediate attention to checking creatine kinase levels and evaluating for cutaneous signs of dermatomyositis.

### [Clinical Pearls] Which specific autoantibody was identified in this case, and what is its known association with dysphagia and interstitial lung disease compared to other myositis-specific antibodies like anti-Jo-1?

The specific autoantibody identified in this case is **anti-small ubiquitin-like modifier-activating enzyme 1 (anti-SAE1)**.

Clinically, anti-SAE1 positivity defines a distinct subset of dermatomyositis characterized by a unique triad of cutaneous features, dysphagia, and interstitial lung disease (ILD). While classic anti-Jo-1 antibodies (associated with antisynthetase syndrome) are the hallmark of ILD and often present with "mechanic's hands" and arthritis, anti-SAE1 antibodies carry a different prognostic profile regarding organ involvement. As highlighted in the discussion of this case, large cohort studies indicate that approximately **46% of patients** with anti-SAE1 dermatomyositis develop dysphagia and roughly **43%** develop ILD. Notably, unlike some other myositis subsets where malignancy is a dominant concern (such as anti-TIF1-$\gamma$), the rate of malignancy in anti-SAE1 positive patients is generally lower, reported around 18% in major reviews.

However, the most critical distinction for bedside recognition involves the presentation of soft tissue edema. While anti-Jo-1 is rarely associated with angioedema-like swelling, anti-SAE1 antibodies—alongside anti-MDA5—are increasingly recognized as markers for severe subcutaneous edema. In this patient, the dramatic neck swelling mimicking angioedema was actually caused by profound inflammatory myositis of the strap muscles rather than histaminergic or bradykinin-mediated vascular permeability. This underscores that when evaluating a patient with "idiopathic" angioedema who lacks response to standard therapies (epinephrine, antihistamines), particularly if they exhibit proximal weakness or subtle cutaneous signs like periungual erythema, testing for myositis-specific antibodies including anti-SAE1 is essential to identify underlying inflammatory myopathy before irreversible airway compromise occurs.

### [Differential Diagnosis] Despite the absence of classic cutaneous signs such as Gottron's papules or a heliotrope rash, which subtle physical exam findings on the hands and nails provided the critical clue to the correct diagnosis?

Despite the absence of the hallmark "heliotrope" rash or Gottron's papules, the critical clues to this diagnosis were found in the **periungual regions**. Specifically, the patient exhibited **periungual erythema** (redness around the nail folds) and **nailfold capillary changes**, including microhemorrhages and avascular areas seen on capillaroscopy.

These findings represent **periungual telangiectasias** and digital ischemia, which are characteristic cutaneous manifestations of dermatomyositis resulting from microvascular injury. While often overshadowed by classic rashes like the heliotrope eruption or Gottron's papules, these nailfold abnormalities are highly specific for inflammatory myopathies. In this case, they served as the pivotal physical exam finding that distinguished autoimmune myositis from idiopathic angioedema or infectious etiologies. Their presence, coupled with unexplained neck swelling and dysphagia, prompted the immediate measurement of creatine kinase, leading to the confirmation of severe dermatomyositis mediated by anti-SAE1 antibodies. As this case illustrates, a meticulous inspection of the hands and nailfolds is essential when evaluating atypical edema or neck swelling to avoid missing a treatable autoimmune condition.

### [Management] Based on the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, why was this patient classified as high-risk for malignancy despite having normal tumor markers and negative initial imaging studies?

Based on the IMACS guidelines for malignancy screening in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, this patient was classified as **high-risk** for cancer because he met criteria across both high-risk and intermediate-risk categories, regardless of his initial negative workup.

According to the IMACS framework, **dermatomyositis (DM)** itself is a standalone high-risk factor for malignancy. Furthermore, the presence of **dysphagia** serves as another independent high-risk predictor. While DM carries a higher overall risk of paraneoplastic malignancy compared to polymyositis or antisynthetase syndrome, specific clinical phenotypes carry even greater weight. In this case, the combination of two distinct high-risk factors automatically elevates the patient's stratification to "high risk," mandating aggressive and comprehensive screening.

Additionally, the patient possessed two intermediate-risk factors: he was **male** and tested positive for **anti-SAE1 antibodies**. While anti-SAE1 positivity alone does not confer the same strong oncogenic association as anti-TIF1-γ or anti-NXP2 antibodies (which are classically linked to higher malignancy rates), it contributes to the cumulative risk profile when paired with other factors like male sex.

It is crucial to understand that this classification drives the *intensity* of the screening protocol, not necessarily the pre-test probability of finding cancer immediately. As seen in this case, a patient can be categorized as high-risk based on clinical phenotype alone—specifically DM plus dysphagia—even when tumor markers are normal and initial cross-sectional imaging (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis) and functional imaging (PET scan) are negative. The high-risk designation justified proceeding with further invasive surveillance, specifically the planned gastroscopy and colonoscopy to rule out occult gastrointestinal malignancies that would not be detected by serum markers or standard CT imaging. Thus, the classification is a proactive measure ensuring no potential malignancy is missed during the period of highest statistical risk following diagnosis.

### [Management] Given the patient's pulmonary involvement characterized by an extrathoracic restrictive pattern and aspiration pneumonitis, what was the rationale for selecting mycophenolate mofetil over methotrexate as a steroid-sparing agent in this specific scenario?

The decision to select mycophenolate mofetil over methotrexate in this patient was driven primarily by the presence of pulmonary involvement and the specific toxicity profiles of these agents in the context of dermatomyositis-associated lung disease.

While both methotrexate and mycophenolate mofetil are accepted first-line steroid-sparing agents for dermatomyositis, methotrexate carries a well-documented risk of inducing or exacerbating interstitial lung disease (ILD) and pulmonary fibrosis. In this case, the patient presented with significant respiratory compromise, evidenced by an extrathoracic restrictive pattern on pulmonary function testing and CT findings of patchy peribronchial consolidation attributed to aspiration pneumonitis. Although the initial CT did not show frank fibrosis, the presence of active parenchymal lung injury and functional restriction created a precarious environment where introducing a known pulmonary toxin like methotrexate could precipitate irreversible decline.

Consequently, mycophenolate mofetil was chosen because it lacks the same association with drug-induced ILD and offers a favorable safety profile for patients with pre-existing pulmonary pathology. This choice aligns with current rheumatologic principles where organ-specific comorbidities dictate agent selection: avoiding hepatotoxicity in liver disease or pulmonary toxicity in those with active lung involvement. Furthermore, the patient's preference for a daily oral regimen supported this selection, ensuring adherence while providing effective immunomodulation without compromising respiratory reserve. Ultimately, this strategy allowed for successful induction of remission while mitigating the risk of worsening his already compromised pulmonary status.
