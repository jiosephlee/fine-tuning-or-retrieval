Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] Based on the International Myositis Assessment and Clinical Studies Group (IMACS) guidelines, why was this patient classified as high-risk for malignancy despite having normal tumor markers and negative initial imaging studies?

Based on the IMACS guidelines for malignancy screening in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, this patient was classified as **high-risk** for cancer because he met criteria across both high-risk and intermediate-risk categories, regardless of his initial negative workup.

According to the IMACS framework, **dermatomyositis (DM)** itself is a standalone high-risk factor for malignancy. Furthermore, the presence of **dysphagia** serves as another independent high-risk predictor. While DM carries a higher overall risk of paraneoplastic malignancy compared to polymyositis or antisynthetase syndrome, specific clinical phenotypes carry even greater weight. In this case, the combination of two distinct high-risk factors automatically elevates the patient's stratification to "high risk," mandating aggressive and comprehensive screening.

Additionally, the patient possessed two intermediate-risk factors: he was **male** and tested positive for **anti-SAE1 antibodies**. While anti-SAE1 positivity alone does not confer the same strong oncogenic association as anti-TIF1-γ or anti-NXP2 antibodies (which are classically linked to higher malignancy rates), it contributes to the cumulative risk profile when paired with other factors like male sex.

It is crucial to understand that this classification drives the *intensity* of the screening protocol, not necessarily the pre-test probability of finding cancer immediately. As seen in this case, a patient can be categorized as high-risk based on clinical phenotype alone—specifically DM plus dysphagia—even when tumor markers are normal and initial cross-sectional imaging (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis) and functional imaging (PET scan) are negative. The high-risk designation justified proceeding with further invasive surveillance, specifically the planned gastroscopy and colonoscopy to rule out occult gastrointestinal malignancies that would not be detected by serum markers or standard CT imaging. Thus, the classification is a proactive measure ensuring no potential malignancy is missed during the period of highest statistical risk following diagnosis.
