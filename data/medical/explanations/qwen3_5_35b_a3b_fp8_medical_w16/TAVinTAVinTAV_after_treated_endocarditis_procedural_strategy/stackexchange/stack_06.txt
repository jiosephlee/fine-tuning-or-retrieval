Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Differential Diagnosis] If this patient requires future coronary angiography or intervention after receiving three generations of TAVI devices with varying stent geometries, what are the primary barriers to access created by this 'triple-stenting' scenario, and what alternative strategies might be considered if standard wire passage fails?

**Clinical Pearl:** In a "triple-stenting" scenario involving three generations of TAVI devices, the primary barrier to future coronary angiography is **geometric distortion and loss of ostial patency** caused by the cumulative metal density of overlapping stent frames. Each successive valve creates a new "neo-skirt" or mesh layer that can obscure the coronary ostia, elevate them relative to the new landing zone, or physically impede catheter engagement. As seen in this case, even with careful commissural alignment and high implantation of the Acurate Neo2 to minimize neo-skirt formation below the coronaries, the sheer volume of metallic strata significantly increases the risk of losing future coronary access.

When standard wire passage fails due to these dense, layered structures, you must consider **alternative strategies beyond simple wire manipulation**. First, utilize **intravascular imaging (IVUS or OCT)** through a microcatheter to precisely map the exact location of the ostium relative to the stacked frames; this often reveals a channel previously obscured by fluoroscopy. Second, employ **cross-over techniques**, advancing a guide catheter from a contralateral site (e.g., brachial or axillary) to achieve better coaxial alignment and backup support. Finally, in extreme cases where standard access is impossible but coronary protection is needed for future interventions, consider **bioprosthetic valve fracture** (if feasible given the existing layers) or specialized anchoring devices designed to create a new conduit. However, as noted in our case discussion, these maneuvers carry heightened risks in triple-valve configurations, reinforcing that meticulous pre-procedural CT planning and strategic commissural alignment during every reintervention are critical to preserving future coronary accessibility.
