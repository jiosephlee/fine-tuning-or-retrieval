**Clinical Teaching Answer**

In the management of adult anaphylaxis, the cornerstone of therapy is immediate intramuscular (IM) epinephrine. Current guidelines recommend a dose of **0.01 mg/kg**, capped at **0.5 mg** for adults weighing more than 50 kg. This recommendation is based on pharmacokinetic principles: epinephrine must achieve sufficient plasma concentrations rapidly to reverse vasodilation, reduce mucosal edema, and stabilize mast cells. In patients exceeding 50 kg, particularly those with higher adipose tissue mass like our patient (104 kg), a fixed dose of 0.3 mg—the content of many standard auto-injectors—is often subtherapeutic. Studies suggest that in heavier adults, 0.3 mg may fail to reach the threshold concentration required for sustained alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction and beta-adrenergic bronchodilation compared to the full 0.5 mg dose.

This pharmacokinetic deficit directly explains the multiphasic course observed in this patient. Upon her initial presentation with hypotensive shock (BP 74/50 mm Hg), she received a single 0.3 mg auto-injected dose via IM route. While she demonstrated transient improvement, the suboptimal dose likely failed to completely arrest the massive mediator release driven by her underlying systemic mastocytosis and high inflammatory burden. Consequently, as the drug's short half-life elapsed (~1–2 minutes for peak effect, rapid clearance), plasma levels dropped below therapeutic thresholds before the inflammatory cascade resolved, precipitating recurrent episodes of anaphylactic shock within hours.
