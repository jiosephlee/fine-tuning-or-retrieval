Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Clinical Pearls] In the context of genetic variant interpretation for MODY, if a novel HNF1A variant is not found in public databases like ClinVar but shows segregation with disease in the family, high AlphaMissense scores, and affects an amino acid adjacent to known pathogenic residues, which ACMG classification criteria are satisfied to designate it as 'Likely Pathogenic'?

To classify the novel *HNF1A* variant **p.N270S** as **"Likely Pathogenic"** in this case, we rely on specific ACMG/AMP criteria that collectively bridge the gap left by the variant's absence in public databases like ClinVar.

First, the criterion **PP3** is satisfied. This computational evidence supports a damaging effect when multiple lines of algorithmic prediction agree. In this patient, the variant achieved a very high **AlphaMissense score of 0.995**, providing strong computational evidence that the amino acid change is deleterious to protein function.

Second, the criterion **PM1** is met. This applies when a missense variant affects an amino acid residue located within a known functional domain or at a site critical for function where other pathogenic variants have been documented. Here, p.N270S resides in the **DNA-binding domain** of *HNF1A*, specifically affecting an amino acid residue **adjacent to known pathogenic residues**. Given that *HNF1A*-MODY mutations predominantly cluster in this DNA-binding domain to disrupt transcriptional regulation of insulin and GLUT2 genes, proximity to established hotspots strongly implies pathogenicity.

Third, the criterion **PS4** (or potentially PM2_Supporting combined with PP1) is fulfilled through clinical observation. While PS4 typically requires statistical enrichment in cases versus controls, in single-case reporting contexts, robust **segregation with disease** serves as powerful corroborating evidence (often mapped to **PP1_Strong**). The case report confirms an autosomal dominant pattern where the variant co-segregates with early-onset diabetes across three generations (maternal grandmother, mother, and brother), aligning perfectly with the clinical phenotype of HNF1A-MODY.

Finally, while the absence from databases satisfies **PM2**, it does not prove pathogenicity on its own; however, when combined with PP3 (high predictive score), PM1 (critical domain location), and PP1 (family segregation), these lines of evidence satisfy the threshold for **"Likely Pathogenic."** This classification justifies treating this novel finding as clinically actionable for precision medicine decisions, such as transitioning from insulin to sulfonylureas or GLP-1 receptor agonists as seen in this patient's successful management.
