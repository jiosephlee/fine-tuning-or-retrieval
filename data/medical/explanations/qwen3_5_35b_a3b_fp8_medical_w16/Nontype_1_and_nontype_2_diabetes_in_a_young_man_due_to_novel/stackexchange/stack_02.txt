Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Non-type 1 and non-type 2 diabetes in a young man due to novel mutation in HNF1A"

### [Diagnostic Reasoning] Which three autoantibodies must be tested and found negative to strongly support a diagnosis of MODY over Type 1 Diabetes in a patient presenting with early-onset hyperglycemia and preserved C-peptide levels?

To distinguish Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young (MODY) from Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM) in a patient with early-onset hyperglycemia and preserved beta-cell function, one must first exclude the autoimmune destruction characteristic of T1DM. While the presence of positive autoantibodies confirms T1DM, their absence does not definitively prove MODY but is a mandatory prerequisite for pursuing genetic testing.

According to the case report provided, the diagnostic algorithm relies heavily on demonstrating a lack of autoimmune markers alongside clinical features like a strong family history and preserved C-peptide levels. Specifically, the text notes that "negative autoantibodies associated with type 1 diabetes" are required before molecular genetic testing becomes the next logical step. The three specific autoantibodies that must be tested and found negative to strongly support a diagnosis of MODY over Type 1 Diabetes are:

1.  **Glutamic Acid Decarboxylase Antibodies (GADA)**
2.  **Insulinoma Antigen-2 Autoantibodies (IA-2A)**
3.  **Zinc Transporter 8 Antibodies (ZnT8A)**

In the patient described in this case, GADA and IA-2A were explicitly noted as negative. Although ZnT8 was not individually listed in the *results* section of this specific abstract, it is cited in the background discussion as one of the standard triad of antibodies included in modern diagnostic algorithms ("including glutamic acid decarboxylase antibodies... insulinoma antigen-2... and zinc transporter 8"). Furthermore, the Discussion references a Russian study that utilized exactly this trio (IAA, GADA, and ZnT8) to screen patients suspected of hereditary diabetes without autoimmune features. Therefore, finding all three negative is essential to rule out T1DM sufficiently to justify proceeding with *HNF1A* genetic sequencing for confirmation.
