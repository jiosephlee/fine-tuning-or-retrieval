# Beyond Autoantibodies: Leveraging Glucagon Stimulation Tests and Family History for MODY Suspicion

We've all been there: the young patient walks in with a BMI of 24.6 kg/m², no acanthosis nigricans, and an HbA1c of 10.2%. They have glycosuria, they're losing weight, and their parents are terrified it's Type 1 Diabetes (T1D). You run the autoimmune panel—negative. You check the C-peptide—it's "okay," but maybe not *perfect*. Do you start them on lifelong insulin? Or do you suspect something else entirely?

This case report of a young man with a **novel HNF1A mutation** serves as a masterclass in why we must look past the binary labels of T1D vs. T2D. The patient presented with classic "Type 1-like" symptoms (weight loss, polydipsia) but lacked the autoimmune signature. He also didn't fit the typical "obese metabolic syndrome" profile of T2D, despite having dyslipidemia. The turning point wasn't just the genetics; it was the clinical reasoning that connected a specific family history pattern with subtle physiological clues that standard screening misses.

### The Diagnostic Dilemma: When the Standard Algorithm Fails

The biggest pitfall in diagnosing Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young (MODY) is assuming that negative autoantibodies automatically rule out T1D or that preserved C-peptide rules out beta-cell failure. In this case, the patient had negative GADA and IA-2A antibodies, yet he required insulin initially because his HbA1c was dangerously high (10.2%).

What made us pause? Two things stood out immediately upon reviewing the history and labs:
1.  **The Pedigree:** This wasn't sporadic diabetes. His mother was diagnosed in her late 20s, his grandmother in her 30s, and his younger brother at age 20. That is a textbook vertical transmission pattern suggestive of an autosomal dominant trait.
2.  **The Physiology:** While his fasting C-peptide looked decent at baseline, the dynamic testing told a different story.

It is easy to get distracted by metabolic syndrome features here—the patient had high triglycerides (4.31 mmol/L) and low HDL (1.01 mmol/L). Historically, we taught ourselves that MODY patients are lean and metabolically pristine compared to T2D patients. But as this case illustrates, rising obesity rates are blurring these lines. Relying solely on BMI or lipid profiles to exclude MODY is dangerous; this patient had features mimicking metabolic syndrome but turned out to be monogenic diabetes driven by an HNF1A defect affecting renal glucose handling (hence the prominent glycosuria) and beta-cell function.

### The Power of Dynamic Testing: Why We Need More Than Fasting C-Peptide

Fasting C-peptide levels can be misleading in early-stage beta-cell dysfunction or during periods of stress hyperglycemia ("glucose toxicity"). In this case, relying on a static snapshot might have led us astray if we hadn't pushed further with functional testing.

The **glucagon stimulation test** proved pivotal here. While a stimulated C-peptide >0.6 nmol/L generally suggests preserved endogenous secretion, this patient sat right on that borderline threshold with a peak of only 0.83 nmol/L after glucagon stimulation (starting at 0.39 nmol/L). This "moderately preserved yet insufficient" response signaled that while he wasn't absolute insulin-deficient like advanced T1D, his beta-cells were failing to mount an adequate secretory surge—a hallmark of HNF1A-MODY progression where beta-cell mass slowly declines over decades rather than abruptly vanishing overnight as in T1D autoimmune destruction.

Furthermore, consider the autonomic neuropathy finding: severely reduced sudomotor function despite only one year of documented hyperglycemia severity in terms of duration (though likely undiagnosed longer). Autonomic dysfunction can sometimes precede overt microvascular complications in monogenic diabetes due to chronic suboptimal control or specific gene-related pathways, serving as another red flag that this isn't "simple" T2D physiology waiting for lifestyle changes alone to fix it forever without targeted therapy optimization later on (though usually seen later). *Correction*: Actually, looking closely at the text again regarding sudomotor testing—it showed severe reduction indicating significant autonomic dysfunction *at diagnosis*. This is unusual for such short duration unless there was prolonged undetected hyperglycemia or it reflects early small fiber involvement common in MODY if left untreated for years prior to diagnosis detection during routine checks? Wait, let's stick strictly to the text provided: "Sudomotor testing demonstrated severely reduced sudomotor function... indicating significant autonomic dysfunction." This finding adds complexity but doesn't change the primary diagnostic pivot which remains family history + antibody status + genetic confirmation. The takeaway is that phenotypic variability exists even within monogenic diseases; don't let one outlier sign deter you from pursuing genetics if your pre-test probability is high enough based on inheritance patterns and antibody negativity.**Back to core logic**: The glucagon test confirmed sufficient residual function to avoid permanent insulin dependence *if* treated correctly later, but insufficient function to handle massive glucose loads without help initially. ### Precision Medicine Starts Before the Sequencing Panel

Why does getting this right matter? Because treating HNF1A-MODY like Type 2 Diabetes leads to unnecessary medication burden and missed opportunities for simpler therapies (like low-dose sulfonylureas), while treating it like Type 1 exposes patients to lifelong injections when oral agents would suffice once glucose toxicity resolves or if managed earlier with appropriate secretagogues/GLP-RA strategies as shown here where GLP-RA worked wonders replacing insulin later on after initial stabilization phase done properly). Also beware SGLT-2 inhibitors! As noted in discussion section: $HNF_{\text{I}}A$ mutations impair renal glucose reabsorption via SGLT2 downregulation naturally; adding an SGLT-2 inhibitor could precipitate severe dehydration/DKA due to synergistic inhibition—another reason knowing your genotype prevents catastrophic iatrogenic errors!

Genetic testing confirmed a novel heterozygous variant (**p.N270S**) in exon 4 of *HNFIA*, absent from major databases but classified pathogenic based on ACMG criteria (location near known pathogenic residues, segregation with disease). Without running this panel based on our clinical suspicion derived from family tree + negative antibodies + dynamic C-peptide curve interpretation—we'd have missed this entirely! And remember: Exeter calculator gave him ~62% probability despite non-European background showing tools need calibration across demographics too! But even rough estimates shouldn't stop us from ordering panels when clinical picture screams "monogenic"!

## Key Takeaways for Clinical Practice

To wrap up this discussion on leveraging clinical clues before ordering expensive genetic panels:

*   **Suspect MODY Early:** If you see early-onset diabetes (<35 yrs) with strong autosomal dominant family history AND negative autoantibodies—even if BMI is normal/high or lipids are abnormal—think MONOGENIC DIABETES first before settling into generic TMD protocols permanently without investigation.**
*   **Don’t Ignore Negative Antibodies Alone:** Negative GADA/IA-2/ZnT8 does NOT guarantee Type II Diabetes until proven otherwise by genetic testing when suspicion arises from pedigree analysis!**
*   **Use Dynamic Testing Wisely:** Glucose-stimulated C-peptide curves provide superior insight into residual β-cell capacity compared static measurements alone helping guide therapeutic choices away from premature permanent insulin initiation.**
*   **Avoid Harmful Drugs:** Remember that patients harboring *HNFIA* mutations possess inherently altered renal glucose handling mechanisms rendering them highly susceptible adverse events related usage Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter Protein Type-II Inhibitors(SGLTI); therefore avoid prescribing these agents unless absolutely necessary under strict specialist supervision following careful risk-benefit assessments prioritizing safety over theoretical efficacy gains!**
*   **Precision Saves Lives & Money:** Identifying monogenic causes enables tailored treatments reducing unnecessary medication burdens preventing costly hospitalizations due preventable complications
