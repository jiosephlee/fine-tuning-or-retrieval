Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] Given the diagnosis of autoimmune HIT with persistent hypercoagulability and severe thrombocytopenia, what is the rationale for switching from a direct thrombin inhibitor like argatroban to fondaparinux, specifically considering the role of antithrombin levels in this transition?

**Clinical Pearl:** In cases of severe autoimmune HIT (aHIT) characterized by persistent hypercoagulability despite adequate direct thrombin inhibitor therapy, transitioning to fondaparinux requires careful attention to the patient's endogenous **antithrombin (AT)** status because fondaparinux is strictly dependent on functional AT to inhibit Factor Xa.

In this specific case, the patient exhibited "treatment failure" with argatroban, evidenced by a paradoxical drop in fibrinogen, rising D-dimers, and new-onset microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (schistocytes), signaling that the underlying hypercoagulable drive of aHIT was not being adequately suppressed. While argatroban acts directly on thrombin independent of cofactors, fondaparinux functions as an indirect inhibitor of Factor Xa by binding to and accelerating the activity of antithrombin. Consequently, if antithrombin levels are depleted—as they often are in severe inflammatory states or disseminated intravascular coagulation-like presentations—the efficacy of fondaparinux can be severely compromised regardless of dosing.

The pivotal decision point in this case was the laboratory finding of **mildly reduced antithrombin levels (0.70 U/mL; reference 0.80–1.40 U/mL)**. Recognizing that even mild reductions can significantly blunt the anticoagulant potency of fondaparinux in a setting where rapid reversal of extreme thrombosis is critical, the team supplemented the patient with **antithrombin concentrate (1000 U)** immediately prior to initiating therapeutic-dose fondaparinux. This strategy ensured that sufficient cofactor was available for the drug to function effectively. Following this optimization, the patient demonstrated a robust clinical response with gradual platelet recovery and resolution of thrombotic complications. Thus, checking and correcting antithrombin levels is a mandatory prerequisite when switching to fondaparinux in severe HIT to ensure therapeutic success.
