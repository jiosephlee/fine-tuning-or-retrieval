Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Management] Why is high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) often utilized in cases of autoimmune HIT, and what specific clinical expectation regarding platelet count recovery should be tempered if a patient demonstrates only a modest response to IVIG as seen in this case?

In this case of severe autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (aHIT), high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is utilized specifically to neutralize circulating pathogenic autoantibodies that possess **heparin-independent platelet-activating properties**. Unlike classic HIT, where antibodies require heparin-PF4 complexes to trigger platelet FcγRIIa receptors, aHIT antibodies can directly activate platelets even in the absence of heparin. IVIG works by saturating these activating Fc receptors on the platelet surface with excess IgG, thereby blocking the interaction between the patient's pathogenic antibodies and their targets, effectively halting the immune-mediated destruction and activation of platelets.

However, clinicians must temper their expectations regarding the speed and magnitude of platelet recovery when administering IVIG in severe aHIT. While classic HIT or mild aHIT often presents with a "brisk" or dramatic rise in platelet count shortly after IVIG initiation, this case illustrates that patients with highly refractory, severe aHIT may demonstrate only a **modest response**, characterized by a slow, incremental rise rather than an immediate normalization. As seen in this patient, despite receiving 300 g of IVIG over three days, the platelet count rose only from 13×10⁹/L to 54×10⁹/L—a significant but incomplete recovery compared to the typical rapid correction. This underscores that the severity of the underlying serological phenotype (evidenced here by partial resistance to Fc-receptor blockade *in vitro*) dictates the clinical course; therefore, a modest initial response to IVIG does not necessarily indicate treatment failure but rather reflects the extreme potency and persistence of the autoimmune drive requiring sustained supportive care and alternative anticoagulation strategies like fondaparinux until full resolution occurs.
