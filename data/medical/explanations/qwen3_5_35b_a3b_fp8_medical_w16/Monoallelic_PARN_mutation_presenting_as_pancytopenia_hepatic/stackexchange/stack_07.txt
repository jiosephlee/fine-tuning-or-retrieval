Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Morning Report] If a patient presents with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and unexplained cytopenias but lacks the classic Dyskeratosis Congenita triad, what diagnostic modality serves as the gold standard alongside genetic sequencing to confirm shortened telomeres?

When evaluating a patient with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and unexplained cytopenias where the classic Dyskeratosis Congenita triad is absent, the gold standard for confirming shortened telomeres—alongside genetic sequencing—is **flow cytometry-based fluorescence *in situ* hybridization (Flow-FISH)**.

While the clinical case you reviewed describes a patient with a monoallelic *PARN* variant and features suggestive of a Telomere Biology Disorder (TBD), it is important to recognize that genetic testing alone can sometimes yield inconclusive results. As noted in the discussion of this case, the patient's specific *PARN* variant was classified as a "Variant of Uncertain Significance" based on current databases, despite strong bioinformatic predictions of pathogenicity. Therefore, demonstrating the functional consequence of the genetic defect—specifically, abnormally short telomeres—is essential for definitive diagnosis.

Pathophysiologically, TBDs arise from defects in telomere maintenance machinery, leading to premature telomere attrition. This manifests clinically as bone marrow failure (pancytopenia) and organ fibrosis (pulmonary or hepatic) because tissues with high turnover rates cannot sustain replication. Flow-FISH allows for the precise quantification of telomere length in lymphocytes relative to age-matched controls. In this context, it provides objective confirmation of the diagnosis when genotype-phenotype correlations are ambiguous or when only monoallelic variants are found, which is common in adult presentations of TBDs involving genes like *PARN*, *TERT*, or *TERC*. Thus, combining genetic sequencing with Flow-FISH ensures accurate identification of the disorder to guide appropriate surveillance and management strategies.
