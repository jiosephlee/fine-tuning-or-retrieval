Title: Blog about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

# Beyond the GI Complaints: Recognizing Systemic Red Flags in GLP-1 Users

We've all seen the "Semaglutide Face" – the weight loss success stories flooding social media. But as the prescribing volume of GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) explodes, so does our responsibility to recognize their less heralded, life-threatening complications. We know they cause nausea, vomiting, and rarely, pancreatitis or gallbladder issues. But what happens when a patient presents not just with a stomach ache, but with **dysphagia** and **proximal muscle weakness**?

This case report forces us to expand our differential diagnosis for anyone starting these agents. It describes a woman in her 60s whose journey began with classic GI intolerance—a single dose of semaglutide triggered severe nausea, vomiting, and acute kidney injury requiring ICU-level support. On paper, that sounds like typical drug intolerance. But here is the twist: four months later, she could barely stand or swallow liquids. Her Creatine Kinase (CK) skyrocketed from a modest 337 U/L during her initial hospitalization to nearly 4,000 U/L.

The punchline? This wasn't simple rhabdomyolysis from dehydration or direct toxin damage that resolves upon stopping the drug. This was **immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM)**. She tested negative for the classic autoantibodies (HMGCR and SRP), making it even harder to diagnose initially. Yet, her muscle biopsy confirmed necrosis with macrophage infiltration, and crucially, she responded dramatically to immunosuppression (steroids + IVIG) once semaglutide was discontinued.

This is likely the first reported case linking semaglutide directly to IMNM. While previous literature has linked tirzepatide to rhabdomyolysis (which typically resolves quickly after drug cessation), this case demonstrates a delayed-onset autoimmune phenomenon where CK levels actually *worsened* significantly months after exposure. If you missed this nuance in your mental model, you might miss this diagnosis entirely in a busy ED or clinic setting.

## The Clinical Course: From Dehydration to Dysphagia

Let's walk through the timeline that often gets overlooked until it's too late. Our patient had no prior history of statin use or family myopathy—two major risk factors usually cited for IMNM. Her story starts 2 years into obesity management with NAFLD when she was prescribed semaglutide.

Within hours of that first injection, she crashed hard: nausea, vomiting, metabolic acidosis, and AKI requiring non-invasive ventilation. While she recovered physically from the acute illness over five days, she was left "deconditioned." Most clinicians would chalk this up to being bedbound for a few days after a sick leave home visit. But over the next four months, instead of recovering strength through rehab, her condition deteriorated progressively.

By month four, she couldn't sit up without assistance. She developed dysphonia and trouble swallowing both solids and liquids—a terrifying sign pointing toward bulbar involvement often seen in neuromuscular emergencies like Myasthenia Gravis or ALS. When she finally presented with these new symptoms alongside severe weakness, her CK was nearly 4x higher than normal ranges for typical rhabdo thresholds in some contexts (though lower than massive crush injuries). The key differentiator here is the **time course**. Drug-induced toxic rhabdomyolysis usually peaks quickly and recedes within days of stopping the agent. An autoimmune process like IMNM can have a lag phase where the immune system continues its assault long after the trigger is removed.

The workup required looking past standard labs. She had negative ANA and specific myositis panels (negative for Jo-1, Mi-2, etc.), which can lead physicians to dismiss inflammatory causes prematurely. However, her muscle biopsy told a different story: dispersed necrotic fibers with CD68+ macrophages (myophagocytosis) and membrane attack complex deposition on muscle fibers—the hallmark of complement-mediated necrosis seen in IMNM. Interestingly, she also had transient cardiac markers (troponin elevation) and MRI evidence of myocardial edema; while likely stress-related or concurrent inflammation given her rapid recovery post-treatment, it underscores that this wasn't just skeletal muscle pathology—it was systemic immune activation affecting multiple tissues.

Do not assume it's just deconditioning from recent illness or weight loss itself ("muscle wasting"). ### Key Take-Homes
*   **Don't ignore bulbar symptoms:** Dysphagia/dysphonia in patients on GLP-1 RAs warrants immediate CK testing; do not attribute solely to GERD or anxiety without ruling out neuromuscular causes first!.
*   **Time matters:** Unlike toxic rhabdomyolysis which resolves rapidly upon cessation (~days), immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy may worsen progressively over weeks/months even after stopping the offending agent!.
*   **Seronegativity is real:** Up to one-third of IMNM cases lack HMGCR/SRP antibodies; rely heavily on clinical context + biopsy findings when serology is negative!.
*   **Biopsy beats bloodwork:** If suspicion remains high despite normal labs/imaging early on—don't delay obtaining muscle biopsy showing necrosis/macrophage infiltration which drives definitive diagnosis!.
*   **Early intervention saves function:** Prompt initiation of steroids + IVIG correlates strongly with return to baseline function; delays risk permanent contractures/fibrosis!.
