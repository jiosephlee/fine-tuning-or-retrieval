Title: Textbook chapter about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

Chapter 2: ## Pathophysiological Basis: Behçet's Disease and Neuro-Inflammation

Behçet's disease (BD) is a chronic, relapsing, multisystem vasculitis characterized primarily by recurrent oral aphthous ulcers, genital ulcers, and uveitis, often accompanied by distinct skin lesions such as erythema nodosum or folliculitis-like rashes. While the disease affects multiple organ systems, neurological involvement—termed neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD)—occurs in approximately 10% to 20% of patients. In the vast majority of cases, neuro-BD manifests as a delayed complication, appearing several years after the initial diagnosis of systemic BD. Consequently, the concurrent presentation of active systemic mucocutaneous symptoms and acute neurological deficits, as seen in this patient, represents a rare clinical phenotype where the central nervous system is involved at the very inception of the disease. Understanding this deviation from the typical timeline is crucial for early recognition; while classic presentations evolve slowly over years, acute neuro-BD can present abruptly with meningoencephalitic features that mimic infectious etiologies.

Acute neuro-BD typically involves two distinct pathological patterns: parenchymal brain lesions and non-parenchymal inflammation. Parenchymal lesions predominantly affect the brainstem and basal ganglia due to their rich vascular supply and susceptibility to small-vessel vasculitis; these patients often present with focal neurological deficits such as hemiparesis or cranial nerve palsies. Conversely, non-parenchymal inflammation presents as aseptic meningitis or meningoencephalitis without discrete structural damage visible on standard imaging. The case presented highlights this latter pattern. The patient exhibited severe headache, fever, and vertigo indicative of meningeal irritation and diffuse cerebral inflammation, yet his brain MRI was entirely normal regarding parenchymal signal abnormalities or mass effect. This underscores a critical diagnostic principle: in the early phases of acute neuro-BD, particularly when the primary pathology is confined to the meninges or perivascular spaces rather than causing frank tissue necrosis or edema detectable by conventional MRI sequences, radiological findings may be absent. Therefore, a normal MRI does not exclude the diagnosis if clinical suspicion remains high based on cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) evidence of aseptic meningitis (lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein) alongside characteristic mucocutaneous findings.

The development of Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone Secretion (SIADH) in this context provides a compelling example of how localized CNS inflammation can disrupt systemic homeostasis through cytokine-mediated pathways. SIADH is defined by euvolemic hyponatremia resulting from excessive release of arginine vasopressin (AVP), leading to impaired free water excretion. While SIADH is classically associated with malignancy or drugs in BD patients (such as cyclophosphamide), this case supports a direct mechanistic link between untreated neuro-inflammation and AVP dysregulation. The proposed pathophysiology centers on monocyte activation within the inflamed CNS environment. Activated monocytes produce proinflammatory cytokines, most notably Interleukin-6 (IL-6). Elevated levels of IL-6 have been documented in the CSF of patients with acute neuro-BD and are known to stimulate hypothalamic neurons to secrete AVP independently of plasma osmolality signals. Although IL-6 levels were not directly measured in this specific case report, the markedly elevated serum C-reactive protein serves as a surrogate marker for this intense systemic inflammatory state driven by IL-6 production. Thus, the patient's hyponatremia was likely not merely coincidental but a downstream consequence of cytokine-driven hypothalamic stimulation triggered by the underlying vasculitic process affecting the central nervous system.
