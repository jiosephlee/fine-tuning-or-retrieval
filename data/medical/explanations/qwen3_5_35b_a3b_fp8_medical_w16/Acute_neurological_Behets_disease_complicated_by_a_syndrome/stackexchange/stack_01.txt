Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Based on the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD), what specific clinical findings in this patient contributed to his definitive diagnosis, and why is the presence of genital aphthosis considered particularly weighty in this scoring system?

Based on the **International Criteria for Behçet's Disease (ICBD)**, this patient met the threshold for a definitive diagnosis with a total score of **five points**. The specific clinical findings contributing to this score were:

*   **Oral aphthosis** (2 points): Documented at presentation.
*   **Genital aphthosis** (2 points): Appeared on day 5 of admission as a painful scrotal ulcer.
*   **Skin lesions** (1 point): Specifically, the "folliculitis-like" rashes noted on the face and trunk.

While the patient also exhibited fever, fatigue, and neurological symptoms, these are not assigned specific numerical weights in the ICBD scoring system itself; rather, they support the clinical picture of systemic involvement once the mucocutaneous criteria are met.

The presence of **genital aphthosis** is considered particularly weighty in this scoring system because it carries high specificity for Behçet's disease compared to oral ulcers alone. Oral aphthae are extremely common in the general population and can be seen in many other conditions, whereas genital ulcers—particularly those resembling herpetic lesions but lacking viral etiology—are much more distinctive of BD. Consequently, the ICBD assigns genital aphthosis double the weight (2 points) of oral aphthosis (2 points vs. 1 point for some older criteria, though both are weighted heavily here relative to skin lesions). In this case, the development of genital ulcers transformed a potentially ambiguous presentation into a definitive diagnosis, allowing clinicians to rule out infectious etiologies like HSV/VZV and proceed with targeted management for neuro-Behçet's disease.
