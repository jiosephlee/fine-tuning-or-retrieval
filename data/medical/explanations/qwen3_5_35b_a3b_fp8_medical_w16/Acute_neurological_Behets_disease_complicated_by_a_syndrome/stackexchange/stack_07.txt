Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Differential Diagnosis] List three distinct etiologies outside of primary CNS pathology that must be rigorously excluded before attributing SIADH to Behçet's disease-related inflammation, citing specific diagnostic modalities mentioned in this case report that helped rule them out.

To attribute SIADH to Behçet's disease-related inflammation, one must first rigorously exclude three distinct categories of non-primary CNS pathology: **infectious etiologies**, **primary CNS malignancy**, and **other systemic autoimmune/inflammatory disorders**.

First, **infectious causes** such as viral meningoencephalitis (specifically HSV or VZV) and bacterial or tuberculous meningitis must be ruled out. In this case, these were definitively excluded through cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis demonstrating aseptic meningitis (lymphocytic pleocytosis without neutrophils), alongside negative CSF cultures, acid-fast stains, tuberculosis DNA testing, and specific polymerase chain reaction assays for HSV and VZV.

Second, **primary CNS malignancy**, particularly lymphoma or metastatic disease, requires exclusion given the potential for paraneoplastic SIADH. The case report confirms this was addressed via a normal brain MRI showing no parenchymal lesions or mass effect and negative CSF cytology which revealed no malignant cells. While the soluble interleukin-2 receptor (sIL-2R) was mildly elevated—a marker sometimes associated with lymphoma—the authors noted this finding was non-specific in the absence of radiographic evidence or other hematologic signs of malignancy.

Third, other **systemic granulomatous diseases**, specifically neurosarcoidosis, must be distinguished from Behçet's. Although neurosarcoidosis can mimic neuro-BD with normal MRI findings in early stages, this etiology was excluded by normal serum angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) levels and normal lysozyme concentrations. Furthermore, the absence of characteristic systemic symptoms like uveitis or hilar adenopathy on imaging supported the rejection of sarcoidosis in favor of Behçet's disease.

Only after systematically excluding infections via CSF microbiology/virology, ruling out neoplasm via MRI/cytology/sIL-2R interpretation, and dismissing sarcoidosis via ACE/lysozyme levels could the diagnosis of acute neuro-Behçet's disease complicated by SIADH be confidently established.
