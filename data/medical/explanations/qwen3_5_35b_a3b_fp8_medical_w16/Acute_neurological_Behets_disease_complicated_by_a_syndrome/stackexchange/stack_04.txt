Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Morning Report] Differentiate the expected cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) profile in acute neuro-Behçet's disease presenting as aseptic meningitis versus tuberculous meningitis and viral encephalitis (e.g., HSV/VZV), specifically focusing on cell count differentials, protein levels, and culture results.

**Clinical Pearl: Distinguishing Acute Neuro-Behçet's from Infectious Meningoencephalitis via CSF Analysis**

In the evaluation of a patient presenting with fever, headache, and neurological deficits, distinguishing **acute neuro-Behçet's disease (neuro-BD)** presenting as aseptic meningitis from infectious etiologies like **tuberculous meningitis (TBM)** or **viral encephalitis (HSV/VZV)** relies heavily on the specific pattern of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) findings combined with clinical context.

Based on the case presented, **acute neuro-BD** manifests as a sterile, inflammatory process characterized by **mild lymphocytic pleocytosis** and moderately elevated protein. In this specific patient, the CSF showed 11 cells/µL (predominantly lymphocytes) and a protein level of 0.97 g/L. Crucially, all microbiological workups—including bacterial smears/cultures, mycobacterial stains/DNA testing for TB, and PCR/antigen assays for HSV and VZV—were negative. This "aseptic" profile in the presence of classic mucocutaneous Behçet's stigmata (oral/genital aphthae) supports a diagnosis of neuro-inflammation rather than infection.

To differentiate this from **tuberculous meningitis**, one must look beyond the cell count. While TBM also presents with lymphocytic pleocytosis, it typically exhibits a much higher white blood cell count (often >50–100 cells/µL), significantly higher protein levels (>1.0 g/L), and critically, low glucose (hypoglycorrhachia). Furthermore, TBM would yield positive acid-fast bacilli smears or nucleic acid amplification tests (NAAT), whereas the case report explicitly notes these were negative in neuro-BD. Similarly, while **viral encephalitis** shares the feature of lymphocytic predominance, it is distinguished by positive PCR or serology for specific viruses (e.g., HSV-1 IgM/IgG or VZV DNA). The case report confirms that the absence of viral antigens and antibodies effectively ruled out HSV/VZV etiology despite the initial clinical suspicion driven by fever and headache.

Therefore, the key differentiator is the combination of a mild-to-moderate inflammatory profile with strictly negative cultures and molecular testing in a patient exhibiting pathognomonic systemic features of Behçet's disease. As highlighted in this case, a normal brain MRI does not exclude acute neuro-BD if CSF shows aseptic meningitis; conversely, the absence of organisms in CSF makes primary infectious causes highly unlikely once clinical suspicion for BD is raised by mucocutaneous findings.
