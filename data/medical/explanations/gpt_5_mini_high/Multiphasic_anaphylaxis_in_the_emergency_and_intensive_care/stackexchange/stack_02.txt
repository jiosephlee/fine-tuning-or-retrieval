Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Multiphasic anaphylaxis in the emergency and intensive care setting"

### [Management] In a 104-kg adult with anaphylactic shock, what is the recommended initial epinephrine dose, route, site, and repeat interval, and what are the clinical implications of using a 0.3 mg auto-injector instead of the guideline-recommended 0.5 mg dose?

Answer (clinical pearl)

- Recommended initial dose: 0.01 mg/kg intramuscularly, with a practical adult cap of 0.5 mg per dose. For a 104‑kg adult this means give 0.5 mg IM (most guidelines simplify to 0.5 mg for adults >50 kg).

- Route and site: intramuscular (IM) into the mid‑anterolateral thigh (vastus lateralis) using epinephrine 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL). IM thigh injection achieves faster, more reliable absorption than subcutaneous or deltoid injections.

- Repeat interval and escalation: repeat the IM dose every 5 minutes as needed until clinical improvement. If there is no meaningful response after 2–3 IM doses, or if the patient remains in shock, escalate to intravenous epinephrine in a monitored setting (small titrated IV boluses or an infusion) and admit to higher‑level care.

Why this matters (clinical reasoning, tied to the case)
- The guideline 0.01 mg/kg dosing is capped at 0.5 mg because most adults weigh >50 kg; therefore a 104‑kg patient should receive the full 0.5 mg IM dose initially. IM epinephrine into the thigh maximizes rapid peak blood levels and reverses airway, bronchospasm and vasodilation more reliably than lower or subcutaneous doses.
- In the published case the patient (104 kg) received a 0.3‑mg auto‑injector in the ED and initially improved, but then had recurrent (multiphasic) episodes requiring further IM doses; later individually prepared 0.5‑mg IM doses were used. The authors note that many commonly available adult auto‑injectors are only 0.3 mg and that retrospective data show an increased need for escalation when 0.3 mg is used as the initial adult dose.

Practical implications of using a 0.3‑mg auto‑injector instead of 0.5 mg
- Underdosing risk: 0.3 mg is below the guideline dose for adults >50 kg. It may produce a partial or temporary response but is more likely to require repeated doses or escalation (additional IM doses, IV epinephrine, ICU care), as illustrated by the case.
- Injection depth matters: in heavier patients an auto‑injector’s needle may deposit drug subcutaneously rather than intramuscularly, further slowing absorption and functionally underdosing the patient.
- Operational trade‑off: do not delay—if a 0.3‑mg auto‑injector is immediately available, give it promptly rather than waiting to draw up a 0.5‑mg syringe. But be prepared to give additional IM epinephrine to reach 0.5 mg and to escalate care if response is incomplete.
- Systems implication: hospitals and prehospital services should consider availability of 0.5‑mg adult devices or ensure rapid ability to draw 0.5 mg IM from ampoules; clinicians should recognize that adults discharged after severe reactions (especially with mast cell disorders) ideally should have access to an appropriate‑dose auto‑injector and specialist follow‑up.

Bottom line: for a 104‑kg adult in anaphylactic shock give 0.5 mg epinephrine IM into the mid‑thigh and repeat q5 minutes as needed. A 0.3‑mg auto‑injector is better than no immediate epinephrine but is likely subtherapeutic in a patient of this weight and may increase the chance of recurrent or refractory episodes, as the case demonstrates.
