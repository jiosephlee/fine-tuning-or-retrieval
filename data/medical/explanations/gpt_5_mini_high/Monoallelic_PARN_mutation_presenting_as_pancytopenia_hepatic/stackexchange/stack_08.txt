Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Monoallelic PARN mutation presenting as pancytopenia, hepatic fibrosis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis"

### [Management] For a young adult diagnosed with or strongly suspected of having a telomere biology disorder, outline key elements of longitudinal surveillance and risk-reduction counseling you would implement across hematologic, pulmonary, hepatic, and cancer risks.

Short answer
In a young adult with a confirmed or strongly suspected telomere biology disorder (TBD) you must adopt a proactive, multidisciplinary surveillance strategy focused on four vulnerable organ systems (bone marrow, lungs, liver, and cancer-prone mucocutaneous/skin sites), coupled with lifestyle and reproductive counselling. The goal is early detection of progressive marrow failure or clonal evolution, timely treatment referral (HSCT, antifibrotic or transplant evaluation), and prevention of avoidable iatrogenic and environmental harms. The patient in the case report—late-20s woman with a monoallelic PARN variant, transient improvement after B12 repletion, established pulmonary fibrosis on CT, and cirrhosis by FibroScan—illustrates why surveillance must be individualized and multidisciplinary.

Practical surveillance and risk‑reduction plan (what to do and why)

1) Baseline characterization (at diagnosis)
- Confirm the diagnosis phenotype and establish objective biomarkers: genetic testing (already done in this case) and telomere length measurement by flow-FISH (gold standard) to document short telomeres if not yet measured.
- Baseline labs and studies: CBC with reticulocyte count and peripheral smear, iron studies, B12/folate (correct reversible contributors as in this case), CMP (AST/ALT, bilirubin, albumin), INR, platelets, viral hepatitis serologies, autoimmune serologies as indicated.
- Hematology work-up: bone marrow biopsy with cytogenetics and a myeloid NGS panel if cytopenias present (the case had a normal marrow initially—this is a baseline to compare).
- Pulmonary: baseline high-resolution CT (HRCT), full pulmonary function tests (spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO), 6-minute-walk test.
- Hepatic: ultrasound + transient elastography (FibroScan—already done: 18.1 kPa consistent with cirrhosis), and baseline HCC-risk assessment.
- Mucocutaneous/oncologic baseline: dermatology and ENT/oral mucosa exam; dental exam.

2) Hematologic surveillance and counseling
- Rationale: TBDs predispose to progressive cytopenias, MDS/AML and transfusion dependence; marrow can be vulnerable even after reversible causes are treated (as in this patient).
- Monitoring:
  - CBC with retic and peripheral smear every 1–3 months while unstable or during work-up, then every 3–6 months if stable. Sooner if new symptoms (bleeding, infections, worsening fatigue).
  - Repeat bone marrow + cytogenetics/NGS for new or worsening cytopenias, transfusion dependence, or abnormal cytogenetics—to detect clonal evolution.
- Management principles:
  - Supportive care (transfusions, infection prevention). Growth factors may be used selectively.
  - Androgen therapy (e.g., danazol) has produced hematologic responses and telomere lengthening in small studies but has hepatotoxicity and uncertain long-term benefit — avoid or use with extreme caution in patients with hepatic fibrosis (this patient’s cirrhosis strongly limits androgen use).
  - Refer early to a hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) center experienced in TBDs for discussion if progressive marrow failure, MDS or leukemia develops. Important transplant principles: screen potential related donors for the familial telomere defect/telomere length and use reduced‑intensity, tailored conditioning because TBD patients are sensitive to myeloablative regimens.

3) Pulmonary surveillance and counseling
- Rationale: monoallelic PARN and other telomere gene carriers commonly develop premature pulmonary fibrosis (this patient already has radiographic fibrosis).
- Monitoring:
  - Baseline HRCT and PFTs (spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO, 6MWT).
  - Reassess PFTs and DLCO every 3–6 months while disease is active or progressive; at least every 6–12 months if stable.
  - Repeat HRCT every 12 months or sooner with clinical decline.
- Management principles and risk reduction:
  - Smoking cessation and avoidance of inhalational occupational exposures are essential.
  - Early referral to an interstitial lung disease (ILD) center; consider antifibrotic therapy (nintedanib or pirfenidone) for progressive fibrotic phenotypes—coordinate with hepatology in this patient because these drugs have hepatic effects.
  - Early lung transplant evaluation in progressive disease is prudent because pulmonary decline can be rapid; pre-transplant assessment must integrate marrow and liver comorbidities.

4) Hepatic surveillance and counseling
- Rationale: hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis are common in adult TBD cohorts; this patient already meets criteria for cirrhosis by FibroScan.
- Monitoring:
  - LFTs (AST/ALT, bilirubin), synthetic function (INR, albumin), and platelets every 3–6 months or sooner with change.
  - Ultrasound (with or without AFP) every 6 months for HCC surveillance when cirrhosis present.
  - Repeat transient elastography periodically (e.g., annually) and hepatology follow-up.
  - Screening endoscopy for varices based on portal hypertension signs (low platelets, splenomegaly, or clinical stigmata).
- Management principles:
  - Abstain from alcohol; avoid hepatotoxic medications (androgens are relatively contraindicated with cirrhosis).
  - Vaccinate for hepatitis A and B if non-immune.
  - Refer to hepatology for cirrhosis management and for transplant assessment if decompensation occurs.

5) Cancer risk reduction and surveillance
- Rationale: TBDs raise risk for MDS/AML and certain solid tumors—especially head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (oral leukoplakia), skin cancers, and lung cancer (esp. with fibrosis).
- Counseling and surveillance:
  - Strong advice to avoid tobacco and heavy alcohol.
  - Oral cavity exams every 3–6 months if mucosal lesions or leukoplakia; low threshold for biopsy of suspicious lesions.
  - Annual dermatology exam (self-skin checks taught to the patient).
  - Age-appropriate standard cancer screening supplemented by heightened vigilance; HCC ultrasound/AFP every 6 months for cirrhosis.
  - Avoid unnecessary ionizing radiation and cytotoxic exposures when alternatives exist; coordinate oncologic therapy in specialized centers if cancer occurs.

6) Reproductive and family counselling
- Offer genetic counselling and cascade testing of first-degree relatives for the PARN variant and telomere length measurement; relatives should be screened before being considered HSCT donors.
- Discuss reproductive options (preimplantation genetic diagnosis) and pregnancy risks — pregnancy may worsen cardiopulmonary or marrow disease; care should be high-risk and multidisciplinary.
- Fertility preservation discussion before cytotoxic therapy or transplant.

7) Vaccination, infection prevention, bone health, and other supportive measures
- Vaccinate against influenza, pneumococcus and SARS-CoV-2; avoid live vaccines if immunosuppressed.
- Screen and treat bone disease (DEXA — this patient has osteoporosis) and supplement vitamin D/calcium; treat per guidelines.
- Provide genetic-disease‑specific psychosocial support and consider referral to a TBD registry or specialized center for longitudinal care and research.

Red flags prompting urgent action
- New transfusion dependence, falling reticulocyte count, new cytogenetic abnormalities, rapidly declining PFTs or DLCO, new hypoxia, hepatic decompensation (ascites, encephalopathy, jaundice), new or enlarging focused mucosal/skin lesions — any of these should prompt expedited specialist review (hematology, ILD center, hepatology, oncology).

Applying this to the reported patient
Because she already has cirrhosis (FibroScan 18.1 kPa) and radiographic pulmonary fibrosis with pancytopenia that initially improved after B12 repletion, immediate steps are: document telomere length (if not done), establish a schedule of CBCs (every 3 months if stable), PFTs every 3–6 months and yearly HRCT, begin HCC surveillance with ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months, refer to ILD and hepatology transplant teams early, avoid androgens given her liver disease, counsel strongly on tobacco/alcohol avoidance, and offer family genetic counselling and testing.
