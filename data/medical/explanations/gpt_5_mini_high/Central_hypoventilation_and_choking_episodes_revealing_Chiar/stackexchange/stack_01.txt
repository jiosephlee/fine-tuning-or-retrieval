Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Central hypoventilation and choking episodes revealing Chiari malformation type I"

### [Morning Report] In an adult with recurrent choking affecting both solids and liquids, normal laryngoscopy and endoscopy, and chronic occipital headaches, what clinical clue should prompt neuroimaging of the posterior fossa, and which imaging modality is preferred?

Short answer
The red flag is neurogenic (bulbar) dysphagia — choking on solids and liquids equally with repeatedly normal laryngoscopy/endoscopy — particularly when it co‑exists with occipital/vertex headache, nocturnal choking or unexplained oxygen desaturations. That combination should prompt imaging of the cranio‑cervical junction. The preferred test is MRI of the brain and cervical spine (midline sagittal T1/T2 with axial cuts at the foramen magnum and, if available, cine phase‑contrast CSF flow studies) rather than non‑contrast CT.

Why
- Dysphagia that affects all consistencies points to a neurogenic (brainstem/bulbar) cause rather than a mechanical luminal obstruction; repeated normal ENT and endoscopic studies therefore raise suspicion for central pathology.  
- Chronic occipital headaches—classically Valsalva‑provoked in Chiari but sometimes atypical as in this case—and unexplained nocturnal desaturations or central apnoea strongly increase the pretest probability of posterior‑fossa compression.  
- MRI is the diagnostic standard for Chiari I: midline sagittal MRI measures tonsillar descent (≥5 mm defines CMI) and visualizes CSF spaces, brainstem compression and syringomyelia. In this report CT retrospectively showed a crowded foramen magnum but MRI confirmed an 11 mm tonsillar descent and explained the patient’s bulbar choking and central hypoventilation features.

Practical tip
Order a dedicated cranio‑cervical MRI (brain + cervical spine) with attention to sagittal T1/T2 and cine flow when available; if sleep‑related respiratory symptoms are present, arrange full polysomnography with capnography and neurosurgical consultation if MRI shows significant tonsillar descent or CSF obstruction.
