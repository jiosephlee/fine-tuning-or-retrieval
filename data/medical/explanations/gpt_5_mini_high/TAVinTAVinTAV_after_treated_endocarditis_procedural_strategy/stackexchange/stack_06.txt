Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Define echocardiographic criteria for prosthetic aortic valve stenosis and for prosthesis–patient mismatch after TAVR (including thresholds for mean gradient, AVA/indexed AVA, and Doppler velocity index), and interpret the following values from this case at two time points: preprocedure mean gradient 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm², DVI 0.24; immediate postprocedure AVA 0.9 cm², indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m², DVI 0.24.

Clinical answer (clinical pearl)

Principles and thresholds
- The same Doppler parameters used for native aortic stenosis are applied to prosthetic valves, but interpretation must account for prosthesis geometry, flow dependence and measurement artefact. Use a combination of mean transvalvular gradient, continuity-equation AVA (and indexed AVA), Doppler velocity index (DVI), and imaging of leaflet motion/structure (TEE/fluoroscopy/CT).
- Common echocardiographic thresholds used in practice:
  - Severe prosthetic aortic stenosis: mean gradient ≥40 mm Hg (peak velocity ≥4.0 m/s), AVA ≤1.0 cm², and/or DVI ≤0.25. Moderate stenosis: mean gradient ~20–39 mm Hg, DVI ~0.25–0.35.
  - Prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM) is defined by indexed effective orifice area (EOA or AVA indexed to BSA). Conventional cutoffs: severe PPM ≤0.65 cm²/m², moderate PPM 0.65–0.85 cm²/m² (adjust lower for obesity: severe ≤0.55 cm²/m², moderate 0.55–0.70 cm²/m²). Some TAVR literature uses a severe cutoff ≈0.6 cm²/m²; either way an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² is clearly in the severe range.
- Practical points: mean gradient is flow‑dependent; continuity-derived AVA relies on accurate LVOT measurement (often difficult with stented valves). DVI (LVOT VTI ÷ aortic VTI) avoids LVOT area and is particularly useful in prosthetic valves—DVI ≤0.25 is strongly suggestive of severe obstruction/very small orifice.

Interpretation in this case
- Preprocedure (presented values): mean gradient 49 mm Hg, AVA 0.5 cm², DVI 0.24.
  - Interpretation: concordant evidence of severe prosthetic valve stenosis. Mean gradient >40 mm Hg, AVA markedly reduced (0.5 cm²) and DVI at the severe threshold (≤0.25) all point to severe obstruction of the prior prosthesis.
- Immediate postprocedure (presented values): AVA 0.9 cm², indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m², DVI 0.24 (the report also notes an immediate mean gradient of 67 mm Hg).
  - Interpretation: although the non‑indexed AVA rose to 0.9 cm², the indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m² places this unequivocally in the severe PPM range. The DVI remained ≤0.25 and the mean gradient was very high (67 mm Hg), consistent with severe residual obstruction from a small effective orifice (patient‑prosthesis mismatch and/or underexpansion of the new valve within layered frames). In other words: the implanted valve was functioning with a small effective orifice for this patient’s body size (severe PPM), producing high gradients immediately after deployment.

Why the apparent discrepancy (AVA up but gradients higher)?
- Continuity‑equation AVA can change with altered flow and is sensitive to LVOT measurement errors (and measurement variability immediately post‑procedure). DVI and gradients reflect the instantaneous hemodynamic burden and are less dependent on LVOT area. Here DVI stayed low and gradients remained very high, so the haemodynamic reality was persistent severe obstruction despite a higher calculated AVA.
- The case explains the clinical course: immediate high gradients were attributed to PPM/underexpansion in the triple‑stented anatomy; post‑dilatation and mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut frame (and settling of the new valve) led to improved gradients and AVA at 1 month (mean gradient 27 mm Hg, AVA 1.1 cm²).

Take‑away teaching points
- Always integrate mean gradient, AVA (and indexed AVA), and DVI when assessing prosthetic valves; in stented valves give particular weight to DVI and to comparison with prior baseline studies.
- Indexed EOA is the metric that defines PPM; an iAVA of 0.4 cm²/m² is severe PPM.
- Immediate post‑TAVR haemodynamics may overestimate obstruction; in valve‑in‑valve scenarios early high gradients may improve after post‑dilatation or as frames expand — but severe PPM identified by low iEOA predicts a substantial haemodynamic burden and should be anticipated at planning.
