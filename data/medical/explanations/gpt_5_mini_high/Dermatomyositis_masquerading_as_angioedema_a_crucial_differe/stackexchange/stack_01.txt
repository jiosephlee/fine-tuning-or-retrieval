Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Morning Report] In an adult with three weeks of progressive, non-tender neck swelling, subtle periungual capillary changes, and new proximal hip-flexor weakness but no urticaria or exposure to ACE inhibitors, which single laboratory test would you order first to redirect the evaluation from angioedema toward an inflammatory myopathy, and what result would you expect if dermatomyositis is the cause?

Order a serum creatine kinase (CK) level first. 

Why: the combination of new proximal hip‑flexor weakness and periungual capillary changes points to an inflammatory myopathy rather than histaminergic or bradykinin‑mediated angioedema. CK is the single most accessible and sensitive screening test for active muscle injury; dermatomyositis causes complement‑mediated microvascular injury and myofibre necrosis with release of intracellular enzymes (CK, aldolase, AST/ALT, LDH).

What result to expect if dermatomyositis is the cause: a marked CK elevation (commonly >1,000 U/L in active myositis). In the published case the CK was 16,184 U/L, which promptly redirected the team from an angioedema workup to myositis testing (anti‑SAE1 antibody, MRI, muscle biopsy) and led to timely immunosuppression. 

Caveat: CK can be normal in amyopathic dermatomyositis, so if clinical suspicion remains despite a normal CK, proceed to myositis‑specific antibodies and muscle imaging/biopsy.
