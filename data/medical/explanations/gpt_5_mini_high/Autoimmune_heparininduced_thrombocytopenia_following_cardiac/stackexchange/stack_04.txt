Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia following cardiac surgery"

### [Morning Report] How do you interpret a serotonin-release assay that shows strong serotonin release at 0, 0.1, and 0.3 U/mL heparin, absent release at 100 U/mL heparin, and only partial inhibition with FcγRIIa blockade (IV.3), and what diagnosis does this profile support?

Bottom line: this SRA pattern—very strong serotonin release in the absence of heparin (0 U/mL) and at low heparin (0.1 and 0.3 U/mL), with loss of activation at very high heparin (100 U/mL), and only partial inhibition by the FcγRIIa‑blocking monoclonal antibody IV.3—is diagnostic of autoimmune HIT (aHIT) caused by heparin‑independent, platelet‑activating anti‑PF4 antibodies.  

Why: the classic SRA for ordinary (heparin‑dependent) HIT shows platelet activation only at low therapeutic heparin concentrations (0.1–0.3 U/mL), little/no activation at 0 U/mL, and suppression at very high heparin (100 U/mL). In contrast, aHIT antibodies activate platelets without added heparin (strong release at 0 U/mL), which is exactly what was observed here (≈96% release at 0 U/mL; 97–99% at 0.1–0.3 U/mL; RR <20%). The absence of release at 100 U/mL is still consistent with an anti‑PF4 mechanism (excess heparin disrupts pathogenic complexes).  

The IV.3 result is important: IV.3 normally abolishes platelet activation in HIT by blocking FcγRIIa; incomplete inhibition (76% release persisting with IV.3 in this case) indicates unusually potent/avidity‑rich antibodies (a novel serologic phenotype) that only partially resist FcγRIIa blockade. Clinically this correlates with a more aggressive, treatment‑resistant form of HIT — which explains the patient’s very low nadir platelets, thrombosis (stroke), and only modest response to high‑dose IVIG in the case.  

Clinical implications: treat as aHIT—immediately stop all heparin, initiate a non‑heparin anticoagulant (argatroban, bivalirudin, or off‑label fondaparinux as used here), and consider high‑dose IVIG to interrupt Fc‑mediated platelet activation. Expect a potentially prolonged, severe course and monitor platelets and markers of coagulation (fibrinogen, D‑dimer); the patient in this report required argatroban + high‑dose IVIG and later fondaparinux with gradual recovery.
