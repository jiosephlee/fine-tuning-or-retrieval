Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Pathophysiology] Which myositis-specific autoantibodies are most strongly associated with IMNM, what common drug exposure is linked to one of them, and how does a seronegative profile influence diagnostic strategy?

Short answer
- Anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP are the two myositis‑specific autoantibodies most strongly linked to immune‑mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM).  
- Statin exposure is classically associated with anti‑HMGCR–positive IMNM.  
- A seronegative result (≈30% of IMNM cases) does not exclude IMNM — it shifts the diagnostic emphasis onto the clinical picture, imaging and, above all, muscle biopsy, and it mandates a careful search for alternative triggers (including drugs) and early clinicopathologic decision‑making rather than waiting for a positive antibody test.

Why — brief pathophysiology and application to the case
- Anti‑HMGCR antibodies target HMG‑CoA reductase, an antigen that becomes upregulated in regenerating myofibres (often after statin exposure), and anti‑HMGCR disease is therefore strongly linked to prior statin use and tends to persist despite statin withdrawal, requiring immunotherapy. Anti‑SRP is another specific marker of IMNM and is often associated with a more fulminant phenotype (very high CK, refractory weakness, and more frequent cardiac involvement).  
- The case illustrates these principles: the authors correctly tested for anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP (both negative), and the muscle biopsy provided the diagnostic proof — scattered necrotic/regenerating fibres, prominent CD68+ macrophage myophagocytosis and membrane attack complex deposition — all classic for IMNM. The patient’s clinical course (persistent weakness and high CK despite stopping semaglutide, then improvement with corticosteroids + IVIG and avoidance of the drug) highlights that a drug other than a statin (here, semaglutide is a suspected trigger) can be implicated even when standard IMNM antibodies are absent.

Practical diagnostic strategy when seronegative IMNM is suspected
- Do not dismiss IMNM if the myositis panel is negative. Up to ~1/3 of IMNM patients are seronegative.  
- Take a focused medication/exposure history (statins, immune checkpoint inhibitors, other recent drugs — and now consider newer agents such as GLP‑1 RAs if temporally related). Stop any plausible offending agent.  
- Use MRI to localize inflamed muscle for biopsy. Proceed to early muscle biopsy when antibody tests are negative or clinical severity is high — histology is the decisive test for necrotizing myopathy. Look specifically for necrotic/regenerating fibres with macrophage‑predominant inflammation and complement (MAC) deposition.  
- Consider repeat or extended serologic testing if clinical suspicion persists, and perform standard adjuncts (CK, EMG as needed, malignancy screen where appropriate).  
- Management decisions should be guided by severity and biopsy: drug‑induced/seronegative cases may respond to withdrawal of the trigger plus a shorter course of immunotherapy (as in this patient), whereas antibody‑positive IMNM (especially anti‑SRP) often requires prolonged, multi‑agent immunosuppression.

Clinical pearl
- Anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP are the “red flags” for IMNM and statins are the classic trigger for anti‑HMGCR disease — but a negative antibody panel does not rule out IMNM. In seronegative patients, rely on history, MRI and muscle biopsy and treat promptly when the clinicopathologic picture supports immune‑mediated necrotizing myopathy.
