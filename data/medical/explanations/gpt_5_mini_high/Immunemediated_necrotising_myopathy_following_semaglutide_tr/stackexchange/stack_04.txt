Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Morning Report] In a patient presenting with progressive proximal weakness, dysphagia, and CK around 4000 U/L four months after a single semaglutide dose, outline an initial diagnostic workup (laboratory tests, imaging, and procedures) to confirm IMNM and exclude mimics.

Short answer
In this clinical setting the diagnostic work-up has four simultaneous goals: (1) document a myopathic process and the IMNM phenotype, (2) obtain IMNM‑specific serology, (3) assess severity (bulbar/respiratory/cardiac) so therapy can’t be delayed, and (4) exclude common mimics (toxic/rhabdomyolysis, endocrine, infectious, paraneoplastic, neuromuscular‑junction disease). The essential initial tests are summarized below and illustrated by what was done in the case (MRI showing proximal muscle edema; negative myositis panel; muscle biopsy with necrosis, CD68+ macrophages and membrane attack complex deposition; cardiac MRI/echo evaluation).

Immediate (same day) — safety and baseline
- Clinical: bedside respiratory testing (FVC, maximal inspiratory pressure/negative inspiratory force) and urgent speech‑language pathology assessment for dysphagia/aspiration risk. (Dysphagia here prompted prompt evaluation and therapy.)
- Basic labs: CK (serial), aldolase, AST/ALT, LDH, CBC, CMP (creatinine, electrolytes, Ca/PO4/Mg) and urinalysis/urine myoglobin to assess for ongoing myoglobinuria and renal risk.
- Cardiac screen: ECG, high‑sensitivity troponin and BNP (the case had a mild troponin rise and very high pro‑BNP prompting echo and cardiac MRI).
- Inflammatory markers: ESR, CRP.
- Thyroid function (TSH) and cortisol if clinically indicated (hypothyroidism can mimic myopathy). (TSH was normal in the case.)

Focused diagnostic testing (24–72 hours)
- Myositis‑specific serologies: anti‑HMGCR and anti‑SRP (these are most closely linked to IMNM), plus an extended myositis panel and ANA/ENA. Interpretation point: up to ~30% of IMNM patients are seronegative — a negative panel does not exclude IMNM (this patient was seronegative).
- Viral/infectious screening: HIV, hepatitis B and C (both potential triggers and important to know before immunosuppression). If prolonged immunosuppression planned, add IGRA for latent TB and hepatitis B surface antigen/core testing.
- Toxic/medication review: careful medication and supplement history (statins, red‑yeast rice, other myotoxic agents). The patient denied statin use in this report.

Imaging and neurophysiology
- MRI of affected muscles with fluid‑sensitive sequences (STIR/T2): detects symmetric proximal muscle edema and helps choose a biopsy site (the case had thigh MRI showing proximal vastus lateralis edema).
- Electromyography/nerve conduction studies (EMG/NCS): demonstrates a myopathic, often “irritable” pattern with fibrillation potentials; can help exclude motor neuron or neuromuscular‑junction disorders (if MG/LEMS suspected perform repetitive stimulation/single‑fiber EMG and AChR/MuSK antibodies).
- Chest imaging and cancer search: high‑resolution chest CT to look for ILD (if relevant) and consider CT chest/abdomen/pelvis or PET‑CT for occult malignancy in older patients or when paraneoplastic disease is a concern. (The case had HRCT chest and abdominal imaging without malignancy.)
- Cardiac imaging: transthoracic echo ± cardiac MRI when troponin/BNP are abnormal or symptoms/signs suggest myocarditis/cardiomyopathy (cardiac MRI in this patient showed myocardial oedema without late gadolinium enhancement).

Definitive diagnostic procedure
- Muscle biopsy (gold standard): perform an open or targeted percutaneous biopsy of a moderately affected muscle (use MRI to avoid end‑stage fatty replaced muscle). Pathology in IMNM classically shows scattered necrotic and regenerating fibres, macrophage‑predominant myophagocytosis, relative paucity of lymphocytes, and complement (C5b‑9/MAC) deposition on sarcolemma — exactly the pattern seen in this case. Arrange frozen tissue for histochemistry and immunostains (CD68, CD3, MHC‑I, MAC).

Differential considerations to exclude and how to test them
- Acute toxic rhabdomyolysis (drug/toxin): usually abrupt, very high CK that falls quickly with hydration and drug withdrawal; test timeline and urine myoglobin, check renal function.
- Statin‑associated IMNM: medication history + anti‑HMGCR serology.
- Inclusion body myositis: older men, finger flexor/ankle dorsiflexor weakness, insidious course, characteristic biopsy (rimmed vacuoles); CK often only mildly elevated.
- Endocrine or metabolic myopathies: TSH, cortisol, electrolytes, targeted metabolic testing if suggested.
- Infectious myositis: fever, focal findings, blood cultures, targeted serologies.
- Neuromuscular junction disorders (e.g., myasthenia gravis): clinical fatigability, normal CK, AChR/MuSK antibodies and electrodiagnostics.

Practical points and next steps
- Because IMNM can be seronegative, muscle MRI + biopsy are crucial when suspicion is high despite negative antibodies — the case’s biopsy was decisive.
- In patients with severe weakness, bulbar dysfunction or rapidly rising CK, do not wait for biopsy to treat: start high‑dose steroids and consider IVIG (as in this patient), after rapidly completing infection/vaccine screening relevant to immunosuppression.
- Always stop suspected offending agents (semaglutide in this report) and document medication/supplement exposure.
- Coordinate care with neurology, rheumatology, pathology, cardiology and speech/swallow therapy.

Bottom line (clinical pearl)
In older patients with subacute progressive proximal weakness, prominent CK elevation and bulbar signs after medication exposure, order urgent safety labs (CK trend, renal, electrolytes), cardiac screening (troponin, echo/CMR if abnormal), myositis serologies (anti‑HMGCR, anti‑SRP), muscle MRI to localize disease, and obtain a targeted muscle biopsy — because antibody negativity does not exclude IMNM and biopsy findings (necrosis with macrophages and MAC deposition) are diagnostic, as they were in this case.
