Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy following semaglutide treatment: a contributing factor?"

### [Differential Diagnosis] After a single exposure to a potentially myotoxic drug, what temporal patterns in symptom progression and creatine kinase (CK) values help distinguish immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) from self-limited toxic rhabdomyolysis?

Short answer: timing and trajectory. Toxic (direct) rhabdomyolysis usually produces an acute, large CK rise within hours–days of exposure that peaks within 24–72 hours and then falls predictably after withdrawal and supportive care (CK half‑life ≈36 hours), with clinical recovery over days–weeks. By contrast, immune‑mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) presents subacutely to chronically — progressive proximal weakness and bulbar symptoms over weeks–months after the trigger, with CK that is persistently elevated or progressively rising despite drug cessation and supportive care, and that only improves with immunotherapy.

Why this distinction matters (pathophysiology + practical clues)
- Toxic rhabdomyolysis = direct myocyte injury → immediate cell lysis and a rapid CK storm that abates when the offending exposure stops and muscle injury ceases. Clinically you expect myalgias, dark urine/AKI, a sharp early CK peak and clear downward kinetics over days–weeks.
- IMNM = immune‑mediated myofibre necrosis (macrophage predominant, complement deposition) → a smouldering or progressive process that continues after drug withdrawal. Patients develop progressive symmetric proximal weakness, often bulbar involvement, and CK that fails to fall (or rises) over weeks–months unless immunosuppression is given.

How the case illustrates the point
- This patient had only a modest CK (337 U/L) at the time of her index hospitalization immediately after a single semaglutide dose, then developed progressive weakness and a 12‑fold rise in CK to 3992 U/L over the next 4 months. That delayed, progressive rise in CK and worsening weakness despite stopping the drug is typical for IMNM and not for self‑limited toxic rhabdomyolysis (the tirzepatide report cited in the paper had an acute CK >57,000 with rapid improvement after drug withdrawal). Muscle biopsy in this patient showed necrotic/regenerating fibres, macrophages and membrane attack complex deposition — the histologic signature of IMNM — and her CK and weakness improved only after corticosteroids + IVIG.

Practical approach (clinical pearl)
- If CK and symptoms peak acutely and decline within days–a few weeks after stopping the drug → think toxic rhabdomyolysis; manage supportively.
- If weakness is progressive and CK remains elevated or rises beyond 2–4 weeks after exposure, or if there are bulbar signs or MRI/biopsy features of inflammatory necrosis → pursue myositis serologies, MRI and muscle biopsy and treat as IMNM (early immunosuppression/IVIG as indicated).
