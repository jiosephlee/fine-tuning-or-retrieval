Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] What is the proposed pathophysiologic link between CNS inflammation in acute neuro-Behçet’s disease and the development of SIADH, and which cytokine is most implicated in stimulating ADH release?

Answer (brief): Acute neuro‑Behçet’s disease can provoke SIADH by CNS inflammation–driven, non‑osmotic stimulation of antidiuretic hormone release. The cytokine most implicated in this process is interleukin‑6 (IL‑6).

Why — pathophysiologic reasoning:
- Neuro‑BD produces intrathecal and systemic inflammation (in this case: CSF lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein, and a marked systemic inflammatory response with CRP 180 mg/L). Even when MRI is normal, microscopic or functional inflammation of hypothalamic/brainstem regions (or activation of circumventricular organs) can alter ADH regulation.
- Proinflammatory cytokines—chiefly IL‑6—are known to rise in neuroinflammatory states and to stimulate non‑osmotic ADH release from magnocellular neurons and the posterior pituitary (and/or act via circumventricular organs that lack a blood–brain barrier). The net effect is inappropriate water retention: hypotonic hyponatraemia with low serum osmolality and inappropriately concentrated urine.
- The clinical data in this case support that mechanism: serum osmolality 267 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality 900 mOsm/kg, urine Na 88 mmol/L, clinically euvolemic, normal TSH and cortisol, and an elevated ADH (22.4 pg/mL) — all diagnostic of SIADH. IL‑6 itself was not measured here, but the very high CRP and the known biology of BD make an IL‑6–mediated pathway plausible.
- Therapeutic corroboration: treatment directed at inflammation (colchicine, which suppresses IL‑6 and other cytokines) coincided with clinical improvement, normalization of sodium, and a fall in ADH to 1.8 pg/mL — consistent with cytokine‑driven, reversible ADH excess.

Clinical pearl: In patients with BD and new hyponatraemia, think SIADH from neuroinflammation (often IL‑6 mediated). Manage acutely with standard SIADH measures (fluid restriction, avoid rapid correction) and treat the underlying CNS inflammation — this can reverse the inappropriate ADH secretion.
