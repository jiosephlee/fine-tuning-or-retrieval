Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Pancreatopleural fistula in childhood"

### [Clinical Pearls] In a child with recurrent, large, unilateral, dark red pleural effusions and minimal abdominal pain, which pleural fluid assays are most diagnostic of a pancreatopleural fistula, and what amylase threshold is considered pathognomonic?

Short answer
- The most diagnostic pleural-fluid assays are pleural fluid amylase (with simultaneous serum amylase) and pleural fluid lipase; when available, amylase isoenzyme (pancreatic vs salivary) typing is helpful.  
- A pleural-fluid amylase ≥1,000 U/L is classically considered pathognomonic for a pancreatopleural fistula (PPF).

Why this matters (clinical reasoning and pathophysiology)
- A PPF results from pancreatic-duct disruption or pseudocyst rupture with retroperitoneal tracking of enzyme-rich fluid into the mediastinum and pleural space. The pleural space is therefore exposed to very high concentrations of pancreatic enzymes, producing a bloody, high‑protein exudate with markedly elevated amylase (and often lipase).  
- Other causes (Boerhaave’s/oesophageal rupture, some malignancies) can also raise pleural amylase, but they usually produce salivary‑type amylase; detection of pancreatic‑type amylase or concordant high pleural lipase strongly favors pancreatic origin.

How to use the tests in practice (diagnostic standard)
- Always send pleural fluid amylase and lipase (and draw serum levels at the same time). A pleural-fluid amylase ≥1,000 U/L is highly specific for PPF; very high values (as in this case — pleural amylase 6,118 U/L and lipase 7,211 U/L) should trigger pancreatic imaging.  
- If available, request amylase isoenzyme typing to distinguish pancreatic from salivary sources.  
- Once enzymes suggest PPF, obtain cross‑sectional imaging: MRCP (sensitivity ~70–80%) to define the fistula and ductal anatomy; CT can show collections but visualizes the tract less reliably; ERCP remains the definitive tool for visualization and therapeutic planning.

Case tie‑in
- In the reported child the recurrent, dark‑red unilateral effusions led to measurement of pleural amylase (6,118 U/L) and lipase (7,211 U/L). Those very high pancreatic enzyme levels, together with MRCP demonstrating a tract from the pancreas to the pleural space, confirmed the diagnosis and guided conservative therapy.

Clinical pearl
- In any child with recurrent, large, unilateral, blood‑stained pleural effusions and little abdominal pain, obtain pleural-fluid amylase/lipase (and isoenzyme typing if possible); a pleural amylase ≥1,000 U/L is essentially diagnostic of pancreatopleural fistula and should prompt MRCP/ERCP for confirmation and management planning.
