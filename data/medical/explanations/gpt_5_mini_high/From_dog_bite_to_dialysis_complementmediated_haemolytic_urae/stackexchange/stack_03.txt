Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "From dog bite to dialysis: complement-mediated haemolytic uraemic syndrome"

### [Morning Report] An adult recovering from Capnocytophaga canimorsus bacteremia develops microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and dialysis-dependent AKI. How would you triage the diagnosis using the PLASMIC score and ADAMTS13 activity (what threshold rules in TTP), what immediate therapy should begin while results are pending, and how does management change once TTP is excluded?

Short answer
- Triage with the PLASMIC score to estimate pretest probability of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency; if the score is high (6–7) treat as TTP immediately. ADAMTS13 activity <10% (severe deficiency) confirms immune or congenital TTP. While ADAMTS13 is pending, start empiric TTP therapy if clinical suspicion is moderate–high: urgent plasma exchange (PEX) plus corticosteroids (and caplacizumab where available). Once ADAMTS13 is not severely deficient (eg, 43.5% in this case) stop TTP-directed therapy and shift management to the alternative TMA diagnosis — in a patient like this with dialysis‑dependent AKI and biopsy and microbiology supporting CM‑HUS, begin complement blockade (eculizumab) after ensuring infection control and administering meningococcal vaccination and antibiotic prophylaxis.

Teaching points and clinical reasoning

1) Use PLASMIC to triage, and draw ADAMTS13 before plasma is given
- The PLASMIC score (Platelet count <30×10^9/L; evidence of Hemolysis; Absence of active cancer; No Solid‑organ/stem‑cell transplant; MCV <90 fL; INR <1.5; Creatinine <2.0 mg/dL) yields 0–7 points. Scores 6–7 = high pretest probability → treat as TTP; 0–4 = low pretest probability → look for alternate causes; 5 = intermediate → use clinical judgment.
- Always obtain an ADAMTS13 sample before starting PEX if feasible because PEX rapidly lowers measurable activity and can remove inhibitors.
- In the reported case the PLASMIC score was 4/7 (low pretest probability) and ADAMTS13 activity returned at 43.5%, effectively excluding TTP.

2) ADAMTS13 threshold to rule in/out TTP
- Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (<10% activity) is the diagnostic threshold for TTP (usually immune‑mediated). Values well above that make TTP unlikely and prompt evaluation for other TMAs (STEC‑HUS, complement‑mediated HUS, sepsis/DIC‑associated TMA, drug‑ or malignancy‑related TMA).

3) Immediate empiric therapy while results are pending
- If PLASMIC is high or clinical suspicion for TTP is strong (neurologic findings, severe thrombocytopenia, hemolysis) do not wait for ADAMTS13: start daily therapeutic plasma exchange with FFP replacement immediately (PEX is lifesaving in TTP), give high‑dose corticosteroids (eg, prednisone 1 mg/kg PO or IV methylprednisolone), and consider caplacizumab if available.
- Avoid routine platelet transfusions in suspected TTP unless there is life‑threatening bleeding or an invasive procedure is required.
- If PLASMIC is low and the presentation fits sepsis/DIC or infection‑associated TMA, prioritize treatment of the infection and supportive care; PEX may be used as a temporizing measure in severe cases but is not disease‑directed for CM‑HUS.

4) Change in management once TTP is excluded
- If ADAMTS13 activity is >10% (as here), stop TTP‑directed therapies (daily PEX and caplacizumab) directed solely at ADAMTS13 deficiency and redirect the workup and treatment.
- For suspected complement‑mediated HUS (CM‑HUS) with ongoing hemolysis and severe AKI: treat the trigger (aggressive antibiotics for bacteremia), and institute complement blockade with eculizumab promptly for severe disease. Do not wait for extended genetic testing to start therapy if clinical severity warrants it.
- Because terminal complement blockade increases risk for Neisseria and other encapsulated organisms, give meningococcal vaccination (MenACWY and MenB) ideally ≥2 weeks before eculizumab. If urgent treatment is required, vaccinate at time of first dose and give prophylactic antibiotics until 2 weeks after vaccination (the team in this report used Apocillin). In this case eculizumab was started after blood cultures became negative and immediately after initial meningococcal vaccination; PEX had been used as a bridge.
- Recognize the role of PEX in CM‑HUS is limited and typically transient; sustained hematologic and renal recovery in CM‑HUS depends on complement blockade (as illustrated in the case where only eculizumab produced durable remission).

Bottom line (clinical pearl)
- Draw ADAMTS13 before plasma when possible. Use the PLASMIC score to decide urgency: high score → immediate PEX ± steroids ± caplacizumab; low score → search for other TMAs. ADAMTS13 <10% = TTP; values well above that (eg 43.5% in this patient) effectively exclude TTP and should prompt transition off TTP therapy and toward targeted treatment (in this case, eculizumab after ensuring infection control and appropriate vaccination/prophylaxis).
