Title: Blog about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

# Maximizing the Orifice: Strategies to Overcome Patient-Prosthesis Mismatch in Triple-Stented Valves

How many "Russian nesting dolls" can you fit inside an aortic valve before the flow just... stops? 

In the era of expanding TAVI indications and improving longevity, we are increasingly moving away from "one and done" interventions toward a lifetime of structural valve management. While TAV-in-TAV procedures are becoming a staple of the interventional toolkit, we are now entering the frontier of TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV. This case presents a fascinating—and technically daunting—challenge: how do you achieve adequate hemodynamics when you have three overlapping frames competing for the same tiny piece of real estate?

### The Clinical Scenario: A Triple-Stented Anatomy
The patient in this case is a man in his late 70s with a high surgical risk (EuroSCORE II of 27.36%). His journey began in 2014 with a Lotus valve. By 2022, that valve had degenerated, leading to a second intervention with an Evolut prosthesis. The complication? He developed prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). After successful antimicrobial treatment, he returned in 2024 with a devastating hemodynamic profile: a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg and a calculated aortic valve area (AVA) of just 0.5 cm².

At 0.5 cm², the valve isn't just "stenotic"—it’s functionally obstructed. To save the patient, the team had to plan a third transcatheter implantation (Acurate neo2). The primary hurdle wasn't just getting the third valve in; it was ensuring that the third valve didn't turn the aortic root into a solid block of metal and tissue.

### The "Orifice Crisis" and the Strategy for Success
The biggest enemy in serial TAVI is Patient-Prosthesis Mismatch (PPM). Every time you add a frame, you lose effective orifice area. In this case, the team utilized three specific strategies to fight back against the shrinking orifice:

**1. Precision Planning via MSCT**
You cannot "wing" a triple-stented anatomy. The team used multislice CT (MSCT) to map the interaction between the three frames. They specifically looked at coronary ostial height and the relationship between the anticipated "neo-skirt" of the new valve and the coronary arteries. This allowed them to determine the exact implantation depth needed to preserve coronary flow without the need for extra-procedural protection.

**2. High Implantation Depth to Prevent Leaflet Folding**
One of the cleverest maneuvers in this case was the deliberate high implantation of the third valve. By seating the Acurate neo2 at the level of the previous Evolut valve leaflets, the team was able to "fix" the pre-existing leaflets against the stent frame. This is a crucial distinction: by pinning those leaflets back, they prevented them from folding inward into the lumen of the new valve, which would have further constricted the already tiny AVA.

**3. Commissural Alignment and "Mechanical Expansion"**
The team prioritized commissural alignment to ensure that the flow dynamics remained as central as possible and to preserve future coronary access. Furthermore, they utilized high-pressure balloon post-dilation. While the goal wasn't necessarily to fracture the previous frames, the high-pressure expansion likely provided enough radial force to "crack" the surrounding structures, allowing the new valve to expand more fully than it would have in a static environment.

### The Result
The results speak for themselves. Post-procedure, the AVA jumped from a dismal 0.5 cm² to 1.1 cm² at one month, eventually stabilizing at 1.1 cm² at one year. The patient remained asymptomatic, showing that with meticulous planning and a strategy-driven approach, we can successfully navigate even the most complex "nesting doll" anatomies.

### Clinical Pearls & Take-Home Messages
*   **CT is Non-Negotiable:** For TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, pre-procedural MSCT is the gold standard for mapping frame-to-frame interactions and assessing coronary clearance.
*   **Think About Leaflet Geometry:** High implantation depth isn't just about staying away from the coronaries; it's about anchoring the previous valve's leaflets to prevent inward folding and further EOA loss.
*   **The Power of Post-Dilation:** In triple-stented cases, high-pressure balloon post-dilation is a critical tool to overcome the cumulative radial resistance of multiple overlapping frames.
*   **Alignment Matters:** Maintaining commissural alignment across multiple generations of valves is essential for optimizing flow and preserving the "last remaining" coronary access.
*   **Lifetime Management:** We must start thinking about the "redo" at the time of the first TAVI, especially in patients with high surgical risk and significant coronary disease.
