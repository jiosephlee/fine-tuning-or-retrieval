Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Clinical Pearls] Before performing a transcatheter reintervention in a patient with a history of PVE, what specific clinical and imaging criteria must be met to confirm that the infection is considered 'resolved'?

Before performing a transcatheter reintervention in a patient with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), it is imperative to confirm that the infection is clinically, microbiologically, and radiologically resolved to minimize the risk of embolic complications and procedure-related failure. 

The standard for confirming resolution involves a triad of criteria:

1.  **Clinical and Pharmacological Status:** The patient must have completed a full course of targeted antimicrobial therapy. Furthermore, the absence of a requirement for ongoing long-term suppressive antibiotic therapy is a key indicator that the infection is no longer active.
2.  **Microbiological Evidence:** Repeated blood cultures must be negative, providing objective evidence that the pathogen (in this case, *Enterococcus faecalis*) has been cleared from the bloodstream.
3.  **Imaging Confirmation:** Definitive imaging is required to rule out persistent infectious focus. In this case, both transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice CT (MSCT) were utilized to confirm the absence of active endocarditis, specifically ensuring there were no remaining vegetations or paravalvular complications.

Establishing these criteria is a critical safety step in the multidisciplinary decision-making process. Once the infection is confirmed as resolved, the clinical focus shifts from infectious management to the management of the primary mechanical pathology—in this patient, the severe prosthetic valve dysfunction and structural degeneration resulting from both the prior infection and progressive wear. This transition allows for a strategy-driven reintervention, such as the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV, guided by anatomical feasibility and hemodynamic optimization.
