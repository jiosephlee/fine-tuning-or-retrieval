Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome"

### [Pathophysiology] Explain the hemodynamic concept of 'patient-prosthesis mismatch' (PPM). In this case, how was it quantified, and what was the specific clinical implication of the findings?

Patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM) is a hemodynamic phenomenon that occurs when the effective orifice area (EOA) of a prosthetic heart valve is insufficient to meet the flow requirements of the patient's cardiac output, leading to an elevated transvalvular pressure gradient despite the successful implantation of a functional prosthesis. It is essentially a discrepancy between the "demand" of the patient’s systemic circulation and the "supply" of the valve’s flow capacity. In the context of serial transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAV-in-TAV), PPM is a significant concern because each successive valve frame is nested within the previous one, progressively narrowing the available lumen and reducing the residual EOA.

In this case, PPM was quantified immediately following the third transcatheter intervention (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV). While the procedure was technically successful—achieving a grade 0 aortic regurgitation and mobile leaflets—the hemodynamics showed an aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.9 cm², an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m², and a Doppler velocity index (DVI) of 0.24. A DVI of less than 0.25 is a standard clinical threshold used to identify severe PPM, indicating that the triple-stented anatomy significantly restricted flow.

The specific clinical implication of these findings was the immediate necessity for a strategy to optimize the residual EOA to avoid chronic heart failure or persistent symptoms. The clinical team addressed this through a "strategy-driven" approach: they utilized high implantation of the third valve (Acurate neo2) to prevent the inward folding of the Evolut leaflets, performed aggressive post-dilation to radially expand the existing frames, and prioritized commissural alignment. These maneuvers successfully mitigated the PPM over time; by one month, the mean gradient decreased from 67 mm Hg to 27 mm Hg, and the AVA increased to 1.1 cm², leading to a sustained functional benefit and clinical stability at the one-year follow-up.
