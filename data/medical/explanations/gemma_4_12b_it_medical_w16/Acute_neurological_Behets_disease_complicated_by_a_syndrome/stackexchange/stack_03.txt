Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] The patient exhibited persistent hiccups and elevated creatine kinase (CK) levels. What clinical condition do these specific findings suggest in the setting of profound hyponatraemia?

In the setting of profound hyponatremia, persistent hiccups and elevated creatine kinase (CK) levels are clinical markers of severe electrolyte disturbance and its systemic complications. Specifically, these findings suggest that the patient is experiencing the secondary effects of severe hyponatremia resulting from the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH).

Persistent hiccups are a recognized, though often underappreciated, clinical clue to severe hyponatremia, particularly when the hyponatremia is secondary to central nervous system (CNS) inflammation, such as meningitis or, as seen in this case, acute neurological Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD). The mechanism involves the profound osmotic shift and inflammatory milieu affecting the neurological pathways regulating the respiratory and diaphragmatic centers.

The elevated CK levels in this patient indicate rhabdomyolysis. While often associated with direct muscle trauma or strenuous exercise, rhabdomyolysis is a documented but frequently overlooked complication of profound hyponatremia. In this clinical context, the severe sodium depletion leads to metabolic and muscular dysfunction, manifesting as muscle breakdown.

In this specific case, the inflammatory process of acute neuro-BD likely triggered SIADH—potentially through the production of proinflammatory cytokines like interleukin-6 (IL-6)—leading to the profound hyponatremia (124 mmol/L) that manifested as both hiccups and rhabdomyolysis. Recognizing these "non-specific" symptoms as potential indicators of severe electrolyte imbalance is critical for timely intervention and the prevention of further systemic complications.
