Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Pathophysiology] While corticosteroids are typically the mainstay of treatment for acute neuro-BD, this patient responded to colchicine monotherapy. What is the pharmacological mechanism of colchicine that may explain its efficacy in suppressing the inflammatory response in this case?

While corticosteroids remain the standard of care for acute neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD), this patient's favorable response to colchicine monotherapy can be explained by the drug's specific immunomodulatory effects on neutrophil dynamics and pro-inflammatory cytokines.

The primary pharmacological mechanism of colchicine involves the inhibition of microtubule polymerization by binding to tubulin. This disruption of the cytoskeleton interferes with the migration, chemotaxis, and activation of neutrophils—the predominant inflammatory cells involved in the pathogenesis of Behçet’s disease. By limiting the recruitment of these cells to the central nervous system (CNS), colchicine can dampen the acute meningoencephalitic response characteristic of neuro-BD.

Furthermore, the resolution of the patient’s syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) likely stems from colchicine's ability to suppress the production of key pro-inflammatory cytokines, specifically interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interleukin-6 (IL-6). As noted in the case discussion, IL-6 is a potent driver of ADH release in neuroinflammatory states. In neuro-BD, aberrant monocyte activation leads to spontaneous IL-6 production, which may directly contribute to the dysregulated ADH secretion seen in this patient. By suppressing the IL-6-mediated inflammatory cascade, colchicine not only addressed the underlying neurological inflammation but also likely mitigated the secondary endocrine complication of SIADH.

In summary, while colchicine is often considered insufficient as a monotherapy for severe neuro-BD, its ability to inhibit neutrophil migration and suppress IL-6-mediated inflammation may provide a viable therapeutic pathway in specific clinical scenarios where corticosteroid therapy is undesirable.
