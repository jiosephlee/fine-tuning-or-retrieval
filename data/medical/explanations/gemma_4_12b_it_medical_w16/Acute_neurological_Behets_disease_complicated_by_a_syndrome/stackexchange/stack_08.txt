Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

### [Clinical Pearls] Does a normal brain MRI during the acute phase of neurological symptoms rule out a diagnosis of neuro-Behçet’s disease? Explain why.

No, a normal brain MRI during the acute phase of neurological symptoms does not rule out a diagnosis of neuro-Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD).

The clinical reasoning for this lies in the diverse ways that neuro-BD can manifest. While neuro-BD frequently involves parenchymal brain lesions—most commonly in the brainstem—it can also present as aseptic meningitis or other forms of CNS inflammation that may not result in visible structural abnormalities or significant signal changes on standard MRI sequences, particularly in the early acute phase.

This case serves as a prime example: despite a brain MRI that revealed no significant abnormalities in the hypothalamus or other brain regions, the patient was diagnosed with acute neuro-BD. The diagnosis was established by integrating the patient's systemic mucocutaneous findings (oral aphthae, folliculitis-like rashes, and genital ulcers) with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis that confirmed aseptic meningitis (mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and increased protein) after infectious, neoplastic, and autoimmune causes were excluded.

In clinical practice, when a patient with known or suspected systemic Behçet’s disease presents with acute neurological symptoms such as fever and headache, a normal MRI should not be viewed as a definitive exclusion of neuro-BD. Instead, clinicians must maintain a high index of suspicion and rely on the constellation of clinical symptoms, CSF findings, and the exclusion of other pathologies to reach a diagnosis.
