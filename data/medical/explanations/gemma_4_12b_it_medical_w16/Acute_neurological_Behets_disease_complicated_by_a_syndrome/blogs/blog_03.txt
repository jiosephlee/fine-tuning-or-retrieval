Title: Blog about the case report: "Acute neurological Behçet's disease complicated by a syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone"

# Beyond the Sodium Level: Hiccups and Rhabdomyolysis as Red Flags for Hyponatremia

When we see a sodium level of 124 mmol/L on a lab report, our clinical reflexes usually kick into high gear regarding fluid restriction and monitoring for cerebral edema. We start checking the volume status, calculating the corrected sodium, and looking for the "why"—is it a medication, a primary endocrine failure, or a systemic inflammatory process? But as this fascinating case of acute neurological Behçet’s disease (neuro-BD) reminds us, the "why" is often hiding in plain sight within the patient's more subtle, "soft" clinical symptoms.

In this case, a man in his 60s presented with a triad of fever, headache, and a persistent, irritating symptom: hiccups. While often dismissed as a minor nuisance or a gastrointestinal quirk, persistent hiccups in the setting of CNS symptoms can be a significant clinical "red flag" for profound hyponatremia, particularly when secondary to meningitis or other forms of CNS inflammation. Here, the hiccups served as a sentinel sign for the underlying Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone secretion (SIADH) triggered by acute neuro-Behçet’s.

The complexity of this case was compounded by another "hidden" laboratory finding: a significantly elevated creatine kinase (CK) level indicating rhabdomyolysis. While we often associate rhabdomyolysis with extreme physical exertion or crush injuries, it is a recognized—though frequently overlooked—complication of severe hyponatremia. The profound electrolyte imbalance can lead to muscle cell membrane instability and metabolic muscle damage. In the whirlwind of managing a patient with a sodium of 124 and a worsening headache, it is easy to overlook the CK and the hiccups, yet together they paint a picture of a patient experiencing a severe, systemic metabolic crisis driven by neuro-inflammation.

The diagnosis of acute neuro-BD was a diagnostic journey in itself. The patient presented with classic mucocutaneous features of Behçet’s disease (BD)—oral aphthae and folliculitis-like rashes—but the neurological involvement was the primary driver of his acute presentation. Because the brain MRI was initially unremarkable (a common finding in the early phases of neuro-BD), the clinical suspicion had to remain high for aseptic meningitis. The link here is the inflammatory cytokine cascade; specifically, elevated IL-6 levels—common in neuro-Behçet’s—can drive the inappropriate release of ADH, leading directly to SIADH.

Perhaps the most surprising takeaway from this case is the management. While corticosteroids are the standard of care for neuro-BD, this patient achieved a resolution of his mucocutaneous symptoms, neurological manifestations, and even his hyponatremia with colchicine monotherapy. While colchicine is traditionally reserved for the "skin and mouth" manifestations of BD, its ability to inhibit neutrophil activation and suppress pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and IL-6 may provide a pathway for managing the underlying inflammatory driver of SIADH in some patients.

**Clinical Pearls & Take-Home Points:**

*   **Listen to the "Soft" Symptoms:** Persistent hiccups should be viewed as a potential neurological "red flag" for severe hyponatremia or CNS irritation (e.g., meningitis, SIADH).
*   **Look for Hidden Complications:** In patients with profound hyponatremia, check the CK. Rhabdomyolysis is a recognized metabolic complication of severe electrolyte shifts that can be easily missed in the acute setting.
*   **Neuro-BD Can be "Radiologically Quiet":** A normal brain MRI does not rule out acute neuro-Behçet’s disease, particularly in the early stages of presentation.
*   **Inflammatory Link:** Consider the role of cytokines (like IL-6) in bridging the gap between systemic inflammatory diseases (like BD) and endocrine complications like SIADH.
*   **Broaden Your Treatment Horizon:** While steroids are standard for neuro-BD, colchicine may have a role in modulating the underlying inflammatory response in specific clinical contexts.
