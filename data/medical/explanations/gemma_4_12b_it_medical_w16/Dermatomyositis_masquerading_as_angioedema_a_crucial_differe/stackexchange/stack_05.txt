Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Dermatomyositis masquerading as angioedema: a crucial differential not to overlook"

### [Management] Why did the clinical team opt for mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab over methotrexate for this patient's management?

The clinical team opted for mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab over methotrexate primarily due to the patient's pulmonary involvement. In the management of dermatomyositis, methotrexate is a standard steroid-sparing agent; however, it carries a significant risk of inducing or exacerbating pulmonary toxicity and interstitial lung disease (ILD). This patient presented with an abnormal pulmonary function test showing an extrathoracic restrictive pattern, and a CT scan revealed pulmonary nodules and peribronchial consolidation. Given these findings, methotrexate was considered potentially hazardous.

Mycophenolate mofetil was selected as a preferred alternative because it is well-tolerated and allows for a daily oral regimen, which was favorable for the patient's management. Furthermore, the decision to employ a more intensive immunosuppressive strategy—including intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) and rituximab—was driven by the severity of the patient's presentation. The patient exhibited a very high creatine kinase level (16,184 U/L), severe dysphagia, and significant cervical soft tissue swelling, necessitating prompt and potent treatment to mitigate multiorgan damage and stabilize his airway and muscle function.
