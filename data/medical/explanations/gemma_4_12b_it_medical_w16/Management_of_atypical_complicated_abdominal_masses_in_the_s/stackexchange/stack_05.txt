Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Differential Diagnosis] During the ultrasound evaluation, the cystic masses mimicked decidualized endometriomas. What imaging characteristics (e.g., vascularity, solid components, or continuity with uterine tissue) are key to differentiating a cystic degenerating leiomyoma from an endometrioma?

In the clinical evaluation of large abdominal masses during pregnancy, differentiating a cystic degenerating leiomyoma from a decidualized endometrioma is a significant diagnostic challenge because both can present as large, unilocular cystic structures with peripheral vascularization. However, several key imaging features are essential for differentiation:

**Continuity with Uterine Tissue**
The most definitive feature for a myomatous origin is the anatomical continuity of the mass with the uterus. In this case, while the upper mass mimicked an endometrioma, the caudal mass was identified as a cystic degenerating leiomyoma because it appeared in continuity with a known posterior intramural-subserosal myoma. A decidualized endometrioma, being an adnexal structure, would typically lack this direct physical connection to the uterine wall, although it may appear "fixed" due to surrounding inflammation or adhesions.

**Solid Components and Wall Characteristics**
Cystic degeneration of a leiomyoma often results in a more heterogeneous internal architecture. While both may show thick walls, the presence of focal solid components—identified in this case as hyperintense T1-T2 weighted components on MRI—is highly suggestive of a degenerating myoma rather than a simple endometrioma. Endometriomas typically present with more homogeneous, "ground-glass" internal echoes on ultrasound.

**Vascularity and Pedicle Identification**
The degree and pattern of vascularity can also provide clues. While both lesions may show peripheral flow, a pedunculated leiomyoma (as seen in the cranial mass of this patient) may exhibit high vascularity and a distinct vascular pedicle. In contrast, an endometrioma's vascularity is usually restricted to the periphery of the cyst wall.

**Clinical Pearl:** When ultrasound findings are equivocal, non-contrast MRI is a valuable tool to better delineate the relationship between an adnexal mass and the uterus. The identification of a "root" or continuity with the uterine fundus or posterior wall should strongly orient the diagnosis toward a myomatous origin over an adnexal process like an endometrioma.
