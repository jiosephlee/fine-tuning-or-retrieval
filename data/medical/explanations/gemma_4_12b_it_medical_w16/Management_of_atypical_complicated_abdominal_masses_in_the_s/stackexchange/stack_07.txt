Title: Clinical Q&A about the case report: "Management of atypical complicated abdominal masses in the second trimester of pregnancy"

### [Management] How does the anatomical location and size of a residual pelvic leiomyoma influence the management of the second stage of labor, and what specific obstetric risk does a large posterior isthmic myoma pose?

The anatomical location and size of a residual leiomyoma are critical determinants in planning the mode of delivery, as they dictate the feasibility of a vaginal birth versus the necessity of a cesarean section. In the management of the second stage of labor, the primary consideration is whether the myoma will physically impede the descent of the fetus. 

As demonstrated in this case, a large posterior isthmic myoma poses a significant risk of **mechanical obstruction to the fetal head progression** during the expulsive phase of labor. Because the isthmus constitutes the lower uterine segment, a large mass in this specific location acts as a "huge obstacle," preventing the head from navigating the birth canal safely. Attempting a vaginal delivery in the presence of such an obstruction can lead to prolonged second-stage labor, increased risk of maternal exhaustion, and potential fetal distress.

Consequently, for myomas that are large, numerous, or located in critical sites like the isthmus or posterior wall, an elective cesarean section is often the preferred management strategy. In this patient, the presence of the approximately 9 cm caudal mass at the posterior isthmic site necessitated an elective cesarean delivery at 37 weeks to circumvent the risk of obstructive labor and ensure a safe outcome for both mother and neonate.
