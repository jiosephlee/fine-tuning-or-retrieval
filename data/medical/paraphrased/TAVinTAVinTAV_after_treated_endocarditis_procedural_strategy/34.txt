Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the various technical difficulties linked to transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve interventions, management approaches should be individualized for each patient. Here, we describe a man in his late seventies with a complex background, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently experienced significant deterioration of his aortic prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. The patient first received a Lotus valve in 2014, followed by a valve-in-valve procedure with an Evolut device in 2022, and subsequently developed degeneration and critical restenosis as a complication of endocarditis. In 2024, a third aortic prosthesis (Acurate neo2) was inserted using the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. As TAVI is increasingly utilized and patient survival continues to improve, such complex re-interventions involving multiple prosthetic devices are likely to become more common, underscoring the importance of refined procedural planning, optimized imaging protocols, and extended follow-up. At one-year after the third TAVI, the patient is symptom-free with stable prosthetic valve performance. This report demonstrates that a third TAVI is achievable after previous prosthetic valve endocarditis, utilizing CT-based planning and documenting successful one-year follow-up, a scenario with limited documentation in the current literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now recognized as a standard therapeutic approach for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, encompassing a wide array of surgical risk categories. With an increasing volume of patients receiving TAVI and improved long-term survival rates, the occurrence of structural valve degeneration and the necessity for subsequent procedures are becoming more frequent in clinical practice.

To address prosthetic valve failure, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) techniques have gained traction as a suitable solution. Nevertheless, these interventions present unique anatomical and technical hurdles, such as the possibility of coronary artery obstruction and a diminished effective orifice area that can lead to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These complexities are heightened with repeated interventions like transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the layering of multiple valve frames demands careful evaluation.

Data on repeated transcatheter valve procedures are scarce, particularly concerning patients who have experienced prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In such intricate cases, thorough preprocedural imaging, customized risk evaluation, and collaborative multidisciplinary discussions are crucial for assessing procedural feasibility and improving outcomes.

This case demonstrates the application of CT-based procedural planning and a strategic transcatheter approach in a patient with several prior TAVI procedures and previously managed PVE, emphasizing important factors in lifelong management of structural valve pathology.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center due to shortness of breath, a tight, spastic sensation in the left chest during exertion (after walking 200–300 meters), as well as dizziness and tiredness.

The patient’s medical background included arterial hypertension, triple-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: right coronary artery (2009), left anterior descending (LAD) artery (2010), repeated LAD intervention (2012), and circumflex artery (2014). He also had third-degree atrioventricular block, for which a pacemaker was implanted in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding episodes beginning in 2022.

Progressive aortic valve degeneration with severe stenosis prompted transcatheter implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) in 2014. By December 2021, he had worsening exertional dyspnoea and echocardiography showed severe stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. By early 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient rose to 83 mm Hg, and ejection fraction decreased to 42%, down from 54% in December.

In May 2022, the patient received a TAV-in-TAV procedure with placement of a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). The intervention was complicated by a pulsatile femoral haematoma and pseudoaneurysm, necessitating open vascular surgical management.

After implantation of the Evolut valve, the left ventricular ejection fraction improved from 42% to 54%, reflecting an early positive haemodynamic outcome. Guideline-based heart failure medical therapy was initiated, consisting of beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted with ongoing fever and deteriorating symptoms of heart failure. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures grew Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient was administered a complete course of targeted antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis PVE, consisting of 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, followed by oral therapy as per the advice of infectious disease specialists. At the time of the third TAVI intervention, antimicrobial therapy was concluded, and no ongoing long-term suppressive antibiotics were prescribed. Assessment including clinical evaluation, repeat blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) confirmed resolution of the infection, with no signs of active endocarditis.

Following the completion of antimicrobial therapy and partial improvement of symptoms, a follow-up in January 2024 showed progressive deterioration of the prosthetic valve, with severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction had normalized to 66%.

Reintervention was elected. Upon admission in June 2024, TTE showed ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a markedly decreased aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², consistent with severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, derived from left ventricular outflow tract assessments and Doppler velocity-time integral measurements. Imaging revealed two superimposed bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT demonstrated a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, indicating pronounced haemodynamic compromise and aiding in the planning for a third transcatheter valve implantation (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was utilised to evaluate anatomical suitability and the risk of coronary artery obstruction. This included review of coronary ostial height, dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, valve-to-coronary distance, and the interaction between previously implanted transcatheter valve frames. Special consideration was given to the alignment between the expected neo-skirt and coronary ostia, and to the practicality of high valve implantation to maintain coronary flow and allow future coronary access. These data were instrumental in determining valve choice, depth of implantation, and procedural approach, enabling the intervention without coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography conducted before reintervention verified the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular pathology.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate triple-stented anatomy, history of infective endocarditis, and significant contraindication to surgery, a third TAVI was planned using a CT-guided approach with a strategic focus on maintaining coronary protection, achieving commissural alignment, and optimizing the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention took place under local anesthesia utilizing both left radial and left femoral arterial access sites. Severe aortic regurgitation was verified with a pigtail catheter aortography (figure 2).

Through the left femoral approach, a Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle. Pre-dilation of the aortic valve was executed using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter while rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute was applied. Concurrent aortography ensured that coronary perfusion was unimpaired (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed at the aortic location. The device was positioned to align with the leaflets of the previously placed Evolut valve, as guided by preprocedural CT modeling, with the intention of maximizing the effective orifice area and minimizing the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

Following valve implantation, post-dilation was carried out with a 20/40 mm balloon under rapid pacing to ensure optimal expansion. Final aortography demonstrated accurate valve placement, continued coronary flow, and the absence of both paravalvular and transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

During valve deployment, commissural alignment was specifically considered to preserve coronary accessibility and lessen future procedural difficulty, utilizing preprocedural CT imaging and fluoroscopic references.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcome and prosthetic valve function were evaluated based on the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, including assessment of haemodynamics, presence of residual regurgitation, and procedure-related complications.

On 7 June 2024, immediate post-procedure TTE demonstrated proper placement of the third transcatheter valve, with full mobility of valve leaflets and absence of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, haemodynamic measurements revealed elevated transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) measured 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

TTE at 1 month post-procedure showed significant improvement: the mean gradient had decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA increased to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodeling was noted with reduced chamber volumes. There was no persistent regurgitation, and valve function remained stable within the moderately stenotic range.

At 3.5 months post-implantation, the patient was asymptomatic and demonstrated good exercise capacity. No clinical features of heart failure or recurrent infection were detected. The prosthetic valve exhibited sustained improvement in haemodynamic indices.

By the 1-year follow-up in June 2025, the patient’s clinical condition was stable with continued activity and no signs of dyspnoea or decompensation. Echocardiography revealed a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. There were no valve-related complications, regurgitation, or recurrence of endocarditis.

These results demonstrate lasting effectiveness and indicate a persistent functional advantage of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach in this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This case illustrates the clinical reasoning and procedural difficulties associated with performing a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with previous TAV-in-TAV procedures and a past episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is now a well-accepted option for treating aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis remains debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for managing valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis advocate for surgical valve replacement as the preferred approach in PVE, especially when heart failure or persistent infection is present. Nevertheless, the guidelines stress the importance of tailoring the therapeutic plan to each patient, with consideration of infection control, surgical risk, and anatomical suitability.

TAVI was initially selected for this patient in 2014 due to elevated surgical risk and significant comorbid conditions, rendering traditional surgical valve replacement less desirable. When the patient later developed structural valve degeneration, a repeat transcatheter approach was chosen in 2022 to restore prosthesis function without the hazards linked to open surgery. After successful management of PVE and with no signs of active infection, the rationale for undertaking a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was based on excessive surgical risk, the anatomical complexity resulting from prior valve implants, and CT evidence indicating feasibility for a procedure that would maintain coronary perfusion and produce satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this patient appeared to result from multiple factors. Although the occurrence of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and mechanical impairment, gradual structural deterioration of the transcatheter devices also contributed substantially. Once infection had resolved and prolonged suppressive antibiotic treatment was deemed unnecessary, management of prosthetic valve dysfunction proceeded similarly to cases without current or recurrent endocarditis. Consequently, procedural planning was primarily based on anatomical suitability, haemodynamic impact, and operative risk rather than the previous infectious episode.

For this patient, antimicrobial therapy had been completed, there were no clinical or imaging indications of ongoing infection, and comprehensive preprocedural assessments—including transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT)—verified the absence of vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, comorbid conditions included chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), diabetes, and a history of gastrointestinal bleeding, and two earlier transcatheter valves were securely integrated into the aortic wall—collectively making open surgery particularly perilous and technically complex. Owing to the extensive fixation of previous prostheses, surgical treatment would have likely required complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports detailing TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures have been documented,37 illustrating a variety of anatomical and technical challenges. The case described here further contributes to this sparse literature, notable for its occurrence in the setting of post-endocarditis degeneration and sustained valve functionality at the 1-year follow-up.

This case highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive lifelong management approaches for structural valve disease. With successive transcatheter reinterventions, aspects such as cumulative frame layering, alignment of the commissures, potential for coronary reaccess, and maintenance of the effective orifice area become progressively complicated. Long-range planning and early discussions regarding future procedural options are vital, beginning with the initial intervention—especially for younger or high-risk individuals.

The patient’s structural valve deterioration involved leaflet fibrosis related to prior degeneration and likely mechanical impairment following infectious insult. The clinical team opted for a third valve implantation—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 device—after preprocedural CT scans and 3D reconstructions confirmed feasibility and adequate clearance of the coronary arteries (see figures 1, 5, and 7). The choice to implant the third transcatheter valve at a high position was intentional. By positioning the device at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets, the native and prior prosthetic leaflets could be fixed against the stent frame, preventing inward leaflet folding that could further compromise the effective orifice area. This technique also reduced the development of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia, helping preserve coronary blood flow and facilitate future coronary interventions. The decision-making was informed by thorough preprocedural CT evaluation and was pivotal in achieving favorable haemodynamics in a triple-stented situation. Despite the inherent complexity associated with triple transcatheter valve placement, the described methodology was technically viable and could be consistently reproduced when guided by advanced imaging and a predefined procedural plan.

The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen for its supra-annular leaflet location, comparatively low frame profile, and advantageous haemodynamic characteristics in restrictive anatomies. Its structural design facilitates commissural alignment, a critical factor in this case to safeguard future coronary access.

A major technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the potential compromise of coronary access resulting from several overlapping stent structures. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the total mesh density could still hinder subsequent coronary interventions. This underscores the importance of precise procedural planning in patients with existing coronary pathology or those for whom future access might be necessary. Preprocedural CT allowed for assessment of the implantation zone, analysis of frame interactions, and estimation of valve-to-coronary artery distances; however, comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis with dedicated software was not undertaken.

The elimination of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve deployment likely resulted from successful commissural alignment, efficient post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut frame, which improved leaflet motion and central haemodynamics. Although deliberate frame fracture was not attempted, high-pressure balloon post-dilatation probably contributed to additional radial expansion of the Evolut, further optimizing valve performance.

This intervention was conducted under institutional authorization for off-label usage of the Acurate neo2 device. Collaborative decision-making included the patient and his family, and the case underwent discussion in a multidisciplinary heart team meeting composed of interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesia professionals.

In the event of premature failure of the current prosthesis, therapeutic alternatives would be severely restricted. Surgical explantation would remain technically complex and associated with high risk. Novel catheter-based techniques such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—including bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—may be considered, though their safety and practicality in a setting with three stent layers remain to be established.
