Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the myriad technical complexities inherent in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve approaches, management decisions must be individualized for each patient scenario. We report the case of a man in his late 70s whose history included two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI) and infective endocarditis, subsequently complicated by severe deterioration of the aortic prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter intervention. His initial procedure was with a Lotus valve in 2014; in 2022, he underwent a valve-in-valve TAVI using an Evolut device. Following this, valve degeneration associated with endocarditis and marked restenosis developed. In 2024, a third valve (Acurate neo2) was placed via a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV method. As indications for TAVI expand and longevity of recipients increases, the frequency of multi-prosthesis interventions is expected to rise, underscoring the importance of enhanced procedural planning, advanced imaging techniques, and vigilant long-term monitoring. After one year, the patient is asymptomatic and the prosthetic valve continues to function normally. This report demonstrates the possibility of a third TAVI following treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by computed tomography-based procedural planning and one-year follow-up, an outcome that remains rarely described in published literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) has become a recognized therapy for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, regardless of surgical risk categories. As TAVI use expands and patient longevity increases, clinicians are encountering structural valve deterioration and repeat intervention more frequently.

To address failed transcatheter prosthetic valves, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) techniques have developed as an effective option. Nonetheless, these procedures present distinct anatomical and technical difficulties, such as the potential for coronary artery obstruction and a diminished effective orifice area, which may lead to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These concerns are heightened with successive interventions, for example transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), in which multiple superimposed valve frames require thorough evaluation.

Data on repeated transcatheter valve procedures is sparse, especially in patients previously diagnosed with prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these intricate situations, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, tailored risk analysis, and collaborative decision-making involving multiple specialties are vital to assess viability and optimize outcomes.

This case demonstrates the significance of CT-based procedural planning and strategic transcatheter treatment for a patient who has undergone several prior TAVI interventions and therapy for PVE, underscoring critical factors in lifelong structural valve disease management.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late 70s was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after presenting with shortness of breath, a spastic feeling on the left side of his chest triggered by physical activity (such as walking 200–300 m), as well as dizziness and tiredness.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: right coronary artery in 2009, left anterior descending artery in 2010, repeat intervention to the LAD in 2012, and circumflex artery in 2014. He also had a third-degree atrioventricular block that necessitated pacemaker insertion in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and had experienced repeated gastrointestinal bleeding since 2022.

In 2014, progressive deterioration of the aortic valve and severe stenosis led to transcatheter implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific). By December 2021, exertional dyspnoea had intensified, and echocardiography revealed severe stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. By early 2022, his mean gradient had reached 83 mm Hg and his ejection fraction (EF) had decreased to 42% (down from 54% in December).

In May 2022, a TAV-in-TAV procedure was performed, implanting a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). This intervention was complicated by the development of a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm, requiring open vascular repair.

After receiving the Evolut TAV-in-TAV, the patient's left ventricular EF increased from 42% to 54%, reflecting a favorable early hemodynamic outcome. He was treated with guideline-based heart failure management, including beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was re-admitted to hospital due to ongoing fever and exacerbation of heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean pressure gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures yielded Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of antimicrobial therapy specifically targeting E. faecalis PVE, which consisted of six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, followed by oral antibiotics as directed by infectious disease specialists. By the time of the third TAVI, the antimicrobial regimen had been finished and no prolonged suppressive antibiotics were in use. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) indicated infection resolution, with no evidence of ongoing endocarditis.

Following the conclusion of antimicrobial therapy and partial improvement in symptoms, the January 2024 follow-up showed deterioration in the function of the prosthetic valve, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean pressure gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, while left ventricular ejection fraction had normalized to 66%.

A plan was established for further intervention. At hospital admission in June 2024, TTE displayed ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly decreased aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², confirming pronounced prosthetic valve malfunction. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, based on measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler-obtained velocity-time integrals. Two overlapping bioprosthetic valves were observed.

MSCT detected a minimum internal diameter of 13.7 mm in the Evolut prosthesis, indicating severe haemodynamic restriction and aiding the planning of a third transcatheter valve placement (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT evaluated anatomical suitability as well as the potential for coronary artery obstruction. This assessment included consideration of coronary ostial height, the dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, distance between the valve and the coronary arteries, and the interaction of the previously inserted transcatheter valve frames. Special focus was placed on the relationship between the planned neo-skirt and the coronary ostia, alongside the potential for high implantation to maintain coronary perfusion and access. These analyses informed choices regarding valve selection, implantation level, and procedural approach, enabling the intervention to occur without the use of coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography conducted prior to the procedure verified there were no signs of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Given the patient’s intricate history of triple stenting, previous infective endocarditis, and elevated surgical risk, a third TAVI was organized as a computed tomography-guided, strategy-oriented intervention prioritizing coronary safeguarding, commissural positioning, and maximizing the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was carried out with the patient under local anesthesia, utilizing left radial and left femoral arterial access points. Aortography conducted with a pigtail catheter demonstrated severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the left femoral approach. The aortic valve underwent balloon pre-dilation using a 22/40 mm catheter while rapid ventricular pacing was applied at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography verified uninterrupted coronary blood flow (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthesis (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed in the aortic valve position. The device was oriented according to the alignment of the Evolut valve leaflets, as planned from preprocedural CT modeling, to maximize the functional orifice area and minimize the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

Balloon post-dilation was conducted with a 20/40 mm balloon during rapid pacing to ensure optimal expansion of the prosthetic valve. Final aortographic imaging verified proper implant location, maintained coronary perfusion, and absence of both perivalvular and transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Valve deployment was performed with attention to commissural alignment, aiming to maintain coronary access and simplify possible future interventions, using preprocedural CT scans and fluoroscopic references for guidance.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and prosthetic valve function were evaluated following the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 criteria, encompassing assessments of haemodynamic performance, presence of residual regurgitation, and complications arising from the procedure.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) performed immediately post-procedure on 7 June 2024 demonstrated correct placement of the third transcatheter valve. The prosthetic leaflets were well-mobile, opening fully, and no aortic regurgitation was observed (grade 0). Nevertheless, significant transvalvular pressure gradients were recorded, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The calculated aortic valve area (AVA) measured 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicating severe patient–prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

At the 1-month follow-up, repeat TTE showed notable haemodynamic improvement: the mean gradient was reduced to 27 mm Hg and the AVA increased to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodelling with decreased chamber volumes was also noted. There was an absence of residual regurgitation, and prosthetic function stabilized within the moderately stenotic range.

By 3.5 months, the patient exhibited no symptoms and maintained good exercise capacity. There were no indications of heart failure or recurrent infection. The valve prosthesis continued to function appropriately, with improved haemodynamic results.

At the 1-year evaluation in June 2025, the patient continued to be stable and physically active, without dyspnoea or clinical signs of decompensation. Echocardiography at this interval revealed a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and an ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. There were no valve-related complications, residual regurgitation, or evidence of recurrent endocarditis.

Collectively, these results demonstrate sustained valve durability and indicate persistent functional benefit from the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV strategy in this high-risk patient population.

Discussion:

This case underscores both the complexities in clinical judgement and the technical considerations encountered when performing a third TAVI in an individual with previous TAV-in-TAV interventions and a background of PVE.

Although TAVI is an established therapy for aortic stenosis, its application in those with a history of endocarditis remains a subject of ongoing debate. The 2025 guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) for valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to recommend surgical valve replacement for PVE, especially when associated with heart failure or unresolved infection. Nonetheless, the guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management plans based on infection control status, operative risk, and the feasibility of intervention.

In 2014, the initial use of TAVI was selected due to the patient’s elevated operative risk and several coexisting conditions that rendered conventional surgical replacement an unattractive option. When the patient later experienced structural valve failure, a repeat transcatheter procedure was carried out in 2022 to reestablish valve competence while mitigating the dangers linked to open surgery. After successful resolution of PVE, and without signs of ongoing infection, a third transcatheter procedure was considered in 2024 due to very high surgical risk, intricate anatomical factors related to prior implanted frames, and CT evidence that the approach could maintain coronary perfusion while providing satisfactory haemodynamic results.

The patient's prosthetic valve failure likely arose from multiple contributing factors. Although the episode of infective endocarditis may have resulted in leaflet damage and subsequent restriction of mobility, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter prosthetic valves was also a major component. After resolving the infection and determining that prolonged suppressive antibiotics were unnecessary, management for prosthetic valve dysfunction followed protocols for individuals without current endocarditis. Thus, procedural choices were informed mainly by anatomical suitability, haemodynamic severity, and surgical risk, rather than by infection history alone.

For this patient, antimicrobial therapy had been completed, with no evidence of ongoing infection either clinically or on imaging. Comprehensive preprocedural assessments—including TEE and MSCT—found no vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. Additionally, the EuroSCORE II was calculated at 27.36%, with substantial comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, past gastrointestinal hemorrhage), and the presence of two prior transcatheter valve implants that showed pronounced integration of stents with the aortic wall, which collectively made open surgery especially risky and technically challenging. The established deep fixation of previous prostheses suggested that surgical management would require complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports describing TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions have been documented,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and procedural difficulties. Our case contributes to this sparse literature, uniquely featuring degeneration following endocarditis and maintained valve function at the one-year mark.

This case highlights the increasing significance of lifelong management approaches in structural valve conditions. Each subsequent transcatheter procedure introduces greater complexity regarding aspects such as frame overlap, commissural positioning, coronary access, and preservation of effective orifice area. Early consideration and discussion of long-term procedural paths are essential from the initial intervention, especially for younger or higher-risk individuals.

The structural deterioration seen in this patient was characterized by leaflet fibrosis arising from earlier degeneration as well as likely mechanical constraint secondary to infection. The clinical team opted to implant a third device—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis—after preprocedural CT imaging and three-dimensional modeling confirmed both feasibility and sufficient coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7). Purposefully high implantation of the third transcatheter valve was selected as a procedural tactic. Placing the prosthesis at the height of the Evolut valve leaflets facilitated fixation of the existing leaflets onto the stent frame, thereby averting additional compromise in the effective orifice area from inward leaflet folding. This method also reduced the development of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia and supported maintenance of coronary flow and access for possible future interventions. Preprocedural CT guidance was central to this approach and enabled achievement of satisfactory haemodynamics in a triple-stented structure. Although the triple transcatheter configuration presents significant complexity, this technique demonstrated technical feasibility and reproducibility with thorough preprocedural imaging and well-defined implantation planning.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet arrangement, comparatively short frame height, and advantageous haemodynamic characteristics in restricted anatomies. Its architecture facilitates commissural alignment, an important factor in this case to ensure future coronary accessibility.

A primary technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the potential compromise of coronary access arising from multiple superimposed stent frames. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the increased mesh density may still complicate subsequent coronary interventions. This underscores the importance of careful procedural planning for patients with established coronary disease or in situations where future coronary entry may be crucial. Preprocedural CT enabled assessment of the landing area, analysis of frame relationships, and approximation of valve-to-coronary distances, although comprehensive three-dimensional expansion evaluation via specialized software was not conducted.

The elimination of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve implantation likely resulted from optimized commissural alignment, effective post-dilatation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previous Evolut frame, thereby enhancing leaflet mobility and central hemodynamics. Although intentional frame fracture was not undertaken, post-dilatation with a high-pressure balloon probably facilitated additional radial expansion of the Evolut, further improving valve performance.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label use of the Acurate neo2 device during the procedure. Shared decision-making incorporated the patient and his family, and the case underwent review by a multidisciplinary heart team consisting of interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging professionals, and anesthesiologists.

If the current prosthesis were to fail early, management options would be severely limited. Surgical explantation would pose substantial technical challenges and significant risk. Innovative catheter-based interventions such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—such as bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—could be contemplated, though their practicality and safety in cases of triple-stented anatomy have not been established.
