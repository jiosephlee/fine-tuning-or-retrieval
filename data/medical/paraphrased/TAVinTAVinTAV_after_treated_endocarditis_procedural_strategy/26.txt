Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the varied technical difficulties encountered during transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve interventions, the management approach for each patient must be individualized. We describe the case of a man in his late 70s with a complex background that includes two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures as well as infective endocarditis, who subsequently developed marked deterioration of the aortic valve prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. The patient initially received a Lotus valve in 2014, underwent a TAV-in-TAV with an Evolut device in 2022, and later developed severe degeneration and restenosis following endocarditis. In 2024, a third prosthetic valve (Acurate neo2) was successfully placed using the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. As TAVI indications broaden and the life expectancy of treated individuals increases, intricate multi-valve procedures may become more common, underscoring the importance of enhanced procedural planning, advanced imaging techniques, and ongoing monitoring. At a 1-year review, the patient remains without symptoms and with sustained valve performance. This case demonstrates the practicality of performing a third TAVI post-treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-guided planning and 1-year follow-up, a scenario infrequently documented in existing literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now widely recognized as a therapeutic option for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, encompassing patients across different categories of surgical risk. With the expanding population undergoing TAVI and improvement in long-term outcomes, clinicians are encountering more cases of structural valve deterioration necessitating repeat procedures.

In response, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have developed as an effective method for addressing malfunctioning transcatheter valves. Nevertheless, these procedures present unique anatomical and technical issues, notably the potential for coronary artery obstruction and diminished effective orifice area, which may lead to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. The complexity of these concerns escalates during further consecutive reinterventions—such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV)—due to the presence of multiple, overlapping valve frames requiring thorough evaluation.

The literature addressing successive transcatheter valve procedures is sparse, especially in patients previously affected by prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In such challenging cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, personalised risk analysis, and interdisciplinary collaboration are imperative to assess procedural feasibility and enhance outcomes.

This report demonstrates the use of CT-guided planning and a strategic approach to transcatheter intervention in a patient with several prior TAVI procedures and resolved PVE, emphasizing critical aspects in the lifelong management of structural valve disease.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after experiencing shortness of breath, a constricting sensation on the left side of his chest during physical activity (such as walking 200–300 m), dizziness, and fatigue.

His medical history was significant for arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and multiple percutaneous coronary interventions: in 2009 (right coronary artery), 2010 (left anterior descending (LAD) artery), 2012 (repeat LAD intervention), and 2014 (circumflex artery). Additional conditions included third-degree atrioventricular block managed by pacemaker implantation in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and repeated gastrointestinal bleeding episodes beginning in 2022.

In 2014, worsening aortic valve pathology and severe stenosis prompted transcatheter placement of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific). By December 2021, exertional dyspnoea had intensified, with echocardiographic evaluation revealing significant bioprosthetic valve stenosis. At the beginning of 2022, the mean valve gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, and the ejection fraction had decreased from 54% (in December) to 42%.

In May 2022, the patient underwent a transcatheter aortic valve-in-valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedure, receiving a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). The procedure was complicated by a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm, necessitating surgical intervention for vascular repair.

Post-Evolut TAV-in-TAV, there was an improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction from 42% to 54%, reflecting a positive early hemodynamic outcome. Guideline-based management for heart failure was initiated, including beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as clinically indicated.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted once more due to ongoing fever and a deterioration in heart failure manifestations. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient measured at 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures demonstrated Enterococcus faecalis, establishing a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of targeted antimicrobial therapy directed at E. faecalis PVE, which included 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, succeeded by oral therapy as per recommendations from infectious disease specialists. By the time of the third TAVI procedure, antimicrobial therapy had concluded, and long-term suppressive antibiotics were not being administered. Clinical evaluation, repeated blood culture tests, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) demonstrated resolution of infection, with no signs of active endocarditis present.

Following the completion of antimicrobial treatment and partial improvement in symptoms, a follow-up in January 2024 showed further decline in prosthetic valve function, marked by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction had normalized to 66%.

The medical team opted for another intervention. Upon admission in June 2024, TTE demonstrated ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², all indicative of severe prosthetic valve dysfunction. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, incorporating left ventricular outflow tract measurements and Doppler-derived velocity-time integrals. Imaging showed two overlapping bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT identified a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, verifying profound haemodynamic impairment and informing the procedural strategy for a third transcatheter valve replacement (see figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was utilized to evaluate anatomical suitability and the potential for coronary artery obstruction. This assessment included measurements of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva size, valve-to-coronary distance, and the interaction between the structures of previously implanted transcatheter valves. Special consideration was given to the positioning of the expected neo-skirt in relation to the coronary ostia and the viability of high implantation to maintain coronary blood flow and future coronary access. These insights contributed to the selection of the valve, implantation depth, and procedural approach, permitting the intervention to be carried out without coronary protection measures.

Transoesophageal echocardiography conducted before the reintervention confirmed there was no evidence of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate triple-stented configuration, history of infective endocarditis, and unacceptable surgical risk, the third TAVI was scheduled as a computed tomography–guided procedure, with particular attention to coronary protection, commissural alignment, and maximizing the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was carried out under local anesthesia utilizing left radial and left femoral arterial entry. Severe aortic regurgitation was confirmed by aortography using a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

Via left femoral access, a Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle. The aortic valve was pre-dilated using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at 180 bpm. Concurrent aortography showed unimpaired coronary flow (figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthesis (size S) was placed at the aortic site. Preprocedural CT modeling facilitated alignment of the device with the Evolut valve leaflets to optimize effective orifice area and minimize the likelihood of patient–prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

To ensure optimal expansion of the implanted valve, post-dilation was performed with a 20/40 mm balloon during rapid pacing. Final aortography demonstrated proper device positioning, continued coronary blood flow, and the absence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Commissural alignment was prioritized throughout valve implantation to maintain coronary access and lessen potential procedural challenges, guided by CT imaging and fluoroscopic markers.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and prosthetic valve function were evaluated using the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, encompassing assessments of haemodynamic performance, detection of residual regurgitation, and procedure-associated complications.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) performed immediately post-procedure on 7 June 2024 verified accurate placement of the third transcatheter valve. Valve leaflets demonstrated unrestricted movement and complete opening, with no evidence of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, haemodynamic data revealed elevated transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was measured at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicating severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

TTE performed one month later demonstrated substantial improvement: mean gradient had decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA increased to 1.1 cm². The left ventricle exhibited evidence of remodelling, with lower chamber volumes. There was no persistent regurgitation, and valve function remained stable within a range consistent with moderate stenosis.

At 3.5 months follow-up, the patient had no symptoms and exhibited preserved exercise capacity. No signs of heart failure or recurrent infection were present. Prosthetic valve performance was maintained, with sustained haemodynamic improvement.

At 1 year post-implantation (June 2025), the patient remained clinically stable and active, without dyspnoea or indications of decompensation. TTE findings included a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. No prosthesis-associated complications, regurgitation, or recurrent endocarditis were identified.

Collectively, these results highlight the durability and ongoing functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedure in this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This report underscores the complexities involved in both clinical decision-making and technical execution when undertaking a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV procedures and a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI has been widely adopted as a treatment for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a previous episode of endocarditis remains contentious. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis advise surgical valve replacement as the preferred approach for PVE, particularly when there is concurrent heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, the guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management to each case, considering infection control, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

The initial decision to proceed with TAVI in 2014 was primarily due to the patient's elevated surgical risk and significant comorbidities, which rendered traditional surgical valve replacement inappropriate. When the patient later developed structural valve dysfunction, a repeat transcatheter approach was undertaken in 2022 to re-establish valve competence while avoiding the hazards associated with open surgical intervention. After successful management of PVE and in the context of no ongoing infection, the selection of a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was predicated on prohibitive operative risk, intricate anatomical factors arising from existing valve frames, and CT confirmation supporting a technique that would maintain coronary patency and provide satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

This patient’s prosthetic valve failure was probably due to several factors. Although infective endocarditis may have caused leaflet damage and subsequent mechanical dysfunction, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter prostheses was also a major contributor. Notably, after confirmation that the infection had resolved and there was no need for prolonged suppressive antibiotics, the management approach for prosthetic valve dysfunction followed protocols similar to those used in patients without current or persistent endocarditis. As a result, procedural decisions were primarily based on anatomical considerations, haemodynamic significance, and operative risk, rather than focusing solely on previous infection history.

In this instance, the patient had finished antimicrobial treatment, had no signs or imaging evidence of ongoing infection, and received comprehensive preprocedural assessment, including TEE and MSCT, both of which excluded vegetations or paravalvular pathology. In addition, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and he had multiple comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, a history of gastrointestinal bleeding), along with two prior transcatheter prostheses with significant stent adherence to the aortic wall—factors that made conventional surgery extremely risky and technically complex. Due to the firm integration of the existing valves, a surgical approach would likely have required replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple case reports have detailed TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and technical hurdles. The present case expands upon this sparse literature, notable for the post-endocarditis deterioration and sustained valve performance at the 1-year mark.

This report highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive, lifelong management in patients with structural valve disease. Each successive transcatheter intervention introduces greater complexity regarding frame stacking, commissural positioning, coronary accessibility, and maintenance of an adequate effective orifice area. Early establishment of long-term procedural plans, beginning at the initial treatment, is crucial—especially in younger individuals or those at elevated risk.

In this patient, structural compromise included leaflet fibrosis as a result of prior degeneration and likely mechanical impairment post-infection. After evaluating preprocedural CT imaging and 3D modelling, which confirmed the possibility and ensured sufficient coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7), the team elected to implant a third valve, the self-expanding Acurate neo2. The third device was placed high, intentionally, at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets. This approach secured the earlier leaflets against the stent frame, preventing further decrease in effective orifice area from inward leaflet folding. Moreover, it reduced neo-skirt development beneath the coronary ostia, supporting both coronary perfusion and future coronary access. Preprocedural CT imaging was pivotal to formulating this strategy, which resulted in satisfactory haemodynamics in a triple-valve configuration. Although triple transcatheter valve placement is inherently complex, the procedure was both technically feasible and replicable when informed by thorough imaging and a structured implantation plan.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen because its leaflets are positioned above the annulus, its frame height is relatively modest, and it offers favourable haemodynamic outcomes in constricted anatomical settings. The prosthesis is engineered to facilitate commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this scenario to maintain access to the coronary arteries for potential future interventions.

One major technical issue associated with TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the possibility of compromised coronary access stemming from multiple overlapping stent frames. Even with efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the accumulated stent mesh may still hinder subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the necessity for careful procedural strategy in patients with established coronary disease or those for whom later coronary access could be required. Preprocedural CT imaging was utilized to measure the landing area, assess possible interactions between stent frames, and approximate distances from the valve to the coronary arteries; however, a dedicated three-dimensional expansion analysis was not performed.

Resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve implantation was likely due to a combination of improved commissural alignment, thorough post-dilatation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut stent, collectively enhancing leaflet function and central flow patterns. Although purposeful frame fracture was not done, high-pressure balloon dilatation probably promoted additional radial expansion of the Evolut frame, contributing to improved valve performance.

The intervention was carried out with institutional authorization for off-label use of the Acurate neo2 valve. Decisions regarding care involved both the patient and his family, and the case underwent review in a multidisciplinary heart team, which included interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesiologists.

If the current prosthesis were to fail ahead of time, management options would be exceedingly limited. Surgical removal would remain complex and pose substantial risk. Newer transcatheter strategies, such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration (for instance, bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction [BASILICA]), may be contemplated, but their practicality and safety in the context of triple-stent configurations are not yet established.
