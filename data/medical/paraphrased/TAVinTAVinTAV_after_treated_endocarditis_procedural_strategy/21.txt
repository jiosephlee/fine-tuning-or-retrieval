Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the variety of technical complexities involved in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, therapeutic approaches should be individually adapted for each patient. This report describes a male patient in his late 70s with a complicated medical background, which included two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently developed marked aortic prosthesis degeneration that necessitated a third transcatheter procedure. The patient initially received a Lotus valve in 2014, underwent a TAV-in-TAV procedure with an Evolut device in 2022, and later experienced degeneration related to endocarditis with significant restenosis. In 2024, an Acurate neo2 valve was placed as a third prosthesis using the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. The expanding use of TAVI and increased life expectancy among these patients suggest that such complex reinterventions involving multiple prosthetic valves may arise more frequently, underlining the importance of advancing procedural planning, enhanced imaging techniques, and long-term monitoring. At one-year follow-up, the patient showed no symptoms and maintained proper valve performance. This case demonstrates the practicality of performing a third TAVI after treatment for prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-based procedural planning and documented one-year follow-up, a scenario infrequently detailed in current literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now recognized as a standard therapeutic approach for patients with severe aortic stenosis, spanning various levels of surgical risk. With an increasing population of individuals receiving TAVI and improved long-term outcomes, clinicians are encountering more cases of structural valve deterioration requiring additional procedures.

Within this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have gained attention as an effective method to address malfunctioning transcatheter valves. Nonetheless, these procedures present distinct anatomical and technical hurdles, such as the potential for coronary artery occlusion and the possibility of a diminished effective orifice area that can result in significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These difficulties become more pronounced during successive reinterventions, including transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the presence of several overlapping valve frames must be evaluated carefully.

Current data on repeated transcatheter valve procedures, especially in patients with previous prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), are sparse. In these complex clinical situations, thorough preprocedural imaging, tailored risk evaluation, and collaborative multidisciplinary decision-making are vital to ensure procedural suitability and optimize patient outcomes.

This report demonstrates the application of computed tomography-guided planning and strategic transcatheter intervention in a patient who previously underwent multiple TAVI procedures and received treatment for PVE, underscoring important factors in the lifelong management of structural valve dysfunction.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late 70s was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after experiencing shortness of breath, a spastic sensation on the left side of the chest during exertion (walking 200–300 m), lightheadedness, and fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat intervention of the LAD in 2012, and the circumflex artery in 2014. Additional chronic conditions comprised third-degree atrioventricular block, necessitating pacemaker placement in 2014; type 2 diabetes mellitus; chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD); and repeated gastrointestinal bleeding episodes since 2022.

In 2014, due to worsening aortic valve degeneration and critical stenosis, he received a transcatheter implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific). By December 2021, his exertional dyspnoea had increased, with echocardiography showing advanced bioprosthetic valve stenosis. Early in 2022, the mean gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, and his left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) decreased to 42% (down from 54% in December).

A TAV-in-TAV procedure was carried out in May 2022, during which a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was implanted. This intervention was complicated by the occurrence of a pulsatile femoral haematoma and pseudoaneurysm, necessitating surgical vascular repair.

Post-procedure, the left ventricular EF improved from 42% to 54%, demonstrating an early positive haemodynamic effect. Management included guideline-based heart failure pharmacotherapy, such as beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics where appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted once more due to ongoing fever and aggravated heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient was treated with a full regimen of targeted antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis PVE, including six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, followed by oral therapy according to the guidance of infectious disease specialists. Upon undergoing the third TAVI procedure, antimicrobial therapy was finished, with no ongoing suppressive antibiotic therapy. Clinical evaluation, repeat blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) confirmed infection resolution, with no signs of active endocarditis.

After antimicrobial therapy concluded and partial symptom improvement was achieved, follow-up in January 2024 indicated further deterioration of prosthetic valve function, now demonstrating severe aortic stenosis along with moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient was 49 mm Hg, and the left ventricular ejection fraction had returned to 66%.

Reintervention was subsequently planned. At admission in June 2024, TTE revealed persistent grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², indicative of severe prosthetic valve dysfunction. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, based on measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler-derived velocity-time integrals. Two overlapping bioprosthetic valves were observed.

MSCT imaging showed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, confirming severe hemodynamic compromise and facilitating planning for a third transcatheter valve implantation (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was used to evaluate anatomical suitability and the potential risk of coronary artery obstruction, involving assessment of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance, and the spatial interaction between previously implanted transcatheter valve frames. Special consideration was given to the relationship between the projected neo-skirt and coronary ostia, along with the viability of high implantation to preserve coronary perfusion and enable future coronary access. This information informed valve choice, implantation depth, and overall procedural approach, allowing the intervention to be conducted without coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography, performed prior to the reintervention, verified the absence of ongoing endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate triple-stent configuration, history of infective endocarditis, and high operative risk, a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was scheduled utilizing CT-guided planning. The procedural approach prioritized coronary protection, commissural alignment, and optimization of the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was conducted with the patient under local anesthesia, employing left radial and left femoral arterial access sites. Severe aortic regurgitation was validated by aortography using a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through femoral access. Pre-dilatation of the native aortic valve was performed using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter with rapid ventricular pacing at 180 bpm. Simultaneous aortography demonstrated persistent coronary artery patency (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed at the aortic position. Alignment with the leaflets of the previously implanted Evolut valve was achieved according to CT-based preprocedural modelling, aiming to enhance effective orifice area and mitigate the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A 20/40 mm balloon was used for post-dilation under rapid pacing to further optimize the expansion of the new valve. Final angiographic assessment showed accurate prosthesis position, sustained coronary flow, and no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

During valve deployment, commissural orientation was carefully considered to maintain future coronary accessibility and decrease procedural complexity, guided by preprocedural CT and fluoroscopic reference points.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcome and performance of the prosthetic valve were evaluated based on Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, encompassing assessment of haemodynamic metrics, residual regurgitation, and complications arising from the procedure.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) conducted immediately following intervention on 7 June 2024 verified proper placement of the third transcatheter valve. The valve leaflets demonstrated normal mobility and full excursion, with no detectable aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, haemodynamic analysis revealed pronounced transvalvular gradients, with a mean value of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was measured at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m²), and Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

At one month post-procedure, repeat TTE demonstrated substantial haemodynamic improvement: the mean gradient declined to 27 mm Hg, and AVA increased to 1.1 cm². Left ventricular remodeling was evident, with decreased chamber volumes. No regurgitation was identified, and the prosthesis continued to function within parameters reflecting moderate stenosis.

By 3.5 months after valve implantation, the patient exhibited no symptoms and sustained good exercise capacity. Clinical evaluation revealed no evidence of heart failure or infection recurrence. Echocardiographic parameters persisted within improved ranges, and the prosthetic valve remained functional.

At the 1-year mark following implantation (June 2025), the patient was clinically stable and remained physically active, with no dyspnoea or signs of decompensation. TTE demonstrated a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and an ejection fraction of 65%. There were no indications of valve dysfunction, regurgitation, or relapse of endocarditis.

Collectively, these results support the sustained durability and ongoing functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach in this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This report underscores the complexities in clinical decision-making and the technical issues involved in conducting a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with two prior TAV-in-TAV interventions and a background of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is now an established therapy for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis remains a matter of debate. The 2025 guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) for valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to endorse surgical valve replacement as the preferred option in PVE, especially when there is coexistent heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, these guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management to the individual patient, with considerations for infection control, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

In this case, TAVI was initially selected in 2014 due to the patient’s elevated operative risk and significant comorbid conditions, which made traditional surgery less suitable. With the onset of structural valve deterioration, a repeat transcatheter procedure was undertaken in 2022 to re-establish valve function while mitigating the dangers associated with reoperation. After the successful management of PVE and absence of ongoing infection, a third transcatheter intervention was pursued in 2024, guided by the persistence of prohibitive operative risk, anatomical complexities related to the prior valve frames, and preprocedural CT imaging that confirmed the feasibility of a technique designed to preserve coronary flow and optimize haemodynamic results.

The etiology of this patient’s prosthetic valve failure appeared to involve multiple factors. Although the episode of infective endocarditis may have resulted in leaflet damage and subsequent mechanical limitation, progressive intrinsic deterioration of the transcatheter prosthetic valves was also a major contributor. Notably, once infection was deemed eradicated and there was no indication for continued suppressive antibiotics, the therapeutic approach to prosthetic valve dysfunction followed the protocol used for patients without active endocarditis. Thus, choices regarding the intervention focused chiefly on anatomical suitability, the degree of haemodynamic compromise, and operative risk, rather than the prior infectious episode alone.

For this patient, antimicrobial treatment was completed with no evidence on clinical assessment or imaging to suggest ongoing infection. Comprehensive preprocedural evaluation—including transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT)—confirmed that there were no vegetations or paravalvular issues present. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and she had multiple substantial comorbidities, such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), diabetes, and a history of gastrointestinal bleeding, alongside two previously deployed transcatheter prostheses that were extensively incorporated into the aortic wall. These factors made conventional surgery exceptionally risky and technically complicated. Due to the profound anchoring of the existing valves, surgical management would probably have required complete replacement of both the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple published case reports have described TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures,37 documenting a range of anatomical and technical challenges. The present case contributes additional insight to this limited literature, distinguished by degeneration following endocarditis and maintained valve function at the one-year mark.

This case emphasizes the increasing significance of comprehensive, lifelong management approaches for structural valve disease. Each subsequent transcatheter intervention introduces added complexity regarding issues such as stent frame layering, commissural alignment, access to coronary arteries, and preservation of the residual effective orifice area. Thus, proactive planning and early dialogue about future procedural pathways are vital—even during the initial procedure—especially for younger or higher-risk individuals.

In this patient, structural deterioration comprised leaflet fibrosis resulting from prior degeneration and likely mechanical constraint induced by previous infection. The treating team elected to implant a third valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis—after preprocedural CT scanning and 3D modeling confirmed both procedural viability and sufficient clearance of the coronary arteries (see figures 1, 5, and 7). A high position for the third transcatheter valve was intentionally selected. Placement at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets enabled the fixation of the old leaflets against the stent frame, effectively preventing further compromise of the effective orifice area by inward leaflet folding. This technique also reduced the risk of generating a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, thereby facilitating preserved coronary flow and ensuring future coronary access. The preprocedural CT informed this strategy, which was critical for attaining satisfactory haemodynamics in a triple-stented configuration. Despite the technical demands posed by a triple transcatheter valve anatomy, the procedure was achievable and could be replicated, provided meticulous imaging and a carefully planned implantation method were employed.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet location, modest frame height, and advantageous haemodynamic properties within constricted anatomies. Its structural design facilitates commissural alignment, which was particularly relevant in this scenario to maintain potential for future coronary access.

A major technical consideration with TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions is the possibility of compromised coronary access arising from multiple superimposed stent frames. Despite attempts to align the commissures, the increased mesh density may still hinder subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the necessity for careful procedural planning in patients with pre-existing coronary pathology or those anticipated to require coronary access. Preprocedural CT imaging allowed for measurement of the intended landing area, assessment of frame interplay, and estimation of distances between the valve and coronaries, though comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis with specialized software was not conducted.

The elimination of the transvalvular gradient after the third valve placement resulted from optimal commissural alignment, thorough post-dilatation, and partial mechanical expansion of the preceding Evolut frame, enhancing leaflet mobility and central flow characteristics. Although deliberate frame fracture was not utilized, the application of high-pressure balloon post-dilation likely led to additional radial enlargement of the Evolut frame, contributing to improved prosthetic valve function.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label use of the Acurate neo2 device in this procedure. The decision-making process included the patient and his family, and was reviewed during a multidisciplinary heart team meeting comprised of interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesiologists.

If the current prosthesis were to deteriorate ahead of schedule, therapeutic choices would be severely constrained. Surgical explantation would pose substantial technical difficulties and carry significant risk. Novel catheter-based approaches, such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration techniques (including BASILICA: bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent coronary artery obstruction), may be contemplated; however, their practicality and safety in the context of triple-stented valves are as yet unproven.
