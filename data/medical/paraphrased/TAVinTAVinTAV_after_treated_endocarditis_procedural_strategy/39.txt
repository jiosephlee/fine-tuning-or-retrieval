Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the numerous technical complexities involved in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, therapeutic approaches should be individualized for each patient. We report the case of a man in his late 70s who had a complicated medical background, which included two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and a history of infective endocarditis. The patient subsequently developed significant degeneration of his aortic bioprosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter intervention. His initial treatment involved placement of a Lotus valve in 2014, which was succeeded by a TAV-in-TAV procedure using an Evolut prosthesis in 2022. Subsequent degeneration associated with endocarditis and marked restenosis occurred. In 2024, a third aortic prosthesis (Acurate neo2) was placed employing a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. As the use of TAVI expands and the survival of these patients increases, complex interventions involving multiple prosthetic valves are likely to be encountered more often. This underscores the importance of advanced procedural planning, optimized imaging modalities, and long-term monitoring strategies. At one-year follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic with normal prosthetic valve performance. This case demonstrates the viability of a third TAVI following treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, supported by CT-based procedural guidance and one-year clinical surveillance, an approach that is infrequently documented in current literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a widely accepted therapeutic approach for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, irrespective of their surgical risk category. With a growing patient population undergoing TAVI and improvements in longevity, structural degeneration of the prosthetic valve and the resultant necessity for repeat interventions are becoming more frequently observed in daily practice.

In this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have gained acceptance as an effective solution for dysfunctional transcatheter valves. Nevertheless, these procedures present unique anatomical and technical hurdles, such as the potential for coronary artery occlusion and a decrease in the effective orifice area that can result in significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These complexities are heightened in further redo procedures, including transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), due to the necessity of accommodating multiple overlapping valve frames.

There is a paucity of data pertaining to repeated transcatheter valve procedures, particularly among patients with antecedent prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In such complicated clinical scenarios, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, tailored risk stratification, and a collaborative multidisciplinary approach are critical to assess procedural viability and enhance outcomes.

The following case demonstrates the application of CT-based planning and strategy-focused transcatheter therapy in a patient with several previous TAVI operations and managed PVE, underscoring significant aspects of long-term structural valve disease management.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center presenting with shortness of breath, a tightness on the left side of his chest triggered by physical activity such as walking 200–300 meters, lightheadedness, and general fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: to the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat procedure for the LAD in 2012, and intervention on the circumflex artery in 2014. He also had third-degree atrioventricular block that necessitated pacemaker insertion in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding episodes starting in 2022.

In 2014, he underwent transcatheter aortic valve implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) due to progressive degeneration and severe stenosis of the aortic valve. By December 2021, his exertional dyspnoea worsened and echocardiography revealed severe bioprosthetic valve stenosis. Early in 2022, the mean valve gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, with left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) decreasing to 42%, down from 54% recorded in December.

A TAV-in-TAV procedure using a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was performed in May 2022. The intervention was complicated by a pulsatile femoral haematoma and formation of a pseudoaneurysm, both requiring open vascular surgery.

Post-procedure, the patient's left ventricular EF increased from 42% to 54%, signifying a positive early hemodynamic effect. His management included guideline-recommended medical therapies for heart failure with agents such as beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted again due to ongoing fever and deteriorating heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, resulting in a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete course of pathogen-directed antimicrobial treatment for PVE attributable to E. faecalis, consisting of six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based therapy, succeeded by oral antibiotics per the advice of infectious disease specialists. By the time of the third TAVI procedure, this antimicrobial regimen had been completed, and there was no ongoing suppressive antibiotic therapy. Repeated clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) corroborated the resolution of infection, with no findings consistent with active endocarditis.

Following completion of antimicrobial therapy and partial alleviation of symptoms, subsequent evaluation in January 2024 indicated deteriorating prosthetic valve function characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient measured 49 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) had improved to 66%.

The team opted to pursue further intervention. Upon hospital admission in June 2024, TTE revealed ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a substantially reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², indicating severe prosthetic valve malfunction. The AVA determination utilized the continuity equation, with measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler-based velocity-time integrals. Imaging showed two superimposed bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT documented a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, verifying pronounced haemodynamic impairment and informing the approach for a third transcatheter valve implantation (see figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was employed to assess anatomical suitability and the risk for coronary obstruction. This included measurements of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva parameters, valve-to-coronary proximity, and the interactions between the frames of previously inserted transcatheter valves. Special consideration was given to the interface between the anticipated neo-skirt and the coronary ostia, and the likelihood of successfully implanting the valve high to maintain coronary perfusion and future access. This data shaped valve selection, depth of implantation, and the procedure itself, enabling the intervention to occur without coronary protection measures.

Transoesophageal echocardiography performed immediately prior to the reintervention confirmed that there was no evidence of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate triple-stented anatomy, history of infective endocarditis, and high surgical risk, the third TAVI was scheduled as a computed tomography-guided, strategy-focused intervention with particular attention paid to coronary protection, commissural alignment, and maximizing the residual effective orifice area.

The intervention took place under local anesthesia, utilizing both left radial and left femoral arterial approaches. A pigtail catheter was used for aortography, which demonstrated severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was inserted into the left ventricle via the left femoral access. Balloon pre-dilation of the aortic valve was achieved with a 22/40 mm catheter while rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute was maintained. Concurrent aortography confirmed that the coronary circulation was unobstructed (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthesis (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed at the aortic valve position. Placement of the device was coordinated with the Evolut valve leaflets, guided by preprocedural CT modelling, to optimise the effective orifice area and decrease the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

Subsequent post-dilation was carried out with a 20/40 mm balloon during rapid pacing to ensure optimal valve expansion. The concluding aortography verified the device was accurately positioned, coronary flow was preserved, and there was no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Commissural alignment was carefully addressed during valve placement to maintain coronary accessibility and minimize future procedural complexity, guided by preprocedural CT findings and fluoroscopic references.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural efficacy and the functionality of the prosthetic valve were evaluated in accordance with Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, encompassing assessment of haemodynamic status, residual regurgitation, and procedure-associated adverse events.

A transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) performed directly after the intervention on 7 June 2024 demonstrated that the third transcatheter valve was successfully positioned. The prosthetic leaflets moved freely, achieving full opening, with no detectable aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Despite this, haemodynamic measurements revealed significant transvalvular obstruction, indicated by a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was calculated at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) measured 0.24, findings which are compatible with severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM).

One month following the procedure, TTE revealed notable improvement: the mean gradient decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA expanded to 1.1 cm². Remodeling of the left ventricle was evident, manifested by diminished chamber volumes. The absence of residual regurgitation was maintained, and valve function remained consistent, falling within the moderately stenotic category.

At 3.5 months post-intervention, the patient continued to experience no symptoms and preserved satisfactory exercise tolerance. There were no indications of heart failure or recurrent infection. The prosthetic valve sustained favourable function, with persistent enhancement in haemodynamic indices.

At the 1-year follow-up in June 2025, the patient was clinically stable and active, without signs of breathlessness or decompensation. TTE results showed a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA at 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. There was no evidence of valve-related adverse events, regurgitation, or recurrence of endocarditis.

These data confirm the lasting effectiveness of the intervention and indicate that the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach provided enduring functional improvement for this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This report underscores the complexities involved in clinical decision-making and technical execution when undertaking a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with two prior TAVI procedures and a previous episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is widely recognized as a standard therapy for aortic stenosis, its application in the context of previous endocarditis remains debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines on managing valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis advocate for surgical valve replacement in cases of PVE, especially where heart failure or persistent infection is present. Nonetheless, these guidelines also recommend tailoring the treatment approach by considering factors such as infection eradication, the risk associated with surgery, and anatomical suitability.

In 2014, TAVI was selected initially because the patient’s elevated surgical risk and multiple comorbid conditions made conventional surgery unsuitable. After developing structural degeneration of the valve, a second transcatheter intervention was performed in 2022 to improve valve function while circumventing the hazards of open-heart surgery. After successful PVE management and confirmation that no active infection was present, a third TAVI was considered in 2024 due to excessive surgical risk and the complexity of existing valve frameworks. Pre-procedural CT assessment confirmed the practicality of a further transcatheter approach aimed at maintaining coronary perfusion and ensuring satisfactory haemodynamic results.

The cause of prosthetic valve malfunction in this patient appeared to be multifactorial. Although a prior episode of infective endocarditis may have induced leaflet damage resulting in impaired mobility, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter prosthetic valves also significantly contributed. Notably, after infection was deemed resolved and no continued suppressive antibiotic treatment was indicated, the therapeutic approach to prosthetic valve dysfunction mirrored that used for individuals without current or persistent endocarditis. Consequently, decisions regarding intervention focused mainly on anatomical suitability, severity of haemodynamic compromise, and surgical risk, rather than solely on the patient’s past infective history.

For this patient, completion of antibiotic treatment was achieved, there were no symptoms or imaging evidence indicative of active infection, and comprehensive pre-procedure workup—including TEE and MSCT—demonstrated the absence of vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. The patient had a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, marked comorbidities such as COPD, diabetes, and prior gastrointestinal hemorrhage, as well as two previously implanted transcatheter valves with extensive stent incorporation into the aortic wall, all of which made conventional surgery particularly perilous and technically difficult. Due to the deep-seated nature of the existing valves, operative intervention would likely have required replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple case reports have described TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures,37 emphasizing the various anatomical and technical difficulties encountered. This particular case contributes to the scarce literature by offering the distinct scenario of valve deterioration following endocarditis, along with preserved valve function observed at one year.

The case highlights the increasing significance of lifelong management approaches in structural valve pathology. Each successive transcatheter intervention introduces additional complexity related to frame stacking, commissural positioning, accessing the coronaries, and maintaining adequate effective orifice area. Comprehensive planning and early conversations about future procedural pathways are essential, even during the initial intervention—especially for patients who are younger or have elevated risk profiles.

Structural deterioration in this patient consisted of leaflet fibrosis secondary to prior degeneration and likely mechanical impairment post-infection. The medical team elected to place a third valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis—after reviewing preprocedural CT imaging and 3D modelling, which confirmed feasibility and sufficient distance from the coronaries (refer to figures 1, 5, and 7). The third transcatheter valve was intentionally implanted at a high position. By deploying the device at the level of the Evolut valve’s leaflets, the team achieved fixation of existing leaflets against the stent structure, thereby preventing additional narrowing of the effective orifice area caused by inward folding of leaflets. Furthermore, this tactic reduced creation of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia and promoted ongoing coronary perfusion and potential for future access. Guidance from preprocedural CT was integral to realizing satisfactory haemodynamics within a triple-stented configuration. Even with the intricate nature of three transcatheter valves, this technique proved technically achievable and reproducible when supported by thorough preprocedural imaging and a well-defined plan for device placement.

The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen because it offers supra-annular leaflet positioning, a comparatively short frame, and provides favorable haemodynamic outcomes in restricted anatomical settings. Additionally, its structure facilitates commissural alignment, an important factor in this scenario to maintain future coronary access.

A major technical issue during TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV cases is the potential compromise of coronary access due to multiple overlapping stent frames. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the aggregated mesh density can still pose difficulties for subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of thorough procedural planning, particularly in patients with pre-existing coronary pathology or anticipated need for future access. Preprocedural CT scanning was utilized to measure the landing area, assess interactions between frames, and estimate distances from the valve to the coronary ostia; however, dedicated software for comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis was not used.

The elimination of the transvalvular gradient after the third valve placement is likely a result of optimal commissural positioning, successful post-dilatation, and partial mechanical widening of the prior Evolut stent, which contributed to enhanced leaflet mobility and improved central blood flow. Although deliberate frame fracture was not pursued, the use of high-pressure balloon post-dilation likely achieved additional radial expansion of the Evolut frame, thereby improving prosthetic valve performance.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label implantation of the Acurate neo2 valve. The patient, together with his family, participated in shared decision-making, and the case was deliberated in a multidisciplinary heart team meeting involving interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

In the event of early failure of the current prosthesis, therapeutic alternatives would be extremely limited. Surgical explantation would remain technically complex and associated with considerable risk. Novel catheter-based techniques—such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration (for example, BASILICA, bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction)—may be considered, though their viability and safety in a triple-stented environment remain to be established.
