Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V. Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the varied technical complexities involved in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions, therapeutic decisions must be individualized for each patient. We describe a man in his late 70s who had a complicated clinical background, including two prior transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI) and infective endocarditis, and subsequently developed significant degeneration of the aortic bioprosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. The patient originally received a Lotus valve in 2014 and later underwent a TAV-in-TAV procedure with an Evolut device in 2022, after which endocarditis-related degeneration and marked restenosis occurred. In 2024, an Acurate neo2 prosthesis was implanted using a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. With TAVI indications broadening and increasing survival among treated individuals, complex interventions involving multiple transcatheter prostheses are likely to become more common, underscoring the importance of enhanced procedural planning, advanced imaging, and diligent long-term follow-up. At the 1-year assessment, the patient remains symptom-free and valve function is maintained. This case demonstrates the practicality of performing a third TAVI procedure following treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, using CT-based procedural planning and 1-year surveillance, a scenario infrequently detailed in published reports.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a recognized therapeutic choice for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, regardless of surgical risk category. With rising numbers of TAVI procedures and enhanced long-term patient survival, clinicians are more frequently encountering cases of structural valve degeneration and the subsequent requirement for repeat procedures.

Within this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedures have become a practical option for addressing failed transcatheter valve implants. Nonetheless, these approaches present distinct anatomical and procedural difficulties, such as a heightened risk of obstructing the coronary arteries and a diminished effective orifice area, which can contribute to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These issues become increasingly complex in repeated interventions like transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the cumulative presence of multiple superimposed valve frames must be carefully evaluated.

There is a paucity of data on repeated transcatheter valve procedures, especially among individuals with prior prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these challenging cases, comprehensive imaging before the intervention, personalized evaluation of risks, and collaborative, multidisciplinary discussions are crucial for assessing procedural suitability and achieving optimal results.

This case demonstrates the significance of CT-guided procedural planning and a strategy-focused approach to transcatheter intervention in a patient with a history of several TAVI procedures and managed PVE, emphasizing important aspects for the long-term management of structural valve disease.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late 70s was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center due to shortness of breath, a spasmodic feeling on the left side of his chest triggered by exertion (specifically after walking 200–300 m), along with complaints of dizziness and tiredness.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, triple-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat LAD procedure in 2012, and the circumflex artery in 2014. Other significant comorbidities were third-degree atrioventricular block managed with pacemaker implantation in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding episodes beginning in 2022.

In 2014, the patient received a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) via transcatheter implantation because of advancing degeneration and critical stenosis of the aortic valve. By December 2021, there was a marked exacerbation of exertional dyspnea, and echocardiography confirmed significant stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. In early 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, and the ejection fraction had decreased to 42%, down from 54% recorded in December.

In May 2022, a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was placed via a TAV-in-TAV procedure. Following the intervention, the patient developed a pulsatile hematoma and pseudoaneurysm at the femoral site, necessitating open surgical vascular repair.

After undergoing the Evolut TAV-in-TAV procedure, the patient's left ventricular ejection fraction increased from 42% to 54%, demonstrating a positive initial haemodynamic outcome. His management included guideline-recommended medical therapy for heart failure, such as beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was re-admitted to the hospital due to ongoing fever and a deterioration of heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient measured at 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient was treated with a complete course of pathogen-directed antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis PVE, consisting of six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, followed by oral antibiotics as advised by infectious disease consultants. By the time the third TAVI was performed, antimicrobial therapy had concluded, and no maintenance suppressive antibiotics were in use. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) all supported clearance of infection, with no indications of ongoing endocarditis.

Upon finishing antibiotic treatment and observing some improvement in symptoms, a follow-up assessment in January 2024 demonstrated a decline in prosthetic valve performance, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean transvalvular gradient measured 49 mm Hg, while left ventricular ejection fraction had returned to 66%.

The team opted to undertake a repeat intervention. Upon admission in June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed ongoing moderate to moderately severe intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation (grade 2–3), a mean gradient across the valve of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly diminished aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², findings that indicated severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, incorporating measurements of the left ventricular outflow tract and velocity-time integrals derived from Doppler. Imaging identified two superimposed bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT identified a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, indicating significant haemodynamic impairment and informing the approach for a third transcatheter valve placement (see figure 1). Anatomical suitability and potential risk of coronary artery obstruction were evaluated with preprocedural MSCT, which involved examining the height of the coronary ostia, the dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, the valve-to-coronary distance, and the interaction among the frames of the pre-existing transcatheter valves. Special emphasis was given to assessing the expected relationship between the neo-skirt and the coronary ostia, as well as the possibility of implanting the valve at a higher position to maintain coronary flow and facilitate future coronary interventions. The results of these assessments influenced choices regarding valve type, implantation depth, and procedural strategy, enabling the procedure to proceed without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was performed prior to the reintervention and confirmed the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular complications.

Treatment:

Due to the patient’s intricate anatomy featuring three stents, history of infective endocarditis, and high risk associated with surgery, a third TAVI procedure was scheduled utilizing CT imaging for strategic planning, prioritizing preservation of coronary artery flow, commissural alignment, and maximization of the remaining effective orifice area.

The procedure was performed under local anaesthesia using left radial and left femoral arterial access. Aortography with a pigtail catheter confirmed severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the left femoral approach. The aortic valve underwent balloon pre-dilatation using a 22/40 mm catheter, with rapid ventricular pacing set at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography demonstrated persistent patency of the coronary arteries (figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (size S) was placed in the aortic location. The alignment of the device was performed to match the leaflets of the Evolut valve, guided by preprocedural CT modeling, in order to optimize the effective orifice area and minimize the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A 20/40 mm balloon was utilized for post-dilation with rapid pacing to achieve optimal valve expansion. Subsequent aortography demonstrated accurate placement, maintained coronary blood flow, and no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (see figure 4).

Valve deployment was performed with attention to commissural alignment, using preprocedural CT scans and fluoroscopic markers to maintain coronary access and minimize the difficulty of subsequent procedures.

Outcome and follow-up:

The assessment of procedural success and prosthetic valve function followed the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, encompassing analysis of hemodynamic performance, detection of any remaining regurgitation, and identification of complications related to the procedure.

A transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) carried out immediately following the intervention on 7 June 2024 verified proper placement of the third transcatheter valve. The leaflets displayed full mobility and opened completely, with no signs of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nevertheless, haemodynamic assessment revealed marked transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient measured at 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was found to be 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) measured 0.24, findings indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

One month following implantation, transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated significant improvement, with a mean gradient of 27 mm Hg and an aortic valve area expanded to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodeling was present, indicated by decreased chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was detected, and valve performance remained stable in the context of moderate stenosis.

At 3.5 months follow-up, the patient continued to exhibit no symptoms and demonstrated satisfactory exercise capacity. There was no evidence of heart failure or return of infection. The prosthetic device continued to function well, and haemodynamic measures remained stable and improved.

One year after implantation (June 2025), the patient continued to be stable and active, exhibiting no shortness of breath or indications of cardiac decompensation. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed a mean transvalvular gradient of 26 mm Hg, an aortic valve area measuring 1.1 cm², and a left ventricular ejection fraction of 65%. There were no complications associated with the valve, no regurgitation, and no recurrence of endocarditis detected.

These findings confirm the durability of the result and suggest a sustained functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention in this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This case highlights the decision-making process and technical challenges of performing a third TAVI in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV procedures and a history of PVE.

Although TAVI is now a well-recognized option for treating aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis is still debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for managing valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis recommend surgical valve replacement as the primary approach for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), especially in the presence of heart failure or persistent infection. Nevertheless, the guidelines highlight the importance of tailoring management to the individual, considering factors such as infection status, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

TAVI was initially selected as the management approach in 2014 due to the patient's elevated surgical risk and presence of several comorbidities, which rendered standard surgical valve replacement inappropriate at that point. When structural deterioration of the valve occurred later, a transcatheter redo procedure was chosen in 2022 to reestablish valve function and circumvent the hazards associated with open surgery. After the successful management of PVE and with no evidence of ongoing infection, a third transcatheter intervention was undertaken in 2024, guided by persistent high surgical risk, intricate anatomical features stemming from previously implanted valve frames, and pre-procedural CT confirmation that this approach would maintain coronary flow and provide satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

The prosthetic valve dysfunction observed in this patient appeared to result from several factors. Although the prior episode of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and contributed to mechanical impairment, advancing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valves was also a key contributor. Once infection was deemed resolved and extended suppressive antibiotic treatment was unnecessary, the approach to treating prosthetic valve dysfunction mirrored that used in cases without current or ongoing endocarditis. Consequently, the choice of procedure focused chiefly on anatomical considerations, the severity of haemodynamic compromise, and surgical risk, rather than solely on the patient’s earlier infection.

In this instance, the patient had finished a course of antimicrobial therapy and exhibited neither clinical evidence nor imaging findings indicative of ongoing infection. Comprehensive preprocedural assessments, which included transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT), corroborated the lack of vegetations or paravalvular pathology. The patient’s operative risk, as determined by a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, was heightened by comorbid conditions such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), diabetes mellitus, and a history of gastrointestinal bleeding. Additionally, the individual had previously received two transcatheter valve implants with substantial stent incorporation into the aortic wall, making conventional surgery exceptionally risky and technically complex. Owing to the deep anchoring of the earlier prosthetic valves, operative management would probably have required complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports have documented TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 noting diverse anatomical and technical difficulties. The present case further contributes to this scant literature, distinguished by the occurrence of degeneration following endocarditis and sustained valve performance after one year.

This report highlights the increasing significance of comprehensive, lifelong management approaches for structural valve pathology. Each subsequent transcatheter procedure introduces added complexities, including overlapping stent frames, achieving proper commissural orientation, maintaining coronary access, and preserving adequate residual effective orifice area. Therefore, proactive and early deliberation regarding procedural sequencing is essential—especially during the initial intervention—most notably for younger individuals or those at elevated risk.

In this patient, structural valve deterioration consisted of leaflet fibrosis from earlier degeneration and likely mechanical restriction post-infection. The care team elected to implant a third prosthesis—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 valve—following assessment with preprocedural CT and 3D modeling, which confirmed both feasibility and sufficient coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7). The third valve was intentionally deployed at a high position. By placing the new valve at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets, the pre-existing leaflets could be pinned against the stent frame, thus averting additional loss of effective orifice area due to leaflet infolding. Additionally, this technique helped to limit the development of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia, facilitating preserved coronary perfusion and future coronary access. Guidance from the preprocedural CT was pivotal, ensuring an optimal haemodynamic outcome despite the challenges posed by a triple-stented configuration. Though technically challenging, this approach was successful and could be replicated when supported by thorough imaging and a well-defined procedural plan.

The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet location, comparatively short frame, and advantageous haemodynamic characteristics in restricted anatomies. The device’s structure facilitates commissural alignment, an important factor in this situation to maintain the possibility of future coronary intervention.

An important technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions is the potential for compromised coronary access resulting from the superimposition of several stent frames. Even with efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the aggregate mesh may hinder subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of thorough procedural strategy, especially in individuals with existing coronary pathology or anticipated future need for coronary access. Preprocedural CT facilitated assessment of the implantation site, allowed evaluation of interactions between stent frames, and helped estimate valve-to-coronary distances, but a comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis with specialized software was not conducted.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve implantation is likely due to improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilatation, and partial mechanical widening of the existing Evolut frame, which together enhanced leaflet mobility and central hemodynamics. Although frame fracture was not deliberately attempted, it is probable that the use of a high-pressure balloon during post-dilatation resulted in additional radial expansion of the Evolut, leading to improved valve performance.

The intervention was carried out following institutional authorization for the off-label application of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis. A collaborative decision-making process included the patient and his family, and the case underwent discussion during a multidisciplinary heart team meeting with participation from interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

If the existing prosthesis were to experience early failure, the available therapeutic alternatives would be very restricted. Removing the device surgically would still pose significant technical challenges and substantial risk. Newer transcatheter approaches, including techniques like bioprosthetic valve fracture or deliberate leaflet laceration—such as the bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA) procedure—could be potential considerations; however, their safety and practicality have not been established in scenarios involving triple-stented anatomy.
