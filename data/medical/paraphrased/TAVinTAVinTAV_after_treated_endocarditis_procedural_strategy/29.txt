Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the various technical complexities inherent in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve interventions, the therapeutic approach must be individualized for each patient. Herein, we describe a patient in his late 70s with a challenging medical history, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI) and infective endocarditis, who subsequently experienced severe deterioration of his aortic valve prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. The initial treatment involved a Lotus valve in 2014; in 2022, a TAV-in-TAV procedure with an Evolut prosthesis was performed, followed by valve degeneration and significant restenosis associated with endocarditis. In 2024, an Acurate neo2 prosthetic valve was placed utilizing a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV technique. With widening indications for TAVI and enhanced survival rates among recipients, such intricate multi-valve interventions are likely to become more common, underscoring the importance of advanced procedural planning, improved imaging, and comprehensive long-term follow-up. After one year, the patient remains symptom-free with sustained valve function. This case demonstrates that a third TAVI following prosthetic valve endocarditis is achievable, with successful CT-based procedural planning and one-year outcome, an approach that is seldom documented in current literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now widely accepted as a treatment for severe aortic stenosis among patients with diverse levels of surgical risk. With increasing numbers of patients undergoing TAVI and improvements in long-term survival, clinicians are more frequently encountering structural valve deterioration and the subsequent need for additional procedures.

In these cases, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) approaches have become an effective option for managing transcatheter valve failure. Nevertheless, these procedures present unique anatomical and technical challenges, such as heightened risk of coronary artery obstruction and decreased effective orifice area, which can result in severe prosthesis-patient mismatch. These issues are exacerbated during sequential reinterventions—for instance, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV)—where the presence of multiple overlapping valve stents must be taken into account.

Available data on repeated transcatheter valve interventions remain sparse, especially for individuals with a previous diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In particularly complex cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, thorough individualized risk evaluation, and collaboration across specialties are crucial to assess procedural viability and achieve the best possible outcomes.

This case demonstrates the importance of CT-based procedural planning and a strategy-driven transcatheter intervention in a patient with several prior TAVI devices and successfully treated PVE, underscoring significant considerations for the long-term management of structural valve pathology.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after experiencing shortness of breath, a spastic feeling on the left side of his chest during exertion such as walking 200–300 m, along with dizziness and fatigue.

His prior medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and multiple percutaneous coronary interventions—specifically in 2009 for the right coronary artery, 2010 for the left anterior descending (LAD) artery, a repeat intervention on the LAD in 2012, and treatment of the circumflex artery in 2014. Other significant health issues encompassed third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker placement in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), as well as recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding beginning in 2022.

Progressive deterioration of the aortic valve and severe stenosis led to transcatheter placement of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) in 2014. By December 2021, the patient’s exertional dyspnoea intensified, and echocardiography confirmed severe stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. At the beginning of 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction had dropped to 42% from 54% noted previously in December.

In May 2022, a TAV-in-TAV procedure was carried out, during which a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was implanted. The procedure was complicated by the development of a pulsatile haematoma and femoral pseudoaneurysm, necessitating open surgical vascular intervention.

Post-Evolut TAV-in-TAV, the left ventricular ejection fraction increased from 42% to 54%, indicating early favorable haemodynamic improvement. The patient was subsequently treated with guideline-directed heart failure therapies, including beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as clinically appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted due to sustained fever and deterioration of heart failure manifestations. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures yielded Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of directed antimicrobial treatment for E. faecalis-associated PVE, which included 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based therapy, succeeded by oral therapy as advised by an infectious disease specialist. By the time the third TAVI procedure was scheduled, antimicrobial therapy had concluded and no prolonged suppressive antibiotics were administered. Clinical evaluation, repeat blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) all supported infection resolution, with no indications of ongoing endocarditis.

Subsequent to finishing antimicrobial therapy and partial improvement in symptoms, follow-up in January 2024 showed a decline in prosthetic valve performance, marked by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, while left ventricular ejection fraction had normalized at 66%.

A decision was taken to undertake another intervention. Upon hospital admission in June 2024, TTE revealed grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation persisted, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly reduced aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², indicative of advanced prosthetic valve dysfunction. The AVA calculation was performed using the continuity equation, utilizing measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler-obtained velocity-time integrals. Imaging showed two overlapping bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT identified a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, signifying pronounced haemodynamic impairment and serving to inform procedural strategy for a third transcatheter valve placement (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was employed to evaluate anatomical suitability and assess the risk of coronary artery obstruction. The analysis covered coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary separation, and frame interactions between previously placed transcatheter valves. Particular attention was paid to the projected neo-skirt’s proximity to the coronary ostia, alongside evaluating the possibility of high valve implantation to maintain coronary flow and future access. These assessments influenced valve selection, depth of implantation, and procedural approach, ultimately allowing intervention to proceed without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography conducted prior to reintervention verified the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient's intricate triple-stent configuration, history of infective endocarditis, and high operative risk, a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was scheduled, utilizing computed tomography guidance and a targeted approach focusing on coronary protection, proper commissural orientation, and maximization of residual effective orifice area.

The intervention was conducted under local anesthesia via left radial and left femoral arterial entry. Severe aortic regurgitation was verified by aortography with a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the left femoral access. The aortic valve was pre-dilated using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter while employing rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortographic imaging demonstrated maintained coronary flow (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthesis (Acurate neo2, size S) was placed in the aortic position. Device alignment with the Evolut valve leaflets was achieved based on preprocedural CT simulation to optimize effective orifice area and mitigate potential patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

Further balloon dilation with a 20/40 mm catheter under rapid pacing was carried out to refine valve expansion. Concluding aortography confirmed optimal placement, sustained coronary circulation, and absence of both paravalvular and transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Commissural orientation was prioritized during deployment to maintain future coronary access and minimize complexity, using guidance from preprocedural CT and fluoroscopic reference points.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and prosthetic valve function were evaluated based on Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 criteria, which encompassed assessment of haemodynamic performance, detection of residual regurgitation, and identification of complications related to the procedure.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) conducted immediately post-procedure on 7 June 2024 verified appropriate positioning of the third transcatheter valve. The prosthetic leaflets exhibited normal mobility and complete excursion, without evidence of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, haemodynamic measurements revealed elevated transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was calculated at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) measured 0.24—findings indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

At one month following implantation, TTE demonstrated significant haemodynamic improvement: mean gradient had decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA had increased to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodeling included reduced chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was detected, and valve performance remained consistent with moderate stenosis.

After 3.5 months, the patient continued to be asymptomatic, exhibiting robust exercise tolerance. There were no signs of heart failure or recurrent infection. The prosthetic valve preserved adequate function, with persistent enhancements in haemodynamic indices.

At the 1-year follow-up in June 2025, the patient was clinically stable and active, lacking dyspnoea or manifestations of decompensation. TTE demonstrated a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) at 65%. There were no valve-associated complications, absence of regurgitation, and no evidence of recurrent endocarditis.

These results support the long-term durability and continued clinical benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV strategy in a patient with elevated procedural risk.

Discussion:

This report underscores the clinical reasoning and technical obstacles encountered during a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient who previously underwent two TAV-in-TAV procedures and had a prior episode of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is now widely recognized as a standard therapy for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis remains debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to recommend surgical valve replacement in cases of PVE, especially when complicated by heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, the guidelines stress the need for individualized management, factoring in infection control, surgical risk assessment, and anatomical suitability.

In 2014, TAVI was selected initially owing to the patient’s elevated surgical risk profile and presence of significant comorbid conditions, rendering traditional surgical replacement unfavorable. With the emergence of structural valve deterioration, a transcatheter repeat intervention was performed in 2022, aiming to re-establish valve function without exposing the patient to the hazards associated with open-heart surgery. After resolution of PVE and in the absence of ongoing infection, the option for a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was chosen due to excessive surgical risk, intricate anatomical issues related to the existing valve structures, and computed tomography demonstrating the feasibility of an approach designed to preserve coronary perfusion and deliver satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this patient appeared to result from several contributing factors. Although a prior episode of infective endocarditis may have induced leaflet damage and subsequent mechanical limitation, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valve prostheses was also a major contributor. Once infection was deemed resolved, and lifelong suppressive antibiotics were not indicated, the approach to treating prosthetic valve dysfunction resembled that for patients without persistent endocarditis. Thus, procedural planning focused on anatomical suitability, haemodynamic urgency, and operative risk, rather than solely on the patient’s infectious history.

For this individual, antimicrobial therapy had been completed, and there was no evidence—clinical or on imaging—of an active infection. Comprehensive preprocedural assessment, including TEE and MSCT, confirmed the absence of vegetations or paravalvular issues. The patient exhibited a EuroSCORE II of 27.36%, with notable comorbidities including COPD, diabetes, and previous gastrointestinal hemorrhage, in addition to two earlier transcatheter prostheses displaying substantial stent incorporation within the aortic wall. These factors rendered conventional surgery extremely high-risk and technically complex. Owing to the deep integration of the earlier valves, operative management would likely have required replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple case reports describe TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures,37 emphasizing a range of anatomical and technical difficulties encountered during these interventions. The current case adds to the sparse literature, featuring the distinct scenario of valve deterioration following endocarditis and with maintained valve function observed at one-year follow-up.

This report highlights the increasing significance of lifelong management approaches for structural valve conditions. As repeat transcatheter procedures become more frequent, factors such as overlapping stent frames, proper commissural orientation, coronary access, and preservation of effective orifice area demand greater attention and complexity. Therefore, comprehensive planning and early discussion of procedural pathways are crucial from the initial intervention—especially in younger individuals or those at elevated risk.

In this patient, structural deterioration was characterized by leaflet fibrosis attributable to earlier degeneration, as well as possible mechanical constraint due to previous infection. Based on preprocedural CT and three-dimensional modelling confirming procedural suitability and sufficient coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7), the clinical team opted to implant a third valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 device. The third transcatheter valve was intentionally implanted high, positioned at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets. This placement fixed the prior leaflets against the stent frame, mitigating further narrowing of the effective orifice area from inward folding of the leaflets. Additionally, this high deployment minimized the development of a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, thus helping to maintain coronary perfusion and enabling potential future coronary interventions. Guided by detailed preprocedural CT evaluation, this strategy was critical for achieving favorable haemodynamics within the triple-stented configuration. Despite the procedural complexity associated with three transcatheter valves, the technique proved technically successful and reproducible when informed by comprehensive imaging and well-defined implantation planning.

The Acurate neo2 device was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet location, comparatively short frame height, and advantageous haemodynamic characteristics in constrained anatomies. Additionally, its design facilitates commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this scenario to maintain future coronary access.

An essential technical issue associated with TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions is the potential difficulty in coronary access arising from the overlapping stent frames. Although efforts were made to achieve commissural alignment, the overall increased mesh density can still create obstacles for subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of detailed procedural planning in patients with existing coronary disease or those who may require coronary access in the future. Preprocedure CT imaging was utilized to assess the landing area, evaluate interactions among stent frames, and measure the distance from the valve to the coronary arteries; however, full three-dimensional expansion evaluation with dedicated software was not conducted.

Resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve placement likely resulted from improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut frame, which enhanced leaflet movement and central blood flow. Although deliberate frame fracture was not executed, post-dilation with a high-pressure balloon presumably led to further radial widening of the Evolut frame, thus promoting optimal valve performance.

Institutional authorization was obtained for the off-label application of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis. Shared decision-making was undertaken with the patient and his family, and the case was discussed during a multidisciplinary heart team meeting comprised of interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesiologists.

If the current prosthesis were to fail prematurely, therapeutic alternatives would be severely constrained. Surgical removal would be technically complex and associated with significant risk. Novel catheter-based approaches such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration (for instance, BASILICA—bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to avert coronary artery obstruction) might be contemplated, though their efficacy and safety within a triple-stented setting remain uncertain.
