Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V. Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the numerous technical complexities associated with transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, therapeutic decisions must be individualized for each patient. We describe a man in his late 70s with a complicated medical background, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI) and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently developed significant degeneration of the aortic bioprosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. His initial treatment involved placement of a Lotus valve in 2014, followed by a TAV-in-TAV procedure utilizing an Evolut prosthesis in 2022. Degeneration and marked restenosis, attributed to endocarditis, prompted a third intervention in 2024, with insertion of an Acurate neo2 valve using the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. As the indications for TAVI continue to broaden and patients experience greater longevity post-procedure, the frequency of intricate multi-valve interventions is likely to increase, underlining the importance of enhanced procedural preparation, optimized imaging, and diligent long-term follow-up. The patient showed no symptoms and maintained good prosthetic valve function at one year post-intervention. This report highlights the practicality of performing a third TAVI in a case of previously treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-based procedural planning and 1-year post-procedural assessment, an approach infrequently documented in the medical literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a recognized therapeutic approach for individuals with severe aortic stenosis spanning various levels of surgical risk. With rising numbers of TAVI procedures performed and enhanced long-term patient survival, structural valve degeneration and subsequent need for repeat procedures are more commonly observed in routine care.

Within this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have become a practical approach to address dysfunctional transcatheter prosthetic valves. Nevertheless, these procedures present distinct anatomical and technical obstacles, such as the potential for coronary artery obstruction and diminished effective orifice area, which may lead to significant prosthesis–patient mismatch. These difficulties are exacerbated in cases of repeated interventions, including transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the presence of multiple superimposed valve frames requires meticulous consideration.

Data on repeated transcatheter valve interventions are still scarce, especially among individuals with prior prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these complicated cases, comprehensive imaging before the procedure, personalized evaluation of risks, and collaborative decision-making among various specialties are critical to assess procedural suitability and enhance outcomes.

This case demonstrates the importance of CT-based procedural planning and tailored transcatheter approaches in a patient with a history of multiple TAVI interventions and managed PVE, underscoring crucial aspects for long-term structural valve disease care.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center due to complaints of shortness of breath, a constricting feeling on the left side of his chest that occurred with exertion such as walking 200–300 meters, along with episodes of dizziness and general weakness.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension and three-vessel coronary artery disease, for which he underwent several percutaneous coronary interventions: the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat procedure on the LAD in 2012, and the circumflex artery in 2014. Additional conditions comprised third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker placement in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and repeated gastrointestinal bleeding episodes since 2022.

In 2014, progressive deterioration of the aortic valve with advanced stenosis necessitated transcatheter placement of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) in this patient. By December 2021, the patient experienced increased exertional dyspnoea, and echocardiography revealed findings compatible with significant stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. In early 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, while the ejection fraction had dropped further to 42%, compared to 54% recorded in December.

In May 2022, the patient had a transcatheter aortic valve-in-valve procedure utilizing a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). The intervention was complicated by the onset of a pulsatile hematoma and pseudoaneurysm at the femoral site, necessitating open surgical vascular repair.

After undergoing the Evolut TAV-in-TAV procedure, the patient exhibited an increase in left ventricular ejection fraction from 42% to 54%, reflecting an early positive haemodynamic outcome. He was treated with heart failure medical therapy consistent with current guidelines, which included beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months afterwards, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted due to ongoing fever and progressive symptoms of heart failure. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient measured at 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures returned positive for Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of targeted antibiotic therapy for E. faecalis-associated PVE, including six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, succeeded by oral antibiotics as directed by infectious diseases specialists. Prior to the third TAVI procedure, the prescribed antimicrobial regimen was concluded, and no ongoing suppressive antibiotic therapy was in use. Clinical evaluation, repeat blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) confirmed that the infection had resolved, with no indicators of active endocarditis present.

Upon completion of antibiotic treatment and experiencing some improvement in symptoms, a follow-up evaluation in January 2024 showed a decline in prosthetic valve performance, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean transvalvular gradient measured 49 mm Hg, and the left ventricular ejection fraction had returned to 66%.

A reintervention was planned. Upon admission in June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed ongoing moderate to moderately severe intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation (grade 2–3), a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly diminished aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², findings indicative of severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. The AVA was derived utilizing the continuity equation, relying on left ventricular outflow tract assessment and Doppler-based velocity-time integrals. Imaging demonstrated two superimposed bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT demonstrated that the Evolut prosthesis had a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm, indicating significant haemodynamic impairment and informing the approach for a planned third transcatheter valve-in-valve procedure (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT imaging was employed to evaluate the anatomical suitability of the intervention and to estimate the likelihood of coronary artery obstruction. This assessment entailed measuring the height of the coronary ostia, the dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, the valve-to-coronary distance, and examining how the frames of previously implanted transcatheter valves interacted. Special emphasis was placed on analyzing the projected position of the neo-skirt in relation to the coronary ostia, in addition to the possibility of a higher valve implantation aimed at maintaining coronary perfusion and future coronary access. These imaging results informed the choice of valve, the implantation level, and the overall procedural plan, making it possible to proceed without employing coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was performed prior to the reintervention and confirmed the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular complications.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate anatomy with three existing stents, history of infective endocarditis, and elevated risk associated with surgery, a third TAVI was scheduled using a CT-guided, strategy-focused approach prioritizing coronary protection, proper commissural alignment, and enhancement of the remaining effective orifice area.

The procedure was performed under local anaesthesia using left radial and left femoral arterial access. Aortography with a pigtail catheter confirmed severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through left femoral access. The aortic valve underwent balloon pre-dilatation using a 22/40 mm catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at a rate of 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography verified that coronary perfusion was maintained without obstruction (figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthetic valve, size S, was deployed in the aortic position. The prosthesis was positioned in alignment with the leaflets of the Evolut valve, guided by preprocedural CT modeling, to optimize the effective orifice area and minimize the potential for patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A 20/40 mm balloon was utilized for post-dilation under rapid pacing to enhance valve expansion. Subsequent aortography demonstrated appropriate valve placement, maintained coronary blood flow, and no evidence of either perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (see figure 4).

During valve implantation, commissural alignment was considered to maintain coronary accessibility and minimize the complexity of subsequent procedures, using preprocedural CT scans and fluoroscopic reference points for guidance.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and function of the prosthetic valve were evaluated based on the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, encompassing assessment of haemodynamic parameters, detection of any remaining regurgitation, and identification of complications related to the procedure.

A transthoracic echocardiogram conducted immediately post-procedure on 7 June 2024 demonstrated that the third transcatheter valve had been successfully implanted. The valve’s leaflets exhibited full mobility and opening, and there was no detectable aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, hemodynamic measurements revealed pronounced transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was measured at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, which is indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

At the one-month follow-up post-implantation, transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated significant progress: the mean gradient measured 27 mm Hg and the aortic valve area (AVA) had risen to 1.1 cm². Remodeling of the left ventricle was apparent, as evidenced by decreased chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was detected, and the prosthetic valve continued to function stably within the range of moderate stenosis.

At 3.5 months follow-up, the patient continued to exhibit no symptoms and demonstrated adequate exercise capacity. There was no evidence of heart failure or recurrence of infection. The prosthetic device functioned well, with persistent enhancements in haemodynamic measurements.

One year after the procedure (June 2025), the patient continued to be clinically stable and active, with no symptoms of dyspnoea or indications of heart failure. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed a mean transvalvular gradient of 26 mm Hg, an aortic valve area of 1.1 cm², and a left ventricular ejection fraction of 65%. There were no observed valve-related issues, regurgitation, or signs of recurrent endocarditis.

These findings confirm the durability of the result and suggest a sustained functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention in this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This case highlights the decision-making process and technical challenges of performing a third TAVI in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV procedures and a history of PVE.

Although TAVI is now a well-recognized option for treating aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis is still debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for treating valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to endorse surgical valve replacement as the preferred approach for prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), especially when patients present with heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, the guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management to the individual, with careful assessment of infection resolution, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

TAVI was initially selected as the treatment approach in 2014 due to the patient’s elevated surgical risk and numerous comorbidities, which rendered traditional surgical valve replacement an unfavorable option at that period. After the onset of structural valve deterioration, a repeat transcatheter procedure was undertaken in 2022 to re-establish valve function, thereby circumventing the hazards associated with open-heart surgery. With successful management of PVE and no evidence of ongoing infection, the rationale for proceeding with a third transcatheter intervention in 2024 included excessive surgical risk, complex anatomy due to existing valve structures, and CT imaging confirming the practicality of a procedure designed to maintain coronary artery patency and provide satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this patient was probably due to several contributing factors. Although the incident of infective endocarditis may have resulted in leaflet damage leading to mechanical impairment, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valves was also a major contributor. Notably, after the infection was deemed resolved and extended antibiotic suppression was unnecessary, the approach to addressing prosthetic valve dysfunction became comparable to that for patients without current or recent endocarditis. As a result, procedural choices were influenced chiefly by anatomical considerations, the degree of haemodynamic compromise, and operative risk, rather than the prior episode of infection itself.

In this instance, the patient had finished antimicrobial treatment and displayed neither clinical nor radiological evidence of persistent infection. Comprehensive preprocedural assessment was performed, including both TEE and MSCT, which each demonstrated no vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was calculated at 27.36%, with multiple notable comorbidities present—COPD, diabetes, and a history of gastrointestinal bleeding. The existence of two previously implanted transcatheter prostheses, both with substantial stent incorporation into the aortic wall, further increased the procedural risk of conventional surgery. Given how firmly the prior valves were anchored, surgical management would probably have required complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple case reports have documented TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 describing a range of anatomic and technical difficulties encountered. This report contributes further to the sparse literature, distinguished by the scenario of valve deterioration following endocarditis and maintained valvular performance at one-year follow-up.

This report highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive long-term management for structural valve disorders. Each subsequent transcatheter procedure introduces added complexities, including issues like multiple frame overlap, commissural orientation, access to coronary arteries, and the maintenance of an adequate residual effective orifice area. Establishing an extended management plan and initiating conversations about possible future interventions are essential from the outset, especially for younger individuals or those considered high-risk.

In this patient, leaflet fibrosis from earlier degeneration and likely mechanical restriction post-infection led to structural valve deterioration. The clinical team selected a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis for implantation as the third valve, utilizing preprocedural CT imaging and 3D modeling that confirmed procedural suitability and sufficient clearance from the coronaries (see figures 1, 5, and 7). The team intentionally adopted a high implantation technique for the third transcatheter valve. Placing the device at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets permitted the pre-existing leaflets to be fixed against the stent frame, effectively mitigating further loss of effective orifice area that could arise from leaflet infolding. Furthermore, this placement strategy reduced the development of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia, helping to maintain coronary perfusion and access for potential future interventions. The procedural approach was informed by preprocedural CT evaluation and was vital in achieving satisfactory hemodynamic performance within a triple-stented configuration. Although implanting three transcatheter valves presents considerable complexity, this technique was shown to be technically achievable and reproducible when underpinned by comprehensive preprocedural imaging and a carefully planned deployment strategy.

The Acurate neo2 device was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet placement, comparatively short frame height, and advantageous haemodynamic profile in restricted anatomies. Its construction also facilitates commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this instance to maintain options for future coronary access.

An important technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the potential for compromised coronary access resulting from several overlapping stent frames. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the increased amount of mesh can still complicate subsequent coronary interventions. This underscores the importance of thorough procedural planning, particularly in patients with existing coronary artery disease or where future coronary access is anticipated. Preprocedural CT scanning facilitated assessment of the landing site, analysis of stent frame interactions, and approximation of the valve-to-coronary distances; however, comprehensive three-dimensional expansion assessment was not conducted with specialized software.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third implantation likely resulted from a synergy of improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the existing Evolut frame, which together enhanced leaflet mobility and central hemodynamics. Although deliberate frame fracture was not pursued, high-pressure balloon post-dilation appears to have promoted additional radial expansion of the Evolut, thereby optimizing valvular performance.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label implantation of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis. The patient and his family participated in shared decision-making, and the case was evaluated during a multidisciplinary heart team discussion that included interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

In the event of early failure of the present prosthesis, available therapeutic alternatives would be severely constrained. Surgical removal would continue to pose substantial technical challenges and carry elevated risk. Newly developed percutaneous approaches, including procedures such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—such as bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—could be contemplated; however, their applicability and safety in cases with triple-stent layering have yet to be established.
