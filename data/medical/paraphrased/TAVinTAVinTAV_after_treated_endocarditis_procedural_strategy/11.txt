Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V. Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the various technical complexities encountered in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, management approaches should be customized for each patient. Here, we describe the case of a man in his late seventies with a complicated clinical background, which included two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently experienced severe degeneration of his aortic prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. The patient first received a Lotus valve in 2014, underwent a TAV-in-TAV implantation with an Evolut device in 2022, and subsequently developed prosthetic degeneration and severe restenosis attributed to endocarditis. In 2024, a third prosthesis (Acurate neo2) was placed using a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. As TAVI indications broaden and patient survival improves, complex interventions involving multiple prostheses are likely to occur more often, underscoring the importance of enhanced procedural planning, advanced imaging modalities, and ongoing long-term monitoring. At one-year post-procedure, the patient remains symptom-free with maintained valve performance. This report demonstrates the practicality of undertaking a third TAVI following treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, with support from CT-guided procedural planning and one-year follow-up—an outcome with limited prior documentation in the literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now widely recognized as a therapeutic approach for severe aortic stenosis in patients with varying degrees of surgical risk. With the expanding population receiving TAVI and enhanced long-term survival, clinicians are more frequently observing cases of structural valve degeneration and consequent requirements for additional procedures.

Within this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedures have become an accepted approach to address dysfunction of previously implanted transcatheter valves. Nonetheless, these treatments present distinct anatomical and procedural complexities, notably an increased risk of coronary artery obstruction and the potential for a diminished effective orifice area leading to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These issues are exacerbated with multiple repeat interventions, such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the presence of several superimposed valve stent frames demands particular attention.

There is a paucity of data on repeated transcatheter valve interventions, especially among individuals with previous prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these challenging cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, personalized risk evaluation, and a collaborative multidisciplinary approach are crucial for assessing procedural viability and enhancing outcomes.

This case demonstrates the importance of using CT-based planning and a strategy-focused transcatheter approach in a patient who had undergone several previous TAVI procedures as well as treatment for PVE, emphasizing critical factors relevant to long-term management of structural valve pathology.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center following complaints of shortness of breath, a spastic feeling localized to the left chest when engaging in physical activity such as walking 200–300 meters, as well as episodes of dizziness and general fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, significant three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: one to the right coronary artery in 2009, another to the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat intervention to the LAD in 2012, and a procedure involving the circumflex artery in 2014. He also had other notable comorbidities, such as a third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker placement in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding episodes beginning in 2022.

In 2014, as a result of worsening aortic valve degeneration with marked stenosis, the patient received a transcatheter Lotus valve (27 mm; Boston Scientific) implantation. By December 2021, the patient experienced increased exertional dyspnoea, and echocardiography demonstrated evidence of severe stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. In early 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient rose to 83 mm Hg, accompanied by a further reduction in ejection fraction (EF) to 42% from 54% in December.

In May 2022, the patient received a TAV-in-TAV procedure with placement of a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). Following the intervention, the patient experienced a pulsatile hematoma and femoral pseudoaneurysm, necessitating open vascular repair.

After the Evolut TAV-in-TAV procedure, the patient's left ventricular ejection fraction increased from 42% to 54%, reflecting an early positive haemodynamic effect. Guideline-directed medical therapy for heart failure was administered, consisting of beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted with ongoing fever and exacerbation of heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures grew Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of targeted antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis–associated PVE, consisting of 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based antibiotics, followed by oral therapy as advised by the infectious disease team. By the time of the third TAVI procedure, antimicrobial therapy had been concluded, with no ongoing long-term suppressive antibiotics. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) demonstrated resolution of the infection, with no indications of active endocarditis.

Following the conclusion of antibiotic treatment and some improvement in symptoms, a follow-up assessment in January 2024 demonstrated deterioration in prosthetic valve performance, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean pressure gradient measured 49 mm Hg, and the left ventricular ejection fraction had normalized to 66%.

It was decided to undertake another intervention. Upon admission in June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated ongoing grade 2–3 regurgitation within the prosthetic aortic valve, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly diminished aortic valve area (AVA) measured at 0.5 cm², indicating severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. The AVA was determined using the continuity equation, utilizing measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and velocity-time integrals obtained by Doppler. Imaging revealed two bioprosthetic valves situated in an overlapping fashion.

MSCT demonstrated a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut valve, indicating significant haemodynamic compromise and informing the procedural approach for a third transcatheter valve placement (figure 1). Anatomical suitability and the likelihood of coronary artery obstruction were assessed preprocedurally using MSCT. This evaluation encompassed measurement of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva size, valve-to-coronary distance, and assessment of how the prior transcatheter valve frames interacted. Special consideration was given to the projected position of the neo-skirt relative to the coronary ostia, as well as to the possibility of performing a high implantation to maintain coronary perfusion and facilitate subsequent coronary interventions. These assessments influenced the choice of valve, positioning, and overall procedural plan, enabling the procedure to proceed without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was performed prior to the reintervention and confirmed the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular complications.

Treatment:

Considering the patient's intricate anatomy with three prior stents, history of infective endocarditis, and unacceptably high operative risk, a third TAVI was scheduled utilizing a CT-guided, strategy-based approach focused on safeguarding the coronary arteries, achieving proper commissural alignment, and maximizing the remaining effective orifice area.

The procedure was performed under local anaesthesia using left radial and left femoral arterial access. Aortography with a pigtail catheter confirmed severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the left femoral approach. The aortic valve was pre-dilated using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter while rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute was applied. Concurrent aortography verified that coronary perfusion was not compromised (figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthetic valve, size S, was deployed in the aortic position. Utilizing preprocedural CT modelling, the device was oriented to match the leaflet position of the Evolut valve, aiming to optimize the effective orifice area and minimize the potential for patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A 20/40 mm balloon was utilized for post-dilation during rapid pacing to achieve optimal valve deployment. The concluding aortogram verified accurate valve placement, maintained coronary flow, and showed no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (see figure 4).

Commissural alignment was considered at the time of valve deployment in order to maintain access to the coronary arteries and decrease the difficulty of subsequent interventions, utilizing information from preprocedural CT scans and fluoroscopic reference points.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural success and prosthetic valve function were evaluated based on the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, which encompass assessment of haemodynamic parameters, detection of any residual regurgitation, and identification of complications associated with the procedure.

A transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) obtained immediately following the intervention on 7 June 2024 demonstrated that the third transcatheter valve was successfully positioned. The prosthetic valve leaflets exhibited good mobility and fully opened, and there was no detectable aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nonetheless, the haemodynamic assessment revealed markedly elevated transvalvular pressure gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) measured 0.9 cm², with an indexed AVA of 0.4 cm²/m², and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, findings indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

At one month post-implantation, transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated significant progress: the mean gradient measured 27 mm Hg, and the aortic valve area had enlarged to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodeling was present, indicated by decreased chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was detected, and the prosthetic valve maintained function consistent with moderate stenosis.

At 3.5 months follow-up, the patient exhibited no symptoms and demonstrated adequate exercise capacity. There was no evidence of heart failure or infectious recurrence. The prosthetic device continued to function well, and haemodynamic measurements remained stable and improved.

One year after the implantation (June 2025), the patient continued to be stable and physically active, exhibiting no dyspnea or indications of decompensation. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, an aortic valve area measuring 1.1 cm², and a left ventricular ejection fraction of 65%. There were no observed valve-associated complications, no regurgitation, and no signs of recurrent endocarditis.

These findings confirm the durability of the result and suggest a sustained functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention in this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This case highlights the decision-making process and technical challenges of performing a third TAVI in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV procedures and a history of PVE.

Although TAVI is now a recognized option for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis is still debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis advocate for surgical valve replacement as the preferred approach in prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), especially when there are complications such as heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, these guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management to each patient, considering factors such as infection resolution, operative risk, and the suitability of the patient's anatomy.

The initial use of TAVI in 2014 was selected due to the patient’s elevated surgical risk and several comorbidities, which rendered standard surgical valve replacement unfavorable at that stage. When the patient later experienced structural valve deterioration, a transcatheter redo procedure was carried out in 2022 to re-establish valve function while minimizing the dangers linked to open-heart surgery. After successfully managing PVE and confirming the absence of ongoing infection, a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was chosen, given the exceedingly high surgical risk and the complexity associated with previous valve frames. Additionally, CT imaging confirmed that a planned approach preserving coronary flow and achieving satisfactory haemodynamics was viable.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this case was probably due to multiple factors. Although the occurrence of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and resulting mechanical restriction, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valve prostheses was also a major contributor. Notably, after infection had been deemed cured and there was no need for prolonged suppressive antibiotic treatment, the approach to managing the prosthetic valve dysfunction followed protocols used for patients without current or persistent endocarditis. As a result, procedural choices were determined chiefly by anatomical suitability, the degree of haemodynamic compromise, and surgical risk assessment, rather than solely by the previous infectious episode.

In this instance, the patient had finished the course of antibiotics and exhibited neither clinical nor radiological evidence of ongoing infection. A comprehensive preprocedural workup was performed, including TEE and MSCT, both of which excluded the presence of vegetations or paravalvular pathologies. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, reflecting a high operative risk, and the medical history included substantial comorbidities such as COPD, diabetes, and previous gastrointestinal bleeding. Two transcatheter valve prostheses had already been placed, both showing marked stent incorporation into the aortic wall, which made conventional surgery exceptionally risky and technically complex. The firm anchorage of the prior prostheses suggested that surgical management would probably involve replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

A number of case reports describing TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions have been documented,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and technical obstacles. Our report contributes additional data to this sparse literature, distinguished by the context of degeneration following endocarditis and maintained valve function after one year.

This case highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive lifetime management approaches for patients with structural valve disorders. Each successive transcatheter reintervention introduces greater complexity regarding factors like multiple frame layers, alignment of commissures, preservation of coronary access, and maintenance of an adequate residual effective orifice area. Careful long-term planning and proactive conversations about future procedural options are essential, beginning as early as the initial intervention—especially for individuals who are younger or present with higher risk profiles.

In this patient, structural valve degeneration was characterized by leaflet fibrosis due to previous deterioration and likely mechanical limitation post-infection. The clinical team elected to implant a third valve, specifically a self-expanding Acurate neo2 prosthesis, after preprocedural CT imaging and 3D modeling confirmed its feasibility and sufficient coronary clearance (figures 1, 5, and 7). A high deployment of the third transcatheter valve was intentionally chosen. Positioning the prosthesis at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets secured the existing leaflets against the stent frame, which helped to avoid additional narrowing of the effective orifice area by preventing inward leaflet folding. This method also reduced the risk of forming a neo-skirt below the coronary ostia, supporting both the maintenance of coronary flow and access for potential future interventions. The choice of technique was informed by careful preprocedural CT analysis and was crucial for attaining a satisfactory haemodynamic outcome in an anatomy with three stented valves. Although implanting three transcatheter valves is a complex procedure, this approach was shown to be technically possible and reproducible when employing comprehensive preprocedural imaging and a clearly defined implantation strategy.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet placement, comparatively short frame profile, and advantageous haemodynamic properties in narrow anatomical settings. Additionally, its structure facilitates commissural alignment, an important factor in this instance to maintain options for subsequent coronary interventions.

An important technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the potential for compromised coronary access resulting from the presence of multiple superimposed stent frames. Even though commissural alignment was pursued, the aggregate mesh density could still complicate subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of careful procedural planning, particularly in individuals with established coronary artery disease or those who may require future coronary access. Preprocedural CT was utilized to measure the landing area, assess interactions between frames, and estimate the distance between the valve and coronary ostia, though a comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis using specialized software was not conducted.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third device implantation is likely due to a combination of improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the existing Evolut stent frame, all of which facilitated better leaflet movement and central hemodynamics. Although intentional frame fracturing was not attempted, the use of high-pressure balloon post-dilation probably promoted additional radial dilation of the Evolut, resulting in improved valvular performance.

Institutional authorization was obtained for the off-label application of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis prior to the procedure. The patient and his family participated in shared decision-making, and the case underwent discussion at a multidisciplinary heart team conference that included interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

If the present prosthesis were to experience early failure, the available therapeutic options would be quite restricted. Removing the device surgically would continue to pose significant technical challenges and involve substantial risk. Newer catheter-based approaches, such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—examples include bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—could be contemplated, though their practicality and safety in the context of a triple-stented arrangement have not yet been clearly established.
