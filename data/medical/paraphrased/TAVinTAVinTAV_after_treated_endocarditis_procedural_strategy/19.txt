Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Owing to the range of technical complexities inherent in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, therapeutic approaches should be individualized based on patient-specific factors. We describe the case of a man in his late 70s with a complicated medical history—including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and a past episode of infective endocarditis—who subsequently developed significant aortic prosthesis deterioration necessitating a third transcatheter intervention. The initial intervention was with a Lotus valve in 2014; in 2022, a TAV-in-TAV using an Evolut prosthesis was performed, after which prosthetic degeneration and severe restenosis secondary to endocarditis occurred. In 2024, a third device (Acurate neo2) was placed via a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. With broader indications for TAVI and increasing life expectancy among treated individuals, the frequency of complex multi-prosthesis procedures is likely to rise, emphasizing the importance of enhanced procedural planning, optimized imaging, and robust long-term follow-up strategies. At 1 year post-procedure, the patient is asymptomatic with maintained valve performance. This report demonstrates the viability of a third TAVI after managing prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-based planning and one-year follow-up, a scenario infrequently documented in existing literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a recognized therapeutic option for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, regardless of their surgical risk. With more patients receiving TAVI and improvements in long-term outcomes, clinicians are increasingly confronted with structural valve degeneration and subsequent requirements for reintervention.

Under these circumstances, the transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) approach has been adopted as a practical method for addressing malfunctioning transcatheter prosthetic valves. Nonetheless, these procedures involve distinct anatomical and procedural difficulties, such as the potential for coronary artery obstruction and a diminished effective orifice area, which may cause significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These issues become more pronounced during sequential interventions—such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV)—where consideration of multiple overlapping valve frames is critical.

Data regarding multiple serial transcatheter valve procedures are scarce, especially in populations with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these complex cases, thorough preprocedural imaging, personalized risk evaluation, and interdisciplinary collaboration are crucial for assessing procedure viability and optimizing results.

The present case demonstrates the importance of computed tomography (CT) based planning and a strategy-focused transcatheter procedure in a patient who previously underwent multiple TAVI interventions and was treated for PVE, emphasizing key aspects relevant to the lifelong management of structural valve pathology.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after experiencing shortness of breath, a spasmodic feeling on the left side of his chest during physical activity (while walking 200–300 meters), dizziness, and fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and multiple percutaneous coronary interventions: the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat LAD procedure in 2012, and intervention on the circumflex artery in 2014. He also had third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker placement in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurring gastrointestinal bleeding episodes since 2022.

In 2014, due to progressive aortic valve deterioration and critical stenosis, a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) was implanted via transcatheter approach. By December 2021, his exertional dyspnoea had intensified, and echocardiography demonstrated marked bioprosthetic valve stenosis. By early 2022, his mean gradient had risen to 83 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) had dropped to 42%, down from 54% in December.

A TAV-in-TAV procedure was performed in May 2022 with placement of a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). This intervention was complicated by the onset of a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm, which required open vascular repair.

After receiving the Evolut TAV-in-TAV, the patient's left ventricular EF increased from 42% to 54%, reflecting a positive early hemodynamic effect. He was treated according to heart failure guidelines, which included beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as needed.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted once more due to ongoing fever and deteriorating heart failure. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, establishing a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of targeted antibiotics for E. faecalis-related PVE, comprising six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, subsequently followed by oral therapy as per infectious disease specialist guidance. By the time of the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), antibiotic therapy was finished and no chronic suppressive antibiotics were required. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice computed tomography (MSCT) verified clearance of infection, with no signs of ongoing endocarditis.

After the antimicrobial regimen was completed and some symptoms improved, follow-up in January 2024 showed progressive dysfunction of the prosthetic valve, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe aortic regurgitation. The mean gradient increased to 49 mm Hg, and the left ventricular ejection fraction had recovered to 66%.

Reintervention was subsequently decided upon. Upon admission in June 2024, TTE indicated continuing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a substantially reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², indicative of severe valve dysfunction. The AVA was calculated via the continuity equation, utilizing left ventricular outflow tract measurements and Doppler velocity-time integral data. Imaging depicted two overlapping bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT showed the Evolut prosthesis possessed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm, confirming pronounced haemodynamic compromise, and informed planning for the upcoming third transcatheter valve implantation (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was utilized to evaluate anatomical suitability and risk of coronary artery obstruction. The assessment encompassed measurements of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva size, valve-to-coronary distance, and interactions among the frames of previously implanted transcatheter valves. Careful attention was given to the correlation between the anticipated neo-skirt and coronary ostia, as well as the viability of high implantation to maintain coronary flow and ensure future coronary access. These anatomical considerations influenced the choice of prosthesis, implantation depth, and procedural approach, permitting valve placement without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography prior to reintervention corroborated the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular abnormalities.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate anatomy with three existing stents, history of infective endocarditis, and extremely high surgical risk, a third TAVI was scheduled as a computed tomography-guided, strategy-focused intervention prioritizing coronary preservation, commissural positioning, and enhancement of the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was carried out under local anesthesia, employing both left radial and left femoral arterial access. Severe aortic regurgitation was verified by aortography using a pigtail catheter (see figure 2).

Through the left femoral approach, a Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle. The aortic valve underwent pre-dilation with a 22/40 mm balloon catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography demonstrated unobstructed coronary flow (see figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (size S) was deployed in the aortic location. The device was oriented with the Evolut valve leaflets, using preprocedural CT guidance, to maximize the effective orifice area and minimize the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

To further optimize valve expansion, post-dilation was carried out using a 20/40 mm balloon with rapid pacing. Final aortography confirmed accurate valve placement, maintained coronary circulation, and no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (see figure 4).

During valve placement, commissural alignment was considered to facilitate future coronary access and decrease procedural complexity, with guidance provided by preprocedural CT imaging and fluoroscopic anatomical markers.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and valve performance were evaluated in accordance with the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, which included assessment of haemodynamic metrics, residual regurgitation, and procedure-related adverse events.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) performed immediately following the intervention on 7 June 2024 verified successful placement of the third transcatheter prosthesis. The prosthetic leaflets demonstrated normal mobility with full excursion and absence of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Despite this, haemodynamic measurements revealed markedly elevated transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was measured at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicating severe patient–prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

One month following implantation, TTE demonstrated significant improvement: mean transvalvular gradient decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA increased to 1.1 cm². Echocardiographic evidence indicated left ventricular remodeling, reflected by reduced chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was present, and prosthetic function remained stable within a moderately stenotic category.

At 3.5 months post-procedure, the patient continued to exhibit absence of symptoms with robust exercise capacity. There were no signs suggestive of heart failure or recurrent infection. The implanted valve preserved its functional integrity, with ongoing improvement in haemodynamic values.

At the 1-year follow-up (June 2025), the patient was clinically well, active, and without shortness of breath or evidence of cardiac decompensation. TTE showed a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. No prosthesis-related complications, regurgitation, or recurrent endocarditis were detected.

Collectively, these results validate the lasting effectiveness and functional improvement achieved with the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV strategy in this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This case illustrates the complexities involved in clinical decision-making and procedural execution when considering a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV interventions and a previous history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is a well-established therapy for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a background of endocarditis continues to be debated. The 2025 guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) regarding the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis recommend surgical valve replacement as the preferred approach for PVE, particularly when complicated by heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, these guidelines stress the importance of tailoring management to the individual patient, accounting for factors such as infection control, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

The initial selection of TAVI in 2014 was influenced by the patient’s considerable operative risk and significant comorbidities, rendering standard surgical replacement less desirable. With the subsequent emergence of structural valve degeneration, a repeat transcatheter approach was chosen in 2022 to re-establish valve competence while minimizing the hazards associated with open-heart surgery. After successful resolution of PVE and confirmation of the absence of ongoing infection, the rationale for a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was predicated on unacceptably high surgical risk, intricate anatomical challenges related to the pre-existing valve structures, and pre-procedural CT demonstrating the practicality of a plan prioritizing both maintenance of coronary perfusion and satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes.

The underlying causes of prosthetic valve dysfunction in this patient were likely complex. Although the incident of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and subsequent restriction of movement, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valve devices was also a significant contributor. Notably, after infection was deemed resolved and no continuing suppressive antibiotics were necessary, management of the prosthetic valve failure paralleled that of cases without active or persistent endocarditis. Consequently, procedural choices were primarily informed by anatomical suitability, the severity of haemodynamic compromise, and surgical risk, rather than solely the patient’s history of infection.

For this patient, antimicrobial therapy had been completed, and there were no clinical or imaging indications of continuing infection. Comprehensive preprocedural assessment was conducted—including TEE and MSCT—which verified there were no vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was calculated at 27.36%, with significant comorbid conditions such as COPD, diabetes, and previous gastrointestinal bleeding. The existence of two prior transcatheter valves with pronounced stent attachment to the aortic wall further heightened both technical and operative risk for conventional surgery. The extent of device fixation meant that surgical replacement would probably have required replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports detailing TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions have been documented,37 each describing distinct anatomical and procedural difficulties. The present case expands upon this limited literature, specifically within the context of degeneration following endocarditis and with sustained valve function observed at 1-year follow-up.

This case highlights the increasing significance of comprehensive, lifetime approaches to managing structural valve disease. As transcatheter repeat procedures are performed, issues such as frame layering, commissural alignment, coronary accessibility, and maintenance of the residual effective orifice area become progressively more challenging. It is imperative to plan ahead and engage in early discussions regarding procedural paths, starting as early as the initial intervention—especially for younger patients or those at elevated risk.

In this instance, the structural deterioration encompassed leaflet fibrosis from previous valve degeneration combined with probable mechanical impairment after infection. The team elected to implant a third prosthetic valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2—following preprocedural CT and 3D modelling that confirmed feasibility and ensured adequate coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7). A high placement of the third transcatheter valve was intentionally chosen. Positioning the device at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets secured the previous leaflets against the stent frame, thus averting further decrease in effective orifice area from leaflet inward folding. Additionally, this technique reduced formation of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia and helped maintain coronary perfusion and potential future access. The chosen approach was based on preprocedural CT analysis, and proved essential to achieving satisfactory haemodynamics in a triple-valve setup. Despite the intricate nature of a three-layered transcatheter valve configuration, the method was technically practical and reproducible given thorough preprocedural imaging and a clearly defined implantation plan.

The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen for its supra-annular leaflet orientation, relatively short frame, and advantageous haemodynamic properties suited to narrow anatomies. Its construction facilitates commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this scenario to maintain potential for future coronary access.

In TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures, a key technical challenge is the risk that multiple overlapping stent frames may impede coronary access. Despite efforts at achieving commissural alignment, the cumulative mesh layers may still hinder subsequent coronary interventions, underscoring the importance of careful procedural planning in patients with established coronary disease or for whom future access is critical. Preprocedural CT was utilized to measure the landing zone, assess the interaction between valve frames, and estimate distances between the valve and coronary ostia, though a comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis using specialized software was not undertaken.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following implantation of the third valve likely resulted from optimized commissural alignment, effective post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut frame, which enhanced leaflet mobility and central flow. Although frame fracture was not intentionally performed, the use of high-pressure balloon post-dilation probably further expanded the radial diameter of the Evolut, thereby improving overall valve function.

The intervention was conducted under institutional authorization for off-label application of the Acurate neo2 device. Decisions were made collaboratively with the patient and his family, and the case was evaluated by a multidisciplinary heart team, comprising interventional cardiologists, cardiothoracic surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesiologists.

Should the current prosthesis experience early failure, therapeutic options would be severely restricted. Surgical explantation would be highly technical and associated with significant risk. Novel catheter-based techniques such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration procedures—like bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to avert iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—may be considered; however, their effectiveness and safety in the context of three overlapping stent frames remain unclear.
