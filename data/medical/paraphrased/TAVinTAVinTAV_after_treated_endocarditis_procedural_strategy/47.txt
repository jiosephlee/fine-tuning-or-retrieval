Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the various technical complexities inherent in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, management approaches should be individualized for each patient. We describe the case of a man in his late seventies with an intricate medical background, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) surgeries and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently developed significant deterioration of his aortic prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter intervention. The patient initially received a Lotus valve in 2014, underwent TAV-in-TAV implantation with an Evolut valve in 2022, and then experienced valve degeneration due to endocarditis and advanced restenosis. In 2024, a third prosthetic valve (Acurate neo2) was delivered using the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV method. As indications for TAVI broaden and patient survival increases, such complex multi-prosthesis interventions are likely to occur more frequently, emphasizing the importance of enhanced procedural planning, optimized imaging, and thorough long-term follow-up. At one-year post-procedure, the patient remains asymptomatic, exhibiting sustained valve performance. This case demonstrates the practicality of performing a third TAVI following treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-based procedural guidance and one-year follow-up, an approach rarely documented in published reports.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a well-established therapy for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, encompassing patients with a wide spectrum of surgical risks. With rising TAVI volumes and enhanced long-term survival, clinicians are more frequently confronted with structural valve degeneration and the necessity for subsequent interventions.

Within this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have become a practical approach for treating dysfunctional transcatheter valves. Nevertheless, these procedures present unique anatomical and technical obstacles, such as the possibility of coronary artery obstruction and diminished effective orifice area, which may lead to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These complexities are heightened during additional reinterventions—such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV)—where navigating multiple nested valve frames requires meticulous planning.

Data on repeated transcatheter valve procedures, especially for patients with antecedent prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), are scant. In such challenging cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, tailored risk evaluation, and a multidisciplinary team approach are vital to assess procedural suitability and improve outcomes.

This report demonstrates the importance of CT-based procedural planning and strategic transcatheter therapy in a patient with a history of multiple TAVI operations and resolved PVE, underscoring crucial aspects relevant to the long-term management of structural valve disease.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after experiencing shortness of breath, a spasmodic sensation on the left side of his chest during physical activity (walking 200–300 meters), along with dizziness and fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: the right coronary artery in 2009, the left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, a repeat LAD intervention in 2012, and the circumflex artery in 2014. Other significant conditions were third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker implantation in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and repeated gastrointestinal bleeding episodes since 2022.

Progressive aortic valve degeneration and severe stenosis led to transcatheter implantation of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) in 2014. By December 2021, the patient’s exertional dyspnoea had intensified, and echocardiography showed severe bioprosthetic valve stenosis. By early 2022, the mean gradient across the valve had risen to 83 mm Hg, with a drop in ejection fraction (EF) from 54% (in December) to 42%.

In May 2022, a TAV-in-TAV procedure was performed using a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic), but the patient developed a pulsatile femoral hematoma and pseudoaneurysm post-procedure, necessitating open vascular surgery.

After the Evolut TAV-in-TAV implantation, left ventricular EF improved from 42% to 54%, demonstrating a positive initial hemodynamic effect. The patient received guideline-based medical therapy for heart failure, including beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as clinically indicated.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted again due to persistent fever and worsening signs of heart failure. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean pressure gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, resulting in a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient completed a full regimen of targeted antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis PVE, including six weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based treatment, succeeded by oral antibiotics per recommendations from infectious disease specialists. By the time of the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), the antimicrobial course was finished, and no chronic suppressive antibiotic regimen was being administered. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) all indicated resolution of infection, with no evidence suggesting ongoing endocarditis.

Following antimicrobial therapy and partial improvement of symptoms, a follow-up assessment in January 2024 showed deterioration in prosthetic valve function, characterized by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean gradient measured 49 mm Hg, while the left ventricular ejection fraction had recovered to 66%.

A reintervention was planned. Upon admission in June 2024, TTE revealed ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly reduced aortic valve area (AVA) of 0.5 cm², indicative of severe prosthetic valve dysfunction. AVA estimation used the continuity equation based on measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler-acquired velocity-time integrals. Imaging demonstrated two overlapping bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT identified a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm in the Evolut valve prosthesis, confirming severe haemodynamic impairment and informing planning for the third transcatheter valve implantation (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT assessed anatomical suitability and the risk for coronary artery obstruction, evaluating coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance, and the interaction between the frames of previously implanted valves. Specific focus was placed on the predicted neo-skirt's relationship to the coronary ostia, along with evaluating the possibility of high valve implantation to maintain coronary flow and permit future coronary access. The findings informed choices regarding valve selection, implantation depth, and procedural approach, enabling the intervention to proceed without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was conducted before reintervention, which verified the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient's challenging anatomy with three prior stents, history of infective endocarditis, and significant operative risk, the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was planned utilizing computed tomography guidance, focusing on coronary protection, proper commissural alignment, and maximization of residual effective orifice area.

The intervention was conducted under local anesthesia with arterial access obtained via the left radial and left femoral sites. Severe aortic regurgitation was verified through aortography employing a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle from the left femoral access. The aortic valve underwent pre-dilation using a 22/40 mm balloon catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography demonstrated that coronary blood flow remained unaffected (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed in the aortic position. Placement was guided by preprocedural CT modeling to align the device with the existing Evolut valve leaflets, aiming to enhance the effective orifice area and minimize the probability of patient-prosthesis mismatch.

A 20/40 mm balloon was utilized for post-dilation under rapid pacing in order to ensure optimal valve expansion. Final aortography demonstrated appropriate valve location, maintained coronary circulation, and confirmed both perivalvular and transvalvular regurgitation were absent (figure 4).

Commissural alignment was addressed during valve implantation to maintain coronary access and lessen potential procedural difficulty in the future, using preprocedural CT scans and fluoroscopic markers for guidance.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcome and valve function were evaluated using Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 standards, which included assessment of haemodynamic metrics, detection of residual regurgitation, and procedure-associated adverse events.

Immediately following the intervention on 7 June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) confirmed correct placement of the third transcatheter prosthesis. The leaflets appeared mobile and fully opened, with no indication of aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Nevertheless, haemodynamic measurements revealed elevated transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The calculated aortic valve area (AVA) measured 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) was 0.24, indicating severe patient–prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

TTE at 1 month post-procedure demonstrated significant improvement: the mean gradient had decreased to 27 mm Hg and AVA had expanded to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodelling was noted, characterized by reduced chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was present, and prosthetic valve performance remained stable, falling within the moderately stenotic category.

At 3.5 months, the patient experienced no symptoms and tolerated physical activity well. There were no clinical signs of heart failure or relapse of infection. The prosthesis continued to function effectively, with persistent enhancement of haemodynamic indices.

By 1 year after implantation (June 2025), the patient stayed clinically well and active, exhibiting neither dyspnoea nor evidence of cardiac decompensation. TTE demonstrated a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA of 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. No prosthesis-related issues, regurgitation, or recurrent endocarditis were observed.

These results provide confirmation of the long-term durability of the intervention and indicate sustained improvement in function following TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV placement in this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This case underscores the complexity of decision-making and procedural difficulties encountered when performing a third TAVI in a patient previously treated with TAV-in-TAV and a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI has become a standard approach for managing aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with previous endocarditis remains subject to debate. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines on the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to advocate surgical valve replacement as the preferred option in PVE, particularly in the presence of heart failure or persistent infection. Nevertheless, the guidelines stress the importance of tailoring the therapeutic strategy to the patient, considering infection resolution, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

In 2014, the patient’s considerable operative risk and multiple concurrent health issues led to selecting TAVI instead of traditional surgical valve replacement. After structural valve degeneration emerged, a transcatheter redo intervention was performed in 2022 to reestablish valve function, thereby avoiding open surgical risks. Following successful PVE therapy and absence of ongoing infection, a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was chosen based on unacceptably high surgical risk, intricate anatomical factors related to prior valve implants, and CT findings supporting the feasibility of an approach intended to maintain coronary patency and deliver satisfactory haemodynamics.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this patient appeared to result from multiple contributing factors. Although the previous occurrence of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and subsequent mechanical limitation, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valves was also a significant component. Notably, after infection was deemed eradicated and no continuing antibiotic therapy was indicated, the management approach for prosthetic valve dysfunction was the same as for patients without active endocarditis. Consequently, procedural planning was determined mainly by anatomical suitability, the severity of haemodynamic compromise, and surgical risk assessment rather than the antecedent infection alone.

In this instance, the patient had successfully completed a course of antimicrobial treatment, lacked both clinical and imaging evidence of active infection, and underwent comprehensive preprocedural assessment with TEE and MSCT, which excluded vegetations or paravalvular abnormalities. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was 27.36% and was complicated by significant underlying conditions, including COPD, diabetes, a history of gastrointestinal bleeding, as well as the presence of two previously deployed transcatheter valves with extensive stent incorporation into the aortic wall. These factors combined to make conventional surgical intervention exceedingly risky and technically challenging. Due to the extensive integration of prior valves, operative management would likely have involved full replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple case reports have described TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and procedural difficulties. The present case contributes further to this limited literature, distinguished by the context of degeneration following endocarditis and sustained valve function at the 1-year mark.

This report highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive, lifetime approaches to managing structural valve disease. Each subsequent transcatheter intervention introduces additional complexity regarding frame stacking, alignment of commissures, accessibility to the coronaries, and maintaining adequate effective orifice area. Thorough long-term planning and early discussions regarding procedural options are essential, starting as early as the initial intervention, especially in patients who are younger or carry increased risk.

In this patient, structural deterioration manifested as leaflet fibrosis secondary to prior degeneration as well as suspected mechanical restriction post-infection. The care team elected to place a third valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 device—after preprocedural CT and 3D modelling indicated feasibility and sufficient coronary clearance (figures 1, 5 and 7). The third valve was intentionally positioned high, at the level of the Evolut valve leaflets, so as to secure the existing leaflets to the stent frame and prevent further narrowing of the effective orifice due to leaflet folding. This placement also limited formation of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia, thus aiding the preservation of coronary patency and enabling future coronary access. Guided by preprocedural CT analysis, this tactic was central to achieving satisfactory haemodynamics in a triple-stented setting. While deploying three transcatheter valves is inherently complex, the procedure was successfully executed and shown to be reproducible when directed by meticulous imaging and a predefined implantation protocol.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen because its leaflets are positioned supra-annularly, its frame height is relatively short, and it provides favourable haemodynamic results in restricted anatomies. Its features facilitate commissural alignment, which was an important consideration in this scenario to maintain the possibility of accessing the coronary arteries in the future.

One major technical challenge associated with TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures is the potential for compromised coronary access caused by several layers of overlapping stent frames. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the combined mesh could still pose obstacles for subsequent coronary interventions. This underscores the importance of careful procedural planning for patients with existing coronary disease or those who may require coronary access at a later date. Preprocedural CT imaging allowed assessment of the landing zone, analysis of frame interactions, and estimation of distances between the valve and the coronary ostia; however, a comprehensive three-dimensional expansion assessment was not conducted with specialized software.

Resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third device implantation was likely due to optimized commissural alignment, adequate post-dilatation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut frame, resulting in improved leaflet motion and central hemodynamics. Although intentional frame fracture was not undertaken, high-pressure balloon post-dilatation may have facilitated further radial expansion of the Evolut frame, thereby enhancing overall valve performance.

The procedure was carried out under institutional permission for the off-label application of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis. The decision-making process involved shared discussions with the patient and his family, and the case was evaluated during a multidisciplinary heart team meeting that included interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

If premature failure of the current prosthesis were to occur, treatment alternatives would be severely restricted. Surgical removal would continue to be both technically complex and high-risk. Novel catheter-based techniques such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration (for example, bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction [BASILICA]) may be considered, although the practicality and safety of these strategies in a triple-stented valve setting have yet to be determined.
