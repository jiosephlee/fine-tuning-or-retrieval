Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V. Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the varying technical difficulties inherent to transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve procedures, therapeutic approaches must be individualized for each patient. Here, we describe a man in his late 70s with a complex medical background, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures and an episode of infective endocarditis, who subsequently experienced severe structural failure of his aortic prosthesis necessitating a third transcatheter intervention. He initially received a Lotus valve in 2014, then underwent TAV-in-TAV with an Evolut device in 2022, which was later compromised by endocarditis and marked restenosis. In 2024, a third valve (Acurate neo2) was successfully placed using a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV strategy. As the scope of TAVI broadens and treated patients live longer, intricate multi-prosthetic valve procedures are likely to become increasingly common, underscoring the importance of advanced procedural planning, imaging techniques, and continued surveillance. At 1-year follow-up, the patient showed no symptoms, with normal prosthetic valve function maintained. This report highlights the viability of performing a third TAVI following prosthetic valve endocarditis, utilizing CT-based planning and documented 1-year follow-up, which remains infrequently described in current literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a recognized therapy for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, applicable to patients spanning various levels of surgical risk. With the rising prevalence of TAVI procedures and greater longevity among recipients, clinicians are more frequently observing structural deterioration of the implanted valve and a growing requirement for subsequent procedures.

In this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) implantation has become an acceptable option for addressing failed transcatheter heart valves. Nonetheless, these procedures carry unique anatomical and technical difficulties, such as heightened risk of coronary artery obstruction and decreased effective orifice area, which can lead to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These complexities are even more pronounced in successive interventions, such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where the presence of multiple superimposed valve frames must be thoroughly evaluated.

Data on repeated transcatheter valve interventions is scarce, especially for individuals with previous prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In these challenging cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, tailored risk evaluation, and collaborative multidisciplinary planning are crucial to assess procedural suitability and enhance outcomes.

This case demonstrates the importance of CT-guided procedural planning and strategic transcatheter intervention in a patient with a history of multiple TAVI procedures and managed PVE, underscoring critical aspects relevant to the long-term care of structural valve disease.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late 70s was hospitalized at the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center due to complaints of dyspnea, a spastic feeling localized to the left chest during exertion (notably when walking 200–300 meters), along with episodes of dizziness and general fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and several percutaneous coronary interventions: in 2009 to the right coronary artery, in 2010 to the left anterior descending (LAD) artery, again to the LAD in 2012, and to the circumflex artery in 2014. Additional conditions comprised third-degree atrioventricular block for which he underwent pacemaker implantation in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and repeated gastrointestinal bleeding episodes occurring since 2022.

In 2014, as a result of advancing aortic valve deterioration and significant stenosis, the patient received a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) via transcatheter implantation. By December 2021, there was a marked exacerbation of exertional dyspnoea, and echocardiography demonstrated severe stenosis of the bioprosthetic valve. At the beginning of 2022, the mean transvalvular gradient rose to 83 mm Hg, while the ejection fraction (EF) further dropped to 42%, down from 54% in December.

In May 2022, a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was implanted using a TAV-in-TAV approach. The intervention was complicated by a pulsatile hematoma and pseudoaneurysm at the femoral access site, necessitating open vascular surgical repair.

After the Evolut TAV-in-TAV procedure, the patient’s left ventricular ejection fraction increased from 42% to 54%, reflecting an early positive haemodynamic effect. The patient received standard heart failure medical management according to guidelines, which included beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted due to ongoing fever and exacerbation of heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures identified Enterococcus faecalis, confirming prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient was administered a complete course of pathogen-directed antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis-related PVE, consisting of 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based regimens, subsequently transitioning to oral therapy as advised by infectious disease consultants. Prior to the third TAVI procedure, antimicrobial therapy had concluded, and there was no ongoing chronic suppressive antibiotic treatment. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) all indicated resolution of infection, with no signs suggestive of active endocarditis.

Upon finishing antimicrobial treatment and experiencing partial alleviation of symptoms, a follow-up evaluation in January 2024 demonstrated deterioration in the function of the prosthetic valve, characterized by significant aortic stenosis along with moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean transvalvular gradient measured 49 mm Hg, while left ventricular ejection fraction had improved to 66%.

A reintervention was planned. Upon admission in June 2024, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated ongoing grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean transvalvular gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a significantly diminished aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², indicating severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. The AVA calculation utilized the continuity equation, derived from measurements of the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler velocity-time integrals. Imaging revealed the presence of two superimposed bioprosthetic valves.

Multislice computed tomography (MSCT) demonstrated that the minimal lumen diameter within the Evolut valve was 13.7 mm, indicating significant haemodynamic impairment and informing the strategy for a third transcatheter valve placement (figure 1). MSCT conducted prior to the procedure evaluated anatomical suitability and the potential risk for coronary obstruction. This assessment involved measurements of coronary ostial height, dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, valve-to-coronary distance, and analysis of the interaction between frames of previously deployed transcatheter valves. Special consideration was given to how the projected neo-skirt would relate to the coronary ostia, as well as the practicability of a high implant position to maintain coronary perfusion and ensure future access. These assessments influenced the selection of the valve, the depth of implantation, and the procedural plan, enabling the procedure to proceed without the need for coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was performed prior to the reintervention and confirmed the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular complications.

Treatment:

Due to the patient’s intricate anatomy with three stents, history of infective endocarditis, and high surgical risk, the third TAVI was scheduled as a CT-directed, strategy-focused intervention, prioritizing coronary protection, commissural orientation, and maximization of the remaining effective orifice area.

The procedure was performed under local anaesthesia using left radial and left femoral arterial access. Aortography with a pigtail catheter confirmed severe aortic regurgitation (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle through the left femoral approach. The aortic valve underwent balloon pre-dilatation utilizing a 22/40 mm catheter while rapid ventricular pacing was maintained at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography demonstrated preserved coronary flow without obstruction (figure 3).

An Acurate neo2 self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (size S) was positioned in the aortic location. Alignment of the prosthesis with the Evolut valve leaflets was achieved according to preprocedural CT planning, aiming to optimize the effective orifice area and minimize the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

A 20/40 mm balloon was utilized for post-dilation with rapid pacing to enhance expansion of the valve. Subsequent aortography demonstrated appropriate placement, maintained coronary blood flow, and showed no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (see figure 4).

During valve implantation, commissural alignment was considered in order to maintain coronary accessibility and minimize the complexity of any subsequent interventions. This process was directed by data from preprocedural CT scans as well as fluoroscopic anatomical references.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural efficacy and the functionality of the prosthetic valve were evaluated based on the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, which encompass assessments of haemodynamic performance, detection of any residual regurgitation, and identification of complications related to the procedure.

A transthoracic echocardiogram conducted directly following the procedure on 7 June 2024 verified appropriate placement of the third transcatheter valve. The valve leaflets demonstrated mobility with full excursion, and no aortic regurgitation was observed (grade 0). Despite this, hemodynamic measurements showed pronounced transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was calculated at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) measured 0.24, findings that indicate severe prosthesis–patient mismatch.

At 1 month post-implantation, transthoracic echocardiography revealed significant progress: the mean gradient measured 27 mm Hg, and the aortic valve area expanded to 1.1 cm². Evidence of left ventricular remodeling was present, indicated by decreased chamber volumes. No residual regurgitation was detected, and the prosthetic valve continued to function within the moderate stenosis spectrum.

At 3.5 months post-procedure, the patient continued to be free of symptoms and demonstrated satisfactory exercise capacity. There were no indications of heart failure or recurrence of infection. The prosthetic device continued to function well, and haemodynamic measurements remained stable and improved.

One year after the implantation (June 2025), the patient continued to be clinically stable and active, with no reports of dyspnea or indications of cardiac decompensation. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed a mean pressure gradient of 26 mm Hg, an aortic valve area of 1.1 cm², and an ejection fraction of 65%. There were no complications related to the valve, no regurgitation, and no signs of recurrent endocarditis.

These findings confirm the durability of the result and suggest a sustained functional benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV intervention in this high-risk patient.

Discussion:

This case highlights the decision-making process and technical challenges of performing a third TAVI in a patient with prior TAV-in-TAV procedures and a history of PVE.

Although TAVI is now a standard intervention for aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis is still debated. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis continue to advocate surgical valve replacement as the preferred option in prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE), especially when patients experience heart failure or persistent infection. Nonetheless, the guidelines highlight the importance of tailoring management decisions to the individual patient, considering factors such as infection resolution, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

TAVI was initially selected as the treatment approach in 2014 due to the patient’s elevated surgical risk and presence of several comorbid conditions, which rendered standard surgical valve replacement unsuitable. When the patient later exhibited signs of structural valve degeneration, a repeat transcatheter procedure was carried out in 2022 to reestablish valve function while circumventing the hazards associated with open-heart surgery. After the successful management of PVE and confirmation that no active infection persisted, the choice to proceed with a third transcatheter intervention in 2024 was made, taking into account prohibitive surgical risk, intricate anatomical factors stemming from the existing valve frames, and CT-based assurance that coronary flow could be maintained and satisfactory haemodynamic results achieved with the proposed strategy.

In this patient, prosthetic valve failure was probably due to multiple contributing factors. The prior episode of infective endocarditis may have led to leaflet damage and later mechanical impairment, but ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter prosthetic valves was also a major factor. Notably, after the infection was deemed cleared and indefinite antibiotic therapy was not needed, the therapeutic approach to prosthetic valve dysfunction was consistent with standard protocols for cases lacking active endocarditis. Consequently, choices regarding further intervention were based chiefly on anatomical suitability, haemodynamic impact, and overall surgical risk, rather than solely on the history of previous infection.

In this instance, the patient had completed a course of antibiotics, exhibited neither clinical nor radiological evidence of ongoing infection, and underwent comprehensive preprocedural assessment, including both TEE and MSCT, which confirmed there were no vegetations or paravalvular complications. Additionally, the patient’s EuroSCORE II was calculated at 27.36%, and notable comorbid conditions included COPD, diabetes, and a history of gastrointestinal bleeding. There were also two previously placed transcatheter valves with substantial stent incorporation into the aortic wall, making conventional surgery exceptionally high-risk and technically challenging. Due to the deeply anchored prior prostheses, a surgical approach would probably have required replacement of the entire aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple published reports have described TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 noting a range of anatomical and technical difficulties encountered. The present case further contributes to this scarce literature, presenting a distinct scenario of degeneration following endocarditis and maintained valve performance at the 1-year follow-up.

This case highlights the increasing relevance of comprehensive, lifelong management approaches for structural valve disorders. Each additional transcatheter procedure adds complexity to factors including frame stacking, commissural orientation, coronary access, and preservation of effective orifice area. Thoughtful, anticipatory planning and early dialogue regarding procedural sequences are essential, starting with the initial intervention, especially for younger individuals or those at elevated risk.

In this case, the patient's valve deterioration was characterized by leaflet fibrosis resulting from earlier degeneration, as well as likely mechanical restriction after the infectious process. The care team opted to place a third valve—an Acurate neo2 self-expanding prosthesis—guided by preprocedural CT scans and 3D modeling, which confirmed technical viability and ensured sufficient distance from the coronary arteries (see figures 1, 5, and 7). The third transcatheter valve was intentionally implanted in a high position as part of the procedural plan. Positioning the device at the same height as the Evolut valve's leaflets enabled compression of the prior leaflets against the stent, helping to avert additional decreases in effective orifice area from inward leaflet infolding. Furthermore, this implantation technique reduced the likelihood of creating a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia, thus safeguarding coronary blood flow and future coronary access. Careful preprocedural CT evaluation informed this approach, which was crucial in achieving satisfactory haemodynamic outcomes in a setting involving three stented valves. Although managing a triple transcatheter valve configuration is complex, the use of comprehensive preprocedural imaging and a predetermined strategy demonstrated that the procedure could be carried out successfully and consistently.

The Acurate neo2 valve was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet configuration, modest frame height, and advantageous haemodynamic properties in constricted anatomies. Its structure facilitates commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this patient to maintain potential future access to the coronary arteries.

An important technical issue in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions is the potential difficulty in accessing the coronary arteries resulting from several superimposed stent frames. Even with efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the increased amount of mesh can still complicate subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of careful procedural preparation, particularly for patients with established coronary artery disease or when future coronary access is anticipated. Preprocedural CT allowed for assessment of the intended deployment area, evaluation of how the frames interacted, and estimation of the distance between the valve and coronary ostia; however, comprehensive three-dimensional analysis of device expansion using specialized software was not undertaken.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve implantation is likely due to optimized commissural alignment, successful post-dilation, and mechanical partial expansion of the previously placed Evolut frame, which resulted in improved leaflet mobility and more favorable central flow. Although a deliberate frame fracture was not undertaken, the use of high-pressure balloon post-dilation probably facilitated additional radial expansion of the Evolut valve, thereby improving its functional performance.

The intervention was conducted with institutional authorization for the off-label application of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis. A shared decision-making process included input from the patient and his relatives, and the case underwent evaluation by a multidisciplinary heart team comprising interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

If the present prosthetic valve were to fail earlier than expected, available management strategies would be greatly restricted. Surgical removal would continue to pose significant technical challenges and would carry considerable risk. Novel transcatheter interventions—such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration techniques, including bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—could potentially be explored; however, their practicality and safety within a scenario involving three overlapping stents have yet to be clearly established.
