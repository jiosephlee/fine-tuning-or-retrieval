Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the various technical complexities involved in transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve interventions, it is essential to individualize treatment strategies for each patient. We describe a male patient in his late 70s who had a challenging clinical course marked by two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) procedures as well as infective endocarditis, subsequently progressing to severe aortic prosthesis degeneration that necessitated a third transcatheter intervention. He initially received a Lotus valve in 2014 and later underwent a TAV-in-TAV with an Evolut prosthesis in 2022. Subsequent degeneration due to endocarditis led to marked restenosis. In 2024, a third prosthetic valve (Acurate neo2) was placed using a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach. With broader indications for TAVI and improved survival among recipients, complex procedures involving multiple prostheses are likely to become more common, underscoring the importance of enhanced procedural planning, advanced imaging techniques, and rigorous long-term monitoring. At a 1-year follow-up, the patient exhibits no symptoms and maintains good valve performance. This report demonstrates the practicality of performing a third TAVI after treated prosthetic valve endocarditis, facilitated by CT-based procedural planning and 1-year clinical surveillance—a scenario rarely documented in existing literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now a well-established therapeutic approach for individuals with severe aortic stenosis, encompassing a wide spectrum of surgical risk levels. With an increasing number of TAVI procedures being performed and improvements in long-term patient survival, clinicians are more frequently encountering cases of structural valve degeneration requiring additional interventions.

In this context, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) interventions have gained acceptance as an effective option for addressing the failure of previous transcatheter valves. Nevertheless, these procedures present unique anatomical and technical issues, such as the threat of obstructing the coronary arteries and the possibility of a diminished effective orifice area, leading to significant prosthesis-patient mismatch. These complexities become even more pronounced with repeated procedures, for example, in transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV) interventions, where the management of several superimposed valve frames must be taken into account.

The body of evidence regarding repeated transcatheter valve interventions is still relatively sparse, especially among those who have previously experienced prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). In such complicated cases, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, thorough individualized risk evaluation, and collaborative decision-making by a multidisciplinary team are critical to assess procedural suitability and enhance outcomes.

This case demonstrates how computed tomography (CT)-based planning and a tailored transcatheter approach were utilized in a patient with multiple preceding TAVI procedures and a background of managed PVE, underlining essential aspects for lifetime care of structural valve pathology.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late seventies was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after presenting with shortness of breath, a spastic sensation localized to the left chest during exertion (noted when walking 200–300 m), episodes of dizziness, and fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and multiple prior percutaneous coronary interventions: right coronary artery in 2009, left anterior descending artery in 2010, repeat LAD intervention in 2012, and circumflex artery in 2014. He also had third-degree atrioventricular block managed with pacemaker insertion in 2014, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurring gastrointestinal bleeding beginning in 2022.

Ongoing degeneration of the aortic valve with severe stenosis led to a transcatheter placement of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific) in 2014. By December 2021, the patient experienced a further decline in symptoms with worsening exertional dyspnoea and echocardiographic evidence of marked bioprosthetic valve stenosis. Early 2022 revealed an increased mean gradient of 83 mm Hg and a reduced ejection fraction (EF) of 42% compared to 54% recorded in December.

In May 2022, he underwent a transcatheter aortic valve-in-valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedure, receiving a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic). Post-operatively, the procedure was complicated by a pulsatile femoral hematoma and the formation of a pseudoaneurysm, necessitating surgical repair.

After implantation of the Evolut valve, left ventricular EF rose from 42% to 54%, reflecting an initial favourable haemodynamic outcome. The patient received evidence-based pharmacological management for heart failure, which included beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as required.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was admitted once more due to ongoing fever and deteriorating heart failure symptoms. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures grew Enterococcus faecalis, establishing a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). Targeted antimicrobial treatment for E. faecalis PVE was initiated, consisting of 6 weeks of intravenous ampicillin-based therapy, succeeded by oral antibiotics in accordance with infectious disease specialist advice. At the point of the third TAVI procedure, antimicrobial therapy had been completed, and long-term suppressive antibiotics were not being administered. Clinical evaluation, repeat blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) affirmed resolution of infection, with no signs of ongoing endocarditis.

Following completion of antimicrobial therapy and partial improvement in symptoms, subsequent follow-up in January 2024 documented worsening function of the prosthetic valve, including severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean transvalvular gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction had returned to 66%.

Reintervention was planned. Upon admission in June 2024, TTE detected persistent grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a notably reduced aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², confirming severe dysfunction of the prosthetic valve. AVA was determined using the continuity equation, utilizing measurements from the left ventricular outflow tract and Doppler velocity-time integrals. Two overlapping bioprosthetic valves were visible on imaging.

MSCT showed a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm within the Evolut prosthesis, demonstrating severe haemodynamic impairment and serving to inform planning for a third transcatheter valve insertion (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT evaluated anatomical suitability and the possibility of coronary artery obstruction, incorporating assessment of coronary ostial height, sinus of Valsalva dimensions, valve-to-coronary distance, and structural interaction of the prior transcatheter valve frames. Special emphasis was placed on the relationship between the anticipated neo-skirt and the coronary ostia, as well as the potential for high implantation to maintain coronary flow and guarantee future access to the coronaries. These parameters influenced the choice of valve, implantation position, and overall procedural approach, enabling the intervention to be completed without coronary protection measures.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was performed pre-reintervention and verified the absence of active endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate anatomy with three prior stent placements, history of infective endocarditis, and high surgical risk, a third TAVI was scheduled as a procedure guided by CT imaging, focusing on coronary protection, precise commissural positioning, and maximizing the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was carried out under local anesthesia, utilizing left radial and left femoral arterial routes. Severe aortic regurgitation was confirmed with aortography using a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

A Safari guidewire was introduced into the left ventricle through the left femoral approach. Pre-dilation of the aortic valve was undertaken with a 22/40 mm balloon catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at 180 bpm. Simultaneous aortographic assessment verified that coronary blood flow was not impeded (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2, size S) was deployed in the aortic position. Alignment of the device with the previously implanted Evolut valve leaflets, guided by pre-procedure CT analysis, was performed to optimize effective orifice area and minimize the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch.

Valve expansion was further optimized by post-dilation using a 20/40 mm balloon during rapid pacing. Final aortography demonstrated correct valve placement, intact coronary flow, and no evidence of perivalvular or transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Attention was given to commissural positioning during valve implantation, aiming to preserve coronary access and simplify potential future interventions, using guidance from CT imaging and fluoroscopic markers.

Outcome and follow-up:

Assessment of procedural outcomes and prosthetic valve function followed the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, encompassing analysis of haemodynamic performance, residual regurgitation, and complications associated with the procedure.

A transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) conducted directly following the intervention on 7 June 2024 verified appropriate deployment of the third transcatheter valve. Leaflet motion appeared normal, with full excursion and absence of aortic regurgitation (graded 0). Nevertheless, haemodynamic assessment revealed notable transvalvular gradients, with a mean gradient measuring 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) was measured at 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and Doppler velocity index (DVI) registered 0.24, indicative of severe prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM).

At the 1-month follow-up, TTE demonstrated substantial haemodynamic improvement; mean gradient declined to 27 mm Hg and AVA expanded to 1.1 cm². Echocardiography also showed evidence of left ventricular remodeling, including decreased chamber volumes. No aortic regurgitation was detected, and prosthetic valve performance was categorized within the moderate stenosis range.

Three and a half months post-procedure, the patient reported no symptoms and was able to tolerate physical activity well. There were no clinical indications of heart failure or infection recurrence. Haemodynamic indices and prosthesis function remained stable with ongoing improvement.

At the 1-year evaluation (June 2025), the patient continued to be clinically stable and engaged in normal activity levels without shortness of breath or decompensatory signs. TTE showed a sustained mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA at 1.1 cm², and ejection fraction (EF) at 65%. There were no prosthesis-associated complications, regurgitation, or relapse of endocarditis.

Collectively, these findings support the long-term stability of the outcome and point to a lasting improvement in function following TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV for this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This case underscores both the clinical decision-making and procedural complexities involved in performing a third TAVI in a patient with previous TAV-in-TAV placements and a background of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is a well-established option for treating aortic stenosis, its application in patients with prior episodes of endocarditis remains contentious. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for managing valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis advocate surgical valve replacement for PVE, especially when complicated by heart failure or persistent infection. Nevertheless, the guidelines recommend that therapeutic choices should be tailored to the individual, factoring in infection resolution, operative risk, and anatomical suitability.

TAVI was initially selected as the preferred approach in 2014 due to the patient’s elevated surgical risk and numerous comorbid conditions, rendering traditional surgical valve replacement less viable. When structural valve deterioration occurred, a redo transcatheter intervention was conducted in 2022 to restore valve functionality without subjecting the patient to open surgery. After the PVE had been successfully managed and in the absence of ongoing infection, the decision to undertake a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 was justified by unacceptable surgical risk, challenging anatomy related to previous valve frames, and CT findings that supported a strategy capable of maintaining coronary flow and delivering adequate haemodynamic results.

The underlying causes of prosthetic valve failure in this patient were probably complex and multifaceted. Although the bout of infective endocarditis may have resulted in leaflet damage with ensuing mechanical impairment, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter valves was also a major contributor. Notably, after infection was deemed resolved and long-term suppressive antibiotics were no longer indicated, the approach to treating prosthetic valve dysfunction mirrored that for patients without current endocarditis. Thus, procedural choices were influenced chiefly by anatomical suitability, haemodynamic impact, and surgical risk factors, rather than solely by the patient’s previous infection.

In this instance, the patient had completed the prescribed antimicrobial regimen and displayed no evidence of infection on clinical assessment or imaging. Comprehensive preprocedural testing was performed, including transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and multislice computed tomography (MSCT), which confirmed there were no vegetations or paravalvular issues. Additionally, the patient’s European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation (EuroSCORE II) was 27.36%. She had substantial comorbid conditions—chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), diabetes, a history of gastrointestinal bleeding—and two prior transcatheter prostheses with robust stent incorporation into the aortic wall, all of which made traditional surgical intervention extremely high-risk and technically challenging. The profound fixation of previous valves indicated that surgery would probably require complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports have described TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and procedural difficulties encountered during these procedures. The present case contributes to the scarce literature, distinguished by the setting of degeneration secondary to endocarditis and sustained valve function at one year follow-up.

This report emphasizes the increasing relevance of comprehensive, lifelong strategies for the management of structural valve disease. Each subsequent transcatheter reintervention intensifies concerns related to frame stacking, commissural alignment, coronary reaccess, and the residual effective orifice area. It is vital to engage in long-term planning and to initiate conversations regarding procedural sequencing early—ideally at the initial intervention—particularly for younger individuals or those at elevated risk.

In this patient, structural deterioration was characterized by leaflet fibrosis resulting from prior degeneration and likely mechanical impairment after infection. The clinical team elected to implant a third valve—a self-expanding Acurate neo2 device—guided by preprocedural CT and three-dimensional modelling, both of which confirmed procedural viability and sufficient coronary clearance (see figures 1, 5, and 7). Purposeful high positioning of the third transcatheter valve was employed as a procedural tactic. By positioning the device at the height of the Evolut valve leaflets, the team secured the existing leaflets against the stent frame, effectively preventing additional reduction of the effective orifice area by averting leaflet inward folding. Furthermore, this method reduced the creation of a neo-skirt beneath the coronary ostia and aided in the preservation of coronary flow and subsequent coronary access. This technique was informed by preprocedural CT evaluation and was crucial for attaining acceptable haemodynamics within a triple-stented configuration. Although a triple transcatheter valve arrangement presents significant technical complexity, it can be successfully executed and replicated when supported by comprehensive preprocedural imaging and a predefined deployment approach.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen due to its supra-annular positioning of leaflets, lower frame height, and favorable hemodynamic profile in restricted anatomical spaces. Its structural features facilitate commissural alignment, which was an important factor in this case to maintain potential access to the coronary arteries in the future.

A major technical challenge in TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV procedures involves the possibility of diminished coronary access resulting from multiple overlapping stent frames. Despite attempts at commissural alignment, the aggregate mesh structure may still complicate subsequent coronary interventions. This underscores the necessity for careful procedural planning in patients with pre-existing coronary artery disease or those where future coronary access is anticipated to be crucial. Preprocedural CT imaging allowed for measurement of the landing zone, assessment of stent frame interactions, and estimation of the distance between the prosthetic valve and coronary ostia; however, comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis with specialized software was not conducted.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve implantation can be explained by an improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilatation, and the partial mechanical expansion of the prior Evolut stent frame, which led to better leaflet mobility and central flow dynamics. Intentional frame fracture was not attempted, yet high-pressure balloon post-dilation likely promoted further radial expansion of the Evolut frame, thereby enhancing valve functionality.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label use of the Acurate neo2 prosthesis in this procedure. The decision-making process was shared with the patient and his family, and the case received review by a multidisciplinary heart team consisting of interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anesthesiologists.

In the event of early failure of the current prosthesis, therapeutic options would be extremely limited. Surgical removal would present substantial technical difficulty and elevated risk. Newer catheter-based approaches, such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—for example, bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction (BASILICA)—may be considered, though their feasibility and safety in triple-stented scenarios have not yet been established.
