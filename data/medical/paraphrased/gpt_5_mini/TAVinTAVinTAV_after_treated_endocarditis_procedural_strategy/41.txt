Title: TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV after treated endocarditis: procedural strategy, imaging planning and 1-year outcome

Evgeny Vladimirovich Merkulov, Goar Kimovna Arutyunyan, Andrey Sergeevich Tereshchenko, Dmitriy V Pevzner, Iiliya Tarasovich Zyuryaev, Mariya Alekseevna Terenicheva, Olga Nickolaevna Ganeeva, Alexey Vladimirovich Knigin

Abstract:

Summary: Due to the variety of technical challenges linked to transcatheter valve-in-transcatheter valve interventions, management must be individualized for each patient. Here, we describe a male patient in his late 70s with a complex medical background, including two previous transcatheter aortic valve implantations (TAVI) and a history of infective endocarditis, who subsequently developed significant prosthetic valve degeneration, ultimately necessitating a third transcatheter procedure. His initial implantation involved a Lotus valve in 2014, after which a TAV-in-TAV using an Evolut device was performed in 2022 in the context of degeneration, complicated by endocarditis-induced valve failure and pronounced restenosis. In 2024, the patient underwent a third valve implantation, receiving an Acurate neo2 device through a TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV method. The increasing indications for TAVI and longer survival of patients are likely to result in more frequent multi-valve procedures, underscoring the importance of advanced procedural planning, refined imaging techniques, and ongoing clinical monitoring. One year post-intervention, the patient exhibits no symptoms and retains effective valve performance. This case highlights the practicality of a third TAVI following previous prosthetic valve endocarditis, planned using CT imaging and monitored with one-year follow-up—an approach rarely detailed in existing literature.

Background:

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is now widely recognized as a treatment for severe aortic stenosis in patients with varying levels of surgical risk. With an increasing patient population undergoing TAVI and improvements in long-term outcomes, clinicians are encountering more cases of structural valve degeneration and subsequent re-intervention requirements.

Under these circumstances, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedures have become an effective method to address failed transcatheter valves. Nonetheless, these interventions pose distinct anatomical and technical challenges, such as the possibility of coronary artery blockage and a decrease in the effective orifice area that may cause severe prosthesis-patient mismatch. These difficulties are further exacerbated during multiple sequential procedures, such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where several overlapping valve frames must be managed.

The available data on multiple sequential transcatheter valve procedures are sparse, particularly in patients with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). For patients in such complex situations, comprehensive preprocedural imaging, personalized risk evaluation, and collaboration across specialties are vital to assess feasibility and optimize outcomes.

This case demonstrates the value of CT-based procedural planning and a strategy-oriented approach to transcatheter intervention for a patient who had previously undergone multiple TAVI procedures and treatment for PVE, underscoring important principles for the ongoing management of structural valve disease.

Case presentation:

A male patient in his late 70s was admitted to the Academician E.I. Chazov National Medical Research Center after reporting dyspnoea, a spasmodic sensation on the left side of his chest triggered by physical activity (walking 200–300 m), along with dizziness and fatigue.

His past medical history included arterial hypertension, three-vessel coronary artery disease, and multiple percutaneous coronary interventions: right coronary artery in 2009, left anterior descending (LAD) artery in 2010, repeat LAD intervention in 2012, and circumflex artery in 2014. He also had third-degree atrioventricular block necessitating pacemaker placement (2014), type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding since 2022.

Progressive degeneration and severe stenosis of the aortic valve in 2014 led to transcatheter placement of a 27 mm Lotus valve (Boston Scientific). By December 2021, the patient’s exertional dyspnoea had worsened, and echocardiography revealed significant bioprosthetic valve stenosis. By early 2022, the mean gradient rose to 83 mm Hg and ejection fraction (EF) decreased to 42% (previously 54% in December).

A TAV-in-TAV procedure with implantation of a 23 mm Evolut valve (Medtronic) was performed in May 2022, which was complicated by the formation of a pulsatile femoral haematoma and pseudoaneurysm, requiring surgical vascular intervention.

After the Evolut TAV-in-TAV intervention, the patient’s left ventricular EF improved from 42% to 54%, demonstrating a positive early haemodynamic outcome. Management included guideline-directed heart failure pharmacotherapy with beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and diuretics as appropriate.

Three months later, in September 2022, the patient was readmitted with ongoing fever and an exacerbation of heart failure signs. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated significant restenosis of the aortic valve prosthesis, with a mean gradient of 35 mm Hg. Blood cultures yielded Enterococcus faecalis, leading to a diagnosis of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE). The patient underwent a complete regimen of pathogen-specific antimicrobial therapy for E. faecalis PVE, including six weeks of intravenous ampicillin, followed by oral antibiotics per infectious diseases specialist guidance. Prior to the third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), antimicrobial treatment was finished and there was no ongoing long-term antibiotic suppression. Clinical evaluation, serial blood cultures, transoesophageal echocardiography, and multislice CT (MSCT) all corroborated infection resolution, with no evidence of current endocarditis.

Upon completion of antimicrobial therapy and partial alleviation of symptoms, subsequent follow-up in January 2024 showed deterioration in prosthetic valve performance, marked by severe aortic stenosis and moderate-to-severe regurgitation. The mean transvalvular gradient had increased to 49 mm Hg, and left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) was restored to 66%.

A plan for further intervention was established. At admission in June 2024, TTE identified persistent grade 2–3 intraprosthetic aortic regurgitation, a mean gradient of 49 mm Hg, and a notably reduced aortic valve area (AVA) measuring 0.5 cm², indicative of significant prosthetic valve dysfunction. The AVA calculation utilized the continuity equation, incorporating left ventricular outflow tract measurements and Doppler-derived velocity-time integrals. Visualization confirmed two overlapping bioprosthetic valves.

MSCT detected a minimum lumen diameter of 13.7 mm in the Evolut prosthesis, affirming severe hemodynamic compromise and informing the procedural approach for a third transcatheter valve implantation (figure 1). Preprocedural MSCT was utilized to evaluate anatomical suitability and the likelihood of coronary artery obstruction. This assessment encompassed coronary ostial height, dimensions of the sinus of Valsalva, valve-to-coronary distance, and interaction of the existing transcatheter valve frames. Special consideration was given to the alignment of the projected neo-skirt and coronary ostia, as well as the practicality of a high implant to maintain coronary perfusion and access for possible future interventions. The results influenced the choice of valve, depth of implantation, and the procedural method, permitting the procedure to proceed without coronary protection.

Transoesophageal echocardiography was conducted prior to the repeat intervention and confirmed the absence of ongoing endocarditis or paravalvular issues.

Treatment:

Considering the patient’s intricate triple-stented anatomy, history of infective endocarditis, and high operative risk, a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was planned using a CT-guided, strategy-focused approach prioritising coronary protection, proper commissural alignment, and optimisation of the remaining effective orifice area.

The intervention was carried out under local anaesthesia, utilizing left radial and left femoral arterial accesses. Severe aortic regurgitation was verified by aortography employing a pigtail catheter (figure 2).

Through the left femoral route, a Safari guidewire was advanced into the left ventricle. The aortic valve underwent balloon pre-dilation with a 22/40 mm catheter during rapid ventricular pacing at 180 beats per minute. Concurrent aortography demonstrated that coronary flow was maintained (figure 3).

A self-expanding bioprosthetic valve (Acurate neo2, size S) was positioned in the aortic site. Preprocedural CT modelling enabled alignment of the device with the Evolut valve leaflets, thereby increasing the effective orifice area and minimizing the likelihood of patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM).

Post-dilation using a 20/40 mm balloon with rapid pacing was performed to further expand the valve. Final aortography revealed appropriate placement, sustained coronary perfusion, and absence of both perivalvular and transvalvular regurgitation (figure 4).

Attention to commissural alignment was maintained during valve deployment in order to maintain coronary access and decrease future procedural difficulties, utilizing preprocedural CT imaging as well as fluoroscopic landmarks.

Outcome and follow-up:

Procedural outcomes and prosthetic valve function were evaluated based on the Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 guidelines, which included assessment of haemodynamic performance, residual regurgitation, and procedural complications.

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) conducted immediately post-procedure on 7 June 2024 demonstrated the successful placement of the third transcatheter valve. The leaflets showed normal mobility with full opening and there was no aortic regurgitation (grade 0). Despite this, haemodynamic analysis revealed considerable transvalvular gradients, with a mean of 67 mm Hg. The aortic valve area (AVA) measured 0.9 cm² (indexed AVA 0.4 cm²/m²), and the Doppler velocity index (DVI) registered at 0.24, indicative of severe prosthesis-patient mismatch (PPM).

Follow-up TTE at 1 month post-implantation demonstrated substantial improvement: the mean gradient decreased to 27 mm Hg, and AVA increased to 1.1 cm². Remodeling of the left ventricle was apparent with reduced chamber volume. Valve function remained stable and there was no residual regurgitation, with haemodynamics consistent with moderate stenosis.

By 3.5 months, the patient continued to be asymptomatic, displaying good exercise capacity. There were no indications of heart failure or recurrent infection. The valve prosthesis sustained favourable haemodynamic performance and functional stability.

One year after the procedure (June 2025), the patient persisted in a stable and active state, without shortness of breath or clinical decompensation. TTE recorded a mean gradient of 26 mm Hg, AVA 1.1 cm², and left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) of 65%. There was no evidence of valve-related complications, regurgitation, or recurrent endocarditis.

Collectively, these results affirm the long-term durability and persistent clinical benefit of the TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV approach for this high-risk individual.

Discussion:

This report illustrates the considerations and procedural complexities associated with performing a third transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a patient with two previous TAV-in-TAV procedures and a background of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

Although TAVI is an established option for treating aortic stenosis, its application in individuals with a history of endocarditis is still subject to debate. The 2025 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines for managing valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis state that surgical valve replacement remains the preferred approach for PVE, particularly when heart failure or uncontrolled infection is present. Nevertheless, these guidelines stress that therapeutic decisions should be tailored, considering infection management, operative risk, and anatomic suitability.

The initial selection of TAVI in 2014 was based on the patient's elevated surgical risk and significant comorbidities, which rendered traditional surgical valve replacement an unfavorable option at that juncture. After subsequent development of structural valve degeneration, a second transcatheter approach was utilized in 2022 to re-establish valve function and avoid the hazards linked with open surgery. With PVE successfully addressed and no evidence of persistent infection, the plan to proceed with a third transcatheter procedure in 2024 reflected an unacceptably high surgical risk, the anatomic complexities from previous valve frames, and the confirmation by CT imaging that it was feasible to proceed with a strategy that would maintain coronary flow and provide adequate haemodynamic outcomes.

The failure of the prosthetic valve in this patient was probably due to several contributing factors. Although the prior episode of infective endocarditis may have resulted in leaflet damage and mechanical limitation, ongoing structural deterioration of the transcatheter prostheses was also a key factor. Notably, after resolution of the infection and the decision that prolonged antibiotic suppression was unnecessary, management of prosthetic valve dysfunction followed the same principles as in patients without persistent or active endocarditis. Accordingly, choices regarding intervention were based chiefly on anatomical suitability, haemodynamic significance, and the patient’s surgical risk, rather than the history of infection alone.

For this patient, antimicrobial treatment was completed and there were neither clinical nor imaging indications of current infection. Comprehensive evaluation prior to intervention included TEE and MSCT, both of which showed no evidence of vegetations or paravalvular issues. Additionally, the patient's EuroSCORE II was 27.36%, and he had notable comorbid conditions such as COPD, diabetes, and previous gastrointestinal bleeding. He also had two prior transcatheter valves with substantial stent integration into the aortic wall, all of which made surgical intervention extremely high risk and technically difficult. Owing to the deep fixation of these devices, any surgical approach would probably have required complete replacement of the aortic root and ascending aorta.

Multiple reports have documented TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions,37 illustrating a range of anatomical and procedural hurdles. The present case contributes to this scarce literature, distinguished by post-endocarditis degeneration and maintained valve performance at 1-year follow-up.

This case highlights the escalating significance of comprehensive, lifelong management approaches for structural valve disease. With successive transcatheter procedures, issues such as overlapping frames, commissural positioning, access to coronaries, and preserved effective orifice area become progressively more intricate. It is essential to engage in long-term planning and initiate early conversations regarding possible procedural pathways—even during the initial treatment—particularly for younger or higher-risk patients.

Structural valve deterioration in this patient was characterized by leaflet fibrosis, resulting from previous degeneration and likely exacerbated by mechanical restriction secondary to infection. Based on preprocedural CT and three-dimensional modeling indicating feasibility and sufficient coronary clearance (figures 1, 5, and 7), the clinical team decided to place a third valve, utilizing a self-expanding Acurate neo2 device. The third valve was intentionally implanted at a high position. By deploying the prosthesis at the same level as the Evolut valve leaflets, the existing leaflets were stabilized against the stent structure, preventing further diminution of effective orifice area due to inward folding. Additionally, this technique reduced formation of a neo-skirt below the coronary orifices, thereby supporting coronary patency and future access. Guidance from preprocedural CT imaging was crucial in achieving favorable haemodynamics within a three-layered stent architecture. Although triple transcatheter valve placement is complex, this method proved achievable and repeatable, provided it was directed by comprehensive imaging and a carefully formulated implantation plan.

The Acurate neo2 was chosen due to its supra-annular leaflet orientation, comparatively low frame height, and favorable haemodynamic properties in constricted anatomies. Additionally, its features allow for commissural alignment, which was particularly important in this instance to maintain access to the coronaries in the future.

A major technical issue encountered during TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV interventions is the potential obstruction to coronary access resulting from multiple overlapping stent frameworks. Despite efforts to achieve commissural alignment, the accumulated mesh could complicate subsequent coronary procedures. This underscores the importance of careful procedural planning for patients with existing coronary pathology or in those where future coronary access might become necessary. Preprocedural CT facilitated assessment of the landing site, interaction of stent frames, and measurement of valve-to-coronary distances; however, comprehensive three-dimensional expansion analysis with specialized software was not carried out.

The resolution of the transvalvular gradient following the third valve placement likely resulted from the improved commissural alignment, successful post-dilation, and partial mechanical expansion of the previously implanted Evolut frame, thus enhancing leaflet movement and central hemodynamics. Although deliberate frame fracture was not attempted, post-dilation with high-pressure balloons probably further expanded the Evolut frame radially, supporting improved valve performance.

Institutional approval was obtained for the off-label deployment of the Acurate neo2 device. Shared decision-making included discussions with the patient and his family, and the case underwent evaluation by a multidisciplinary heart team comprising interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, imaging experts, and anaesthesiologists.

In the event that the present prosthesis fails early, treatment alternatives would be very restricted. Surgical removal would be technically challenging and carry considerable risk. Novel percutaneous strategies, such as bioprosthetic valve fracture or leaflet laceration—for example, BASILICA (bioprosthetic or native aortic scallop intentional laceration to prevent iatrogenic coronary artery obstruction)—could be contemplated, though their effectiveness and safety in settings with three overlapping stents remain indeterminate.
