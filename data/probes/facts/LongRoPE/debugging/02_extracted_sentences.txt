tagged_sentences: \title{LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens}

\begin{abstract}
[BOS] Large context window is a desirable feature in large language models (LLMs). [EOS]

[BOS] However, due to high fine-tuning costs, scarcity of long texts, and catastrophic values introduced by new token positions, current extended context windows are limited to around 128k tokens. [EOS]

[BOS] This paper introduces LongRoPE that,  for the first time, extends the context window of pre-trained LLMs to an impressive  \textbf{2048k} tokens,  with up to only 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training lengths, while maintaining performance at the original short context window. [EOS]

[BOS] This is achieved by three key innovations: (i) we identify and exploit two forms of non-uniformities in positional interpolation through an efficient  search, providing a better initialization for fine-tuning and  enabling an 8$\times$ extension in non-fine-tuning scenarios; (ii) we introduce a progressive extension strategy that first fine-tunes a 256k length LLM and then conducts a second  positional interpolation on the fine-tuned extended LLM to achieve a 2048k context window; (iii) we readjust LongRoPE on 8k length to recover the short context window performance. [EOS]

[BOS] Extensive experiments on LLaMA2 and Mistral across various tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of our method. [EOS]

[BOS] Models extended via LongRoPE retain the original architecture with minor modifications to the positional embedding,  and can reuse most pre-existing optimizations. [EOS]

[BOS] Code will be available at \texttt{https://github.com/microsoft/LongRoPE} [EOS]
\end{abstract}

tagged_sentences: [BOS]Large Language Models (LLMs), despite remarkable success on various tasks~\cite{gpt4,llama2}, often suffer from limited context window size, e.g., LLaMA2's 4096 token limit~\cite{llama2}.[EOS] [BOS]Beyond the context window, LLM's performance declines due to the additional positions that the model has not been trained on.[EOS] [BOS]This poses challenges in important scenarios like in-context learning with numerous examples~\cite{Huang2023FewerIM} and LLM agents~\cite{park2023generative,madaan2023selfrefine}.[EOS] 

[BOS]Recent works show that a pre-trained LLM context window can be extended to around 128k by fine-tuning on longer texts~\cite{longlora,pi,yarn,zhang2024soaring,liu2023scaling}.[EOS] [BOS]There are three major obstacles to further extend the context window.[EOS] [BOS]First, untrained new position indices introduce many catastrophic values, leading to out-of-distribution issues and  making fine-tuning difficult to converge~\cite{pi}.[EOS] [BOS]This is particularly challenging when an extension from 4k to  $>$1000k introduces more than 90\% new positions.[EOS] [BOS]Second, fine-tuning usually requires texts of corresponding lengths.[EOS] [BOS]However, long texts in current datasets, especially those exceeding 1000k, are limited.[EOS] [BOS]Moreover, training on extra-long texts is computationally expensive, requiring prohibitively extensive training hours and GPU resources.[EOS] [BOS]Third, when extending to extremely long context windows, the attention becomes dispersed as it's spread thinly across numerous token positions, degrading performance on the original short context~\cite{pi}.[EOS] 

[BOS]One approach to mitigate the first challenge is to interpolate RoPE positional embedding~\cite{rope,pi},  which downscales new position indices to the pretrained range, as shown in Fig.\ref{fig:overview}.[EOS] [BOS]Position Interpolation (PI) ~\cite{pi} linearly interpolates RoPE's rotary angles by the
extension ratio.[EOS] [BOS]NTK~\cite{ntk,dynamicntk} advocates unequal interpolation and extrapolation across RoPE dimensions.[EOS] [BOS]YaRN~\cite{yarn} categorizes RoPE dimensions into three frequency-based groups and applies extrapolation, NTK, and linear interpolations, respectively.[EOS] [BOS]However,  positional embedding exhibits \emph{complex non-uniform information entropy} in the Transformer architecture.[EOS] [BOS]Such subtle non-uniformity is not effectively leveraged by existing approaches, leading to information loss and hence limiting the context window size.[EOS] 

[BOS]Section~\ref{sec:analysis} reveals two key findings empirically: \textbf{(1)} Effective positional interpolation should consider two forms of non-uniformities: varying RoPE dimensions and token positions.[EOS] [BOS]Lower RoPE dimensions and initial starting token positions benefit from less interpolation, but the optimal solutions depend on the target extended length.[EOS]
  [BOS]\textbf{(2)} By considering these non-uniformities into positional interpolation, we can effectively retain information in the original RoPE,  particularly key dimensions and token positions.[EOS] [BOS]This minimizes the loss caused by positional interpolation, and thus provides better initialization for fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]Moreover, it  allows an 8$\times$ extension in non-fine-tuning scenarios.[EOS] 

[BOS]Motivated by the findings, we introduce \textbf{LongRoPE},  an effective method that extends the LLM context window beyond 2 \emph{million} tokens.[EOS] [BOS]LongRoPE is based on three key innovations.[EOS] [BOS]First, {LongRoPE} fully exploits multidimensional non-uniformities in positional interpolation.[EOS] [BOS]It identifies effective rescale factors for RoPE's rotation angles for each RoPE dimension, based on token positions.[EOS] [BOS]As the search space that identifies rescale factors expands exponentially with the target extension ratio, {LongRoPE} introduces an evolutionary search algorithm with two optimization techniques to boost search efficiency.[EOS] [BOS]Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows an example of the searched  rescaled RoPE.[EOS]

[BOS]Then, {LongRoPE} leverages an efficient, progressive extension strategy to achieve a 2048k context window without the need of direct fine-tuning on texts with extremely long lengths, which are scarce and hardly available.[EOS] [BOS]The strategy begins by searching for a 256k length on the pre-trained LLM and fine-tuning it under this length.[EOS] [BOS]Then, as our non-uniform positional interpolation allows for an 8$\times$ extension in non-fine-tuning settings, we conduct a second search for new RoPE rescale factors on the fine-tuned extended LLM.[EOS] [BOS]This ultimately achieves the 2048k context window for LLaMA2 and Mistral~\cite{mistral}.[EOS] 

[BOS]Finally, to mitigate performance degradation on the original (shorter) context window, {LongRoPE} continues to adjust the RoPE rescale factors on the extended LLM.[EOS] [BOS]Similar to scaling up from 256k to 2048k, we scale down to 4k and 8k context windows on the 256k fine-tuned LLM using our search algorithm to encourage less positional interpolation.[EOS] [BOS]During inference, if the sequence length is less than 8k, we update RoPE with the searched rescale factors.[EOS] 

[BOS]Extensive experiments across different LLMs and various long-context tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of our method.[EOS] [BOS]We show that LongRoPE is highly effective in maintaining low perplexity from 4k to 2048k evaluation length,  achieving over 90\% passkey retrieval accuracy, and delivering  comparable accuracy on standard benchmarks designed within the 4096 context window.[EOS] [BOS]LongRoPE can be applied to any LLMs based on RoPE embedding.[EOS] [BOS]We will release our code and LongRoPE-2048k models.[EOS]

subsection: Preliminary
tagged_sentences: [BOS]Transformer models require explicit positional information, often in the form of position embedding, to represent the order of input tokens.[EOS]

[BOS]Our work focuses on the RoPE~\cite{rope} position embedding, which is widely used in recent  LLMs.[EOS]

[BOS]For a token at position index $n$, its corresponding RoPE encoding can be simplified as follows:

\begin{equation}
		\fontsize{7.8}{7.8} \selectfont
	\label{eq:rope}
	\left[ cos(n\theta_0), sin(n\theta_0), cos(n\theta_1), \cdot\cdot\cdot, cos(n\theta_{d/2-1}), sin(n\theta_{d/2-1}) \right]   
\end{equation}

where $d$ is the embedding dimension, 
 $n\theta_i$ is the rotary angle of token at position $n$, 
$\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}$ represents the rotation frequencies.[EOS]

[BOS]In RoPE, the default base value of $\theta$ is 10000.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{\emph{Context window extension ratio $s$} and positional interpolation}.[EOS]

[BOS]We define $s$ as  the ratio of extended context length $L'$ to the original length $L$: $s=\frac{L'}{L}$.[EOS]

[BOS]To extend the context window from $L$ to $L'$, current positional interpolation methods suggest downscaling rotation frequencies $\theta_i$ based on extension ratio $s$.[EOS]

[BOS]Let $\beta=\theta^{2/d}$, and $\lambda$ denote the actual rescale factor related to $s$, we   unify these positional interpolation methods as follows:

\begin{equation}	
	\fontsize{7.5}{7.5} \selectfont
	\label{eq:unified_rope}
	\begin{aligned}
		\left[cos\left(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}\right), sin \left(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}\right), cos\left(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^1}\right), \cdot\cdot\cdot,  sin \left(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^{d/2-1}}\right) \right]   
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Linear positional interpolation (PI)}.[EOS]

[BOS]PI~\cite{pi}  suggests linear interpolation of position indices within the pre-trained length limit.[EOS]

[BOS]For a target extension ratio $s$, the rotation angles of all positions are linearly reduced by $\lambda=s$  across all RoPE dimensions.[EOS]

[BOS]However, this makes the position information very ``crowded", hindering the model's ability distinguish closely positioned tokens.[EOS]

[BOS]Therefore, PI tends to underperform at high extension ratios.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{NTK-based interpolation and extrapolation}.[EOS]

[BOS]~\cite{ntk,dynamicntk} look at RoPE from an information encoding perspective  and apply the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory~\cite{jacot2018neural,tancik2020fourier}.[EOS]

[BOS]To mitigate the crowded-positions issue in PI, they suggest to distribute interpolation pressure across RoPE dimensions.[EOS]

[BOS]It scales lower (high frequency) dimensions less and higher (low frequency) dimensions more, resulting in  both positional interpolation and extrapolation, where $\lambda=s^{i}$.[EOS]

[BOS]The improved dynamic NTK~\cite{dynamicntk}  adjusts the extension ratio at each position based on the current sequence length.[EOS]

[BOS]Unlike PI, which necessitates fine-tuning, NTK-aware methods can extend context windows in non-fine-tuning scenarios, but usually with a maximum extension ratio of 4$\times$.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{YaRN}~\cite{yarn} introduces a significant improvement to positional interpolation performance.[EOS]

[BOS]It divides RoPE dimensions into three frequency-based groups, each with a different interpolation strategy.[EOS]

[BOS]High frequency dimensions undergo extrapolation ($\lambda$=1), while low frequency dimensions use linear interpolation (PI).[EOS]

[BOS]The RoPE dimensions that fall in-between employs the NTK.[EOS]

[BOS]The key of YaRN lies in its grouping of RoPE dimensions, which currently depends on human-led empirical experiments.[EOS]

[BOS]This may result in sub-optimal performance for new LLMs.[EOS]

subsection: Study on Non-uniform Positional Interpolation
tagged_sentences: [BOS]Inspired by NTK and YaRN, we notice their gains from non-linearity, specifically in considering different frequencies across RoPE dimensions for specialized interpolation and extrapolation.[EOS]

[BOS]However, current non-linearities heavily rely on human-designed rules.[EOS]
[BOS]This naturally raises two questions: (1) Is the current positional interpolation optimal? (2) Are there unexplored  non-linearities? [EOS]

[BOS]To answer these questions, we use evolution search (see Sec.~\ref{sec:method}) to discover better non-uniform positional interpolations for LLaMA2-7B.[EOS]
[BOS]The search is guided by perplexity, using 5 random samples from PG19~\cite{pg19} validation set.[EOS]
[BOS] Through our empirical analysis, we reveal the following key findings.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Finding 1}: \textit{RoPE dimensions exhibit substantial non-uniformities, which are not effectively handled by current positional interpolation methods.}[EOS]

[BOS]We  search the optimal $\lambda$ for each RoPE dimension in Eq.~\ref{eq:unified_rope}.[EOS]
[BOS]Table~\ref{tbl:exp1} compares the perplexity of LLaMA2-7B under different  methods on PG19 and Proof-pile~\cite{proof-pile} test sets, without fine-tuning.[EOS]
[BOS]Our searched solution shows significant improvements, suggesting that current linear (PI) and non-uniform (Dynamic-NTK and YaRN) interpolations are sub-optimal.[EOS]
[BOS]Notably, YaRN underperforms  than PI and NTK on PG19, as it doesn't reach the target context window length for non-fine-tuned LLM.[EOS]
[BOS]For example, YaRN's perplexity spikes after 7k in an 8k context size.[EOS]

[BOS]Through our search,  the rescaled factors $\lambda$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:unified_rope} become non-uniform, differing from the fixed scale $s$ in PI, NTK's formula calculation, and YaRN's group-wise calculation.[EOS]
[BOS]These non-uniform factors significantly improve LLaMA2's language modeling performance (i.e., perplexity) for 8k and 16k context windows without fine-tuning.[EOS]
[BOS]This is because the resulting positional embedding effectively preserves the original RoPE, especially key dimensions, thus reducing LLM's difficulty in distinguishing close token positions.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Finding 2}: \textit{RoPE for the initial tokens in the input sequence should be extrapolated with less interpolation.}[EOS]

[BOS]For the initial $\hat{n}$ tokens in input sequences, we hypothesize that their RoPE should do less interpolation.[EOS]
[BOS]This is because they receive large attention scores, making them crucial to attention layers, as observed in  Streaming LLM~\cite{streamingllm} and LM-Infinite~\cite{han2023lminfinite}.[EOS]
[BOS]To verify this, we extend the context window to 8k and 16k using PI and NTK, keeping the first  $\hat n$ (0,2, ..., 256) tokens without interpolation.[EOS]
[BOS]When $\hat n$=0, it reverts to the original PI and NTK.[EOS]
[BOS]Table~\ref{tbl:tokenposition} highlights two key observations: \textbf{(1)} retaining the starting tokens without position interpolation indeed improves the performance.[EOS]
[BOS]\textbf{(2)} The optimal number of starting tokens, $\hat n$, depends on the target extension length.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Finding 3}: \textit{Non-uniform positional interpolation effectively extends LLM context window in both fine-tuning and non-fine-tuning settings.}[EOS]

[BOS]While we've shown that our searched non-uniform position interpolation significantly improves the extension performance at 8k and 16k without fine-tuning, longer extensions require fine-tuning.[EOS]
[BOS]As such, we fine-tune LLaMA2-7B with our searched RoPE for a 64k context window size (see Appendix for settings).[EOS]
[BOS]As Table~\ref{tbl:finetune} shows, our method significantly outperforms PI and YaRN, both before and after fine-tuning LLaMA2-7B.[EOS]
[BOS]This is due to our effective use of non-uniform positional interpolation, minimizing information loss and providing a better initialization for fine-tuning.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Summary}.[EOS] [BOS]Our study uncovers two non-uniformities: varying RoPE dimensions and token positions.[EOS]
[BOS]Utilizing these non-uniformities effectively in positional interpolation greatly improves LLM context extension performance.[EOS]

subsection: Problem Formulation
tagged_sentences: [BOS]The two non-uniformities can lead to a vast solution space and introduce complexities in optimization.[EOS] To address it,  we frame the multidimensional non-uniform position interpolation optimization problem as a  search problem.

[BOS]For a LLM targeting a  context window size of $L'$ and lengthy input documents $\mathbf{X}$, where each $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}$ surpasses $L'$ in token length, we denote the original rotary angle of the $i^{th}$ dimension in RoPE embedding at token position $n$ as  $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$.[EOS] The optimization problem is then formulated as follows:

[BOS]\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
		\label{eq:problem}

\underset {\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}; \, |\mathbf{x}|\ge L'}{ \operatorname {arg\,min} } \,  \mathcal{L}\, \left( \text{LLM} (\text{RoPE}, \mathbf{X})\right),	\text{where \;} 
\\
\scalebox{0.93}{
$\underset {\begin{subarray}{l} i=0,\cdot\cdot,\frac{d}{2}-1;\\ n\in[ 0,|\mathbf{x}|);\end{subarray}}{\text{RoPE}_(n)}= {\left[\cdot\cdot,cos\left(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})\times\frac{n}{\beta^i}\right),  sin\left(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})\times\frac{n}{\beta^i}\right),\cdot\cdot\right] }$}\\
	\text{where \;} \mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})=\begin{cases}
	1&\mbox{\;  $n<\hat{n}$}\\
{\frac{1}{\lambda}_{i}}&\mbox{\; $n\geq\hat{n}$}
\end{cases}
	\end{gathered}
\end{equation}[EOS]

[BOS]where we introduce a set of rescale factors, $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$,  to cover the two forms of non-uniformities.[EOS]

[BOS]$\hat{\lambda_i}$  and $\hat{n}$ denote the non-uniformity of RoPE dimensions and token positions, respectively.[EOS]

[BOS]Specifically, we use $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$  to rescale the rotation angle for the $i^{th}$ RoPE dimension, where $\hat\lambda_{i}$ is the rescale factor and $\hat{n}$ is token position threshold.[EOS]

[BOS]For initial $\hat{n}$-1 token positions, the rescale factor $\hat\lambda_{i}$ will not take effect, and the original RoPE rotary angle $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$ is used.[EOS]

[BOS]For tokens at positions $n\ge\hat{n}$, the rescale factor is applied.[EOS]

[BOS]Given a target context window size of $L'$, our objective is to find the optimal rescale factors ($\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{0},\hat{n})$, $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{1},\hat{n})$ ,...$\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$...) from the $1^{st}$ to $d^{th}$ RoPE dimension.[EOS]

[BOS]As a result, the target $\text{LLM}$, with the rescaled $\text{RoPE}$, can achieve a minimum next token prediction loss, $\mathcal{L}$ (i.e., the perplexity), for input samples $\mathbf{X}$ with a token length of $L'$.[EOS]

subsection: Searching the Non-uniform Position Interpolation
tagged_sentences: [BOS]To solve the problem in Eq.~\ref{eq:problem}, we now introduce our simple yet highly effective method, which searches for the optimal RoPE rescale factors to fully exploit the multidimensional non-uniformities in position embedding.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textbf{Search space}.[EOS] [BOS]We  design a large search space to include the two non-uniformies.[EOS]  [BOS]Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace} illustrates the search space.[EOS]  [BOS]Specifically, we allow the search of a specialized rescale factor for each dimension in RoPE embedding.[EOS] [BOS]To simply search space design, we search $\lambda_i$ and $\hat{n}$ instead of searching for $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$, where  $\hat{\lambda}_{i}=1/\lambda_i$.[EOS] 
 [BOS]As shown in Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace}, $\lambda_i$ is allowed to search from a minimum value of 1.0 (i.e., direct extrapolation) to a maximum value of $s\times1.25$ (i.e., larger interpolation than PI) with a step size of 0.01, where $s$ is the target context window extension ratio.[EOS]

[BOS]$\hat{n}$ controls the number of initial token positions that are retained without position interpolation (i.e., use the original RoPE embedding).[EOS] [BOS]Empirically, we allow $\hat{n}$ to search from \{0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 64, 128, 256\}.[EOS] [BOS]When $\hat{n}=0$, all token positions use the searched rescale factors.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Evolution-based search}.[EOS] [BOS]Our search space in Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace} spans numerous positional interpolation solutions, posing a significant challenge for efficient exploration.[EOS]  [BOS]For example, a $s=4\times$ extension leads to  $400^{128/2}\times14$=4$\times10^{167}$ choices.[EOS] [BOS]With larger extension ratio,  the search space  expands exponentially.[EOS] [BOS]To address this, we use evolution search~\cite{guo2020single}  and introduce  two optimization techniques to greatly boost search efficiency.[EOS] [BOS]Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} illustrates the overall search procedure.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textit{Optimized initial population generation}.[EOS] [BOS]Instead of initializing a population  of $P$ rescale factors randomly, we add the three RoPE rescale factors corresponding to PI, NTK, and YaRN as individuals into the initial population.[EOS] [BOS]For the remaining $P$-3 individuals, we randomly mutate the three rescale factors with a probability of $p$.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textit{Monotonically non-decreasing constraint}.[EOS] [BOS]After generating the initial population, we compute LLM perplexity for each individual.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically, we apply the corresponding RoPE rescale factors to the target LLM and compute the perplexity of  input $\mathbf{X}$.[EOS] [BOS]The top-k individuals become parents for evolution.[EOS] [BOS]However, the vast search space can cause naive mutation and crossover to explore poor solutions, leading to unnecessary perplexity computations.[EOS] [BOS]This is particularly inefficient when $L'$ is large, given the time-consuming inference  of each perplexity calculation.[EOS]

[BOS]To address this, we impose a non-decreasing monotonicity constraint on the sampled RoPE rescaled factors: $\lambda_i\le \lambda_{i+1}$.[EOS] [BOS]Only RoPE that satisfies this constraint is applied to LLM for perplexity evaluation, significantly reducing the search costs.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically,   we require that $\lambda_i$ increases monotonically with the RoPE dimension (i.e., $i$=0,...,63).[EOS] [BOS]This dimensional monotonicity is based on the NTK theory~\cite{jacot2018neural,tancik2020fourier,ntk}, suggesting that lower dimensions with higher frequency requires less interpolation (i.e., a smaller $\lambda_i$), and higher dimensions with lower frequency can do more interpolation (i.e.,  a larger $\lambda_i$).[EOS]

\begin{algorithm}[t]
	\small
	\caption{The search algorithm}
	\textbf{Input:} target LLM, input samples $\mathbf{X}$,   population size $P$, mutation size $N_1$, crossover size $N_2$, max iterations $\mathcal{T}$, mutate probability $p$\\

	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\label{alg:search}
		\STATE Top-k=$\phi$;	
		\STATE $\text{P}_0$=\textit{Initialize\_population\_with\_optimization} ($P$, $\mathbf{X}$, $p$); 
		\FOR{$i$=$1$ to $\mathcal{T}$}
		\STATE \textit{Compute\_perplexity} (LLM, $\text{P}_{i-1}$, $\mathbf{X}$);
		\STATE  Top-k = \textit{Update\_Topk} (Top-k, $\text{P}_{i-1}$);
		\STATE$\text{P}_{mutation}$=\textit{Mutation\_with\_mono\_constraint} (Top-k, $N_1$, $p$); 
		\STATE $\text{P}_{crossover}$=\textit{Crossover\_with\_mono\_constraint} (Top-k, $N_2$);
		\STATE $\text{P}_i$ = $\text{P}_{mutation}$ $\cup$ $\text{P}_{crossover}$ $\cup$ Top-k;
		\ENDFOR
		\STATE Return the individual with lowest perplexity in Top-k;
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

[BOS]\textbf{8$\times$ extension without fine-tuning}.[EOS] [BOS]Our evolutionary search  effectively identifies non-uniform RoPE rescale factors, preserving key dimensions and positions to minimize interpolation-induced information loss.[EOS] [BOS]As depicted in Fig.\ref{fig:non-ft}, our method is able to extend LLaMA2's context window from 4k to 32k without fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]In contrast, existing methods such as PI, and non-uniform NTK and YaRN cause perplexity to spike after 2$\times$ extension.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Progressive extension to 2048k}.[EOS] [BOS]We now introduce our method  to extend the context window of pre-trained LLMs from the traditional 4k to over 2048k.[EOS] [BOS]As demonstrated, our non-uniform positional interpolation can achieve 8$\times$ extension without fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]For larger extensions (i.e., 512$\times$) is required, fine-tuning is necessary.[EOS] [BOS]One method is to search for RoPE rescaled factors under the target 2048k size and then fine-tune.[EOS] [BOS]However, this faces challenges due to the prohibitively expensive training resources.[EOS] [BOS]Moreover,  based on our experiences, it's challenging to well fine-tune the LLMs under a  large extension ratio (see Appendix).[EOS] 

[BOS]Fortunately, LongRoPE is effective for both the original and fine-tuned extended LLM.[EOS] [BOS]Therefore, we introduce an efficient, progressive method that achieves the target 2048k with just 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training length.[EOS]

 [BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Extending pre-trained LLM to 256k with LongRoPE search}.[EOS] [BOS]Taking LLaMA2 as an example, we conduct search for target context window size of 128k and 256k.[EOS] [BOS]The extension ratio at this stage is 32$\times$ and 64$\times$, respectively.[EOS]

 [BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Fine-tuning to 256k}.[EOS] [BOS]Then, we fine-tune the pre-trained LLM to achieve the context window size of 256k.[EOS] 	[BOS]Specifically, we first fine-tune LLaMA2 for 400 steps using the RoPE rescaled factors for 128k.[EOS] [BOS]Then, we replace the RoPE rescaled factors to 256k on the finished checkpoint and conduct an additional 600 steps of fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]This method proves more efficient than directly fine-tuning to 256k.[EOS]

 [BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Extending fine-tuned extended LLM to 2048k with LongRoPE search}.[EOS] [BOS]Finally, we perform a secondary  search on the fine-tuned 256k-length LLM.[EOS] [BOS]This ultimately results in an extremely large context window size of 2048k without further fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]The final extension ratio is  $512\times$.[EOS]	

[BOS]\textbf{Shorter context window recovery}.[EOS]  [BOS]After extending to an extremely long 2048k context window, we notice a performance drop within the original context window.[EOS] [BOS]This is a known issue of positional interpolation~\cite{pi}, as it forces position embedding in higher dimensions within the original context window to reside in a much narrower region, negatively affecting the language model's performance.[EOS] [BOS]With a 512$\times$ extension ratio, positions within the original 4k context window become particularly crowded.[EOS]

[BOS]To mitigate this, we perform an extra evolution search on the extended LLM to adjust RoPE rescale factors for short context lengths (e.g., 4k and 8k).[EOS] [BOS]We reduce the maximum allowed searched $\lambda$ due to less positional interpolation required for shorter lengths.[EOS] [BOS]During inference, the LLM dynamically adjusts the corresponding RoPE rescale factors.[EOS]

\begin{gathered}
		\label{eq:problem}

\underset {\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}; \, |\mathbf{x}|\ge L'}{ \operatorname {arg\,min} } \,  \mathcal{L}\, \left( \text{LLM} (\text{RoPE}, \mathbf{X})\right),	\text{where \;} 
\\
\scalebox{0.93}{
$\underset {\begin{subarray}{l} i=0,\cdot\cdot,\frac{d}{2}-1;\\ n\in[ 0,|\mathbf{x}|);\end{subarray}}{\text{RoPE}_(n)}= {\left[\cdot\cdot,cos\left(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})\times\frac{n}{\beta^i}\right),  sin\left(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})\times\frac{n}{\beta^i}\right),\cdot\cdot\right] }$}\\
	\text{where \;} \mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})=\begin{cases}
	1&\mbox{\;  $n<\hat{n}$}\\
{\frac{1}{\lambda}_{i}}&\mbox{\; $n\geq\hat{n}$}
\end{cases}
	\end{gathered}
\end{equation}[EOS]

[BOS]where we introduce a set of rescale factors, $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$,  to cover the two forms of non-uniformities.[EOS] [BOS]$\hat{\lambda_i}$  and $\hat{n}$ denote the non-uniformity of RoPE dimensions and token positions, respectively.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically, we use $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$  to rescale the rotation angle for the $i^{th}$ RoPE dimension, where $\hat\lambda_{i}$ is the rescale factor and $\hat{n}$ is token position threshold.[EOS] [BOS]For initial $\hat{n}$-1 token positions, the rescale factor $\hat\lambda_{i}$ will not take effect, and the original RoPE rotary angle $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$ is used.[EOS] [BOS]For tokens at positions $n\ge\hat{n}$, the rescale factor is applied.[EOS]

[BOS]Given a target context window size of $L'$, our objective is to find the optimal rescale factors ($\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{0},\hat{n})$, $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{1},\hat{n})$ ,...$\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$...) from the $1^{st}$ to $d^{th}$ RoPE dimension.[EOS] [BOS]As a result, the target $\text{LLM}$, with the rescaled $\text{RoPE}$, can achieve a minimum next token prediction loss, $\mathcal{L}$ (i.e., the perplexity), for input samples $\mathbf{X}$ with a token length of $L'$.[EOS]

tagged_sentences: [BOS]To solve the problem in Eq.~\ref{eq:problem}, we now introduce our simple yet highly effective method, which searches for the optimal RoPE rescale factors to fully exploit the multidimensional non-uniformities in position embedding.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textbf{Search space}.[EOS] [BOS]We  design a large search space to include the two non-uniformies.[EOS] [BOS]Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace} illustrates the search space.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically, we allow the search of a specialized rescale factor for each dimension in RoPE embedding.[EOS] [BOS]To simply search space design, we search $\lambda_i$ and $\hat{n}$ instead of searching for $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$, where  $\hat{\lambda}_{i}=1/\lambda_i$.[EOS] 
 [BOS]As shown in Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace}, $\lambda_i$ is allowed to search from a minimum value of 1.0 (i.e., direct extrapolation) to a maximum value of $s\times1.25$ (i.e., larger interpolation than PI) with a step size of 0.01, where $s$ is the target context window extension ratio.[EOS]

[BOS]$\hat{n}$ controls the number of initial token positions that are retained without position interpolation (i.e., use the original RoPE embedding).[EOS] [BOS]Empirically, we allow $\hat{n}$ to search from \{0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 64, 128, 256\}.[EOS] [BOS]When $\hat{n}=0$, all token positions use the searched rescale factors.[EOS]

[BOS]\textbf{Evolution-based search}.[EOS] [BOS]Our search space in Table~\ref{tbl:searchspace} spans numerous positional interpolation solutions, posing a significant challenge for efficient exploration.[EOS]  [BOS]For example, a $s=4\times$ extension leads to  $400^{128/2}\times14$=4$\times10^{167}$ choices.[EOS] [BOS]With larger extension ratio,  the search space  expands exponentially.[EOS] [BOS]To address this, we use evolution search~\cite{guo2020single}  and introduce  two optimization techniques to greatly boost search efficiency.[EOS] [BOS]Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} illustrates the overall search procedure.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textit{Optimized initial population generation}.[EOS] [BOS]Instead of initializing a population  of $P$ rescale factors randomly, we add the three RoPE rescale factors corresponding to PI, NTK, and YaRN as individuals into the initial population.[EOS] [BOS]For the remaining $P$-3 individuals, we randomly mutate the three rescale factors with a probability of $p$.[EOS] 

[BOS]\textit{Monotonically non-decreasing constraint}.[EOS] [BOS]After generating the initial population, we compute LLM perplexity for each individual.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically, we apply the corresponding RoPE rescale factors to the target LLM and compute the perplexity of  input $\mathbf{X}$.[EOS] [BOS]The top-k individuals become parents for evolution.[EOS] [BOS]However, the vast search space can cause naive mutation and crossover to explore poor solutions, leading to unnecessary perplexity computations.[EOS] [BOS]This is particularly inefficient when $L'$ is large, given the time-consuming inference  of each perplexity calculation.[EOS]

[BOS]To address this, we impose a non-decreasing monotonicity constraint on the sampled RoPE rescaled factors: $\lambda_i\le \lambda_{i+1}$.[EOS] [BOS]Only RoPE that satisfies this constraint is applied to LLM for perplexity evaluation, significantly reducing the search costs.[EOS] [BOS]Specifically,   we require that $\lambda_i$ increases monotonically with the RoPE dimension (i.e., $i$=0,...,63).[EOS] [BOS]This dimensional monotonicity is based on the NTK theory~\cite{jacot2018neural,tancik2020fourier,ntk}, suggesting that lower dimensions with higher frequency requires less interpolation (i.e., a smaller $\lambda_i$), and higher dimensions with lower frequency can do more interpolation (i.e.,  a larger $\lambda_i$).[EOS]

\begin{algorithm}[t]
	\small
	\caption{The search algorithm}
	\textbf{Input:} target LLM, input samples $\mathbf{X}$,   population size $P$, mutation size $N_1$, crossover size $N_2$, max iterations $\mathcal{T}$, mutate probability $p$\\

	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\label{alg:search}
		\STATE Top-k=$\phi$;	
		\STATE $\text{P}_0$=\textit{Initialize\_population\_with\_optimization} ($P$, $\mathbf{X}$, $p$); 
		\FOR{$i$=$1$ to $\mathcal{T}$}
		\STATE \textit{Compute\_perplexity} (LLM, $\text{P}_{i-1}$, $\mathbf{X}$);
		\STATE  Top-k = \textit{Update\_Topk} (Top-k, $\text{P}_{i-1}$);
		\STATE$\text{P}_{mutation}$=\textit{Mutation\_with\_mono\_constraint} (Top-k, $N_1$, $p$); 
		\STATE $\text{P}_{crossover}$=\textit{Crossover\_with\_mono\_constraint} (Top-k, $N_2$);
		\STATE $\text{P}_i$ = $\text{P}_{mutation}$ $\cup$ $\text{P}_{crossover}$ $\cup$ Top-k;
		\ENDFOR
		\STATE Return the individual with lowest perplexity in Top-k;
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

[BOS]\textbf{Progressive extension to 2048k}.[EOS] [BOS]We now introduce our method  to extend the context window of pre-trained LLMs from the traditional 4k to over 2048k.[EOS] [BOS]As demonstrated, our non-uniform positional interpolation can achieve 8$\times$ extension without fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]For larger extensions (i.e., 512$\times$) is required, fine-tuning is necessary.[EOS] [BOS]One method is to search for RoPE rescaled factors under the target 2048k size and then fine-tune.[EOS] [BOS]However, this faces challenges due to the prohibitively expensive training resources.[EOS] [BOS]Moreover,  based on our experiences, it's challenging to well fine-tune the LLMs under a  large extension ratio (see Appendix).[EOS] 

[BOS]Fortunately, LongRoPE is effective for both the original and fine-tuned extended LLM.[EOS] [BOS]Therefore, we introduce an efficient, progressive method that achieves the target 2048k with just 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training length.[EOS]

[BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Extending pre-trained LLM to 256k with LongRoPE search}.[EOS] [BOS]Taking LLaMA2 as an example, we conduct search for target context window size of 128k and 256k.[EOS] [BOS]The extension ratio at this stage is 32$\times$ and 64$\times$, respectively.[EOS]

 [BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Fine-tuning to 256k}.[EOS] [BOS]Then, we fine-tune the pre-trained LLM to achieve the context window size of 256k.[EOS] 	[BOS]Specifically, we first fine-tune LLaMA2 for 400 steps using the RoPE rescaled factors for 128k.[EOS] [BOS]Then, we replace the RoPE rescaled factors to 256k on the finished checkpoint and conduct an additional 600 steps of fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]This method proves more efficient than directly fine-tuning to 256k.[EOS]

 [BOS]$\Diamond$ \textit{Extending fine-tuned extended LLM to 2048k with LongRoPE search}.[EOS] [BOS]Finally, we perform a secondary  search on the fine-tuned 256k-length LLM.[EOS] [BOS]This ultimately results in an extremely large context window size of 2048k without further fine-tuning.[EOS] [BOS]The final extension ratio is  $512\times$.[EOS]	

[BOS]\textbf{Shorter context window recovery}.[EOS]  [BOS]After extending to an extremely long 2048k context window, we notice a performance drop within the original context window.[EOS] [BOS]This is a known issue of positional interpolation~\cite{pi}, as it forces position embedding in higher dimensions within the original context window to reside in a much narrower region, negatively affecting the language model's performance.[EOS] [BOS]With a 512$\times$ extension ratio, positions within the original 4k context window become particularly crowded.[EOS]

[BOS]To mitigate this, we perform an extra evolution search on the extended LLM to adjust RoPE rescale factors for short context lengths (e.g., 4k and 8k).[EOS] [BOS]We reduce the maximum allowed searched $\lambda$ due to less positional interpolation required for shorter lengths.[EOS] [BOS]During inference, the LLM dynamically adjusts the corresponding RoPE rescale factors.[EOS]
