section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: PPF is an extremely rare and infrequent complication of pancreatic disease in the paediatric population.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Until now, we have only 47 documented cases of PPF in the paediatric age group.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: It can be observed in acute and chronic pancreatitis or paediatric trauma.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Exact incidence is unknown in children; it, however, complicates around 0.4% cases of pancreatitis overall.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Serine peptidase inhibitor Kazal type 1 (SPINK1) and PRSS1 mutations feature prominently, accounting for up to 30% of paediatric chronic pancreatitis.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Blunt abdominal injury is also an important non-hereditary trigger, comprising 32% of the 25-patient cohort analysed by Yang et al.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Idiopathic cases continue to appear which may be due to probable under-recognised genetic or ductal variants.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Pancreatic fluid can track from a disrupted pancreatic duct or pseudocyst through natural diaphragmatic hiatuses, usually the aortic or oesophageal hiatus, or less commonly via direct trans-diaphragmatic erosion, creating a fistulous connection to the pleural cavity.

----- Row 7 -----

raw_knowledge_statement: The source of accumulation of fluid in PPF is due to external forces or bacterial infection, resulting in the rupture of the pancreatic duct or the pancreatic pseudocyst and leakage of the pancreatic fluid.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: If the rupture occurs in the anterior part of the pancreas, pancreatic fluid will leak into the abdominal cavity, resulting in ascites.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: If the rupture occurs in the posterior part of the pancreas, pancreatic fluid will instead leak into the retroperitoneal space, following the path of least resistance upward, enter the mediastinum through the aorta or oesophageal hiatus, and break through the pleura to form a pleural effusion.

----- Row 7 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: An internal pancreatic fistula which acts like a leaky pipe, diverting pancreatic secretions into the chest.
