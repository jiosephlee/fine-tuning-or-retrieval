Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### Why were costs awarded to the appellant despite only a partial victory on appeal?
Question:
The judgment states “Costs to appellant,” even though the court affirmed factor two and only vacated factors six and one. Under Fed. R. App. P. 39(a)(4) and Federal Circuit Rule 39, how does the court determine the prevailing party when the result is affirmed-in-part, vacated-in-part, and remanded? What principles guide awarding costs in mixed outcomes like this?
Answer:
Short answer: Because Rule 39(a)(4) leaves allocation of costs to the court when an appeal is affirmed-in-part and vacated-in-part, the Federal Circuit exercised that discretion here and treated Apex as the “prevailing party” for purposes of appellate costs. The opinion shows why: the court affirmed only one DuPont factor but vacated two others and remanded for further proceedings — a substantive change in the Board’s disposition — and the court therefore ordered “Costs to appellant.”

Explanation and legal basis

- The controlling rule. Fed. R. App. P. 39(a)(4) (as reflected in the Federal Circuit’s rules and practice) provides that when an appeal is “affirmed in part, reversed in part, modified, or vacated,” costs “are taxed only as the court orders.” That is, in mixed outcomes the court has discretion to allocate costs rather than being bound automatically to tax them to appellant or appellee.

- What the court did in this opinion. The panel expressly affirmed the Board’s finding on the second DuPont factor, but it “vacate[d] the Board’s finding with respect to the sixth DuPont factor and remand[ed] for reconsideration” and likewise remanded the first factor (because reconsideration of factor six could affect commercial strength and overall commercial impression). See opinion, Sections III.A–C. The judgment concludes: “AFFIRMED-IN-PART, VACATED-IN-PART, AND REMANDED” and then plainly: “COSTS — Costs to appellant.”

- Principles that guide the discretionary award in mixed outcomes. Where Rule 39 gives the court a choice, appellate courts generally allocate costs to reflect which party obtained the consequential relief on appeal. Relevant considerations (all implicit in the court’s exercise of discretion here) include:
  - whether the appellant obtained meaningful relief (reversal or vacatur of dispositive findings or remand for reconsideration that alters the Board’s outcome),
  - whether the appeal changed the legal or factual posture in a way that vindicates the appellant’s principal objectives,
  - whether one party prevailed on the issues most central to the dispute rather than only on peripheral points,
  - and practical fairness (e.g., whether one party substantially prevailed overall).

Applied to Apex Bank v. CC Serve: although the panel left one DuPont factor intact, it vacated two of the Board’s factor findings (six and one) and remanded for reconsideration in light of the Board’s own finding that the parties’ services are highly similar. That vacatur/remand is more than a narrow technical correction — it requires the Board to revisit the strength and commercial impression of the registered mark and could alter the ultimate registration outcome. The court therefore exercised the Rule 39(a)(4) discretion to treat Apex as the prevailing party for purposes of taxing appellate costs and entered the single-line award “Costs to appellant.”

Takeaway for similar appeals: when an appeal produces mixed results, you cannot assume costs will automatically be taxed to the loser; Rule 39 gives the court discretion, and courts will often award costs to the party that achieved the substantial or dispositive relief on appeal (including vacatur/remand that meaningfully advances that party’s position).
