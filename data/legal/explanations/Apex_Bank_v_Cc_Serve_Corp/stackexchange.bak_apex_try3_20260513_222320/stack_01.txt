Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### Why does the Federal Circuit have jurisdiction over this TTAB appeal, and why are C.C.P.A. cases like DuPont binding?
Question:
The opinion cites 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B) for jurisdiction and relies heavily on C.C.P.A. precedent such as DuPont. Can someone explain the statutory path that routes TTAB appeals to the Federal Circuit (as opposed to regional circuits), and why pre-1982 C.C.P.A. trademark decisions are treated as binding on the Federal Circuit?
Answer:
Short answer
- Jurisdiction: The Federal Circuit heard this appeal because it is an appeal from a Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) decision and the court expressly invoked 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B) as its statutory basis for jurisdiction (Opinion, I). That is the route that channels TTAB/PTO appeals to the Federal Circuit.
- C.C.P.A. precedents like DuPont are treated as binding authority for TTAB appeals because the Federal Circuit relies on the established body of PTO/TTAB law developed by the C.C.P.A.; the opinion shows this by using Application of E.I. DuPont DeNemours & Co., 476 F.2d 1357 (C.C.P.A. 1973) as the governing statement of the likelihood-of-confusion standard (Opinion, II).

Why that matters (short explanation of the doctrinal posture)
- Statutory route: The opinion begins by stating jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B). That statute is the court’s cited hook for appellate review of USPTO/TTAB decisions; accordingly the Federal Circuit is the appellate forum for TTAB decisions in this case (Opinion, I).
- Governing legal standard: The DuPont decision (cited as a C.C.P.A. case in the opinion) supplies the multi‑factor likelihood‑of‑confusion test the Board and this court apply. The opinion treats DuPont as the controlling statement of law: “A trademark opposition under Section 2(d) of the Lanham Act requires registration refusal when ‘confusion is likely…’ Application of E.I. DuPont DeNemours & Co., 476 F.2d 1357, 1360 (C.C.P.A. 1973)” (Opinion, II).
- Role of precedent and review standards: The opinion makes clear how the Federal Circuit handles TTAB appeals: legal conclusions are reviewed de novo and factual findings (including findings under each DuPont factor) are tested for substantial evidence. The court therefore applies DuPont as the legal standard and examines the Board’s factual findings against the substantial‑evidence standard (Opinion, II; see also citations to M2 Software and Bose in Opinion, II).

Putting it together
- Because the Federal Circuit is the statutorily designated appellate forum for PTO/TTAB decisions in cases of this type (the court cites 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B)), the court applies the body of law that governs TTAB adjudications. DuPont is part of that body of law (it is the canonical statement of the likelihood‑of‑confusion factors for TTAB matters), so the Federal Circuit treats DuPont and like C.C.P.A. decisions as the controlling precedent when resolving TTAB appeals — which is exactly what the opinion does throughout its analysis (Opinion, II–III).
