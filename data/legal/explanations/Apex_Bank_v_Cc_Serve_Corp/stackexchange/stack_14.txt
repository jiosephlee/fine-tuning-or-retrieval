### Why did the court affirm factor two despite Apex’s argument that CC Serve is 'not providing banking services' itself?
Question:
Apex said CC Serve partners with a bank that issues the cards, so CC Serve isn’t doing 'banking.' The court still affirmed high similarity. Is the rule that the Board looks to the identifications in the registration/application, not the actual use details, and that 'credit card services' are encompassed within 'banking and financing services' as a matter of definition? How do partnerships with issuing banks affect this analysis, if at all?
Answer:
Short answer: The court affirmed the Board’s finding on DuPont factor two because the Board reasonably concluded—based on the parties’ pleaded identifications, dictionary meanings, and the record evidence—that CC Serve’s “credit card services” fall within the same field as Apex’s identified “banking and financing services,” and that the services are thus highly similar (indeed “legally identical, in part”). Apex’s contractual arrangement with issuing banks did not negate CC Serve’s own services (issuing/servicing credit-card accounts), so it did not defeat the Board’s similarity finding.

Why that outcome follows from the law and the opinion

- Legal standard: The second DuPont factor asks whether the goods/services are related or marketed in a way that could cause consumers to believe they come from the same source. They need not be identical; a showing that they are related or that their marketing overlaps is sufficient. (Opinion cites Coach Servs., 668 F.3d 1356, 1369; J.A. 24.)

- What the Board actually did: Faced with Apex’s ITU application for “banking and financing services” and CC Serve’s registration for “credit card services,” the Board first defined what the identified services mean (not by investigating every actual business arrangement, but by construing the identifications and available definitions). The Board determined that “credit card services” includes issuing credit cards, processing transactions, managing accounts, providing monitoring/alerts, etc. (J.A. 25). It also looked to dictionary definitions showing that “banking,” “bank,” and “finance” can encompass extending credit and providing funds via credit cards. On that basis the Board concluded the services are “legally identical, in part,” and highly similar (J.A. 27–28, 33–34).

- Why CC Serve’s partnership with banks didn’t change the result: Apex argued that because CC Serve “joins with a bank” and the bank issues the cards, CC Serve is not “providing banking services” itself. The court rejected that argument as “unavailing” because the Board’s focus was on the services as identified and as commonly understood (J.A. 25, 27–28). The record shows CC Serve “joins with a bank, and the bank issues ASPIRE‑branded credit cards and associated accounts” but that “the accounts are serviced by CC Serve and its affiliates” (J.A. 6930). Those activities—issuing/servicing credit-card accounts, authorization, processing, account management—are within the scope of “credit card services” as the Board defined them, and overlap with banking/financing as defined. Thus, the contractual structure (bank issues card; CC Serve services the accounts) does not make CC Serve’s services unrelated to banking/finance for DuPont purposes.

- Substantial-evidence review and the court’s holding: The Federal Circuit reviews the Board’s factual findings for substantial evidence. Because the Board based its finding on the identifications, dictionary definitions, and record evidence (including how CC Serve actually services accounts), the court held there was substantial evidence to affirm the second DuPont factor (J.A. 24–28; opinion section III.A). The court did not adopt a rule that only the identification controls regardless of use; rather, it affirmed that where the identification and record evidence support overlap (and CC Serve’s own role includes account servicing and related credit-card functions), the services are sufficiently related to weigh heavily toward confusion.

Bottom line: The Board and the court treated the parties’ pleaded/service identifications and the record evidence (including CC Serve’s role in servicing ASPIRE accounts) as dispositive for determining relatedness. Apex’s emphasis on the issuing bank partnership did not overcome the Board’s reasonable conclusion that CC Serve’s credit‑card activities overlap with banking and financing services, so factor two was affirmed.
