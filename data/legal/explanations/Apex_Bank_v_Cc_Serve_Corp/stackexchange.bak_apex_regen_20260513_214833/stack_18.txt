Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### Why were costs awarded to the appellant despite an affirm-in-part, vacate-in-part outcome?
Question:
The judgment awards costs to Apex even though the Federal Circuit affirmed the Board on factor two and only vacated on factors six and one. Under Federal Circuit Rule 39 or related authority, how is the 'prevailing party' determined in a mixed-result administrative appeal, and does a remand for further proceedings typically justify awarding costs to the appellant?
Answer:
Short answer
The Federal Circuit exercised its discretionary authority to award costs to Apex because Apex obtained meaningful relief on appeal — the court “affirm[ed]-in-part, vacate[d]-in-part, and remand[ed]” the Board’s decision — and thus was the prevailing party for purposes of awarding costs. The opinion’s mandate and the separate “COSTS / Costs to appellant.” line make that judgment explicit.

Why that is legally defensible
- Rule controlling: Fed. Cir. R. 39 governs taxation of costs and identifies the “prevailing party” as the one entitled to costs. Which party is the prevailing party is for the court to decide in the exercise of its discretion when an appeal yields mixed results.
- How the opinion applies the rule: the court here did more than merely affirm; it vacated two DuPont-factor findings (six and one) and remanded for further proceedings. The opinion treats those vacaturs as meaningful appellate relief: it explains that the Board’s sixth-factor analysis was legally flawed and that reconsideration of factor six may affect factor one and the overall likelihood-of-confusion analysis. See slip op. at Sections III.B–C (vacating factor six as legally flawed and remanding factor one for reconsideration in light of that error).
- Practical significance: a remand that requires the Board to reconsider and potentially alter its ultimate legal conclusion is classic appellate relief. The court’s disposition—“AFFIRMED-IN-PART, VACATED-IN-PART, AND REMANDED” — together with the explicit award (“Costs to appellant.”) demonstrates the court’s view that Apex achieved prevailing-party status despite the partial affirmance.

Do remands generally justify costs?
- Yes, they can. When an appellant obtains vacatur and remand for further proceedings based on reversible error, a court may reasonably treat the appellant as having prevailed on the appeal (even if not obtaining complete reversal) and award costs accordingly. The opinion here articulates reversible legal error (narrowing the sixth-factor scope) and ties that error to potential downstream effects on the first factor and the overall analysis, which supports awarding costs to Apex.

Takeaway for mixed-result appeals
- The determining point is whether the appellant obtained meaningful relief from the appellate court. A partial affirmance plus vacatur/remand can make the appellant the prevailing party for costs. The Federal Circuit made that determination in this opinion: it found reversible error as to factors six and one, remanded for reconsideration, and awarded costs to Apex.
