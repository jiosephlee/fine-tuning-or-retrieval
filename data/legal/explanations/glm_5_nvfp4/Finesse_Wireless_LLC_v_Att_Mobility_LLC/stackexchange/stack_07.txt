Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### Why vacate damages rather than remand for a new trial?
Question:
The Federal Circuit reversed JMOL on noninfringement and simply 'vacated' the damages award. Since the infringement verdict is gone, what happens to the damages? Does 'vacated' mean the damages issue is moot, or does it leave open the possibility of a new damages trial if there's a new infringement finding on remand? And why didn't the court need to address the defendants' alternative argument for a new trial on damages?
Answer:
### Answer

The Federal Circuit vacated the damages award because once the court reversed JMOL and found noninfringement as a matter of law, there was no longer any basis for damages. This is a straightforward consequence of the court's holding on liability.

**What "vacated" means here**

When a court vacates a damages award after reversing a finding of liability, it nullifies the award entirely. The damages issue becomes moot because damages are purely derivative of liability—there can be no compensation for infringement where there is no infringement.

This differs from a remand for a new trial, which would be appropriate if the court found a defect in the damages proceeding itself (such as erroneous jury instructions or prejudicial evidence) but left the underlying liability finding intact.

**Why the court didn't reach the alternative damages arguments**

The court explicitly stated the basis for vacatur: "Because we reverse the denial of JMOL of noninfringement for all asserted claims of the '134 and '775 patents, we vacate the damages award."

The court did not need to address Appellants' alternative argument for a new trial on damages because the noninfringement holding eliminated any possibility of recovery. Reaching the damages issues would have been unnecessary and would have constituted an advisory opinion. This is consistent with the principle of judicial economy—courts avoid deciding issues that are rendered moot by other holdings.

**What happens on remand**

On remand, there is no damages issue to retry. The reversal of JMOL on noninfringement means the defendants do not infringe as a matter of law. The vacatur of damages is not a determination that the damages calculation was erroneous; it simply reflects that the damages award cannot stand when the predicate finding of infringement has been eliminated. If Finesse cannot establish infringement on remand (and here, the Federal Circuit found the evidence legally insufficient to support any infringement finding), there can be no damages.
