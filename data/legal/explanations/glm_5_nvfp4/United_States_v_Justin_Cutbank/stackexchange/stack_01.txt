Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Why is prior possession of the same firearm considered 'intrinsic evidence' rather than Rule 404(b) propensity evidence?
Question:
I'm reading United States v. Cutbank and the court holds that D.F.'s testimony about seeing Cutbank with the sawed-off rifle before the incident is 'intrinsic evidence' and not subject to Rule 404(b). But isn't the whole point of 404(b) to prevent the jury from convicting someone because they're a 'bad person' who has done bad things before? If the defendant possessed the same gun before, that seems like it would invite the jury to think 'he's the type of person who walks around with sawed-off rifles.' How does the 'same weapon' factor make this intrinsic rather than extrinsic propensity evidence? And what's the practical boundary between intrinsic and 404(b) evidence in possession cases?
Answer:
## The Distinction Between Intrinsic Evidence and 404(b) Evidence

Your confusion is understandable, but the key distinction here is that **evidence of prior possession of the *same* firearm is fundamentally different from evidence of prior possession of a *different* firearm**.

### The "Same Weapon" Factor

The court's holding relies on the principle, established in *United States v. Battle*, that "Prior possession of a firearm is directly relevant to proving later possession of that same weapon because it helps establish ownership or control of the weapon." 774 F.3d at 511.

This is not propensity evidence because it does not ask the jury to reason: "Cutbank possessed a gun before, therefore he is the type of person who would possess a gun on this occasion." Rather, it asks the jury to reason: "Cutbank possessed *this specific weapon* before, which makes it more likely that he possessed *this same weapon* on the charged date."

### Why This Is Intrinsic

Evidence is "intrinsic" when it is directly relevant to an element of the charged offense and forms part of the narrative of the crime itself. Here, the charge was possession of the sawed-off Marlin .22 caliber rifle found along Cutbank's flight path. D.F.'s testimony that she had seen Cutbank with that same rifle on prior occasions directly connects Cutbank to that weapon—it helps prove **identity** and **possession**, both elements of the charged offense.

This is distinct from classic 404(b) scenarios where:
- The government offers evidence that the defendant possessed a *different* firearm on a prior occasion, OR
- The evidence serves only to show "bad character" or criminal disposition

### The Practical Boundary

The practical line drawn by courts in possession cases is:

| Evidence Type | Classification | Why |
|---------------|----------------|-----|
| Prior possession of the *same* weapon | Intrinsic | Goes to ownership, control, or continuous possession of the specific weapon charged |
| Prior possession of a *different* weapon | 404(b) | Only shows propensity or general familiarity with firearms |

The court's analysis is consistent with the purpose of 404(b): to prevent convictions based on character rather than evidence. Testimony that Cutbank previously possessed *this* rifle is not character evidence—it is direct proof linking him to the contraband at issue.
