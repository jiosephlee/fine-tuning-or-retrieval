Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer"

### What is the relationship between section 1216 investigations and section 1214 notice requirements?
Question:
AFL argued that if OSC investigated under section 1216, it might provide the notices required under section 1214. The court notes that AFL 'acknowledges cases holding that OSC's decision whether to follow section 1214's procedural requirements in a section 1216 investigation is discretionary.' Does this mean section 1216 investigations are procedurally different from prohibited personnel practice investigations under section 1214? If OSC investigates a FOIA violation under 1216, what procedural rights (if any) does a complainant have?
Answer:
### Answer

Based on the court's opinion, section 1216 investigations and section 1214 investigations are procedurally distinct in a key respect: **the notice and procedural requirements that apply to section 1214 investigations are discretionary when OSC investigates under section 1216.**

The opinion explains the statutory framework clearly:

- **Section 1214** governs investigations into "prohibited personnel practices" and includes specific procedural requirements, such as "provisions for notifying the complaining party about the progress of an investigation."

- **Section 1216** governs investigations into other forms of misconduct, including FOIA violations. Under § 1216(c), OSC "may investigate and seek corrective action under section 1214 and disciplinary action under section 1215 in the same way as if a prohibited personnel practice were involved."

The critical word is "may." The court notes that AFL "acknowledges cases holding that OSC's 'decision whether to follow section 1214's procedural requirements' in a section 1216 investigation is 'discretionary,'" citing *Carson v. U.S. Office of Special Counsel*, 633 F.3d 487, 495–96 (6th Cir. 2011). The court emphasizes that AFL "does not contend that OSC must engraft section 1214 notice requirements onto its section 1216 investigations."

Thus, if OSC investigates a FOIA violation under § 1216, **a complainant has no entitlement to the notice and information requirements that would apply in a § 1214 prohibited personnel practice investigation.** Any such procedural protections are within OSC's discretion to provide.

This distinction matters for standing purposes. AFL could not establish an informational injury based on a right to receive § 1214 notices because, as the court held, "Absent any alleged entitlement to receive information under section 1214, AFL lacks a concrete injury sufficient to support its second standing theory."
