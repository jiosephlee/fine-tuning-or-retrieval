Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### What does "strict adherence" mean in Louisiana insurance cancellation law, and why didn't the missing signature violate it?
Question:
The opinion cites Louisiana cases saying that "strict adherence" to statutory cancellation procedures is required, and that "any defect in this process results in an ineffective cancellation." But then the court says the lack of a signature on the certification doesn't violate the statute because the statute doesn't explicitly require one. How does this square with the strict adherence standard? If courts require strict adherence, shouldn't that mean the finance company needs to strictly follow every detail? Is there a difference between "strict adherence" and adding requirements that aren't in the text?
Answer:
## Strict adherence means strict adherence to what the statute actually requires

The distinction the court draws is straightforward: "strict adherence" means the finance company must comply precisely with **what the statute specifies**—not with additional formalities a court might think are good practice.

The court makes this explicit:

> "'Strict adherence' to the procedures for cancellation is not an invitation for courts to add to the procedures."

This is the key sentence. The court is drawing a line between (1) requiring that every statutory element be satisfied exactly, and (2) grafting onto the statute requirements the legislature did not include.

## Why the signature wasn't required

The statute requires the finance company to send "a copy of the notice of cancellation together with a statement **certifying**" four items. La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a). The word "certifying" does not, on its face, require a signature.

The court notes:
- No Louisiana case has held that a signature is required
- The statute identifies "the insurance premium finance company" as the actor, not an individual employee
- The *Benitez* case mentioned lack of signature as one of several deficiencies, but did not hold that absence of a signature alone would invalidate a cancellation where other elements were present

Because the statute does not explicitly or implicitly require a signature, requiring one would be judicial legislation—adding to the statutory scheme rather than enforcing it.

## The two purposes of strict adherence

The court cites *Britten v. Reavis* for the proposition that strict adherence serves two functions:
1. Fixing the exact time and date of cancellation
2. Granting a minimum time during which the default may be cured

The court's holding is consistent with both purposes. GoAuto's electronic system produces a verifiable record of when cancellation was requested and effected. Nothing about a missing signature undermines either purpose.

## In summary

"Strict adherence" is not the same as "hyper-technical compliance with imagined formalities." It means the finance company must satisfy every requirement the statute articulates—but courts may not supplement those requirements. The legislature could have required a signature; it did not. That choice is left to the legislature, not the courts.
