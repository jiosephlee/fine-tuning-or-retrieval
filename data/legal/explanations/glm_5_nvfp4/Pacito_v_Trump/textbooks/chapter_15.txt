Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

Chapter 15: # The Administrative Procedure Act Claims

## The Plaintiffs' APA Claims in This Case

The plaintiffs in *Pacito v. Trump* brought claims under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) challenging the government's termination of funding for the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP). Specifically, the plaintiffs alleged that the defunding of domestic resettlement services violated provisions of the APA. This claim was distinct from their challenge to Executive Order 14163's suspension of refugee admissions, which the court analyzed under the Immigration and Nationality Act and the President's statutory authority under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f).

The APA challenge focused on the government's obligation to provide "reception and placement" services to refugees who had already been admitted to the United States. When the State Department terminated all cooperative agreements with resettlement organizations in February 2025, the plaintiffs argued that this action violated statutory mandates governing refugee resettlement. The organizational plaintiffs—three entities that had cooperative agreements with the State Department to provide these services—had standing to challenge the terminations, as they were directly affected by the funding cutoff.

## The Elements of an APA Claim and How They Apply Here

The APA provides a framework for judicial review of agency actions. Under 5 U.S.C. § 706(2), a reviewing court shall hold unlawful and set aside agency actions that are "arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law." This latter phrase—"not in accordance with law"—is central to the plaintiffs' APA claim in this case.

To succeed on an APA claim alleging that agency action is "not in accordance with law," a plaintiff must demonstrate that the agency violated a statutory mandate. The court must determine what the relevant statute requires and then assess whether the agency's action comports with that requirement. If the statute imposes a mandatory duty on the agency, the agency lacks discretion to disregard that duty, and a court can order compliance.

In this case, the plaintiffs' APA claim required the court to interpret 8 U.S.C. § 1522, which governs domestic refugee resettlement services. The question was whether § 1522 creates mandatory obligations that the government must fulfill, or whether it merely authorizes the government to provide services at its discretion. This statutory interpretation question was essential to determining whether the government's termination of funding violated the APA.

## Why the Court Found the Government Unlikely to Prevail on the APA Claim Regarding § 1522

Applying the stay factors, the court concluded that the government was "not likely to prevail on at least one of plaintiffs' challenges under the APA." The court's analysis rested on its interpretation of 8 U.S.C. § 1522, which the court read as imposing mandatory obligations on the government to provide certain services to refugees who have been admitted to the United States.

The court identified several provisions of § 1522 that it found to be mandatory in nature. Section 1522(a)(1)(A) requires the Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement "to the extent of available appropriations" to provide employment training and placement, English language training, cash assistance, and ensure equitable participation by women in training programs. The court noted that § 1522(d)(2)(B) requires the Director to assume legal and financial responsibility for unaccompanied refugee minors "if necessary" during interim periods before placement. Section 1522(e)(7)(A) requires the Secretary of Health and Human Services to "develop and implement alternative projects" providing interim support, medical services, and case management for refugees who have been in the United States less than thirty-six months.

Crucially, the court observed that Congress had appropriated substantial funds for refugee assistance—$3.178 billion for fiscal year 2025, reduced by $800 million through the Rescissions Act of 2025. The "to the extent of available appropriations" language in § 1522(a)(1)(A) thus did not excuse non-performance, because appropriations were in fact available.

The court also found significant the government's admission that it had terminated all cooperative agreements providing resettlement services while expressing uncertainty about whether any progress had been made toward securing replacement service providers. The government had represented to the district court that it was "actively preparing a request for proposals for a new resettlement agency," yet at oral argument could not confirm whether any progress had been made. This uncertainty about the government's ability to fulfill its statutory obligations weighed against the government's request for a complete stay.

Based on this analysis, the court directed the government to "reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522 to refugees who have been admitted to the United States."

## The Distinction Between APA Review of Statutory Interpretation Versus Agency Discretion

This case illustrates an important distinction in APA litigation: the difference between challenges to agency actions as "contrary to law" versus challenges to agency actions as "arbitrary and capricious" or an abuse of discretion. When a statute mandates specific action, an agency has no discretion to act contrary to that mandate, and a court can order the agency to comply. However, when a statute grants an agency discretion to act, courts have limited ability to intervene.

The majority and dissent articulated fundamentally different views of whether § 1522 creates mandatory duties or discretionary authority. The majority read provisions using "shall" as imposing mandatory obligations, particularly where appropriations were available and services were statutorily required. Judge Lee's partial dissent, by contrast, emphasized that the section's title uses "Authorization"—which he defined as permissive—and that the specific funding provisions in subsections (b) through (e) state that the Director "is authorized" to provide assistance.

Judge Lee argued that the "shall" language in § 1522(a) applies only "[i]n providing assistance under this section"—meaning it imposes conditions on discretionary funding decisions, not independent mandates. Under this reading, if the government chooses to fund refugee programs, it must do so in accordance with certain conditions; but the government retains discretion whether to fund those programs at all.

This interpretive disagreement reflects a broader tension in administrative law: determining when statutory language creates enforceable duties versus when it grants agencies flexibility. The majority's approach focused on the mandatory phrasing of specific provisions, while the dissent looked to structural signals like the section title and permissive language in the funding provisions.

## How the APA Interacts with the Immigration Context

The immigration context adds complexity to APA analysis because of the significant deference courts traditionally accord to the political branches in immigration matters. The court acknowledged that the President has "broad discretion" in immigration matters, citing *Trump v. Hawaii*. However, the court distinguished between the President's authority to suspend refugee admissions under § 1182(f)—where the court found sweeping presidential discretion—and the government's obligations to provide services to refugees who have already been admitted.

This distinction is crucial. The court deferred to the President's determination that refugee admissions would be detrimental to the United States, holding that the government was likely to prevail on the challenge to Executive Order 14163's suspension of admissions. But the court applied a different analysis to post-admission resettlement services, finding that once refugees are admitted, different statutory provisions govern the government's obligations.

The court's approach recognizes that executive authority over admissions does not automatically extend to all aspects of immigration policy. Different statutory schemes may impose different constraints, and Congress can create mandatory obligations that limit executive discretion even in immigration matters. The key question becomes one of statutory interpretation: what did Congress require, and what did it leave to agency discretion?

## The Significance of the Court's Finding on the APA Claim for Future Litigation

The court's treatment of the APA claim has several implications for future litigation involving refugee policy and immigration administration. First, it suggests that courts may scrutinize executive actions affecting post-admission services more closely than actions affecting admissions themselves. While the President's § 1182(f) authority to suspend entry received extreme deference, statutory obligations to serve already-admitted refugees were treated as judicially enforceable mandates.

Second, the case demonstrates that appropriations matter. The court specifically noted that Congress had appropriated funds for refugee assistance, making the "to the extent of available appropriations" limitation inapplicable. Future litigants challenging executive defunding of statutory programs may similarly point to available appropriations as evidence that agencies cannot claim funding limitations to excuse non-compliance.

Third, the court's remedy—ordering reinstatement of cooperative agreements—shows that courts may order specific performance when statutory mandates are clear. Rather than simply prohibiting the government from taking certain actions, the court affirmatively required the government to reinstate agreements necessary to provide required services.

Finally, the split between the majority and Judge Lee's dissent highlights ongoing uncertainty about how to interpret statutory schemes that combine mandatory language with authorization language. Courts will continue to grapple with whether provisions like § 1522 create enforceable rights or discretionary programs, and the outcome of this appeal may provide additional guidance on this question when the court issues its merits opinion.
