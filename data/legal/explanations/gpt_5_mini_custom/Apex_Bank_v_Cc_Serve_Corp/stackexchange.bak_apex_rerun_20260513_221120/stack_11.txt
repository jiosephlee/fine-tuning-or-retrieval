Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### On remand, can the Board reopen the record to consider additional third-party evidence, or is it limited to the existing record?
Question:
The Federal Circuit vacates factor six (and thus one) for legal error in scoping. Procedurally, what happens next at the TTAB—does it reassess the sixth and first factors on the existing record, or can the parties introduce new evidence of third-party registrations/uses consistent with the broader 'similar services' scope? What rules or precedents govern record reopening on remand from the Federal Circuit in TTAB appeals?
Answer:
Short answer
The Federal Circuit’s opinion does not lock the Board into the existing administrative record. The court vacated the Board’s sixth‑factor and (consequently) first‑factor findings and remanded for reconsideration “in light of” the Board’s factual finding that the parties’ services are highly similar (see J.A. 27–28, 33–34, 51–52). That language contemplates that the Board must reexamine the sixth (and then the first) factor with the proper scope of “similar services” and therefore may consider third‑party registrations/uses that are relevant to that broader scope. Practically, a party seeking to add third‑party evidence on remand should move the Board for leave to reopen the record and explain the relevance of the new material to the broadened similarity determination.

Why (and what the opinion says)
- The Court affirmed the Board’s finding that the parties’ services are “highly similar” and even “partially legally identical.” J.A. 27–28, 33–34. That factual finding is the premise for vacating the sixth‑factor ruling: the Board had limited its sixth‑factor inquiry only to marks used on credit‑card services, but the Court held that, given the Board’s own finding about service similarity, the universe of comparable third‑party marks should not have been so narrowly defined. J.A. 47–48, 51–52.
- The Court therefore “vacate[d] the Board’s finding with respect to the sixth DuPont factor and remand[ed] for reconsideration of the appropriate scope of third‑party marks eligible for consideration in view of the Board’s factual finding that the parties’ services are highly similar.” J.A. 51–52 (emphasis added). The Court also vacated the first factor because the commercial‑strength inquiry under factor six could affect the marks’ “commercial impression.” J.A. 52.

What that means procedurally at the TTAB
- The Board must reconsider factors six and one consistent with the Court’s instructions: use the same scope of “similar services” adopted under factor two when assessing which third‑party marks are “similar” for factor six, and then reassess commercial impression under factor one in light of that result. J.A. 27–28, 51–52.
- The opinion does not expressly prohibit the Board from admitting additional third‑party evidence on remand. Because the Court remanded for a proper scope determination of marks “eligible for consideration,” the Board will need to consider whatever evidence is properly before it that bears on that broader universe of similar marks. In other words, the remedial instruction contemplates a broader evidentiary inquiry than the one the Board performed.

Practical consequences and recommended procedure (what parties should do)
- A party who wishes the Board to consider new third‑party registrations/uses should promptly move the Board for leave to reopen the record on remand and submit the proposed evidence with a proffer explaining its relevance to the broader “similar services” scope the Court directed the Board to apply (i.e., show how the new third‑party material relates to the services the Board already found highly similar). The Court’s remand makes clear that such material is the kind of evidence that could affect factors six and one. See J.A. 51–52.
- The opposing party should be given an opportunity to respond to any newly proffered third‑party evidence so the Board can make fact findings with a complete and adversarially tested record before issuing its new DuPont determinations.
- If neither party requests reopening, the Board still must reweigh factors one and six in light of the Court’s instruction using the evidence already of record. But the Board should be alert that the Court’s remand contemplates a different scope for the universe of third‑party marks, and the Board must explain why it excludes or includes particular third‑party materials if it relies only on the existing record.

Bottom line
The Federal Circuit’s remand requires the Board to apply a broader “similar services” lens when reconsidering DuPont factors six and one (see J.A. 27–28, 51–52). That reconsideration may legitimately include additional third‑party registrations or uses relevant to that broader scope, but such evidence should be placed before the Board by motion for leave to reopen the record (with supporting explanation) so that the Board can consider it and give the opposing party an opportunity to respond.
