Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

Chapter 3: # Factor Two: Similarity of the Services—Affirmance

## Board’s task: compare CC Serve’s “credit card services” to Apex’s “banking and financing services”

Under the DuPont framework, the second factor requires the Board to assess whether the parties’ identified goods or services are related in such a way that confusion about source is likely. The Board’s task is factual: compare the specific services recited in the respective registrations/applications and decide whether they are related or, in the extremes, identical. The Federal Circuit reviews the Board’s factual findings on this point for substantial evidence and its legal conclusions de novo. In this case the Board compared CC Serve’s registered “credit card services” to Apex’s applied-for “banking and financing services” and concluded that the services are highly similar and legally identical in part.

## Defining the identified services: interpreting “credit card services” after ID Manual deletion

A threshold issue the Board faced was the meaning of the term “credit card services,” because that entry had been deleted from the Trademark ID Manual after CC Serve’s registration issued. The Board nonetheless construed the phrase based on the application/registration language and the record, defining “credit card services” to encompass issuing credit cards to finance purchases, counseling regarding credit card debt, processing payments and transactions, authorizing transactions, monitoring and alert services, managing accounts, and providing access to credit scores. That textual and record-based construction of the recited services framed the scope of comparison with Apex’s broader “banking and financing services” identification.

## Dictionary definitions: “bank,” “banking,” and “finance” include credit extension via cards

To determine whether banking and financing services reach the same territory as credit card services, the Board relied on ordinary meanings from dictionary definitions. Those definitions showed that “bank,” “banking,” and “finance” commonly include the extension of credit and provision of funds—activities that can be carried out by issuing credit cards or otherwise facilitating credit-based transactions. Because credit card issuance and account management are means of extending consumer credit, the Board found that the core activities in CC Serve’s recitation fall within the ordinary scope of “banking and financing” services as recited by Apex.

## Third-party registrations: showing single-source offerings that include both service types

The Board also considered third-party registrations that list both credit-card-related services and broader banking/finance services under single marks. Such evidence serves a familiar purpose in DuPont analyses: it shows that services of the listed types may emanate from a common source under a single mark. The Board found that the presence of registrations covering both credit-card services and banking/finance services supported the conclusion that the services are of a type that may be offered together and thus are related for likelihood-of-confusion purposes.

## Board’s conclusion: high similarity and partial legal identity; factor two weighs heavily for CC Serve

Weighing the defined scope of “credit card services,” the ordinary meanings of “banking/finance,” CC Serve’s demonstrated use, and the third-party registrations, the Board concluded that Apex’s banking and financing services and CC Serve’s credit card services are highly similar and legally identical in part. Because similarity can be established even when services are not identical so long as they are related in a way that could cause source confusion, the Board ruled that the second DuPont factor strongly favors CC Serve. The Board therefore gave the factor substantial weight in its overall likelihood-of-confusion determination.

## Apex’s arguments and the Federal Circuit’s rejection: why substantial evidence supports affirmance

Apex argued on appeal that the services are not the same and are not directly competitive because CC Serve is not itself a bank but instead partners with banks to provide credit card programs. The court rejected that argument as unavailing. The governing principle is that services need not be offered by the same kind of commercial actor to be related; what matters is whether the services themselves overlap or are commonly offered together in the marketplace. The Board’s definition of “credit card services,” the dictionary evidence showing “banking/finance” includes credit extension, CC Serve’s operational description (issuing and servicing cards through bank partners), and the third-party registrations together provided “such relevant evidence as a reasonable mind might accept” to support the Board’s finding. Because substantial evidence supports the factual determinations underlying the second DuPont factor, the Federal Circuit affirmed the Board’s conclusion that the parties’ services are highly similar and that factor two weighs heavily in favor of the registrant, CC Serve.
