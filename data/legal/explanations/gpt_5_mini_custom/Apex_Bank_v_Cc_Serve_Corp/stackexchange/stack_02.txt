Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Under DuPont factor six, must the TTAB consider third-party "Aspire" marks used for broader banking/financing services, not just credit cards?
Question:
The Board defined the relevant public for factor six as consumers of 'credit card services' and treated other uses of Aspire for banking/financing as 'essentially irrelevant.' The Federal Circuit said that was too narrow, especially since factor two had already found the parties’ services 'highly similar' and even 'legally identical, in part.' Is there a rule that the 'similarity' scope used under factor two should carry over to factor six? In practice, how broadly should 'similar goods and services' be drawn in financial services when the identifications include 'banking and financing services' on one side and 'credit card services' on the other?
Answer:
Short answer
Yes. Under the Federal Circuit’s decision in Apex Bank v. CC Serve, when the Board has already found the parties’ services to be “highly similar” (or partially “legally identical”) under DuPont factor two, it must use that same scope of similarity when deciding which third‑party “Aspire” (or Aspire‑formative) marks are “on similar goods” for DuPont factor six. The sixth factor requires consideration of marks used on similar — not identical — goods, so third‑party uses tied to broader “banking and financing services” cannot be excluded simply because the opponent’s registration is limited to “credit card services.” (Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp., Fed. Cir. Sept. 25, 2025, No. 2023‑2143.)

Why (the legal rule and doctrine)
- DuPont factor six asks about “the number and nature of similar marks in use on similar goods.” Evidence of third‑party use shows whether a mark is relatively weak and thus entitled to a narrower scope of protection. The governing principle is that the goods/services need only be similar, not identical. (Apex citing DuPont §6; Olde Tyme Foods; Juice Generation; Palm Bay.)  
- In Apex the Board had already found the services “highly similar” and partly “legally identical” under factor two (credit card services overlap with “banking”/“finance” insofar as both involve issuing credit, managing accounts, etc.). The Federal Circuit held it was error for the Board then to narrow its factor‑six inquiry to consumers of only “credit card services” and to ignore third‑party Aspire uses in the broader banking/finance field. The Court reasoned there is no reason to apply a different or more stringent similarity standard under factor six than the Board applied under factor two; thus the same scope should be retained. (Apex, J.A. 27–28, 47–51.)

Practical application in financial‑services cases
- Start with the Board’s factor‑two finding: define the universe of “similar” services according to that factual finding. If factor two found credit card services overlap with banking/financing (e.g., issuing cards, account servicing, extending credit), the relevant universe for factor six includes third‑party marks used on those banking/finance services. (Apex, J.A. 25–28, 33–34, 47.)  
- Don’t exclude third‑party registrations/uses merely because they are not labeled exactly as “credit card services.” If the third party’s service falls within the scope the Board already found similar (e.g., credit issuance, account management, payment processing, loans), it is relevant for factor six. (Apex: “we find the Board’s definition of similarity to be too narrow.”)  
- The Board still evaluates the quantity and nature of third‑party uses: it may conclude that a handful of third‑party uses does not show weakness, whereas a crowded field does. Apex vacated the Board’s factor‑six finding so the Board can reconsider the number/nature of third‑party Aspire uses once the proper scope is applied. (Apex, J.A. 47–51; Jack Wolfskin / Juice Generation discussion cited therein.)  
- Because factor six bears on commercial strength, and commercial strength affects the marks’ overall commercial impression, a re‑run of factor six may require reconsideration of factor one as well — exactly what the Court directed in Apex. (Apex, remand instructions.)

Bottom line for counsel and the Board
- If factor two finds substantial overlap between credit‑card services and broader banking/financing services, the Board must treat third‑party uses of the same formative (e.g., “Aspire”) across that broader banking/finance scope as relevant under factor six. The inquiry remains whether those third‑party marks are used on services “similar” to the parties’ services (similar — not identical), and the Board must reassess factor six (and, if necessary, factor one) accordingly. (Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp., No. 2023‑2143.)
