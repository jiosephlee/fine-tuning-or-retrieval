Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Why were costs awarded to the appellant despite only a partial victory (affirmed in part, vacated in part)?
Question:
The judgment states 'Costs to appellant' even though the Federal Circuit affirmed the Board on DuPont factor two and only vacated and remanded on factors six and one. Under Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 39 and the Federal Circuit’s local rules, how are costs allocated in a mixed outcome like this? Does obtaining a vacatur and remand on key issues generally make the appellant the 'prevailing party' for cost purposes, or is this a discretionary call the panel makes case by case?
Answer:
Short answer
- The Federal Rules (Rule 39) let the court award taxable costs to the “prevailing party,” but who is the prevailing party on a mixed outcome is a matter for the court to decide.  
- A vacatur and remand on important issues often suffices to make an appellant the prevailing party for cost purposes, but it is not automatic — it is a discretionary, case‑by‑case determination.  
- Apex Bank v. CC Serve illustrates that discretion: the court affirmed one DuPont factor, vacated two others and remanded, and then (in the same judgment) directed “Costs to appellant.”

Why — legal doctrine and practice
- FRAP 39 governs taxation of costs and contemplates awarding costs to the prevailing party; the appellate court or clerk implements that rule consistent with the court’s view of who prevailed. The Federal Circuit’s local rules implement the mechanics (what to file, what items are taxable, deadlines), but they do not change that the court determines who is the prevailing party for purposes of awarding costs.  
- “Prevailing party” is a pragmatic concept. Courts routinely treat as prevailing the party that obtained the relief sought on appeal — including partial relief that materially alters the parties’ legal positions (for example, vacatur of agency findings, remand for further proceedings, reversal of dispositive rulings). Whether a partial win counts as prevailing depends on the substance of the relief (did it change the legal outcome or secure meaningful relief?) and is resolved by the court in the exercise of its discretion.

How the opinion applies
- In Apex Bank v. CC Serve the panel: affirmed the Board on DuPont factor two, but “vacate[d] the Board’s findings with respect to DuPont factors six and one and remand[ed]” for reconsideration (see opinion, Sections III.B–C). The judgment then states “AFFIRMED‑IN‑PART, VACATED‑IN‑PART, AND REMANDED” followed by “COSTS / Costs to appellant.”  
- That disposition shows the court treated Apex’s partial success (vacatur and remand on two DuPont factors, including the sixth factor whose reconsideration the panel linked to commercial strength and might affect the ultimate likelihood‑of‑confusion result) as sufficient relief to designate Apex the prevailing party for cost purposes. In short: the panel used its discretion to award costs to the appellant after granting meaningful appellate relief.

Practical points for advocates
- On a mixed result, emphasize in any bill of costs or motion for costs why the vacatur/remand was material (it changed the Board’s analysis or will likely affect the ultimate disposition).  
- Be prepared for the court to weigh the degree and importance of success rather than applying a strict all‑or‑nothing rule. The Federal Circuit may award costs to a partially successful appellant when the appellate remedy is meaningful (as it did here), but it could also deny costs or split them in other circumstances.

Conclusion
Apex Bank demonstrates that vacatur and remand on important issues can make an appellant the prevailing party for FRAP 39 purposes, but the decision to award costs is discretionary and assessed case by case.
