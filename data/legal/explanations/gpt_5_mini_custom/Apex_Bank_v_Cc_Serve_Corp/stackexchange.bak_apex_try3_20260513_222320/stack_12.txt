Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### On remand, is the Board bound by the Federal Circuit’s factor-two finding, or can it revisit it under the mandate rule?
Question:
The court affirmed the Board’s analysis of the second DuPont factor but vacated and remanded as to factors six and one. Under the mandate rule/law-of-the-case, is the TTAB precluded from reopening factor two or other affirmed findings, and must it limit its reconsideration to the scope-of-similarity under factor six and the resulting commercial impression under factor one?
Answer:
Short answer: The Board is bound by the Federal Circuit’s affirmed finding on the second DuPont factor and may not relitigate that factual determination on remand absent new evidence or changed circumstances. The Board must use that finding (that the parties’ services are “highly similar” and partially legally identical) as the starting point when reexamining factors six and one, and it must expand its inquiry under factor six to include third‑party “Aspire” uses for the broader banking/finance services that the court said are within the scope of similarity.

Why: the opinion’s directives and the governing standard

- The court expressly “affirm[ed] the Board’s finding as to the second DuPont factor” and stated that “substantial evidence supports the Board’s finding that the parties’ services are highly similar.” That factual finding was therefore decided on appeal. The opinion also explains the applicable review standard: “[w]e review the [B]oard’s legal conclusions de novo, and its findings of fact for substantial evidence.” Because the panel affirmed the Board’s factual finding under that standard, the Board must accept that factual finding on remand unless there is a new factual basis that would justify revisiting it.

- The court vacated only factors six and one and remanded for reconsideration “in light of its finding that the parties’ services are highly similar.” The opinion squarely instructs the Board that “when the Board has already made a factual finding that the services are highly similar … the Board should retain the same scope in its consideration of similarity under the other factors.” That is a clear mandate about the scope and frame of the remand.

What the Board may and must do on remand

- Must accept the affirmed second‑factor finding: The Board should not reopen or redecide the second DuPont factor as part of this remand. The court’s affirmance is the law of the case for this factual issue; the Board’s further analyses must proceed against that backdrop.

- Must expand the universe of third‑party marks for factor six: Because the court found the Board erred by restricting factor six to credit‑card uses only and instructed the Board to retain the same scope used in its factor‑two analysis, the Board must consider third‑party “Aspire” (and Aspire‑formative) uses across the related banking and finance services the court found similar. The Board should reassess the number and nature of similar marks with that broader scope.

- Must reexamine the overall commercial impression for factor one in light of the adjusted factor‑six analysis: The opinion vacated factor one because the court concluded that a different assessment of commercial strength under factor six “may result in a different determination of the mark’s commercial strength or weakness and affect the overall commercial impression.” The Board therefore must redetermine the marks’ appearance, sound, connotation, and commercial impression against the affirmed factual backdrop.

When the Board may revisit factor two

- The mandate rule and law‑of‑the‑case doctrine do not absolutely freeze the record against any possible new development. If, on remand, new evidence is properly introduced (for example, evidence that did not exist or was unavailable at the time of the original proceedings) or if the Board identifies a clearly articulated basis showing the prior factual finding is no longer supported, the Board may justify reconsidering the second factor — but it must explain why that evidence or changed circumstance warrants departing from the appellate court’s prior affirmance. The opinion itself gives no reason to reopen factor two; it directs the Board to proceed with factor six and factor one in light of the affirmed second‑factor finding.

Bottom line: The Board is constrained by the Federal Circuit’s affirmed finding on factor two and must apply that finding when redoing factors six and one. It should broaden the factor‑six inquiry to include banking/finance uses the court said are similar and then reassess commercial impression under factor one. Reopening factor two is permissible only if new, material evidence or circumstances justify it and the Board explains why that evidence overcomes the appellate court’s prior substantial‑evidence determination.
