Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.

### Does the TTAB receive any deference on how it applies the DuPont framework, or are those legal judgments reviewed de novo?
Question:
The opinion reviews legal conclusions de novo and factual findings for substantial evidence, and corrects the Board’s legal framing of factor six. Is there any Chevron or Skidmore deference owed to the PTO/TTAB in interpreting or structuring the DuPont factors, or is the multi-factor framework purely judicial and therefore policed de novo by the Federal Circuit?
Answer:
Short answer
No — at least as applied in this opinion, the Federal Circuit gave no Chevron- or Skidmore-type deference to the TTAB’s legal framing of the DuPont analysis. The court reiterated that it reviews the Board’s legal conclusions de novo and will correct legal errors in how the Board defines or applies the DuPont factors, while leaving factual findings (e.g., scope of services, existence/extent of third‑party use) to substantial‑evidence review.

Why (grounded in the opinion)
- The court starts by stating the controlling standard: “We review the [B]oard’s legal conclusions de novo, and its findings of fact for substantial evidence.” (Section II (citing M2 Software)). It then reiterates that “Likelihood of confusion is a question of law, based on findings of relevant underlying facts, namely findings under the DuPont factors.” (Id.).
- The court treated the Board’s treatment of the sixth DuPont factor as a legal error. It explains that the Board “restricted the universe of marks it considered” in a way that conflicted with its prior factual finding that the parties’ services were “highly similar,” and says explicitly “That was an error.” (Section III.B). The court vacated the Board’s sixth‑factor finding and remanded for reconsideration under the correct legal framing.
- The court also vacated the first‑factor analysis for reconsideration because the legal error on factor six could alter the mark’s commercial strength and therefore the legal analysis of “appearance, sound, connotation and commercial impression.” (Section III.C).

What this means in practice
- Legal questions about how to frame or apply DuPont (e.g., what legal standard governs similarity under a factor, whether the Board may narrow the universe of comparable marks) are reviewed de novo by the Federal Circuit and are subject to correction when they conflict with the court’s legal standards.
- Factual determinations underlying those questions (e.g., the Board’s finding that the parties’ services are “highly similar,” the number and nature of third‑party uses) remain reviewable only for substantial evidence.

Caveat
The opinion does not discuss Chevron or Skidmore by name; it resolves the matter by applying the familiar Federal Circuit standard—de novo review of legal conclusions and substantial‑evidence review of facts—and by correcting the Board’s legal framing. So, at least as to the DuPont framework in this decision, the Board’s legal structuring is not entitled to agency‑style deference.
