Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer"

### How are § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) (FOIA’s OSC referral provision) and § 1216 (general OSC authority) supposed to interact?
Question:
The district court held that FOIA’s three triggers in § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) do not limit OSC’s authority under § 1216 to investigate arbitrary or capricious FOIA withholdings. The D.C. Circuit didn’t reach merits but recites that holding. Statutorily, is § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) best read as an additional mandatory-investigation pathway when its predicates are met, without precluding § 1216 investigations otherwise? If so, what is the practical difference between a § 1216-initiated investigation and a § 552(a)(4)(F)(i)-triggered one, especially regarding what information, if any, must be shared with the complainant?
Answer:
Short answer
Read together (and as the district court read them in the opinion), § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) is a separate, FOIA‑specific command that creates an additional, mandatory referral path to OSC when its three predicates occur. It does not, by its text and as the district court concluded, strip OSC of the independent authority to investigate alleged arbitrary FOIA withholdings under 5 U.S.C. § 1216. The practical difference is mostly procedural and remedial: the FOIA trigger compels a prompt proceeding directed at whether the employee “primarily responsible” should be disciplined, while §1216 authorizes OSC to investigate allegations more broadly but leaves OSC discretion about how (and whether) to apply §1214’s procedural/notice rules and what information to share with a complainant. The D.C. Circuit’s opinion recites these points and rests the case on standing; it does not alter that statutory interaction.

Why (and where the opinion says this)
- §1216(a)(3) says OSC “shall … conduct an investigation of any allegation concerning arbitrary or capricious withholding of information prohibited under section 552.” The opinion therefore treats §1216 as a broad statutory grant of investigatory authority over alleged FOIA withholding abuses. (Op. I.A.)
- FOIA’s separate provision, § 552(a)(4)(F)(i), provides that if a court (1) orders production, (2) awards attorneys’ fees, and (3) “specifically question[s] whether the agency ‘acted arbitrarily or capriciously with respect to the withholding,’” then OSC “shall promptly initiate a proceeding to determine whether disciplinary action is warranted against the officer or employee who was primarily responsible for the withholding.” The opinion sets this up as a distinct, mandatory referral mechanism in FOIA. (Op. I.A.)
- The district court — and the opinion recounts this ruling — concluded that the FOIA referral provision does not limit OSC’s independent §1216 power to investigate alleged FOIA violations. The opinion therefore frames the two provisions as complementary: §552(a)(4)(F)(i) supplies a mandatory, case‑specific referral when its predicates are met, but it does not nullify §1216’s separate investigatory grant. (Op. I.B.)

Practical differences (especially about information sharing)
- Character of the duty. A §552(a)(4)(F)(i) event creates a clear, FOIA‑based duty to “promptly initiate” a proceeding to examine disciplinary liability of the employee “primarily responsible.” By contrast §1216 is a general investigatory command (“shall … conduct an investigation”) covering allegations of arbitrary FOIA withholdings, but the practical contours of how OSC handles such §1216 investigations (e.g., whether to pursue corrective or disciplinary remedies) are implemented through the §1214/§1215 machinery and involve OSC exercise of enforcement discretion. (Op. I.A–B; II.)
- Who is the focus. The FOIA referral is targeted at whether the employee primarily responsible should face discipline; §1216 authorizes OSC to investigate allegations about agency practice or policy as well as individual misconduct. (Op. I.A–B.)
- Notice and complainant information. Section 1214 contains procedural provisions “including provisions for notifying the complaining party about the progress of an investigation.” The opinion also observes that §1216(c) authorizes OSC to “investigate and seek corrective action under section 1214 and disciplinary action under section 1215 in the same way as if a prohibited personnel practice were involved.” But the opinion also recounts that OSC’s decision whether to follow §1214’s procedural requirements in a §1216 investigation has been treated as discretionary (the opinion cites that point and the district court’s reliance on it). Thus:
  - If OSC conducts a §1216 investigation and elects to proceed under §1214’s procedures, the complaining party may receive the notices §1214 provides.
  - If OSC conducts a §1216 investigation but does not invoke §1214 procedures, there is no statutory entitlement to receive investigative materials or progress notices merely from §1216 itself.
  - The FOIA §552(a)(4)(F)(i) referral requires OSC to “promptly initiate” a proceeding but does not, on its face, create a separate statutory right for the FOIA requester to obtain OSC’s investigatory materials or compel OSC to apply §1214 notice rules. The opinion relied on this point to deny AFL an informational‑injury basis for standing: even if OSC were required to investigate under §1216, AFL had no statutory entitlement to the investigatory information it sought. (Op. I.A; II.)

Takeaway for litigation strategy and expectations
- Treat §552(a)(4)(F)(i) as an additional, mandatory route to compel OSC action when a FOIA litigant can establish the three FOIA predicates; it does not extinguish §1216.
- Don’t assume that a successful §1216 investigation (or an OSC proceeding under the FOIA referral) creates a statutory right for the FOIA requester to receive OSC’s investigative files or guaranteed status updates unless OSC applies §1214 procedures (and the decision to do so in §1216 investigations has been viewed as discretionary in the cases the opinion recounts).
- The opinion itself did not decide the full merits of competing constructions; it recited the district court’s view that §552(a)(4)(F)(i) does not limit §1216 and then disposed of the case on standing grounds. (Op. II–III.)
