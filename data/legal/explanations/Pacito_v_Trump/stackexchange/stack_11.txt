### Which agencies actually have the statutory duty under § 1522: State (PRM) or HHS (ORR)?
Question:
The order cites 45 C.F.R. part 400 (HHS/ORR regulations) yet discusses State’s termination of R&P cooperative agreements. If § 1522(a) duties are lodged in ORR/HHS, can the court compel State to reinstate its agreements to fulfill § 1522 obligations? How do the distinct roles of PRM’s R&P program and ORR’s domestic services interface under § 1522?
Answer:
Short answer
- The opinion treats the substantive statutory duties in 8 U.S.C. § 1522 as incumbent on the Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR) within HHS (see the court’s text describing § 1522(a)(1)(A) duties).  
- At the same time, § 1522 expressly contemplates that reception-and-placement services may be provided through contracts, grants, or cooperative agreements (§ 1522(b)(1)(A)(ii)), and the State Department historically administered those cooperative agreements. The district court therefore ordered the State Department to reinstate the cooperative agreements it had terminated because those agreements were the mechanism by which the government—through ORR’s statutory duties—actually delivered R&P services.

Why (legal reasoning tied to the opinion)
1. Who the statute names as the duty‑bearer: the opinion identifies the Director of ORR/HHS as the actor to whom § 1522’s substantive duties are directed. The opinion quotes § 1522(a)(1)(A) and ties provisions about employment training, English instruction, cash assistance, and equal opportunities directly to “the Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement—an office within the Department of Health and Human Services” (Opinion, ¶ beginning “For reasons to be explained…” and the paragraph that follows). That plainly frames ORR/HHS as the statutory office charged with those resettlement responsibilities.

2. The role of cooperative agreements and State: the opinion also explains that “these resettlement services…may be provided through the use of contracts, grants, or cooperative agreements with ‘public or private nonprofit agencies.’” (citing § 1522(b)(1)(A)(ii)). It then records that the organizational plaintiffs are agencies that historically had cooperative agreements “with which the Department of State has had cooperative agreements,” and that the State Department terminated those agreements in February 2025. The district court concluded (and this Court directed) that the government must “reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522” because the State’s termination impaired the government’s ability to provide services statutorily required under § 1522.

3. Remedy logic in the opinion: the Court’s remedial directive is grounded in the interaction of (a) the statute assigning substantive duties to ORR/HHS and (b) the fact that those duties were being fulfilled via State‑administered cooperative agreements that the government terminated. The opinion explicitly links ORR’s statutory obligations with the cooperative agreements State had used and directs reinstatement on the ground that the government is “uncertain regarding its ability to provide the reception and placement services statutorily mandated under 8 U.S.C. § 1522” and had “terminated all cooperative agreements to provide such services.” Thus the Court ordered restoration of the practical apparatus (the agreements) by which the statutory duties were being performed.

Counterpoint the opinion records: discretionary/authorization argument
- Judge Lee’s concurrence/dissent argues § 1522 is best read as authorizing programs, not mandating them—pointing to the statute’s title (“Authorization for programs…”) and the repeated use of “is authorized” in subsections (b)–(e), and characterizes the mandatory‑looking language in § 1522(a) as conditional (“in providing assistance under this section… ‘shall’ operate in a particular way”). The concurrence therefore disputes that § 1522 imposes a judicially enforceable affirmative duty to operate R&P programs at all.

Practical synthesis
- Under the Court’s opinion: the substantive legal obligations under § 1522 are those of ORR/HHS; State’s role is operational (administering cooperative agreements that effectuate those obligations). Because State had been the vehicle for providing the statutorily required R&P services and then terminated its agreements, the court ordered State to reinstate the agreements as necessary to allow the government to meet § 1522 responsibilities.  
- The opinion also records disagreement: one judge views § 1522 as authorizing rather than mandating action, which is relevant to the merits and to whether a court may compel the executive to act.

Bottom line for your question
- Statutory duty (substantive obligations) — HHS/ORR.  
- Operational responsibility for delivering those services can rest with State through cooperative agreements; the court can order reinstatement of those agreements if their termination frustrates the government’s ability to carry out ORR’s § 1522 duties (as the opinion directs).
