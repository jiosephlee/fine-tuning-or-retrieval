### What is the basis for organizational standing for the resettlement agencies under the APA?
Question:
The organizational plaintiffs had cooperative agreements with State. Do they have Article III standing based on lost funding and programmatic disruption, and do their interests fall within the zone of interests of § 1522 and the APA? Does their standing hinge on whether § 1522 creates enforceable obligations rather than pure authorization?
Answer:
Short answer
The panel’s majority treated the organizations as having organizational Article III standing: they suffered a concrete, particularized injury (termination of cooperative agreements, lost funding, and programmatic disruption); that injury was caused by the Executive Order and the State Department’s termination of the agreements; and a favorable court order reinstating cooperative agreements and funding would redress the harm. The court also held the organizations’ interests fall within the “zone of interests” of 8 U.S.C. § 1522 and the APA because § 1522 expressly contemplates use of cooperative agreements and targets the provision of reception-and-placement services that the organizational plaintiffs perform. Judge Lee disagreed on statutory interpretation—reading § 1522 as authorization rather than a mandatory duty—but that disagreement is about the legal predicate for relief, not the core standing analysis.

Why the organizations clear Article III standing here
The opinion identifies the three familiar Article III elements and ties them to the organizations’ facts:

- Injury-in-fact: The government “terminated every cooperative agreement to provide reception and placement services” (opinion, facts) and “terminated all cooperative agreements” in February 2025 — a concrete financial loss and programmatic disruption to organizations whose mission and operations are to provide those services. That is a classic tangible injury for organizational standing (lost funds and lost ability to provide services).

- Causation: The injury was caused by the Executive Order and the State Department’s subsequent termination of the cooperative agreements (the opinion attributes the terminations to the government’s actions and describes the government’s suspension of funding).

- Redressability: The panel directed that the government “reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522,” indicating that a judicial remedy (reinstatement) would cure the organizations’ injury. The court therefore treated redressability as satisfied.

Why the organizations’ interests fall within the zone of interests of § 1522 / APA
The opinion rests the organizations’ entitlement to relief on § 1522’s structure and text:

- § 1522 expressly contemplates that resettlement “services . . . may be provided through the use of contracts, grants, or cooperative agreements with ‘public or private nonprofit agencies.’” The organizational plaintiffs are precisely such agencies and were operating under cooperative agreements with the State Department.

- The opinion quotes and summarizes § 1522(a) (the “shall” language about making available resources “to the extent of available appropriations”) and examples of specific service provisions (duty language about unaccompanied minors, subsection (e)(7)(A) on alternative projects). The court concluded the government “is not likely to prevail on at least one of plaintiffs’ challenges under the APA” because § 1522 requires the government to provide certain reception-and-placement services and the government’s conduct threatened the statutory scheme.

Because § 1522 targets the provision of the very services that these organizations perform and expressly authorizes use of cooperative agreements with agencies like them, the organizations’ interests are within the zone of interests that § 1522 protects; they are therefore proper plaintiffs under the APA.

Does standing depend on whether § 1522 is mandatory or merely permissive?
No — standing and the merits are distinct questions, and the panel treated them separately:

- Standing: The organizations’ concrete injury, causation, and redressability exist regardless of whether § 1522 is read as mandatory or permissive. The panel’s majority found those Article III prerequisites satisfied by the terminated agreements, lost funding, and the prospect of reinstatement.

- Merits (enforceability of § 1522): Whether § 1522 imposes an enforceable duty that the court can compel is a merits question. The majority read § 1522 as imposing obligations sufficient to support relief and so ordered reinstatement of cooperative agreements. Judge Lee, in partial dissent, argued § 1522 is best read as authorizing—permissive—action by the executive (citing the statute’s title “Authorization for programs…” and the repeated “is authorized” phrasing), and would therefore deny the district court power to mandate government provision of services. That disagreement affects whether the organizations ultimately win relief, but it does not negate that they have Article III standing to raise their APA claims.

Bottom line
Under the panel majority: organizational standing is grounded in the concrete economic and programmatic harms caused by the termination of their cooperative agreements, and those harms fall within the zone of interests of § 1522 because Congress expressly contemplated cooperative agreements with agencies like the plaintiffs and set statutory expectations about reception-and-placement services. Whether the statutory language is construed as mandatory or merely authorizing is a merits issue (on which the panel is divided) that determines entitlement to relief, not the existence of Article III standing.
