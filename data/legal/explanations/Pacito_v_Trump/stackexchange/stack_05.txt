Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: Pacito v. Trump

### Can the Executive suspend or withhold refugee-related funds that Congress has appropriated, consistent with the Impoundment Control Act?
Question:
The order notes Congress appropriated several billion dollars for migration and refugee assistance in FY 2025, with a partial rescission. Yet the State Department suspended all USRAP funding and terminated cooperative agreements, purportedly under an EO reevaluating foreign aid. Is this a lawful "programmatic delay" or does it raise an impoundment issue if the Executive withholds or delays funds for policy reasons? Do different rules apply to State Department foreign assistance funds versus HHS’s ORR funds, and how might those distinctions matter for APA or statutory claims?
Answer:
Short answer
- Pacito makes clear that a presidential suspension of refugee admissions under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f) is likely lawful and receives great deference from the courts. (The Ninth Circuit said the government is “likely to prevail” on that claim, citing Trump v. Hawaii.)
- But the panel also held that the government is not likely to prevail on plaintiffs’ APA/statutory challenge to the shutdown of the operational resettlement program: because Congress has authorized and Congress has appropriated funds for reception-and‑placement services under 8 U.S.C. § 1522, the court directed reinstatement of cooperative agreements necessary to provide those statutorily described services to refugees already admitted. In short: suspension of admissions ≠ automatic license to withhold or terminate statutorily required resettlement services. (The court ordered the government to reinstate the cooperative agreements needed to provide §1522 services.)

What the opinion says about the governing legal standards
- Presidential authority over entry. The panel relied on the Supreme Court’s approach in Trump v. Hawaii: § 1182(f) “exudes deference” and the sole statutory prerequisite is that the President “find[]” entry would be detrimental. The Ninth Circuit therefore concluded the government is likely to prevail on the claim challenging the suspension of admissions. (Pacito pp. quoting Hawaii.)
- Statutory duties for reception and placement. The opinion describes § 1522 as creating duties tied to “available appropriations” and identifies specific statutory text requiring the Director of ORR to make resources available “to the extent of available appropriations” for employment, language training, cash and medical assistance, and to assume responsibility for unaccompanied children when necessary. The court found the plaintiffs’ APA/statutory challenge to the defunding of those services likely to succeed and thus ordered reinstatement of the cooperative agreements that provide those services. (Pacito pp., discussion of §1522 and the court’s directive to reinstate agreements.)
- Internal disagreement on scope. Judge Lee’s partial dissent emphasized that §1522 is titled an “Authorization for programs…” and repeatedly uses “is authorized,” arguing the provision is permissive rather than mandatory. The majority and the concurrence thus frame competing statutory readings—one treating §1522 as imposing enforceable obligations for services to admitted refugees (the majority), the other treating it as discretionary (Judge Lee).

How that bears on the Impoundment Control Act (ICA) and programmatic delays
- Pacito does not rule on the ICA. The panel’s interim order resolves the specific preliminary‑injunction issues presented: it upholds broad presidential deference over admissions but enjoins the government’s elimination of the operational resettlement infrastructure insofar as necessary to satisfy §1522 obligations. The opinion does not analyze or decide ICA principles or the specific statutory requirements for deferral/rescission under the ICA.
- Practically speaking (what Pacito shows you can argue): if the Executive withholds or terminates appropriated funding in a way that frustrates statutory duties (as the court viewed here with respect to §1522 services), courts are willing to enjoin that withholding under APA/statutory authority. By contrast, the panel treats the pure policy decision to stop admissions as a different animal—one where judicial inquiry is limited. That contrast is the key lesson: where appropriations are tied to a statutory duty to provide particular services, courts will scrutinize agency action that prevents those services irrespective of deference on admission policy.

State Department foreign‑assistance funds v. HHS/ORR funds — why the distinction mattered in Pacito
- HHS/ORR funds: §1522 assigns (or at least contemplates) responsibilities for reception and placement to ORR/the Director, and the Ninth Circuit concluded that the government cannot simply walk away from providing those services when (1) Congress has appropriated funds for migration and refugee assistance and (2) the Executive terminated the cooperative agreements that have historically delivered the §1522 services. The court therefore ordered reinstatement of the cooperative agreements necessary to carry out §1522 services to admitted refugees.
- State Department cooperative agreements and foreign‑assistance instruments: the State Department’s termination of cooperative agreements was the mechanism that prevented delivery of the §1522 services. Pacito does not hold that all State Department foreign‑assistance funds are immune from programmatic suspensions; rather, it ties the State Department’s contractual decisions to HHS’s statutory duties. If a State Department funding decision defeats a separate statute’s mandatory duties, a court can—and here did—force restoration of the programmatic apparatus needed to fulfill those duties. If a foreign‑assistance appropriation funds purely discretionary foreign policy programs with no separate statutory duty to a domestic beneficiary, different (and generally more deferential) rules may apply—but Pacito does not resolve that broader question.

Implications for APA or statutory claims
- APA/§ 706(2)(A) (arbitrary and capricious) and statutory‑interpretation claims were the vehicle used here. The Ninth Circuit found plaintiffs likely to succeed on such a claim with respect to the resettlement services. That shows a successful path for plaintiffs who can show that an agency’s withholding or termination of funds frustrates clear statutory responsibilities or lacks a reasoned explanation.
- Where the statute uses mandatory language (or creates a discrete statutory duty tied to appropriations), courts will scrutinize executive action that disables performance of that duty even if the underlying policy (e.g., suspending admissions) is highly deferential.

Bottom line (for your exam or brief)
- Under Pacito: the President has broad, deferential authority to suspend refugee admissions under §1182(f), but that authority does not automatically permit the Executive to cut off the operational funding and contracts that Congress and statute have tied to delivery of reception and placement services to refugees already admitted. The Ninth Circuit ordered reinstatement of the cooperative agreements necessary to deliver §1522 services and indicated plaintiffs are likely to prevail on their APA/statutory challenge to the funding termination. Pacito does not resolve ICA questions; if you need to argue ICA issues you must note that the opinion did not address them and focus instead on the statutory‑duty and APA theories the court relied upon.
