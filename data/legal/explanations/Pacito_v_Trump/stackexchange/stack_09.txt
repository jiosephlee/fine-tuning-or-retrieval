### Is the termination of refugee cooperative agreements reviewable under the APA or committed to agency discretion?
Question:
State terminated all domestic R&P agreements and most overseas processing agreements. Are those termination decisions ‘final agency action’ not committed to agency discretion by law (§ 701(a)(2))? What legal standards (statute, regulation, uniform guidance) cabin the agency’s discretion enough to permit arbitrary-and-capricious or contrary-to-law review?
Answer:
Short answer
Yes — at least according to the panel majority, the termination of the cooperative agreements is reviewable under the APA and is not committed to agency discretion under 5 U.S.C. § 701(a)(2). The court treated the terminations as final agency action subject to arbitrary-and-capricious and contrary-to-law review because Congress and agency law (statute plus regulations and reporting requirements) supply legal standards that cabin the Executive’s discretion. Judge Lee’s partial dissent offers the opposing view that §1522 is merely permissive, but the majority directed reinstatement of the agreements for the subset of refugees covered by the injunction, relying on mandatory statutory language and implementing regulations.

Why this is reviewable (legal doctrine and how it applies to the opinion)
Two separate doctrines control whether an agency decision like termination of cooperative agreements is judicially reviewable under the APA:

1) Final agency action (Bennett v. Spear)
- The decision must (a) consummate the agency’s decisionmaking process, and (b) produce legal consequences or rights and obligations.
- The opinion shows the terminations were consummated and produced concrete legal consequences: the State Department “terminated all cooperative agreements” (Opinion Background ¶—Feb. 2025 terminations), and the panel directed the government “to reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522 to refugees who have been admitted to the United States.” That remedy presumes the terminations were final and caused legal effects (loss of statutorily mandated services).

2) The “committed to agency discretion” exception, 5 U.S.C. § 701(a)(2)
- If a statute “is drawn so that a court would have no meaningful standard against which to judge the agency’s exercise of discretion,” review is foreclosed.
- The panel majority rejected that exception here because the Refugee Act/INA provisions and agency rules supply standards. The court relied on 8 U.S.C. § 1522’s substantive provisions (see Discussion ¶¶ on §1522), the implementing regulations (45 C.F.R. §§ 400.200 et seq.), and statutory accountability/reporting in §1523 as furnishing legal benchmarks. The court therefore concluded the terminations were not immune from review under §701(a)(2).

What statutory/regulatory/other standards the court relied on as constraining discretion
The opinion identifies (and uses) specific legal materials that cabin agency discretion:

- 8 U.S.C. § 1522 (statute)
  - §1522(a)(1)(A): requires the Director of ORR, “to the extent of available appropriations,” to make available sufficient resources for employment training and placement, provide English-language training, insure cash assistance is made available in a way that does not discourage self-sufficiency, and insure equal opportunity for women (Opinion Discussion ¶1 on §1522).
  - §1522(d)(2)(B): for unaccompanied minors, the Director “shall assume legal responsibility (including financial responsibility) … and is authorized to make necessary decisions” during interim placement (Opinion Discussion ¶ on §1522(d)(2)(B)).
  - §1522(e)(7)(A): the Secretary “shall develop and implement” alternative projects for cash/medical assistance for refugees who have been in the U.S. less than 36 months, “under which refugees are provided interim support, medical services, support services, and case management, as needed” (Opinion Discussion ¶ on §1522(e)(7)(A)).
  - The court treated these “shall” provisions and the statutory structure (including the title “Authorization for programs…” and the “to the extent of available appropriations” qualifier) as giving concrete duties and limits (the majority and Judge Lee disagree on interpretation, see Lee concurrence/dissent).

- 45 C.F.R. §§ 400.200 et seq. (regulations)
  - The opinion cites these implementing regulations as describing “the federal funding available for refugee services,” i.e., concrete programmatic rules against which the agency’s termination can be judged (Opinion Discussion ¶ referencing 45 C.F.R.).

- Appropriations and reporting provisions
  - The opinion notes Congress appropriated funds for FY2025 (Pub. L. No. 119-4 §1101(a)(11), later reduced by a rescission), and references §1523’s reporting requirement, which imposes accountability duties on the Secretary (Opinion Discussion ¶ on appropriations and §1523). Those statutory and appropriations details supply context and objective benchmarks.

How those materials translate into judicial standards
- The court found (at least preliminarily for preliminary-injunction purposes) that §1522 contains mandatory language (“shall”) and program-specific duties that are not so vague as to leave “no law to apply.” That means the arbitrary-and-capricious standard (and review for actions “contrary to law”) applies: the court can ask whether the agency’s termination was reasonable, adequately explained, and consistent with statutory duties (see opinion’s direction to reinstate agreements in light of the government’s uncertainty about meeting §1522 obligations).

Counterpoint from Judge Lee
- Judge Lee (concurring/dissenting) argues §1522 is largely authorizing, not obligatory: the title uses “Authorization,” many subsections read “is authorized,” and the “to the extent of available appropriations” qualifier signals permissiveness. Lee would treat the statute as leaving the Executive discretion not to operate the programs and therefore would find the terminations non-reviewable or at least not remediable by court order. The majority, however, read §1522 as imposing enforceable duties in the circumstances before the court (Opinion ¶s on §1522 and the remedial direction).

Practical takeaways for future APA challenges
- Finality: If you can show the agency’s action is a consummated decision that produces concrete legal effects (loss of funding, loss of specifically described services, legal responsibility to certain classes), Bennett’s finality test is satisfied.
- Law to apply: Demonstrate statutory text or binding regulations that impose duties or conditions (mandatory verbs, program-specific requirements, reporting/accountability provisions, implementing regs) so a court has standards to judge the agency. The panel majority relied on §1522’s “shall” language, the 45 C.F.R. regime, appropriations context, and reporting requirements to conclude there is “law to apply.”
- Beware of the “to the extent of appropriations” language: courts will consider it, but it does not automatically defeat review if the statute otherwise imposes duties and Congress has appropriated funds (the opinion discusses appropriations for FY2025 and the rescission, which the court viewed as relevant background rather than an absolute bar).

Conclusion
Under the panel majority, the February 2025 terminations are reviewable APA final agency actions because they were final and produced legal consequences, and §1522 together with 45 C.F.R. §400.200 et seq., appropriations history, and §1523 reporting requirements provide judicially manageable standards to test the lawfulness of the terminations. Judge Lee offers the contrary statutory-interpretation view (that §1522 is permissive), but the court’s interim remedy — reinstatement of agreements to provide §1522 services — shows the majority’s conclusion that agency discretion here is sufficiently cabined to permit arbitrary-and-capricious and contrary-to-law review.
