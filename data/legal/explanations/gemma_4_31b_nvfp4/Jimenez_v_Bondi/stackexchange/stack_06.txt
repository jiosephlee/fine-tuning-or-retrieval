Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Jimenez v. Bondi"

### Why does a failure to qualify for asylum automatically result in a denial of Withholding of Removal?
Question:
The conclusion of the opinion states that because the petitioners failed to meet the asylum standards, they 'necessarily fail to meet the higher standard for withholding of removal.' I thought withholding of removal had a different burden of proof (clear probability vs. well-founded fear). Why does the failure of the asylum claim automatically preclude the withholding claim in this instance?
Answer:
***

While you are correct that asylum and withholding of removal have different burdens of proof regarding the *probability* of future harm (a "well-founded fear" versus a "clear probability"), both forms of relief share the same fundamental eligibility requirements. Specifically, an applicant must demonstrate that they were persecuted (or fear persecution) **on account of** a protected ground and that the government is **unable or unwilling** to protect them.

In *Jimenez v. Bondi*, the court did not deny the claims based on the *degree* of the petitioners' fear, but rather on the *absence* of the underlying legal elements required for either claim. 

The court found that the petitioners failed to establish three core requirements:

1.  **Persecution:** The court determined that the threats and emotional distress suffered by the petitioners did not rise to the level of "persecution" under the INA.
2.  **Nexus:** The court found that the harm was not "on account of" a protected ground. Instead, it concluded that the predominant motive was Jimenez’s professional involvement in a property dispute and his perceived role as a "snitch" for the prosecutor's office, rendering the political opinion "tangential and subordinate."
3.  **Government Protection:** The court found no evidence that the Colombian government was unable or unwilling to protect the petitioners, noting that the petitioners had minimal contact with authorities and that the government had actually attempted searches and seizures at the property in question.

Because the court found that there was no persecution and no nexus to a protected ground, there is no "fear of persecution" to measure. If a petitioner cannot establish the basic elements of persecution and nexus, they cannot meet *any* standard of probability. Consequently, if the evidence is insufficient to establish a "well-founded fear" (the lower asylum standard), it is logically impossible for it to establish a "clear probability" (the higher withholding standard).
