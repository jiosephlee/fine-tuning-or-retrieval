Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### What does it mean for a FOIA withholding category to be 'overbroad'?
Question:
In Section IV(C), the court mentions that when an agency withholds documents by category, those categories must be 'sufficiently distinct' so the court can determine if the exemption applies. Farahi argued the categories were overbroad, but the court said he 'forfeited' the argument because he didn't explain why. I don't understand what an 'overbroad category' looks like in practice. What is an example of a category that is 'sufficiently distinct' versus one that is too broad?
Answer:
***

# What does it mean for a FOIA withholding category to be 'overbroad'?

In the context of FOIA litigation, agencies often withhold documents by grouping them into "categories" (e.g., "investigative reports" or "internal memos") rather than providing a line-by-line justification for every single page. 

As explained in *Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation*, a withholding category is **overbroad** if it is not "**sufficiently distinct**" to allow a court to determine whether the claimed exemption actually applies to the documents in that group.

### The Legal Standard
The core requirement is that categories must be defined with enough specificity that a court can perform its judicial review. If a category is too vague or encompasses too many different types of records, the court cannot possibly determine if the agency is applying the exemption correctly.

According to the opinion, the standard (citing *CREW* and *Gallant*) is that categories must be:
> "...sufficiently distinct to allow a court to determine whether the specific claimed exemptions are properly applied to each category."

### Practical Application: "Sufficiently Distinct" vs. "Overbroad"
While the court in *Farahi* did not provide a list of examples (because the plaintiff "forfeited" the argument by failing to explain *why* the FBI's categories were problematic), the legal logic functions as follows:

*   **Sufficiently Distinct:** A category that describes a specific type of record or a specific functional group of documents. For example, if an agency creates a category for "records compiled for the purpose of a specific ongoing criminal investigation," a court can evaluate whether those records meet the requirements of Exemption 7(A).
*   **Overbroad:** A category that is so general it blends exempt and non-exempt material together without a clear distinction. For example, if an agency categorized documents simply as "all records pertaining to the subject's file," that would likely be overbroad because it doesn't allow the court to distinguish between law enforcement records (which may be exempt) and purely administrative records (which are generally not).

### Why the Argument Failed in *Farahi*
In this specific case, Farahi argued that the FBI's categories were overbroad. However, the court did not analyze whether the categories were actually too broad. Instead, it ruled that Farahi **forfeited** the argument. 

To succeed on this claim, a requester cannot simply label the categories "overbroad"; they must provide a "specific explanation" as to why the agency's definitions fail to meet the "sufficiently distinct" standard. Because Farahi failed to provide that explanation, the court declined to review the merits of the category definitions.
