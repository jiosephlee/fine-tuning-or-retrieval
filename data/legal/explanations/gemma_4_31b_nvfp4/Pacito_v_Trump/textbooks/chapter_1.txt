Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

Chapter 1: # Factual Background and Procedural Posture

## Executive Order 14163: The Suspension of the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP)

The dispute in *Pacito v. Trump* began on January 20, 2025, when President Trump issued Executive Order (EO) 14163, "Realigning the United States Refugee Admissions Program." In this order, the President determined that the entry of refugees under the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) was detrimental to the interests of the United States. Relying on the authority granted by 8 U.S.C. §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a), the President suspended the entry of refugees under the USRAP pending further findings. Under § 1182(f), the President possesses broad authority to suspend the entry of any class of aliens if he finds such entry to be detrimental to the national interest.

## Executive Order 14169: The Suspension of Funding for USRAP and Domestic Resettlement

Shortly after the suspension of refugee entry, the administration moved to dismantle the financial infrastructure supporting the program. On January 24, 2025, the Department of State suspended all funding for the USRAP. This action was purportedly based on a separate executive order, EO 14169, titled "Reevaluating and Realigning United States Foreign Aid." Notably, this funding suspension extended beyond the overseas processing of refugees; it also included the funding for domestic resettlement services provided to refugees who had already been admitted to the United States.

## The Plaintiffs: Individual Refugees and Cooperative Resettlement Organizations

The plaintiffs in this matter consist of two distinct groups. The first group is comprised of refugees—some who had already been admitted to the U.S. and others who had been approved for resettlement but remained outside the country. The second group consists of three organizations that operated under cooperative agreements with the State Department. These organizations were responsible for the overseas processing of refugee applicants and the provision of resettlement services for those arriving in the U.S. Together, these plaintiffs challenged the administration's actions in the Western District of Washington, arguing that the suspension of the refugee program violated the Refugee Act of 1980 and that the defunding of the USRAP violated the Administrative Procedure Act (APA).

## The District Court's Preliminary Injunctions (February and March 2025)

The district court initially intervened on February 25, 2025, by orally issuing a preliminary injunction that prohibited the government from implementing the suspension of the refugee program. On February 28, the court issued a written order clarifying that this injunction also prohibited the government from suspending USRAP funding. 

The conflict intensified when the government terminated nearly all cooperative agreements for refugee processing and placement. In response, the district court issued a second preliminary injunction on March 24, 2025, ordering the State Department to reinstate the cooperative agreements that had been terminated after the February 25 ruling. The government subsequently appealed both the February 28 and March 24 orders to the Ninth Circuit.

## The Sequence of Administrative and Limited Stays Issued by the Ninth Circuit

The procedural history in the Ninth Circuit is characterized by a series of shifting stays and "carveouts." On March 25, 2025, a motions panel of the Ninth Circuit partially stayed the district court’s February 28 order. While the court acknowledged the President's broad authority under § 1182(f), it created a "limited carveout," refusing to stay the injunction as it applied to individuals who had been conditionally approved for refugee status before January 20, 2025. This carveout was further clarified by court orders on April 21 and May 9, 2025.

The tension peaked in July 2025. After the district court established a framework to enforce the "limited carveout," the government filed an emergency motion for clarification and amendment. On July 18, 2025, the Ninth Circuit issued an administrative stay of the district court's enforcement framework. This sequence of events led to the expedited oral argument on September 3, 2025, and the subsequent September 12 order, which vacated the previous administrative stays and issued a comprehensive stay pending the final resolution of the appeal.
