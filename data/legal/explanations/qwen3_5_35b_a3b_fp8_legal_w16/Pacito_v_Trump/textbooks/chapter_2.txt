Chapter 2: # The Two-Pronged Challenge: Entry Suspension vs. Service Funding

The *Pacito* litigation presents a complex procedural landscape defined by a bifurcated legal challenge. While the case originated from a single political moment—the issuance of Executive Order No. 14163 on January 20, 2025—the legal disputes diverge sharply based on the specific rights asserted and the statutory authorities implicated. The Ninth Circuit's interim order distinguishes between the President's broad, deferential power to suspend the *admission* of refugees and the more constrained statutory duties regarding *services* for those already within or conditionally approved for entry. Understanding this split is essential to grasping why the appellate court vacated previous stays regarding funding while maintaining them regarding admissions, ultimately leading to a partial reinstatement of service contracts pending full briefing on the merits.

## Plaintiffs' Profile: Previously Admitted Refugees, Approved Applicants, and Service Provider Organizations

The standing and interests of the plaintiffs are central to understanding why the court treated the two prongs of the case differently. The complaint was brought by two distinct groups: individuals and organizations. The individual plaintiffs consist of refugees who had already been admitted to the United States prior to January 2025, alongside individuals who had received conditional approval for resettlement but remained outside the country at the time of the Executive Order's issuance. These individuals represent competing equities; those inside the country face potential loss of support services if funding is cut, while those awaiting admission face an absolute bar on entry.

Complementing these individual litigants are three non-profit organizations that serve as cooperative partners with the Department of State. These entities hold formal cooperative agreements designed to provide overseas processing for prospective refugees and manage reception and placement services for those once admitted domestically. Their involvement transforms what might otherwise be a purely humanitarian dispute into one involving contractual expectations and administrative continuity. By challenging both the suspension of admissions and the termination of these agreements, these organizations argued that they possessed a direct stake in preserving the operational framework required to execute refugee resettlement under existing statutes like 8 U.S.C. § 1522. This diverse plaintiff base allowed for arguments spanning constitutional immigration powers, statutory interpretation of welfare mandates, and administrative procedure regarding contract terminations.

## Executive Order No. 14163: Suspending Entry under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f)

The first pillar of the government's action involved Executive Order No. 14163, titled "Realigning the United States Refugee Admissions Program." Issued pursuant to Presidential authority found in 8 U.S.C. §§ 1182(f) and 1185(a), this order declared that entering refugees under the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) would be detrimental to U.S. interests and directed an immediate suspension of such entry pending further findings. This invocation relies on one of Congress's broadest delegations of power in immigration law, allowing the President to suspend entry whenever he deems it necessary for national interest without explicit judicial scrutiny of his underlying rationale beyond his own finding.

The Ninth Circuit addressed this prong with significant deference rooted in precedent, specifically *Trump v. Hawaii*, 585 U.S. 667 (2018). In its analysis, the court emphasized that Section 1182(f) vests "ample power" in the President and exudes deference to his determinations regarding national security and foreign relations challenges inherent in immigration control. The text requires only that the President "find" that entry would be detrimental; it does not require independent judicial verification that such detriment actually exists or that less restrictive means were available. Consequently, when evaluating whether a stay should be granted against this aspect of Executive Order No. 14163, the court concluded that because there is no textual limit on this authority beyond the President's finding itself, plaintiffs faced a low probability of success on this specific claim during an expedited review phase. This high threshold for review explains why the appellate panel maintained stays protecting presidential authority over border admissions throughout much of this litigation timeline until further notice on appeal was fully briefed on other grounds entirely unrelated to exclusion powers alone?
