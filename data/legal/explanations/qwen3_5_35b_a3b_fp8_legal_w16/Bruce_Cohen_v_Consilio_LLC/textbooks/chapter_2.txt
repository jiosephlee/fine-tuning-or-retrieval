Chapter 2: # Chapter 2: Standard of Review and Legal Framework for Summary Judgment

### De Novo Review Defined: Examining Evidence Without Deference to Lower Court Findings
Upon receiving an appeal challenging a district court's grant of summary judgment, the Eighth Circuit applies a "de novo" standard of review. This phrase signifies that the appellate court reviews the lower court's decision anew, exercising independent judgment rather than deferring to the trial judge's conclusions. As established in *Karlen v. Jones Lang LaSalle Americas, Inc.* and reaffirmed in *Placzek v. Mayo Clinic*, this rigorous approach requires the panel to examine the record completely afresh, treating their role as identical to what it was below. In *Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC*, this means the Eighth Circuit judges whether Consilio deserved summary judgment based solely on the evidence presented at that time, effectively stepping into the shoes of the district court to make its own determination of whether the law mandates dismissal or allows the case to proceed. Unlike factual determinations by a jury or bench trials where an appellate court grants significant deference to credibility assessments, summary judgment presents pure questions of law mixed with undisputed facts; therefore, no special weight is attached to the original rulings beyond ensuring correct procedure was followed.
