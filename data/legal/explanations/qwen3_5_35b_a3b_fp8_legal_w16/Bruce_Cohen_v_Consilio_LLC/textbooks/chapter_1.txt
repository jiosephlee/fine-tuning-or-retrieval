Chapter 1: # Chapter 1: Case Background and Procedural Posture

To understand *Cohen v. Consilio, LLC*, one must first establish the factual landscape of the employment relationship and the specific sequence of events that precipitated the litigation. Bruce Cohen commenced work with Consilio, LLC in 2018 in Minnesota as an hourly licensed attorney document reviewer. This classification placed Cohen in a distinct category of employee subject to complex interactions between professional licensing requirements and wage regulations. For approximately one year, the terms of his compensation remained stable until a pivotal administrative shift occurred. In July 2019, Consilio distributed an email to Cohen and other similar Minnesota-based attorneys detailing a forthcoming "premium overtime policy." Effective August 2019, this policy fundamentally altered their remuneration structure by eliminating premium overtime pay entirely. Under the new regime, Cohen and his peers would receive pay solely "at [their] base pay rate" for every hour worked, effectively nullifying any additional compensation typically due for hours exceeding standard workweeks.

Disputes over this unilateral change led directly to the initiation of formal litigation in August 2020. Cohen filed suit representing himself alongside a proposed class of similarly situated employees. He asserted that Consilio’s failure to remit overtime premiums constituted violations of three primary Minnesota statutes: the Minnesota Payment of Wages Act (MPWA), codified at Minn. Stat. Ann. §§ 181.101 and 181.171; the Minnesota Fair Labor Standards Act (MFLSA), found at Minn. Stat. Ann. §§ 177.25 and 177.27; and the Minnesota Wage Theft Act (MWTA), located at Minn. Stat. Ann. §§ 181.032(f) and 181.d(4). Pursuant to these claims, Cohen sought various remedies including back pay for unpaid overtime, liquidated damages to compensate for delayed payment, statutory penalties intended to punish egregious violations, and injunctive relief requiring Consilio to rescind its controversial policy changes immediately after paying out significant sums toward prior claims?
