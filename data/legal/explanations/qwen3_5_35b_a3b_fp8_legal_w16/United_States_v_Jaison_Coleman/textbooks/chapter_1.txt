Chapter 1: # Chapter 1: Case Background and Procedural Posture

## The Emergency Call and Initial Contact
The factual foundation of *United States v. Coleman* rests on a high-stakes emergency response captured by body-worn cameras. Just after 4:00 a.m. on April 20, 2023, Officer Jamie Kizer of the Marshfield Police Department received a 911 call reporting a domestic disturbance involving a threat of homicide. The caller, Lisa Coleman (the defendant's wife), reported that her stepfather, Jaison Coleman, had threatened to kill her and was still inside the home. Upon arriving at the scene, Officer Kizer knocked and announced his presence. Lisa appeared on the porch and provided partial information, stating that her children were inside but hesitating to confirm their immediate safety or whereabouts beyond noting they were in "her" bedroom. Her evasive answers regarding the children's well-being signaled potential distress to the officer. When Kizer pressed further on whether the children were going to be okay, Lisa replied only that she "hoped so," maintaining ambiguity rather than providing reassurance.

Kizer then shifted his line of questioning to determine the location of her husband. When Lisa indicated she did not know where Coleman was located inside, Kizer made his first formal request for entry. Lisa immediately responded with hesitation and a clear refusal, stating, "um … I'd prefer you not." At this juncture, the situation remained tense; Lisa excused herself to retrieve shoes and disappeared from view for a minute. During this interval, showing signs of concern for her safety and potentially that of others inside due to her absence from the doorway, Officer Kizer cracked the door slightly and called out repeatedly to ensure she was not in trouble or incapacitated inside. This initial phase established a dynamic where Lisa possessed agency—she could deny access—but also demonstrated confusion or fear that prevented her from providing definitive assurances about the environment within the home.

## The Critical Exchange: Negotiation for Consent
The pivotal moment in this case occurred when Lisa returned to the doorway approximately one minute later. Officer Kizer resumed his inquiry, asking specifically about her husband's identity after establishing that he was indeed Jaison Coleman. The dialogue then moved toward resolving the uncertainty regarding the children's safety and the potential threat posed by Coleman. Kizer asked if Coleman was calmer at that moment and re-raised the question of whether the children were okay. Lisa's response was fragmented but affirmative enough to suggest relief: "They're ... They're okay right this second."

It was immediately following this affirmation that Officer Kizer articulated his intent to enter physically rather than verbally assessing risk from afar. He stated, "Okay, um, I'm sensing that we almost need to come inside to make sure of that." This phrasing—specifically the use of "need"—became a focal point for defense counsel later in litigation regarding whether coercion rendered any consent involuntary. However, looking at it through the lens of totality of circumstances at 4:00 a.m., this statement reflected an officer exercising professional judgment based on ambiguous prior responses rather than issuing an order backed by forceful authority. Crucially, Lisa responded simply with "Okay," signaling agreement without protest or resistance. Following this verbal consent, she proactively guided officers past friendly dogs—which she assured would not bite—and directed them precisely where her children were sleeping in one room while identifying another room as likely housing her husband. By opening the door fully and facilitating entry by moving obstacles (the dogs), Lisa transformed from an ambivalent responder into an active participant in granting access to law enforcement officials who had previously been politely rebuffed moments earlier.

## Entry and Discovery Inside the Home
Once inside based on Lisa's explicit consent ("Okay"), Officer Kizer conducted what amounted to a protective sweep consistent with his expressed purpose: ensuring family safety and verifying Coleman's status relative to domestic threats. The officers scanned the premises but did not initially locate Coleman until checking separate rooms; they eventually found him in a back bedroom distinct from where he had allegedly been staying during arguments described by witnesses later on site—a detail corroborated by testimony regarding previous fights between Liza and Jaison where he became loud and aggressive.
