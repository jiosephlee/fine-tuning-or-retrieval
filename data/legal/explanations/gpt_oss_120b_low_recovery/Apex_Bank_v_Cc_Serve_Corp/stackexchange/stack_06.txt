Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### What standard does the court apply to determine whether services are “legally identical, in part” under the second DuPont factor?
Question:
The Board concluded that banking/finance services “legally” include issuing credit cards, and the appellate court affirmed that finding. What legal authority or test governs this “legal identity” analysis, and how does it differ from a purely factual similarity assessment?
Answer:
**Answer**

The Federal Circuit holds that the “legal‑identity” inquiry in the second DuPont factor is a **question of law**, not a factual comparison of how the services are marketed or who actually performs them.  

In *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the court explains that the Board’s determination that credit‑card services and “banking and financing services” are *“legally identical, in part”* rests on **dictionary definitions and the express language of the Lanham Act’s service classifications**, not on a comparison of the parties’ business practices.  The opinion states:

> “The Board determined that ‘credit card services’ … includes … issuing credit cards … The Board determined that the dictionary definitions for ‘banking,’ ‘bank,’ and ‘finance’ **encompass extending credit or providing funds through the issuance of credit cards**, Apex and CC Serve’s services are ‘legally identical, in part.’”  (¶ 27‑28)

Because the similarity analysis under the second DuPont factor asks whether “the respective products are related in some manner and/or … could give rise to the mistaken belief that they emanate from the same source,” the court treats the **legal categorization of the services** as the controlling standard.  The court further clarifies that this legal conclusion is reviewed **de novo**:

> “We review the Board’s legal conclusions de novo, and its findings of fact for substantial evidence.”  (¶ II)

Thus, the governing test is:

1. **Identify the statutory or dictionary meaning of each service term** (e.g., “credit‑card services,” “banking,” “finance”).  
2. **Determine whether those meanings fall within the same legal category** under the Lanham Act’s International Classification of Goods and Services (or other controlling legal definitions).  
3. If they do, the services are deemed “legally identical, in part,” satisfying the similarity requirement of the second DuPont factor.

### How this differs from a purely factual similarity assessment

A factual similarity analysis would look at **how the services are actually performed**, market overlap, consumer perception, and competitive relationship.  The *Apex* opinion expressly rejects that approach for the legal‑identity prong, noting that the Board’s conclusion does **not** require the services to be identical in practice; it only requires that the **law recognizes them as falling under the same category**.

The court distinguishes this from the sixth DuPont factor, where “similarity” is evaluated **factually**—the number and nature of third‑party marks on similar goods must be considered in the context of the factual similarity already found under the second factor.  The court remands the sixth factor because the Board improperly narrowed the factual scope, but it leaves the **legal‑identity** determination untouched.

**In short:**  
- The standard for “legally identical, in part” is a **legal‑category test** based on statutory/dictionary definitions, reviewed de novo.  
- It is **not** a factual comparison of business operations or market competition; those facts come into play for the other DuPont factors, but the legal identity question is decided on the law alone.
