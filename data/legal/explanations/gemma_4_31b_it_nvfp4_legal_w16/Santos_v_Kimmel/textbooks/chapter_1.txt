Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Santos v. Kimmel"

Chapter 1: # Case Background and Procedural Posture

The dispute in *Santos v. Kimmel* arises from a series of comedic segments aired on the late-night television show *Jimmy Kimmel Live!* (JKL). The plaintiff, former Congressman George Santos, alleged that Jimmy Kimmel used various fictitious identities to solicit personalized videos from him through Cameo, a platform where users pay for celebrity shout-outs. After Santos fulfilled these requests and provided the videos, Kimmel aired them on JKL as part of a mocking series titled “Will Santos Say It?” Santos subsequently filed suit, asserting copyright infringement and several state law claims, including breach of contract and fraudulent inducement.

The parties to this litigation include George Santos as the plaintiff and three defendants: Jimmy Kimmel, the American Broadcasting Companies, Inc. (ABC), which airs JKL, and The Walt Disney Company, the parent company of ABC. By naming both the creator of the content and the corporate entities responsible for broadcasting it, Santos sought to hold the entire production chain liable for the use of his videos.

The case first proceeded in the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York. The defendants moved to dismiss the complaint under Rule 12(b)(6) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure. A Rule 12(b)(6) motion asserts that the plaintiff has failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted; essentially, it argues that even if all the factual allegations in the complaint are true, there is no legal basis for a recovery. The District Court granted the motion, concluding that the copyright claims were barred by the fair use doctrine and that the state law claims were either preempted by the Copyright Act or otherwise failed to state a valid legal claim.

On appeal to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, the court applied a *de novo* standard of review. In the context of a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal, *de novo* review means the appellate court examines the legal issues from scratch, giving no deference to the District Court’s legal conclusions. To conduct this review, the Second Circuit considered the facts alleged in the complaint, any exhibits attached to it, and documents incorporated by reference. Additionally, the court utilized judicial notice to verify copyright registrations as published in the Copyright Office’s registry, a procedural tool that allows the court to accept certain undisputed official facts as true without requiring formal evidence.
