### Under what circumstances does the Copyright Act 'preempt' state law breach of contract claims?
Question:
The court mentions that the District Court dismissed Santos's contract claims because they were 'preempted by the Copyright Act.' I don't understand how a contract claim (which is about an agreement between parties) can be preempted by copyright law (which is about the right to control a work). What does it mean for a contract claim to be 'qualitatively' similar to a copyright claim in a way that triggers preemption?

***

### Under what circumstances does the Copyright Act 'preempt' state law breach of contract claims?

Based on the provided opinion in *Santos v. Kimmel*, the court **does not** establish the specific circumstances or the legal standard under which the Copyright Act preempts state law breach of contract claims.

While the District Court originally dismissed George Santos’s contract claims on the grounds that they were "preempted by the Copyright Act," the Second Circuit Court of Appeals explicitly declined to rule on the preemption issue.

The relevant portion of the opinion states:

> "Santos responds that those claims are ‘qualitatively different from a copyright infringement claim’ and thus not preempted. *Melendez v. Sirius XM Radio, Inc.*, 50 F.4th 294, 302 (2d Cir. 2022) (quotation marks omitted). **We need not resolve this issue, however, because Santos’s state law contract claims fail to state a claim for relief under state law.**"

In short, while the opinion acknowledges the argument that a claim must be "qualitatively different" from a copyright claim to avoid preemption, the court bypassed the legal analysis of preemption entirely. The court instead affirmed the dismissal of the contract claims based on the merits of state law:
1. **Direct Breach of Contract:** Santos was not a party to the Terms of Service he alleged were breached.
2. **Implied Contract:** The complaint failed to allege the essential terms of a contract or a "meeting of the minds."

Because the claims were legally insufficient under state law, the court found it unnecessary to determine whether they were preempted by federal copyright law.
