Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Santos v. Kimmel"

### Is an actual 'out-of-pocket' monetary loss required for a fraudulent inducement claim to survive a motion to dismiss?
Question:
The court affirmed the dismissal of the fraudulent inducement claim because Santos failed to allege any 'out-of-pocket loss.' I thought that fraudulent inducement was about the deception and the fact that the plaintiff relied on that deception to their detriment. If someone is tricked into providing a service, is that not 'detriment,' or does New York law specifically require a quantifiable cash loss for the claim to be valid?
Answer:
***

**Yes, under New York law, a plaintiff must allege an actual "out-of-pocket" monetary loss for a fraudulent inducement claim to survive a motion to dismiss.**

While you are correct that the general concept of fraudulent inducement involves reliance on a deception to one's detriment, New York law applies a more specific and stringent requirement regarding the nature of that detriment. As the Second Circuit clarified in *Santos v. Kimmel*, the "detriment" must be an actual out-of-pocket loss.

In *Santos*, the plaintiff alleged that he was tricked into providing personalized videos through the Cameo platform by a user (Jimmy Kimmel) who used fictitious names. Although Santos fulfilled these requests—effectively providing a service based on a deception—the court held that this was insufficient to sustain the claim. The court explicitly affirmed the dismissal of the fraudulent inducement claim because Santos "failed to allege that he suffered any actual out-of-pocket loss as the victim of the alleged fraud."

To support this requirement, the court relied on New York state precedents, specifically citing *Lama Holding Co. v. Smith Barney Inc.* and *Connaughton v. Chipotle Mexican Grill, Inc.* 

In short, for the purposes of pleading a fraudulent inducement claim in New York, the mere provision of a service or the loss of time/effort does not satisfy the requirement of "detriment"; the plaintiff must allege a quantifiable monetary loss.
