Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### What is the procedural effect of 'vacating' the damages award versus 'reversing' it?
Question:
The court reverses the denial of JMOL on non-infringement and then 'vacates' the damages award. I'm confused by the terminology. Since the court found there was no infringement, the damages logically cannot exist, but why use the term 'vacate' for the damages while using 'reverse' for the JMOL ruling? Does this have different legal implications for the finality of the judgment?
Answer:
***

### The difference between "reversing" a ruling and "vacating" an award

In appellate practice, the terms "reverse" and "vacate" describe different procedural actions. While they often appear together in a final judgment, they target different types of lower-court outputs: legal rulings and resulting orders.

**Reversing the JMOL Ruling**
To **reverse** is to change a legal determination made by the lower court. In this case, the district court had issued a legal ruling by *denying* the defendants' motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law (JMOL) of non-infringement. By reversing this denial, the Federal Circuit is correcting a legal error—specifically, the court's conclusion that there was substantial evidence to support the jury's infringement verdict.

Because the court found that "no reasonable jury could have found" infringement for either the ’134 or ’775 patents, it reversed the district court's decision. The procedural effect of this reversal is that the legal finding of infringement is overturned, and a finding of non-infringement is established.

**Vacating the Damages Award**
To **vacate** is to void, cancel, or set aside a specific order or judgment. Unlike a legal ruling (which is "right" or "wrong"), a damages award is a consequential remedy. 

The court does not "reverse" the $166,303,391 lump-sum damages award because it is not correcting a legal interpretation of the award itself; rather, it is removing the award's legal basis. As the opinion states: *"Because we reverse the denial of JMOL of noninfringement... we vacate the damages award."* 

Once the finding of infringement was reversed, the damages award became legally unsupported. Vacating the award wipes the slate clean, effectively treating the award as if it had never been entered.

**Summary of Procedural Effect**
| Term | Target | Procedural Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reverse** | Legal Ruling (Denial of JMOL) | Overturns the legal conclusion; substitutes the lower court's "Wrong" with "Right." |
| **Vacate** | Resulting Order (Damages Award) | Renders the specific monetary judgment void; removes the remedy. |

Regarding finality: both actions contribute to the finality of the appellate judgment. By reversing the JMOL denial and vacating the damages, the Federal Circuit has fully resolved the merits of the infringement claims and stripped the plaintiff of the financial judgment, leaving no further basis for the damages to be recovered.
