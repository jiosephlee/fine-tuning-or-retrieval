Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

Chapter 3: # Presidential Authority to Suspend Entry under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f)

In *Pacito v. Trump*, the court addressed whether the President possesses the legal authority to suspend the entry of refugees into the United States. The central legal question revolved around the scope of the President's power under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f) and the degree of deference that federal courts must afford the executive branch when the President invokes this statutory authority to restrict immigration.

## The statutory text of 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f)

The authority for Executive Order 14163, which suspended the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP), is rooted in 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f). This statute provides that whenever the President finds that the entry of any aliens, or any class of aliens, "would be detrimental to the interest of the United States," he may issue a proclamation to suspend such entry for a period he deems necessary. Additionally, the statute allows the President to "impose on the entry of aliens any restrictions he may deem to be appropriate." 

In this case, the President utilized this authority to determine that the entry of refugees under the USRAP was detrimental to U.S. interests and subsequently directed that their entry be suspended. The court's analysis focused on whether this action exceeded the textual limits of the statute.

## Application of the Trump v. Hawaii precedent

To determine the validity of the suspension, the Ninth Circuit relied heavily on the Supreme Court's decision in *Trump v. Hawaii* (2018). The court noted that the authority granted by § 1182(f) "exudes deference" to the executive branch. According to the *Hawaii* precedent, the statute vests the President with "ample power" to impose entry restrictions that go beyond those specifically enumerated elsewhere in the Immigration and Nationality Act.

By applying this precedent, the court established that the President's power to control the borders and determine who may enter the country is exceptionally broad. This level of deference means that the court is not looking for a detailed administrative record or a complex set of justifications, but is instead assessing whether the President acted within the broad permissions granted by Congress.

## The 'detrimental to the interests of the United States' finding

A critical component of the court's reasoning was the interpretation of the "finding" requirement in § 1182(f). The statute requires the President to find that the entry of a class of aliens "would be detrimental to the interest of the United States." The court clarified that this is the "sole prerequisite" for exercising the power to suspend entry.

In *Pacito*, the court found that the President had undoubtedly fulfilled this requirement by stating in Executive Order 14163 that the entry of refugees under the USRAP would be detrimental to the United States. Because the statute only requires that the President *make* such a finding—rather than prove it to a specific evidentiary standard—the court accepted the finding as a valid trigger for the suspension of admissions.

## Judicial restraint regarding the President's justifications for entry restrictions

The court emphasized a strict standard of judicial restraint when reviewing the President's justifications for entry restrictions. Citing *Trump v. Hawaii*, the court stated that it cannot engage in a "searching inquiry into the persuasiveness" of the President's reasons for finding an entry to be detrimental.

This means that the judiciary will not second-guess the wisdom, logic, or accuracy of the President's determination. As long as the President has issued a finding of detriment, the court will not scrutinize the evidence behind that finding or demand that the President provide a detailed explanation to the court to justify the restriction.

## The court's conclusion on the validity of the suspension of admissions

Applying these standards, the court concluded that the government was likely to prevail on the merits regarding the validity of the suspension of refugee admissions. Because the language of § 1182(f) is clear and the Executive Order did not exceed any textual limits on the President's authority, the court found the suspension legally permissible.

Consequently, as part of its stay pending appeal, the court stayed the district court's preliminary injunctions to the extent they prohibited the suspension of USRAP admissions. The court determined that the broad discretion conferred on the President regarding domestic and foreign interests weighed heavily in favor of the United States.
