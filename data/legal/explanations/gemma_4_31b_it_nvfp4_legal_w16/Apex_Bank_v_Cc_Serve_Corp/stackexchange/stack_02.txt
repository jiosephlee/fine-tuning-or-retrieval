Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### How can services be 'legally identical in part' if the parties provide different services?
Question:
In this opinion, Apex Bank argues that they provide banking services and CC Serve provides credit card services, and that they aren't directly competitive. However, the court affirmed the Board's finding that the services are 'legally identical, in part.' I don't understand how two different business models (a retail bank vs. a credit card service provider) can be considered 'legally identical' just because a dictionary definition of 'banking' includes extending credit. Is this a common way the Board defines service similarity?
Answer:
### How can services be 'legally identical in part' if the parties provide different services?

To understand how the court reached this conclusion, you have to look at how the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) analyzes the **second *DuPont* factor**, which assesses the similarity of the parties' goods or services.

Under the *DuPont* analysis, services do not need to be identical to be considered similar. The legal standard is whether the products are "related in some manner" or if the circumstances of their marketing could lead a consumer to mistakenly believe they "emanate from the same source."

In *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the "legally identical in part" finding is not a statement that the two business models are the same, but rather that the **legal/linguistic definitions** of the services overlap. Here is the breakdown of the court's reasoning:

**1. Definitional Overlap**
The Board looked at the descriptions of the services: CC Serve provides "credit card services," while Apex Bank provides "banking and financing services." To determine if these were similar, the Board turned to dictionary definitions. They found that the terms "banking," "bank," and "finance" encompass the act of extending credit or providing funds through the issuance of credit cards. 

Because "banking and financing" is a broad category that includes the specific activity of providing "credit card services," the two are "legally identical" at the point where those activities overlap. In other words, a credit card service is a *type* of banking/financing service.

**2. Source Emanation**
The Board supplemented this definitional approach with evidence of third-party registrations. They found that other companies frequently offer both credit card services and general banking services under a single mark. This supported the conclusion that these services are of a type that "may emanate from a single source," which is the core inquiry of the second *DuPont* factor.

**Summary**
The court rejected Apex Bank's argument that the parties were not "directly competitive" because the second *DuPont* factor does not require direct competition; it requires a relationship between the services. Because the broad definition of "banking" swallows the specific service of "credit card processing," the services are legally identical in the areas where they overlap, regardless of whether the parties' actual business operations differ.
