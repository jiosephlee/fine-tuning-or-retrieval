Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### Why could the Ninth Circuit stay the district court's preliminary injunctions before issuing its full merits opinion?
Question:
I'm confused about the procedural posture here. The panel says it heard expedited argument, that a full opinion will come later, but in the meantime this order itself "shall constitute a stay pending appeal." How is the court able to effectively decide major parts of the case through a stay order before releasing the full opinion? Is this just an application of the normal Nken v. Holder stay factors, or does it mean the panel has already substantially decided the merits?
Answer:
The short answer is: **yes, this is a normal stay-pending-appeal order under *Nken v. Holder*, and no, it is not the same thing as a final merits decision**.

### Why the Ninth Circuit could do this

Under *Nken*, a court of appeals may stay a district court injunction while the appeal is pending if the movant shows:

1. a **strong likelihood of success on the merits**,
2. **irreparable injury** absent a stay,
3. that the stay will not substantially injure the other side, and
4. that the **public interest** favors a stay.

The opinion expressly cites that standard and says those are “well established.”

So the panel did **not** need to issue its full merits opinion before acting. It only needed to decide, preliminarily and under the stay standard, whether the government had made the required showing to justify temporarily suspending the district court’s injunctions.

### What the order shows procedurally

The order says:

- the case was argued on an expedited basis,
- an opinion “will issue in due course,” and
- **“In the interim, we now vacate our administrative stay… [and] issue this stay pending final resolution of this appeal.”**

That language is classic stay-order language. It means the panel is making an **interim equitable decision** to preserve the status quo while the appeal is being decided, not rendering its final precedential merits disposition.

### But didn’t the panel already say who is likely to win?

In one sense, yes: a stay ruling necessarily involves a **predictive merits assessment**. The panel says the government “is likely to prevail” on the challenge to the refugee-admissions suspension, and also says it is “not likely to prevail on at least one” APA challenge.

That is enough for a stay. *Nken* requires a “strong showing” on likelihood of success, not a final merits holding. Courts often resolve stay motions by making a provisional assessment of the merits based on the briefing and argument they have at that moment.

### Why the order can be so consequential

A stay can have major practical effects because it **suspends the district court’s injunction** while the appeal is pending. Here, the panel stayed the preliminary injunctions “in their entirety except as specified,” and separately directed reinstatement of certain cooperative agreements. That is still procedurally a stay order, not the final appellate judgment.

The key point is that the panel is acting under its authority to manage relief **during the pendency of the appeal**. The stay preserves what the panel sees as the legally correct interim posture until it issues its full opinion.

### Bottom line

The Ninth Circuit was not “deciding the case through a stay order” in the final sense. It was doing what appellate courts routinely do under *Nken*: making a **preliminary merits and equities determination** sufficient to decide whether the district court’s injunctions should remain paused while the appeal is fully resolved.

So the order reflects **substantial provisional views on the merits**, but **not a final merits decision**.
