Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Why couldn't defense counsel impeach the witness by reading from the police report if the report described her prior inconsistent statements?
Question:
The opinion says the district court let defense counsel ask C.F. about what she told police, but would not allow counsel to read portions of the police report into the record without proper foundation because the report was not authored by C.F. I'm confused about the mechanics here. If the goal is impeachment by prior inconsistent statement, why isn't it enough that the report memorializes what she allegedly said? Does the defense need to establish that C.F. adopted the report, or call the officer who wrote it, or satisfy some separate authentication requirement before using it as extrinsic impeachment evidence? I'm trying to understand the interaction between Rule 613 and authentication rules in this setting.
Answer:
Because **Rule 613 only gets you so far**.

What defense counsel was trying to do here was not simply ask C.F. about her own prior statement; counsel wanted to **introduce the police report itself** as impeachment evidence. That triggers a separate problem: **the report had to be admissible as a piece of evidence in its own right**, which means it had to be **authenticated**.

The Eighth Circuit’s answer in *Cutbank* is basically:

1. **You may cross-examine the witness about her prior inconsistent statement**  
   The district court allowed that. Counsel could ask C.F. what she told police, which is the classic Rule 613 use.

2. **But if you want to read the report aloud as extrinsic evidence, you must authenticate it**  
   The court relied on *United States v. Cumbie*, which it quoted for the proposition that “extrinsic evidence may be used for impeachment by prior inconsistent statement, [but] such extrinsic evidence must still be authenticated.”

So the key distinction is:

- **Questioning C.F. about what she said** = no need to prove the report itself is genuine, because you are impeaching through her testimony.
- **Reading from the police report** = you are now offering the report as evidence, so you must show what it is and where it came from.

That is why the district court said the report was not enough just because it **memorialized** allegedly inconsistent statements. A police report is **not C.F.’s statement** just because it records what an officer says she said. Unless the defense established some basis to use that document—typically by **calling the officer who wrote it**, or otherwise authenticating it, or showing that C.F. **adopted** it as her own statement—the court could properly refuse to let counsel read from it.

In short, **Rule 613 governs the impeachment use of prior statements; authentication governs the document you are using to prove the statement.** The opinion treats those as separate requirements, and the defense failed the second one.
