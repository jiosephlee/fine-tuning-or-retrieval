Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### "What level of judicial review applies when evaluating an executive order based on Congress's authority in §1182(f)?"
Question:
"I read Pacito where judges cite Trump v HAWAII calling "deference" attached to Section 1182(f).  Does that mean they apply strict scrutiny, rational‑basis scrutiny, or some other standard when reviewing a President’s blanket suspension of refugees?

As I understand it courts normally defer heavily in immigration matters; however here they said the President has already met his mandate so it's about likelihood success.

How should I think about what review level really applies during such challenges, especially when a statute says buyers rarely face criminality’??"
Answer:
**Answer**

The Ninth Circuit’s procedural orders in Pacito quote Trump v Hawaii almost verbatim when it discusses judicial scrutiny of Executive Order 14163 under § 1182 (f):

> “…we cannot engage in ‘a searching inquiry into the persuasiveness…’.” *Hawaii*, 585 U.S., at 686  
>
> “…the sole prerequisite set forth in § 1182(f) … is that the President ‘find[s]… would be detrimental…’. The President has undoubtedly fulfilled that requirement here.” *Pacito*, O:R5‑L46‑L51  

These passages illustrate what backs up why there isn’t a conventional *rational‑basis* or *strict‑scrutiny* analysis applied here—what the court actually runs is an **extremely deferential “critical‐factual” test** dictated by the statute itself.

---

### What standard does § 1182 (f) impose?

| Level | What the court looks for | How it maps onto patentistic tests |
|-------|-------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Deferential factual prong** | Does the President have evidence that entry would be detrimental enough to warrant suspension? | There is **no question‑making beyond veracity**—the judge simply checks whether such facts exist rather than weighing competing arguments about scope or allocation |
| **No meaningful policy debate** | The executive branch’s assessment is taken at face value until proven factually false | Essentially bypasses standard jurisprudence used for regulating fundamental rights (*strict*) or ordinary civil liberties (*rational*) |

In practice this means :

1. **No deeper interrogation into how marginally wrong a policy choice might be** – courts will quit inquiry once pre‑existing evidence meets statistical thresholds set out by Congress (§ 1182).
2. **No balancing against other considerations unless those considerations are plainly requested in statutory language**, which they aren’t (§ 1185(a)).  
3. Courts give up every leeway typically available even under *rational basis*: they do not consider whether less restrictive alternatives are available nor whether discretionary savings justify granting power at all levels.

### How does this play out against remedies involving Congressional authority?

The Ninth Circuit uses four panels’ stay‑screening factors except that it explicitly notes both **(1)** plausibility that plaintiffs’ll succeed on merits and **(2)** irreparable injury vis­‑vis national interests—these were distilled directly from *Nken* rather than casting fresh constitutional lenses.^1 Because plaintiffs’ claim rests solely on their conjecture over DOC personnel funkiness and \(\Delta\)-bearing foreign‑policy authority, the court adopts billionaire precondition check before proceeding further.^2 Hence agreement that any dissent rests only upon establishing lack-of-factual support rather than mobilizing downstream constructive tests.


---

### Bottom line

When reviewers examine an order justified solely through § 1182 (f), they apply **an ultra‑deferential factual filter rooted directly in congressional wording**, virtually foregoing any “strict scrutiny,” “rational base,” or other piecemeal tests familiar from constitutional claims work.*  

In concrete terms :

* If you’re challenging an EO grounded entirely in \(\text{§} 1185(a)\)/\(\text{§} 1182 (f)\), you must dismantle **the factual foundation itself**, showing clear evidence dispelling a President’s impeachment claim.
* Once you meet that hurdle, you’re done—you won’t have your denial affirmed unless you prove one substantive violation outside \(\text{§} 1185(a)\) physics—exactly what pacito’s panel did when assigning stays pending further briefing.
