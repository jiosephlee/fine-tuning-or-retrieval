Answer:
**Short answer**

* In Pacito’s appearance “an *administrative* stay” was simply another way the Ninth Circuit described its ordinary relief—an injunction against enforcing a district‑court judgment—but one made precisely because the lower‑court ruling would require changes in how federal agencies carry out their statutory duties.  
* A *judicial* stay (“the March 25 / April 21 / May 9 stays”) was an ordinary temporary restraining measure against a post‑injunction decree made directly by a district judge.  
* All these commands were issued—and later revoked—by the same circuit panel; reversal is therefore only possible through rehearing/availing oneself before this same panel or before higher courts (en‑ banc review, Supreme Court), not through independent agency action.  
* When a court ceases its own temporary disciple it does so through routine appellate procedure (§ 10(b)(1) of Rule 10); unlike an agency’s revocation request—which would involve separate notice‑and‑comment procedures—it simply changes course based on newly‑emerging facts concerning how government services are provided under §§ 1520–1525 and how those actions affect plaintiffs’ rights.

### What separates “administrative” from “judicial” stays?

| Feature | Judicial Stay | Administrative Stay |
|---------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Origin** | Issued directly by any federal court when resolving controversies over ordinary trial‑court rulings.| Issued when the adverse action causing dispute involves *agency implementation* of statutes whose correct performance normally falls within APA/agency regulation territories.| 
| **Purpose** | To halt execution of any lower‑court judgment pending appeal.| To pause enforcement while federal agencies reorganize contract/co‑operative agreements pursuant to immigration statutes—the standstill pertains more narrowly to how government officers will **implement** policy than merely preserving existing rights.|
