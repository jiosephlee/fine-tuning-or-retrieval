Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Santos v. Kimmel"

### What is the pleading requirement for a fraudulent inducement claim under New York law?
Question:
The court dismissed Santos’s fraudulent inducement claim for failing to allege an out‑of‑pocket loss. I understand that the claim must contain an alleged loss, but what specifics does New York require? Must the plaintiff state the exact amount or type of loss, or can a general statement suffice? How does that affect the pleadability of the claim?
Answer:
**Answer**

Under New York law a fraudulent‑inducement claim is not complete unless the complaint sets out **an actual loss suffered by the plaintiff**.  
The Supreme Court of New York has held that a claim for fraudulent inducement “requires the plaintiff to show he incurred an out‑of‑pocket loss” (see **Lama Holding Co. v. Smith Barney Inc., 88 N.Y.2d 413, 421 (1996)**).  This is the loss that connects the fraud to the damages the plaintiff claims.

### What the pleading must contain

| Element | What the complaint must allege | Court’s guidance |
|---------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| **Loss type** | The plaintiff must identify the loss as an *out‑of‑pocket* loss—money, property, or a direct expense that was paid. | “Santos failed to allege that he suffered any actual out‑of‑pocket loss as the victim of the alleged fraud.” (Santos v. Kimmel, 2d Cir. 2025, § II, final paragraph.) |
| **Loss amount** | A specific dollar figure is **not required** at pleading stage.  The complaint may simply state “$X was lost” or “Santos suffered a loss of funds.” | The 2d Cir. opinion affirms the standard in *Lama* and *Connaughton v. Chipotle Mexican Grill, Inc., 29 N.Y.3d 137, 142–43 (2017)* that the amount does not have to be exact. |
| **Connection to fraud** | The loss must be shown as a direct result of the defendant’s misrepresentation. | “…Santos’s fraudulent‐inducement claim for failure to allege any out‑of‑pocket loss as required under New York law.” |
| **Omitted or vague loss** | A statement such as “I was harmed” or “I suffered a loss” without any reference to a tangible, out‑of‑pocket loss is *insufficient* and can be struck under Rule 12(b)(6). | **Santos did not allege any type or amount of loss.** The appellate court therefore dismissed the claim as failing to state a cause of action. |

### Practical effect on pleadability

* **Missing the loss element is fatal.** A fraudulent‑inducement claim that does not allege an actual out‑of‑pocket loss falls outside New York’s statutory definition of the claim and is dismissed.  
* **A general “loss” can work if it is specific enough.** If the complaint says, “Santos lost $5,000 when the defendant misrepresented X,” the claim may stand. The exact number need not be perfected; the amount may be refined at trial.  
* **Intangible or speculative losses do not satisfy the requirement.** Failure to show a concrete out‑of‑pocket loss—e.g., “lost reputation” or “felt cheated”—would not survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion.

**Bottom line:** In New York a fraudulent‑inducement claim must name an *actual* out‑of‑pocket loss. The amount may be left imprecise, but the complaint must identify the loss as a specific, tangible injury incurred because of the fraud. The ruling in *Santos v. Kimmel* demonstrates that a complaint that merely says the plaintiff was deceived, without further alleging a concrete loss, is insufficient and will be dismissed.
