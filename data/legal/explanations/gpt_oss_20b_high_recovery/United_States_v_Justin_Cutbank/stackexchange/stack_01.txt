Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### What does Rule 404(b) consider intrinsic evidence in a felon‑in‑possession case?
Question:
In the opinion the district court allowed D.F. to testify that she had seen Cutbank with the sawed‑off Marlin before the incident, citing United States v. Battle. I’m confused about what makes this testimony “intrinsic” versus “extrinsic.” Does any prior possession of a firearm automatically satisfy Rule 404(b) in a felon‑in‑possession or is there a higher threshold? What distinguishing factors determine whether such prior‑possession evidence is admissible?
Answer:
**Rule 404(b) and the “intrinsic‑vs‑extrinsic” test**  
Rule 404(b) excludes evidence of a person’s character “for the purpose of proving that on a particular occasion the person acted in conformity with that character trait.”  But it expressly allows evidence that is *relevant to an element* of an offense—what the court calls **intrinsic** evidence.  The Supreme Court has held that a prior act that is needed to establish an element of the crime is intrinsically admissible, while evidence that merely shows a habit or propensity is extrinsic and normally excluded.

---

### What the 8th Cir. calls *intrinsic* in a felon‑in‑possession case  

In *United States v. Battle*, 774 F.3d 504, 511 (8th Cir. 2014) the court emphasized that evidence of a defendant’s prior *ownership or control of the specific weapon charged* is intrinsically relevant:

> “Prior possession of a firearm is *directly relevant* to proving later possession of that *same weapon* because it helps establish ownership or control of the weapon.” ___ *Battle*

The *Cutbank* opinion follows this authority:

> “*We agree.* *Prior possession of a firearm is directly relevant to proving later possession of that same weapon because it helps establish ownership or control of the weapon.*” ___ *Cutbank*, § A(A) (quoting *Battle*)

In short:

| • | Condition for intrinsic evidence |
|---|------------------------------------|
| 1 | The evidence concerns the *same firearm* that is the subject of the felon‑in‑possession charge. |
| 2 | It is used to establish an element of the offense—here, that Cutbank *possessed* that gun. |
| 3 | The view is that proving continuous possession or ownership eliminates the need to speculate about the defendant’s motive or character. |

Because the evidence meets those criteria, the District Court allowed D.F.’s testimony that she had seen Cutbank with the sawed‑off Marlin “handful of times” before the incident.

---

### “Any prior possession*‑does‑not* automatically satisfy 404(b)**  

- Prior possession of a *different* firearm, or only “he used guns before,” is *extrinsic*; it is a habit of gun use, not a fact needed to prove the current charge.  
- Extrinsic evidence must be weighed against the prejudice–probative‑value balancing test of 404(b).  The court in *Cutbank* noted this, but because the evidence was *intrinsic*, that balancing was unnecessary—intrinsic evidence is admissible unless prejudice outweighs probative value (which the court found it did not).

The distinguishing factor is **relevance to a specific element** (ownership/control of the exact weapon) versus mere demonstration of a general propensity to possess firearms.

---

### Key take‑aways for students

| Question | What the law says (from *Cutbank* and *Battle*) |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Does any prior possession automatically satisfy 404(b)? | **No.** Only *prior possession that is directly relevant to proving ownership/control of the specific weapon charged* is intrinsic. |
| What determines admissibility of a prior‑possession claim? | 1. Is the evidence about the *same weapon*? <br>2. Is it used to prove a fact needed for the offense (e.g., possession or continuous possession)? <br>3. If it is intrinsic, it is admissible; if it is extrinsic, it must survive the 404(b) prejudice–probative‑value test. |
| How did the Board decide it was intrinsic in the case? | The witness said she had seen Cutbank holding that very sawed‑off Marlin “handful of times” before the incident, tying directly to the *possession* element of the 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) charge. |

Thus, in a felon‑in‑possession case, *intrinsic* prior‑possession evidence is admissible when it represents the defendant’s prior ownership or control of the exact firearm charged, whereas general or unrelated prior ownership is considered *extrinsic* and normally excluded under Rule 404(b).
