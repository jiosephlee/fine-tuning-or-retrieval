Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Does an altered or obliterated serial number automatically trigger a 4‑level enhancement?
Question:
The opinion mentions a 2‑level increase for an altered serial number. Does that always apply, or is there more nuance? Under what circumstances would a court discount the serial‑number alteration when applying § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B), and how is that distinguished from the 4‑level enhancement?
Answer:
**Short answer**

No.  
A firearm whose serial number has been altered or obliterated only triggers the *feature‑based* enhancement – a 2‑/3‑level increase under § 2K2.1(b)(6). That increase is evaluated and applied *separately* from the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) four‑level increase, which is triggered simply by the fact that the defendant *used or possessed* a firearm in connection with any other felony offense, regardless of whether the serial number was changed.

---

### How the opinion treats the two upgrades

| Guideline factor | How it’s described in the Cutbank opinion | How the court applied it |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Altered / obliterated serial number** | “a three‑level increase because the firearm had an altered/obliterated serial number” (the PSR) | The district court accepted the PSR’s factual finding and added 3 levels to the base offense level. |
| **Possession / use of firearm in another felony** | “a four‑level increase because he possessed the firearm in connection with another felony offense” (the PSR) | The court also accepted this finding and added 4 levels. |
| **Total level** | “for a total offense level of 35.” | The court’s sentencing range (292‑365 months) was based on that level. |

The appellate court expressly affirmed that “the district court did not err … in finding that the facts in the PSR establish that the gun was used in connection with another felony.”  (Cutbank, 292‑360 m.)

---

### Why the serial‑number change is **not** the same as the 4‑level increase

* § 2K2.1(b)(6) lists several **feature‑based** increases, one of which is a **2‑ or 3‑level** increase for a "weapon or ammunition with an altered or obliterated serial number."  The guidelines treat that as a *separate* factor that adds to the basic offense level.

* § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) is a wholly different provision: a **4‑level** increase is applied whenever “any firearm or ammunition was used or possessed in connection with another felony offense, **regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought or a conviction obtained.”**  The serial number is irrelevant to that provision.

Because the two provisions apply to different facts, a court can **discard the serial‑number enhancement** without affecting the 4‑level increase.  

- The court discards the serial‑number factor only if it finds no credible evidence that the serial number was altered or obliterated.  
- It can still apply the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) 4‑level increase if the firearm was in fact used or possessed in the commission of a separate felony, exactly as it was in Cutbank’s case.

---

### In Cutbank’s case

1. The PSR provided factual evidence (the gun was a sawed‑off Marlin .22 that bore an altered or missing serial number, evidence collected by forensic science).  The district court accepted that evidence and awarded the 3‑level increase for that feature.  
2. The PSR also provided evidence that the same gun was used during the violence against D.F. and in the escape from the police, thereby satisfying the requirement for the 4‑level enhancement.  
3. The appellate court affirmed because there was no indication that either of these factual foundations was flawed.

---

### Bottom line

*An altered or obliterated serial number does **not** automatically trigger a four‑level increase.*  
*The court treats the serial‑number factor as a distinct, lower‑level enhancement that can be omitted if the evidence is lacking, but the four‑level “felony‑firearm‑use” enhancement stands on its own and remains in effect as long as the weapon was used or possessed in the course of another felony offense.*
