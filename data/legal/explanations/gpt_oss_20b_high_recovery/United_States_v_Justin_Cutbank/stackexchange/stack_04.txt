Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### How is the 4‑level USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) firearms enhancement calculated?
Question:
The district court applied a 4‑level increase because the firearm was used in connection with another felony. The opinion states this applies regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought or a conviction obtained. What exact facts must the district court find to justify this enhancement, and how does the presence of an altered or obliterated serial number factor into that calculation?
Answer:
**Short answer**

Under § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) the *four‑level* increase applies when the defendant **used or possessed a firearm or ammunition in connection with another federal, state or local felony offense** – the other felony need not be charged or convicted.  
The court must find from the record that the gun was in the defendant’s possession **while the defendant was committing a separate felony** (e.g., the kidnapping/assault of D.F. or the robbery of her phone). The mere presence of an altered or obliterated serial number is a **separate three‑level** increase; it does not affect the four‑level rule but is added to it.

---

### 1.  What facts must the district court establish for the 4‑level increase?

| Needed fact | Where it comes from in the case | How the opinion treats it |
|-------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **(a) The defendant possessed a firearm** | PSR: “the defendant possessed a sawed‑off Marlin .22” and “DNA on the gun matched Cutbank” | The district court cites this fact without error. |
| **(b) A separate felony was committed** | PSR: “He had … other felony convictions” and “… the gun was used in the kidnapping/assault of D.F. and the robbery of her phone” | The opinion notes that the “PSR’s factual statements … establish that the gun was ’used … in connection with another felony offense.’” |
| **(c) The firearm was used or possessed *in connection* with that felony** | The PSR puts the gun in the defendant’s hands during the assault on D.F. and the escape from the police; the same weapon was used to threaten the victim and to facilitate the theft of the cell phone. | The Court says the requirement is satisfied “regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought, or a conviction obtained.” The factual basis in the PSR is deemed sufficient. |

The court therefore accepted the PSR’s findings that the sawed‑off Marlin was used *in connection with a different felony* (the kidnapping / assault of D.F. and the robbery of her phone). No actual “charge” or “conviction” for that other felony is a prerequisite; the factual connection alone is enough.

---

### 2.  How does an altered/obliterated serial number factor in?

* § 2K2.1(b)(4)(C) gives an **independent three‑level** increase when a firearm has an altered or obliterated serial number.  
* In *Cutbank*, the PSR also observed that the Marlin’s serial number was “altered/obliterated.” That fact alone triggers the 3‑level increase.  
* The serial‑number increase is **additive** to the 4‑level increase. The opinion explains the final offense level:

> “...a base offense level of 26 … plus a **three‑level increase** because the firearm had an altered/obliterated serial number, a two‑level increase because he recklessly created a risk of death … and a **four‑level increase** because he possessed the firearm in connection with another felony offense … for a total offense level of 35.”

The serial‑number fact does **not** affect the existence of the 4‑level increase; it merely adds an additional set of levels that are summed with the base level and any other Guideline increases.

---

### Bottom line

The district court only has to show

1. **The defendant had the firearm**; and  
2. **The firearm was used/possessed in the course of *another* felony** (identified or inferred from the record, not necessarily charged in the current case).

Once that is established, the 4‑level increase applies automatically, regardless of any charges or convictions related to that "other" felony. The altered‑serial‑number fact is a separate, additive enhancement. In *Cutbank*, the PSR provided both sets of facts, so the court’s application of the 4‑level and the 3‑level increases is correct, and the resulting offense level of 35 is within the advisory range.
