Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Constantinescu"

Chapter 1: # Background and Procedural History

## Defendants’ identities and social‑media influence  

The case concerns a group of nine defendants: Edward Constantinescu, Perry “PJ” Matlock, John Rybarczyk, Gary Deel, Stefan Hrvatin, Tom Cooperman, Mitchell Hennessey, and Daniel Knight.  Each defendant has cultivated large followings on multiple social‑media platforms, presenting themselves as experienced traders of stocks and other securities.  By consistently posting their trading activity and purported market analyses, they have positioned themselves as credible sources of investment advice to a broad audience of followers who are unaware of the defendants’ intent to manipulate markets.

## Description of the alleged pump‑and‑dump scheme  

The indictment alleges that the defendant group conducted a coordinated “pump‐and‐dump” scheme.  Under this scheme, the defendants would first purchase a security and then use their social‑media presence to disseminate false and misleading information about the security, thereby inflating its price.  The exaggerated, unsubstantiated claims were purported to create artificial demand, encouraging other investors—including their followers—to buy the security at the inflated price.  Once the price rose, the defendants would sell (or “dump”) the security, thereby profiting from the artificial price increase.

## Indictment contents: conspiracy and individual counts, estimated profits  

United States v. Constantinescu is filed under docket No. 24‑20143.  The indictment charges the defendants with conspiracy to commit securities fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1349) and, for each defendant, multiple counts of securities fraud under 18 U.S.C. §§ 1348 and 2.  The indictment alleges that the defendants collectively profited an estimated $114 million from the scheme.  Each defendant is alleged to have been an active participant in the false‑information component, thereby fulfilling the material element of the alleged fraud.

## Knight’s guilty plea and other defendants’ motion to dismiss  

Among the nine defendants, Daniel Knight entered into a guilty plea for the offenses charged against him.  Following Knight’s plea, the remaining defendants filed a motion to dismiss the indictment.  The motion argued that the indictment failed to establish a violation of the federal securities‑fraud statutes because it did not allege a deprivation of a traditional property interest.  The defendants claimed that the indictment merely alleged that they sought to deprive their followers of potentially valuable economic information, rather than money or other property.

## District court’s reasoning for dismissal (failure to allege deprivation of property interest)  

The United States District Court, after considering the defendants’ motion, concluded that the indictment could not survive.  The court held that the indictment did not sufficiently allege a scheme to deprive the followers of a protected property interest.  According to the court, the alleged conduct—depriving followers of potential economic information—did not constitute a “deprivation of property” within the meaning of the federal fraud statutes.  Consequently, the district court dismissed the indictment, finding that the indictment did not state an offense.

## Timeline of proceedings from indictment to appeal  

- **Grand jury indictment**: The federal grand jury returns an indictment against the nine defendants, docketed as No. 24‑20143.  The indictment alleges conspiracy to commit securities fraud and multiple counts of securities fraud, with an estimated $114 million in profits.  
- **Knight’s plea**: Daniel Knight enters a guilty plea for the charges brought against him.  
- **Motion to dismiss**: The remaining defendants file a motion to dismiss the indictment, arguing that the indictment fails to state an offense.  
- **District court dismissal**: The district court grants the motion and dismisses the indictment, holding that the indictment does not allege a deprivation of a traditional property interest.  
- **Appeal**: The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit receives the appeal.  On October 2, 2025, the Fifth Circuit issues its decision, reversing the district court’s dismissal and remanding the case for further proceedings consistent with its opinion.  

This procedural history sets the stage for the Fifth Circuit’s analysis, which focuses on whether the indictment adequately alleged the elements of securities fraud, particularly the existence of a scheme to defraud and the requisite intent.
