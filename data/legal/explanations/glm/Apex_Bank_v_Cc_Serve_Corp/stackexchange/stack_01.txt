Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Why does the Federal Circuit have jurisdiction over a TTAB opposition appeal instead of the district court?
Question:
I'm reading Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp., and the court says it has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B) to review a Trademark Trial and Appeal Board decision. I thought trademark disputes typically go to federal district court. Can someone explain why TTAB opposition decisions are appealed directly to the Federal Circuit rather than a district court, and what types of TTAB decisions fall under this jurisdictional statute?
Answer:
The Federal Circuit has jurisdiction here because 28 U.S.C. § 1295(a)(4)(B) grants it exclusive jurisdiction over appeals from TTAB decisions "with respect to applications for registration of trademarks." An opposition is precisely that — a challenge to a pending application for registration.

In this case, Apex filed intent-to-use applications with the PTO to register the ASPIRE BANK marks. CC Serve then initiated an opposition before the TTAB, arguing that Apex's marks were confusingly similar to CC Serve's registered ASPIRE mark. The Board sustained the opposition under Section 2(d) of the Lanham Act, effectively refusing to register Apex's marks. When Apex appealed, the Federal Circuit had jurisdiction because the Board's decision was one "with respect to applications for registration" — Apex's pending applications were the subject of the proceeding.

Your confusion likely stems from conflating two distinct categories of trademark disputes:

1. **TTAB administrative proceedings** — These are proceedings within the PTO about whether a mark should be registered. An opposition is one type: a third party challenges an applicant's pending application before it matures into a registration. The TTAB's decision is an administrative determination about registrability.

2. **Trademark infringement litigation** — These are civil actions in federal district court where a trademark owner alleges that another party's use of a mark infringes the owner's rights. This is what people typically mean when they say trademark disputes go to district court.

The opinion involves the first category. CC Serve did not sue Apex for trademark infringement. It filed an opposition with the TTAB — an administrative proceeding to block registration of Apex's marks. The appeal is from an agency decision about registrability, not from a district court judgment about infringement.

As for what types of TTAB decisions fall under § 1295(a)(4)(B), the opinion establishes that at least oppositions qualify. The statutory basis the opinion cites — decisions "with respect to applications for registration of trademarks" — encompasses TTAB proceedings where the subject is an application for registration. An opposition fits this description because it challenges a pending application, as the facts here demonstrate: CC Serve opposed Apex's "intent-to-use applications" to register the ASPIRE BANK marks, and the Board's decision refusing those applications falls squarely within the Federal Circuit's jurisdiction.
