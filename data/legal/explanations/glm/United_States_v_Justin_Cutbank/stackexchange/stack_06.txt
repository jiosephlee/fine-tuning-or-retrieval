Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### How do ACCA statutory penalties interact with the Armed Career Criminal Guideline when they produce different offense levels?
Question:
In Cutbank, the jury found the defendant had three or more prior violent felony convictions, triggering Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) enhanced penalties under 18 U.S.C. § 924(e). The PSR calculated a total offense level of 35, but Cutbank argued that the Armed Career Criminal Guideline (USSG § 4B1.4) would have produced a total offense level of 34. The court noted that "the Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies" under USSG § 4B1.4(b).

I'm having trouble understanding the relationship between the ACCA statute and the Armed Career Criminal Guideline. The ACCA sets minimum statutory penalties (15-year minimum), but how does that translate into a guidelines calculation? Is the § 4B1.4 guideline simply the guidelines' attempt to operationalize the ACCA, and if so, why would there be a discrepancy between the offense level produced by the general firearm guidelines (with enhancements) versus the ACCA guideline? When the court says "the greater offense level applies," is this a cross-reference rule within the guidelines themselves? Could the lower ACCA guideline level ever control, or does the enhancement structure always produce a higher level?
Answer:
## How ACCA and the Armed Career Criminal Guideline Interact

You're essentially right that § 4B1.4 is the Guidelines' mechanism for operationalizing the ACCA, but the key to understanding the discrepancy is recognizing that a defendant sentenced under the ACCA can have **two independent guideline calculations running in parallel**, and the Guidelines themselves provide a rule for which one controls.

### Two Paths to an Offense Level

When a defendant qualifies as an armed career criminal under 18 U.S.C. § 924(e), the sentencing court may calculate the offense level through two separate routes:

1. **The general firearm guideline path (§ 2K2.1):** This is the default guideline for felon-in-possession offenses. It starts with a base offense level and then applies specific offense characteristics — here, enhancements for a sawed-off firearm, an obliterated serial number, reckless flight creating risk of death, and possession in connection with another felony. In Cutbank's case, these stacked to produce a total offense level of **35**.

2. **The Armed Career Criminal Guideline path (§ 4B1.4):** This guideline provides a self-contained calculation for armed career criminals, with its own base offense level and enhancements tied to the ACCA's predicate-conviction structure. In Cutbank's case, this path would have produced a total offense level of **34**.

### Why the Discrepancy Exists

The two paths produce different numbers because they are structured differently. Section 2K2.1 builds the offense level through a series of specific offense characteristics tied to the circumstances of the firearm possession — the type of weapon, the serial number, the defendant's conduct during flight, and the connection to other felonies. Section 4B1.4, by contrast, calculates the offense level primarily by reference to the defendant's status as an armed career criminal and the number and nature of qualifying predicate convictions. These are simply different enhancement structures, and there is no reason they will always converge on the same number. In Cutbank's case, the § 2K2.1 enhancements (particularly the four-level enhancement for possession in connection with another felony) pushed the general guideline calculation one level higher than the § 4B1.4 calculation.

### The "Greater Offense Level Applies" Rule

The court's statement that "the Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies" refers to **USSG § 4B1.4(b)**, which is an internal Guidelines cross-reference rule — not a statutory command from the ACCA itself. Section 4B1.4(b) directs the sentencing court to compare the offense level produced under § 4B1.4 with the offense level produced under the otherwise applicable guideline (here, § 2K2.1 with its enhancements) and to **use whichever is greater**. This functions as a one-way floor: the armed career criminal designation can never *reduce* the offense level below what the general guideline would produce, but it can *raise* it if the § 4B1.4 calculation comes out higher.

### Could the Lower Level Ever Control?

No. Under § 4B1.4(b), the lower of the two offense levels will never control. The greater always governs the advisory range calculation. If the § 4B1.4 path produces a higher level than § 2K2.1, the § 4B1.4 level applies. If — as in Cutbank's case — the § 2K2.1 path produces a higher level, that level applies. The defendant cannot elect the lower calculation, and the court is not free to choose between them; the Guidelines mandate the greater.

### The Statutory Minimum Operates Separately

It's worth keeping distinct the Guidelines calculation (which produces an advisory range) from the ACCA's statutory minimum (which produces a mandatory floor). The 15-year mandatory minimum under § 924(e)(1) operates as a statutory constraint on the sentence regardless of what the Guidelines range says. If the Guidelines range calculated under the "greater offense level" rule fell below the statutory minimum, the statutory minimum would control as the actual sentence. But in Cutbank's case, the Guidelines range of 292–365 months already exceeded the 15-year (180-month) minimum, so the statutory minimum did not affect the calculation.

### Summary

- **§ 4B1.4 is the Guidelines' implementation of the ACCA**, but it runs alongside the general § 2K2.1 calculation.
- **Discrepancies arise because the two provisions use different enhancement structures** — one tied to the circumstances of the offense, the other tied to the defendant's armed career criminal status.
- **§ 4B1.4(b) is a Guidelines cross-reference rule** that resolves the discrepancy by mandating the greater of the two offense levels.
- **The lower level can never control** under this framework.
- **The ACCA's statutory minimum operates independently** as a floor on the actual sentence, separate from the Guidelines range calculation.
