Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

Chapter 2: # Louisiana's Statutory Framework for Cancellation of Financed Insurance Policies

## The Statutory Mechanism for Cancellation by a Premium Finance Company

When an insured finances insurance premiums through a premium finance company, Louisiana law provides a specific statutory mechanism for cancelling the policy upon default. The governing statute, Louisiana Revised Statute § 9:3550(G)(3)(a), authorizes a premium finance company to request cancellation of an insurance policy "by sending to the insurer, by depositing in the mail or with a private carrier, or via electronic mail . . . a copy of the notice of cancellation together with a statement certifying" certain required information. The statute thus contemplates a two-step process: first, the premium finance company sends notice of cancellation to the insured upon default; second, the finance company sends to the insurer a copy of that notice along with a certification statement. Once the insurer receives this notice and statement, the statute provides that the insurer "shall consider that cancellation . . . has been requested . . . and the insurer may proceed to cancel such contract." La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i).

In this case, the financing arrangement involved American Premium Assistance Company, LLC ("APAC"), which paid GoAuto the full premium in exchange for monthly installment payments from the insureds. Each financing agreement granted APAC a power of attorney authorizing it to cancel the insurance policies upon failure to pay. Each of the plaintiffs' policies was cancelled as a result of nonpayment. The dispute centered on whether GoAuto's cancellation procedures satisfied the statutory requirements.

## The Four Certification Requirements

The statute specifies four items that the premium finance company must certify in its notice to the insurer:

1. **Valid power of attorney**: The premium finance agreement contains a valid power of attorney as provided in the statute. La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(i).

2. **Uncured default**: The premium finance agreement is in default and the default has not been timely cured. La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(ii).

3. **Notice sent to insured**: Upon default, a notice of cancellation was sent to the insured, specifying the date of sending by the premium finance company to the insured. La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(iii).

4. **Copies sent to interested parties**: Copies of the notice of cancellation were sent to all persons shown by the premium finance agreement to have an interest in any loss that may occur thereunder, specifying the names and addresses of any governmental agencies, holders of a security interest in the insured property, or third parties to whom the insurance premium finance company has sent notice. La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(iv).

In practice, APAC's cancellation request to GoAuto was sent on APAC's letterhead and certified all four elements: that the premium finance agreement contained a valid power of attorney, that the agreement was in default and the default had not been timely cured, that a ten-day notice of cancellation had been sent to the insured, and that copies of the notice had been sent to all persons shown by the agreement to have an interest in any loss.

## The Strict Adherence Doctrine

Louisiana courts apply a demanding standard when evaluating whether a premium finance company has properly exercised its statutory power of attorney to cancel an insurance policy. As the Fifth Circuit explained, quoting Louisiana appellate authority: "Louisiana courts require strict adherence to these statutory requirements when a finance company exercises its power of attorney to cancel an insurance policy." *Delatte v. Lemotte*, 633 So. 2d 686, 689 (La. App. Cir. 1993). Strict adherence means that the premium finance company and the insurer must follow each procedural step the statute prescribes. The court emphasized that "'[s]trict adherence' to the procedures for cancellation is not an invitation for courts to add to the procedures." In other words, strict adherence requires faithful compliance with what the statute actually says, but it does not authorize courts to supplement the statute with additional requirements that the legislature did not enact.

## The Dual Purpose of Strict Adherence

The strict adherence doctrine serves two identifiable functions. According to *Britten v. Reavis*, 503 So. 2d 1149, 1154 (La. App. Cir. 1987), strict adherence to the statute "(1) it provides a clear method of fixing the exact time and date of cancellation, and (2) it grants a minimum time during which the default may be cured." The first function addresses certainty: when the statutory procedures are followed precisely, all parties can know exactly when cancellation takes effect. The second function protects the insured: by requiring that notice be sent and a cure period be observed before cancellation occurs, the statute ensures that the insured has a meaningful opportunity to remedy the default and maintain coverage. These dual purposes explain why Louisiana courts hold finance companies and insurers to exacting procedural compliance.

## Consequences of Noncompliance

The consequence of failing to strictly adhere to the statute is severe. The court quoted *Eaglin v. Champion Ins. Co.*, 558 So. 2d 284, 287 (La. App. Cir. 1990), for the proposition that "[a]ny defect in this process results in an ineffective cancellation of the policy." This all-or-nothing approach means that even a seemingly minor procedural defect can render a cancellation ineffective, leaving the insurer on the hook for a policy it believed had been terminated. It is against this backdrop that the plaintiffs in *Williams* argued that GoAuto's procedures fell short of statutory requirements. They pointed to two alleged defects: the absence of a signature on APAC's certification at the time of cancellation and the claim that GoAuto never properly "received" the cancellation request. The plaintiffs' strategy reflected the strict adherence doctrine: if they could identify any defect in the process, the cancellation would be ineffective as a matter of law.

## GoAuto's Cancellation Procedures

The court set out GoAuto's cancellation procedures in detail. The process unfolds in the following sequence. When an insured fails to make timely payment, APAC sends notice to the insured that their policy will be cancelled if no payment is submitted within ten days. If no payment is submitted by midnight on the tenth day, APAC's computer system automatically sends GoAuto an e-mail containing a copy of the notice of cancellation along with a request for cancellation. This request is sent on APAC's letterhead and certifies the four items required by the statute. Upon receipt of that e-mail, GoAuto's computer system automatically cancels the policy—no human involvement is required for cancellation to take effect. Only later in the morning, after the policy has already been cancelled, does an APAC employee affix a signature stamp to the printed cancellation documents. A GoAuto deponent explained the computer-to-computer interaction: APAC's system "makes a call to a GoAuto web service to request cancellation," and the GoAuto web service acts as "a listener" that performs the cancellation when called.

## Pre-2017 and Post-2017 Document Handling

The physical handling of cancellation documents changed in 2017. Prior to 2017, after an APAC employee stamped the printed cancellation documents with a signature, the employee would physically take the documents to a filing cabinet shared by GoAuto and APAC. The shared filing room, however, belonged to GoAuto Management Services, not GoAuto Insurance Company itself. Since 2017, the signed cancellation document is physically taken to a GoAuto employee who stamps the document as "received" and then places it in the shared filing cabinet. The plaintiffs argued that this post-2017 procedure still did not satisfy the statutory requirement of "receipt" because the employee who affixes the "received" stamp is an APAC employee, not a GoAuto employee. The court rejected this argument, concluding that GoAuto's method of receiving the electronic notice and reacting to it through its computer system satisfied the statute. The court noted that the statute is "a modern statute set in current times, and company computers do much that individuals once did."
