Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### Why did the Ninth Circuit vacate its prior stays rather than simply modify them?
Question:
The court vacates its administrative stay of July 18, its prior stay of March 25 (as amended), and then issues a new stay pending appeal. I'm confused about the procedural significance of "vacating" a stay versus "modifying" one. Does vacating the prior stays change the legal posture in a meaningful way, or is this just housekeeping? And what's the difference between the "administrative stay" of July 18 and the substantive stay of March 25—are they treated differently procedurally?
Answer:
## Why the Court Vacated Rather Than Modified

The short answer is that the new stay order has materially different terms from the prior stays, and vacating the old stays eliminates any ambiguity about which provisions remain operative.

### The new stay replaces the old carveout with a different one

The March 25 stay (as amended on April 21 and May 9) created a "limited carveout" preserving the district court's injunction "to the extent [it] applies to individuals who were conditionally approved for refugee status by [USCIS] before January 20, 2025." That carveout concerned **admissions**—specific refugees who had cleared a threshold approval step before the executive order issued.

The September 12 order eliminates that carveout. It stays the district court's injunctions "in their entirety, except as noted below"—and the new exception is categorically different. It concerns not admissions but **services**: the court directs the government to "reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522 to refugees who have been **admitted** to the United States." So the protected class shifts from conditionally-approved-but-not-yet-admitted refugees to already-admitted refugees, and the subject matter shifts from entry to post-entry resettlement funding.

If the court had simply "modified" the March 25 stay, a litigant could argue that the old carveout (conditionally approved individuals) survived alongside the new one (services for admitted refugees). By vacating the prior stays wholesale and issuing a single new stay with its own self-contained exceptions, the panel forecloses that argument. The only surviving protections are those expressly stated in the September 12 order.

### The procedural posture has changed

The March 25 stay was issued by a motions panel before full briefing and argument. The September 12 order issues after expedited oral argument, with the panel now in a position to assess the merits more concretely. The order reflects a bifurcated merits assessment that the prior stays did not: the government is likely to prevail on the § 1182(f) challenge to the executive order's suspension of admissions, but is **not** likely to prevail on the APA challenge to defunding reception and placement services under 8 U.S.C. § 1522. A fresh stay order captures that refined analysis more cleanly than serial modifications to a pre-argument order would.

### Administrative stay vs. stay pending appeal

The July 18 administrative stay was a temporary measure preserving the status quo while the panel considered the government's emergency motion regarding the district court's July 14 enforcement order. An administrative stay is issued without full analysis of the *Nken* factors; its purpose is simply to hold things in place pending consideration of whether a more durable stay is warranted.

The March 25 stay, by contrast, was a stay pending appeal—an order assessing (at least implicitly) the four *Nken* factors and intended to remain in effect through the appeal. The September 12 order is likewise a stay pending appeal, but one issued after full briefing and argument, with explicit *Nken* analysis.

Procedurally, vacating the administrative stay is straightforward: it was always meant to be temporary, and it is superseded by the new stay pending appeal. Vacating the March 25 stay serves the different function described above—replacing an interim stay with a considered one on different terms.

### Practical effect of the vacatur

The vacatur also moots the government's July 17 emergency motion, which the court denies as moot. That motion sought to enforce, clarify, or amend the March 25 stay in light of the district court's July 14 enforcement order. Once the panel vacates both the March 25 stay and the July 18 administrative stay and replaces them with a new comprehensive stay, there is no longer any live dispute about the scope or enforcement of the prior stays.

### Summary

The vacatur is not mere housekeeping. It resets the terms of the stay to reflect the panel's post-argument assessment of the merits, replaces the prior admissions-related carveout with a new services-related carveout, and eliminates potential confusion about which of the accumulated stay orders and amendments remain in force. The new order is the single operative stay, and its exceptions are the only exceptions.
