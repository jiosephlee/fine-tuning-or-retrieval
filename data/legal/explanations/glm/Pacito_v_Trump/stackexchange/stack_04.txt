Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### What APA violation supports the court's refusal to stay the injunction on resettlement services?
Question:
The opinion mentions the plaintiffs brought claims "under various provisions of the Administrative Procedure Act" regarding the defunding of USRAP, and the court says the government is "not likely to prevail on at least one" APA challenge. But the opinion doesn't specify which APA section is at issue or what the alleged violation is. Is this an arbitrary and capricious challenge under § 706(2)(A) to the termination of cooperative agreements? Or could it be something else, like agency action "not in accordance with law" under § 706(2)(C) because the government failed to fulfill a statutory mandate under § 1522? What's the most likely APA theory here?
Answer:
# The most likely APA theory is § 706(2)(C): agency action "not in accordance with law"

The opinion's reasoning points to a challenge under **5 U.S.C. § 706(2)(C)**—agency action "not in accordance with law"—rather than an arbitrary and capricious challenge under § 706(2)(A). Here's why:

## The court's reasoning is statutory, not procedural

The panel's analysis is structured around interpreting 8 U.S.C. § 1522 and whether it imposes a **mandatory duty** on the government to provide reception and placement services. The court emphasizes the mandatory language throughout the statute:

- § 1522(a)(1)(A) "requires the Director" to provide certain services
- § 1522(d)(2)(B) states "the Director **shall** assume legal responsibility" for unaccompanied minors
- § 1522(e)(7)(A) states "the Secretary **shall** develop and implement" support projects
- Services "may be provided through" cooperative agreements with nonprofit agencies under § 1522(b)(1)(A)(ii)

The court then notes that the government terminated the cooperative agreements—the statutory mechanism for providing these services—and, critically, that the government "was unaware whether any progress had been made toward securing a new agency or agencies." The court's concern is not that the termination decision was poorly reasoned (which would be the hallmark of arbitrary and capricious review), but that the government **cannot demonstrate it is fulfilling its statutory obligation**.

## The dissent confirms this framing

Judge Lee's partial dissent is telling. She does not argue that the government provided an inadequate explanation for its decision (an arbitrary-and-capricious-type objection). Instead, she disputes the **statutory interpretation** itself, arguing that § 1522 is permissive ("Authorization for programs"), not mandatory. Her entire dissent is a debate about whether the statute creates a duty the government can violate—which is the core question in a § 706(2)(C) "not in accordance with law" challenge.

If the operative theory were arbitrary and capricious review under § 706(2)(A), we would expect both the majority and the dissent to discuss whether the agency examined the relevant data, articulated a satisfactory explanation, or considered alternatives. Neither does. The disagreement is purely about **what the statute requires**.

## Why not § 706(2)(A)?

An arbitrary and capricious challenge would focus on the *process* of the termination decision—whether the State Department offered a reasoned explanation, considered reliance interests of the cooperative agreement holders, or engaged in reasoned decisionmaking. Nothing in the opinion discusses the adequacy of the agency's explanation. The court doesn't quote any agency reasoning, doesn't discuss the administrative record, and doesn't apply the "reasoned decisionmaking" framework from *State Farm* or *Fox Television*.

Instead, the opinion treats the termination of cooperative agreements as problematic **because it leaves the government without a mechanism to fulfill a statutory mandate**, not because the decision to terminate was itself unreasoned.

## Bottom line

The court's holding is that the government is "not likely to prevail" because its actions conflict with a statutory mandate under § 1522. That is a textbook § 706(2)(C) claim: agency action "not in accordance with law." The opinion signals that the full merits opinion will explain this in detail, but the structure of the analysis—statutory interpretation of mandatory language, the government's inability to show compliance, and the dissent's counter-argument about the statute being merely permissive—strongly indicates the APA theory is "not in accordance with law," not arbitrary and capricious.
