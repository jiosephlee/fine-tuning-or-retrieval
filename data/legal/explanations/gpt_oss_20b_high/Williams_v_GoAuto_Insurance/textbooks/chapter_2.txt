Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

Chapter 2: # Louisiana Law Governing Cancellation Of Financed Policies

This section explains how Louisiana’s statutes regulate the cancellation of insurance policies financed through third‑party payment arrangements. It focuses specifically on La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G), whose language prescribes both *what* must appear in an insurer’s notice request to cancel and *when* the insurer must act upon receipt thereof.

---

## Textual Requirements (§ 9 3550(G)(3)(a) (i)–(iv)

La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G)(3) obligates a premium‑finance company that exercises its power‑of‑attorney authority toward an insurer “to send…[the] notice…together with a statement certifying”:

1. **Validity of the Power‑of‑Attorney** – The finance contract contains a valid power‑of‑attorney giving APAC authority over policy cancellation.(La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G)[(1)]).

2. **Un‑cured Default** – The finance agreement is in default and has not been timely cured.(La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G)[(2)]).

3. **Notice Sent Upon Default** – Upon default an insured received notice specifying when APAC initially sent it.(La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G)[(3)]).

4. **Copies Sent To All Interested Parties** – Copies were made available to everyone specified in the finance agreement who might have an interest in loss under the policy.(La Rev Stat § 9 3550(G)[(4)]).

These declarative statements need not contain signatures or identifying staff names unless a separate statute imposes those particulars—as no such requirement exists here, courts have interpreted “certifying” quite broadly enough that printed statements suffice.[^1]

[^1]: See *Lopez v Mississippi Automobile Insurance Co.*, recognizing documented mass‑mail notices were sufficient despite lacking individual author signatures.

---

## Necessity for Insurer Receipt (§ 9 3550(G)(3)[b] (i))

Once the insurer receives APAC’s certified notice described above, La Rev Stat mandates “the insurer shall consider [the] cancellation … requested …” so long as no cure occurs.(La Rev Stat §\,9\,3550[G][b]\,(i)).  
In practice this means an insurer can act upon electronic delivery (email via an authorized host), physical mailings, or private carriers—terms coextensive with contemporary business practices following standard postal doctrine quoted in *Huntley v. Bayer MaterialScience*.[^2]

[^2]: The Fifth Circuit figures e‑mail delivered through APAC’s Web service constitutes “receipt” because it places control duty squarely upon GoAuto’s system regarding future actions concerning coverage status.


---

## Doctrine Noted Under _Delatte_ v _Lemotte_ et al.

The courts consistently reiterate two intertwined doctrinal points originating largely in _Delatte_ v _Lemotte_:

1. **Strict Adherence Is Required** – Courts call out “strict adherence” when discussing cancelled financed policies because any procedural lapse undermines statutory certainty goals.(see *Delatte*, 633 So.\,^2 686).

2. **Any Defect Produces Ineffective Cancellation** – Failure in any homogenous element—invalid POA, improper default documentation, unsent copies—renders cancellation void until remedied.(see *Eaglin v Champion*, 558 So.\,^2 Id.; _Britten_ ,503 So.\,^2 Id.).

These principles ensure parties cannot rely merely on general contractual belief when tender abandonment occurs under state mandate.
