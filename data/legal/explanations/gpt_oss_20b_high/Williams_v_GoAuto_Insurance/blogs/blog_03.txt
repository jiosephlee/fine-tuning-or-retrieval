Title: Blog about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

## Receipt Revisited: What It Means Under Louisiana Cancellation Law

The Fifth Circuit’s recent affirmation—*Williams v. GoAuto Insurance*—may at first blush appear routine: a fee‑based insurance agent simply obeys a fine‑printed statute. But for Louisiana’s sophisticated auctioneer‑style insurance market, where premiums are typically financed through a chain of intermediaries known as premium‑finance companies, the question “What does ‘receipt’ actually buy an insurer?” is far from trivial. In *Williams*, the court turned to plain‑English dictionary meanings and drawn analogies from *Huntley v. Bayer MaterialScience* to convince itself that a computerized acknowledgment suffices under § 9 3550(G)(3)(b)(i). The decision walks a narrow course between stare decisis and modern technology—and its effects reverberate across any statute that merely demands “receipt” or “possession.”

### Why This Matter at All

Louisiana’s finance‑policy regime spells out precisely which parties may cancel a covered policy and under what conditions that cancellation is valid. A key hurdle is statutory compliance with § 9 3550(G)(3)(b)(i): *upon receipt of such notice of cancellation…the insurer shall consider that cancellation has been requested*. When an insured fails to pay its installment loan from American Premium Assistance Company (APAC), APAC spots the default and sends GoAuto an electronic notification—a load‑balancing routine that simultaneously appends the signed certificate printer stamp via APAC’s proprietary web service interface.

The plaintiffs insisted GoAuto was acting without “strict adherence” because **no physical signature preceded cancellation**—the document was sent automatically by software before any APAC employee stamped it offline later that day. Their argument rested on two premises:

1. **Receipt** means some tangible hand‑off.
2. **Certifying** means there must be a named individual’s signature affixed at the moment of delivery.

If true, GoAuto would pointlessly hit its compliance wall even though the law applies within seconds after database log entry—a reality check about whether old statutes correctly speak modern systems.

### How the Ninth Workshop Tolerates Digital Receipts

As Judge Southwick noted while affirming district‑court findings:

> The term *receipt* … has “the act of receiving something,”and *receive* … means “to come into possession.”

While drafted decades ago—most legislature left vague the subtle distinction between *message transmitted* & *document received*—the real question was *when does possession/materialize.* In the case at hand, APAC transmitted its copy electronically into GoAuto’s computer system on the midnight terminus day ; incorporated within APAC’s print‐out workflow was also an address field forwarding ballots en masse to any party specified under § 9 3550(G)(3)(a). Judges reasoned electronic media equals a *private carrier*.
