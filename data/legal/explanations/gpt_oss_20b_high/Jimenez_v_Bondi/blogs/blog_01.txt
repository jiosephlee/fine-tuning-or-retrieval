# Perception Versusality – Why Harassing Phone Calls Won’t Count as Asylum  

## Why This Decision Matters  

In *Jimenez v. Bondi*, Judge Tymkovich shapes asylum jurisprudence yet again around what counts as “persecution.” The case involving Colombian nationals who fled Bogotá after a series of threatening phone calls illustrates how the federal courts continue to insist on more concrete evidence than mere intimidation when adjudicating political‑opinion claims under INA § 1101(a)(42)(A). Because asylum remains one of U.S. immigration authorities’ most discretionary instruments—available only when an applicant meets “past persecution” or a “well‑founded fear” test—clarifying where intimidation ends and persecution begins has practical consequences for millions hopeful waves arriving at U.S. ports every year. For practitioners, *Jimenez* signals that prosecutors will continue demanding physical injury—or at least an overt lawyer‑handed assault—beyond psychosocial distress whenever state actors pop up in any capacity—even parasitically linked gangs acting against an individual’s political stance.  

The Ninth Coast’s decision builds upon waiting-watcher guidance predicated on *Wiransane* v.^​Ashcroft^1*, *Karki*^2*, *Rivera‑Barrientos*^3*, and others that cemented a “threshold” test whereby a threat alone cannot suffice unless corroborated by grave physical harm or embodied violence “at least one central reason” overtly tied to a protected ground.*4* By explicitly rejecting petitioner phone‑call intimidation devoid of direct bodily injury—or even trickle‑down psychological trauma—in many elite jurisdictions these rulings sharpen limits surrounding **“political intimidation”** claims generated amid Colombia’s evolved paramilitary landscape where criminal groups operate sediscursively beneath governmental auspices.  

For testimony practitioners reciting cross‑border narratives today—from Mexican labor volunteers nagging PMC kin saw creeps couched doubt odor specification [ref], these redecorations underscore careful pleading design required thereafter.•
