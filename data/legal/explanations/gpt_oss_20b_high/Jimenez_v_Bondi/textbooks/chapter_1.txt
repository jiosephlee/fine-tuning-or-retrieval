Chapter 1: # Case Overview and Procedural History  

This chapter traces Jimenez v.\* Bondi from its origins in Colombia through its journey across multiple courts culminating in a Tenth Circuit petition filed under 8 U.S.C. § 1252(a)(1). Each step highlights how an immigrant entrant’s personal history intersects with statutory requirements governing asylum relief and removal proceedings.  

## Petitioners’ identity, nationality, and immigration status  

Wilson Leonardo Canon Jiménez is a Colombian national who was born and resides in Bogotá’s central district along with his wife Angelica Parada Duarte and their two minor sons. The family had operated a furniture factory there before migrating together without formal admission or parole into the United States in December 2022. Under U.S. immigration law their presence therefore constituted unlawful entry pending subsequent removal proceedings triggered when DHS served them individual Notices to Appear (NTA).  

## Political activities in Colombia and associated threats  

Since 2012 the couple had been active members of Colombia’s Liberal Party. In late May 2022 they undertook door‑to‑door canvassing during Federico Gutierrez’s presidential campaign—a rival candidate to then President Gustavo Petro—while travelling through local neighborhoods seeking votes. Between late May and early June 2022 Jiménez documented several hostile phone calls warning him “to stay away” from politics or continue receiving threats directed at his political affiliation; one colleague was abducted and murdered shortly thereafter despite having received comparable warnings early that summer. These events set the stage for continued intimidation against both members despite sustained campaigning activities which neither spouse ceased before leaving Colombia toward migration.\*  

## Property dispute involving Don Rafa’s narco‑trafficking group  

Jiménez’s younger career phase included an internship at Colombia’s National Prosecutor’s Office where he protected victims seeking justice against domestic abuse cases . In June 2022 he was contacted by a woman from San Bernardo who requested legal advice concerning a landlord‑tenant dispute over her property––the latter belonged legally now solely to her following divorce settlement yet still occupied by her ex‑husband Rafael Augusto Montañez Lancheros (“Don Rafa”), who identified himself publicly as an influential criminal cartel leader closely aligned with President Petro’s administration . Jiménez visited the property looking first for resolution yet learned it had previously been sealed down on health concerns linked to drug trafficking; covert searches conducted repeatedly failed . During this visit he encountered villagers who deemed him an “informant” further amplifying his perceived opposition . Subsequent threats materialized directly around this encounter—including gun‑point intimidation as well as assertions that “Don Rafa” would eliminate “snitches”—showing escalated violence triggered more directly by Raymond’s investigative stance rather than purely salience hobby pursuit.\*
