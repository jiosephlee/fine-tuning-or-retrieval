Chapter 3: # Evaluating Past Persecution

Past‑persecution is the cornerstone of any asylum claim. As the second‑level court explained, U.S. asylum law does not “define persecution,” so adjudicators must consult precedent when deciding whether an alleged experience constitutes a plea‑worthy event that rises outside ordinary social tensions and threatens an applicant’s life or liberty beyond mere intimidation.【Wiransane v.*Ashcroft*, 366 F.3d 889】【Ritonga v.*Holder*, 633 F.3d 971】  

  
## Persecution definition in case law (Wiransane v.*Ashcroft* ; Ritonga v.*Holder*)

In **Wiransane**, the court articulated that *persecution* must involve “the infliction of suffering or hurt upon a person who differs … in a manner regarded as offensive.” The distinction struck between ordinary insults afforded by free speech standards versus deliberate acts designed expressly to create fear that one’s personal dignity is threatened because he belongs to a protected category**.”** The same standard was reiterated in **Ritonga**, where appellate judges remarked that attempts at discipline—such as police isolation units and lengthy interrogations—become politically motivated crimes when motivated by harmful intent tied directly to an immunized group**.”**

Against this backdrop, issues pertaining solely to intimidation without demonstrable assault do not satisfy the requisite offensiveness threshold for past‑persecution nor do they constitute an unlawful activity aimed merely at “restrictions.”  

  
## Threshold: more than mere threats or restrictions  

The conceptual threshold revises `suspected` fears into real‑world criminality rooted completely within motive‑driven acts rather than abstract insecurity. A preponderant standard cannot be met merely when someone receives harassing messages because otherwise legitimate policy—yields valid sanctions—for treasonable actions could obliterate legitimate opinion exercise**.”** Accordingly, meaningfully demonstrable forms such as targeted violence, abduction, repeated detentions capped at hours―or any compelable bodily injuries―constitute something far beyond ordinary fractional jeopardy**.”**

Accordingly whether fearful expression crosses into actual political insult hangs entirely upon whether victims fell victim…to homicidal intent beyond an invading mindshare attack**.”**
