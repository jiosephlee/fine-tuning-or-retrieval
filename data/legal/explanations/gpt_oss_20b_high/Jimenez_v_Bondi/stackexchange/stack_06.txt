### Short Answer  

When an alien claims a *well‑founded fear* under §1158(b)(1), federal courts apply four separate predicates that all have been affirmed outright in **Jiménez v. Bondi**, Tenth Circuit (2025):

| Predicate | What must be proved | Key indicator in Jiménez |
|-----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| 1️⃣ Past persecution | An event(s) rising “to a level …of persecution”; physical/personal harm beyond mere intimidation.| Courts distinguish ordinary threats from true punishment (`Karki & Brandy`, held shown in Jiménez). |
| 2️⃣ Nexus / Central reason | The persecuted conduct was motivated by one *statutorily protected* ground on which it was at least one **central reason**, not a tangential factor.| Politically driven motive required (`Rivera‐Barrientos`, `Dallakoti`). Jiménez found politics were peripheral because actions stemmed from property disputes rather than opposition stance. |
| 3️⃣ Government protection / Unwilling–or–Unable | Evidence that domestic authorities were either unable or unwilling to stop/ deter persecutors (`Singh`). The applicant must document attempts/ failures at police intervention.| In Jiménez no reliable report showed Colombian police stopping Don Rafa’s men—or offering meaningful protection—so this bar could not be thwarted. |
| 4️⃣ Safe internal relocation | Showing you could reasonably relocate elsewhere inside your home country so future terror would cease (`Rivera‐Barrientos`). If such move is possible, the presumed well‑founded preference may lapse.| Jiménez noted relatives remain unharmed elsewhere; while petitioners briefly stayed at Antioquia countryside without incident, overall they could solve risk through domestic shift → denied withdrawal basis upon omission                 |

Because each predicate was weakly supported—or outright missing—Judicial review collapsed once records weighed against any reasonable adjudicator as the appellate panels required (`Rivera‐Barrientos` substantive evidence standard). The Court thus denied both asylum and withholding relief.

#### How it reads out

If you are trying warrant an asylum claim through “well–founded fear,” here's what you need versus what happened here:

1🚫 *Threats alone ≠ past persecution* → Jiménez had phone calls and men threatening them → not enough harm ("threats alone …are insufficient").

2🚫 *Politics had no central footing* → The forced entry showed motives tied to property conflicts > campaign stance → politics was peripheral (“central reason” rule).

3🚫 *Unwilling Indian gov’t uncaught proof* → Police interview ambiguous → no documented failed intervention → Colombia apparently able/willing (= defeat ‘unable/or unwilling’ claim)

4✅ If future risk resolved simply by moving nowhere else inside Colombia = potential hard backbone for denying ‘withholding’. Jiménez had family elsewhere & had tried rural stay = potential escapable route → counts against their claim even absent passport coverage

Bottom line — All four pillars needed tallied together for revocation.\n\n*
