Chapter 2: # II – Executive Authority Under Section 1182(f)

The conflict resolved at this stage hinges on how far Congress has meant ‘discretion’ to stretch when it hands the President the power under § 1182(f) to suspend refugee admissions at his discretion. In *Pacito*, the Ninth Circuit confronted Congress’s layered framing—starting with the Refugee Act’s broad policy mandate down through § 1182(f)’s authorizing language—and analyzed whether the president’s action met the required “detrimental‑interest” threshold while obeying subsequent procedural rules set out by statute and earlier precedent such as *Trump v.* Hawaii.*

## Federal Statutory Foundations

Congress arranged its regulatory architecture like an onion: at its core lies the Refugee Act’s substantive eligibility framework—what persons can be considered refugees—and beneath this sits § 1182(f)’s discretionary tool allowing the president to halt admission altogether when “the entry … would be detrimental.”  
§ 1182(f) requires only that the president “find[es]” an adverse national‑interest condition and then issue a proclamation lasting “for such period as he shall deem necessary.” Nothing else is contained in statute—the clause is neither prescriptive nor diagnostic beyond these two ingredients—therefore courts accept that burden rests solely upon an executive finding supported by factual grounding rather than legislative specificity.*

A distinct layer exists opposite this nominal “blank‑space” instrument—the procedural scaffolding surrounding how such findings manifest agency actions (e.g., proclamations). By keeping the statutory requirement huskily simple, Congress deliberately left room for future administrative mechanisms—but lawmakers have never been shrill about needing further oversight beyond Parliament’s own fiscal appropriations ends.*

In *Pacito*, prosecutors centred their argument around whether President Trump’s *Executive Order No.* 14163 sufficiently found that refugee admission threatened U.S. interests before it could trigger § 1182(f)’s door‑stop clause.*
