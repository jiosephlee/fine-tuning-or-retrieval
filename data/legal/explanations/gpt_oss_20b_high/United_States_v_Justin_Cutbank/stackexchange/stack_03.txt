Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### "Can police reports serve as impeachment witnesses without explicit authentication?"
Question:
"In Section E the trial court let C.F.`e disclosures made to investigators remain intact yet refused defense men entering portions of those reports without foundation—Cumbie requires authentication even though the declarant testified on‐the‐record.” Can someone outline what rules dictate authentication necessity here?”
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
No. A police report can only be introduced as an impeaching exhibit when the party presenting it first proves its authenticity under Rule 901(a). Even if a witness has already talked about the contents “on‑the‑record,” that oral testimony does not satisfy the authentication requirement; § E of *United States v. Cutbank* affirms that any remote (extrinsic) record used for impeachment must itself be authenticated—and *Cumbie* spells out why rule‑required foundation is mandatory.

---

### What the law says

| Creditor | Provision | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed.R.Evid. 901(a)** | *Authentication or Identification.* A person who introduces an item of evidence “must prove its authenticity…by any reliable means.” | The person who wants to use an item (e.g., a police report) bears the burden of proving that paper is what it claims to be—authorization, date of origin, contents match claimed facts,‑or any other established method of proof. |
| **Fed.R.Evid. 403** | *Exclusionary power.* Evidence may be excluded if its probative value is substantially outweighed by danger of unfair prejudice or confusion.| Not directly involved here; authenticating adequately satisfies these concerns so that admissibility issues remain due only to lack of authentication rather than prejudice concerns per se. |
| **United States v.\*\*Cumbie*** (8th Cir. 2022) | Recognizes **no “implicit” waiver** of authentication when an eyewitness has later recounted the same content orally | Any external document introduced solely for impeachment—or even as corroboration—still requires foundational proof under § 901(a). |
| **United States v.\*\*Cutbank*** (this opinion) – Section E | “Although extrinsic evidence may be used for impeachment…such extrinsic evidence must still be authenticated.” | The district court’s refusal to allow defense officers to introduce portions of police reports without a proper foundation was upheld as correct under rule‑law and *Cumbie*. |

---

### Why oral testimony isn't enough

1. **Rule 901(a) applies to *any* exhibit** – whether created by the witness or by an unrelated party.  
2. An eyewitness testifying about events with some officer does **not** confer *authentication* on records written afterward or compiled by investigative staff after those events took place – there is no obvious link proving identity/contents beyond doubt.
3. The appellate panel’s wording makes clear that “prior inconsistent statements” are admissible only when “the identify/contents of each consistent/extraneous statement … are verified.” Curiously enough, partial questions could suffice—for instance:

   - Which investigator entered into Police File #2345?  
   - Does File #2345 bear your director’s written certification?  

   Those answers would satisfy § 901(a); absent them, tried counsel was correct in blocking presentation.

---

### How a defense counsel should proceed

| Step | Example Question | What does it prove? |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify reporter/investigator** | “Which officer drafted Section B of Police Report #5657?” | Links paper to source authority over its preparation long enough to trigger evidentiary acceptance process. |
| **Establish originality/entirety** | “Do you personally certify that you drafted every paragraph in Report #5657?” | Meets rule’s acceptance criterion that a primary source knows true nature/content of file at creation time.| 
| **Tie into claim** | “When did you complete this file relative to Certifying Officer Thames’ write‑up?” | Bridges timeline gaps ensuring information at issue correlates directly with suspect’s alleged actions.|

If these are answered freely during foundational questioning *before* introduction—or via signed exhibits/ affidavits—the report becomes admissible female help vs defendant's cross‑examination powers Mapped onto opposition…

### Bottom line

> **Police reports cannot function automatically as admissible impeaching witnesses merely because a defendant acknowledges them verbally on record; they require explicit preparation/Authentication per § 901(a).**  
> This holds even though *Cumbie* acknowledged cautions against exempting such documents from baseline Rule 901 prerequisites—an approach affirmed squarely in section E of *Cutbank* where the district court rightly required separate foundations for defense-length investigational excerpts before permitting them as evidentiary folio on trial.

So your defense team should always precede narrative-impeachment questions about a third‑party report with solid identity/authentication groundwork…and—and Chief Justice Rogers could weigh pass‑ ach!
