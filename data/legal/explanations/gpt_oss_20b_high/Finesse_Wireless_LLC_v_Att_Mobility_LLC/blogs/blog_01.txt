# Expert Missteps Cost Millions  

## Why This Case Matters  

The Federal Circuit’s September 2025 decision in *Finesse Wireless LLC v.~AT&T Mobility LLC* removed a $166 million lump‑sum damage award simply because the plaintiff’s key scientific witness could not demonstrate how its patented intermodulation‑reduction technique actually operated inside a competitor’s handset. Ruling based largely on contradictions in Dr Jonathan Wells’ testimony demonstrates once again that an otherwise unassailable jury verdict can evaporate when an appellate court deems the evidentiary support “substantially lacking.” For litigators—especially those handling complex wireless technologies—the case serves as a stark reminder that winning record has meaning only if it supports claimant theory clearly enough that a reasonable jury could come away believing infringement occurred.



## A Brief Primer on Intermodulation and the Patented Method  

Both patents at issue set out methods for cancelling intermodulation products (“IMPs”) generated when a radio transmitter’s higher‑order products mix inside passive circuitry and contaminate desired receiver tones—a problem routinely addressed through techniques nicknamed passive‑improvement modulation cancellation (“PIM‑C”). The ’134 patent claimed a multi‑step procedure beginning with “over‑sampling” a received passband containing both “signals of interest” (the useful data) and “interference‐generating signals” (those capable of producing IMPs). Filters were then applied at two stages—first isolating each class—and then computed estimates of every predicted IMP were subtracted from what entered a closed‐loop phase adjustment stage designed specifically to cancel interference at sub‑sample precision.



The ’775 patent continued this approach, specifying a digital scheme that removes passive IMPs entirely within a receiver system co‑located with its transmitter. At least seven third‑order multiplications were required—performed serially using copies (“S₁–S₃”) of transmitter outputs—to cancel every passively generated intermodulation product.



Both schemes were entangled with circuitry detailed in Nokia’s design documents supplied during trial; however crucial differences emerged regarding whether those documents truly reflected hardware capable of satisfying every listed limitation.
