Chapter 1: # Parties & Pre‑Trial Context  

The litigation arose when **Finesse Wireless LLC** sued **AT&T Mobility LLC** alleging patent infringement under Title 35 U.S.C.. Finesse pursued damages for infringing use of technologies embodied in mobile‐phone radios sold through AT&T’s network infrastructure and developed largely by **Nokia**—the so‑called PIM‑cancellation feature utilized in Nokia’s handset radio designs.  

**Nokia** intervened after discovery showed it supplied much of the radio circuitry subject to attack. The venture therefore became a three‑party action with Finesse as plaintiff against AT&T and Nokia acting primarily as intervenor defending the defendants’ positions while also supporting their waivers.  

The core intellectual‑property dispute centered upon **U.S. Patent No 7,346,134** (“the ‘134” patent) and **U.S. Patent No 9,548,775** (“the ‘775” patent). Both grants pertain to methods developed expressly for reducing intermodulation product (*IMP*) interference inside radio receivers used in handheld devices:

* The ‘134’ patent discloses an approach wherein received *passband* signals are oversampled into digital form *“a bit stream”*, one set being deemed *signals of interest* while another set are identified as *interference‑generating signals*. An estimate is then computed for every offending IMP derived from those interference‑generating signals and subsequently canceled out using phase–amplitude adjustments conducted in a closed loop.
* The ‘775’ patent exposes a mathematical framework whereby three input signals (*S₁*, *S₂*, *S₃*) undergo seven third‑order multiplications —produced via non–linear operations stepped out t­o represent higher order artefacts—before various filtering stages craft IMP cancellation waves slated expressly for passive receiver components arranged adjacent to an transmitter HPA.*
