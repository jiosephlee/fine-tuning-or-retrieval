Answer:
**Rule 12(b)(6): Why a Fair‑Use allegation alone can end an infringement suit**

In *Santos v. Kimmel*, the Second Circuit upheld a motion‑to‑dismiss that relied **only** on the plaintiff’s complaint drafting an alleged “fair‑use” defense against each copyright claim.  Because rule 12(b)(6) permits courts to consider “the facts alleged in the complaint, documents attached…and documents incorporated by reference,” courts may weigh all material that would normally go into a full inquiry before deciding whether any infringement survives an affirmative defense.^[1]

### How *TCA Television Corp.* fits into this framework

In *TCA Television Corp.*, Reed carved out an exception by emphasizing how significant role Kimmel played made his reuse entirely **non‑transformative**, effectively rendering it non‑fair.:^[8]

> “…Kimmel ‘instigated…creates…the original Works’, rendering his safety designer.”[^9]

this striking assessment persuaded judges across circuits—notably New York—to refuse dismissal purely based upon supposed transformiveness when a creator instigated creation ^[10].
