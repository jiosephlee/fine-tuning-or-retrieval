Chapter 2: # Statutory Foundations for OSC Enforcement  

The Office of Special Counsel (OSC) derives both its investigative power over alleged wrongful personnel conduct and its capacity to enforce compliance with the Freedom‑of‑Information Act (FOIA) from a cluster of civil‑service statutes codified chiefly in Title VII of the United States Code (§§ 5‑U.S.C.=U.S.C.) . These provisions split into two conceptual families:  
1) **Procedural mandates** that compel discriminating agencies or officials who violate protected personnel rights or unlawfully deny records—to stop wrongdoing through *corrective actions*—and  
2) **Discretionary tools** governed by Agency Chiefs (the President’s Staffing Council [PSC]) allowing remedial approaches when formal adjudications fall short of fully policing agency conduct (**“discretionary enforcement”**).  

The following sections lay out how every relevant statute frames OSC’s duties within each family—and what designates whether an investigation proceeds automatically or only upon scope‑setting cues such as a court order.
