Answer:
**Answer**

In *America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer* the Court explains precisely why an Office of Special Counsel (OSC) investigation does **not** automatically “culminate” in an obligation for OSC—or any other agency—to bring matters before the Merit Systems Protection Board (MSPB).  

---

### 1. Where does statutory authority begin?

* **§ 5 U.S.C. § 1214** – “Corrective action” for **prohibited personnel practices**  
* **§ 5 U.S.C. § 1215** – “Disciplinary action” for officials who violate civil‑service rules  
* **§ 5 U.S.C. § 1216** – Powers specific to **FOIA‑related conduct**

Section 1216(a)(3) expressly allows OSC *to* investigate alleged arbitrary or capricious withholding of information (`...“shall … conduct an investigation…` – *Id.* § 1216(a)(3)). Part c adds one further statutorily‑driven *option*:  

> *“If satisfied… ‘OSC may investigate and seek corrective action under § 1214 and disciplinary action under § 1215 in the same way as if a prohibited personnel practice were involved.’* — *Id.* § 1216(c)

Notice two key elements:

1. **“May”** – The reliance on *discretion*, there being no statutory mandate that an IFC‑MOSP commence proceedings at will.
2. **Trigger requirement** – Section 552(a)(4)(F)(i) tells us exactly when OSC must act on FOIA violations
   (_“if …court orders production of records …gets attorneys’ fees …and questions whether …arbitrary or capricious withholding”_).

In *Greer* those three predicates were missing _in our view_, so OSC was **not required**—though fully empowered—to open an investigative file at all ([section 2]).

---

### 3. What does “…schedule responsibility towards MSMBP” mean?

The court never says”, “…an oscillator schedule responsibility….” It merely concerns two levels of causation:

1️⃣ A party’s **informational injury** arises from denial by DMV/DOJ itself, not from OS​C’s judgment (« … any informational injury … traceable to DOJ—not OSC »).  

2️⃣ A speculative causal chain – OS​C → MSBP → DOJ’s compliance – cannot satisfy Article III redressability (*see FDA v…*, *Heckler v…*) so MOSP has no remedy‐based acreage invoiced here (*Hecate Energy//*cite*).

Hence OS​C’s refusal—or delay—does nothing formal for MSBP because MSBP’s jurisdiction starts from an existing factual claim *bracketed* by §552(a),(F,i).

---

### Bottom‐line conclusion

OS​C is empowered—but not compelled—to file APPs with MSBP after finding a FOIA violation under §12216(c). A lack of triggering predicates means OS​C can stay silent without violating any statutory duty (§552(a),(F,i)). Consequently the “oscillatory stop‑investigation” observed in this case simply reflects lawful discretionary restraint rather than an indictment that MSBP duties are abandoned or omitted.\*

---

\*(Bottom line; there’s no procedural norm compelling MSBP involvement beyond what §§12214–12216 permit.)
