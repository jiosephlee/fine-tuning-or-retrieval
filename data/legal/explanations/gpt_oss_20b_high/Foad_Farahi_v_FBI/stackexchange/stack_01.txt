Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### What constitutes ‘pending’ or ‘reasonably anticipated’ enforcement proceedings under § 552(b)(7)(A)?
Question:
In *Foad Farahi v. FBI*, the court found FBI’s investigation was still ’ongoing’ more than three years after the initial request—even though there was no formal indictment against Mr. Farahi yet.

I’m unclear how courts determine whether an investigation satisfies ‘pending’ or ‘reasonably anticipated’. Do you need an indictment, trial docket, or conviction?

* How do appellate courts apply *CREW* 746 F. 3d 1082’s “ongoing criminal investigations count as ’proceedings’ ” standard?

* Does an immigration removal proceeding qualify as a forceful “enforcement proceeding”?

* What are the evidentiary requirements—based on affidavits, internal memos—or civil judgments required to establish pendency when only an affidavit supports it?

* Why did Judge Katsas rely primarily on changes in FBI’s operatives’ statements (Bender’s confirmation March 2023) rather than direct evidence contrary to earlier determinations?
Answer:
**Short answer**

For Exemption 7(A), an investigation counts as a *pending* or *reasonably anticipated* enforcement proceeding whenever it is an active law‑enforcement effort whose eventual outcome could suffer harm if its underlying evidence were disclosed—regardless of whether an indictment, trial docket, or conviction currently exists.   
The court looks at sworn agency testimony (affidavits/declarations), whatever brief update indicates that the same investigative activity continues without interruption, and whether a related civil action such as an immigration‑removal proceeding is still open.  Either type alone suffices; no formal criminal charge is required unless the agency specifically makes that connection.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. What “pending” and “reasonably anticipated” mean

* **Statutory text** – § 552(b)(7)(A) requires that disclosure “could reasonably be expected to interfere with **enforcement proceedings** that are **pending or reasonably anticipated**.”  
* **CREW precedent** – *Citizens for Resp.& Ethics v DOJ*, 746 F.3d 1082 (D.C.~Cir.~2014), held that “ongoing criminal investigations count as ’proceedings’,” and a proceeding need only exist *enough* that it could be completed later (“...the investigation continues to gather evidence...and that case would be jeopardized …”)【999】.  
* **Application in *Farahi*** – the district court adopted exactly this language:[R]=“Ongoing criminal investigations…count … [as]’proceedings.’”  
   The court therefore treated the FBI’s counter‑terrorism inquiry into Mr Farahi—not its status at trial—as sufficient proof of pending work.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. Do you need an indictment, trial docket or conviction?

No.  
The standard looks at whether there is *current investigative activity*. In *Farahi* the court underscores this point when it says,

> “Therefore…the record‑holders may show such proceedings simply by attesting to their existence.”【999】

### 3.Federal immigration‑removal Proceedings

An immigration‑removal proceeding falls squarely within § 552(b)(7)(A)’s definition of enforcement actions because it is a federal civil action intended to compel compliance with federal law (*such actions are routinely protected wherever they serve law‑enforcement ends*, see *CNSS*).  
In *Farahi* the record links DHS removal actions with FBI monitoring (“...DHS presented documents…with live testimony from an FBI agent…”【468–489】); therefore a prevailing removal order was deemed another overt council endorsed during FOIA challenge.

### 4․ Evidentiary expectations when only affidavits support pendency  

The government must satisfy FOIA’s burden de novo:\*

1\. **Affidavits admissible** under FOIA are ordinary sworn statements made by officials who supervise or possess knowledge *directly relating to* the investigation[^1].  
2\. A single statement proving continuity (“investigations remain ongoing”) suffices **if** either  
   ‑ one other document already established existence (*Hardy*) **or**  
   ‑ prior determinations were silent about termination while new events raise doubts about breakage.^[2]  
3\. When no judge‑held contradictory facts exist—particularly when all available records remain sealed—a non‑declassifying witness affirming continuity satisfies “reasonable specificity.”

### 5․ Why did Judge Katsas emphasize Bender’s March 2023 update instead of contradicting material?

Bender was appointed Acting Section Chief after Hardy left; he had full authority over RIDS records examined in both motions (**same timeframe**) so his responsibility was purely _confirmation_.  

The appellate court treats updates differently than first assertions because—

1\. Hardy's affidences already established existent ongoing work (§IV.B(2)).

“The government submitted ... Bender declaration not **to establish** pendency anew … rather simply ***to confirm nothing changed*** while its initial summary‐judgment motion was pending.”【858–861】

Because no challenge existed against Bender’s version of events—the district court granted summary judgment without prejudice after reviewing his supplemental ex‑parte declaration—the appellate court found he rightly treated Bender’s statement as a continuation rather than new evidence.

### Bottom line

Exemption 7(A)’s trigger sits on two elements:

| Element | What Courts Look For • In Farahi | Typical Evidentiary Practice |
|---------|----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Compiled for law‑enforcement purposes | The FBI itself testified via Hardy/Affidavit showing national‑security & terrorism tests | Agency declarations from persons overseeing inquiries |
| Release could interfere with **pending/**or **reasonably anticipated** proceedings | Hardys affirmed “ongoing IT investigations” ; Bender confirmed continuation | Affidavit stating continued activity + record preparation suffices; No indictments required unless expressly asserted |

Thus even more than three years later—and without any formal indictment—the court reasonably found interference risk persisted because investigators continued gathering evidence tied directly to potential future prosecutions (or an active NH immigration proceeding). Any FOIA challenger seeking disclosure around this point must show either remediation via segregation (which here was denied), or convincingly argue investigators have actually ceased activity instead of merely updated their status.*
