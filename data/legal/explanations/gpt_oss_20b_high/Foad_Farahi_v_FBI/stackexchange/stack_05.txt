Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### "Sufficient detail" required in affidavits establishing irreducible exemption
Question:
"The Court found Bender’s short statement acceptable—a simple confirmation family mentioning nothing had changed—but argued Hardys detailed submissions laid groundwork.

Hence my doubt:
❓ What threshold must authorities meet beyond confirming ongoing status? Must they delineate investigative methods,
or enumerate suspect persons involved?
⚖️ Does merely naming interrogation agencies involved meet standard mandated before overlapping exemptions arise?
📚 Devised principles from CREW indicating category formulation needed clear demarcations—how distant survive persistent category doctrines borrowers find prudent lag shift once surge demanded plush jurisdiction allegation...
Answer:
**Answer**

Under FOIA § 552(b)(7)(A), an agency’s assertion that disclosure would “reasonably interfere” with enrollment requires two elements **(i)** that the information was compiled “for law‑enforcement purposes,” and **(ii)** that its release could reasonably endanger **enforcement proceedings that are *pending* or *reasonably anticipated*** (Crew v. DOJ).  
In *Foad Farahi v. FBI* Judge Katsas acknowledged that an agency “may show such proceedings simply by attesting to their ‘existence’” (*Crew*, id., at 1099). Thus an affidavit does not have to spell out investigative methods or name every suspect involved—only provide credible evidence that an active investigation continues and would suffer interference if unredacted data were revealed (*Jud.” B*. A.*).

### What counts as “reasonable specificity?”

Judge Katsas noted two reasons why Hardy’s extensive postings were adequate:

1. The **public Hardy declaration** affirms that : “…investigations were compiled …in furtherance…pending law‑enforcement proceedings…” (*J.A.* 42).  
2. The **ex‑parte Hardy declaration** elaborates on those proceedings’ nature and scope (*J.A.* 49).

Because these documents already detailed how Farahi’s file relates to a continuing terrorism investigation, any later statement merely confirming **continuity** needed only a brief affirmation—the practice magistrate described as "**an update**," not a new claim (*J.A.* 131).

The court rejected Farahi’s contention that Bender’s terse declaration was “conclusory.” It pointed out—in response—to *“the evidence presented”*—that it was read **in concert** with Hardy’s earlier affidavits (“the Bender declaration can reasonably be understood as an update…not a standalone document”) (*IV.B*, para 6).

### Naming agencies is insufficient without context

Simply saying “the FBI’s interrogating unit” tells us which body archived data; it provides no proof that another persistent investigation exists nor explains why releasing such data could hamper it, failing how CREW requires authorities demonstrate pendency (`“a concrete prospective law‑enforcement proceeding continues to exist.”` *IV.C*, para 6). Unless coupled with supporting evidence (e.g., open‑house stories, new custodial claims), a lone nod to an agency alone will not satisfy § 552(b)(7)(A).

### Segregability—category formulation matters  

FOIA demands agencies segregate non‑exempt material where possible (*§ 552(a)(8)(A)(ii)*). In *Farahi*, judge remarked that FBI had provided a “detailed justification” explaining why exempt material
was interwoven so tightly (“no meaningful information…reasonably segregable”) (*V*, para 4). When assessing category breadth, courts refer back to *CREW*, which stresses categories “are sufficiently distinct …to allow”
a court a determination over each claim (*CREW*, id., at 1088).

In short:

| Question | How the Court answered |
|----------|------------------------|
| What surpasses mere confirmation of status?| Existing affidavits need only establish proof of continuation, not exhaustive investigative detail |
| Must investigators list methods/suspects?| No—unless needed when an isolated statement would otherwise lack proof of pending affairs |
| Does naming interrogating agencies suffice?| Only if tied inevitably behind substantive evidence proving continued work |
| How must category boundaries appear?| They must be clear enough for a court to independently decide whether parts qualify under particular exemptions |

This explains why Judge Katsas found Bender’s short statement adequate while recycling Hardy’s fuller disclosures formed full evidentiary support for Federal Bureau’s exemption claim.|
