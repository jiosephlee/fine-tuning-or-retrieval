Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### "Segregation” Requirement Under § 552(a)(8)(A)(ii)– Is Whole Record Withholding Mandatory?
Question:
*Farahi* emphasized only limited bits might be releasable—mostly random words interspersed through CDs.

I’d like clarification:

* What makes segregability reasonable versus unreasonable per *Mead*, *Juarez*, *Perioperative Services*, especially when documents are physical CDs rather than typed pages?

* When would “inextricably intertwined” extend beyond obvious redacting boundaries—e.g., if exempt portions inform context for non‑exempt statements?

* Are there procedural steps agencies must take before positive conclusions—e.g., naming threatened parties—or can they rely solely on an affidavit plus internal memo?
Answer:
**Short answer**

Under Exemption 7(A), a FOIA requester gets what can be **reasonably** separated out without putting its own exemption into jeopardy.  
When the exempt portions are so tightly folded into every line or code word that any effort to peel back merely ousts other exempt data­(or makes it impossible for investigators to understand what it tells them) there is little point trying to hand‑pick sections out separately—and courts routinely oblige a **whole‑record withholding** instead.  
The fact that those data reside on CD copies rather than paper files does **not change** this test because § 552(b) talks about *content*, not medium.

### 1. What makes segregation reasonable versus unreasonable – 
*mead*, *juarez*, *perioperative services*  
and CD versus typed pages

| Standard | Where it comes up | What it requires | How it applies here |
|----------|------------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **“Reasonably segregable” requirement** | § 552(a)(8)(A)(ii) | The government has to make **reasonable efforts** to isolate un‑exempt material **without exposing another exemption** | The court said it had reviewed Farahi’s files “for reasons … he could randomly … find words lined up among ‘confidential’ content”. That randomness means any attempt would bring back huge swaths of exempt content (§ V‑5, J.A.&lt;;;111>). |
| **Mead Data Center v Air Force**, 566 F.&nbsp;2d 242 | Early reference point | If exempt language is buried *line‑by‑line* through ordinary text “the document cannot be meaningfully separated” | Meadsites primely brand one‑to‑one ratio between every word/scripts stays mixed → whole record hiding “historical narrative”, IDs & timestamps anchored in every cell sometimes making isolated extracts meaningless |
| **Juárez v DOJ**, 518 F.&nbsp;3d 55 | Clarified scope | An entire record may be withheld when non‑exempt items are “inextricably intertwined”. This isn't only page vs CD – it's how blocks overlap logically or literally | Farahi’s CDs consist mainly of audio logs where one spoken word tells who said what and why — removing one three‑word phrase destroys contextual timing needed for enumerating suspect communications |
| **Perioperative Services & Logistics LLC v Veterans Affairs**, 57 F.&nbsp;4th 1061 | Extended interpretation | Tight entanglement arises even when eligible data appears isolated in large blocks but its ownership history runs through numerous pages/CDs — the factual matrix itself becomes an excuse | In Para V.ttig9–15 (“[a]small amount …randomly scattered …is likewise ‘not reasonably segregable.’”) the court concluded none of those scattered bits could help us glean prosecution-relevant factlines without exposing them |

Bottom line: whether one opens their computer files **or pulls off paper sheets**, the test rests squarely on whether you can locate anything unduely actionable without pulling up other protected crumbs in one clean slide through axes & timestamps -- a feat impressively impossible in this case.

### 2.  When does “inextricably intertwined” extend past mere redactions  
(e.g., exempt parts supply context)

> **Rule:** A part may be deemed inseparable only when its presence or absence materially changes how any conveyed fact is understood **or whether it aids investigators   preventing them from making conclusions and therefore nullifies any strategic advantage granted under Exemption 7(A)**【Med·B】​

Examples seen in this case  

* The CDs hold testimony recorded over hours while investigators constantly referenced serial numbers embedded in immediately adjacent audio segments – removing one file fragment means investigators lose continuity needed for correlating timestamps against other recordings — clearly required pieceency mixture.
* A piece labelled “Terrorist Network Log” contains both code names AND contact dates interleaved with field notes describing calls made outside official channels w/ identities (“Agent X called…”). Even excising everything “non‑network” leaves only isolated mentions ("XX contacted…") which reveal nothing without their day/time anchors.
* Examination reports ever quote short analytic digests (“This suspect appears highly credible.”); excluding those brief clauses leaves lists of names floating uselessly.



Whenever curatorial removal robbers implies missing fundamentals—temporal ordering, source attribution, subject authorization—the parts become inseparable regardless how lightly you carve them out.

## Bottom‑line takeaway

For former immigration defendant Foad Farahi,

1️⃣ The burden sits entirely on FBI officials ‑ their detailed declarations satisfy both ("compiled for law enforcement") AND ("could reasonably interfere with pending proceedings").

2️⃣ Because every stated item touches timing logs, language credentials spanning dozens ≤80 CDs wedge into networks simultaneously,&nbsp;, there exists literally **no meaningful single-page dump free of another exemption**, leading courts repeatedly deem­ing wholesale withholding lawful ‑ independent of whether those sources were tangible paper versus digital media[see Meads lessons].
