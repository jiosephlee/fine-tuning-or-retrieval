Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### "Pending" versus "reasonably anticipated" enforcement proceedings – timing matters?
Question:
"The Court relied on statements made over eight years ago—Hardy's declaration outlining ongoing investigations—and later reaffirmed their pendency via Bender’s brief update dated March 2023.

My confusion lies here:

✔ Is there a statute‑set cutoff time after which an investigation ceases to count as latterly "pending"?
❓ On what factual basis did the Court decide that investigative work recorded nearly over ten years prior could still interfere with future prosecutions?
💡 Are mere claims that an investigation "continued gathering evidence" sufficient farther down the timeline?

Could any decision—say CREW or Juarez—clarify when "reasonably anticipated" ends? How do courts react against stale affidavits showing little actionable activity?
Answer:
**Key take‑away**

FOIA ­Exemption 7(A) protects records whose release would “reasonably be expected to interfere with enforcement proceedings” **that are either pending or reasonably anticipated** *(U.S.C § 552(b)(7)(A))*​—and there is no statutory expiration clock on “pendency.”  
The Court ruled that an interrogation made eight years ago could still be regarded as pending **as long as the agency shows that the investigation is still active**, and a later affidavit that merely repeats “the investigation continues” is adequate only when the affidavit is furnished **in support of prior factual background** and when it confirms nothing has changed since the last update (**citations below**).

---

### 1 ‑ No statutory cutoff

FOIA does not limit Exemption 7(A) by days or months (*Exemption clause does not contain a deadline*).  
The Seventh‑Circuit simply applies its own factual standard—“there must be an existing law‑enforcement proceeding or one that is reasonably anticipated” *(see *Citizens for Responsibility & Ethics in Washington v. DOJ*, 746 F.3d 1082, at 1096)*​.  
A proceeding remains “pending” until evidence demonstrates it has ended conclusively, making time itself irrelevant except insofar as it undermines the evidentiary basis presented by the agency (*cf.* *Mapother*, 3 F.3d 1533).

---

### 2 ‑ Why records drafted ten years ago still matter  

In this case the FBI’s original declaration from Hardy (2019) already established three elements critical under Exemption 7(A):

| Element | Hardy’s declaration | Court’s reading |
|---------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Compiled for law‑enforcement purposes** | Red‑flagged alien ties linked to terrorism | Accepted unambiguously *(J.A., at 49)* |
| **Interference realistic** | Ongoing investigative work could tip off subjects | Accepted *(J.A., at 49‑50)* |
| **Pending / reasonably anticipated proceeding** | Assertion that an eventual criminal case was possible | Apparently met until confirmation required |

Because Hardy’s affidavits were corroborated later by Bender’s third‑party check-up—the Directorate contacting an agent who confirmed “continuing inquiry”—the Court treated Bender’s brief affirmation **not as fresh proof alone**, but as **an update confirming Hardy’s earlier finding** *(J.A., at 131)*​.  

The Court emphasized continuity rather than any fixed deadline (“the core inquiry “…continues”), viewing statements made years apart as *one continuous chain*, thereby safeguarding records even while they are more than a decade old *(J.A., at 138–39)*​—a conclusion entirely consonant with *Juárezv.*  

---

### 3 ‑ Are plain “ongoing” claims enough?

Under *CREW*, vague assertions about ongoing gathering can be deficient unless supported by additional details linking them to an accepted law‑enforcement program *(“vague nature” testimony isn’t enough)*​ – see supra (*CREW*, at 1098).  
In practice this means an affidavit saying only “the investigation continues” will be rejected **unless paired with corroborating facts**, such as whether new files were opened, agents retained, or communications preserved—all elements present in Hardys’ qualifiers and re‑affirmed indirectly by Bender.*

Hence fairness requires periodic updates urging confirmation—not simply repetition—so timeliness itself is judged against demonstrable activity rather than a clock limit.*

---

### 4 ‑ What do other precedent cases say about a fade­out?

| Decision | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **CREW** *(746 F.~3d 1082)* | Defines which types count as “just ‘ongoing’,” establishes review standard for plausibility |
| **Juárezv.* *(518 F.~3d 54)* | Explicitly treats ongoing criminal investigations themselves as valid enforceable proceedings regardless of charges yet filed |
| **Clementev.* *(867 F.~3d 111)* | Reinforces deference once agency credible indications exist |
| **Mead Data Cent.*, Inc.* *(566\u…)* | Extends segregation doctrine—if documentation is intertwined with exempt material you cannot cherry‑pick words alone |

None set a cut‑off period. Instead they recognize two conditions remaining constant over time:

1. The agency signals continued investigative activity (*Pearlmere*).  
2. Nothing sophisticated emerged indicating completion or dismissal (*implied through absence of indictments,* discovery filings,* see _Jerusalem_).

When those conditions hold—as shown in this case via Hardys’ original claims plus Bender’s contemporaneous confirmation—the exclusion persists regardless how many years lurk between them.*

---

## Bottom line

FOIA ­Exemption 7(A) hinges entirely on whether the release **could realistically interfere** with some law‑enforcement action that either already exists (**pending**) or will likely occur (**reasonably anticipated**). There is no statutory expiration point; instead courts upholds propriety only so long as there is credible proof—whether recent or re‑substantiated—that an investigation remains alive and capable compromising an eventual prosecution.
As long as an agency continues to confirm this status through timely updates—as it did through Hardy’s assertive outlines combined with Bender’s March‑2023 check–in—the records remain exempted even though hard truth sits ten years off back table.*
