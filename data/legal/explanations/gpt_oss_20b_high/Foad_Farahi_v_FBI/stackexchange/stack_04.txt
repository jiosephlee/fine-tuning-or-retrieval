### Why fragmented extraction does not work

The statutory duty contains two intersecting predicates:

1. **Statutory requirement:** Agencies must take *reasonable steps* to delete only exempt portions held together with non‑exempt ones (§ 552(a)(8)(A)(ii)).  
2. **Case law qualifier:** Courts have held that deletion may occur only when you can isolate some block(s) whose removal leaves meaningful — i.e., not trivial — data behind (<em>Mead Data Center</em>,<br><em>Perioperative Servs.</em>).  

When excerpts survive only because you cut out one or two words per very long sheet, those words do not convey anything useful outside their immediate context (“… tiny blips”). Consequently the Court found there was **no materially useful portion left behind**, so it approved full-withholding instead (`“no meaningful information was reasonably segregable.”` ‑ ¶ V).

The purpose behind permitting minimal excerpts is precisely when entire documents are cleanly partitioned into separate folders or files identified clearly as “non-exempt.” Cutting off lone lines scattered over hundreds of pages fails this when you cannot identify what those isolated pieces mean apart from their surrounding text—a situation explicitly called out in both precedent cases quoted above.

### Objective framework used by courts

The Court repeatedly refers back to two anchors found elsewhere it previously treated under FOIAlat precedent:

#### A. Inextricability Test  
Is there ever any combination of appositional queries you could run on this composite file that would recover all said pop-up messages without resorting back into one contiguous block we've already confirmed wholly holds purely exempt data?
- If yes → return everything wholesale (`Juarez v. DOJ`).  
- If no → proceed with finer disassembly (`Mead` / `Perioperative`).  

#### B. Category Distinction Rule  
When an agency rows up claims against files “by category,” those categories themselves have to have political borders wide enough so they equate exactly questions asked under § 552(b).  
Joining together dozens overlapping sheets into one label justifies deletion only if you show unequivocally how those sheets intersect beyond marginal coincidence (`CREW`, `Gallant`).  

In practical terms you open your bunches once colour–coded (red = definitely intercepted cases moats), then export everything grey until you find provenance privacy elements uncorrupted – otherwise just surrender them whole.^[1]

### Can plaintiffs challenge without any prior hearing?

Yes—the statutory structure gives them exactly this remedy through appellate review over Summary Judgment statements alone––anything put forward de jure during rebuttal hearings becomes part of precedent record once published (`§III` review standard). In practice governments prefer sealed affidavits because plaintiffs cannot read them prior—but once those affidavits become attached files on docket they remain part of evidence that decide upper courts precisely…

Your plan for lowering barriers might sound elegantly theoretical yet hinges ultimately on whether agents render their anti‑separation omissions transparently enough during trial phase—any opacity opens doorway anywhere submission eventually reaches appellate level (`§III` de novo benchmark).
