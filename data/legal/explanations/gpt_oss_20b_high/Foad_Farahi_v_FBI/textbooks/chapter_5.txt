# Holding & Practical Lessons  

Foia litigation often asks judges whether an agency’s refusal falls squarely within one of Congress’ defensible exemptions—and how much faith they should place in an agency’s own determination when national‑security concerns loom large. In *Farahi v.\ U.S.* the Third Circuit ultimately held that all records requested were rightfully withheld under Foia exemption § 552(b)(7)(A); it confirmed Congress’ carve‑out protecting documents tied to active law‑enforcement inquiries even when years elapse between disclosure requests and agency reviews.^*  

---

## Final ruling reaffirmed district court’s determination that complete withholding was justified  

Foia does not compel agencies merely because demanders obtain vast volumes of paper or digital media.^† Rather it requires an agency first establish three intertwined elements when invoking § 552(b)(7)(A):  

1. **“Law‑enforcement purpose.”**  
2. **“Risk that disclosure would interfere” with procedures pressed toward criminal adjudication, tying exploitation or gathering efforts together.^‡**  
3. **Full disclosure cannot comply safely because portions mingled irreversibly throughout the record.^§**

In *Farahi*, judges confirmed Confederation criteria were met despite questions raised over timing or lack detailed justification supplied initially.*
