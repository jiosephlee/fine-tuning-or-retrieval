Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### How does the FOIA exemption framework handle the interplay between Exemption 7(A) and the requirement to release ‘reasonably segregable’ portions?
Question:
The opinion notes that the government must disclose any reasonably segregable portion of a record.  But if Exemption 7(A) applies to the entire record, does that automatically preclude the release of non‑exempt material even if it is interleaved?  What are the precedents for partial disclosure when part of a record is exempt but part is not?
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
Yes.  Exemption 7(A) can shield an entire record, and when a record is exempt the agency is still required to *attempt* to separate and disclose any parts that are **reasonably segregable**.  If the exempt portions are so intertwined with non‑exempt material that the agency can’t in a practical sense separate the two, the entire record may be withheld.  The *Farahi* opinion confirms that the FBI may withhold the whole file because the exempt evidence is “inextricably intertwined” with unexempt data, and because no “meaningful” non‑exempt portions can be isolated without compromising the agency’s interests.

---

### 1.  The statutory framework

FOIA § 552(a)(8)(A)(ii) and § 552(b) require that *“any reasonably segregable portion of a record shall be provided…after deletion of the portions which are exempt.”*  In other words, the exemption does **not** grant an agency a blanket right to keep an entire record.  The agency must determine whether the exempt and non‑exempt parts can be separated.  If they cannot, the record may be withheld in its entirety.

FOIA § 552(b)(7)(A) protects records “compiled for law‑enforcement purposes” that “could reasonably be expected to interfere with enforcement proceedings.”  The court repeatedly stresses that the agency bears the burden of showing this exemption applies and that the disclosure would endanger pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement actions.

---

### 2.  *Farahi* on the interplay

The *Farahi* opinion turns directly to the segregability requirement in § V:

> “Even if a FOIA request encompasses protected records, the agency still must take ‘reasonable steps’ to ‘segregate and release nonexempt information.’”  
> …  
> “The district court explained that the FBI had provided a ‘detailed justification’ for why the protected records were **not reasonably segregable** … and found that this submission satisfied the agency’s burden.”  
> “If a small amount of non‑exempt material is randomly ‘interspersed line‑by‑line throughout the document,’ that material is not likely to be ‘reasonably segregable.’”

Thus the court reinforces the principle that **interleaving** exempt and non‑exempt text can defeat segregation.  It cites *Mead Data Center* and *Perioperative Services* to show that text that is “randomly interspersed” is not reasonably segregable.

---

### 3.  Precedent for partial disclosure

In the “interleaved” context, two cases in the same circuit are most relevant:

| Case | Holding | Key point |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Juarez v. DOJ**, 518 F.3d 54 (2008) | The agency may withhold an entire record when the exempt portion is “inextricably intertwined” with non‑exempt portions. | Exemption triggers a “no‑segregation” bar if intermingling is too tight. |
| **Mead Data Cent. v. U.S. Dept. of Air Force**, 566 F.2d 242 (1977) | The agency need not disclose non‑exempt material that is “randomly line‑by‑line interspersed.” | Lines of non‑exempt text that are spread through exempt pages cannot be segregated. |

*Creat-then*—the *Farahi* court expressly adopts *Juarez* and *Mead* in its analysis (see the discussion in Part V).  Thus, the *Farahi* opinion does not “invent” a new rule; it re‑asserts the already established doctrine that **the interplay of 7(A) and segregation is a factual question** adjudicated on the record.  When the exemption covers the whole file and a “detailed justification” shows that the exempt bits are inseparable from the rest, full withholding is permissible.

---

### 4.  Practical implications for a FOIA request

1. **Agency’s burden** – The agency must (i) show that the file is exempt and (ii) explain why any exempt portions cannot be segregated.  
2. **Plaintiff’s burden** – The requester must argue that:
   - The agency’s claim of intermixing is overly broad or incorrect; or  
   - There is a “meaningful” segment that can be isolated without violating the exemption.
3. **Court’s review** – The court looks at the evidence (affidavits, declarations, document analysis).  It will give deference to the agency’s expertise but will trigger a *strict* segregation test when the file is interleaved.

---

### 5.  Bottom line

- **Exemption 7(A) does not, by itself, bar the release of non‑exempt material.**  
- **The agency is required to segregate and release any reasonably segregable portions.**  
- **If the exempt and non‑exempt parts are so entangled that segregation is not practically achievable, the entire record may be withheld.**  
- The *Farahi* opinion exemplifies this doctrine: the FBI’s detailed declaration that its exempt records are “inextricably intertwined” with the rest satisfied the segregation requirement, allowing the court to uphold the total withholding.

**Hence, partial disclosure is permitted only where the agency can demonstrate segregation is possible; where the std test fails, the whole record can be exempted, as in *Farahi*.**
