Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer"

Chapter 1: # I. Case Background and Procedural History

## Chapter Description  

This chapter offers a concise narrative of the facts, the parties, and the procedural journey that led the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit to consider the case. It explains how America First Legal Foundation (AFL) framed its FOIA claim, the Office of Special Counsel’s (OSC) response, and the district court’s rulings before the appellate panel applied a de novo review.

## Parties & Their Roles: AFL, OSC, DOJ  

America First Legal Foundation is a conservative advocacy organization that frequently files FOIA suits against federal agencies. The foundation’s surge of interest in the case centered on a particular FOIA request for a series of emails exchanged between an outside attorney and the Department of Justice (DOJ) Civil Division.  

The Office of Special Counsel is an independent investigative arm that protects federal civil service employees from prohibited personnel practices and, per § 1216(a)(3) of the Civil Service Reform Act, may investigate alleged arbitrary or capricious withholding of information prohibited under FOIA.  

The Department of Justice, as the responding agency, denied AFL’s FOIA request on the ground that it does not have a policy of searching employees’ email accounts without consent, a policy that AFL argued was arbitrary and capricious.

## FOIA Request for DOJ Emails & DOJ’s Denial  

In late 2024, AFL filed a FOIA request under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a) for all email communications involving an outside attorney within the DOJ Civil Division. The DOJ declined to provide the records, citing an internal policy that barred employees from searching email accounts without the employee’s consent. AFL moved to compel compliance, asserting that the DOJ’s policy violated the FOIA’s statutory mandate that agencies shall not arbitrarily or capriciously withhold records.

## OSC’s Refusal to Investigate under § 1216(a)(3)  

AFL simultaneously filed a complaint seeking OSC’s intervention under 5 U.S.C. § 1216(a)(3), which requires OSC to investigate any “alleged arbitrary or capricious withholding of information prohibited under section 552.” AFL claimed that OSC’s omission to investigate was an infringement of its procedural rights and, indirectly, a denial of FOIA.

OSC responded that the statutory trigger for an investigation under § 1216(a)(3) also requires that a court have already awarded attorneys’ fees, ordered production and specifically questioned the agency’s alleged arbitrary or capricious withholding—conditions that had not yet been met. OSC thus dismissed the request for an investigation. AFL argued that § 1216(a)(3) alone mandated an investigation, regardless of the presence of a court order.

## District Court Rulings: Partial Victory, Remand, Final Dismissal  

The district court granted AFL’s motion to dismiss some claims but held that OSC’s refusal to investigate was a legal error because, per the court’s reading of the statute, § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) did not limit OSC’s investigatory authority. The court remanded the case to OSC to reconsider its decision, “under the legal error” found.

After OSC again declined to investigate, AFL filed a motion for final judgment. The district court dismissed the remaining claims for failure to state a claim.

## Appellate Review under De novo Standard  

AFL appealed, seeking de novo review of its standing and the district court’s relief order. The appellate panel, applying the standard that appellate review of a district court’s decision on the merits is "de novo" (i.e., a fresh consideration of the facts and law), set the stage for the discussion on whether AFL possessed Article III standing to litigate the matter at all. The appellate court’s holding vacated the district court’s dismissal and remanded with instructions to dismiss the case for lack of subject‑matter jurisdiction, emphasizing that AFL did not demonstrate the required “injury-in-fact” element of Article III standing.
