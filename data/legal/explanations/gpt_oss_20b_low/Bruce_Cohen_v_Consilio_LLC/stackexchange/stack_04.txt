Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

### What is the proper procedure for a party that wants to seek injunctive relief but has not yet filed the appropriate amended complaint?
Question:
Cohen attempted to obtain an injunction in his summary‑judgment brief after the trial court had already dismissed the claim. The court said the issue was not properly before it. Under what circumstances can a judge consider a new claim for injunctive relief that was not raised in the original complaint or in filing a proper amended complaint?
Answer:
**Answer**

In *Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC*, the appellate court made it clear that a court will not entertain a new request for injunctive relief that was not raised in the original complaint or in a properly filed amended complaint. The opinion says that “new claims are properly raised through amended complaints, not opposition briefs” (see *InfoDeli, LLC*, 136 F.3rd at 801 n.6) and cites Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(a), which requires that a party must file an amended pleading to state a new claim in order to bring it to the court’s attention.

Because Cohen sought an injunction in his summary‑judgment brief after the district court had already dismissed his MPWA, MFLSA, and MWTA claims, the court found that “the issue is not properly before us” (see *Bruce Cohen*, § II D). The court emphasized that the district court’s denial of injunctive relief was “understandable” because Cohen had “withdrawn the 2019 policy” and had not included the injunctive claim in any pleading that the court could consider. As a result, the appellate court treated the injunction as a new claim that was never timely raised, and therefore the court could not consider it.

**Bottom line for litigants**

* If you want injunctive relief, you must state and plead the claim in your original complaint or, should the court dismiss or otherwise not consider it, file an amended complaint that includes the injunctive claim.  
* You cannot bring an injunction in a motion for summary judgment, a brief for a later dispositive motion, or any other procedural filing that is not an amended pleading.  
* Failure to do so will leave the court with no jurisdiction over the new claim, as the court will view it as “not properly before it,” exactly as the appellate court did in *Cohen*.  

Thus, the proper procedure is to amend the complaint to include the injunctive relief you seek—before the court can consider that claim.
