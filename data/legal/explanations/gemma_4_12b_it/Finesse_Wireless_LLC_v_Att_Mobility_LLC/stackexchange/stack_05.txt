Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### Why did the court vacate the damages award instead of merely remanding it for a new trial or a different calculation?
Question:
The court reversed the JMOL of noninfringement and then vacated the damages award. Usually, if a court finds there was no infringement, the damages are naturally void. But I'm confused about the procedural step: since the court also reviewed the denial of a 'new trial on damages,' does vacating the award imply that the original calculation was flawed, or is it simply a procedural consequence of the noninfringement finding?
Answer:
The court vacated the damages award because the legal basis for those damages—a finding of infringement—was overturned. In patent litigation, damages are a secondary remedy that can only flow from a primary finding of liability (infringement).

### The Dependency of Damages on Infringement
To understand why the court vacated the award rather than remanding it for a new calculation, it is helpful to view the judgment as a two-step logical progression:
1.  **Liability:** Did the defendant infringe the patent?
2.  **Remedy:** If so, how much should the defendant pay?

If an appellate court determines that the evidence was insufficient to support a finding of infringement (which is what happens when a court reverses a denial of JMOL of noninfringement), the "Liability" step fails. Because the "Remedy" step is entirely dependent on the "Liability" step, the damages award becomes legally unsupported. There is no need to remand for a "different calculation" because there is no longer a legal basis to perform any calculation at all.

### Application to the Opinion
In *Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC*, the court conducted a de novo review of the JMOL of noninfringement. It concluded that:
*   For the **’134 patent**, the infringement verdict was not supported by substantial evidence because the plaintiff’s expert provided "self-contradictory testimony."
*   For the **’775 patent**, no reasonable jury could have found infringement because the evidence failed to show the "seven claimed multiplications" required by the patent.

By reversing the district court’s denial of JMOL of noninfringement, the Federal Circuit effectively ruled that the jury's finding of infringement was incorrect as a matter of law. The court's statement—*"Because we reverse the denial of JMOL of noninfringement... we vacate the damages award"*—indicates that the vacatur is a direct procedural consequence of the reversal of the infringement finding.

### Why Not a New Trial on Damages?
You noted that the court also reviewed the denial of a "new trial on damages." Generally, a motion for a new trial on damages is granted when the court agrees that **infringement did occur**, but the jury’s **calculation** of the money owed was flawed (e.g., the lump sum was disproportionate or the calculation method was erroneous). 

In this case, the court did not need to address whether the $166 million figure was "correct" because it determined that the defendant did not infringe in the first place. If the court had found that infringement *did* occur but the calculation was flawed, it would have granted the new trial. However, since the court found that infringement *did not* occur, the calculation became moot, necessitating a vacatur of the award rather than a remand for a recalculation.
