Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Jaison Coleman"

### What constitutes 'objective reasonableness' regarding the scope of consent in a welfare check?
Question:
The court found that the officers did not exceed the scope of Lisa's consent when they scanned the house and found Coleman. Lisa’s consent was specifically linked to 'making sure' the kids were okay. Does the 'objective reasonableness' standard imply that a 'welfare check' inherently includes a search of the entire premises, or are there specific boundaries to how far an officer can roam once they've been let in for a specific purpose?
Answer:
In *United States v. Jaison Coleman*, the court clarifies that the "objective reasonableness" standard does not grant officers an inherent right to search an entire premises during a welfare check. Instead, the scope of consent is defined by what a "typical reasonable person" would understand the consent to encompass based on the specific dialogue exchanged between the officer and the person providing that consent.

Under this standard, the boundaries of a search are dictated by the specific purpose of the entry. In this case, the consent was specifically linked to the officers "making sure" the children were okay. The court determined that the officers did not exceed this scope because their subsequent actions—scanning the home, locating everyone, and asking basic questions—were "necessarily" required to achieve that stated purpose. Specifically, to verify the children's welfare and confirm that Coleman "presented no danger to them or anyone else," the officers had to be able to see where the children were and identify the location of the adults in the home.

Therefore, the "objective reasonableness" standard implies that while there are specific boundaries to how far an officer can roam, those boundaries are flexible and determined by the goals of the specific encounter. If a task (such as scanning the house) is a necessary component of the purpose for which the consent was granted (a welfare check), that task falls within the scope of the consent. The scope is not a blanket authorization for a full forensic search; rather, it is a functional scope tied to the objectives communicated during the initial interaction.
