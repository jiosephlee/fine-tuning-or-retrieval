Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

### Why was the plaintiff's request for injunctive relief rejected despite being raised in his summary judgment brief?
Question:
I'm confused about the court's reasoning in Section II(D). Cohen actually asked for an 'affirmative injunctive order' in his summary judgment brief. Why couldn't the court just consider that request? I thought the purpose of a summary judgment brief was to argue the merits of the claims, but the court says it wasn't 'properly' raised. Does this mean he should have filed an amended complaint specifically for the injunction?
Answer:
The court rejected the plaintiff’s request for an "affirmative injunctive order" because he attempted to seek a different form of relief in his summary judgment brief than what was originally requested in his complaint.

The core legal issue here involves the scope of a summary judgment brief versus the scope of the pleadings. While you are correct that the purpose of a summary judgment brief is to argue the merits of the claims, those merits must be rooted in the claims as defined by the initial complaint. 

In Section II(D), the court identifies a discrepancy in Cohen's filings:

1.  **The Complaint:** Cohen originally sought "certain specific forms of injunctive relief."
2.  **The Summary Judgment Brief:** Cohen "changed course" and requested an "affirmative injunctive order that Consilio rescind its illegal overtime policy."

The court determined that because Cohen changed the nature of the relief he was seeking, his request in the brief constituted a "new claim." The court relied on *InfoDeli, LLC* to establish the rule that "new claims are properly raised through amended complaints, not opposition briefs." 

Essentially, a party cannot use a summary judgment brief to "amend" the lawsuit or introduce a new theory of relief that was not part of the original pleading. Because the district court did not consider the new request, the Court of Appeals ruled that the issue was not "properly" before them for review.

To answer your specific question: Yes, your intuition was correct. Because Cohen wanted to seek a different type of injunction than the one he originally pleaded, he should have filed an amended complaint pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(a) to properly bring that new request before the court.
