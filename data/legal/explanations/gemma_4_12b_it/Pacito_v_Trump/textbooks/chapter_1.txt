Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

Chapter 1: # Case Background and Procedural History

## Executive Order 14163: Suspension of the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP)

The litigation stems from a series of executive actions taken by the President in early 2025. On January 20, 2025, President Trump issued Executive Order (EO) 14163, titled “Realigning the United States Refugee Admissions Program.” Under the authority of 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f), the President determined that allowing refugees to enter the United States under the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) would be "detrimental to the interests of the United States." Consequently, the Order directed a suspension of refugee entry pending further findings. 

## Executive Order 14169: Suspension of USRAP funding

Following the suspension of entry, the government took further action regarding the logistics of the program. On January 24, 2025, the Department of State suspended all funding for the USRAP. This action was purportedly taken under Executive Order 14169, "Reevaluating and Realigning United States Foreign Aid." This funding suspension was significant because it included the money used for domestic resettlement services—the logistical support required to help refugees integrate into American communities after they arrive in the country.

## The Plaintiffs: Refugees and non-profit resettlement organizations

The plaintiffs in this case represent two distinct groups affected by these executive orders. The first group consists of individual refugees who had either already been admitted to the United States or had been approved for resettlement but were still outside the country awaiting arrival. The second group consists of three non-profit organizations. These organizations held cooperative agreements with the State Department to provide overseas processing for refugee applications and to provide resettlement services for those already admitted to the U.S. Because the executive orders halted funding and suspended the program, both the individuals and the organizations faced significant disruptions to their status and operations.

## Legal Claims: Violations of the Refugee Act of 1980 and the Administrative Procedure Act (APA)

The plaintiffs filed suit in the Western District of Washington in February 2025, seeking to block the executive orders. Their legal challenge rests on two primary pillars. First, they allege that the suspension of the refugee program under EO 14163 violates the Refugee Act of 1980, which serves as the general rule for the treatment of refugees and displaced persons. Second, they argue that the defunding of the USRAP under EO 14169 violates the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), which governs how federal agencies may issue regulations and allocate resources.

## District Court Preliminary Injunctions: The initial prohibition of the Executive Orders

The district court acted quickly, issuing a series of preliminary injunctions to halt the government's actions. On February 25, 2025, the court issued an oral preliminary injunction prohibiting the enforcement of the primary sections of EO 14163. In response, the government moved to terminate every cooperative agreement for refugee reception and placement services in the United States. 

The district court followed this with a written order on February 28, 2025, which expanded the scope of the injunction to specifically prohibit the suspension of USRAP funding. On March 24, 2025, the court issued a second preliminary injunction ordering the State Department to reinstate all the cooperative agreements it had terminated following the initial February ruling.

## The Appeals Process: Government motions for stays and the Ninth Circuit's initial stay orders

The government appealed these injunctions, leading to a complex series of "stays"—temporary freezes on the lower court's orders—within the Ninth Circuit. On March 25, 2025, a motions panel of the Ninth Circuit granted a partial stay of the February 28 order. The court noted that the President's authority under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f) "exudes deference," yet the court refused to stay the injunction for individuals who had been conditionally approved for refugee status before January 20, 2025. This created a "limited carveout" where some refugees were still protected by the injunction while others were not.

The procedural history grew more complex in July 2025. After the district court attempted to establish a framework to manage this carveout, the government filed an emergency motion. On July 18, 2025, the Ninth Circuit issued an administrative stay of that framework. The court then scheduled expedited oral argument for September 3, 2025, resulting in the current order, which vacates the previous stays and issues a new stay pending a final decision on the merits.
