Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### How did strict-compliance doctrine apply to the sequence problem: cancellation before later-added signature stamp?
Question:
Plaintiffs argued the certification lacked a signature when the policy auto-cancelled, and only later did an APAC employee add a signature stamp to printed documents. Given Louisiana’s strict-compliance rule, does the timing mismatch matter if the statute does not require a signature at all? More broadly, under Louisiana cancellation cases, do later-curative steps (like post-cancellation stamping) ever validate an earlier defective cancellation, or is compliance measured strictly at the moment the insurer acts on the cancellation request?
Answer:
Short answer
- The timing mismatch here (signature stamp added after the policy already auto-cancelled) did not invalidate the cancellation because Louisiana’s statute does not require a signature on the finance company’s certification. Williams v. GoAuto, 5th Cir. No. 24‑30646 (Oct. 3, 2025).
- More generally, Louisiana measures compliance against the statutory requirements at the time the insurer receives the notice/certification. If the statutory prerequisites are met at that moment, later “curative” steps are unnecessary; if they are not met, courts have treated the cancellation as ineffective (because Louisiana requires strict adherence), but courts will not add requirements (like a signature) that the statute does not contain.

Why — doctrine and how the opinion applies it
1) Signature requirement: the statute requires “a statement certifying” certain facts, but it does not mention a signature. The Fifth Circuit held that courts should not import extra-formalities into the statute: “Strict adherence … is not an invitation for courts to add to the procedures.” The panel therefore concluded the statute does not require a signed or individually identified signer on the finance company’s certificate. Williams, slip op. § I.

2) Moment of compliance — “receipt”: the statute conditions the insurer’s ability to cancel on “[u]pon receipt of such notice of cancellation and statement from the premium finance company.” The court accepted common-law dictionary meanings — physical or electronic possession/delivery — and held that receipt can occur when the insurer’s systems take possession (here, GoAuto’s web service receiving APAC’s automated e-mail), even if no human acts on it until later. Because GoAuto’s computer received the email and automatically cancelled the policy, the statutory “receipt” occurred before the later-applied signature stamp. Williams, slip op. § I.

3) Strict-compliance principle and later-curative acts: Louisiana case law requires strict adherence and treats defects in the prescribed process as rendering a cancellation ineffective (citing Delatte, Eaglin, Britten). But the Fifth Circuit emphasized that “strict adherence” means following the statute’s actual requirements; courts should not create additional ones (e.g., a signature) where the statute is silent. Thus:
- If the statutory elements are present at the time the insurer receives the notice, the cancellation is valid even if some ministerial act (like a stamp applied later) occurs afterward.
- If a required statutory element is missing at the time of receipt, later after‑the‑fact steps cannot supply a requirement the statute mandated in the first instance; under Louisiana precedent, a defective process can lead to an ineffective cancellation.

Practical rule distilled from Williams
- Compliance is measured against the statute’s listed requirements at the moment of “receipt” by the insurer (which can be electronic receipt by the insurer’s systems).
- Courts will enforce strict compliance with the statute’s actual text but will not impose extra-textual form requirements such as a signature unless the statute requires it.
- Post‑cancellation “cures” cannot retroactively satisfy a statutory requirement that was absent at the time of receipt; conversely, if the statutory requirements were already satisfied upon receipt, later clerical steps are immaterial.

Application to your hypo
- Because § 9:3550(G)(3)(a) doesn’t require a signature and GoAuto’s system received APAC’s emailed certification before cancelling, the later-added signature stamp did not matter in Williams. If a case presented a missing statutory element (e.g., no certification at all, or failure to send required notices to third parties), a subsequent stamp or signature would not save the cancellation under Louisiana’s strict-adherence precedent.
