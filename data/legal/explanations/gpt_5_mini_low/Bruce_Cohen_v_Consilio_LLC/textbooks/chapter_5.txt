Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

Chapter 5: # MWTA Claim and Injunctive Relief: Preservation and Statutory Limits

This chapter explains the Eighth Circuit’s treatment of Cohen’s Minnesota Wage Theft Act (MWTA) claim and his request for injunctive relief. The court affirmed the district court’s dismissal of the MWTA claim and declined to consider Cohen’s newly framed request for an affirmative injunction rescinding Consilio’s 2019 overtime policy. Understanding these rulings requires attention to (1) the statutory theory Cohen advanced under the MWTA, (2) the factual stipulation that he had received all money he claimed to be owed, (3) the absence of legal authority permitting the civil penalties he sought under the MWTA in the posture of this case, and (4) basic preservation principles governing appellate review of undeveloped arguments and new claims.

MWTA theory: failure to provide required notice of overtime policy change (§ 181.032(f), (d)(4))
Cohen’s MWTA claim rested on the allegation that Consilio failed to give statutorily required notice of its change in overtime policy—an asserted violation of Minn. Stat. Ann. § 181.032(f) and subsection (d)(4). The MWTA provision allegedly implicated requires employers to provide certain notices when employment terms change. Cohen’s legal theory was that lack of that notice triggered statutory penalties against Consilio.

Stipulation: all owed pay received; absence of cited authority for civil penalties under MWTA in this posture
A key factual and procedural backdrop is the parties’ stipulation that Cohen “has been paid for all overtime wages he alleges he is owed in this lawsuit,” and that he does not dispute the liquidated damages he received under the MFLSA. Because Cohen had been paid for the monetary relief he initially sought, the remaining MWTA claim turned on whether the statute independently authorized civil penalties or other relief in these circumstances. On appeal, Cohen did not point to statutory authority that would support civil penalties available to him under the MWTA given the stipulated payments. The panel therefore had no meaningful legal basis before it to reverse the dismissal on the MWTA penalty claim.

Waiver on appeal for undeveloped arguments (Milligan)
The court disposed of Cohen’s MWTA argument in part on preservation grounds. Citing Milligan v. City of Red Oak, the Eighth Circuit reiterated that an appellant who presses an issue without developing argument or citation of authority on appeal effectively waives the point. Cohen’s brief offered little substantive legal argument or statutory interpretation to show that the MWTA conferred the penalties he sought in this posture. Because he failed to support the appellate contention with meaningful argument or legal authority, the court declined to disturb the district court’s dismissal.

Injunctive relief: complaint sought specific relief; later request to rescind 2019 policy raised only in summary judgment briefing
Cohen also sought injunctive relief, but the form of relief he asked for shifted during the case. His complaint requested particular injunctive remedies, and only later—during briefing on summary judgment—did he pivot and expressly ask the district court to order Consilio to rescind its 2019 overtime policy. The district court understandably did not address this new formulation because it was not pleaded as a claim for relief.

Procedural rule: new claims or materially different injunctive theories must be raised by amended complaint (InfoDeli; Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a))
The court relied on basic pleading and amendment rules in rejecting Cohen’s attempt to obtain a materially different injunction without an amended complaint. The opinion cites InfoDeli, LLC for the proposition that new claims should be raised through an amended complaint, not by a party’s brief in opposition to summary judgment. This is consistent with Rule 15(a) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, which governs amendment of pleadings and protects the defendant’s right to know and respond to the precise relief sought. Because Cohen sought a different form of injunctive relief only in his summary-judgment papers and did not amend his complaint, the issue was not properly before the district court or the appellate court.

Holding: district court’s dismissal of MWTA and refusal to consider new injunctive request affirmed
The net result is straightforward. Given the parties’ stipulation that Cohen had been paid all wages and liquidated damages he claimed, the absence of developed appellate argument or statutory authority authorizing the MWTA penalties he sought, and the failure to plead an amended request for rescission of the policy, the Eighth Circuit affirmed the district court’s dismissal of Cohen’s MWTA claim and refused to consider his newly asserted injunctive remedy. The opinion emphasizes preservation: undeveloped appellate arguments are waived, and new claims or materially different theories of relief must be presented through proper pleading, not briefed for the first time at summary judgment.
