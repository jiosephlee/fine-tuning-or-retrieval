Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Can a § 3553(a) 'not greater than necessary' argument overcome a within-Guidelines sentence when ACCA mandates a high minimum?
Question:
Cutbank argued that applying the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) enhancement resulted in a sentence conflicting with 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a) because it was 'greater than necessary,' even though it fell within the Guidelines range. The court affirmed simply because the sentence was within-range and the judge considered the factors. Since the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) already forces a 15-year minimum based on his priors, isn't almost any within-Guidelines sentence going to be harsh? What specific factual showing does a defendant need to make to convince an appellate court that a within-Guidelines sentence is substantively unreasonable under § 3553(a), especially when statutory mandatory minimums are driving the baseline?
Answer:
Based on *United States v. Cutbank*, a defendant faces a steep burden in challenging a within-Guidelines sentence as substantively unreasonable under § 3553(a), even when statutory mandatory minimums like the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) drive the baseline. The Eighth Circuit's affirmation here illustrates that general arguments about severity are insufficient; the appellant must demonstrate that the district court failed to meaningfully consider the specific circumstances of the case or that the sentence is disproportionate to the individual defendant's conduct and history.

**The Presumption of Reasonableness and Procedural Sufficiency**
The controlling standard articulated in *Cutbank* is that a sentence falling within a properly calculated Guidelines range is presumed reasonable on appeal. To overcome this presumption, a defendant cannot simply argue that the resulting term is "harsh" due to the interplay between sentencing enhancements and statutory minima. Instead, the appellate review focuses on whether the district court committed procedural error by failing to consider the § 3553(a) factors.

In *Cutbank*, the defendant argued that applying the four-level enhancement under USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) created a sentence "greater than necessary." The Eighth Circuit rejected this because the record showed the district court had reviewed the Presentence Investigation Report (PSR), considered sentencing memoranda, and explicitly reviewed § 3553(a) factors to determine if a variance was warranted. The court held that it does not require a "mechanical recitation" of every factor; rather, it must simply be clear from the record that the judge "actually considered" them. Because the judge acknowledged Cutbank's arguments regarding necessity and still imposed a within-range sentence, the appellate court presumes those arguments were considered and rejected.

**The Necessity of Specific Factual Showings**
To succeed on a substantive reasonableness challenge in this context, a defendant must do more than point to the high floor established by ACCA. As demonstrated in *Cutbank*, the appellate court looks to the **specific facts of the instant offense and the defendant's history** to validate the severity of the sentence.

The court affirmed the 292-month sentence not merely because it was within guidelines, but because the specific aggravating facts rendered such a term proportionate under § 3553(a). The opinion highlights:
*   **Nature of the Offense:** The crime involved domestic abuse, kidnapping, holding a victim at gunpoint in front of her children, and assaulting police officers during flight.
*   **Criminal History:** The defendant had nearly a dozen prior felony convictions, many involving violence and firearms.

The court concluded that given these specific facts, imposing a within-range term was not an abuse of discretion. Therefore, to convince an appellate court that a within-Guidelines sentence is unreasonable despite ACCA mandates, a defendant must show that their specific conduct or history presents "mitigating circumstances so unusual" that they render even the low end of an ACCA-driven Guidelines range excessive. Generalized claims that the statutory framework forces harsh outcomes are inadequate where, as in *Cutbank*, the underlying conduct independently justifies severe punishment under the § 3553(a) analysis.
