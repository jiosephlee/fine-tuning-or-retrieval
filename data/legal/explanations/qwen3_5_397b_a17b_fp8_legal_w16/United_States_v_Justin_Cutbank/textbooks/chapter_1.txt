Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

Chapter 1: # Case Context, Factual Background, and Procedural Posture

Understanding a judicial opinion begins with mastering the underlying facts and the procedural path the case traveled to reach the appellate court. In *United States v. Cutbank*, the Eighth Circuit reviews a conviction and sentence stemming from a volatile domestic incident that escalated into a violent confrontation with law enforcement. To grasp the court's reasoning, one must first appreciate the severity of the defendant's conduct, the physical evidence linking him to the crime, and the specific statutory mandates that governed his prosecution. This chapter outlines the factual narrative, the recovery of critical evidence, the statutory framework of the charges, and the procedural history that frames the issues on appeal. By establishing these foundations, we can better understand why the appellate court ultimately affirmed the district court's decisions regarding both the trial process and the sentencing calculation.

## Factual Narrative: The Domestic Incident and Subsequent Standoff

The events giving rise to this prosecution occurred during the night of November 19–20, 2020. The defendant, Justin Cutbank, having consumed alcohol and methamphetamine, engaged in a terrifying episode of domestic violence against his girlfriend, identified in the record as D.F. Cutbank awakened D.F., pointed a firearm directly at her face, and forced her into a closet. While holding her at gunpoint for what she testified felt like hours, he made paranoid delusions about "listening devices" in their home and threats regarding people waiting outside to harm them. During this ordeal, Cutbank threatened D.F.'s life and physically struck her across the face with the gun. His control over the situation extended beyond D.F.; he eventually ordered her to wake others sleeping in the house, including her two teenage sons and a housemate, C.F. The incident concluded only when Cutbank fled the residence, taking D.F.'s cell phone and the firearm with him.

Following his escape from D.F.'s home, Cutbank's behavior remained erratic and dangerous. Police received a separate call reporting a man refusing to leave a garage located approximately half a mile from D.F.'s residence. Upon arrival, officers found Cutbank barricaded inside. Over several hours, he made incoherent statements and claimed to be armed. When officers finally attempted to detain him, Cutbank resisted violently, biting an officer in the face and neck and attempting to grab the officer's service weapon. This sequence of events—from the initial hostage situation in the home to the armed standoff and physical assault on police—provides the contextual backdrop for the charges and underscores the gravity of the conduct considered by both the jury and the sentencing judge.
