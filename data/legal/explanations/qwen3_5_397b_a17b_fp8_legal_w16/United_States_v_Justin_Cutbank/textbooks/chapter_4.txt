Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

Chapter 4: # Sentencing Framework: ACCA, Guidelines Calculations, and Section 3553(a) Reasonableness

In *United States v. Justin Cutbank*, the Eighth Circuit affirms a significant prison term imposed under the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA), offering a practical illustration of how mandatory statutory minimums interact with the advisory Sentencing Guidelines. The appellant, convicted of being a felon in possession of a firearm, faced a complex sentencing landscape where a jury finding triggered a fifteen-year statutory floor, while specific conduct led to further upward adjustments under the Guidelines. This chapter dissects the court's reasoning regarding the calculation of the advisory range, the application of the "connection with another felony" enhancement, and the rigorous standard required to challenge a sentence as substantively unreasonable on appeal. By examining *Cutbank*, students learn that even when a defendant argues a sentence violates the core principles of 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a), appellate courts afford district judges broad discretion provided the procedural steps of calculation and consideration are followed.

## The Interplay Between ACCA Mandatory Minimums and Advisory Guidelines Ranges

The sentencing framework in *Cutbank* begins with the intersection of statute and guideline. The jury found that Cutbank had three or more prior violent felony convictions, which automatically triggered the enhanced penalty provisions of 18 U.S.C. § 924(e), known as the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA). Under ACCA, a defendant faces a mandatory minimum sentence of fifteen years (180 months) imprisonment. However, the district court does not stop at the statutory minimum; it must also calculate the advisory sentencing range under the United States Sentencing Guidelines (USSG).

In this case, the Presentence Investigation Report (PSR) calculated a base offense level of 26, reflecting the nature of the firearm (a sawed-off rifle) and Cutbank's criminal history. Through various enhancements—including those for an altered serial number, reckless creation of risk during flight, and possession in connection with another felony—the total offense level reached 35. Combined with a Criminal History Category of VI, this yielded an advisory guidelines range of 292 to 365 months. The opinion highlights a critical mechanism in federal sentencing: when the ACCA statutory minimum interacts with the Guidelines, the court applies whichever results in the greater offense level or longer sentence. As noted in USSG § 4B1.4(b), if the ACCA provision yields a higher range than the standard career offender guidelines, the higher range controls. Here, although Cutbank argued that applying certain enhancements conflicted with sentencing principles because they pushed the sentence well above the ACCA floor, the court clarified that the Guidelines operate independently to determine the advisory range, which then serves as the starting point for judicial discretion.

## Analyzing USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B): Possession 'In Connection With' Another Felony

A central issue on appeal was the application of USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B), which mandates a four-level increase if a defendant used or possessed a firearm "in connection with another felony offense." Cutbank contended that applying this enhancement resulted in a sentence that was "greater than necessary" under § 3553(a). To understand the court's rejection of this argument, one must look atthe underlying facts established in the PSR and adopted by the district court. The "other felony" in question was not a separate conviction obtained in a different trial, but rather the criminal conduct inherent in the night of November 19-20, 2020.

The facts supporting this enhancement were stark: Cutbank pointed a gun at his girlfriend's face, held her hostage in a closet for hours, threatened her life, and struck her with the weapon. He subsequently fled while armed, assaulting police officers and biting one in an attempt to seize their firearm. These actions constituted distinct felony offenses under state law, such as kidnapping, assault, and resisting arrest with violence. The Guidelines commentary explicitly states that this enhancement applies regardless of whether charges were brought or convictions obtained for these collateral felonies. The Eighth Circuit reasoned that because the PSR detailed these events and Cutbank raised no objection to those factual findings at sentencing, the district court did not clearly err in concluding that his possession of Marlin .22 caliber rifle was integrally linked to these violent acts. The enhancement was not punitive double-counting but a reflection ofthe heightened danger posed when firearms are utilized during independent criminal episodes.
