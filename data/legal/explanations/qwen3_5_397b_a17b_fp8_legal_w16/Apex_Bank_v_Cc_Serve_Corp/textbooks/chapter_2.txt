Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

Chapter 2: # Analysis of Related Goods and Services (DuPont Factor Two)

In *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the Federal Circuit addressed a critical threshold issue in trademark opposition proceedings: whether the services offered by the applicant and the registrant are sufficiently related to support a finding of likelihood of confusion. Under the second factor of the *DuPont* analysis, the court examines the similarity of the parties' goods or services. This factor does not require that the services be identical; rather, it asks whether they are related in such a manner, or marketed under such circumstances, that consumers might mistakenly believe they emanate from the same source. In this case, the court affirmed the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board's conclusion that CC Serve's "credit card services" and Apex Bank's proposed "banking and financing services" were highly similar, weighing heavily in favor of finding a likelihood of confusion. Understanding this portion of the opinion requires dissecting how the court interprets service descriptions, evaluates evidentiary support for relatedness, and rejects arguments based on strict competition.

## Defining the Scope of Registered Services: Interpreting 'Credit Card Services'

The analysis began with a necessary interpretive step regarding the scope of CC Serve's existing registration. CC Serve held a registration for the mark ASPIRE covering "credit card services," issued in 1998. However, during the pendency of Apex's application, the specific entry for "credit card services" had been deleted from the USPTO's Trademark ID Manual. Consequently, the Board could not rely on a standardized manual definition to determine the breadth of CC Serve's rights. Instead, it had to construe the ordinary meaning of the phrase as it existed at the time of registration and as understood in commerce.

The Board determined that "credit card services" is a broad term encompassing more than just the physical issuance of a plastic card. It includes counseling regarding credit card debt, processing payments and transactions, authorizing charges, monitoring accounts, sending alerts, managing accounts, and providing access to credit scores. This expansive definition was crucial because Apex Bank argued it did not currently offer credit cards. However, Apex sought to register its ASPIRE BANK marks for "banking and financing services." By defining CC Serve's services broadly to include account management and financial transaction processing, the Board established a significant overlap with the traditional functions of banking institutions. The Federal Circuit upheld this interpretation, noting that substantial evidence supported the Board's construction of the registered services.

## The Legal Standard for Relatedness: Common Source Mistaken Belief

The core legal principle guiding Factor Two is that confusion arises not only from direct competition but from relatedness that suggests a common origin. As cited in *Apex Bank*, quoting *Coach Services Inc. v. Triumph Learning LLC*, services are considered related if "the respective products are related in some manner and/or [if] the circumstances surrounding their marketing are such that they could give rise to the mistaken belief that they emanate from the same source." This standard protects consumers who might reasonably assume that a company offering one type of financial service would naturally expand into another under the same brand name.

In applying this standard, the court looked beyond whether Apex and CC Serve were currently selling competing products side-by-side. Instead, it focused on whether a consumer encountering an "ASPIRE BANK" for banking services would logically assume it was connected to the existing "ASPIRE" credit card services. Given that banks frequently issue credit cards and manage credit accounts, and that non-bank entities often partner with banks to provide these very services (as CC Serve did), the commercial reality suggested a strong nexus between the two fields. The court reinforced that identity is not required; a high degree of similarity where consumers expect single-source provision is sufficient to satisfy this factor.

## Evidence of Overlap: Dictionary Definitions and Third-Party Registrations

To substantiate its finding that banking/financing services and credit card services are legally identical in part, both the Board and appellate court relied on objective evidence found within record citations J.A. 27–28 and J.A. 33–34 first through dictionary definitions then third-party trademark registrations then . The Board examined standard dictionary definitions for "bank," "banking," and "finance." These definitions revealed that extending credit and providing funds—often achieved through credit cards—are inherent components of banking activities therefore establishing conceptual overlap between Apex's proposed offerings . Furthermore , look at market behavior proved instructive ; numerous third - party registrations demonstrated single entities holding trademarks covering both credit card operations alongside general banking functions . Such registrations serve as probative evidence indicating industry practice recognizes these sectors as complementary rather than distinct silos thereby supporting inference consumers perceive potential unity source when encountering similar marks across these domains . This combination linguistic analysis empirical marketplace data provided robust foundation concluding second factor favored opposer significantly .
