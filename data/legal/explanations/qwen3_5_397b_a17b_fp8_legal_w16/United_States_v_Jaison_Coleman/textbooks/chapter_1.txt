Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Jaison Coleman"

Chapter 1: # Factual Background and Procedural Posture

Understanding a judicial opinion begins with a firm grasp of the facts and the procedural journey the case has taken to reach the appellate court. In *United States v. Coleman*, the Seventh Circuit reviews a conviction stemming from a tense domestic disturbance call in Marshfield, Wisconsin. The central legal conflict arises not from whether the defendant possessed a firearm—he admitted to doing so—but from whether the police officers violated the Fourth Amendment when they entered his home without a warrant. Because the resolution of this case hinges entirely on the validity of consent given by the defendant's wife, Lisa Coleman, the specific sequence of events at the doorstep is critical. The court's analysis relies heavily on objective evidence provided by body camera footage, which eliminates factual disputes regarding the tone, timing, and content of the interaction between Officer Jamie Kizer and Lisa. This chapter details those events and traces the case from the initial police response through the district court's denial of the motion to suppress, culminating in Coleman's conditional guilty plea and subsequent appeal.

## The Initial 911 Call and Arrival at the Scene

The narrative begins just after 4:00 a.m. on April 20, 2023, when Officer Jamie Kizer responded to a 911 call reporting domestic violence. The caller alleged that Jaison Coleman had threatened to kill his wife, Lisa, and was still inside their residence. Upon arrival, Officer Kizer knocked on the door and announced his presence. Lisa answered by stepping onto the porch, immediately informing the officer that her son and daughter were inside the home. However, her demeanor suggested distress; when asked if her children were safe, she hesitated, stating only that they were in her bedroom. When Officer Kizer pressed further, asking if the kids were going to be okay, Lisa wavered again, replying that she "hope[d] so." This initial exchange established an atmosphere of uncertainty regarding the safety of the occupants inside, setting the stage for the officer's subsequent actions. The factual record, derived primarily from body camera footage as noted by the court citing *United States v. Norville*, provides an undisputed account of these early moments, showing an officer attempting to assess a volatile situation while dealing with a hesitant resident.

## The Dialogue at the Doorstep: Hesitation, Denial, and Ultimate Consent

The crux of the Fourth Amendment issue lies in the evolving dialogue between Officer Kizer and Lisa regarding entry into the home. Initially, when Officer Kizer asked where her husband was located and subsequently requested permission to enter, Lisa explicitly refused. She paused before stating, "um … I'd prefer you not." Following this denial, a break in conversation occurred as Lisa went back inside ostensibly to retrieve her shoes. Concern grew when she did not return promptly; Officer Kizer cracked the door to check on her welfare before she reappeared outside. Once she returned to the doorway, the officer renewed his inquiries about her husband's status and temperament. It was during this second attempt to gain entry that the pivotal exchange occurred. When asked if her husband was calmer and if the children were okay, Lisa gave an ambiguous response about their current status. Officer Kizer then stated, "Okay, um, I'm sensing that we almost need to come inside to make sure of that." To this statement of perceived necessity based on child welfare concerns, Lisa replied simply, "Okay." She then provided logistical details about where the children were sleeping and assured the officers that while her dogs might jump on them, they would not bite. Finally, she physically opened the door and facilitated entry by moving her dogs aside. This progression—from an initial refusal to an eventual affirmative agreement accompanied by cooperative action—formsthe basis of the government's argument that valid consent was obtained.
