Title: Blog about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

# Procedural Limbo: The Practical Consequences of a Partial Stay Pending Merits Decision

On September 12, 2025, the Ninth Circuit issued an order in *Pacito v. Trump* that attempts to thread a needle so fine it may well snap under the weight of its own contradictions. In a procedural maneuver that halts the enforcement of President Trump's suspension of the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) while simultaneously commanding the executive branch to resume funding for refugee resettlement services, the panel has created a unique and potentially unworkable interim regime. By staying the district court's injunction regarding future admissions but expressly directing the government to reinstate cooperative agreements for those already admitted or approved, the court has separated the pipe from the faucet. The result is a logistical paradox: the administration is ordered to maintain the infrastructure for receiving refugees even as it retains the authority to stop them from arriving. This order does more than manage the status quo pending a merits decision; it exposes the friction between broad presidential powers over entry and specific statutory mandates regarding domestic care, leaving agencies in a state of operational limbo.

The core of the Ninth Circuit's reasoning rests on a sharp distinction between two separate legal questions: the President's authority to suspend entry under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f) and the Executive Branch's obligation to fund resettlement services under 8 U.S.C. § 1522. Relying heavily on *Trump v. Hawaii*, the panel conceded that the government is likely to succeed on the merits regarding the suspension of entry. The court acknowledged that § 1182(f) "exudes deference" and that the President's finding that refugee entry would be detrimental to national interests satisfies the statute's sole prerequisite. Consequently, the district court's preliminary injunction blocking Executive Order No. 14163 was stayed in its entirety concerning future admissions. However, the panel drew a bright line at the defunding of domestic services. Citing the mandatory language ("shall") found in various subsections of § 1522, particularly those governing unaccompanied minors and cash assistance, the court concluded the government is unlikely to prevail on its argument that these provisions are merely permissive authorizations. Because Congress has appropriated funds and imposed specific duties on the Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement, the panel held that terminating cooperative agreements violates the Administrative Procedure Act.

This bifurcated approach creates an immediate administrative nightmare for the State Department and Health and Human Services (HHS). The order directs these agencies to reinstate cooperative agreements with nonprofit organizations that were terminated months ago, many of which have since dissolved their specialized staffing or reallocated resources. Yet, these same agencies are operating under a valid stay that permits them to halt new refugee arrivals indefinitely. The practical consequence is a mandate to keep reception centers open, caseworkers employed, and housing reserves ready for a population that may never arrive if the suspension holds on appeal. While Judge Lee's dissent correctly identifies this tension by arguing that § 1522 is purely an authorization contingent on executive discretion, the majority's reading forces agencies into a costly holding pattern. They must spend appropriated funds to maintain capacity without any certainty of utilization, effectively judicializing budget execution in a way that strains separation-of-powers norms during an interlocutory phase.

Perhaps more troubling than the logistical strain is the doctrinal uncertainty sown by issuing a directive on likely success without a corresponding merits opinion. The panel explicitly stated that an opinion explaining their reasoning "will issue in due course," yet they have already commanded specific agency action based on that unpublished analysis. This leaves lower courts and litigants in other circuits guessing at the boundaries of § 1522's mandamus potential. Does "available appropriations" create an affirmative duty to spend regardless of policy shifts? If Congress reduces funding in future years, does the duty evaporate, or does it trigger a constitutional appropriation crisis? By resolving these complex statutory interpretation questions in a summary order rather than a full opinion, the Ninth Circuit has deprived stakeholders of precedential guidance precisely when it is needed most. Agencies tasked with compliance lack clarity on whether they must restore every terminated contract or only those essential for current beneficiaries, inviting further litigation over contempt and scope before September's order can even be fully implemented.

The downstream effects of *Pacito* extend beyond this specific refugee controversy. The order signals a willingness among appellate courts to dissect immigration executive orders into discrete components—entry versus benefits—and apply different standards of review to each. While *Trump v. Hawaii* settled much regarding entry bans, it left open questions about how such bans interact with statutes governing those already within U.S. jurisdiction or legally cleared for entry but stranded abroad by policy changes like travel suspensions or visa caps coming down stream.. By carving out protections for conditionally approved refugees and mandating service continuity via § 1522 ,the Ninth Circuit suggests there are statutory firewalls limiting total executive control once legislative processes have advanced individual cases toward completion . However ,without definitive text accompanying this stay ,lower courts face difficult task distinguishing between permissible pauses processing versus unlawful abandonment existing commitments . Until full opinion arrives ,government operates blindfolded trying balance competing directives : uphold sovereignty borders while fulfilling welfare obligations designed system presupposes steady flow entrants—a contradiction unresolved stay itself .
