Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### What is the procedural effect of vacating previous stays while simultaneously issuing a new 'stay pending appeal' with a specific carveout?
Question:
The operative paragraph of this order is procedurally dense. The court vacates its prior administrative stay (July 18) and its prior partial stay (March 25, as amended). It then states that the District Court's preliminary injunctions are 'stayed in their entirety, except as noted below.' The exception is a directive for the government to reinstate cooperative agreements for refugees already admitted. Procedurally, does this mean the District Court's injunction blocking the refugee ban remains inactive (because it is stayed), while only the injunction regarding domestic services is active? How does this hybrid approach work practically? Is this effectively a modification of the stay rather than a simple grant or denial?
Answer:
Yes, your reading of the procedural effect is correct. The order creates a **hybrid stay** that functionally modifies the scope of the District Court's preliminary injunctions rather than simply granting or denying a stay in full.

Here is how the mechanism works based on the text of *Pacito v. Trump*:

### 1. The General Rule: A Broad Stay
The operative language states: "The district court's February 28... and March 24... preliminary injunctions are **stayed in their entirety**."
*   **Effect:** This suspends the legal force of the District Court's orders preventing the government from enforcing the refugee entry ban (Executive Order 14163). Consequently, the government may immediately resume suspending refugee entries and processing, as the injunction blocking those actions is now inactive.

### 2. The Exception: A Mandatory Carveout
The phrase "**except as noted below**" triggers a specific limitation on that stay. The "note below" directs the government to "reinstate such cooperative agreements necessary to provide the reception and placement services described in § 1522 to refugees who have been admitted to the United States."
*   **Effect:** This portion of the District Court's injunction (specifically the March 24 order regarding domestic services for already-admitted refugees) remains **active and enforceable**. The stay does not apply to this specific subset of relief.

### 3. Practical Application
Practically, this splits the litigation into two tracks pending the final opinion:
*   **Track A (Entry Ban):** The government prevails temporarily. They can halt new refugee admissions because the injunction stopping them is stayed.
*   **Track B (Domestic Services):** The plaintiffs prevail temporarily. The government must continue funding and operating resettlement services for refugees already inside the U.S. because the injunction requiring this is *not* stayed.

### Legal Characterization
You asked if this is effectively a modification of the stay. Precisely. By vacating its prior partial stay (which had a different carveout for conditionally approved individuals abroad) and issuing this new order, the Ninth Circuit is engaging in a **partial stay pending appeal**.

Under the *Nken v. Holder* standard cited in the opinion, a court balances four factors. Here, the panel found:
1.  **Likelihood of Success/Irreparable Harm:** Favored the government regarding the *entry suspension* (citing *Trump v. Hawaii* and § 1182(f)), justifying a stay on that front.
2.  **Likelihood of Success/Irreparable Harm:** Favored the plaintiffs regarding *domestic services*. The panel explicitly stated the government is "not likely to prevail" on the APA challenge regarding § 1522 mandates for admitted refugees, and highlighted the risk of leaving admitted refugees without statutory support.

Therefore, the court tailored the stay to match its preliminary assessment of the merits: staying the injunction where the government is likely to win, but preserving it where the plaintiffs are likely to win. This avoids rendering either party's potential victory on appeal moot due to irreversible changes during the pendency of the case.
