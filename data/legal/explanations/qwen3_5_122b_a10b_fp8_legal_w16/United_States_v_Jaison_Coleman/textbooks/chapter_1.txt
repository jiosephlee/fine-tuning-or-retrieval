Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Jaison Coleman"

Chapter 1: # Factual Background and Procedural Posture

## The 911 Call and Initial Police Response at 4:00 a.m.
To understand the legal dispute in *United States v. Coleman*, one must first grasp the high-stakes environment in which the police action occurred. The case arises from a domestic disturbance call received just after 4:00 a.m. on April 20, 2023, in Marshfield, Wisconsin. A woman reported that her stepfather, Jaison Coleman, had threatened to kill her mother and remained inside the residence. Officer Jamie Kizer responded to this emergency call, a scenario the Seventh Circuit notes involves some of the most difficult responsibilities facing law enforcement. Upon arrival, Officer Kizer knocked on the door and identified himself. Lisa Coleman emerged onto the porch, acknowledging that her children were inside but immediately displaying hesitation when pressed about their safety. Her initial responses were ambiguous; she stated only that the children were in her bedroom and later expressed merely that she "hope[d]" they were okay. This initial ambiguity set the stage for a complex interaction regarding whether the officers had lawful authority to cross the threshold of the home without a warrant.

## Analysis of Body Camera Footage: The Evolving Dialogue
The factual record in this case is uniquely robust because it relies heavily on body camera footage rather than conflicting witness testimony. As noted by the court citing *United States v. Norville*, video evidence can "evaporate any factual dispute" regarding what actually transpired between an officer and a citizen. The footage reveals a nuanced evolution in Officer Kizer's approach as he attempted to secure entry to ensure child safety. Initially, when asked directly if he could enter after Lisa claimed she did not know where her husband was located, Lisa explicitly declined, stating, "um … I'd prefer you not." However, after a pause during which Lisa went inside to retrieve her shoes but failed to return promptly, Officer Kizer grew concerned enough to crack the door and call out to verify her well-being. When Lisa eventually re-emerged, identifying Jaison Coleman as the subject of threats and admitting uncertainty about his current state of mind or location relative to the children, the dynamic shifted again toward determining immediate safety risks inside the dwelling.

## The Critical Exchange: 'I'm sensing that we almost need to come inside'
The central factual pivot point of this opinion occurs during a specific verbal exchange captured on video after Lisa returned to the doorway for a second time. Officer Kizer sought clarification on whether Coleman was calmer and specifically asked if the children were okay. Lisa struggled verbally before stating, "They're okay right this second," though her speech trailed off into unintelligibility. It was at this precise moment that Officer Kizer stated, "Okay, um, I'm sensing that we almost need to come inside to make sure of that." Immediately following this statement—which Coleman argues constitutes coercion due to its use of mandatory language ("need")—Lisa replied simply with "Okay." She then provided specific details about where her children were located (her bedroom), assured officers that their dogs would jump on them but not bite them, and physically opened the door while brushing her pets aside to facilitate entry. This sequence is critical because it represents the alleged moment of consent that validates the officers' subsequent presence in private areas of the home without a warrant or exigent circumstances justification at that exact instant.

## Police Entry, Welfare Check, and Discovery of Firearms
Once admitted based on Lisa's apparent consent, Officer Kizer and his colleagues conducted a welfare check consistent with their stated purpose of ensuring child safety and assessing potential danger from Coleman. Lisa led them directly to one back bedroom where she indicated her children were staying; however, Coleman was not present there. When asked where he might be located inside the house again receiving an uncertain answer from Lisa regarding his whereabouts—the officers proceeded to canvass other rooms quickly out of necessity for officer safety and victim protection.
