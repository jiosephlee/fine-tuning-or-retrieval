Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### Does a 'partial stay' of a preliminary injunction mean the lower court's order remains effective for the non-stayed portions, or is the entire injunction voided until the appellate court rules?
Question:
I'm reading *Pacito v. Trump* and the Ninth Circuit says they are staying the district court's preliminary injunctions "in their entirety except as specified." However, they explicitly carve out an exception where they direct the government to reinstate cooperative agreements for existing refugees. If the appellate court stays an injunction, doesn't that usually freeze everything? How can the district court's order still be partially operative (regarding reinstatement) while being stayed otherwise? Is this a unique procedural mechanism for mixed rulings on statutory vs. constitutional claims?
Answer:
**A partial stay functions exactly as you suspect: it suspends the effectiveness of the lower court's order only to the extent specified, leaving the remainder fully operative.**

There is no special procedural mechanism required for mixed statutory versus constitutional claims to allow this; rather, it is a fundamental feature of how appellate courts exercise their equitable power to grant stays. A stay is not an automatic "voiding" of the district court's judgment; it is a temporary suspension of its enforceability. When an appellate court issues a **partial stay**, it is exercising discretion to balance the equities (the four-factor test from *Nken v. Holder*) differently across distinct parts of the relief granted below.

In *Pacito v. Trump*, the Ninth Circuit applied this logic by distinguishing between the President's authority to suspend refugee *admissions* and his obligation to provide *resettlement services* to those already admitted.

### 1. The Mechanics of a Partial Stay
The Court's language—"stayed in their entirety except as specified"—confirms that the district court's injunctions are frozen everywhere **except** where the appellate court explicitly carves out an exception.
*   **Stayed Portions:** The injunctions prohibiting the suspension of refugee admissions under 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f) are stayed. Consequently, the Executive Order suspending admissions is allowed to proceed despite the district court's contrary ruling.
*   **Non-Stayed Portions:** The directive requiring the reinstatement of cooperative agreements for refugees already in the U.S. remains active. Because this portion was *not* stayed, the government must comply with it immediately while the appeal on other issues continues.

### 2. Why This Happens: Discrete Legal Standards
The Court did not need a unique procedural vehicle because different legal standards apply to different parts of the district court's order, leading to different outcomes on the "likelihood of success on the merits" prong of the stay analysis (*Nken*).

*   **Admissions Suspension (§ 1182(f)):** The majority found that under *Trump v. Hawaii*, the President has broad, deferential authority under § 1182(f). They concluded the government was "likely to prevail" on this specific claim, warranting a stay of that portion of the injunction to prevent irreparable harm to national security interests during litigation.
*   **Resettlement Services (§ 1522):** Conversely, regarding reception and placement services for refugees already admitted, the majority analyzed 8 U.S.C. § 1522 and concluded that statutory language ("shall," "insure") created a mandatory duty, not merely discretionary authorization (contrary to Judge Lee's dissent). Finding that "the government is not likely to prevail" on this APA/statutory interpretation claim, they refused to stay this portion of the injunction.

### Conclusion
A partial stay does not render a lower court order void; it renders it **partially ineffective**. In *Pacito*, this means:
1.  The Executive Order banning new entries is valid and enforceable (because that part of the injunction is stayed).
2.  The State Department must reinstate cooperative agreements for existing refugees (because that part of the injunction remains in force).

This approach allows appellate courts to preserve their jurisdiction and manage complex cases without forcing all-or-nothing outcomes when different legal questions within a single case present distinct equities and likelihoods of success on appeal.
