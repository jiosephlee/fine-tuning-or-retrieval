Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Jimenez v. Bondi"

Chapter 1: # Case Overview, Facts, and Procedural History

## The Petitioners' Profile: Political Affiliation and Professional Roles

The petitioners in *Jimenez v. Bondi* are Wilson Leonardo Canon Jimenez, his wife Angelica Parada Duarte, and their two minor sons. Originally from Bogotá, Colombia, the family operated a furniture factory prior to their departure for the United States in December 2022. Central to their legal claim is their long-standing affiliation with the Colombian Liberal Party, a political organization they joined in 2012. Their engagement extended beyond passive membership; during the 2022 presidential election cycle, they actively campaigned for Federico Gutiérrez, a candidate opposing then-President Gustavo Petro. This activism involved direct voter outreach, including door-to-door canvassing in local neighborhoods.

Parallel to their political activities, Jimenez maintained a significant professional profile within Colombia's legal and governmental sectors. Holding a provisional law license while completing his law studies in 2022, Jimenez served as an intern for the National Prosecutor's Office. His duties included assisting victims of domestic abuse and extortion. This dual identity—as a visible political operative for an opposition party and a representative of state prosecutorial authority—created the complex factual matrix from which their asylum claim arose. The petitioners' ability to articulate these distinct roles was critical to understanding how the court analyzed the motives behind the threats they received.

## Chronology of Harm: From Campaign Threats to Property Dispute Escalation

The timeline of harm begins in late May 2022, shortly after the petitioners intensified their campaign efforts for President-elect Gutiérrez. Jimenez began receiving threatening phone calls once or twice weekly specifically referencing his involvement with the Liberal Party. These threats were not isolated; other party leaders faced similar intimidation, culminating in the kidnapping and murder of a colleague who had reported receiving threats. Despite this violence and the palpable danger surrounding them, the petitioners remained undeterred and continued their political work throughout June 2022.

However, the nature of the threat escalated significantly following a separate event unrelated to politics initially: a property dispute involving Jimenez's legal internship work. In June 2022, a client approached Jimenez regarding an unpaid rent issue involving her former husband's tenancy at her property located in San Bernardo. When Jimenez attempted to mediate directly with the tenant—a common practice for lawyers at that stage—he encountered hostility. The tenant warned him not to "look for trouble" and refused negotiation. Through his investigation into the property title and usage, Jimenez uncovered that the tenant was Rafael Augusto Montanez Lancheros ("Don Rafa"), a known leader of narco-trafficking guerrillas politically aligned with President Petro's administration. Furthermore, Jimenez discovered that Don Rafa was using the property as a base for narcotics trafficking and prostitution, operating despite sealed health department violations and previous unsuccessful police raids.

## The 'Don Rafa' Incident: Intersecting Criminal Enterprise and Political Activity

The intersection of Jimenez's professional actions and his political identity created a volatile situation that formed the core of the factual dispute on appeal. Shortly after discovering Don Rafa's identity and informing local authorities about his operations—including speaking with a police officer who advised him to stay away—Jimenez received tangible retaliation that blended accusations of political betrayal with criminal grievances. He first received a threatening brochure labeled "death to snitches from Liberal Party." While he initially dismissed this as generic political harassment, subsequent events suggested otherwise.

While working late at his furniture factory one evening two months later, two armed men confronted Jimenez at gunpoint. They explicitly accused him of being a "snitch" for the prosecutor's office due to his investigations into Don Rafa's property operations but simultaneously linked this accusation to his political standing as someone "in politics." The attackers ordered him to leave his neighborhood under threat of death from Don Rafa himself roughly one month later returned with an associate to assault an employee at Jimenez's factory while searching for the petitioners' whereabouts; although they found no one home at that moment (as Petitioners were traveling), they reiterated that Don Rafa had authorized lethal action if Petitioners were seen again because they were viewed as snitches interfering with criminal enterprise while holding political influence. The tension reached its peak when one attacker physically grabbed Jimenez's young son outside school days later—an act prompting immediate flight—to ensure he understood exactly how serious those combined threats really were! Ultimately though—it wasn't just pure ideology driving these individuals forward anymore either since there existed clear overlap between both spheres where personal vendettas could easily morph into something far more deadly indeed!
