Chapter 1: # Case Context and Procedural History

## Petitioner Profile: Political Affiliation vs. Professional Role

Understanding the petitioners in *Jimenez v. Bondi* requires a clear distinction between their dual identities, as the tension between these roles forms the core of the legal dispute. Wilson Leonardo Canon Jimenez and his wife, Angelica Parada Duarte, are natives of Bogotá, Colombia, who maintained active lives in both the political and professional spheres prior to their arrival in the United States. Politically, the couple were long-standing members of the Colombian Liberal Party, having joined in 2012. Their involvement was substantive rather than symbolic; during the 2022 presidential election cycle, they actively campaigned for candidate Federico Gutierrez, engaging in grassroots activities such as canvassing neighborhoods to solicit votes. This political engagement placed them squarely within the sphere of "political opinion," a statutorily protected ground for asylum claims.

Simultaneously, Jimenez occupied a distinct professional role that inadvertently intersected with high-level criminal activity. Holding a provisional law license while completing his legal studies, Jimenez worked as an intern for the National Prosecutor's Office in Colombia. In this capacity, he assisted victims of domestic abuse and extortion. His professional duties led him to represent a client in a property dispute involving her former husband and tenant, Rafael Augusto Montanez Lancheros ("Don Rafa"). Crucially, Jimenez's identity as a lawyer acting on behalf of a client brought him into direct conflict with Don Rafa, who led a narco-trafficking guerrilla group aligned with then-President Gustavo Petro. The court's analysis hinges on determining whether subsequent harms were driven by Jimenez's political activism or his professional interference with Don Rafa's illicit operations.

## Chronology of Harm: From Campaign Threats to Escalation

The timeline of events presented in *Jimenez* reveals a critical shift in the nature and source of threats against the family. Initially, beginning in late May 2022, Jimenez received threatening phone calls related to his Liberal Party campaigning. These threats coincided with violence against other party members, including the kidnapping and murder of a colleague who had also reported threats. Despite this climate of danger and escalating rhetoric—including a brochure found at their factory reading "death to snitches from Liberal Party"—the petitioners remained undeterred and continued their political work. Notably, at this stage in the chronology, there was no evidence linking these specific threats directly to Don Rafa or connecting them to Jimenez's legal work; they appeared to be generalized intimidation tactics common among rival political factions or criminal groups opposing liberal candidates.

The trajectory changed fundamentally in June 2022 when Jimenez intervened in his client's property dispute involving Don Rafa's rental operation used for narco-trafficking and prostitution. Upon discovering that law enforcement attempts to seize the property had failed and receiving warnings from local police to "stay away," Jiminez persisted in investigating the premises shortly thereafter. It was only after this intervention—and specifically after Don Rafa became aware that Jimenez was acting as a lawyer investigating his illegal enterprise—that the threats escalated significantly toward physical violence. The narrative arc suggests that while political hostility existed initially, the most severe and specific acts of aggression occurred after Jiminez crossed paths with Don Rafa through his professional duties, blurring the line between political persecution and retaliation for interfering with criminal enterprises.

## Key Incidents: Gunpoint Confrontations and Abduction Attempts

The factual record details several specific incidents that formed the basis of Petitioners' claim for past persecution. Following their investigation into Don Rafa's property, two men confronted Jiminez at gunpoint while he was working at his family's furniture factory at night. During this encounter, one assailant explicitly accused Jiminez of being a "snitch" for the prosecutor's office and blamed him for recent police searches at Don Rafa's location before ordering him to leave the neighborhood. Although no physical injury occurred during this confrontation—something Petitioners conceded—the incident represented an immediate threat to life and liberty tied directly to Jiminez's legal activities rather than solely his voting record or campaign support.
