Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

Chapter 1: # Case Overview: Facts, Charges, and Procedural Posture

## Factual Background: Domestic Disturbance, Hostage Situation, and Flight to a Neighbor's Garage

The case of *United States v. Cutbank* arises from a volatile sequence of events beginning on the night of November 19–20, 2020. The underlying criminal conduct involved severe domestic violence coupled with armed intimidation. After consuming alcohol and methamphetamine, the defendant, Justin Cutbank, awakened his girlfriend, D.F., and forced her into a closet at gunpoint. During this ordeal, Cutbank expressed paranoid delusions regarding "listening devices" and individuals waiting outside to harm him. He maintained D.F. in custody for several hours, threatening her life and physically assaulting her by striking her across the face with the firearm. Escalating the danger to others present in the home, Cutbank commanded D.F. to wake her two teenage sons and a housemate, C.F. Following these acts of coercion and battery, Cutbank fled the scene accompanied by D.F.'s cell phone and the weapon used to threaten her family members.

Cutbank's flight led him approximately half a mile away to a separate residence where he entered a garage and barricaded himself inside despite requests from occupants to leave. Upon arrival by law enforcement officers responding to reports of an intruder refusing to vacate premises, Cutbank engaged in erratic behavior over several hours while claiming possession of a weapon. The confrontation culminated in physical resistance when officers attempted detention; specifically, Cutbank bit an officer on the face and neck and attempted to seize the officer's service pistol. This factual narrative established the gravity of the immediate threat posed by Cutbank and provided the context for his subsequent prosecution for possessing a firearm as a prohibited person.

## Evidence Recovery: Seizure of the Sawed-Off Marlin .22 Rifle and DNA Matching

Following the standoff at the garage, investigators conducted searches along the route Cutbank likely traveled between his girlfriend's residence and the location where he was apprehended. These efforts yielded critical physical evidence linking Cutbank directly to the crime scene and the specific instrumentality of his offenses. Searchers recovered D.F.'s stolen cell phone, a butcher-style knife, and most significantly for this prosecution, a Marlin .22 caliber rifle that had been modified with a sawed-off barrel and stock. Under federal law, such modifications generally render a firearm illegal per se regardless of intent to use it criminally once possessed by certain categories of persons. Crucially, forensic analysis confirmed that DNA found on the seized rifle matched Justin Cutbank. Furthermore, at trial, both D.F. and C.F., witnesses who had been held hostage earlier that evening, positively identified this specific weapon as one they had observed in Cutbank's possession prior to that night's incidents. This chain of recovery connected the defendant physically to an illegally configured firearm during commission of violent acts.

## Charges Filed: Violations of 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(g)(1) and 924(e)
