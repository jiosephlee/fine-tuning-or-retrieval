Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Does CC Serve’s partnering with issuing banks affect whether it really offers 'credit card services' for §2(d) purposes?
Question:
CC Serve partners with a bank that issues ASPIRE-branded cards and accounts, while CC Serve services the accounts and manages the program. Apex argues CC Serve is 'not providing banking services' itself. For priority and similarity-of-services, does providing program-management/servicing under the mark qualify as 'credit card services' even if a partner bank is the legal issuer? Is this analyzed under the 'related company' doctrine (Lanham Act §1055) or quality/control principles, and does it affect the second DuPont factor analysis?
Answer:
Short answer
Yes. In Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp. the Federal Circuit treats CC Serve’s role as a program manager/servicer as “credit card services” for §2(d) purposes. The court affirmed the Board’s conclusion that CC Serve’s activities (joining with a bank that issues ASPIRE cards and servicing the accounts) fall within the Board’s broad definition of “credit card services” and therefore are highly similar—indeed “legally identical, in part”—to Apex’s banking/finance services for DuPont factor two. See J.A. 6927–30, J.A. 25, J.A. 27–28.

Why (legal standard and the Court’s application)
- Governing test: DuPont factor two asks whether the parties’ goods/services are related or marketed such that consumers might believe they come from the same source; “the services need not be identical” — only relatedness or marketing circumstances that could cause confusion. (Court cites Coach Servs., and restates that principle. J.A. 24–25.)
- How the Board defined “credit card services”: the Board expressly included a range of functions under that identification—“issuing credit cards for use to finance purchases as well as counseling regarding credit card debt, processing credit card payments and transactions, credit card authorization, credit card monitoring and alerts, managing credit card accounts, and providing access to credit scores.” J.A. 25.
- Factual fit: CC Serve “joins with a bank, and the bank issues ASPIRE‑branded credit cards and associated accounts to customers,” and “the accounts are serviced by CC Serve and its affiliates.” The court rejected Apex’s contention that CC Serve’s partnership role meant it was “not providing banking services” and affirmed the Board’s finding that the services are highly similar (partially legally identical). J.A. 6930; J.A. 27–28; see also J.A. 35 (second DuPont factor weighed heavily for confusion).

What doctrines were applied (and what were not)
- The opinion resolves this question under the DuPont likelihood‑of‑confusion framework—specifically the second DuPont factor (similarity of services). It treats the practical functions performed under the ASPIRE mark (including program management and account servicing) as part of the “credit card services” category for that inquiry. J.A. 24–28; J.A. 35.
- The opinion does not invoke a separate “related company” doctrine or a distinct Lanham Act §1055/quality‑control analysis to resolve whether a servicer counts as the provider of the identified services. In short, the Court used the DuPont relatedness/marketing test rather than a standalone related‑company/quality‑control doctrine in this case.

Practical takeaway from the opinion
- On this record, branding and functional role matter more than the label “issuer” vs. “bank”: providing program management, servicing and other credit‑card functions under the ASPIRE mark was enough for the Board (and the Court) to treat CC Serve as offering “credit card services” for §2(d) analysis. J.A. 25, J.A. 6930.
- The court did, however, remand other DuPont factors (first and sixth) for further consideration in light of the Board’s finding of high similarity—so while the servicer role sufficed for factor two here, the overall likelihood‑of‑confusion outcome may turn on those other factors on remand. J.A. 47, J.A. 51–52; see also discussion vacating factors one and six.

Bottom line
If a party provides program management/servicing under a mark (even where a banking partner is the formal issuer), Apex v. CC Serve shows those activities can qualify as the identified “credit card services” for DuPont factor two. The Court treated that question as part of the services‑similarity inquiry rather than by applying a separate related‑company or quality‑control doctrine.
