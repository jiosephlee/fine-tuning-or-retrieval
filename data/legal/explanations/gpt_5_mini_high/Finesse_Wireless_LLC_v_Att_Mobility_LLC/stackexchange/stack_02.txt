Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### For the ’134 patent, do the ‘interference generating signals’ have to be physically present at the ADC input, or can a downstream modeled PIM signal satisfy limitation 1[a]?
Question:
Claim 1[a] requires over-sampling a passband of received signals that includes both signals of interest and interference generating signals. The patentee’s expert mapped the ‘interference generating signals’ to a modeled PIM path that is created after the RF ADC in an FPGA. The panel held that cannot satisfy limitation 1[a] because you cannot sample a signal that does not yet exist. Could the ‘interference generating signals’ instead be the actual RF interference present at the receiver front-end (even if the model is generated later), and if so, what level of explicit testimony or mapping is required at trial to tie those pre-ADC signals to the claim language?
Answer:
Short answer
- The Court treated limitation 1[a] as requiring that the “interference generating signals” be part of the passband actually sampled by the RF ADC — i.e., physically present at the ADC input when sampling occurs. A modeled PIM signal that is generated later downstream in the FPGA cannot itself satisfy 1[a] because “you cannot sample a signal that does not yet exist.” Finesse Wireless v. AT&T, No. 2024‑1039, slip op. (Fed. Cir. Sept. 24, 2025).
- That said, actual RF interference present at the receiver front end can satisfy 1[a] — but the patentee must present clear, non‑contradictory mapping and evidence at trial tying those pre‑ADC signals to the claim language (i.e., show that the RF ADC actually sampled those signals).

Why (what the opinion says)
- The panel reversed JMOL for the ’134 claims because Finesse’s expert expressly mapped “interference generating signals” to the modeled PIM path, and the modeled PIM path was generated in the GROOT FPGA after sampling. The court held that if the modeled PIM path is the claimed “interference generating signals,” limitation 1[a] cannot be met “because the accused radios’ receiver does not sample a passband of received signals that includes interference generating signals since those signals are not generated until after sampling occurs.” Slip op. at pp. 21–22.
- The court also stressed that the plaintiff bears the burden to prove the accused device samples a passband that includes both the signals of interest and the interference generating signals, and that ambiguous or self‑contradictory expert testimony will not carry that burden. The panel faulted the expert for failing to testify unambiguously that x1 and x2 were the pre‑ADC “signals of interest” and “interference generating signals,” and for repeatedly tying the claim terms to the modeled PIM rather than to concrete pre‑ADC waveforms. Slip op. at pp. 10–12, 21–22; citing Johns Hopkins Univ. v. Datascope Corp., 543 F.3d 1342, 1349 (Fed. Cir. 2008) (self‑contradictory expert testimony may be insufficient).

What you must prove at trial to avoid the same pitfall
If you want to rely on front‑end RF interference (not a post‑ADC model) to satisfy limitation 1[a], the record must clearly show three things, with consistent testimony and documentary/experimental support:

1) Explicit mapping of claim terms to physical signals
   - The expert must unambiguously state which physical waveform(s) correspond to “signals of interest” and which correspond to “interference generating signals” (e.g., “x1 = DL(TX) reference = signal of interest; x2 = RF carrier at ADC input = interference generating signal”). The Court reversed because the expert repeatedly tied the claim language to the modeled PIM path and never clearly mapped x1/x2 to the claimed terms. Slip op. at pp. 10–12, 21.

2) Proof those signals are “received signals” sampled by the RF ADC
   - Demonstrate (with schematics, vendor docs, timing diagrams, chip datasheets, oscilloscope captures, scope traces, or other measurements) that the identified interference waveform exists at the ADC input at the time of sampling and is therefore part of the sampled passband. The opinion turned on the fact the modeled PIM path was generated downstream of the ADC, and so it could not be what the ADC sampled. Slip op. at p. 21.

3) Technical linkage showing the interference is capable of generating the claimed IMPs in‑band
   - Explain why the identified RF interference is an “interference generating signal” within the meaning of the claim (i.e., capable of generating intermodulation products in‑band of the signals of interest). That is part of the limitation’s substantive content and was Finesse’s burden to prove.

Practical points drawn from the opinion
- Don’t rely on a figure’s legend or a shorthand (“red path = DL(TX) reference & modeled PIM path”) without separately and clearly identifying which physical waveform in that figure is which claim element. The court faulted Finesse for letting the legend and scattered testimony create an inference rather than providing a straightforward mapping. Slip op. at pp. 10–12.
- Avoid internally inconsistent expert testimony. The panel emphasized that the plaintiff’s case failed because the expert’s testimony was confusing and self‑contradictory about what was sampled. Slip op. at pp. 10–12; see also Johns Hopkins.
- Documentary corroboration helps. The stronger the documentary or measurement evidence tying the names/labels in vendor documentation (x1, x2, DL(TX), modeled PIM) to physical nodes and sampling points, the less vulnerable the testimony is to being characterized as speculative or contradictory.

Bottom line
- A modeled PIM signal produced after the ADC cannot satisfy limitation 1[a] because it was not a “received signal” sampled by the RF ADC. Finesse, slip op. at p. 21.
- The patentee can meet 1[a] by showing the actual RF interference at the front end was sampled by the ADC — but must do so with clear, consistent expert mapping and contemporaneous documentary or measurement evidence that the ADC sampled those pre‑ADC interference waveforms. The appellate decision reversed here precisely because that clear mapping and evidence were lacking.
