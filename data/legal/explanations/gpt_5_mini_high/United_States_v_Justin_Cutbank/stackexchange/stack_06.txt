Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Is base offense level 26 under § 2K2.1(a) correct for a sawed-off rifle plus two prior crimes of violence?
Question:
The opinion says the PSR set a base offense level of 26 because the offense involved a § 5845(a) sawed-off firearm and Cutbank had at least two prior crimes of violence. I’m having trouble mapping that to the text of § 2K2.1(a) in the current Guidelines: some subsections hinge on the type of firearm (e.g., § 5845(a) firearms), others on number of prior crimes of violence or controlled substance offenses, and some combinations set different base levels (e.g., 22, 24, or 26). Which exact subsection(s) of § 2K2.1(a) in effect at the time support a 26 here, and why isn’t the correct base offense level 22 or 24 instead?
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — the PSR’s choice of base offense level 26 was correct. Under the edition of USSG §2K2.1 in effect for Cutbank, §2K2.1(a) lists alternative “base offense level” buckets and you pick the greatest one that fits the facts. One alternative gives a base level of 26 for an offense involving a firearm described in 26 U.S.C. § 5845(a) (a short‑barreled/sawed‑off weapon); another alternative gives a base level of 26 where the defendant committed the instant offense after at least two prior felony convictions for a crime of violence or a controlled‑substance offense. Because the PSR found both: (1) the Marlin .22 had a sawed‑off barrel/stock (a § 5845(a) firearm) and (2) Cutbank had at least two prior crimes of violence, the PSR properly selected the 26 “bucket.” The district court adopted the PSR without objection, and the Eighth Circuit affirmed.

Why not 22 or 24? Those lower base levels are alternative buckets in §2K2.1(a) tied to different fact patterns (for example, fewer or different priors or a different kind of firearm). You do not combine or average the alternatives — you apply the single greatest alternative that matches the facts. Because one (indeed two) of the alternatives that carry a 26 base level applied here, 26 — not 22 or 24 — was correct.

Support in the opinion: the court summarizes the PSR’s reasoning: “The Presentence Investigation Report (PSR) recommended a base offense level of 26 because the offense involved a sawed‑off firearm described in 26 U.S.C. § 5845(a) and Cutback had at least two prior convictions for crimes of violence,” and then describes the other enhancements that produced the total offense level the court adopted.
