Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Does applying § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) on top of ACCA, while also considering § 4B1.4’s “greater of” rule, risk impermissible double-counting?
Question:
The panel notes the Armed Career Criminal Guideline (§ 4B1.4) would have yielded offense level 34, but the § 2K2.1 route with enhancements produced 35, so the greater applied. If the same conduct (possessing the gun "in connection with" another felony) could both trigger § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) and satisfy § 4B1.4(b)(3)(A)’s 34 level, is that double-counting? Or does the "use the greater of" directive resolve any double-counting concern because only the higher path is used? Is there Eighth Circuit authority squarely addressing this interaction?
Answer:
Short answer
No—at least in Cutbank the Eighth Circuit treated this as a guidelines‑mechanics issue, not impermissible double‑counting. The court upheld the §2K2.1(b)(6)(B) four‑level enhancement and then applied USSG §4B1.4(b)’s “greater of” rule (compute both offense‑level routes and take the higher), and it found no error or §3553(a) violation. United States v. Cutbank, No. 24‑1225, slip op. at Section III (8th Cir. Oct. 7, 2025).

Why (short explanation tied to the opinion)
- The PSR in Cutbank calculated a total offense level of 35 by applying the §2K2.1 enhancements (including the four‑level §2K2.1(b)(6)(B) enhancement). The Armed Career Criminal Guideline would have produced total offense level 34. The Guidelines themselves resolve that conflict: “The Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies. See USSG § 4B1.4(b).” Slip op., § III.
- The panel reasoned that the district court adopted the PSR’s facts (so the finding that the gun was “in connection with” another felony was not clearly erroneous) and that the Guidelines’ text prescribes the answer here—compute both routes and use the higher offense level. The court therefore affirmed the calculation and the within‑Guidelines sentence. Slip op., § III.

What this means for a double‑counting challenge
- “Double‑counting” challenges succeed when a sentencing court impermissibly uses the same conduct twice in a way the Guidelines do not authorize. Cutbank shows that where the Guidelines expressly provide a tie‑breaker (here §4B1.4(b)’s “greater of” instruction), applying the §2K2.1 enhancement and then selecting the higher offense level under §4B1.4(b) is not treated as impermissible double‑counting in the Eighth Circuit facts presented.
- The panel did consider (and reject) the defendant’s related §3553(a) argument that the enhancement produced an excessive sentence, but it did so on the record showing the district court considered the §3553(a) factors and imposed a within‑Guidelines term (which is presumptively reasonable on appeal). Slip op., § III.

Is there Eighth Circuit authority squarely on point?
- Yes: United States v. Cutbank itself is the controlling Eighth Circuit panel decision that addresses this interaction and affirms applying §2K2.1(b)(6)(B) when the court then uses USSG §4B1.4(b) to select the greater offense level. The opinion does not undertake a broader doctrinal analysis of “double‑counting” beyond applying the Guidelines’ own rule, but it is the on‑point Eighth Circuit treatment of the precise situation you describe.
