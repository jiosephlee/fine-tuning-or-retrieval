Chapter 3: # Chapter 3: Sentencing Guidelines: Calculation and Specific Enhancements

## Determining the Base Offense Level: Sawed-off Firearm and Creating Risk of Death

When reviewing the applicable sentencing guidelines, the Sentencing Commission follows a specific mathematical process to determine the appropriate range of imprisonment. In *Cutbank*, the appellate court explained that the starting point is the base offense level, which is then adjusted by specific enhancements found in the Presentence Investigation Report (PSR). In this case, the PSR recommended a base offense level of 26 because the offense involved the possession of a sawed-off firearm, as defined by 26 U.S.C. § 5845(a). From this base, the calculation was increased by adding levels for specific conduct. The district court applied a three-level increase because the firearm had an altered or obliterated serial number. Additionally, the court applied a two-level increase because Cutbank recklessly created a risk of death or serious bodily injury while fleeing from police. Finally, a four-level increase was applied for possessing the firearm in connection with another felony offense. This resulted in a total offense level of 35. Students must note that the district court is required to begin all sentencing proceedings by correctly calculating the applicable Guidelines range, and failing to do so is considered a significant procedural error that courts of appeals review strictly.

## USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B): Application of the 'Connection to Another Felony' Enhancement

A critical component of the sentencing calculation in *Cutbank* is the application of the United States Sentencing Guidelines Amendment § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B). This provision applies four offense levels to a defendant's sentence if they used or possessed any firearm in connection with another federal, state, or local felony offense. The Guidelines Commentary, specifically note (n.14(C)), clarifies that this enhancement applies regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought for the connection offense or a conviction was obtained. In the district court, this enhancement was adopted without objection from Cutbank. Because the court adopted the PSR's factual statements regarding Cutbank's prior felony convictions, the appellate court determined there was no error in finding that the facts established that the gun was used in connection with another felony. On appeal, the standard of review for these discretionary findings is lenient; the court assumes the district court "[a]ctually considered the § 3553(a) factors in determining the sentence" unless the record indicates otherwise, and the Guidelines range is presumed reasonable if the sentence falls within it.

## ACCA Eligibility: Finding Three Prior Violent Felony Convictions

The *Cutbank* opinion illustrates how the presence of prior violent felonies can function differently under the statutory enhancement versus the Guidelines suggestion. The jury found that Cutbank had at least three prior violent felony convictions. This factual finding triggered the Armed Career Criminal Act's enhanced penalty provisions under 18 U.S.C. § 924(e), which imposes a mandatory minimum sentence. While this statutory enhancement is separate from the calculation of the Guidelines base offense level, the record notes that an application of the "Armed Career Criminal Guideline" would have produced a total offense level of 34. This indicates that the Guidelines provide a specific method for calculating offense levels based on the accumulation of violent felony priors. Understanding this distinction is vital: while the ACCA penalty is a statutory mandate based on prior convictions, the Guidelines calculation serves to determine the specific sentencing range. In this case, the district court's sentencing determination involved this calculation alongside specific offense enhancements, demonstrating how multiple layers of prior conviction data feed into the final advisory range.

## Conflict Resolution: When the Guidelines Exceed Other Possible Calculations

Prudential application of the Guidelines requires resolving conflicts when multiple calculation paths result in different totals. The *Cutbank* court clarified that when the Guidelines indicate that one calculation method proceeds differently than another—such as the specific offense characteristics versus the Armed Career Criminal Account—specific provisions govern which number controls. The court cited USSG § 4B1.4(b), noting that the Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies. In *Cutbank*, the PSR calculated a total offense level of 35, whereas the ACCA-specific guideline calculation would have resulted in a level of 34. Because the PSR calculation relied on the "greater offense level" rule, the 35 count governed the final calculation. This approach ensures that procedural deference to the sentencing court is maintained only after the court has correctly calculated the applicable range. Finally, because the district court imposed a sentence within the applicable advisory Guidelines range, the appellate court applied a presumption of reasonableness, focusing on whether the burden of the § 3553(a) factors warranted a departure from the calculated range.
