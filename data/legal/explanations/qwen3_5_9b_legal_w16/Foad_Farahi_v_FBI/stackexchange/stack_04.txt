Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### How does the D.C. Circuit define 'interference' with a law enforcement proceeding in Exemption 7(A) cases?
Question:
The court cites *CREW* for requiring 'tipping off' targets as a justifiable reason for withholding. Beyond just 'tipping off' suspects, does the court require evidence that the investigation will *collapse* if the information is released? Or does the mere possibility that evidence might be discovered by a person of interest work without showing that it would terminate the specific enforcement proceedings?
Answer:
The short answer is that the D.C. Circuit in *Farahi* defines "interference" to include the "tipping off" of targets, and it does **not** require evidence that the specific enforcement proceedings will collapse or terminate simply due to the possibility of release. Instead, the standard is whether disclosure "could reasonably be expected to interfere" with proceedings that are "pending or reasonably anticipated."

Here are the specific standards from *Farahi* regarding "interference":

**1. "Tipping Off" is a Sufficient Justification**
The *Farahi* panel explicitly acknowledges that disclosure of information regarding an ongoing investigation interferes when it tips off targets about the scope of the investigation. The court notes this is a "widely accepted justification in Exemption 7(A) cases."

> "Disclosure of information regarding an ongoing investigation could reasonably be expected to interfere with it by tipping off the targets about the scope of the investigation—a widely accepted justification in Exemption 7(A) cases."
> (*Farahi*, Section IV.B)

The court held that the FBI met its burden by showing that the disclosure "could reasonably be expected to interfere with pending or reasonably anticipated enforcement proceedings" via this mechanism.

**2. The Standard is "Jeopardy" or "Hindrance," Not "Collapse"**
While you asked if the investigation must "collapse," the opinion cites *United States v. Juarez* (via quotation) to clarify that a proceeding does not need to be known as "jeopardized" in the sense of aborting entirely for Exemption 7(A) to apply. Rather, the focus is on the premature release of evidence gathering.

> "So long as the investigation continues to gather evidence for a possible future criminal case, and that case would be jeopardized by the premature release of that evidence, Exemption 7(A) applies."
> (*Farahi*, Section IV.B quoting *Juarez*)

**3. Proceedings Must Be "Pending or Reasonably Anticipated"**
As established in *CREW* (cited at Section I), the proceedings must be pending or reasonably anticipated. In *Farahi*, the D.C. Circuit upheld the FBI's showing that the investigation remained "ongoing." The court rejected the argument that the passage of time made the proceedings stale because the FBI provided updated declarations confirming the investigation continued.

**Conclusion**
To answer your specific question: The court does **not** require you to show that the specific enforcement proceedings will terminate or collapse. It requires a showing that:
1.  The proceedings are "pending or reasonably anticipated"; and
2.  Release of the information could reasonably be expected to "interfere" with them (e.g., by "tipping off" targets with the scope of the investigation).

In *Farahi*, the court affirmed that the "mere" justification of tipping off agents about the scope of the investigation was adequate, provided the investigation itself remained ongoing.
