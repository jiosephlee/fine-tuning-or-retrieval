Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

Chapter 1: # Chapter 1: Case Background and Procedural History

## Executive Order No. 14163: Suspending USRAP and Funding

To understand the litigation and the procedural history in *Pacito v. Trump*, students must first examine the factual predicate established in January 2025. On January 20, 2025, President Trump issued Executive Order No. 14163, entitled “Realigning the United States Refugee Admissions Program.” This order relied on the authority of 8 U.S.C. § 1182(f), the Statutory Detriment Clause. Specifically, President Trump determined that entry into the United States of refugees under the U.S. Refugee Admissions Program (USRAP) would be detrimental to the interests of the United States. Consequently, the Order suspended such entry pending further findings.

Pursuant to a different executive order, Executive Order No. 14169 dated January 20, 2025, the Department of State suspended all funding for the USRAP program on January 24, 2025. This suspension of funding purportedly was under authority of “Reevaluating and Realigning United States Foreign Aid.” Notably, at the time this funding was suspended, it covered domestic resettlement services for refugees who had already been admitted to the United States. These specific factual predicates—the suspension of admissions and the suspension of funding—are the central actions challenged in this case.

## Plaintiffs' Identity and Claims Under the Refugee Act and APA

This chapter establishes the parties and their specific legal claims. The plaintiffs comprise two groups: refugees who were previously admitted to the United States or approved for resettlement but remained outside the country at the time of the lawsuit; and three organizations holding cooperative agreements with the State Department for overseas processing and resettlement services filed in February 2025.

Regarding legal standards, the plaintiffs alleged that Executive Order 14163 violated the Refugee Act of 1980, codified in various provisions of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA). The plaintiffs' claim regarding the defunding of USRAP violations of the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), 5 U.S.C. § 551 et seq. The Refugee Act of 1980 is referenced as a general rule for the ongoing treatment of all refugees and displaced persons. Furthermore, the case has implications for 8 U.S.C. § 1522, which addresses domestic resettlement frameworks.

## District Court Rulings: Preliminary Injunctions (February - March 2025)

The procedural history begins in February 2025 when the plaintiffs filed suit in the Western District of Washington. Shortly after filing, on February 25, 2025, the district court orally issued a preliminary injunction. This order prohibited the enforcement of specific sections (§§ 3(a), (b), (c), and 4) of Executive Order No. 14163. While the court initially granted this relief orally, the government terminated cooperative agreements for reception and placement services on February 26, 2025, following the oral ruling.

By February 28, 2025, the district court issued a written order to clarify the scope of the preliminary injunction. This written order specified that it also prohibited the suspension of USRAP funding. On March 24, 2025, the district court issued a second preliminary injunction. Unlike the first order, this order was more specific to the plaintiff organizations; it required the State Department to reinstate all cooperative agreements that had been terminated after the February 25 ruling. The government filed notices of appeal on February 28 and March 25, 2025, initiating the appellate process.

## Procedural Timeline of Appeals and Prior Stays

Once the notices of appeal were filed, the Ninth Circuit engaged in a series of interlocutory stays. On March 25, 2025, a motions panel granted a stay pending appeal in part. However, that panel denied the motion for a stay to the extent the injunction applied to individuals who were conditionally approved for refugee status before January 20, 2025. Following this, the Ninth Circuit issued clarifying orders on April 21 and May 9 regarding this "limited carveout from the stay."

The procedural timeline accelerated significantly after oral argument. In July 2025, the district court ordered a magistrate judge to oversee an enforcement framework for the limited carveout. Meanwhile, the Ninth Circuit noted the government filed an emergency motion on July 17 to enforce, clarify, or amend the stay order. In response, the Ninth Circuit issued an administrative stay on July 18, 2025, staying the district court’s July 14 order. Prior to the current panel's decision on September 12, 2025, the court had vacated the previous administrative and stays of March 25.

## Expedited Oral Argument and Expedited Order

Following the filings and briefing, the Ninth Circuit expedited the process for this matter. Parties were directed to briefing on the limited carveout before hearing. Oral argument was scheduled for an expedited basis on September 3, 2025. Following argument, the court found that the case was submitted for decision and would issue a full opinion in due course.

This current Panel Order serves as an "interim" remedy. The court decided to vacate the administrative stay issued on July 18 and vacate the stay of March 25, as amended on April 21 and May 9. Instead of relying on previous stays, the court issued this current order as a stay pending the final resolution of the appeal. This order illustrates a common appellate procedure where an earlier interlocutory order is superseded to ensure clarity as the case approaches a final disposition.

## Scope of the District Court Orders Subject to Appeal

The critical procedural outcome of this opinion is the scope of the stay it issues vis-à-vis the district court orders. The autumn order specifies that the district court’s orders of February 28, 2006, and March 24, 2025 are stayed in their entirety. A stay of a preliminary injunction generally means that the injunction does not apply, allowing the government to enforce the actions originally prohibited (the suspension of the REFRESAP program and funding).

However, the Order contains an explicit exception to the stay regarding the service mandates. Under 8 U.S.C. § 1522, the text notes that the government is obligated to provide reception and placement services. Because the government was not secured a new agency or agencies to provide these services and the mandate was statutory, the Order directs the government to reinstate cooperative agreements necessary for such services. This command stands despite the broader stay of the district court's injunctions. The distinction highlights the court's procedural focus: while the general suspension of the refugee program and the validity of the Executive Order are stayed pending the final opinion, the specific statutory obligation to reinstate agreements for admitted refugees is enforced through this interim order. This leads to a situation where the injunction is technically stayed, but the litigation is temporarily paused except for the specific reinstatement of funding agreements.
