### What satisfies subpart (iv)’s requirement to specify names and addresses of interested parties?
Question:
Section 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(iv) requires the certification to specify the names and addresses of any governmental agencies, secured parties, or third parties to whom notice was sent. The sample certification in the record says copies were sent but doesn’t list specific names/addresses. Did the court infer there were no such parties, making a list unnecessary, or does a general statement meet 'strict adherence' to subpart (iv)?
Answer:
Short answer
The Fifth Circuit treated APAC’s blanket certification that “Copies of the Ten Day Notice of Cancellation were sent to all persons shown by the Premium Finance Agreement to have an interest…” (and that copies were enclosed/affidavit available) as meeting the statutory certification requirement. The court did not require a printed list of names and addresses and did not (on this record) infer there were no interested parties; instead it declined to add to the statutory requirements and accepted the company’s certified statement as sufficient.

Why — the court’s reasoning tied to the opinion
- The statute requires the premium finance company to “certif[y]” that “Copies of the notice of cancellation were sent to all persons shown by the premium finance agreement … specifying the names and addresses of any governmental agencies, holders of a security interest … or third parties to whom the insurance premium finance company has sent notice of cancellation.” La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(iv) (quoted in the opinion).
- The actual APAC cancellation request (quoted in the opinion) said, in relevant part: “(4) Copies of the Ten Day Notice of Cancellation were sent to all persons shown by the Premium Finance Agreement to have an interest in any loss which may occur there under.” It also noted copies were enclosed and affidavit proof of mailing was available upon request.
- The court emphasized that Louisiana law requires “strict adherence” to the statute but also warned that courts should not judicially add requirements beyond the statute: “Strict adherence to the procedures for cancellation is not an invitation for courts to add to the procedures.” (Opinion, discussion of signature requirement.)
- Applying that principle, the Fifth Circuit rejected imposing extra-formalities (e.g., a named signatory) and concluded that the company can “certify information without a named employee being identified.” By parity, the court accepted the company’s certification that copies were sent to the interested parties rather than insisting on a separately itemized list in every instance.

What the opinion does and does not hold (important for doctrinal limits)
- The opinion does not hold that an express, itemized list of names and addresses can never be required under Louisiana law; it holds that in this case the statutorily required “certifying” was satisfied by APAC’s statement that copies were sent to all persons shown on the finance agreement (plus the enclosures/affidavit-available statement), and that courts should not add extra formal requirements not in the statute.
- The court also did not treat the absence of a list as meaning there were no interested parties; it accepted the certification as a substantive assertion by the finance company that the notices were sent to whoever the agreement showed as interested.

Bottom line for practice
If a premium finance company issues a certification repeating the statutory language that copies were sent to all persons shown by the finance agreement (and can produce the enclosures or proof of mailing on request), this opinion treats that as sufficient under § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(iv). A practitioner seeking to avoid dispute should still consider including an appended list of recipients and addresses or contemporaneous proof of mailing to make the factual record clear, but the Fifth Circuit here declined to require that extra step as a matter of statutory interpretation.
