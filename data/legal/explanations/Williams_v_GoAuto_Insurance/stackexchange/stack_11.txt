### Does 'receipt' require possession by the specific corporate entity (GoAuto) versus a management affiliate (GoAuto Management Services)?
Question:
Before 2017, the physical filing room was owned by GoAuto Management Services, not GoAuto; after 2017 an APAC employee stamped 'received' and placed the document in a shared cabinet. Since § 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i) requires 'receipt' by the insurer, how did the panel treat corporate separateness and agency between GoAuto and its management affiliate? Does receipt by a shared service entity count as receipt by the insurer under Louisiana law?
Answer:
Short answer
The panel did not treat the management affiliate’s possession as automatically satisfying the statute by imputing receipt to GoAuto. Instead it treated “receipt” as functional possession by the insurer and held that GoAuto’s own computer/web‑service receiving APAC’s electronic cancellation request satisfied the statute. The opinion therefore accepts that a shared‑service handling can suffice, but only because GoAuto itself (via its computer/web service) took possession — not because the affiliate’s filing room or an affiliate employee’s stamp alone converted the affiliate’s possession into GoAuto’s receipt.

Explanation and support from the opinion
- Corporate separateness/agency: The opinion explicitly notes the corporate distinction and that employees performing services for both companies “are employed by GoAuto Management Services, LLC.” It does not treat this fact as rendering the affiliate’s possession the insurer’s receipt. The plaintiffs argued the shared filing room (owned pre‑2017 by GoAuto Management Services) and the APAC employee who stamps documents post‑2017 meant GoAuto never “received” the notice. The panel considered that argument but did not adopt the view that affiliate possession automatically counts as insurer receipt. (See Facts ¶¶ describing shared filing room and personnel; Discussion § I (receipt argument).)

- How the panel defined “receipt”: The court looked to common law definitions (Black’s) — “the act of receiving something, esp. by taking physical possession” — and concluded that “GoAuto may cancel a policy once it takes possession, delivery, or custody of a request for cancellation.” The decisive fact for the panel was that APAC’s system “makes a call to a GoAuto web service to request cancellation,” and “when it hears its name . . . it performs the activity.” The GoAuto computer system receives the e‑mailed notice in the early morning and automatically cancels the policy. The court held that this method of the insurer’s computer receiving and acting on the notice “satisfies the statute.” (Discussion § I, paragraphs defining receipt and describing the web service interaction.)

- What the panel did not do: The panel declined to rely on a district‑court theory that an APAC employee delivering printed notices “neatly fits the definition of private carrier,” and expressly said it would not consider that alternative because it found the e‑mail/web‑service receipt adequate. The court also refused to require a human signature on the certification. It did not pierce the corporate veil or treat the affiliate’s custody as equivalent to GoAuto’s receipt absent evidence that GoAuto itself received or took custody (here, via its web service). (Discussion § I, final paragraphs.)

Practical takeaway
Under this opinion, receipt under La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i) is receipt by the insurer — meaning the insurer (or its system) must take possession, delivery, or custody of the cancellation request. A management affiliate’s mere possession or stamping does not automatically satisfy the statute; it will count only if the insurer itself has taken possession (which in modern practice may occur when the insurer’s computer/web service receives and acts on an electronic notice).
