Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Can 'credit card services' and 'banking services' be considered 'legally identical' if one party doesn't offer credit cards?
Question:
Apex Bank does not offer credit cards, only banking and financing services. However, the Board found the parties' services to be 'legally identical, in part.' The court affirmed this finding under DuPont Factor 2. How can services be legally identical when one party's business model (issuing credit cards via a bank partner) differs from the other's (direct banking services), especially when Apex explicitly stated they do not offer credit cards?
Answer:
**Short Answer:** Yes. Under *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, services can be found "legally identical, in part" even if one party does not currently offer the specific product, provided the legal definition of their broader service category encompasses that product. The Federal Circuit affirmed the Board's finding because the dictionary definitions of "banking" and "finance" include extending credit via credit cards, creating a legal overlap regardless of Apex's specific business model.

### The Legal Standard: DuPont Factor 2

To understand this holding, you must look at the standard for the second *DuPont* factor (similarity of goods/services). As the opinion states in **Section III.A**, the Federal Circuit clarified that:

> "The services need not be identical—the evidence need only establish that 'the respective products are related in some manner and/or [that] the circumstances surrounding their marketing are such that they could give rise to the mistaken belief that they emanate from the same source.'"

The Court cited *Coach Servs. Inc. v. Triumph Learning LLC* for the proposition that strict identity is not required. However, in this specific case, the Board went further and found the services were "legally identical, in part."

### Why the Court Affirmed "Legally Identical"

Your confusion stems from the fact that Apex explicitly does not issue credit cards. However, the Board’s analysis (affirmed by the Court) relied on the **legal scope of the service classifications**, not just the specific current offerings of the applicant.

The opinion outlines the Board's reasoning in **Section III.A**:

1.  **Broad Definition of Credit Card Services:** The Board defined "credit card services" to include "issuing credit cards for use to finance purchases... managing credit card accounts, and providing access to credit scores" (J.A. 25).
2.  **Dictionary Definitions of Banking:** Crucially, the Board looked at the dictionary definitions for "banking," "bank," and "finance." The opinion notes:
    > "The Board determined that because the dictionary definitions for 'banking,' 'bank,' and 'finance' encompass extending credit or providing funds through the issuance of credit cards, Apex and CC Serve’s services are 'legally identical, in part.'" (J.A. 27–28)
3.  **Substantial Evidence:** The Court reviewed this finding for "substantial evidence." Because the legal definition of Apex's service category ("banking and financing services") encompasses the specific activity CC Serve performs ("credit card services"), there is a legal overlap.

### Reconciling with Apex's Business Model

You asked how this holds when Apex "explicitly stated they do not offer credit cards." The opinion addresses this in **Section III.A** when discussing Apex's argument on appeal:

> "Apex argues that the parties' services are 'not the same and . . . are not directly competitive with each other,' especially because CC Serve is partnering with banks to manage credit card programs—'not providing banking services' itself... We find Apex's argument unavailing."

The Court rejected the argument that the lack of direct competition or current product overlap negates the legal similarity of the service categories. The determination of "legally identical, in part" rests on the fact that **banking services legally include the capacity to issue credit cards**, even if Apex chooses not to exercise that capacity currently.

### Conclusion

The Court affirmed the Board's finding because **DuPont Factor 2 assesses the relationship between the service categories**, not merely the specific product mix of the companies involved. Since the legal definition of "banking and financing services" encompasses "issuing credit cards," the services are legally identical in that specific aspect, satisfying the standard for similarity under the Lanham Act.

*Note: While the Court affirmed Factor 2, it vacated Factors 1 and 6 because the Board inconsistently narrowed the scope of "similar services" for those factors (see Section III.B).*
