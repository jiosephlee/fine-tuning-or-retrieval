Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### Does a remand on DuPont Factor 6 automatically require a remand on Factor 1 regarding commercial impression?
Question:
The court vacated Factor 1 (similarity of marks) solely because reconsideration of Factor 6 (third-party marks) 'may result in a different determination of the mark's commercial strength or weakness and affect the overall commercial impression.' Is there a binding precedent that links the strength of a mark (Factor 6) directly to the commercial impression analysis (Factor 1) in this manner?
Answer:
Based on the opinion in *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the Federal Circuit treated the remand of DuPont Factor 6 as necessitating a remand of Factor 1, but this requirement was grounded in the specific interdependence of mark strength and commercial impression rather than a universal rule applicable to all Factor 6 remands.

**The Legal Standard and Factors**
The opinion defines the first DuPont factor as an analysis of the "similarity or dissimilarity of the marks in their entireties as to appearance, sound, connotation and commercial impression." The Court notes that this factor focuses on whether consumer confusion is likely based on the overall "commercial impression."

The sixth DuPont factor considers the "number and nature of similar marks in use on similar goods." Citing *Palm Bay Imps.* and *Juice Generation*, the Court explains that evidence under this factor is relevant to demonstrate that a mark is "relatively weak and entitled to only a narrow scope of protection."

**The Linkage in *Apex Bank***
In Section III.C, the Court explicitly links the two factors. The Board had erred in Factor 6 by improperly narrowing the scope of third-party marks considered. The Court reasoned that correcting this error meant "reconsideration of the sixth DuPont factor may result in a different determination of the mark’s commercial strength or weakness."

Because the Court found that "commercial impression informs the analysis under the first DuPont factor," and because the strength of the mark affects that impression, the Court concluded it "must similarly vacate the Board’s analysis" of Factor 1.

**Conclusion**
*Apex Bank* establishes that a remand on Factor 6 requires a remand on Factor 1 when the corrected Factor 6 analysis could alter the assessment of the mark's strength. While the opinion does not state this is "automatic" in every instance, it holds that when Factor 6 evidence informs the mark's strength, and that strength informs the commercial impression, the two factors are legally intertwined, necessitating a joint remand to ensure the commercial impression analysis reflects the corrected strength assessment.
