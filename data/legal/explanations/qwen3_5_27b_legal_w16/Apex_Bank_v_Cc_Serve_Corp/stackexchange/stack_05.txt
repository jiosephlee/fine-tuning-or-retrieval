Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### What is the proper methodology for defining services when a Trademark ID Manual entry has been deleted?
Question:
The Board noted that the entry for 'credit card services' was deleted from the Trademark ID Manual after CC Serve's registration. Consequently, the Board used dictionary definitions to define the services. Is this the standard approach for defining goods and services in an opposition proceeding when the official manual classification has changed or been removed?
Answer:
Based on the opinion in *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the Federal Circuit affirmed the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board’s methodology of using dictionary definitions to define services when the relevant Trademark ID Manual entry has been deleted.

In this case, the Board faced a situation where the entry for "credit card services" was deleted from the Trademark ID Manual after CC Serve’s registration had already issued. To determine the scope of CC Serve’s services for the likelihood-of-confusion analysis, the Board relied on dictionary definitions. The Board defined "credit card services" to encompass "issuing credit cards for use to finance purchases as well as counseling regarding credit card debt, processing credit card payments and transactions, credit card authorization, credit card monitoring and alerts, managing credit card accounts, and providing access to credit scores."

The Federal Circuit reviewed this determination under the **second *DuPont* factor**, which assesses the similarity of the parties’ goods and services. The Court noted that services need not be identical; rather, the evidence must establish that the products are related in some manner or that the marketing circumstances could give rise to a mistaken belief of a common source.

The Court held that substantial evidence supported the Board’s finding that the parties’ services were highly similar based on the definitions used. Specifically, the opinion states: "Because the entry for 'credit card services' was deleted from the Trademark ID Manual after CC Serve’s registration, the Board first determined the meaning of the identified services... We affirm the Board’s finding as to the second DuPont factor."

However, the opinion provides an important caveat regarding the consistency of this definition across other factors. While the Court affirmed the definition used for the second factor, it vacated the Board’s analysis of the **sixth *DuPont* factor** (the number and nature of similar marks in use on similar goods). The Court reasoned that because the Board had already found the services to be "highly similar" (and partially "legally identical") under the second factor, it erred by narrowing the scope of "similar goods" too strictly when considering third-party marks under the sixth factor. The Court instructed that the Board should retain the same scope of similarity across factors.

**Summary for Practice:**
According to *Apex Bank*, using dictionary definitions to define services is a permissible methodology when the Trademark ID Manual entry is no longer available, provided the resulting definition is supported by substantial evidence. However, once a scope of similarity is established for one *DuPont* factor, that scope should generally remain consistent when analyzing other factors, such as the commercial strength of the mark based on third-party use.
