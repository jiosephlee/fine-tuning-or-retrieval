Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### Why does the Federal Circuit apply regional circuit law when reviewing a denial of JMOL in a patent case?
Question:
In *Finesse Wireless v. AT&T*, the court explicitly states it reviews the district court's denial of JMOL under the law of the regional circuit (here, the Fifth Circuit), yet it reviews claim construction de novo under Federal Circuit law. I am confused about the jurisdictional split here. Since patent law is supposed to be national to promote uniformity, why does the standard for reviewing a procedural motion like JMOL depend on the regional circuit, while substantive patent issues do not? Does this create a risk of forum shopping based on the regional circuit's JMOL standards?
Answer:
### Answer

Based on the opinion in *Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC*, the Federal Circuit applies regional circuit law to the denial of a Judgment as a Matter of Law (JMOL) because it treats the standard of review for that procedural motion as governed by the law of the regional circuit from which the appeal originates, whereas it treats claim construction as a substantive question of patent law subject to *de novo* review.

The opinion explicitly distinguishes between these two standards in the **Discussion** section. Regarding the JMOL motion, the court states:

> "We review a district court’s denial of JMOL under the law of the regional circuit, here the Fifth Circuit. *Wi-LAN, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, 811 F.3d 455, 461 (Fed. Cir. 2016)."

In contrast, regarding the interpretation of the patent claims, the court states:

> "We review... claim construction *de novo* and any underlying factual findings supporting the district court’s construction for clear error. *Wi-LAN*, 811 F.3d at 461."

**The Doctrinal Split**
The opinion demonstrates that this split is a matter of established precedent (citing *Wi-LAN*) rather than an ad hoc decision. The Federal Circuit applies regional circuit law to the JMOL standard because it concerns the sufficiency of the evidence and the jury's role, which are procedural matters tied to the district court's jurisdiction. However, the Federal Circuit reserves *de novo* review for claim construction because it involves the interpretation of federal patent law, which the Federal Circuit oversees to ensure national uniformity.

**Regarding Forum Shopping**
While the opinion does not explicitly discuss the policy implications of forum shopping regarding JMOL standards, it applies the regional standard consistently regardless of the substantive patent outcome. The court notes that while the *standard* for reviewing the JMOL denial comes from the regional circuit (the Fifth Circuit's *de novo* standard), the Federal Circuit still applies its own test for the underlying factual finding:

> "We review a jury’s finding of infringement for substantial evidence. *Apple Inc. v. Wi-LAN Inc.*, 25 F.4th 960, 969 (Fed. Cir. 2022)."

Thus, while the procedural hurdle for overturning a JMOL denial varies by region, the Federal Circuit retains oversight over whether the jury's infringement finding is supported by substantial evidence under Federal Circuit law.
