Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### Why did the Federal Circuit vacate the damages award rather than remand for a new damages trial after reversing the infringement finding?
Question:
The court reversed the denial of JMOL of noninfringement for all asserted claims and subsequently vacated the damages award. I would have expected the court to remand for a new trial on damages, perhaps to see if there was infringement on non-asserted claims or if a different damages calculation was possible. Why is the procedural remedy simply vacatur when the underlying infringement finding is overturned? Does the vacatur imply that no damages are recoverable at all, or is it just clearing the slate for a potential retrial on infringement?
Answer:
**Short Answer:** The Federal Circuit vacated the damages award because the award was legally predicated on the jury's finding of infringement for *all* asserted claims. Since the Court reversed the denial of JMOL of noninfringement for every single claim that supported the damages calculation, there was no remaining legal basis for the award. A remand for a new damages trial would be futile without a finding of liability.

**Detailed Explanation**

The procedural remedy of vacatur in this context flows directly from the Court's disposition of the infringement claims. As the opinion states in the **Damages** section:

> "Because we reverse the denial of JMOL of noninfringement for all asserted claims of the ’134 and ’775 patents, we vacate the damages award."

This sentence establishes the causal link: the damages award cannot stand independently of the infringement findings. The jury awarded a lump sum for the "remaining life of the ’134 and ’775 patents" based on its finding that the accused radios infringed the specific claims asserted by Finesse.

**1. Why Vacatur Instead of Remand for Damages?**
A remand for a new damages trial is designed to recalculate compensation when liability exists but the amount is erroneous. Here, the Court found that the evidence was insufficient to support infringement on *any* of the asserted claims.
*   For the **'134 patent**, the Court held there was no substantial evidence that the accused radios sampled the required "interference generating signals."
*   For the **'775 patent**, the Court held no reasonable jury could find the accused radios performed the "seven claimed multiplications."

Because the Court reversed the infringement finding for **all** asserted claims, the predicate for the damages award was eliminated. There is no damages calculation to be performed if there is no infringement.

**2. Does Vacatur Imply No Damages Are Recoverable?**
For the claims at issue in this litigation, yes. The vacatur signifies that the $166,303,391 award is nullified because the liability supporting it has been overturned. The opinion does not discuss non-asserted claims or potential future litigation; it strictly addresses the claims asserted in this action. Since the Court reversed the JMOL denial for "all asserted claims," the damages award tied to those claims is extinguished.

**3. Is This Clearing the Slate for a Retrial on Infringement?**
Based on the standard applied in the opinion, a retrial on infringement is unlikely. The Court reviews the denial of JMOL under the Fifth Circuit standard, reversing only if "reasonable jurors could not arrive at any contrary conclusion."

By reversing the denial of JMOL, the Court concluded that the evidence was legally insufficient to support the jury's verdict ("the jury’s infringement verdict is not supported by substantial evidence"). Unlike a grant of a new trial, which implies the evidence was conflicting or the trial was flawed but a retrial might yield a different result, a JMOL reversal indicates that no reasonable jury could find infringement based on the evidence presented. Therefore, the vacatur effectively ends the case on these claims rather than resetting the slate for a second attempt at proving infringement.
