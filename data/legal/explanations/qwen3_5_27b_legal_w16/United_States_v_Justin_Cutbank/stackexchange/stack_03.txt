Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### How does the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) interact with USSG § 4B1.4(b) when calculating the Guidelines range?
Question:
The opinion states that the jury found Cutbank had three prior violent felonies, triggering ACCA penalties. However, the PSR calculated a base offense level under § 2K2.1 resulting in a level of 35, while the 'Armed Career Criminal Guideline' (§ 4B1.4) would have produced level 34. The court says 'The Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies.' I thought ACCA imposed a mandatory statutory minimum of 15 years. How does the Guidelines calculation interact with the ACCA statutory minimum? Does the Guidelines level determine the starting point for the statutory range, or are they separate calculations that just happen to overlap?
Answer:
Based on *United States v. Cutbank*, the interaction between the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA) and the Sentencing Guidelines is governed by a "greater offense level" rule for the Guidelines calculation, while the statutory penalty is triggered separately by the jury's finding.

### 1. The Guidelines Calculation: The "Greater Offense Level" Rule
The opinion clarifies that when a defendant is subject to ACCA penalties, the court must calculate the Guidelines range using the higher of the applicable offense levels.

In *Cutbank*, the Presentence Investigation Report (PSR) calculated an offense level of 35 under § 2K2.1 (felon in possession), while the specific ACCA Guideline (§ 4B1.4) would have produced a level of 34. The Eighth Circuit affirmed the district court's application of the higher level, citing the explicit instruction in the Guidelines:

> "The Guidelines provide that the greater offense level applies. See USSG § 4B1.4(b)."

This resolves the calculation conflict: the court does not simply use the ACCA Guideline (§ 4B1.4) because the statute was triggered; it compares the specific offense guideline (§ 2K2.1) against the ACCA guideline and selects the higher number to determine the advisory range.

### 2. Statutory Trigger vs. Guidelines Range
The opinion treats the statutory enhancement and the Guidelines calculation as distinct but converging steps in the sentencing process:

*   **Statutory Trigger:** The jury’s finding of three prior violent felonies triggered the "enhanced penalties" of the ACCA statute.
*   **Guidelines Calculation:** The PSR calculated the advisory range based on the "greater offense level" rule described above.
*   **Sentencing:** The district court imposed a sentence "within the advisory guidelines range."

The opinion emphasizes that the Guidelines range serves as the starting point for the sentencing proceeding. Citing *Gall v. United States*, the court notes:

> "a district court should begin all sentencing proceedings by correctly calculating the applicable Guidelines range."

While the opinion does not explicitly detail the mathematical interaction between the statutory minimum and the Guidelines floor, it confirms that the sentence imposed (292 months) was determined to be reasonable because it fell within the calculated Guidelines range, which was derived using the higher offense level.

### Summary
To answer your specific question: According to *Cutbank*, the Guidelines calculation and the statutory ACCA trigger are separate calculations that interact at the sentencing phase. The jury finding activates the statute, but the **Guidelines level determines the advisory range** by applying the "greater offense level" rule under § 4B1.4(b). The court then sentences within that advisory range, ensuring the sentence complies with the statutory enhancement while adhering to the Guidelines framework.
