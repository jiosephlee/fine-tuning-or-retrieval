Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Does USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) require a separate conviction for the 'another felony offense' enhancement?
Question:
In the sentencing section, the court applied a four-level enhancement under § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) for possession 'in connection with another felony offense.' The commentary says this applies 'regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought, or a conviction obtained.' The facts mention Cutbank threatened his girlfriend and barricaded himself, which sounds like domestic assault or kidnapping, but I don't see a conviction for those specific acts in the opinion. How can the court enhance the sentence based on a 'felony offense' if he wasn't charged or convicted of that underlying felony? What is the burden of proof for that underlying conduct at sentencing?
Answer:
**No, a separate conviction is not required.**

Under *United States v. Cutbank*, 8th Cir. 2025, the application of the four-level enhancement under USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) does not depend on whether the defendant was charged with or convicted of the underlying "another felony offense."

### The Legal Standard
As the Eighth Circuit explains in Section III of the opinion, the relevant authority is found in the Commentary to the Sentencing Guidelines. Specifically, **§ 2K2.1, comment. (n.14(C))** states that the enhancement applies if the defendant used or possessed a firearm in connection with another federal, state, or local felony offense:

> "regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought, or a conviction obtained."

This language explicitly decouples the sentencing enhancement from the requirement of a separate criminal conviction for the underlying conduct.

### Application in *Cutbank*
In *Cutbank*, the defendant argued that the enhancement was improperly applied because he was not separately convicted of the underlying acts (such as domestic abuse or kidnapping). The court rejected this argument.

1.  **Factual Basis:** The Presentence Investigation Report (PSR) detailed conduct where Cutbank pointed a gun at his girlfriend, threatened her life, and barricaded himself with police. The appellate court noted that the offense of conviction involved "domestic abuse, kidnaping, assault in the victim’s home in the presence of her children, and injuring a police officer."
2.  **Procedural Adoption:** At sentencing, the district court "adopted, without objection, the PSR’s factual statements." Because the facts in the PSR established the underlying felony conduct, the court found no error in applying the enhancement.
3.  **Standard of Review:** The Eighth Circuit reviewed the district court's finding for clear error, citing *United States v. Dunn*. Since the PSR facts were adopted without objection and supported by the record, the appellate court affirmed that the facts established the gun was used in connection with another felony.

### Summary for Law Students
When analyzing § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B):
*   **Conviction Status:** A separate conviction for the underlying felony is **not** necessary.
*   **Proof Requirement:** The enhancement turns on whether the *conduct* constitutes a felony, not on the procedural status of a charge for that conduct.
*   **Process:** If the PSR facts describing the underlying felony are adopted by the district court (often without objection), the enhancement applies based on the Commentary's directive.

The *Cutbank* opinion confirms that the Guidelines Commentary controls here, allowing the sentencing court to enhance the penalty based on the factual circumstances of the offense, even absent a specific conviction for those circumstances.
