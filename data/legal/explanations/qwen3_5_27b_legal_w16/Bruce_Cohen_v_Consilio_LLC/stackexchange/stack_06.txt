Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

### What is the procedural effect of a concurring opinion that vacates issues the majority affirms?
Question:
The majority opinion concludes with 'We otherwise affirm,' but Judge Kobes' concurrence states, 'I also vacate the district court's ruling as to attorney’s fees, costs, and prejudgment interest.' As a student, I'm confused about the binding effect of this. Does the concurrence's view on fees become part of the mandate, or does the majority's 'affirm' control? How does the Eighth Circuit handle discrepancies between the majority judgment and a concurrence regarding specific remedies?
Answer:
**The majority opinion controls the judgment and mandate; the concurrence does not.**

In *Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC*, the procedural effect of Judge Kobes' concurrence is limited to his individual view. It does not alter the binding judgment of the Eighth Circuit, which is established by the majority opinion.

**1. The Majority Opinion Constitutes the Judgment**
The text of the opinion distinguishes between the Court's holding and a judge's individual concurrence. The majority opinion concludes with the operative directive:

> "We vacate the district court’s order regarding Cohen’s claim under the MFLSA and remand for further proceedings. **We otherwise affirm.**"

This "We otherwise affirm" language is the judgment of the Court. It indicates that all issues not explicitly vacated (such as the MPWA, MWTA, and injunctive relief claims) are affirmed. Because the majority did not explicitly vacate the ruling on attorney’s fees, costs, or prejudgment interest, those items fall within the scope of the "otherwise affirm" directive.

**2. Concurrences Do Not Alter the Mandate**
Judge Kobes' opinion is labeled "KOBES, Circuit Judge, **concurring specially**." Unlike the majority, which speaks for the Court using "We," the concurrence speaks for the judge using "I" ("I also vacate...").

In appellate procedure, a concurring opinion explains the judge's reasoning or additional views but does not modify the majority's disposition unless the majority opinion itself incorporates the concurrence's language into the final order. Here, the majority's final order is distinct and separate from the concurrence. Therefore, the district court must follow the majority's mandate to affirm the fees, costs, and interest, not the concurrence's request to vacate them.

**3. Application to the Mandate**
When the Eighth Circuit issues its mandate to the district court, it will reflect the majority's disposition:
*   **MFLSA Claim:** Vacated and remanded (per majority).
*   **Fees, Costs, Interest:** Affirmed (per majority's "otherwise affirm").
*   **Concurrence View:** Not binding on the lower court.

**Conclusion**
The majority's "We otherwise affirm" controls. The concurrence's view on fees does not become part of the mandate. The district court must proceed based on the majority's order to affirm the fees, costs, and prejudgment interest.
