Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Jimenez v. Bondi"

### What is the scope of review when the BIA affirms an IJ decision without a separate opinion?
Question:
In Section II.A, the court states that because a single BIA member affirmed the IJ's decision, they review the BIA's decision but are 'not precluded from consulting the IJ's more complete explanation.' Procedurally, how does the appellate court weigh the BIA's brief affirmation against the IJ's detailed findings? If the BIA affirms without explanation, does the IJ's reasoning become the primary basis for the substantial evidence review, or does the lack of BIA reasoning limit the scope of what can be challenged?
Answer:
**Short Answer:** When the BIA affirms an IJ decision without a separate opinion, the appellate court reviews the BIA's decision but is permitted to consult the IJ's opinion for the detailed factual reasoning. The lack of a separate BIA opinion does not limit the scope of review; rather, the IJ's findings are treated as the explanation for the BIA's affirmation and are subject to the same substantial evidence standard.

### The Standard of Review

In *Jimenez v. Bondi*, the Tenth Circuit explicitly addresses this procedural posture in **Section II.A (Standard of Review)**. The court states:

> "Because a single member of the BIA affirmed the IJ’s decision, we review the BIA’s decision, but 'we are not precluded from consulting the IJ’s more complete explanation of those same grounds.'"

This establishes two key principles for your question:
1.  **The Final Order is the BIA's:** Technically, the court is reviewing the Board's decision, not the IJ's.
2.  **The IJ's Reasoning is Consulted:** The court is not restricted to the BIA's bare affirmation. It looks to the IJ's opinion to understand the "grounds" for the decision.

### How the Court Weighs the Findings

Regarding your question on how the court weighs the BIA's brief affirmation against the IJ's detailed findings, *Jimenez* demonstrates that the court treats the IJ's reasoning as the substantive basis for the BIA's affirmation.

Throughout the discussion, the court does not isolate the BIA's decision from the IJ's findings. Instead, it applies the **substantial evidence standard** to the record supporting both. For example:

*   **In Section II.D (Nexus):** The court writes, "there is substantial evidence supporting the **BIA and IJ’s findings** that Petitioners were primarily targeted because Jimenez threatened Don Rafa’s revenue and security."
*   **In Section II.E (Government Protection):** The court notes, "Again, there is substantial evidence in the record supporting the **BIA’s and IJ’s findings**."

This indicates that when the BIA affirms without explanation, the IJ's detailed findings effectively become the agency's reasoned decision for appellate review purposes. The court does not view the lack of BIA reasoning as a limitation on what can be challenged; rather, it reviews the administrative record to ensure that the IJ's findings (which the BIA adopted) are supported by substantial evidence.

### Conclusion

To answer your specific procedural question: The IJ's reasoning becomes the primary basis for the factual review because the BIA has adopted it by affirmation. As *Jimenez* confirms, the appellate court is "not precluded from consulting the IJ's more complete explanation." Therefore, the scope of review is not limited by the BIA's silence; the court reviews the IJ's detailed findings under the substantial evidence standard as if they were the BIA's own.
