Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### Does 'certifying' a statement in a statute legally necessitate a physical signature if the text is silent?
Question:
The court emphasizes 'strict adherence' to the Louisiana statute but then concludes that a signature is not required because the word 'certifying' doesn't explicitly mention one. I'm struggling to understand the boundary here: if the court is being 'strict,' why are they allowing the omission of a signature? At what point does 'strict adherence' turn into 'judicial interpretation' of what the legislature intended?
Answer:
### Answer

In the context of the provided opinion, "certifying" a statement does **not** legally necessitate a physical signature if the statute is silent on the matter. The court’s logic rests on a standard of statutory interpretation where "strict adherence" means the court must follow the text as written—including its omissions—without adding external requirements.

#### The Doctrine of Plain Meaning
The court applies a "plain meaning" approach to statutory interpretation. Under this principle, if the legislature’s intent is clear from the text, the court should not look beyond the words provided. The court notes that "the statute makes no explicit reference to a signature." Consequently, because the legislature chose not to include a signature requirement in the mandatory list of procedures, the court cannot "read into" the statute a requirement that isn't there.

#### Understanding "Strict Adherence" vs. "Judicial Interpretation"
Your confusion stems from how the term "strict adherence" is being used. In many legal contexts, students associate "strict" with a rigorous application of all possible implications of a word. However, in this opinion, the court defines "strict adherence" as a **limitation on judicial discretion**.

The court explicitly states:
> “Strict adherence” to the procedures for cancellation is not an invitation for courts to add to the procedures.

When the court says it is being "strict," it means it is refusing to engage in the type of "judicial interpretation" you are concerned about. To the court, "strict adherence" means:
1.  **Strict inclusion:** The finance company *must* include all four specific points of certification listed in La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(i)–(iv).
2.  **Strict exclusion:** The court *cannot* require any additional formalities (like a signature) that the legislature did not explicitly demand.

#### The Identity of the "Certifier"
The court further supports this by looking at the subject of the verb. The statute states that it is "the insurance premium finance company" who effects cancellation by "certifying" certain information. Because the statute attributes the act of certification to the **entity** (the company) rather than an **individual**, the court finds no reason to require a specific person's signature to validate the company's certification.

#### Summary
The court is not being "lenient" by omitting the signature; rather, it is being "strict" by refusing to expand the statute’s requirements beyond the provided text. In this framework, a signature would be an "extra" requirement. By holding that no signature is needed, the court is strictly adhering to the text's silence.
