Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### What is the difference between the 'substantial evidence' standard and the 'overwhelming' evidence standard in this opinion?
Question:
I'm confused about the standard of review. The court says it reviews the district court’s denial of JMOL of noninfringement under a standard where it reverses only if the evidence points 'so strongly and overwhelmingly' to one side that a reasonable jury could not arrive at any contrary conclusion. However, it says it reviews the jury’s finding of infringement for 'substantial evidence.' Why are there two different standards being applied to the same underlying issue of infringement?
Answer:
The distinction between the two standards arises from the different **procedural postures** the court is reviewing. Although both standards involve the same underlying facts (whether the defendants' radios infringed the patents), the court is evaluating two different legal questions: one regarding the validity of the jury's verdict and the other regarding the trial court's decision to let that verdict stand.

**1. The "Substantial Evidence" Standard (Reviewing the Jury's Verdict)**
The court uses the "substantial evidence" standard to review the jury’s actual finding of infringement. Under this standard, the appellate court defers to the jury's role as the finder of fact. The court will only reverse the finding if there was not enough evidence for a reasonable jury to have reached that conclusion. Essentially, this standard asks: *"Was the jury's conclusion reasonable based on the evidence presented?"*

**2. The "Overwhelming" Standard (Reviewing the Denial of JMOL)**
The court uses the "overwhelming" standard to review the district court’s **denial of a Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law (JMOL)**. A JMOL motion is a request for the judge to rule in favor of one party immediately because the opposing party's evidence is so weak that no reasonable jury could possibly find in their favor. 

Because the district court *denied* the defendants' motion to have the case decided by the judge, the appellate court must decide if the district court made a mistake. To reverse that denial, the appellate court requires a much higher burden: the evidence must be so one-sided that "reasonable jurors could not arrive at any contrary conclusion." Essentially, this standard asks: *"Was the evidence so lopsided that the judge should have stopped the trial and ruled for the defendants immediately?"*

**Summary of the Difference**
The confusion stems from the fact that these are two different "hurdles":

*   **Substantial Evidence** is the hurdle the plaintiff's evidence had to clear to get a jury verdict. If the evidence was "substantial," the verdict stands.
*   **Overwhelming Evidence** is the hurdle the defendants' arguments had to clear to get the judge to overrule that jury verdict. Because the defendants failed to show that the evidence was "overwhelmingly" in their favor, the district court was correct to let the jury's "substantial" evidence-based verdict stand. 

In this specific opinion, the court ultimately found that the evidence was **not** substantial enough to support the infringement verdict (specifically regarding the '134 and '775 patents), leading it to reverse the denial of JMOL.
