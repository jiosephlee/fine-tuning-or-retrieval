Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer

### Could the district court properly remand to OSC for reconsideration if Article III standing was lacking from the start?
Question:
The district court said OSC committed a legal error in reading § 552(a)(4)(F)(i) to limit § 1216, but then declined to order an investigation and instead remanded to OSC. The D.C. Circuit vacated for lack of standing. Under Steel Co., courts must assure jurisdiction before reaching the merits. Was the district court’s remand itself a merits-based action taken without jurisdiction? More generally, can a court remand to an agency for further consideration if the plaintiff lacks standing, or must it dismiss outright without any substantive guidance?
Answer:
Short answer: No. Under Steel Co. and the D.C. Circuit’s decision in this case, a court must resolve Article III standing before reaching the merits, and it may not enter a merits‑based remand while lacking jurisdiction. Because the district court reached a legal conclusion on OSC’s authority and remanded for reconsideration without first ensuring AFL had Article III standing, the appellate court vacated and instructed dismissal for lack of subject‑matter jurisdiction.

Why: the governing rule and how the opinion applies it
- Article III standing is a threshold, jurisdictional requirement: courts must assure a “case or controversy” before resolving the merits. The panel expressly invoked Steel Co. at the start of Part II: “Before reaching the merits, we must consider whether AFL satisfied the requirements for Article III standing, a question bearing on whether the district court had subject‑matter jurisdiction. Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Env’t, 523 U.S. 83, 101–02 (1998).”
- The district court here concluded OSC had committed a legal error in reading the statutes and then remanded to OSC for further consideration. That was a merits determination (it resolved the statutory question and directed further agency action founded on that resolution).
- The appellate opinion holds that AFL lacked Article III standing and therefore that the district court lacked subject‑matter jurisdiction to enter its merits‑based judgment/remand. The court’s ultimate remedy was to “vacate the district court’s judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss for lack of subject‑matter jurisdiction.”

Bottom line for practice
- If a plaintiff lacks Article III standing, the district court has no power to grant merits relief or to remand on the basis of a merits ruling; the proper course is dismissal for lack of subject‑matter jurisdiction. The D.C. Circuit’s disposition in America First Legal Foundation v. Jamieson Greer embodies that rule: resolve standing first; if it fails, vacate any merits disposition and dismiss.
