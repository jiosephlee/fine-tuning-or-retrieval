Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### What does 'legally identical, in part' mean in the DuPont factor analysis for similarity of services?
Question:
In Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp., the Board found that Apex's 'banking and financing services' and CC Serve's 'credit card services' are 'legally identical, in part' because dictionary definitions of 'banking' and 'finance' encompass extending credit through credit cards. I'm confused about what 'legally identical, in part' means as a legal standard. Does it mean the services overlap but aren't fully identical? And how does this relate to the test from Coach Services that products need only be 'related in some manner'? Is 'legally identical' a higher degree of similarity than 'related,' and does it automatically trigger a finding that the factor weighs in favor of confusion, or is it just one piece of evidence?
Answer:
---

**Short Answer**

"Legally identical, in part" is a factual finding that the parties' services share a common legal scope—here, because the dictionary definitions of "banking" and "finance" encompass the extension of credit through credit cards. It is a higher degree of similarity than merely being "related in some manner" under *Coach Services*, but it does not automatically resolve the DuPont factor; it is evidence that the Board weighs alongside other evidence to determine whether the factor favors confusion.

---

**Explanation**

**1. What "legally identical, in part" means**

The Board used "legally identical, in part" to describe a specific factual finding: that a portion of Apex's identified services—"[b]anking and financing services"—legally encompasses what CC Serve does—issuing credit cards to finance purchases. The Board reached this conclusion by consulting dictionary definitions of "banking," "bank," and "finance," each of which includes extending credit or providing funds through credit cards. Because the *legal scope* of Apex's services overlaps with CC Serve's services in this respect, the services are "legally identical" as to that overlapping part, even though Apex offers a broader array of services (checking accounts, mortgages, loans) and does not itself offer credit cards.

So your intuition is essentially correct: the services overlap but are not fully identical. The phrase captures partial overlap at the level of legal definition rather than complete identity of all services offered.

**2. Relationship to the *Coach Services* "related in some manner" standard**

The *Coach Services* formulation—that products need only be "related in some manner"—sets the threshold for the second DuPont factor: services need not be identical or directly competitive. The Board's finding of "legally identical, in part" is a stronger conclusion than "related." It means that a subset of the services is not merely related but is legally the *same* service. The Federal Circuit affirmed this finding, noting that "substantial evidence supports the Board's finding that the parties' services are highly similar."

So yes, "legally identical" represents a higher degree of similarity than "related." The *Coach Services* standard is the floor; "legally identical, in part" is a finding well above that floor.

**3. Whether it automatically triggers a finding in favor of confusion**

No. The Board did not treat "legally identical, in part" as dispositive. It considered this finding alongside other evidence—specifically, third-party registrations that cover both credit card services and banking/financing services—to support its conclusion that "the services are of a type that may emanate from a single source under one mark." Only after weighing this combined evidence did the Board conclude that the second factor "weighed heavily in favor of finding likelihood of confusion."

The "legally identical, in part" finding is thus one important piece of the puzzle, not an automatic trigger. It strengthens the case for confusion, but the Board still needed to assess the totality of the evidence under the factor before reaching its conclusion.

**4. Why the distinction matters on appeal**

The Federal Circuit's disposition of the sixth DuPont factor illustrates why the scope of "similarity" matters. The Board found the services "highly similar—in fact, partially legally identical" under the second factor, but then applied a narrower definition of similarity under the sixth factor, limiting consideration to marks used for credit card services alone. The court held this was error: having already found the services highly similar (and partially legally identical), the Board could not impose "a different and more stringent legal standard for similarity" under the sixth factor. The court required the Board to maintain a consistent scope of similarity across factors on remand.

This confirms that "legally identical, in part" is a factual finding about the degree of overlap that carries forward across the DuPont analysis—it does not automatically resolve any single factor, but it shapes the framework within which each factor is evaluated.
