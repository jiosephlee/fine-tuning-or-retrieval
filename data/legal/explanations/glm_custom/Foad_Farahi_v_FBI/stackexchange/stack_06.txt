Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### Can a terse supplemental FOIA declaration cure an earlier detailed one, or must each declaration stand alone?
Question:
In Farahi, the court acknowledges that the Bender declaration, "considered in isolation, appears surprisingly terse"—it just says investigations "remain ongoing." But the court says context matters: the original Hardy declaration had detailed the investigation, and Bender's declaration was just an update. Farahi argued this was too conclusory under CREW. My question is: in FOIA Exemption 7(A) cases, does each declaration need to independently satisfy the government's burden, or can the court read a later declaration as a supplement to an earlier one? What if the earlier declaration is years old—can a one-line update really keep the burden satisfied?
Answer:
## Answer

**Short answer:** In the D.C. Circuit, FOIA declarations can be read together in context; a later supplemental declaration does not need to independently re-establish what an earlier, detailed declaration already established. But the court's willingness to accept a terse update depends on several contextual factors present in *Farahi*.

---

### The Governing Standard

Under Exemption 7(A), the government must show that withheld records were compiled for law enforcement purposes and that their disclosure "could reasonably be expected to interfere with enforcement proceedings" that are "pending or reasonably anticipated." *CREW v. DOJ*, 746 F.3d 1082, 1096 (D.C. Cir. 2014). The government may meet this burden by attesting to the "existence" of ongoing proceedings, and the FBI's invocation of Exemption 7 is entitled to deference, particularly in national-security contexts. *Id.* at 1099; *Ctr. for Nat'l Sec. Stud. v. DOJ*, 331 F.3d 918, 927–28 (D.C. Cir. 2003).

However, *CREW* also cautioned that a "vague nature" of an assertion that investigations remain ongoing may not always suffice. 746 F.3d at 1098. That is the principle Farahi invoked against the Bender declaration.

### How *Farahi* Resolves the Question

The court explicitly held that the Bender declaration should **not** be considered in isolation. Writing for the court, Judge Katsas acknowledged that "the Bender declaration, considered in isolation, appears surprisingly terse"—it stated only that investigations "remain ongoing." But the court explained that "context counsels against considering the Bender declaration in isolation" for three reasons:

1. **The earlier Hardy declarations had already established the predicate.** The public Hardy declaration attested to the existence of pending enforcement proceedings, and the ex parte Hardy declaration supplied "considerable detail about the nature and scope of the investigation." The Bender declaration was not submitted to establish pendency "from scratch."

2. **The purpose of the supplemental declaration was narrow.** The government submitted the Bender declaration "only to confirm that nothing had changed while its initial summary-judgment motion was pending." The district court had already concluded the FBI "seemingly" met its burden; it simply ordered an update due to the passage of time.

3. **The breadth of the underlying investigation lent plausibility.** The court noted the seriousness and scope of the FBI's public allegations against Farahi—which included connections to Khaled Sheikh Mohamed, Jose Padilla, the Holy Land Foundation, and others—as corroborating context that made the representation of ongoing investigation plausible.

Thus, the court concluded that the Bender declaration "can reasonably be understood as an update to the Hardy declarations, not as a standalone document."

### What This Means for the "Years-Old Declaration" Concern

Your concern about staleness is legitimate, and the district court shared it—that is precisely why it denied the first summary-judgment motion without prejudice and ordered the FBI to update its assessment. But *Farahi* establishes that the cure for staleness does not require the government to start over with a fully detailed new declaration. Rather:

- The **original** declaration must have established the Exemption 7(A) predicate with reasonable specificity.
- The **supplemental** declaration need only confirm that circumstances have not changed—provided the court can review the two together in context.
- The **plausibility** of the "still ongoing" representation may be bolstered by the nature and breadth of the underlying investigation, as reflected in the record.

That said, the court left room for the opposite result in different circumstances. If there had been no detailed prior declaration, or if the original declaration had been conclusory in the first instance, a terse one-line update likely would not suffice under *CREW*. The court's holding is best read as: **a supplemental declaration may be terse when it supplements, rather than replaces, an adequate prior submission.**
