### Did the panel apply the correct standards of review to the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) 'in connection with' enhancement?
Question:
The court cited Dunn for the standard of review and said the district court did not 'clearly err' in finding the gun was used in connection with another felony. My understanding is that guideline application often involves mixed questions: factual findings (clear error) and the legal interpretation of 'in connection with' (de novo). Did the panel conflate factual and legal components, or is Eighth Circuit precedent to review the 'in connection with' determination as a factual finding when the facts are undisputed?
Answer:
Short answer: The panel applied the correct Eighth Circuit approach. Where the district court adopts undisputed PSR facts and there is no legal challenge to the meaning of those facts, the “in connection with” enhancement is treated as a factual finding reviewed for clear error; if the parties dispute the legal meaning of the facts, the usual split (factual findings = clear error; legal questions = de novo) applies.

Explanation and authority from the opinion

- The opinion itself frames the issue as factual because “the district court adopted, without objection, the PSR’s factual statements regarding Cutback’s prior felony convictions.” It then explains why reversal is inappropriate: “Thus, the court did not err, much less clearly err, in finding that the facts in the PSR establish that the gun was used in connection with another felony. See United States v. Dunn, 723 F.3d 919, 929-30 (8th Cir. 2013) (standard of review).” (Sentencing Issue, ¶1–2.) That is the panel signaling it applied the clear-error standard to the enhancement determination because the factual predicate was undisputed and adopted.

- Legal doctrine the panel relied on: the usual division of review for guideline determinations — a district court’s factual findings are reviewed for clear error, and legal questions are reviewed de novo — is embedded in the Eighth Circuit’s Dunn precedent the panel cites. But the panel’s application here is consistent with treating the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) question as one turning on adopted factual findings (the PSR) rather than a contested legal interpretation of “in connection with.”

- Practical consequence shown in the opinion: because Cutbank did not object to the PSR facts and the court adopted them, the appellate court did not re-litigate the legal meaning of those facts; it reviewed whether the district court’s factual finding was clearly erroneous and found it was not. The opinion therefore concludes the enhancement was properly applied and that the court’s calculation error burden was not met.

How to read this for future cases

- If the factual record is uncontested and the district court adopts the PSR, the Eighth Circuit will treat the enhancement determination as a factual finding and apply clear-error review (as the panel did here).

- If a defendant contests either (a) the underlying facts relied on at sentencing, or (b) the legal standard or meaning to be applied to those facts (i.e., argues a particular set of facts cannot as a matter of law satisfy “in connection with”), the appellate court will have to decide whether a legal question is presented; if so, the legal component is reviewed de novo while any subsidiary factual findings remain subject to clear-error review.

Bottom line: The panel did not conflate standards; it applied clear-error review because the district court adopted undisputed PSR facts and invoked Dunn for that standard. If the factual predicate had been disputed or the parties had advanced a legal interpretation of “in connection with,” de novo review of that legal question would be appropriate.
