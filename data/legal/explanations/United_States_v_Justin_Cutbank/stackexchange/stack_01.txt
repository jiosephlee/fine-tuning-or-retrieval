Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: United States v. Justin Cutbank

### What makes prior sightings of the same gun “intrinsic” evidence rather than Rule 404(b) propensity evidence?
Question:
The panel affirmed allowing D.F. and C.F. to testify that they had seen Cutbank with the sawed-off Marlin .22 rifle before the night in question, calling it intrinsic evidence relevant to continuous possession and citing United States v. Battle. I'm confused about the boundary between intrinsic evidence and 404(b) 'other acts' propensity evidence. Why is prior possession of the same weapon considered intrinsic rather than a prior bad act offered to show character? What factors does the Eighth Circuit look at (same weapon, temporal proximity, narrative completeness) to label such evidence intrinsic, and how is that consistent with Rule 404(b)'s prohibition on propensity reasoning?
Answer:
Short answer: Because the witnesses’ testimony about having seen Cutbank with that same sawed-off Marlin rifle before the night in question went to a fact “intrinsic” to the charged offense—identity, ownership/control, and continuous possession of the very weapon at issue—rather than to some independent bad-act or character trait. The Eighth Circuit treated the testimony as directly relevant to proving possession of that specific gun (citing United States v. Battle), not as “other acts” offered to show propensity, so Rule 404(b) did not bar it.

Why that matters (legal doctrine and standard)
- Rule 404(b) governs “other acts” evidence offered for non‑propensity purposes (e.g., motive, intent, absence of mistake). It is exclusionary when prosecutors try to use prior bad acts to show the defendant’s character or propensity to commit the crime.
- By contrast, evidence that is part of the same transaction or that is “intrinsic” to the charged offense is not governed by Rule 404(b) and is admissible if relevant and not unfairly prejudicial. Courts ask whether the evidence is probative of an element of the charged offense (or necessary to give the jury a complete, non‑misleading narrative), rather than whether it merely shows the defendant acted in conformity with propensity.

What the opinion says and how the Eighth Circuit applies it
- The panel framed the issue explicitly: Cutbank argued D.F. and C.F. seeing him with the rifle earlier was propensity evidence under Rule 404(b), while the government and the district court said it was intrinsic to the possession charge. The court agreed with the government: “Prior possession of a firearm is directly relevant to proving later possession of that same weapon because it helps establish ownership or control of the weapon.” Opinion § II.A (quoting Battle, 774 F.3d at 511).
- The court pointed to the witnesses’ testimony that they had seen the same sawed‑off rifle with Cutbank a “handful of times” (D.F.) and “once or twice” (C.F.) before the incident. See ¶¶ II.A, II.C. That testimony ties the defendant to this particular weapon across time, supporting continuous possession/ownership rather than serving merely to show bad character.

Factors the Eighth Circuit treats as making such evidence intrinsic (as shown in this opinion and Battle)
- Same weapon/identity: The testimony concerned the exact gun recovered and DNA‑linked to Cutbank. Evidence about prior possession of that same item is probative of ownership/control and identity of the weapon at the time of the offense.
- Relevance to an element (possession/continuous control): The prior sightings were offered to prove later possession of the same firearm (an element of § 922(g)), not to prove he was the kind of person who carries guns. The court emphasized the direct probative link: “helps establish ownership or control.”
- Narrative completeness/temporal nexus: The testimony filled in the background that the defendant had possessed or exercised control over that particular firearm before the charged incident—i.e., continuity of possession. The witnesses’ temporal statements (“handful of times,” “once or twice”) are close enough to establish a pattern of possession relevant to the charged event.
- Not an independent bad act: The testimony was not about a separate, uncharged criminal episode offered to prove, for example, intent, motive, or propensity; it concerned the same object and the same course of conduct leading to the charged offense.

How that fits Rule 404(b)’s ban on propensity reasoning
- Rule 404(b) seeks to prevent juries from inferring guilt because a defendant is a “bad person.” The Eighth Circuit’s intrinsic/404(b) line focuses on whether the evidence is probative of an element of the charged crime (or necessary to tell the story) rather than whether it shows bad character.
- Here, because the prior sightings tied Cutbank to the identical weapon and helped prove control/continuous possession (a direct element of the prosecution’s case), the testimony was not being used as propensity evidence and thus was not subject to 404(b)’s exclusionary rule. The court relied on that doctrinal distinction when affirming the district court (¶ II.A), citing Battle.

Practical delimitation (what would make similar evidence 404(b) instead)
- If the government introduced testimony about other guns or other separate violent episodes merely to show Cutbank’s character as violent or as someone who carries weapons generally, that would be classic 404(b) territory.
- If the prior incident involved a different weapon or no clear link to the charged firearm, or if the prior act was temporally remote and not probative of ownership/control of the specific gun, the court would more likely treat it as “other acts” evidence requiring 404(b) analysis.

Conclusion
The panel treated the prior sightings as intrinsic because they directly connected Cutbank to the very weapon charged in the indictment and helped prove continuous possession/ownership—precisely the sort of direct, probative evidence that Rule 404(b) does not reach. The opinion rests on that nexus to the element of possession (citing Battle) and the witnesses’ testimony that they had seen the same sawed‑off rifle with Cutbank before the night in question.
