Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: United States v. Justin Cutbank

Chapter 1: # Case Background and Procedural Posture

## Incident overview (Nov. 19–20, 2020)
The events giving rise to this prosecution occurred overnight on November 19–20, 2020. According to the government’s proof at trial, Justin Cutbank—after drinking and using methamphetamine—awoke his girlfriend, D.F., pointed a gun at her face, forced her into a closet, and made paranoid accusations about “listening devices” and people outside waiting to harm him. D.F. testified Cutbank kept her in the closet at gunpoint for what felt like hours, threatened to kill her, struck her across the face with the gun, and then ordered her to wake others in the house (including her two teenage sons and a housemate, C.F.). Cutbank then fled the home with D.F.’s cell phone and the firearm. D.F. called the police.

## Police response at nearby garage
Separately, police received a report from a house about a man refusing to leave a garage about a half mile from D.F.’s residence. When officers arrived, Cutbank had barricaded himself inside the garage, made erratic statements, and told officers he had a weapon. During hours of contact, Cutbank resisted attempts to detain him, bit an officer in the face and neck, and grabbed at an officer’s gun. Those events prompted a search of the route Cutbank may have taken between D.F.’s home and the garage.

## Recovered evidence along route
Officers searching the likely path recovered several items: D.F.’s cell phone, a butcher-style knife, and a Marlin .22 caliber rifle with a sawed-off barrel and stock. DNA testing matched Cutbank to the rifle. Those physical items linked the garage encounter to the assault at D.F.’s home and provided key forensic evidence of Cutbank’s connection to the weapon.

## Witness identification
At trial, both D.F. and C.F. identified the same sawed-off Marlin .22 rifle as a weapon they had observed Cutbank possess on prior occasions. D.F. testified she had seen the rifle a “handful of times” before the night in question; C.F. testified she had seen the gun used “once or twice” before. Those witness identifications were central to the government’s theory that Cutbank had possessed and exercised control over the firearm before and during the charged incident.

## Trial results
A jury convicted Cutbank of being a felon in possession of a firearm in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1). The jury also found that Cutbank had previously been convicted of at least three violent felonies, a factual finding that triggers the enhanced penalty regime of 18 U.S.C. § 924(e) (the Armed Career Criminal Act, or ACCA). Multiple evidentiary rulings at trial—about prior possession testimony, hearsay and limiting instructions, impeachment materials, and related matters—were later raised on appeal.

## Sentencing: Guidelines calculations and sentence imposed
The Presentence Investigation Report (PSR) computed the applicable advisory Guidelines range as follows. The PSR used a base offense level of 26 based on the offense involving a sawed-off firearm (as described in 26 U.S.C. § 5845(a)), added a three-level increase for an altered/obliterated serial number, a two-level increase for recklessly creating a risk of death or serious bodily injury while fleeing police, and a four-level enhancement under USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) for possession of a firearm “in connection with” another felony offense. Those adjustments produced a total offense level of 35. Combined with a Criminal History Category VI, the PSR’s calculation yielded an advisory Guidelines range of 292 to 365 months’ imprisonment. The district court adopted the PSR’s factual statements without objection and imposed a within-Guidelines sentence of 292 months.

## Issues preserved for appeal
On appeal to the Eighth Circuit, Cutbank challenged several trial rulings as evidentiary and procedural errors that he said deprived him of a fair trial—claims that the brief presented with minimal legal citation or analysis. He also challenged the district court’s application of the four-level USSG § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) enhancement (possession “in connection with” another felony) and argued that the resulting within-Guidelines sentence conflicted with the sentencing statute’s reasonableness requirement under 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a). The Eighth Circuit reviewed those contentions, considered the preserved record (including the district court’s adoption of the PSR), and affirmed the conviction and sentence.
