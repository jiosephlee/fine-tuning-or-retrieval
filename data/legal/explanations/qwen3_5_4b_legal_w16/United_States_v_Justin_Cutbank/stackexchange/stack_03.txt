Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### What is the procedural standard for Confrontation Clause limitations when a district court refuses initial jury instructions on hearsay?
Question:
The court addressed the absence of a limiting instruction for D.F.'s statements in Section II.B, citing *United States v. Charboneau*. It seems the instruction was given at the 'close of trial' or implied. Is there a specific 8th Circuit rule that allows a controlling instruction at the end of the trial to cure a lack of instruction during trial, and how does this strictly limit the Confrontation Clause violations under the right to cross-examine existing evidence?
Answer:
### Answer

The Eighth Circuit provides specific procedural flexibility regarding jury instructions on hearsay and the Confrontation Clause, governed primarily by an **abuse of discretion** standard.

**1. Procedural Standard for Refusal of Initial Instructions**
When reviewing a district court's refusal to provide an initial limiting instruction on hearsay, the Eighth Circuit applies a standard of **abuse of discretion** (*United States v. Bennett*, 765 F.3d 887, 899 (8th Cir. 2014), quoted in *Cutbank*). Pursuant to the rule articulated in *Cutbank* (Section II.B), a district court is **not required** to instruct the jury on hearsay limitations at the moment evidence is admitted if the court provides an appropriate instruction at the **close of trial**.

**2. Rule on Closing Instruction**
The Eighth Circuit has established that the absence of a controlling instruction during testimony does not constitute a reversible error if an appropriate instruction is provided at the end of the trial. In *United States v. Cutbank* (Section II.B), the court stated:
> "The court 'need not instruct the jury regarding each item of evidence at the time the evidence is admitted if the court provides an appropriate instruction at the close of trial.' *Id.* Here, the record demonstrates the court did not admit hearsay during the trial. In addition, D.F. testified."

**3. Limiting Confrontation Clause Violations**
Regarding the Confrontation Clause, the Eighth Circuit determinacy is satisfied under *United States v. Charboneau*, 613 F.3d 860, 861 (8th Cir. 2010). The clause is satisfied "when the hearsay declarants . . . actually appear in court and testify in person." Because D.F. testified in person at trial (*United States v. Cutbank*, Section II.B), the specific procedural issue of whether the instruction was given initially is rendered moot. The court found that "The Confrontation Clause is satisfied 'when the hearsay declarants . . . actually appear in court and testify in person.'" (Id.). Consequently, a control instruction at the close of trial effectively cures any limitation concerns, as the presence and testimony of the declarant itself satisfies the Clause requirements.
