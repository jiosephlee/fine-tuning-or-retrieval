Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

Chapter 1: # Procedural Posture and Factual Background

## Overview of the Eighth Circuit Appeal Process

This appellate opinion arises from the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, an intermediate appellate court possessing jurisdiction over criminal appeals within its geographic jurisdiction. The opinion, authored by Circuit Judge Loken, details the appellate court's review of the district court's rulings regarding the defendant's conviction and sentence. In federal criminal procedure, the Court of Appeals generally reviews the district court's decision for abuse of discretion or reversible error. This stage provides the appellate court an opportunity to correct procedural errors made during the trial, such as misconduct by the court or improper instructions to the jury, or to overturn a sentence that falls outside the applicable guidelines. The opinion opens by establishing the procedural posture, noting the defendant's conviction and the specific arguments brought against it. The appeal was docketed as No. 24-1225, and the appellate court's disposition is a summary affirmation of the lower court's judgments.

## Statutory Elements: 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) (Felon in Possession) and 924(e) (Armed Career Criminal)

To understand the trial court's proceedings and the scope of the appeal, one must examine the statutory elements at issue under 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(g)(1) and 924(e). The first statute, § 922(g)(1), establishes the crime of being a "felon in possession of a firearm." This charge requires proof that the defendant is a prior felon, who is currently in possession of a controlled firearm. The appeal in this case challenged whether the jury was required to demonstrate prior possession or if the evidence supported the finding of possession under this statute. The second statute, § 924(e), embodies the Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA), which mandates a statutory sentencing enhancement for defendants with multiple prior violent felony convictions. Combined, these statutes form the basis of the indictment and the district court's sentencing guidelines application. The appellate court specifically noted that the enhancement under § 924(e) applied because the jury found Cutbank had been convicted of at least three prior violent felonies, triggering the enhanced penalty provisions for the Armed Career Criminal Act.

## Factual Narrative: The November 2020 Incident at D.F.'s Residence

The factual underpinning of the case centers on domestic violence and firearm use on November 19-20, 2020. The defendant, Justin Cutbank, was intoxicated and under the influence of methamphetamine when he confronted his girlfriend, D.F. At the incident location, Cutbank pointed a firearm at D.F.'s face and ordered her into a closet. He held her at gunpoint for an extended period, making paranoid accusations regarding listening devices and threatening her life. Cutbank later commanded D.F. to rouse others sleeping in the house, including two teenage sons and a housemate. Cutbank eventually fled the premises with D.F.'s cell phone and the firearm. Officers arrived in response to calls, and there were multiple points of contact with law enforcement, including a report from a separate house half a mile away regarding Cutbank's presence. The narrative establishes the criminal conduct that led to the trial proceedings, specifically highlighting the use of a firearm in a domestic setting under aggravated circumstances.

## Evidence Collection: Police Logistics, Wire Logs, and DNA Match

The evidence collected at the scene and after the initial arrest formed the basis of the government's case. Police interveners found a barrel sawed-off pistol and a stock had been retrieved from the path Cutbank likely used between the victims' residence and the garage. In addition to the physical evidence, officers recovered a Brittle-style knife, a Prince cell phone, and a Marlin .22 caliber rifle with a sawed-off barrel and stock. The opinion notes that DNA evidence from the gun matched Cutbank. Testimonial evidence included confessions made by D.F. and C.F., a housemate. While the subtopic title references wire logs, the opinion relies primarily on witness testimony for the prior possession of the weapon and police observations of the defendant. The search methods and the likelihood of using the destroyed firearm were confirmed through physical evidence found near the escape path. The defense argued that some prior use of the weapon was being admitted as propensity evidence which did not require witness testimony, relying solely on physical evidence and witness testimony.

## Scope of Appeal: Challenges to Conviction Versus Sentence

Cutbank appealed both his conviction and sentence, raising distinct arguments regarding the trial process and the sentencing calculation. Regarding conviction, he argued that the district court engaged in multiple evidentiary and procedural errors that deprived him of a fair trial and did not apply the guidelines correctly. Specific arguments included objections to the admission of the prior possession evidence, the jury instruction regarding hearsay, and the Cross-Examination of witnesses. Regarding the sentence, Cutbank argued that the four-level sentencing enhancement for firearm use connected to another felony offense affected the sentence because the enhancement is eliminated under the Armed Career Criminal Act. The Eighth Circuit affirmed the district court's ruling on the evidentiary issues, stating that the error was harmless and the rationality of applying the ACCA guidelines was supported by the record.
