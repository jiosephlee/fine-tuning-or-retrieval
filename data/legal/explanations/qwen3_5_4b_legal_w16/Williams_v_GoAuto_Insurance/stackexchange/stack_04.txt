Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### What is the legal function of 'strict adherence' to statutory cancellation procedures in Louisiana insurance law?
Question:
The opinion states that the strict adherence requires 'a clear method of fixing the exact time and date... [and] allows a minimum time during which the default may be cured.' Beyond ensuring the insurance company has notice, what is the underlying policy reason within the state insurance scheme that allows the court to strictly enforce the finance company's notice timing over equitable concerns for the insured's rushing behavior?
Answer:
### Answer

In *Williams v. GoAuto Insurance*, the 5th Circuit identifies the underlying policy reasons for the "strict adherence" doctrine by defining its statutory functions within the Louisiana insurance financing scheme. While the plaintiffs argued that the insurance company's timing procedures should be evaluated against equitable concerns regarding the insured’s conduct, the court clarified that strict adherence is required to satisfy two specific statutory objectives.

First, strict adherence provides a "clear method of fixing the exact time and date of cancellation." This ensures definiteness regarding when the insurance coverage terminates. Second, and crucially regarding the "insured's rushing behavior," strict adherence "grants a minimum time during which the default may be cured." 

By implicitly mandating a specific statutory window for performance, the law prevents the cure period from becoming indefinite or subject to equitable discretion. Consequently, the court does not allow equitable concerns to override the statutory requirements because the legislature designed the scheme to provide a mechanical process that fixes timing and guarantees a specific period for default payment. As the court reasoned, "curative time" is a statutory function, not one left to judicial weighing of equity.
