Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### What is the standard of review when appealing a specific Trademark opposition refusal in the Federal Circuit?
Question:
The opinion states that legal conclusions are reviewed "de novo," but factual findings are reviewed for "substantial evidence." Can someone break down the specific roles of the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) versus the Court of Appeals here? Does the appeals court retry the facts or just see if the Board's conclusions make sense?
Answer:
### Answer

Based on the court opinion in *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the Federal Circuit applies a bifurcated standard of review when appealing a Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) decision. The standard hinges on the distinction between the TTAB's **findings of fact** and the **legal conclusions** drawn from those findings.

#### 1. The Standard of Review
As the court explicitly states in the appellate opinion:
> "We review the [B]oard's legal conclusions *de novo*, and its findings of fact for substantial evidence." (Opinion at 5).

This establishes two distinct tiers of deference depending on the issue presented:

*   **De Novo Review for Legal Conclusions:** The Federal Circuit acts as the final tribunal for legal interpretation. It applies this standard to any legal question, including whether the TTAB was correct in identifying relevant DuPont factors as questions of law or fact.
*   **Substantial Evidence Review for Factual Findings:** The standard is defined as "such relevant evidence as a reasonable mind might accept as adequate to support a conclusion." (Opinion at 5, citing *Cons. Edison Co. of N.Y. v. NLRB*). This is a highly deferential standard; the court does not reweigh the evidence but checks if any non-frivolous evidence exists to support the Board's finding.

#### 2. TTAB vs. Federal Circuit Roles
This case illustrates that the FCircuit does not "retry the facts." Instead, it determines whether the Board’s factual findings, supported by the evidence in the record, were sufficient to sustain its legal conclusions.

In *Apex Bank*, the Federal Circuit recognized this dual role:
*   **The TTAB:** "Determined the meaning of the identified services... and [considered third-party registrations] to support its finding that 'the services are of a type that may emanate from a single source under one mark.'" (Opinion at 5).
*   **The Court:** Reviewed whether those service descriptions and evidence truly warranted the factual conclusion.

The opinion notes that specific issues might trigger remand rather than a new factual hearing. For instance, under the Sixth DuPont factor, the Board limited the scope of similar marks to credit card services. However, because the TTAB had already factually found the party services were "highly similar" (Second Factor), the Court determined the Board's *legal application* of that first fact was too narrow. The Court did not ask the Board to gather new evidence, but rather directed the Board to "reconsider the appropriate scope of third-party marks eligible for consideration in view of the Board's factual finding." (Opinion at 11).

#### 3. The Hybrid Nature of the DuPont Factors
While the overall standard is split between law and fact, the likelihood-of-confusion analysis creates a complex hybrid. The opinion clarifies:
> "Likelihood of confusion is a question of law, based on findings of relevant underlying facts, namely findings under the DuPont factors." (Opinion at 5).

Thus, the Federal Circuit reviews the strength of the factual findings under the DuPont factors to determine the ultimate legal legal conclusion on confusion.

*   **Question of Fact:** The Court held that "Each of the [thirteen] DuPont factors presents a question of fact, findings with regard to which we test for substantial evidence when called into question on appeal." (Opinion at 5, citing *M2 Software*). Therefore, the Court asked if the Board had "substantial evidence" for its finding regarding the similarity of goods or the commercial strength of a prior mark.
*   **Question of Law:** The Second Factor's conclusion was "affirmed" because the Board carefully considered the descriptions and had "substantial evidence to support the Board's finding that the parties' services are highly similar." (Opinion at 6).
*   **Vacating for Legal Error:** However, where the Court found the Board had applied the law incorrectly *to* the facts (such as limiting the scope of similar goods despite a factual finding of service similarity), the Court remanded. As the Court held: "The Board's analysis was legally flawed... We thus vacate the Board's finding with respect to the sixth DuPont factor and remand." (Opinion at 11–12).

#### Conclusion
In summary, under the controlling precedent in *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.*, the Federal Circuit **does not retry factual findings** but determines if those findings are supported by substantial evidence. Legal conclusions regarding the applicability of the Lanham Act and the DuPont factors are reviewed **de novo**, meaning the Court will correct errors where the TTAB misapplied the law to the accepted facts. When the Court finds the Board's analysis of the law essential to the outcome (e.g., defining the universe of marks), it vacates the finding and remands the specific issue for the Board to apply the correct legal standard to the prior-identified facts.
