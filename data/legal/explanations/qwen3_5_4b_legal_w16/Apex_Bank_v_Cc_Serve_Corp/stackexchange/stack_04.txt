Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

### What is the doctrinal link between the Sixth Factor (Strength) and the First Factor (Appearance) that necessitates remanding both?
Question:
The opinion vacates both the Sixth and First DuPont factors. Since the Sixth Factor considers commercial strength (based on crowded fields like 'Aspire'), why does assessing the First Factor (similarity of impression) depend on recalculating the Sixth Factor? The opinion notes reconsidering strength might affect commercial impression, but can someone explain how these factors are legally interdependent for confusion analysis?
Answer:
### Why the Sixth and First Factors Are Linked in *Apex Bank*

The doctrinal link between the Sixth Factor (Strength) and the First Factor (Appearance/Impression) in *Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp.* is that the **overall commercial impression** of the mark, which the First Factor requires the Board to evaluate, cannot be accurately determined without a correct factual assessment of the **commercial strength** of the prior mark, which is the specific inquiry of the Sixth Factor.

The Court reasoned that these factors are legally interdependent because a mistake in calculating the number and nature of similar marks (Sixth Factor) alters the factual record upon which the commercial impression is based. Specifically, the opinion establishes this causal chain in Section III.C:

1.  **Strength Determines Impression:** The Court explicitly stated that the First Factor considers marks "as to appearance, sound, connotation and commercial impression." Because the Court held that "commercial impression informs the analysis under the first DuPont factor," it found that an error in the Sixth Factor "may result in a different determination of the mark’s commercial strength or weakness and affect the overall commercial impression."
2.  **Scope Error Cascades:** The Board erred in the Sixth Factor analysis because it improperly narrowed the relevant public. While the Second Factor (Goods/Services) found the parties' services were "highly similar," the Board restricted the Sixth Factor (similar marks in use) to only "credit card services" marks. As the Court noted in Section III.B, the Sixth Factor requires consideration of similar marks for "similar goods and services," not identical ones. By ignoring marks related to other banking services, the Board skewed the count of competing marks, thereby correctly ignoring the Board's failure to show the mark's relative weakness.
3.  **Remand Necessity:** Because the scope of the "similar marks" determines the "commercial strength," and commercial strength impacts the "commercial impression," the Board could not properly analyze the First Factor on remand. As the Court wrote in Section III.C, the Board "must similarly vacate the Board’s analysis here" regarding the First Factor to ensure the commercial impression is calculated based on the corrected commercial strength analysis.

In summary, the First Factor is remanded alongside the Sixth Factor because the "commercial impression" is a weighing factor that relies on the factual foundation laid by the "market strength"—once that foundation is corrected, the weighing of the commercial impression must likewise be reset to say it potentially supports a different finding on likelihood of confusion.
