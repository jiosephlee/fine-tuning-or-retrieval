Title: Casebook chapter about the opinion: "Apex Bank v. CC Serve Corp."

Chapter 3: # Affirmance: Analysis of Similarity of Goods and Services (Second Factor)

## Board's Definition of Credit Card Services vs. Banking Services

To understand the court's affirmation of the Board's finding on the second DuPont factor, students must first grasp how the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) interpreted the services involved. The second DuPont factor assesses the similarity of the parties' goods and/or services, examining whether the respective products are related in a manner such that they could give rise to a mistaken belief that they emanate from the same source. On appeal, the Federal Circuit looked specifically at the services offered by CC Serve—credit card services—and Apex Bank—banking and financing services. As outlined in the opinion, the Board began by analyzing the meaning of these service categories. Because the Trademark Manual for Examiners had deleted the entry for "credit card services" after CC Serve's registration, the Board first determined the prevailing meaning of the identified services. The Board defined "credit card services" as issuing credit cards for finance and debt counseling, processing payments, authorization, monitoring, and accessing credit scores.

For the second DuPont factor, the Federal Circuit agreed with the Board that the services provided by the two parties fell within the relevant universe. While the parties disputed whether their services were identical, the standard is not strict identity; the evidence must only establish that the products are related in some manner. The Board determined that the definitions of "banking" and "finance" encompass extending credit or providing funds through the issuance of credit cards. Consequently, the Board concluded that Apex's services (banking/financing) and CC Serve's services (credit cards) are "legally identical, in part." The appellate court noted that the Board did not have to define the services in identical terms for every fact, only that the definitions of the relevant goods provided substantial evidence to the Board that the services were sufficiently related to warrant consideration under the second DuPont factor.

## Economic Integration: The Link Between Credit Cards and Lending Funds

A critical aspect of the resemblance analysis in this case was the economic integration between the parties' activities. CC Serve initiated litigation as the entity that issues credit cards under the name ASPIRE, partnering with a bank to issue branded cards. On the other hand, Apex Bank is a retail bank chartered in Tennessee that offers various financial products, including loans and accounts, but did not offer credit cards. However, Apex planned to offer an internet bank under the ASPIRE BANK word and design marks for "banking and financing services." The opinion highlights that the economic fallout of these services requires consideration of the relationship between issuing cards and banking.

The Board determined that because extending credit or providing funds is the essence of credit card services and banking services, there is a strong link between the two. The Board reasoned that if a customer sees the credit card, they could be misled into believing that the credit card funds are issued by the bank provider, and vice versa. The Board recognized that the service descriptions implied that the services offered by each party were of a type that could emanate from a single source. The Federal Circuit confirmed that this economic integration, analyzed through the lens of the Board's definitions and dictionaries, creates a relationship strong enough to sustain a finding of similarity under the second DuPont factor. The court emphasized that evidence must only show a relatedness in marketing or function, not necessarily legal identity of the specific product.

## The Board's Conclusive Reasoning on 'Legal Identity' and Substantial Evidence

When the Federal Circuit reviews the Board's analysis, it generally applies a mixed standard: legal conclusions are reviewed de novo, while findings of fact must be supported by substantial evidence. To understand the affirmation in this section, students should note how the court defined the threshold standard used. Substantial evidence is defined as "such relevant evidence as a reasonable mind might accept as adequate to support a conclusion." In this specific case, the Board presented a detailed analysis of the service definitions supported by third-party registrations. The opinion indicates that the Board supported its finding by citing (J.A. 33–34), noting that "the services are of a type that may emanate from a single source under one mark."

The Federal Circuit acted as the ultimate arbiter of whether this evidence met the substantial evidence threshold. The court reasoned that because the Board's conclusion was supported by dictionary definitions and third-party registrations, the evidence was adequate to uphold the Board's conclusion that services were highly similar. The appellate court found that it did not err in applying the substantial evidence standard to the second DuPont factor. The opinion confirms that individual findings under the DuPont factors are questions of fact, and the Federal Circuit tests these for substantial evidence, deferring to the Board's weight assignment unless that weight assignment lacks support. Therefore, the Board's assessment that services were "legally identical, in part" held up under scrutiny, leading to the affirmation of that specific factor.

## Why the Court Found No Error in Determining High Service Similarity

Finally, the court addressed the appellant's specific objections to the Board's determination. Apex argued on appeal that the parties' services were not the same and were not directly competitive, particularly noting that CC Serve was not providing banking services directly but was partnering with banks to manage credit card programs. The Federal Circuit rejected this argument as unavailing. The court stated that the Board carefully considered the descriptions of each party's services, and substantial evidence supports the Board's finding. Apex's key reliance on the fact that CC Serve joins with a bank versus Apex being the bank itself did not negate the finding of related services.

The court held that the Board's finding that the parties' services are highly similar was supported by relevant evidence and appealed to the authority of the Board's detailed reasoning. By affirming the Board's analysis, the court clarified that the second DuPont factor does not demand identical goods, but rather a demonstrated relatedness that could cause confusion. The opinion explicitly notes that the Board's analysis should be given deference since the members of the board were the first to make these findings and were tasked with applying the relevant standards. Consequently, the court found no merit in Apex's claim that the Board erred in concluding that the second DuPont factor weighed heavily in favor of finding likelihood of confusion, resulting in an affirmation of that specific factor while requiring remand for other factors where similar marks defined differently.
