Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### Why did the plaintiff forfeit his arguments regarding 'overbroad' categories when withholding documents within Exemption 7(A)?
Question:
Section IV.C mentions that when an agency withholds documents on a category-by-category basis, it must use categories that are 'sufficiently distinct' to allow a court to determine exemptions. The plaintiff here conceded that the various categories identified by the FBI were overbroad. On what grounds does the D.C. Circuit hold that a plaintiff forfeits these arguments, and is there a procedural timeline requirement for challenging the distinctness of withholding categories during summary judgment?
Answer:
**Answer**

Based on the D.C. Circuit’s opinion in *Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation*, the plaintiff forfeited his arguments regarding the overbreadth of the FBI's withholding categories because he failed to provide a specific explanation supporting those claims. The opinion does not establish a rigid procedural timeline (e.g., a specific filing date) as the reason for the forfeiture; rather, it emphasizes the **substantive requirement** to offer concrete reasoning to preserve the argument.

### Why the Plaintiff Forfeited the Argument
The plaintiff, Farahi, conceded that the FBI had seized the categorization of his files and argued they were overbroad. However, the appellate court found the argument defective on its merits and thus waived it entirely. According to Section IV.C of the opinion, the court held:

> "Farahi briefly objects that the government did not adequately justify how it defined the categories of documents... But Farahi provides **no specific explanation** for why the various categories identified by the FBI were overbroad. Farahi accordingly **forfeited his arguments on this score.**"

The forfeiture was not due to a procedural error regarding when the objection was filed, but rather because the argument lacked the necessary detail. To avoid forfeiture, a plaintiff must articulate with specificity why the categories are constitutionally or statistically overbroad.

### Relevant Legal Standard & Precedent
The court clarified that under FOIA, when an agency withholds documents on a category-by-category basis, the categories used must be **"sufficiently distinct to allow a court to determine exemptions."**

*Foad Farahi v. FBI*, 10th Cir. (D.C. Cir. 2025), cited *CREW, 746 F.4th at 1088* (quoting *Gallant v. NLRB*, 26 F.3d 168, 173 (D.C. Cir. 1994)) for the distinctness requirement.
*   **Key Takeaway:** The categories placed on the record must allow the reviewing court to determine which specific exemptions apply to each portion of the withheld evidence.
*   **Forfeiture Rationale:** The court explicitly relied on **Khine v. DHS**, 943 F.3d 959, 967 (D.C. Cir. 2019), to conclude that without a specific explanation, the plaintiff forfeits the claim. This implies that general assertions of overbreadth are insufficient to sustain an objection that is procedurally barred from further adjudication.

### On Procedural Timeline Requirements
You asked whether there is a procedural timeline requirement for challenging the distinctness of withholding categories during summary judgment. **The provided opinion does not impose a specific temporal deadline (such as a hearing or briefing cut-off) for this challenge.**

Instead, the forfeiture resulted from the **absence of substantive specificity**. While standard FOIA litigation typically requires parties to address segregability and categorization at the summary judgment stage to avoid surprise arguments on appeal, the D.C. Circuit here focused the forfeiture on the quality of the explanation ("no specific explanation") rather than the timing of the complaint. The opinion implies that if the agency's categories are challenged, the challenger must provide concrete legal or factual reasons for the overbreadth, regardless of the stage at which it is formally raised, to avoid losing the issue.

### Summary
The plaintiff lost the opportunity to argue regarding overbroad categories because he failed to provide a **specific explanation** as to *why* they were overbroad. The D.C. Circuit treats the lack of this supporting detail as a failure to substantiate the claim, effectively forfeiting the argument under *Khine*. While the specific procedural deadline for raising this is not detailed in the opinion, the practical effect is that an entirely conclusory challenge at the appellate stage was considered procedurally barred due to the lack of the requisite evidentiary support.
