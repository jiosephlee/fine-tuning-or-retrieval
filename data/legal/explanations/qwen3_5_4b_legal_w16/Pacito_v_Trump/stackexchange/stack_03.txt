Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### Does the intersection of 'authorization' and 'shall' in 8 U.S.C. § 1522 create a mandatory spending obligation despite the 'to the extent of available appropriations' language?
Question:
Judge Lee's concurrence argues that 8 U.S.C. § 1522 is merely an authorization to the executive branch, citing the use of the word 'authorized' throughout the funding provisions (b)-(e). However, the majority orders the State Department to reinstate cooperative agreements. If the 'to the extent of available appropriations' clause (in 8 U.S.C. § 1522) is read as discretionary, how does the majority justify using the APA to 'reinstate' agreements that rely on appropriated funds that may be unavailable? Does the court interpret 'Conditions and Considerations' to mean that if assistance is funded, those conditions must be met, rather than funding itself being mandatory?
Answer:
Based on the panel order and opinions provided in *Pacito v. Trump*, 25-1313/25-1939 (9th Cir. 2025), here is an analysis of the statutory interpretation and the Majority's justification for reinstating agreements despite funding limitations.

### The Major versus the Interim Order
First, it is important to distinguish the procedural posture. The text provided is an **Interim Stay Order**, not the final opinion on the merits. The majority explicitly states in the proceeding of the Order that "An opinion on the merits will issue in due course." However, under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), courts may issue orders based on statutory interpretation to enforce an agency's duties pending final resolution (as seen here). The question assumes the Majority has reached a conclusion on the merits of § 1522. Based on the Order issued, the court discerned that there is a statutory duty to provide services.

### Legal Doctrine: Authorization vs. Mandatory Spending
The core legal issue relies on statutory construction regarding the distinction between **authorization** (permissive spending) and **mandatory spending** (obligation to spend).
*   **Judge Lee’s Concurrence (The Interpretation Cited):** Judge Lee argues that the explicit use of the word "authorized" in § 1522(b)–(e) constitutes a permissive grant of power to the executive branch. Under this view, the "shall" language in § 1522(a)(1)(A) "Conditions and Considerations" is conditional; it mandates specific *considerations* (e.g., economic self-sufficiency) only **if** the government chooses to provide the discretionary funding.
*   **The Majority’s Order (The Interpretation Analyzed):** The Majority (in the Order) reads the provision more broadly. It cites § 1522(a)(1)(A), which "requires" the Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement "to the extent of available appropriations"—to provide specific services. The court treats § 1522(a) as creating a mandatory obligation to provide services *assuming* appropriations are available, rather than leaving the obligation to funds.

### Application to "Conditions and Considerations"
To answer the specific question regarding the intersection of "authorization" and "shall":

1.  **Statutory Conditions Trigger the Spending Obligation:** The Majority interprets the "shall" in § 1522(a) not as a discretionary trigger for the *spending* power, but as a **condition** attached to the statutory obligation. In the text, the court states: "Section 1522(a)(1)(A) requires the Director... 'to the extent of available appropriations'... to [specifically enumerate services]."
2.  **Appropriations as a Fact, Not a Limitation:** The crux of the majority’s justification for using the APA to reinstate agreements lies in the factual determination of the "available" nature of the appropriations. In the Order, the court notes: "We note that for FY 2025, Congress has appropriated $3.178 billion... which was reduced by $800 million in the Rescissions Act of 2025."
    *   Because specific appropriations exist for FY 2025, the condition "to the extent of available appropriations" is triggered.
    *   Therefore, the "shall" in § 1522(a) is not discretionary; it is mandatory *to the extent that the appropriations exist*.
3.  **APA Justification for Reinstatement:** The Majority orders the reinstatement of cooperative agreements necessary to provide "reception and placement services described in § 1522" rather than new admissions. This is because the APA analysis separates the *suspension of admissions* (a matter left to the President's deference) from the *provision of services to those already admitted*. The court concludes that while the President may suspend entry (Executive Order No. 14163), the 8 U.S.C. § 1522 mandate to provide services to refugees currently within the system creates a distinct, enforceable duty via the APA.

### Conclusion
The Majority interprets "Conditions and Considerations" (§ 1522(a)) to mean that the statutory "shall" language imposes a mandatory obligation to provide services when funds are available under the appropriation, effectively overriding the "authorization" language of subsections (b)–(e) regarding the *services* already admitted. In the Order, the court relies on the FY 2025 appropriated funds to solidify the "available" constraint, thereby justifying the APA order to reinstitute agreements to meet the "shall" requirement of service provision.
