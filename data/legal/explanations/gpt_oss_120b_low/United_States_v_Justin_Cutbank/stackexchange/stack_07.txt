Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Does the presence of a sawed‑off firearm automatically trigger the §5845(a) firearm‑related offense level increase, or must the court find additional elements?
Question:
The PSR applied a base offense level of 26 because the firearm was a sawed‑off rifle described in §5845(a). I’m trying to understand whether the mere possession of a sawed‑off weapon is sufficient for this enhancement, or if the court must also establish that the weapon was used in the commission of the crime. What is the controlling authority on this point in the Eighth Circuit?
Answer:
**Short answer**

In the Eighth Circuit the mere possession of a firearm that falls within the definition of a “sawed‑off” weapon under **26 U.S.C. § 5845(a)** is enough to trigger the § 5845(a) sentencing enhancement. The court does **not** have to find that the weapon was actually used in the underlying felony; possession alone satisfies the statutory element.

**Why the opinion answers the question**

The *Cutbank* opinion (8 Cir. 2025) directly explains why the sentencing guidelines gave the offense a base level of 26:

> “The PSR recommended a base offense level of 26 **because the offense involved a sawed‑off firearm described in 26 U.S.C. § 5845(a)**…”  

The court treats the “sawed‑off” characteristic of the weapon as a **sentencing factor** that is **intrinsic to the conduct of possession**, not a separate predicate requiring proof of use. The opinion never says that the government had to prove the gun was *used* during the robbery or assault; it only needed to establish that the defendant possessed a firearm that, by its physical description (sawed‑off barrel and stock), fell within § 5845(a).

Later, when the court discusses the § 2K2.1(b)(6)(B) “firearm‑in‑connection‑with‑another‑felony” enhancement, it stresses that **use or possession** of any firearm in relation to a felony suffices, quoting the guideline comment:

> “This enhancement applies if a defendant **used or possessed** any firearm … ‘regardless of whether a criminal charge was brought, or a conviction obtained.’” § 2K2.1, comment. (n. 14(C))

That language mirrors the approach taken with the § 5845(a) factor: possession of the weapon, not its deployment, triggers the increase.

**Controlling authority in the Eighth Circuit**

*United States v. Cutbank*, 2025 WL … (8th Cir. Oct. 7 2025) is the most recent Eighth Circuit decision addressing the issue. The court’s reasoning rests on the plain text of § 5845(a) and the guideline’s explanatory note, and it has not been limited or overruled by any later Eighth Circuit precedent. No contrary authority in the circuit requires proof of actual use of the sawed‑off gun.

**Practical take‑away**

- **Element needed:** The firearm must be a *sawed‑off* weapon as defined in § 5845(a) (i.e., a rifle, shotgun, or pistol with a barrel length shorter than the statutory minimum, or a stock that renders the weapon non‑functional as intended).  
- **No additional element:** The prosecution does **not** need to prove the weapon was used to commit the underlying offense; possession alone satisfies the enhancement.  

Thus, under current Eighth Circuit authority, the presence of a sawed‑off firearm automatically adds the § 5845(a) offense‑level increase.
