Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### Are categorical withholdings permissible under FOIA, and what are the requirements for the categories to be sufficiently distinct?
Question:
Farahi argued that the FBI’s categories for withholding were overbroad and not sufficiently distinct. The court dismissed this argument, citing *CREW* and *Khine*. What are the legal criteria for an agency’s categorical withholding scheme to satisfy FOIA’s requirement that categories be "sufficiently distinct"? How have courts evaluated the sufficiency of such categories in prior cases?
Answer:
**Short answer**

Under FOIA an agency may withhold records on a “category‑by‑category” basis only if each category is **(1) clearly defined**, **(2) internally consistent**, and **(3) sufficiently distinct from the other categories** that a court can evaluate whether the exemption asserted for a given category is properly applied.  The D.C. Circuit has articulated this requirement in *Citizens for Responsible & Ethics in Washington v. DOJ*, 746 F.3d 1082, 1088 (D.C. Cir. 2014) (quoting *Gallant v. NLRB*, 26 F.3d 168, 173 (D.C. Cir. 1994)).  A plaintiff who does not point out how a particular category fails that test forfeits the argument, as held in *Khine v. DHS*, 943 F.3d 959, 967 (D.C. Cir. 2019).

**What “sufficiently distinct” means**

1. **Clear definition of the scope** – The agency must describe, in its withholding notice, what factual material falls within the category.  A label such as “national‑security investigations” is not enough unless the notice explains what types of documents, subjects, or investigative phases are covered.

2. **Non‑overlap with other categories** – The categories must not be so broad that the same document could plausibly be placed in two or more categories.  If overlap exists, a court cannot determine which exemption applies to a particular record, defeating the statutory requirement that “each category … be sufficiently distinct to allow a court to determine whether the specific claimed exemptions are properly applied” (*CREW* at 1088).

3. **Logical separation of exempt from non‑exempt material** – The agency must show that, within a given category, the exempt material is not “inextricably intertwined” with non‑exempt material.  When the category is too amorphous, the agency cannot satisfy the “reasonable segregation” requirement of 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(8)(A)(ii).

**How courts have applied the test**

- ***CREW* (2014)** – The court rejected a government‑provided categorical list that lumped together “documents related to ongoing investigations” without specifying what kinds of documents (e.g., surveillance logs, interview notes, intelligence summaries) were covered.  Because the list did not delineate the boundaries between categories, the court held the categories were not “sufficiently distinct” and ordered more detailed explanations.

- ***Khine* (2019)** – The plaintiff challenged a Department of Homeland Security withholding that used a single category called “immigration‑related national‑security investigations.”  The district court found the category overly broad and indistinguishable from other categories such as “general immigration enforcement.”  The D.C. Circuit affirmed, emphasizing that a plaintiff must point out the deficiency; once the plaintiff failed to show how the categories overlapped, the argument was deemed forfeited.

- **Subsequent cases** (e.g., *Juarez v. DOJ*, 518 F.3d 54, 61 (D.C. Cir. 2008); *Mead Data Center, Inc. v. Air Force*, 566 F.2d 242, 260 (D.C. Cir. 1977)) treat the “sufficiently distinct” requirement as a gate‑keeping step before the agency can rely on Exemption 7(A).  If the categories pass the distinctness test, the agency must still demonstrate that each category’s records are either wholly exempt or not reasonably segregable.

**Take‑away for practitioners**

When drafting a categorical withholding notice:

1. **Define each category in plain, concrete terms** (e.g., “electronic communications between FBI field agents and informants concerning the subject’s alleged recruitment activities between 2018‑2022”).

2. **Explain why the category does not overlap** with any other category listed in the notice (perhaps by providing a brief table mapping document types to categories).

3. **Show how the category satisfies the segregation requirement**, either by evidencing that the documents are wholly exempt or by describing why any non‑exempt portions are intermingled and cannot be separated.

If the agency fails to meet these criteria, a court will deem the categorical withholding improper, as the *Farahi* opinion notes that the FBI’s categories were not “overbroad” or “insufficiently distinct” because the court found them adequately defined and distinct, relying on *CREW* and *Khine* for the legal standard.
