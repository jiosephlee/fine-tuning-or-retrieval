Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### What foundation is required before using a police report as extrinsic evidence to impeach a witness’s prior inconsistent statement?
Question:
Cutbank appealed because he wanted his counsel read portions of the police report detailing C.F.’s statements during cross‑examination; the trial judge barred this until proper authentication was shown, citing United States v. Cumbie. What specific steps satisfy the authentication requirement for putting an out‑of‑court document into evidence for impeachment purposes under Federal Rules of Evidence §§ 613 and 801(d)? Can an officer testify that he authored or received the report instead of producing an original copy?
Answer:
**Short answer**

To use a police report as *extrinsic* proof of a witness’s prior‑inconsistent statement you must first lay a **foundation under Fed. R. Evid. §§ 901–903** before you can read any portion of the report under **§ 613(b)** and treat the statement as admissible under **§ 801(d)(1)(A)**.  

In *United States v. Justin Cutbank*, the Eighth Circuit reiterated that “although extrinsic evidence may be used for impeachment by prior inconsistent statement, such extrinsic evidence **must still be authenticated**” (28 F.4th 907, 914 (Cumbie)). The court therefore correctly required Cutbank’s counsel to prove the report’s authenticity before allowing any excerpts to be read.

Below are the **step‑by‑step requirements** that satisfy that authentication demand, followed by a brief discussion of whether an officer’s testimony alone can stand in for the actual report.

---

### 1️⃣ Identify the document and its maker

* **Rule 901(b)(1)** allows a witness who has personal knowledge of how the document was created or obtained to authenticate it.
* In practice you call **the responding police officer (or another custodian)** and have him say, for example:
  
  > “I am Detective John Doe, I authored/received this incident report on Nov 20 2020 while working in the [X] precinct. It was prepared contemporaneously with my investigation and placed in the precinct’s official case file.”

* That testimony establishes **(a)** what the document is (a police incident report), **(b)** who prepared it, **(c)** when it was made, and **(d)** that it has been kept in the regular course of business—a key element of authenticity.

### 2️⃣ Show it was kept in regular government custody

* If you prefer to rely on the **self‑authenticating “official record” exception** (**Rule 902(8)**), you need only prove that *the agency maintains such records* and *the witness is authorized to speak about their regularity*. The same officer’s oath about his role satisfies this.
* Once an official record is identified as “kept in regular course,” no additional proof of its contents is required—the document authenticates itself once produced.

### 3️⃣ Satisfy the **Best‑Evidence Rule** (Rule 1002)

* Even after authentication, you generally must produce **the original** of the police report.
* If production of the original is impossible despite diligent effort, a **duplicate** may be admitted provided:
  * You demonstrate why the original isn’t available,
  * You establish that the duplicate is accurate,
  * No genuine dispute exists as to its authenticity (**Rule 1003**).

> *In Cutbank*, the trial judge rejected counsel’s attempt to read portions of a written report “without proper foundation,” precisely because these foundational steps had not been satisfied; it did not reach a determination on whether an original had been produced—only that authentication was missing.

### 4️⃣ Offer the excerpt

* Once authentication and best‑evidence requirements are met, you may mark the report as an exhibit,
* Then either **read verbatim** (“The report states…”) or have your witness **paraphrase** it while pointing to the exhibit.
* Because § 613(b) permits extrinsic proof of prior inconsistent statements when they are *not* offered for their truth, no further hearsay analysis is needed after authentication.

---

## Can an officer simply testify “I wrote/received it” without producing a copy?

**No—testimony alone does not replace production**, though his testimony *does* satisfy the *authentication* prong:

| Requirement | What an officer’s oath can do | What still remains |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity & custody** | Officer’s personal knowledge authenticates (§ 901(b)(1)). | Must still satisfy § 1002 (original) or § 1003 (valid duplicate). |
| **Self‑authentication** | Under § 902(8) his declaration that this is an official police record makes it self‑authentic once produced.| You still need *the actual report* or an acceptable duplicate before any narrative can be read into evidence.|

If you cannot produce even a copy, you would have to invoke one of § 1003’s exceptions (e.g., loss despite diligent search). Courts have repeatedly refused to admit “the contents” of a document merely on oral description when neither original nor reliable duplicate is offered—a principle consistent with *Cumbie's* emphasis on authenticating out‑of‑court writings before using them for impeachment.

---

### Putting it together for your case

1. Call Officer ___[name]___ and have him testify that he prepared/received C.F.’s statement in a police incident report dated __[date]__, filed in __[precinct]__.
2. Ask him whether his department routinely files such reports in its official records system (establishes §§ 902(8) & ​901(b)).
3. Produce the **original** incident report—or if unavailable after showing diligent search, offer an accurate duplicate accompanied by a declaration that it faithfully reproduces the original.
4​.​ Mark it as Exhibit ___X___.
5​.​ Read into evidence any portion relevant to C.F.’s prior inconsistent statement while pointing at Exhibit ___X___.
6​.​ Rely on § 613(b) for admissibility and § 801(d)(1)(A) for non‑hearsay status once those foundations are laid.

Following those steps satisfies precisely what *Cutbank*’s appellate court described as “proper authentication” and allows your cross‑examination line about C.F.’s earlier statements to proceed without error.
