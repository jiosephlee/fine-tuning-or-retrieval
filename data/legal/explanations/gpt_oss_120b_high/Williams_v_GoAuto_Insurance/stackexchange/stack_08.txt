Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### How do power-of-attorney provisions in premium-finance agreements allocate responsibility for meeting statutory cancellation requirements between finance company and insurer?
Question:
'APAC holds power-of-attorney allowing it to cancel policies upon default and must send certified notices to GoAuto per statute.' Yet GoAuto cancels automatically upon receiving APAC's electronic request. Who bears ultimate responsibility—must APAC certify compliance before sending its request while GoAuto merely needs "receipt"—if any step is missing which party is liable for violating La Rev Stat § 9:3550(G)?
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
Under Louisiana’s premium‑finance cancellation statute the *finance company* (APAC) bears the substantive burden of complying with every statutory condition and must “certify”—by means of a written statement—that those conditions are satisfied before it requests cancellation. The *insurer* (GoAuto) has only a minimal duty: once it **receives** the certified request (whether physically or electronically) it may cancel the policy. Consequently, if any element of the statutory checklist is missing or false, liability lies with APAC; GoAuto would be liable only if it cancelled without first having a proper “receipt” of the certified notice.

Below is a step‑by‑step explanation drawn from **Williams v. GoAuto Insurance**, 5th Cir. No. 24‑30646 (Oct. 3 2025).

---

### 1️⃣ The statutory scheme (La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G))

The law requires a **premium‑finance company** to send *to the insurer*:

1. **A copy of the notice of cancellation**  
2. **A statement certifying** that:
   - The financing agreement contains a valid power‑of‑attorney;
   - The agreement is in default and the default has not been cured;
   - A ten‑day cancellation notice was sent to the insured;
   - Copies of that notice were sent to all interested third parties.

(§ 9:3550(G)(3)(a)(i)–(iv)).  

The statute then provides that *upon receipt* of “such notice … and statement,” the insurer *may* proceed with cancellation (§ 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i)).

---

### 2️⃣ What “certifying” means

The Fifth Circuit read “certifying” as **a factual affirmation**, *not* a requirement that a particular employee sign the document:

- The opinion notes that “the statute makes no explicit reference to a signature” and that “the word ‘certifying’ would have to create such a requirement” if Congress had meant it (Williams, ¶ III.A).  
- No Louisiana precedent has imposed a signature requirement; decisions such as *Delatte v. Lemotte* and *Eaglin v. Champion* focus on strict compliance with content, not on who signs it.

Thus APAP’s e‑mail that includes a paragraph beginning “APAC certifies that …” satisfies the certification duty even though no individual’s handwritten signature appears on it.

---

### 3️⃣ What counts as “receipt”

The court accepted **electronic receipt** as sufficient:

- It applied Black’s Law Dictionary definitions (“receipt” = taking possession or custody) and concluded that once GoAuto’s computer system receives the e‑mail and processes it through a web service, GoAuto has “taken possession” in a modern sense (§ III.A).  
- The opinion rejects the plaintiffs’ argument that physical handling by an employee is required and affirms the district court’s view that an e‑mail plus an automated web‑service call satisfy § 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i).

Therefore GoAuto’s automatic cancellation triggered by its system constitutes lawful “receipt.”

---

### 4️⃣ Allocation of responsibility

| Party | Statutory duty | What failure triggers liability |
|-------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| **APAC (finance company)** | • Draft and send the cancellation notice<br>• Include all four statements under § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)<br>• Ensure each statement is true | A false or omitted certification (e.g., failing to confirm a valid POA or that a ten‑day notice was sent) means APAC violated the statute and is liable under § 9:3550(G). |
| **GoAuto (insurer)** | • Receive the certified notice (electronically or otherwise)<br>• Cancel only after receipt | If GoAuto cancels *without* having received a compliant certified request, it would be in violation of § 9:3550(G)(3)(b). The court found this did not occur here because its system captured the e‑mail before cancellation.|

The Fifth Circuit emphasized that **the finance company carries the substantive compliance burden**; once its certified request reaches the insurer, any subsequent action by the insurer is permissible so long as “receipt” has occurred.

---

### 5️⃣ Application to your hypothetical

- **APAC must certify before sending** – The certification must be attached to the same communication that goes to GoAuto (the e‑mail). The presence of an electronic stamp later in the morning does not affect compliance because cancellation occurs after receipt.
- **GoAuto’s duty ends at receipt** – As soon as its computer systems ingest APAC’s e‑mail (“receipt”), it may cancel automatically.
- **Liability for a missing step** – If APAC omitted any required certification element (e.g., failed to disclose that a ten‑day notice was actually mailed), APAC—not GoAuto—would be responsible for any violation of § 9:3550(G). Conversely, if GoAuto cancelled before its system logged receipt, GoAuto would be at fault.

---

### 6️⃣ Key takeaways

1. **Finance company = certifier** – It must ensure all statutory prerequisites are true and evidenced in its request.
2. **Insurer = receiver** – It need only obtain a “receipt,” which modern courts treat as satisfied by electronic transmission.
3. **No signature requirement** – A plain statement labelled “certifies” suffices under current jurisprudence.
4. **Liability follows performance** – Failure on either side triggers liability only if that side neglects its specific statutory duty.

In *Williams v. GoAuto*, the Fifth Circuit concluded that both parties performed their respective duties correctly; therefore no violation of La Rev Stat § 9:3550(G) occurred and no liability attached to either party.

--- 

*If you encounter a situation where an insurance premium finance arrangement uses only an electronic request without any accompanying certification statement, remember that under Louisiana law—and according to *Williams*—the lack of such certification would place fault on the finance company.*
