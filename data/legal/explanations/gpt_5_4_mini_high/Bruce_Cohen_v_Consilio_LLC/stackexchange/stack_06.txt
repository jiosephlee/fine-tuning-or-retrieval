Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

### Why was the Minnesota Wage Theft Act claim treated as waived or unsupported on appeal?
Question:
For the MWTA claim, Cohen argued that Consilio failed to provide the required notice of a change in its overtime policy under Minn. Stat. § 181.032. The Eighth Circuit says Cohen received all pay owed and “points to no statutory authority allowing for civil penalties under this statute,” and then says that with “no meaningful argument in support of reversal,” it will not disturb the dismissal. Is this saying the claim failed on the merits because the statute does not create a private penalty remedy, or is it saying the issue was waived on appeal because Cohen's brief did not develop the argument enough? I'm trying to understand whether this is a substantive holding or mainly an appellate briefing/default rule.
Answer:
It is primarily an **appellate briefing/forfeiture ruling**, not a full merits holding.

The key language is:

- Cohen “**points to no statutory authority allowing for civil penalties under this statute**,” and
- “**[w]ith no meaningful argument in support of reversal, we will not disturb** the district court’s order dismissing this claim,”
- with a citation to **Milligan v. City of Red Oak**, which says an issue is waived when the brief contains no supporting argument or legal authority.

That tells you the panel was saying: **Cohen did not adequately develop the MWTA issue on appeal**, so the court would not consider it further.

### What the court did *not* do
The opinion does **not** give a full statutory analysis and does **not** expressly hold, as a standalone merits ruling, that the MWTA categorically cannot support private civil penalties. If the court had wanted to resolve that question on the merits, it would have analyzed § 181.032 the way it did the MPWA claim.

### Why the wording matters
The court’s reasoning is basically:

1. Cohen had already stipulated he was paid all wages owed.
2. He sought penalties.
3. But he **did not identify any statutory basis** for private civil penalties under § 181.032.
4. Because he offered **no meaningful argument for reversal**, the court treated the issue as **waived/forfeited on appeal** and affirmed the dismissal.

So the best reading is: **the claim failed on appeal mainly because Cohen did not properly brief or support it, and the court declined to reach the merits.** The reference to the lack of statutory authority is part of that explanation, not a fully developed merits holding.
