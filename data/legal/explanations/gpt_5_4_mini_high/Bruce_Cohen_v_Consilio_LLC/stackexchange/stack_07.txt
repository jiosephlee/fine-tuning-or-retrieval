Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Bruce Cohen v. Consilio, LLC"

### When does changing the requested injunction in summary judgment briefing become an improper new claim under Rule 15?
Question:
Cohen apparently asked for certain specific forms of injunctive relief in his complaint, but later argued in summary judgment that he should get an order requiring Consilio to rescind the 2019 overtime policy. The court says that issue was not properly before it because new claims must be raised through an amended complaint, not opposition briefs. I'm not sure where the line is here. If a plaintiff already pleaded a claim for injunctive relief, why isn't a different formulation of the injunction just a new remedy theory rather than a new claim? What made this change significant enough that Rule 15 was required?
Answer:
The line is between **amending the pleaded case** and **arguing for a different form of relief within the case that was actually pleaded**.

In *Cohen v. Consilio*, the Eighth Circuit treated Cohen’s rescission request as the former, not the latter, because:

1. **His complaint asked for certain specific injunctive relief**, not for an order requiring rescission of the 2019 overtime policy.
2. **He later “changed course” in summary judgment briefing** and asked for a different injunction.
3. Under the court’s rule, **“[n]ew claims are properly raised through amended complaints, not opposition briefs.”** (*InfoDeli*; Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a).)

So the problem was not that an injunction is always a “new claim.” It’s that **this particular injunction was a materially different request for relief that was not contained in the complaint**. A party can usually refine or support the remedies already pleaded, but cannot use summary judgment briefing to **expand the case to a new remedy theory that changes what the court is being asked to decide**.

A useful way to think about it:

- **Permissible:** “I pleaded injunctive relief, and this is the specific injunction that fits that pleaded claim.”
- **Not permissible without amendment:** “I pleaded one kind of injunction, but now I want the court to order a different, more specific affirmative command against a specific policy.”

That is why the court said the rescission issue was **not properly before it** and should have been raised by **amending the complaint**, not by briefing.
