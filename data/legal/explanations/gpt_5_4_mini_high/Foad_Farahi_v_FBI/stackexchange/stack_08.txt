Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Foad Farahi v. Federal Bureau of Investigation"

### How can 10,000+ pages and 80 CDs contain no reasonably segregable nonexempt information?
Question:
The segregability holding is what confuses me most. The FBI located about 10,750 pages and 80 CDs, and the court still accepted that no meaningful nonexempt information was reasonably segregable from the withheld material. I understand that isolated words or phrases don't have to be released if they are just random scraps, but it still seems intuitive that some factual material should be separable from exempt material in a file that large. How do courts decide when nonexempt information is too intertwined to require disclosure, and how much explanation must the agency give to justify withholding entire records?
Answer:
The short answer is: **FOIA segregability is not a volume test**. A giant file does not create a presumption that something releasable must be in it.

Under FOIA, an agency must release **“any reasonably segregable portion”** of a record after deleting exempt material. But the key word is **reasonably**. If the nonexempt material is so intertwined with exempt material that separating it would leave only scraps, phrases, or fragments with no real informational value, the agency may withhold the record in full.

That is exactly the doctrine the court invoked here:

- **Whole records may be withheld** when nonexempt portions are **“inextricably intertwined”** with exempt portions.
- A court may rely on agency declarations that explain, with **reasonable specificity**, why further segregation is not possible.
- And as the opinion notes, the FOIA inquiry is about **information**, not just isolated words on a page.

So the fact that the FBI located **10,750 pages and 80 CDs** does not by itself mean there must be meaningful segregable material. Those records can still consist largely of ongoing-investigation material, repetitive entries, or fragments that would be meaningless if stripped of the exempt context. The D.C. Circuit specifically said that while **individual words or phrases** might technically be severable, that is “neither here nor there” if what remains is not **meaningful information**.

On the explanation point: the agency does **not** have to give a public page-by-page explanation for every withheld document. What it must do is give enough detail for the court to assess segregability. Here, the district court initially wanted more information, and the FBI provided updated public and **ex parte** declarations; after reviewing those, the court was satisfied that no meaningful nonexempt information was reasonably segregable.

So the holding is not “nothing in a huge file can ever be segregated.” It is: **on this record, and based on the sealed and public declarations, the FBI showed that any remaining nonexempt material was too intertwined and too trivial to require disclosure.**
