Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Pacito v. Trump"

### Does 8 U.S.C. § 1522 impose a mandatory duty to provide refugee resettlement services, or merely authorize the Executive to do so?
Question:
The majority and Judge Lee seem to read § 1522 in very different ways. The panel points to language like "shall" and specific subsections requiring the government to provide certain services, especially for unaccompanied minors and recent refugees. Judge Lee says the section's title and the repeated use of "authorized" in subsections (b)-(e) show that the statute is basically permissive, with subsection (a) just supplying conditions if the Executive chooses to fund programs. How would a court ordinarily resolve this kind of textual conflict between 'authorized' and 'shall'? Is the majority reading § 1522 as a mandatory spending statute, or only as a mandatory program-administration statute once appropriated funds exist?
Answer:
**Short answer:** In this order, the **majority reads § 1522 as imposing mandatory duties on the Executive to provide specified refugee resettlement services, but only within the limits of available appropriations**. It is **not** reading the statute as a freestanding command that Congress must appropriate money. So the better description is **mandatory program administration once funding exists**, not a mandatory spending statute in the sense of requiring new appropriations.

### How the panel is reading the statute

The majority focuses on the operative text, especially:

- **§ 1522(a)(1)(A)**: the Director must act **“to the extent of available appropriations”** and is told that, in providing assistance, he **“shall”** make resources available, provide English training opportunities, ensure cash assistance is made available in a non-discouraging way, and ensure equal opportunities for women.
- **§ 1522(d)(2)(B)**: during the interim period for an unaccompanied refugee child, the Director **“shall assume legal responsibility”** if necessary.
- **§ 1522(e)(7)(A)**: the Secretary **“shall develop and implement”** alternative projects for refugees under 36 months in the U.S.

On that reading, Congress did more than merely authorize a program; it **directed that certain services be provided**. The order then says the government is “not likely to prevail” because the statute makes these services **“statutorily mandated”** and directs reinstatement of cooperative agreements needed to provide them.

### How Judge Lee reads it

Judge Lee reads the same statute as **permissive overall**:

- The title says **“Authorization for programs...”**
- Subsections **(b)–(e)** repeatedly say the Director/Secretary is **“authorized”** to provide assistance.
- Section **(a)**, in her view, supplies only **conditions and considerations** if the Executive chooses to operate those authorized programs.

So, for Judge Lee, the statute authorizes refugee resettlement services but does not require the Executive to keep the programs running.

### How courts ordinarily resolve “authorized” vs. “shall”

Ordinarily, a court tries to **harmonize** the provisions rather than let one word erase the other:

1. **“Shall” is ordinarily mandatory** unless context shows otherwise.
2. **“Authorized” is ordinarily permissive**, but it does not necessarily negate other mandatory language in the same statute.
3. **A title cannot override operative text**; it can help resolve ambiguity, but it is not controlling.
4. If there is tension, courts usually read the statute so that **the authorization to create a program and the duty to administer it in a certain way can coexist**.

That is essentially what the majority appears to be doing here: reading **“authorized”** in subsections **(b)–(e)** as granting program authority, while reading **“shall”** in **(a), (d), and (e)** as imposing binding obligations on how the authorized program must operate.

### Bottom line

So, based on this opinion:

- **Not** a pure mandatory spending statute.
- **Yes** to a mandatory duty to provide the specified resettlement services **once appropriations are available**.
- Judge Lee would call the whole scheme authorization-only, but the panel majority is treating at least some of § 1522 as **mandatory program requirements** for admitted refugees, limited by the phrase **“to the extent of available appropriations.”**

Since this is still a **stay order pending a later merits opinion**, it reflects the panel’s interim reading rather than a final merits holding—but that is the reading the majority is currently applying.
