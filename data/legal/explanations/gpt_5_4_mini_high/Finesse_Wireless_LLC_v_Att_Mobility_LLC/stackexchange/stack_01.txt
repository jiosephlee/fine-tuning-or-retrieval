Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Finesse Wireless LLC v. AT&T Mobility LLC"

### Why does the Federal Circuit apply Fifth Circuit law to JMOL and new-trial issues in a patent appeal?
Question:
I'm confused about the standard of review discussion at the beginning of the opinion. This is a patent case in the Federal Circuit, but the court says it reviews the denial of JMOL and the denial of a new trial under Fifth Circuit law, while it reviews claim construction under Federal Circuit law. How does that split work? Is the idea that some issues are considered 'procedural' and therefore governed by the regional circuit, while patent-specific substantive issues stay under Federal Circuit precedent? If so, where exactly is the line drawn?
Answer:
Yes — that’s basically the right way to think about it, but the cleaner way to say it is:

**The Federal Circuit uses regional-circuit law for ordinary civil-procedure issues, and Federal Circuit law for patent-specific issues.**

That is exactly what the panel is doing in *Finesse Wireless*.

### Why JMOL and new trial are under Fifth Circuit law
A motion for JMOL under Rule 50 and a motion for a new trial under Rule 59 are **general post-trial procedural motions**, not patent doctrines. So the Federal Circuit applies the **law of the regional circuit where the district court sits**. Here, the case was tried in the Eastern District of Texas, so the relevant regional circuit is the Fifth Circuit.

That’s why the opinion says:

> “We review a district court’s denial of JMOL under the law of the regional circuit, here the Fifth Circuit.”  
> “We review a district court’s denial of a motion for a new trial under regional circuit law.”

The Federal Circuit cites *Wi-LAN* for that rule.

### Why claim construction is under Federal Circuit law
Claim construction is different because it is **patent-specific**. It defines the scope of the patent right itself, so the Federal Circuit applies its own precedent to keep patent law uniform nationwide.

That is why the same opinion says:

> “We review claim construction de novo…”

and then applies Federal Circuit claim-construction doctrine.

### Where the line is drawn
The line is not perfectly “procedural vs. substantive,” though that’s a useful shorthand. More accurately:

- **Regional circuit law** governs **general civil-procedure issues** and other questions **not unique to patent law**  
  - e.g., JMOL, new trial, many evidentiary and trial-management questions
- **Federal Circuit law** governs **issues unique to patent law**  
  - e.g., claim construction, infringement standards, validity doctrines, prosecution-history disclaimer

### In this opinion
The court is doing both at once:

1. **Regional circuit law** tells the court how to review the district court’s denial of JMOL/new trial.
2. **Federal Circuit patent law** tells the court how to interpret the patent claims and assess the infringement verdict.

So the split is not accidental — it is the Federal Circuit’s standard choice-of-law approach in patent appeals.
