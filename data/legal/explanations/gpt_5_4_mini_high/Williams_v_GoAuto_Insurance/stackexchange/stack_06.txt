Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### How did deciding only the cancellation-procedure issue justify dismissing all claims with prejudice?
Question:
The opinion says the parties filed summary judgment motions 'solely' on whether GoAuto's cancellation procedure complied with Louisiana law. But the complaint also alleged misrepresentations and breach of good faith and fair dealing. After finding no statutory violation, the district court dismissed all claims against GoAuto with prejudice, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed. Was every other claim necessarily derivative of the theory that the policies were never effectively cancelled? I'm trying to understand the procedural link between winning on the statutory-compliance issue and wiping out the entire case against GoAuto.
Answer:
Yes — in this opinion, the cancellation question was the **dispositive predicate issue** for the claims against GoAuto.

The Fifth Circuit’s framing makes that clear. It says the former insureds “claim[ed] GoAuto’s cancellation procedures did not comply with the statute, **resulting in misrepresentations and breach of good faith and fair dealing**.” In other words, the misrepresentation and bad-faith theories were not independent freestanding claims; they were tied to the alleged premise that the policies were **not effectively cancelled** under Louisiana law.

That matters because under Louisiana law, as the opinion notes, if the statutory cancellation procedure is not strictly followed, “the cancellation [is] ineffective.” So the plaintiffs’ case against GoAuto depended on proving that the cancellations were invalid. Once the district court held — and the Fifth Circuit affirmed — that GoAuto’s procedure **did comply** with La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550, the plaintiffs lost the factual/legal premise needed for every claim against GoAuto based on ineffective cancellation.

That is why the district court could dismiss “all claims against GoAuto with prejudice” after resolving the single cancellation issue on summary judgment:

- **If cancellation was invalid**, plaintiffs might have a basis to say the policies remained in force and GoAuto’s conduct was wrongful.
- **If cancellation was valid**, then that theory collapses, and the related misrepresentation/good-faith claims fail as a matter of law.

So the procedural link is that the parties’ summary-judgment motions were directed to the one issue that underlay the entire case against GoAuto. Once that issue was decided in GoAuto’s favor, there was nothing left to adjudicate, and dismissal with prejudice was the proper merits disposition.
