Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "Williams v. GoAuto Insurance"

### How can a premium finance company cancel an insurance policy under a power of attorney?
Question:
I'm confused about the basic setup of this case. APAC wasn't the insurer; it was the company that financed the insureds' premiums. But the opinion says the finance agreements gave APAC a power of attorney allowing it to cancel the GoAuto policies if the insureds defaulted on their installment payments. Normally I think of cancellation as something the insurer does. Is APAC legally acting as the insured's agent when it sends the cancellation request, or is this some special statutory mechanism unique to premium finance arrangements? I'm trying to understand why Louisiana lets a third-party finance company trigger cancellation at all.
Answer:
Yes. In Louisiana, this is a **special statutory premium-finance cancellation procedure**, and the **power of attorney in the finance agreement is what lets the finance company act for the insured for this limited purpose**.

The Fifth Circuit’s description of the statute is the key:

- Under **La. Rev. Stat. § 9:3550(G)(3)(a)**, when the insured defaults, the **insurance-premium finance company may request cancellation** by sending the insurer a copy of the notice of cancellation **together with a certification** that:
  1. the premium finance agreement contains a **valid power of attorney**,
  2. the agreement is in default and not cured,
  3. notice was sent to the insured, and
  4. notice was sent to other interested persons.

- Then, under **§ 9:3550(G)(3)(b)(i)**, **upon receipt** of that notice and statement, the insurer may proceed to cancel.

So APAC is **not acting as the insurer** and it is **not cancelling the policy in the ordinary sense by itself**. Rather, it is acting **as the insured’s authorized agent under the power of attorney**, within a **statutorily created mechanism** for financed policies.

That is why the court emphasized that Louisiana requires **strict adherence** to the statute: if the finance company does not comply exactly, the cancellation is ineffective. The statute is what makes the third-party trigger lawful; the power of attorney is the insured’s authorization that the statute requires.

In short: **APAC can trigger cancellation because Louisiana law expressly permits a premium finance company, armed with a valid power of attorney, to request cancellation after default.**
