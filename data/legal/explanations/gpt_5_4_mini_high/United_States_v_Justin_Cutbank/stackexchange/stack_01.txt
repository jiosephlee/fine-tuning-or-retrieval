Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### When is prior possession of the same gun treated as intrinsic evidence instead of Rule 404(b) propensity evidence?
Question:
In this felon-in-possession case, two witnesses were allowed to testify that they had seen the defendant with the same sawed-off rifle before the charged night. I thought evidence of prior bad acts or prior gun possession usually raises Rule 404(b) problems. The Eighth Circuit says this was "intrinsic evidence" relevant to proving possession and showing "continuous possession" of the same firearm. What exactly makes evidence intrinsic here, and why doesn't the jury just use it as forbidden propensity evidence?
Answer:
In *Cutbank*, the prior-firearm testimony was treated as **intrinsic** because it went to the **charged possession itself**, not to some separate “other act” used to show bad character.

The Eighth Circuit’s point is basically this:

- The government had to prove Cutbank **possessed a firearm** on the charged night.
- The witnesses said they had seen him with **the same sawed-off Marlin .22** before that night.
- That makes the prior sightings **directly relevant** to whether he possessed **that same gun** during the charged incident.
- It also supports the idea of **continuous possession** of the firearm before the offense.

That is why the court quoted *Battle*:  
> “Prior possession of a firearm is directly relevant to proving later possession of that same weapon because it helps establish ownership or control of the weapon.”

So the evidence is not being used for the forbidden inference, “he handled guns before, so he’s the kind of person who commits gun crimes.” Instead, the inference is narrower and non-propensity-based:

> “He had this same gun before, so he likely had it when the offense occurred.”

That is an **element-proving** inference, not a character inference.

### Why this avoids Rule 404(b)
Rule 404(b) applies to **other acts** offered to prove propensity. But when the evidence is about the **same firearm** and helps prove **possession, ownership, control, or continuity**, the Eighth Circuit treats it as **intrinsic** to the offense itself. In other words, it is part of the story of the charged crime, not an unrelated prior bad act.

### Important caveat
Calling evidence “intrinsic” does **not** mean it is automatically admissible. It still has to survive ordinary relevance and Rule 403 balancing. But the key point is that it is **not barred by 404(b)** when it directly proves the charged possession of the same gun.

So in *Cutbank*, the prior sightings were admissible because they were evidence that he possessed **that specific rifle**, not evidence that he was generally a gun possessor.
