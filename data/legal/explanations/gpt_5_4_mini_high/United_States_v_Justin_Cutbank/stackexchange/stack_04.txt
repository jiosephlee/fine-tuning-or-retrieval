Title: Stack Exchange about the opinion: "United States v. Justin Cutbank"

### Why couldn't defense counsel impeach the witness by reading from a police report the witness did not write?
Question:
The defense wanted to cross-examine C.F. using prior statements she allegedly made to a police investigator, but the district court would not let counsel read portions of the police report into the record because the report was not authored by C.F. The Eighth Circuit said extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement must still be authenticated. I'm confused about what foundation would have been enough here. If the report records what the witness said, why isn't that enough for impeachment?
Answer:
Because the defense was trying to use **extrinsic evidence** of a prior inconsistent statement, and extrinsic evidence still has to be **authenticated** before it can be read to the jury.

The key distinction in the opinion is this:

- **Cross-examining C.F. about what she allegedly said** to police was allowed.
- **Reading from the police report itself** was not allowed without foundation, because the report was **written by the investigator, not by C.F.**

That matters because a police report is not automatically the witness’s own statement. It is the officer’s document, which may contain:

1. the officer’s summary or paraphrase,
2. the officer’s wording, and
3. only partly, the witness’s actual words.

So before counsel could read the report as impeachment, they needed to lay a foundation showing that the excerpt was in fact **a reliable record of C.F.’s prior statement**—not just the officer’s characterization of it.

That is what the Eighth Circuit meant when it said:

> “Although extrinsic evidence may be used for impeachment by prior inconsistent statement, such extrinsic evidence must still be authenticated.”  
> *United States v. Cumbie*, quoted in the opinion.

### What would have been enough?
The opinion doesn’t spell out every possible method, but the basic foundation would be something like:

- testimony from the **officer who took the statement** or wrote the report, confirming that C.F. made the statement and that the report accurately reflects it; or
- some other basis showing the report excerpt is a **true and accurate account** of what C.F. said.

Without that, the court was entitled to treat the report as an unauthenticated writing authored by someone else.

### Why isn’t the report enough by itself?
Because impeachment by prior inconsistent statement is about the **witness’s words**, not the investigator’s writing. If the report is not authenticated, the jury would be hearing an out-of-court document without assurance that it accurately captured C.F.’s statement. That is why the court allowed questioning about the statement but not reading the report verbatim into evidence.

So the short answer is: **the defense could impeach C.F. with her prior statement, but not by reading an unauthenticated police report that merely recorded that statement.**
