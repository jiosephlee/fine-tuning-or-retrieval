\begin{abstract}
Building on advancements like BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, the landscape of 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs) is rapidly evolving. In this study, we present a 1-bit LLM variation called \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, which represents all model parameters as ternary values chosen from \{-1, 0, 1\}. This architecture achieves parity with full-precision Transformer LLMs (using FP16 or BF16), maintaining similar levels of perplexity and performance across downstream tasks, while dramatically reducing costs in latency, memory usage, computational throughput, and energy demands. Significantly, the 1.58-bit LLM establishes a novel scaling law and training methodology that enables both efficient and high-performing future generations of LLMs. Moreover, this development introduces new computational paradigms and paves the way for specialized hardware tailored for 1-bit LLM workloads.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

The proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to considerable advances in artificial intelligence, particularly within natural language processing. Although these increasingly powerful models exhibit outstanding results across diverse tasks, their scale raises substantial obstacles for deployment and has intensified worries regarding the environmental footprint and financial cost associated with their significant energy demands.

A promising solution to these issues lies in post-training quantization, which seeks to compress model weights and activations by reducing their numerical precision~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. This technique substantially decreases both the memory load and computation required for LLM inference, supporting a shift from 16-bit representations down to even 4-bit alternatives~\cite{gptq, awq}. Nevertheless, despite its widespread adoption in production LLMs, post-training quantization remains a sub-optimal strategy.

Recent advancements in 1-bit model architectures, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, suggest a viable pathway for lowering the operational costs of LLMs without sacrificing accuracy. Standard LLMs typically utilize 16-bit floating-point representations (either FP16 or BF16), and their computations are largely dominated by matrix multiplications. Consequently, the primary computational expense arises from floating-point addition and multiplication. In contrast, BitNet's matrix multiplication exclusively utilizes integer addition, resulting in substantial reductions in energy consumption for LLMs. Given that power often constitutes the fundamental bottleneck in modern hardware accelerators, these energy savings may directly enhance computational throughput.

Beyond computation, the expense associated with shuttling model parameters from DRAM into the on-chip accelerator memory (such as SRAM) during inference is notable. Efforts to expand SRAM have aimed to improve throughput, but such expansions can incur costs far exceeding those of DRAM. 1-bit LLMs, in comparison to their full-precision counterparts, possess considerably smaller memory requirements in terms of both capacity and bandwidth. This property leads to reductions in both the financial and temporal costs of loading weights from DRAM, facilitating faster and more efficient inference.

Here, we present a novel 1-bit LLM extension named \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, characterized by ternary parameters that can assume values in \{-1, 0, 1\}. By introducing the zero value to the initial 1-bit BitNet design, BitNet b1.58 effectively encodes its parameters using 1.58 bits per entry in binary terms. This new variant preserves all key advantages of the original BitNet, including its fundamentally distinct computation framework that eliminates almost all multiplication during matrix multiplications and offers high optimization potential. BitNet b1.58 maintains equivalent energy efficiency and continues to excel in memory use, throughput, and latency relative to FP16 LLM benchmarks. In addition, BitNet b1.58 confers two further benefits: first, the integration of zero supports explicit feature filtering, which enhances its representational capacity and can lead to notable performance gains for 1-bit LLMs; second, experimental evidence demonstrates that, beginning at the 3B parameter scale and with equivalent configurations (such as model size and training tokens), BitNet b1.58 matches FP16 models in terms of perplexity and downstream task performance.

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} builds upon the BitNet model, a Transformer variant in which the standard \emph{nn.Linear} layers are substituted by \emph{BitLinear} layers. Training is performed from the ground up, employing weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations at 8 bits. While closely related to the initial BitNet, this version features several notable modifications, outlined below.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} To limit the weights to values of -1, 0, or +1, we employ the \emph{absmean} quantization function. This approach initially rescales the weight matrix using the mean of its absolute values and subsequently maps each entry to the closest integer in \{-1, 0, +1\}:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\end{equation}

The activation quantization strategy remains consistent with the implementation in BitNet, aside from a key change: activations are not rescaled to the interval $[0, Q_b]$ prior to the application of non-linearities. Rather, they are uniformly normalized to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ on a per-token basis, thereby eliminating zero-point quantization. This adjustment not only streamlines both coding and system optimization but, according to our experiments, incurs negligible impact on overall model performance.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

LLaMA~\cite{llama, llama2} serves as a predominant template for open-source LLM backbones. To better align with the open-source community, the architecture of \text{BitNet b1.58} incorporates components inspired by LLaMA. In particular, it utilizes RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, and rotary embeddings~\cite{rope}, and excludes bias parameters throughout the network. This design choice ensures that \text{BitNet b1.58} can be easily plugged into widely used open-source toolkits, such as Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}}, requiring minimal adaptation.

\section{Results}

We benchmarked \text{BitNet b1.58} against our FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} replication across multiple model scales. For an equitable evaluation, both architectures underwent pre-training on the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama} for 100 billion tokens. Their zero-shot capabilities were then tested across a diverse set of language understanding tasks: ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. In addition, we assessed validation perplexity using the WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4} corpora.

The runtime GPU memory footprint and inference latency were evaluated for both \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58}. All measurements were carried out using the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} framework, which provides efficient implementations for large-scale language model inference on GPUs. To further optimize \text{BitNet b1.58}, Ladder’s 2-bit kernel~\cite{ladder} was incorporated. We recorded the inference time per generated token, as this metric predominantly determines overall serving costs.

Perplexity and associated computational costs are provided in Table~\ref{tab:ppl} for both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. Notably, starting from the 3B parameter scale, \text{BitNet b1.58} approaches the perplexity achieved by the full-precision \text{LLaMA LLM}, while realizing 2.71x higher inference throughput and using only 28\% (i.e., 3.55x less) GPU memory. Specifically, with the 3.9B configuration, \text{BitNet b1.58} delivers 2.4x faster inference, requires 3.32x less memory, and also exceeds the performance of \text{LLaMA LLM} at the 3B scale.

Detailed breakdowns of zero-shot accuracy on downstream tasks can be found in Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot}. The assessment employed the \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}} methodology. These outcomes indicate a diminishing accuracy gap between \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} as model sizes scale up. Of particular significance, from the 3B size onward, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves parity with the full-precision reference. Mirroring perplexity trends, \text{BitNet b1.58} with 3.9B parameters outperforms \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B in both task accuracy and resource efficiency, underscoring its Pareto superiority over leading LLM systems.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

We expanded our experiments to include models of 7B, 13B, and 70B parameters and systematically assessed the associated computational cost. Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} presents the patterns observed in both latency and memory usage. Notably, the speed-up effect is more pronounced with increased model scale. Specifically, \text{BitNet b1.58} at 70B parameters demonstrates a 4.1-fold reduction in inference time relative to the \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline. This improvement can be attributed to the fact that the computational burden of \emph{nn.Linear} layers grows proportionally with model size. A parallel pattern appears in memory usage: since the embedding layer remains at full precision, its contribution to total memory consumption decreases as the overall model becomes larger. The reported values for both latency and memory are based on inference with a 2-bit kernel, leaving additional potential for future cost optimizations.

\paragraph{Energy}

Our analysis also includes an estimation of the energy required for arithmetic operations in both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. The evaluation focuses predominantly on matrix multiplication, which is the primary driver of computational cost in LLMs. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:energy}, most of the energy for \text{BitNet b1.58} comes from INT8 addition, whereas \text{LLaMA LLM} consumes energy through both FP16 addition and multiplication. Based on the energy consumption model outlined in~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, matrix multiplications on 7nm process nodes using \text{BitNet b1.58} achieve a 71.4-fold reduction in arithmetic operations energy compared to \text{LLaMA LLM}. Furthermore, we calculated end-to-end energy consumption for processing 512 tokens, demonstrating that \text{BitNet b1.58} grows even more energy efficient relative to the FP16-based \text{LLaMA LLM} as model size increases. This trend arises because the relative computational load of \emph{nn.Linear} grows with model scale, whereas other components contribute less to the overall cost in larger architectures.

\paragraph{Throughput}

To assess throughput, we conducted a comparison between \text{BitNet b1.58} and the \text{LLaMA LLM} model with 70B parameters, deploying both on two 80GB A100 GPUs. Pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe} was utilized to accommodate the 70B \text{LLaMA LLM} across the devices. The batch size was progressively increased up to the available GPU memory limit, maintaining a sequence length of 512 throughout the evaluation. According to Table~\ref{tab:throughput}, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B is capable of supporting batch sizes that are up to eleven times larger than those manageable by \text{LLaMA LLM}. This translates into throughput that is 8.9 times higher for \text{BitNet b1.58}.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling law connecting model efficiency and inference cost.} Drawing from the data in Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and Figure~\ref{fig:energy}, an approximate correspondence between models operating in 1.58-bit and 16-bit precision can be established as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The 13B BitNet b1.58 outperforms the 3B FP16 LLM regarding latency, memory footprint, and energy efficiency.
    \item The 30B BitNet b1.58 surpasses the 7B FP16 LLM on the same three efficiency measures.
    \item The 70B BitNet b1.58 exceeds the 13B FP16 LLM in latency, memory use, and energy consumption.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

Token count is a key determinant in the development of LLMs. To evaluate token-level scaling for \text{BitNet b1.58}, we trained this model on 2T tokens, following the training data methodology employed for StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, the leading open-source 3B model. Both \text{BitNet b1.58} and StableLM-3B were assessed using a benchmark suite comprised of Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}. Table~\ref{tab:2t} presents the reported zero-shot accuracy; for tasks quantified by accuracy and normalized accuracy, their mean is used. The reported performance of StableLM 3B at 2T tokens is sourced directly from its original technical report. Our evaluation demonstrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} delivers consistently higher results across all benchmark tasks, providing evidence that 1.58-bit LLMs exhibit strong generalization as well.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as an efficient and scalable option for large language models, primarily by reducing the overall computational FLOPs required. However, their practical scalability has been constrained by high memory demands and the overhead incurred through inter-device communication. Adopting 1.58-bit LLMs has the potential to alleviate these issues. By lowering the memory footprint, fewer hardware units are necessary to host MoE models. In addition, the reduced size of activations helps minimize network communication costs during activation transfer. Ideally, this could enable entire models to reside on a single processing chip, thereby removing such overheads altogether.

\textbf{Native Support of Long Sequence in LLMs}

Efficient handling of long sequences has become increasingly essential for modern LLM applications. A persistent obstacle in long-sequence inference is the extensive memory consumption related to key-value (KV) caches. \text{BitNet b1.58} marks a significant advance by compressing activations from 16 bits to 8 bits, effectively doubling the maximum sequence length manageable with fixed resources. For 1.58-bit LLMs, activations could be compressed even further—to 4 bits or less—without loss of information, a direction we plan to investigate in future research.

\textbf{LLMs on Edge and Mobile}

Deploying 1.58-bit LLMs offers significant improvements for running language models on edge and mobile hardware, where constraints in both memory and processing power often limit model size and effectiveness. Due to their substantially decreased memory and energy needs, 1.58-bit LLMs can be more readily deployed on such resource-constrained platforms, unlocking a host of applications that previously would have been impractical. This paves the way for more sophisticated uses of LLMs in portable contexts. Additionally, since 1.58-bit LLMs are more compatible with CPUs—which dominate edge and mobile systems—\text{BitNet b1.58} can be executed efficiently on these platforms, offering further performance and capability gains.

\textbf{New Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Emerging efforts, such as those exemplified by Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}}, illustrate the feasibility and advantages of specialized hardware (e.g., LPUs) optimized for LLM workloads. Building on these developments, we propose and encourage the design of dedicated hardware and systems tailored specifically to the needs and computational properties of 1-bit LLMs, in light of the innovative computational paradigm established by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}.
