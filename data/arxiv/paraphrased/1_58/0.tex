\title{The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits}

\begin{abstract}
Emerging developments such as BitNet~\cite{bitnet} are ushering in innovative directions for 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs). In this study, we propose a ternary 1-bit LLM variant called \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, where each model parameter (weight) can take values from the set \{-1, 0, 1\}. This approach demonstrates that, for an equivalent model size and number of training tokens, our ternary model can achieve perplexity and downstream task results on par with its full-precision counterparts (FP16 or BF16 Transformers), yet offers remarkable reductions in latency, memory usage, throughput requirements, and energy demands. Significantly, this 1.58-bit LLM formulation establishes a novel scaling law and provides a new methodology for training future high-performing, cost-efficient LLMs. Additionally, it sets the stage for an alternative computational paradigm, enabling specialized hardware architectures optimized specifically for 1-bit LLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

Over the past few years, there has been substantial progress in the development and scalability of Large Language Models (LLMs) within the AI domain. While these models exhibit impressive abilities across numerous natural language processing applications, their ever-expanding scale introduces difficulties in practical implementation and intensifies worries about both ecological and financial costs arising from elevated energy requirements.

A common strategy for tackling these issues involves applying post-training quantization to produce low-bit models suitable for inference~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. By lowering the bit-width of both weights and activations, this method substantially decreases the memory footprint and computational demands of LLMs. Recent developments have focused on transitioning from 16-bit representations down to more compact 4-bit formats~\cite{gptq, awq}. Nevertheless, despite its prevalence in production-level LLM deployments, post-training quantization remains a sub-optimal solution.

Recent advancements in 1-bit model designs, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, indicate a valuable approach for lowering the computational expenses of LLMs without sacrificing effectiveness. Standard LLM implementations utilize 16-bit floating-point representations (such as FP16 or BF16), and matrix multiplication accounts for most of their computational workload. Consequently, the predominant source of computational overhead arises from floating-point arithmetic operations. In comparison, BitNet restricts its matrix multiplications to integer addition, resulting in substantial reductions in energy consumption for LLMs. Given that power consumption often sets the upper bound for computation on many hardware platforms, these energy reductions may also contribute to increased processing speed.

Beyond computation, moving model parameters from DRAM into the on-chip accelerator's memory, such as SRAM, can impose substantial overhead during inference. Efforts to increase the size of SRAM for enhanced throughput often result in costs that greatly exceed those of DRAM. 1-bit LLMs, relative to their full-precision counterparts, demand considerably less memory, both in terms of storage capacity and bandwidth. As a result, transferring weights from DRAM becomes less resource-intensive and quicker, enabling more rapid and cost-effective inference.

In this study, we present a notable 1-bit LLM variation termed \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, wherein each model parameter is ternary, assuming values from the set \{-1, 0, 1\}. By incorporating an additional 0 value into the standard 1-bit BitNet framework, the model operates at 1.58 bits within the binary numeral system. \text{BitNet b1.58} preserves all major strengths of its 1-bit predecessor, including its novel computation scheme that almost entirely eliminates multiplication in matrix operations, allowing for extensive optimization. It also maintains the same energy usage as the original 1-bit BitNet, while offering superior efficiency in memory, throughput, and latency relative to FP16 LLM baselines. Moreover, \text{BitNet b1.58} introduces two key improvements. First, it enhances representational power by explicitly supporting feature filtering—enabled by permitting weight values of 0—which considerably boosts the capabilities of 1-bit LLMs. Second, experimental results indicate that, beginning at the 3B parameter scale, \text{BitNet b1.58} is able to achieve parity with full-precision (i.e., FP16) baselines on both perplexity and downstream task metrics under comparable configurations (e.g., model size and training data volume).

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} utilizes the BitNet architecture, a type of Transformer where the traditional \emph{nn.Linear} layer is substituted with \emph{BitLinear}. The model is initialized and trained from the beginning, employing weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations at 8 bits. It departs from the original BitNet by incorporating several adjustments, outlined as follows.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} To ensure weights are limited to the set \{-1, 0, +1\}, we employ the \emph{absmean} quantization function. This function initially normalizes the weight matrix by dividing by its mean absolute value, followed by rounding each entry to its nearest value in \{-1, 0, +1\}:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\end{equation}

The quantization method applied to activations mirrors that utilized in BitNet, with the exception that, prior to the application of non-linear functions, activations are not rescaled to the $[0, Q_b]$ interval. Rather, for each token, activations are mapped to the range $[-Q_b, Q_b]$, thereby eliminating the necessity for zero-point quantization. This adjustment streamlines both the implementation and broader system optimization processes, and according to our experimental results, has only a minimal impact on performance.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

The LLaMA~\cite{llama, llama2} architecture has become the standard foundation for many open-source LLM initiatives. To align with and support this ecosystem, \text{BitNet b1.58} has been engineered with LLaMA-inspired structural elements. Notably, our model incorporates RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, rotary embedding~\cite{rope}, and excludes all bias terms. This design choice ensures that \text{BitNet b1.58} can be readily deployed within major open-source platforms such as Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}}, requiring minimal adaptation.

\section{Results}

We conducted a comparative analysis between \text{BitNet b1.58} and our re-implemented FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} across multiple parameter scales. Both models underwent pre-training for 100 billion tokens using the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama} to maintain experimental parity. For evaluation, we assessed zero-shot generalization on several language understanding benchmarks, including ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. Additionally, we measured validation perplexity using the WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4} corpora.

We evaluated both the runtime GPU memory usage and inference latency for \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58}. The measurements were obtained utilizing the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} framework, an inference engine tailored for low-latency LLM execution on GPUs. Additionally, for \text{BitNet b1.58}, we incorporated the Ladder~\cite{ladder} 2-bit kernel into our testing environment. To highlight the dominant expense in inference, we present the results in terms of time consumed per generated output token.

Table~\ref{tab:ppl} presents both the perplexity values and computational costs associated with \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. The results indicate that, in terms of perplexity, \text{BitNet b1.58} reaches parity with the full precision \text{LLaMA LLM} at a 3B parameter scale, yet achieves 2.71 times higher speed and utilizes 3.55 times less GPU memory. Notably, the 3.9B variant of \text{BitNet b1.58} is 2.4 times faster, requires 3.32 times less memory, and delivers considerably superior performance compared to the 3B version of \text{LLaMA LLM}.

Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot} presents a comprehensive breakdown of zero-shot accuracy across the evaluated end tasks. For this assessment, we adhered to the methodology outlined by \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}}. The data indicate that, with increased model size, the difference in performance between \text{BitNet b1.58} and the \text{LLaMA LLM} diminishes. Notably, from the 3B model size onwards, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves parity with the full-precision baseline. Echoing the trends observed in perplexity, the outcomes on end tasks highlight that \text{BitNet b1.58} at 3.9B surpasses \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B, all while requiring less memory and offering reduced latency. These results underscore \text{BitNet b1.58} as a Pareto improvement over current state-of-the-art LLMs.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

We extended our experiments by increasing the model sizes to 7B, 13B, and 70B, subsequently analyzing the associated costs. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy}, both latency and memory utilization exhibit clear scaling patterns, with acceleration gains becoming more pronounced as model size grows. Notably, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B demonstrates a 4.1-fold improvement in speed compared to the \text{LLaMA LLM} reference model. This improvement is attributed to the fact that the computational overhead of \emph{nn.Linear} increases with larger model sizes. Memory usage follows a comparable pattern because, although embeddings are maintained in full precision, their relative memory footprint diminishes as model scale increases. All measurements for latency and memory employed a 2-bit kernel, indicating that additional cost reductions are feasible with further optimization.

\paragraph{Energy}

We additionally analyze the energy requirements associated with arithmetic operations for both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. The analysis predominantly emphasizes matrix multiplication, as it represents the principal energy expenditure in large language models. Figure~\ref{fig:energy} displays the breakdown of energy consumption. Most of the energy consumed by \text{BitNet b1.58} originates from INT8 addition operations, whereas \text{LLaMA LLM} expends energy primarily on FP16 additions and multiplications. Referencing the energy estimation model from~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, \text{BitNet b1.58} demonstrates a 71.4-fold reduction in arithmetic operations energy for matrix multiplications on 7nm technology compared to its counterpart. We extend this analysis by providing end-to-end energy consumption measurements for models processing 512 tokens. Our findings indicate that as model sizes increase, \text{BitNet b1.58} exhibits a greater relative advantage in energy efficiency over the FP16-based \text{LLaMA LLM}. This trend emerges because the proportion of energy devoted to \emph{nn.Linear} operations rises with larger models, while the contribution from other parts becomes less significant as model scale increases.

\paragraph{Throughput}

We evaluate the throughput performance of \text{BitNet b1.58} and the 70B-parameter \text{LLaMA LLM} across two 80GB A100 GPUs, employing pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe} to enable execution of the \text{LLaMA LLM} 70B model. By progressively increasing the batch size to the maximum capacity permitted by the GPU memory at a fixed sequence length of 512, we observed notable differences. As presented in Table~\ref{tab:throughput}, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B accommodates a batch size up to 11 times larger than that of \text{LLaMA LLM}, which translates to an 8.9-fold improvement in throughput.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling relationship between model accuracy and inference resource demands}. By examining Figures~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and \ref{fig:energy}, we observe the following correspondences for model size efficiency when comparing 1.58-bit and 16-bit representations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The 13B BitNet b1.58 outperforms the 3B FP16 LLM regarding latency, memory footprint, and energy usage.
    \item The 30B BitNet b1.58 demonstrates greater efficiency than the 7B FP16 LLM across latency, memory requirements, and energy consumption.
    \item The 70B BitNet b1.58 surpasses the 13B FP16 LLM in latency, memory usage, and energy savings.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

The quantity of training tokens plays a significant role in the effectiveness of LLMs. To assess how \text{BitNet b1.58} performs when scaled with respect to token count, we trained a \text{BitNet b1.58} model on 2T tokens, adhering to the data preparation strategy employed by StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, which represents the current leading open-source 3B model. Evaluation for both models was conducted using a suite of benchmarks, including Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}. The zero-shot accuracy outcomes are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:2t}. For tasks assessed with both accuracy and normalized accuracy metrics, we averaged the two scores. The results for StableLM 3B trained on 2T tokens are referenced from its official technical report. Our analysis demonstrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} consistently outperforms its counterpart across all evaluated tasks, suggesting that 1.58-bit LLMs are also highly capable of generalizing to diverse tasks.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have demonstrated notable cost-efficiency for large language models (LLMs). Although MoE methods decrease computation in terms of FLOPs, their utility is constrained by substantial memory demands and considerable inter-chip communication requirements. The advent of 1.58-bit LLMs offers solutions to these obstacles. The decreased memory usage permits MoE models to be deployed across fewer devices. In addition, lower memory requirements translate to a marked reduction in the communication overhead for transmitting activations between nodes. Ideally, if the whole model fits within the memory of one chip, communication overhead is eliminated altogether.

\textbf{Native Support of Long Sequence in LLMs}

With the rise of LLMs, efficiently managing extended input sequences has emerged as a key requirement. A significant obstacle in processing lengthy sequences is the substantial memory usage caused by KV cache storage. \text{BitNet b1.58} makes considerable progress toward enabling native support for longer contexts by lowering activation precision from 16 bits to 8 bits. This reduction permits a doubling of context window length while maintaining identical resource constraints. Moreover, activations can potentially be compressed losslessly to 4 bits or below, particularly for 1.58-bit LLMs—a direction we propose for future investigation.

\textbf{LLMs on Edge and Mobile}

Deploying 1.58-bit LLMs presents a significant opportunity to boost the effectiveness of language models operating on edge and mobile platforms. Such platforms frequently encounter constraints in both memory and processing capacity, which can hamper the implementation and efficiency of traditional LLMs. With their notably lower demands for memory and power, 1.58-bit LLMs can be feasibly integrated into these environments, unlocking new possibilities for applications that were once infeasible. As a result, this advancement has the potential to substantially broaden the functionality of edge and mobile devices, fostering innovative uses of LLMs. In addition, 1.58-bit LLMs are especially compatible with CPUs—the primary type of processor in edge and mobile contexts—rendering it possible for models such as \text{BitNet b1.58} to operate with heightened efficiency and further expand the computational capabilities of these devices.

\textbf{New Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Recent developments such as~Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}} have shown encouraging outcomes and significant promise in constructing specialized hardware, such as LPUs, tailored for LLMs. Building on these advancements, we propose and advocate for the creation of novel hardware and system architectures that are explicitly fine-tuned for 1-bit LLMs, taking advantage of the innovative computation paradigm introduced in BitNet~\cite{bitnet}.
