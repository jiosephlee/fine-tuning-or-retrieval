\begin{abstract}
The latest advances, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, are ushering in a new generation of 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs). In this study, we present a novel 1-bit LLM called \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, which is characterized by ternary parameters \{-1, 0, 1\} for all weights. Remarkably, BitNet b1.58 attains parity with full-precision (FP16 or BF16) Transformer LLMs of comparable size and token count regarding both perplexity and downstream performance, while offering substantial gains in efficiency—drastically reducing latency, memory usage, computational throughput, and energy demand. Perhaps most significantly, our 1.58-bit LLM establishes a fresh scaling law and provides new guidelines for training future LLMs that do not compromise on performance while delivering considerable efficiency. In addition, it supports the development of novel computational paradigms and lays the groundwork for specialized hardware tailored to 1-bit LLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

Artificial intelligence has recently experienced swift advancement in both the scale and effectiveness of Large Language Models (LLMs). These models have achieved outstanding results across various tasks in natural language processing. However, the escalation in their parameter counts has made practical deployment increasingly challenging, and it has sparked significant debate over sustainability concerns related to elevated energy demands and costs.

A viable solution to these issues is the adoption of post-training quantization techniques to compress models for inference~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. By reducing the bit-width of model weights and activations, quantization notably lowers memory overheads and computational expense for LLMs. While the field has moved from standard 16-bit formats to more compact 4-bit alternatives~\cite{gptq, awq}, post-training quantization remains an imperfect method, despite its extensive adoption within industrial applications of LLMs.

Advancements in 1-bit neural network architectures, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, offer a compelling avenue for lowering the operational costs of LLMs without compromising their efficacy. Traditional LLMs typically employ 16-bit floating-point representations (such as FP16 or BF16), and the most resource-intensive component is their extensive reliance on matrix multiplications. The predominant cost stems from floating-point arithmetic operations. In contrast, BitNet's matrix multiplications require only integer additions, leading to substantial reductions in energy consumption. Since power often dictates the ceiling of computational performance for hardware accelerators, these efficiency gains also translate into accelerated computation.

Beyond computational efficiency, the movement of model weights from off-chip DRAM to on-chip memory (for example, SRAM) represents another significant expense during inference. While increasing the size of on-chip SRAM can bolster throughput, it does so at a much higher price than DRAM. 1-bit LLMs, however, substantially minimize memory usage in terms of both required storage capacity and memory bandwidth relative to full-precision counterparts. This can greatly curtail both the time and expense associated with loading parameters from DRAM, thus enabling more rapid and efficient inference.

This paper introduces a notable 1-bit LLM design termed \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, in which every parameter adopts a ternary value from \{-1, 0, 1\}. The augmentation of a zero value extends the original 1-bit BitNet to an effective precision of 1.58 bits per parameter in binary notation. \text{BitNet b1.58} preserves all advantageous properties of the original BitNet, particularly the shift to a computation model that eschews most multiplications during matrix operations, offering substantial opportunities for further computational optimization. Its memory, throughput, and latency advantages over FP16 LLMs are maintained, and its energy profile remains consistent with its predecessor. Moreover, \text{BitNet b1.58} provides two noteworthy improvements: first, the incorporation of the 0 state enhances its representational strength by facilitating explicit feature selection within the weight tensors, which can lead to measurable gains in 1-bit LLM performance. Second, empirical results demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves parity with full-precision FP16 models regarding both perplexity and downstream task accuracy, beginning at a 3B parameter scale under identical training and model conditions.

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} derives from the BitNet framework, a type of Transformer architecture where the standard \emph{nn.Linear} operations are substituted with \emph{BitLinear} modules. The model is trained entirely from the beginning, employing weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations quantized to 8 bits. In contrast to the initial BitNet, several adjustments have been incorporated, outlined as follows.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} To restrict weight values to the set $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, we utilize an \emph{absmean} quantization approach. This procedure rescales the weight matrix by its mean absolute value before rounding every entry to the nearest value in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\end{equation}

The activation quantization utilizes the same methodology as that of BitNet, except that activations are not rescaled to the interval $[0, Q_b]$ prior to the application of nonlinearities. Instead, activations are adjusted within $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ for each token, thereby eliminating the necessity for zero-point quantization. This adjustment enhances simplicity and efficiency from an implementation and system design perspective, while empirical results demonstrate that any impact on model performance is marginal.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

The LLaMA architecture~\cite{llama, llama2} has become the standard foundation for many open-source large language models. Aligning with open-source developments, our construction of \text{BitNet b1.58} leverages components inspired by LLaMA. More precisely, we incorporate RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, rotary embeddings~\cite{rope}, and omit all bias terms. This compatibility ensures that \text{BitNet b1.58} is easily adoptable in popular open-source libraries (such as Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}}) with minimal modification required.

\section{Results}

We conducted a comparison between \text{BitNet b1.58} and our re-implemented FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} models across multiple scales. Both model families were pre-trained on the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama} using 100 billion tokens to standardize experimental conditions. For evaluation, we measured zero-shot accuracy across several benchmark language understanding tasks, including ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. Additionally, we computed the validation perplexity on WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4} datasets to further assess model quality.

We also examined GPU memory consumption and inference latency for both \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58}. Measurements were taken utilizing the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} framework, which provides optimized GPU-based large language model inference. The Ladder~\cite{ladder} library’s 2-bit kernel was included for \text{BitNet b1.58}. The primary inference cost—runtime per generated token—was reported.

Table~\ref{tab:ppl} provides an overview of perplexity metrics alongside computational cost for both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. At the 3B parameter scale, \text{BitNet b1.58} attains comparable perplexity to its full-precision \text{LLaMA LLM} counterpart while being 2.71 times quicker and requiring 3.55 times less GPU memory. Notably, the 3.9B version of \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves a 2.4$\times$ speedup, uses 3.32$\times$ less memory, and surpasses \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B in performance.

A comprehensive breakdown of zero-shot task performance is presented in Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot}. The evaluation adhered to the protocol in \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}}. The outcomes indicate that the accuracy differential between \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} diminishes as model scale grows, with \text{BitNet b1.58} reaching parity with the full-precision baseline at the 3B parameter threshold. In agreement with the perplexity findings, \text{BitNet b1.58} 3.9B surpasses \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B on downstream tasks while imposing significantly reduced resource requirements. This establishes \text{BitNet b1.58} as a clear Pareto improvement relative to contemporary state-of-the-art language models.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

The study also expanded the model sizes to 7B, 13B, and 70B, with a corresponding assessment of computational cost. As depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy}, both latency and memory usage exhibit a consistent pattern, where larger models benefit from greater speed-up. Notably, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B demonstrates a 4.1-fold speed improvement compared to the \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline. This acceleration stems from the fact that the computational overhead for \emph{nn.Linear} escalates with increased model size. Memory utilization mirrors this trajectory; although the embedding layer retains full precision, its relative contribution to total memory consumption declines as the model size grows. All measurements for latency and memory leveraged a 2-bit kernel, indicating further opportunities for cost reduction through future optimizations.

\paragraph{Energy}

We have additionally analyzed the energy requirements associated with arithmetic operations in both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. Our investigation centers on matrix multiplication, which predominates in determining LLM computational expenses. Figure~\ref{fig:energy} presents the breakdown of energy usage, showing that \text{BitNet b1.58} primarily relies on INT8 addition operations, whereas \text{LLaMA LLM} predominantly performs both FP16 addition and FP16 multiplication. Based on the energy model described in~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves a 71.4-fold reduction in energy consumed by arithmetic operations for matrix multiplication when using 7nm chips. We also report holistic energy expenditures for full model inference on sequences containing 512 tokens. The findings indicate that \text{BitNet b1.58} yields progressively greater energy efficiency gains relative to the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline as model size increases. This improvement results from the enlarging proportion of \emph{nn.Linear} computations in bigger models, while the cost contribution of other components diminishes.

\paragraph{Throughput}

We assess the throughput capabilities of \text{BitNet b1.58} in comparison to the \text{LLaMA LLM} with 70B parameters, utilizing two 80GB A100 GPUs and employing pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe} to enable execution of the \text{LLaMA LLM} 70B model on this hardware. The batch size was incrementally increased until the GPU memory limit was reached, maintaining a sequence length of 512. According to the results in Table~\ref{tab:throughput}, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B accommodates a batch size up to eleven times greater than that of \text{LLaMA LLM}, yielding a throughput improvement by a factor of 8.9.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling law linking model capability to inference efficiency.} Based on findings illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and \ref{fig:energy}, a correspondence can be drawn between certain 1.58-bit and 16-bit model sizes regarding performance and resource demands.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The 13B BitNet b1.58 model surpasses the 3B FP16 LLM in terms of latency, memory consumption, and energy efficiency.
    \item The 30B BitNet b1.58 outperforms the 7B FP16 LLM across latency, memory use, and energy expenditure.
    \item The 70B BitNet b1.58 exhibits better latency, memory, and energy metrics than the 13B FP16 LLM.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

Training token quantity critically impacts LLM outcomes. To examine the token scalability of \text{BitNet b1.58}, we trained a model with 2T tokens using the data strategy of StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, the leading open-source 3B model. Both models were assessed using a benchmark suite comprising Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}, with zero-shot accuracies documented in Table~\ref{tab:2t}. For benchmarks reporting both accuracy and normalized accuracy, their mean is used. StableLM 3b results at 2T tokens are cited from its technical documentation. The results demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} outperforms on every evaluated task, implying that 1.58-bit LLMs possess robust generalization abilities.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has established itself as an efficient solution to reduce computational FLOPs in large language models (LLMs). Nevertheless, the substantial memory demands and significant inter-chip communication required remain obstacles to broader adoption and deployment. These issues are effectively mitigated by employing 1.58-bit LLMs. The decreased memory footprint minimizes the hardware resources needed for deploying MoE architectures and markedly lessens the volume of activation data transmitted over communication links. Ideally, this approach enables fitting the entire MoE model onto a single chip, thereby eliminating interconnect overhead altogether.

\textbf{Native Support of Long Sequence in LLMs}

The capability to process long sequences natively has emerged as an essential requirement for contemporary LLM applications. A key bottleneck in long sequence inference involves the substantial memory utilized by KV caches. \text{BitNet b1.58} advances the support for extended context by compressing activation precision from 16 bits down to 8 bits, which effectively doubles the maximum manageable sequence length with unchanged memory resources. Furthermore, additional lossless compression is conceivable, possibly achieving 4-bit storage or even less with 1.58-bit LLMs—a direction we aim to explore in future research.

\textbf{LLMs on Edge and Mobile}

The introduction of 1.58-bit LLMs provides an opportunity to enhance the practicality of language models on edge and mobile platforms, which typically have stringent constraints regarding memory and computational throughput. These limitations often curtail both the capability and size of deployable models. Thanks to their lower memory usage and energy demands, 1.58-bit LLMs facilitate deployment on such devices, unlocking a range of use cases that were previously unattainable. This development substantially broadens the potential of edge and mobile computing by enabling more sophisticated applications for LLMs. Furthermore, the architecture of 1.58-bit LLMs is particularly well-suited for CPUs, the predominant compute unit in edge and mobile hardware, thereby permitting efficient execution of \text{BitNet b1.58} and maximizing performance gains for these platforms.

\textbf{New Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Innovations exemplified by~Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}} underscore the promise of developing dedicated hardware platforms—such as LPUs—tailored for LLM acceleration. Building on this momentum, we propose the design and development of new hardware and system architectures optimized for 1-bit LLM computation, taking advantage of the novel computational framework pioneered by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}.
