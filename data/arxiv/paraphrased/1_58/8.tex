\title{The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits}

\begin{abstract}
Recent advancements, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, are heralding a transformative period characterized by 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs). In this paper, we present \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, a 1-bit LLM variant where each model parameter is limited to ternary values drawn from \{-1, 0, 1\}. This approach achieves parity with conventional full-precision Transformer LLMs—those trained in FP16 or BF16—regarding both perplexity and downstream task results, provided model size and training data are kept constant. Moreover, this methodology offers substantial reductions in latency, memory footprint, throughput limitations, and energy demands. Importantly, the 1.58-bit LLM introduces an updated scaling law along with methodologies for the next wave of LLMs that aim for both superior performance and efficiency. Beyond algorithmic advancements, it encourages new computational strategies and paves the way for the development of hardware purpose-built for 1-bit LLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

The past few years have witnessed significant expansion in both the scope and size of Large Language Models (LLMs), yielding exceptional outcomes across diverse natural language processing applications. However, this relentless scaling up introduces considerable obstacles in practical deployment and exacerbates concerns regarding the financial and ecological tolls, especially owing to the surging energy requirements.

One solution gaining traction involves employing post-training quantization to generate compact, low-bit models for inference~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. This strategy lowers the numerical precision of the weights and activations, thereby substantially reducing memory consumption and computational burden for LLMs. While the community has moved from traditional 16-bit representations down to 4-bit alternatives~\cite{gptq, awq}, post-training quantization remains a compromise. Although pervasive in current industrial applications of LLMs, this approach falls short of optimality.

Recent advancements in 1-bit model designs, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, offer encouraging possibilities for cutting down the operational costs of LLMs without significant loss in accuracy. Standard LLMs typically utilize 16-bit floating-point formats (such as FP16 or BF16), with matrix multiplication constituting the predominant operation. Consequently, the principal computational expense in these models arises from floating-point arithmetic operations, namely addition and multiplication. In contrast, BitNet achieves matrix multiplication using only integer addition, thereby resulting in a substantial reduction in energy consumption associated with LLMs. Given that power constraints often define the computational limits of hardware, these energy savings can also be harnessed to accelerate computation.

Beyond compute efficiency, the transfer of model parameters from DRAM to on-chip memory (e.g., SRAM) during inference is another resource-intensive aspect. While expanding SRAM capacities has been considered as a way to enhance throughput, this strategy substantially increases hardware costs relative to DRAM. When contrasted with full-precision networks, 1-bit LLMs exhibit a dramatically reduced memory requirement, both in terms of overall storage and memory bandwidth. This reduction facilitates faster, more cost-effective loading of model parameters from DRAM, which in turn yields speedier and more efficient inference processes.

Within this context, we present a new 1-bit LLM model named \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, where each parameter can take one of three possible values: \{-1, 0, 1\}. This innovation extends the original 1-bit BitNet, which lacked the zero value, leading to a representation size of 1.58 bits in the binary system. \text{BitNet b1.58} preserves all the principal advantages of BitNet, including its novel approach to computation—greatly diminishing multiplication operations required for matrix products and enabling significant optimization. Additionally, it continues to deliver the low energy consumption characteristic of the original, and achieves marked improvements in memory efficiency, throughput, and latency over conventional FP16-based LLMs. Moreover, \text{BitNet b1.58} introduces two notable benefits: it enhances model expressiveness by enabling explicit feature filtering, thanks to the inclusion of zero weights—a mechanism that substantially boosts the capability of 1-bit LLMs; and our experimental results demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} can achieve parity with full-precision (FP16) models, matching their performance in terms of perplexity and downstream tasks from a 3B parameter scale onward, assuming identical configurations in model scale and training corpus.

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} builds upon the BitNet framework, a Transformer model in which \emph{nn.Linear} layers are substituted with \emph{BitLinear} counterparts. This variant is trained from the ground up using weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations quantized to 8 bits. In relation to the original BitNet, several adjustments have been incorporated, as detailed below.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} To ensure that weight values are limited to the set \{-1, 0, +1\}, we employ an \emph{absmean} quantization method. This approach rescales the weight matrix by its mean absolute value, and each entry is subsequently quantized to the nearest element in \{-1, 0, +1\}:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\end{equation}

For the activation quantization, we maintain the procedure used in BitNet. However, rather than scaling the activations prior to non-linear functions into the interval $[0, Q_b]$, we rescale them to the range $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ for each token, thereby omitting zero-point quantization. This modification both simplifies the implementation and is beneficial for system-level efficiency, without causing any substantial degradation in performance according to our experimental evaluations.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

The LLaMA architecture~\cite{llama, llama2} has become the standard template for open-source LLM development. In an effort to facilitate integration with the broader open-source ecosystem, \text{BitNet b1.58} adopts components similar to those in LLaMA. Specifically, it incorporates RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, rotary embedding~\cite{rope}, and excludes bias terms from the model. This architectural alignment ensures that \text{BitNet b1.58} can be readily deployed in major open-source platforms (including Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}}) with minimal adaptation required.

\section{Results}

We conducted a comparative study between \text{BitNet b1.58} and our own implementation of FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} across multiple parameter scales. To maintain experimental integrity, both model families were pre-trained on the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama}, with exposure to 100 billion tokens. The models were evaluated in a zero-shot setting on a diverse suite of language benchmarks: ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. Additionally, validation perplexity metrics were obtained on the WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4} corpora.

For inference benchmarking, both the GPU memory usage and inference latency of \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58} were systematically measured. These experiments employed the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} framework, recognized for its efficient LLM inference capabilities on GPUs. Integration of the 2-bit kernel from Ladder~\cite{ladder} enabled optimal deployment of \text{BitNet b1.58}. As inference cost is predominantly influenced by the time needed per generated token, this metric was specifically reported.

The comparative results in Table~\ref{tab:ppl} highlight both perplexity and resource requirements for \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. Notably, at the 3B parameter scale, \text{BitNet b1.58} matches the full-precision \text{LLaMA LLM} in perplexity while achieving a 2.71$\times$ speedup and a 3.55$\times$ reduction in GPU memory consumption. Specifically, \text{BitNet b1.58} at 3.9B parameters demonstrates a 2.4$\times$ speed advantage, a 3.32$\times$ lower memory footprint, and clear gains over the \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B counterpart.

Detailed zero-shot accuracy findings are presented in Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot}. We adopted the evaluation protocol of \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}} for consistency. Results indicate a diminishing performance gap between \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} as model capacity increases. Crucially, at and beyond the 3B scale, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves parity with the full-precision baseline. Echoing the perplexity trends, end-task evaluations further illustrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} 3.9B surpasses \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B in accuracy while operating at reduced memory and latency costs, signifying a Pareto improvement over established LLM architectures.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

We extended our experiments to encompass model sizes of 7B, 13B, and 70B, performing a corresponding analysis of computational cost. Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} presents the observed patterns for both latency and memory usage, demonstrating that performance improvements become more pronounced with larger models. Notably, \text{BitNet b1.58} at 70B achieves a 4.1-fold reduction in inference time relative to the \text{LLaMA LLM} reference model. This performance gain can be attributed to the scaling behavior of \emph{nn.Linear}, where its computational demands increase in line with model size. A similar relationship is found for memory utilization; since embeddings remain at full precision, their relative memory impact diminishes in larger architectures. Measurements for latency and memory relied on a 2-bit computational kernel, indicating potential for additional optimizations that could yield further reductions in resource requirements.

\paragraph{Energy}

We assessed the estimated energy consumed by arithmetic operations in both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}, with special emphasis on matrix multiplication, which predominantly influences large language model computational expense. The breakdown of energy usage is visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:energy}. In the case of \text{BitNet b1.58}, INT8 additions comprise the main portion of computation, while \text{LLaMA LLM} operations are divided between FP16 additions and multiplications. Drawing on the energy model from~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, we determined that \text{BitNet b1.58} delivers a 71.4-fold decrease in energy consumption for matrix multiplications when implemented on 7nm technology. Additionally, we evaluated total energy costs in end-to-end settings for sequences of 512 tokens. Our findings reveal that the energy efficiency of \text{BitNet b1.58} relative to the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} improves further as model scale increases. This trend results from the expanding proportion of computational workload assigned to \emph{nn.Linear} layers with larger models, coupled with a decreasing share of cost arising from other architectural components.

\paragraph{Throughput}

We evaluate and contrast the throughput achieved by \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} with 70B parameters, both executed on two 80GB A100 GPUs. To make it feasible for \text{LLaMA LLM} 70B to fit into device memory, we employ pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe}. The batch size is incrementally raised until the GPU memory ceiling is reached, using a fixed sequence length of 512. As presented in Table~\ref{tab:throughput}, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B accommodates up to 11 times greater batch sizes than \text{LLaMA LLM}, yielding an overall throughput boost of 8.9 times.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling law linking model effectiveness and inference efficiency.} Based on Figures~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and \ref{fig:energy}, we observe the following correspondences between model sizes for 1.58-bit and 16-bit representations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The 13B BitNet b1.58 surpasses the 3B FP16 LLM in latency, memory footprint, and energy demands.
    \item The 30B BitNet b1.58 delivers better efficiency in terms of latency, memory requirements, and energy usage when compared to the 7B FP16 LLM.
    \item The 70B BitNet b1.58 proves more resource-efficient—regarding latency, memory consumption, and energy—than the 13B FP16 LLM.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

The total number of training tokens is an essential consideration for LLM development. To evaluate the scalability of \text{BitNet b1.58} with respect to data volume, we trained it on 2T tokens using the data strategy outlined in StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, a leading open-source model at the 3B parameter scale. Both \text{BitNet b1.58} and StableLM-3B underwent evaluation across a benchmark suite including Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}. Zero-shot accuracy scores are reported in Table~\ref{tab:2t}. For benchmarks with both accuracy and normalized accuracy metrics, we average their values. The StableLM-3B results at the 2T token scale are quoted directly from its original report. Our experimental outcomes demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} delivers better performance across all evaluation tasks, underscoring the strong generalization ability of 1.58-bit LLMs.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm has established itself as an efficient and scalable strategy for large language models (LLMs), notably decreasing the total number of floating-point operations required. However, two persistent bottlenecks—substantial memory demands and extensive inter-chip communication—hinder broader deployment. These constraints can be alleviated by employing 1.58-bit LLM architectures. With a minimized memory requirement, fewer computational devices are necessary for MoE implementation. Additionally, communication overhead for activation transfers is greatly reduced, and, in optimal scenarios where the entire model fits onto a single chip, these overheads are essentially eliminated.

\textbf{Native Support for Long Sequence Processing in LLMs}

As LLMs become increasingly prevalent, native handling of long sequences has emerged as a paramount feature. The primary technical obstacle for processing extended sequences during inference stems from the substantial memory needs associated with storing KV caches. The \text{BitNet b1.58} model marks meaningful progress by halving activation precision from 16 bits to 8 bits, thereby enabling a twofold increase in context length without added resource costs. Moreover, by further compressing activations—potentially down to 4 bits or even less for 1.58-bit LLMs, without loss of information—there remains significant opportunity for further improvement, which is reserved for future exploration.

\textbf{Deployment of LLMs on Edge and Mobile Platforms}

Integrating 1.58-bit LLMs offers considerable benefits for resource-constrained environments such as edge and mobile devices. Limitations in both memory capacity and computational resources on these platforms typically restrict LLM adoption. However, the lower memory and power requirements of 1.58-bit LLMs make it feasible to deploy substantial models, opening up new possibilities and extending capabilities for these devices. Such advances expand the application landscape for LLMs in mobile and edge computing. Furthermore, since most edge and mobile environments predominantly utilize CPUs, and 1.58-bit LLMs are well-aligned with CPU execution, \text{BitNet b1.58} models can be operated with improved efficiency, delivering enhanced performance and expanding functionality in these domains.

\textbf{Emerging Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Recent innovations, such as those exemplified by Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}}, have highlighted the feasibility and potential benefits of developing dedicated hardware (such as language processing units, LPUs) tailored for LLM tasks. Building upon this foundation, we advocate for further research and innovation aimed at designing new computing architectures and systems expressly optimized for the computational patterns of 1-bit LLMs, as introduced by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, to fully leverage this emerging computation paradigm.
