\begin{abstract}
Emerging work, exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, heralds the advent of 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs). Here, we propose a new 1-bit LLM variant, \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, whose parameters are exclusively drawn from the ternary set \{-1, 0, 1\}. Remarkably, this approach achieves parity with full-precision Transformer LLMs (FP16 or BF16) of equivalent architecture and training scale, both in perplexity and downstream task efficacy. Moreover, this model offers substantial improvements in latency, memory usage, throughput, and energy efficiency. Beyond its efficiency gains, the 1.58-bit LLM introduces a novel scaling law and establishes a new methodology for training future LLMs that are simultaneously performant and resource-friendly. This innovation not only fosters a shift in computational paradigms but also creates new opportunities for hardware explicitly optimized for 1-bit LLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has accelerated dramatically, enabling unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding and generation. Nonetheless, as model sizes have grown, practical concerns around deployment have intensified, particularly regarding their escalating energy demands and resultant environmental as well as economic costs.

A prominent strategy to mitigate these issues is the application of post-training quantization methods, which aim to create compact, low-bitwidth models optimized for inference~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. These approaches operate by lowering the precision of both weights and activations, yielding significant savings in memory footprint and computation. There has been a continual movement from standard 16-bit formats toward even smaller representations, such as 4-bit models~\cite{gptq, awq}. However, while such quantization techniques are commonplace in industry applications of LLMs, they often result in less-than-ideal performance due to inherent limitations post-training.

Advancements in 1-bit model designs, as exemplified by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, indicate a promising avenue for reducing the computational demands of large language models (LLMs) without compromising on their efficacy. Conventional LLMs generally utilize 16-bit floating-point representations (such as FP16 or BF16), and most of their computational workload centers around matrix multiplications. As a result, the predominant source of computational expense stems from floating-point additions and multiplications. By contrast, BitNet confines its matrix multiplication operations to integer additions alone, achieving significant reductions in energy consumption for LLM inference. Since power is a primary constraint on computational throughput in many hardware architectures, these energy savings can be translated directly into increased computational speed.

Beyond just computation, the expense of moving model parameters from off-chip DRAM into on-chip memory resources (such as SRAM) is non-trivial during inference. Solutions have been proposed that scale up on-chip SRAM to enhance throughput, but this substantially increases system cost relative to DRAM. In contrast, 1-bit LLMs offer marked reductions in both memory bandwidth and storage requirements, alleviating bottlenecks associated with parameter loading from DRAM and improving inference speed and efficiency.

This paper presents \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, an innovative 1-bit LLM variant characterized by ternary parameters, each adopting one of \{-1, 0, 1\}. By extending the original BitNet’s binary representation with an explicit zero value, we arrive at an effective 1.58 bits per parameter in the binary system. \text{BitNet b1.58} preserves the advantageous computation scheme of BitNet—requiring minimal multiplication in matrix operations and remaining highly amenable to optimization. It matches the original BitNet’s energy profile while outperforming traditional FP16 LLMs regarding memory usage, data throughput, and latency. Importantly, \text{BitNet b1.58} introduces two distinct improvements: (1) the enhanced modeling capability enabled by ternary weights—specifically, the use of zero values allows for explicit feature selection and pruning, which notably boosts the representational power of 1-bit LLMs; and (2) empirical results demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} can reach parity with full-precision baselines (FP16) concerning both perplexity and downstream task metrics, given identical settings (model size, number of training tokens, etc.) for models starting at the 3B parameter scale.

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} extends the BitNet model, a Transformer variant that swaps the standard \emph{nn.Linear} layers for \emph{BitLinear} alternatives. This version is trained anew using weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations quantized to 8 bits. In relation to the original BitNet, this model includes several modifications, which we outline below.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} In order to limit the weights to values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, we utilize a quantization approach based on the \emph{absmean}. This method involves normalizing the weight matrix by dividing by the mean of the absolute values, followed by rounding each element to the nearest integer among $\{-1, 0, +1\}$:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\end{equation}

The same quantization method as used in BitNet is applied to the activations, with one key adjustment: rather than scaling pre-nonlinearity activations to fit $[0, Q_b]$, we rescale activations per token to the interval $[-Q_b, Q_b]$, effectively removing zero-point quantization. This adjustment streamlines both code implementation and system optimization, with our experiments showing minimal influence on performance.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

Given that the LLaMA~\cite{llama, llama2} architecture has become a standard foundation in the open-source LLM ecosystem, \text{BitNet b1.58} has been designed to closely mirror LLaMA's structure. This includes adopting RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, and rotary embeddings~\cite{rope}, as well as omitting all bias terms. As a result, \text{BitNet b1.58} can be readily deployed within widely-used open-source frameworks, such as Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}}, requiring only minor modifications.

\section{Results}

We conducted a comparison between \text{BitNet b1.58} and our implementation of the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} across multiple model scales. For consistency, both models underwent pre-training on the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama} using 100 billion tokens. We assessed zero-shot capabilities on a suite of language benchmarks, namely ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. Validation perplexity was also recorded on WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4}.

In terms of operational metrics, we evaluated GPU memory footprint and inference latency for both \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58}. Measurements were obtained utilizing the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} library, which is optimized for minimizing latency during LLM inference on GPUs. Notably, the 2-bit kernel from Ladder~\cite{ladder} was incorporated for \text{BitNet b1.58}. For inference efficiency, we reported latency as the time required per generated token.

Results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:ppl}, detailing both perplexity and computational costs for \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. The table reveals that, starting at a 3B model size, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves comparable perplexity to the full-precision \text{LLaMA LLM}, while delivering a 2.71$\times$ reduction in latency and a 3.55$\times$ decrease in GPU memory consumption. In particular, the 3.9B variant of \text{BitNet b1.58} is 2.4$\times$ faster and uses 3.32$\times$ less memory, yet substantially outperforms the \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B model.

Comprehensive zero-shot accuracy for all downstream tasks is presented in Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot}. We employed the protocol established by \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}} for these evaluations. The findings indicate that, as model size grows, the accuracy gap between \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} diminishes. Significantly, \text{BitNet b1.58} matches full-precision performance beginning from the 3B size. Consistent with perplexity results, on downstream tasks, \text{BitNet b1.58} 3.9B outperforms \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B, all while utilizing less memory and incurring lower latency. This evidence positions \text{BitNet b1.58} as a Pareto optimal enhancement over leading large language models.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

We expanded our experiments to include larger models at 7B, 13B, and 70B parameters, analyzing their associated costs. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy}, latency and memory usage trends reveal that acceleration gains improve with increased model scale. Notably, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B achieves a speed-up factor of 4.1 over the \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline, attributed to the fact that \emph{nn.Linear} computation time escalates as models grow. Memory usage displays a parallel pattern, since the embedding layer—remaining in full precision—accounts for a relatively smaller fraction of total memory in larger networks. Both performance metrics were gathered using a 2-bit kernel, implying that further reductions in cost are attainable through additional kernel optimization.

\paragraph{Energy}

To assess energy efficiency, we estimated the arithmetic operation energy consumption of both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. Our analysis prioritizes matrix multiplication, given its dominant contribution to the computational cost in large language models. Figure~\ref{fig:energy} depicts the breakdown of energy expenditure: most of the computation for \text{BitNet b1.58} is performed using INT8 addition, whereas \text{LLaMA LLM} primarily relies on FP16 addition and FP16 multiplication. Drawing on the energy model presented in~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, we determined that \text{BitNet b1.58} reduces matrix multiplication energy consumption for arithmetic operations by a factor of 71.4 on 7nm hardware. We also report end-to-end energy usage for processing 512-token sequences. The results indicate that as model size increases, \text{BitNet b1.58} demonstrates progressively greater energy efficiency relative to the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline. This improvement arises because the relative share of \emph{nn.Linear} operations grows with model scale, while the proportional costs of other components decline in larger models.

\paragraph{Throughput}

We assess the throughput of both \text{BitNet b1.58} and the 70B-parameter \text{LLaMA LLM} models using two 80GB A100 GPUs, leveraging pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe} to enable the execution of \text{LLaMA LLM} 70B on these devices. By incrementally increasing the batch size until the GPUs’ memory capacity was reached, while maintaining a sequence length of 512, we observed that \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B sustains a batch size up to 11 times larger than that of \text{LLaMA LLM}. This leads to an 8.9-fold increase in throughput, as presented in Table~\ref{tab:throughput}.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling dynamic between inference cost and model effectiveness.} Drawing on Figures~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and \ref{fig:energy}, we can express the following comparative relations for various model sizes using 1.58-bit versus 16-bit precision:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A 13B \text{BitNet b1.58} model surpasses a 3B FP16 LLM regarding latency, memory efficiency, and energy utilization.
    \item A 30B \text{BitNet b1.58} model outperforms a 7B FP16 LLM on latency, memory use, and power consumption.
    \item A 70B \text{BitNet b1.58} model is more advantageous than a 13B FP16 LLM with respect to latency, memory demands, and energy usage.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

Training token count plays a vital role in large language models. To evaluate the scalability of \text{BitNet b1.58} with respect to token volume, we trained a \text{BitNet b1.58} model using 2T tokens, adhering to the dataset preparation methods outlined for StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, which is recognized as the leading open-source model of its scale. Both models underwent evaluation on a composite benchmark comprising Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}. Zero-shot accuracy metrics are reported in Table~\ref{tab:2t}; for evaluations involving both accuracy and normalized accuracy, we report the mean value. StableLM 3b's 2T token performance metrics are sourced from its technical documentation. Our analysis demonstrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} consistently outperforms across all evaluated tasks, implying robust generalization for 1.58-bit LLMs.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as a resource-efficient strategy for large language models (LLMs), substantially lowering computational FLOPs. However, the practical deployment of MoE models remains hampered by substantial memory demands and significant inter-device communication costs. The introduction of 1.58-bit LLMs provides viable solutions to these limitations. Specifically, their reduced memory requirements decrease the number of devices necessary for deployment and dramatically lessen network activation transfer overhead. Ideally, with sufficient memory efficiency, entire MoE models might reside on a single chip, eliminating communication overheads altogether.

\textbf{Native Support of Long Sequence in LLMs}

Handling extensive input sequences has become increasingly crucial in LLM applications. One prominent issue is the elevated memory usage arising from key-value (KV) caches during inference on long sequences. \text{BitNet b1.58} addresses this concern by reducing activation precision from 16 bits to 8 bits, thereby enabling a twofold increase in context length with identical hardware constraints. Prospects exist for further, lossless compression—down to 4 bits or even less for 1.58-bit LLMs—suggesting promising avenues for subsequent investigation.

\textbf{LLMs on Edge and Mobile}

Deploying 1.58-bit LLMs can greatly enhance the feasibility and efficiency of language models on edge and mobile platforms, which commonly face strict limitations in terms of memory capacity and computational resources. The minimized memory and energy consumption characteristics of 1.58-bit LLMs enable their integration into such resource-constrained environments, broadening the spectrum of possible applications beyond previous constraints. As a result, these advancements could markedly elevate the functionality of edge and mobile devices. In addition, the architecture of 1.58-bit LLMs is well-suited to CPU-based systems—the predominant processing units in these devices—allowing \text{BitNet b1.58} to achieve higher operational efficiency and expanded capabilities on such platforms.

\textbf{New Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Innovative hardware solutions such as~Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}} illustrate the promise of developing dedicated platforms (e.g., LPUs) for LLM acceleration. Expanding on these efforts, we advocate for focused research and development targeting new hardware and system designs tailored specifically for 1-bit LLMs, motivated by the paradigm shift introduced by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}.
