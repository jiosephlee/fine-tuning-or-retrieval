\title{The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits}

\begin{abstract}
Recent advancements, like BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, are ushering in a new age of 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs). In this study, we present a 1-bit LLM variant called \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, where each parameter (or weight) of the LLM is ternarized to \{-1, 0, 1\}. This model achieves comparable performance to full-precision (i.e., FP16 or BF16) Transformer LLMs of equivalent model size and training data in terms of perplexity and downstream tasks, while significantly improving efficiency in latency, memory usage, throughput, and energy consumption. More importantly, the 1.58-bit LLM introduces a novel scaling law and training methodology for developing future LLMs that balance high performance with cost efficiency. Additionally, it establishes a new computational paradigm and facilitates the design of specialized hardware tailored for 1-bit LLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Era of 1-bit LLMs}

In recent years, the AI community has witnessed rapid expansion in the scale and capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). These models have exhibited exceptional results across various natural language processing applications; however, their growing sizes have led to deployment challenges and raised environmental and economic concerns due to substantial energy consumption.

One strategy to tackle these issues involves applying post-training quantization to produce low-bit models for inference~\cite{smoothquant, gptq, quip, quip_sharp}. This approach lowers the precision of weights and activations, thereby greatly decreasing the memory footprint and computational demands of LLMs. The prevailing trend has been to reduce bit-widths from 16 bits down to smaller precisions, such as 4-bit models~\cite{gptq, awq}. Nonetheless, post-training quantization remains suboptimal despite its widespread adoption in industry-scale LLMs.

Recent research on 1-bit model architectures, such as BitNet~\cite{bitnet}, offers a promising avenue for lowering the expense of LLMs while preserving their effectiveness. Standard LLMs utilize 16-bit floating-point representations (i.e., FP16 or BF16), and the majority of their operations consist of matrix multiplication. Consequently, the principal computational expense arises from floating-point addition and multiplication. In contrast, BitNet's matrix multiplication relies solely on integer addition, which drastically reduces the energy consumption associated with LLMs. Since power consumption often limits computational performance in many hardware chips, these energy savings can translate into accelerated computation.

Beyond computation, transferring model parameters from DRAM to the on-chip accelerator’s memory (e.g., SRAM) can incur significant costs during inference. Some approaches have attempted to increase SRAM size to enhance throughput, but this leads to substantially higher expenses compared to DRAM. Relative to full-precision models, 1-bit LLMs have a considerably smaller memory footprint in terms of both capacity and bandwidth. This reduction can greatly decrease the expense and latency of loading weights from DRAM, resulting in faster and more efficient inference.

In this paper, we propose a notable 1-bit LLM variant named \textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58}}, where each parameter is ternary, assuming values from \{-1, 0, 1\}. By introducing the additional 0 value to the original 1-bit BitNet, we achieve 1.58 bits in the binary system. \text{BitNet b1.58} preserves all advantages of the original 1-bit BitNet, including the novel computational approach that minimizes multiplication operations during matrix multiplication and allows for substantial optimization. It also maintains the same energy consumption as the original 1-bit BitNet, while exhibiting significantly better memory efficiency, throughput, and latency compared to FP16 LLM baselines. Moreover, \text{BitNet b1.58} provides two further benefits. First, its modeling power is enhanced because the explicit support for feature filtering—enabled by incorporating 0 into the model weights—can notably boost 1-bit LLM performance. Second, our experiments demonstrate that \text{BitNet b1.58} can achieve parity with full precision (i.e., FP16) baselines in terms of perplexity and end-task results, starting at a model size of 3B, when using identical configurations (e.g., model size, training tokens, etc.).

\section{BitNet b1.58}

\text{BitNet b1.58} is derived from the BitNet architecture, a Transformer model that substitutes \emph{nn.Linear} layers with \emph{BitLinear}. It is trained from scratch using weights quantized to 1.58 bits and activations quantized to 8 bits. In contrast to the original BitNet, several modifications have been made, which we outline below.

\paragraph{Quantization Function.} To restrict weights to the set \{-1, 0, +1\}, we employ an \emph{absmean} quantization method. This approach first normalizes the weight matrix by dividing it by its mean absolute value, then rounds each element to the closest integer within \{-1, 0, +1\}:

\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max\left(a, \min\left(b, \mathrm{round}(x)\right)\right),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\end{equation}

The activation quantization follows the same procedure as in BitNet, with one key difference: instead of scaling activations before the non-linear operations into the range $[0, Q_b]$, we scale them per token into the interval $[-Q_b, Q_b]$. This adjustment eliminates the need for zero-point quantization, simplifying both the implementation and system-level optimization while having a negligible impact on performance in our experiments.

\paragraph{LLaMA-alike Components.}

The LLaMA architecture~\cite{llama, llama2} has become the standard foundation for many open-source large language models. To align with the open-source ecosystem, \text{BitNet b1.58} incorporates LLaMA-inspired components. In particular, it integrates RMSNorm~\cite{rmsnorm}, SwiGLU~\cite{swiglu}, rotary embeddings~\cite{rope}, and omits all bias terms. Consequently, \text{BitNet b1.58} can be easily incorporated into widely used open-source frameworks such as Huggingface, vLLM~\cite{vllm}, and llama.cpp\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp}} with minimal modification.

\section{Results}

We conducted a comparison between \text{BitNet b1.58} and our replicated FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} across multiple model sizes. To guarantee an equitable comparison, both models were pre-trained on the RedPajama dataset~\cite{redpajama} using 100 billion tokens. We assessed zero-shot performance across various language benchmarks, such as ARC-Easy~\cite{arc}, ARC-Challenge~\cite{arc}, Hellaswag~\cite{hellaswag}, Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, OpenbookQA~\cite{openbookqa}, and BoolQ~\cite{boolq}. Additionally, we reported validation perplexity on the WikiText2~\cite{wikitext2} and C4~\cite{c4} datasets.

We also measured GPU memory usage and latency for both \text{LLaMA LLM} and \text{BitNet b1.58}. These metrics were obtained using the FasterTransformer\footnote{\url{https://github.com/NVIDIA/FasterTransformer}} framework, which is optimized for minimizing LLM inference latency on GPUs. For \text{BitNet b1.58}, the 2-bit kernel from Ladder~\cite{ladder} was integrated. We presented the timing per output token, as this is the primary contributor to inference cost.

Table~\ref{tab:ppl} provides a summary of perplexity and resource consumption for \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. It demonstrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves comparable perplexity to full precision \text{LLaMA LLM} beginning at a 3B model size, while delivering 2.71 times faster inference speed and utilizing 3.55 times less GPU memory. Notably, the 3.9B \text{BitNet b1.58} model is 2.4 times faster and requires 3.32 times less memory, yet significantly outperforms the \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B model.

Detailed zero-shot accuracy outcomes on downstream tasks are presented in Table~\ref{tab:zero-shot}. The evaluation pipeline followed \emph{lm-evaluation-harness}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}}. Results indicate that the performance gap between \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} diminishes as model size grows. Crucially, \text{BitNet b1.58} attains parity with the full precision baseline starting at 3B. Consistent with perplexity observations, the end-task results reveal that the 3.9B \text{BitNet b1.58} surpasses the \text{LLaMA LLM} 3B, all while reducing memory and latency demands. This illustrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} represents a Pareto improvement over current state-of-the-art LLMs.

\paragraph{Memory and Latency}

We expanded the model sizes to 7B, 13B, and 70B and assessed the associated costs. Figure~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} depicts the patterns of latency and memory usage, demonstrating that the speed-up improves as the model size increases. Notably, \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B operates 4.1 times faster than the \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline. This improvement arises because the computational expense of \emph{nn.Linear} scales with the model size. Memory usage exhibits a comparable pattern, as the embedding remains in full precision but constitutes a smaller fraction of memory in larger models. Both latency and memory metrics were obtained using a 2-bit kernel, indicating potential for further cost reductions through additional optimizations.

\paragraph{Energy}

We also estimated the energy consumption of arithmetic operations for both \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM}. Our primary focus was on matrix multiplication calculations, since they represent the largest energy cost component for LLMs. Figure~\ref{fig:energy} shows the breakdown of energy usage. The bulk of \text{BitNet b1.58}'s energy is consumed by INT8 addition operations, whereas \text{LLaMA LLM} involves both FP16 addition and FP16 multiplication. Based on the energy model from~\cite{energycost, pokebnn}, \text{BitNet b1.58} reduces arithmetic operation energy for matrix multiplication by a factor of 71.4 on 7nm chips. We also presented end-to-end energy costs for models processing 512 tokens. Our findings indicate that as model size increases, \text{BitNet b1.58} achieves greater energy efficiency relative to the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline. This efficiency gain is because the proportion of \emph{nn.Linear} operations rises with model size, while the relative cost from other components diminishes in larger models.

\paragraph{Throughput}

We evaluate the throughput of \text{BitNet b1.58} and \text{LLaMA LLM} with 70 billion parameters on two 80GB A100 GPUs, employing pipeline parallelism~\cite{gpipe} to enable running the \text{LLaMA LLM} 70B on these devices. We progressively increased the batch size until the GPU memory capacity was fully utilized, using a sequence length of 512. Table~\ref{tab:throughput} demonstrates that \text{BitNet b1.58} 70B can accommodate a batch size up to 11 times larger than that of \text{LLaMA LLM}, leading to an 8.9-fold increase in throughput.

\textbf{\text{BitNet b1.58} introduces a novel scaling law relating model performance to inference cost}. As a benchmark, we observe the following equivalences between models of various sizes using 1.58-bit and 16-bit precisions, based on results shown in Figures~\ref{fig:latency-memory-energy} and \ref{fig:energy}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The 13B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient regarding latency, memory consumption, and energy use compared to the 3B FP16 LLM.
    \item The 30B BitNet b1.58 model outperforms the 7B FP16 LLM in terms of latency, memory usage, and energy efficiency.
    \item The 70B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient in latency, memory, and energy metrics than the 13B FP16 LLM.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training with 2T Tokens}

The total number of training tokens is a key factor for LLM performance. To assess the token scalability of \text{BitNet b1.58}, we trained a \text{BitNet b1.58} model on 2 trillion tokens, following the data protocol used by StableLM-3B~\cite{StableLM-3B-4E1T}, currently the leading open-source 3B model. Both models were tested on a benchmark suite comprising Winogrande~\cite{winoGrande}, PIQA~\cite{piqa}, SciQ~\cite{sciq}, LAMBADA~\cite{lambada}, and ARC-easy~\cite{arc}. Zero-shot accuracy results are presented in Table~\ref{tab:2t}. For tasks evaluated with both accuracy and normalized accuracy, we report their average. The StableLM 3B results at 2T tokens are cited directly from its technical report. Our results indicate that \text{BitNet b1.58} surpasses StableLM on every evaluated task, demonstrating that 1.58-bit LLMs possess strong generalization abilities.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}

\textbf{1-bit Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have demonstrated a cost-efficient strategy for large language models (LLMs). Although they notably decrease computational FLOPs, their deployment and use are restricted by substantial memory demands and inter-chip communication overhead. These issues can be mitigated by 1.58-bit LLMs. First, the diminished memory usage reduces the number of devices necessary to run MoE models. Additionally, it greatly lessens the cost associated with transferring activations across networks. Ultimately, if the entire model fits onto a single chip, such overhead would be eliminated entirely.

\textbf{Native Support of Long Sequence in LLMs}

In the current LLM landscape, supporting long sequences natively has become a crucial requirement. A key obstacle in long sequence inference is the memory consumed by KV caches. \text{BitNet b1.58} marks a substantial advancement toward native long sequence support by cutting the activation bit-width from 16 bits to 8 bits, effectively doubling the context length within the same resource limits. This can potentially be further compressed losslessly down to 4 bits or even lower for 1.58-bit LLMs, which remains an avenue for future exploration.

\textbf{LLMs on Edge and Mobile}

Deploying 1.58-bit LLMs holds great promise for enhancing the performance of language models on edge and mobile platforms. Such devices often face constraints in memory capacity and computational resources, limiting the scale and effectiveness of LLMs. However, the lower memory and power consumption of 1.58-bit LLMs make it feasible to run them on these devices, opening possibilities for applications that were previously unattainable. This advancement can significantly boost the functionality of edge and mobile systems and facilitate novel use cases for LLMs. Furthermore, 1.58-bit LLMs are more compatible with CPUs, which predominantly serve as the main processors in edge and mobile devices. Consequently, \text{BitNet b1.58} can be executed efficiently on such hardware, further enhancing their performance and utility.

\textbf{New Hardware for 1-bit LLMs}

Recent developments such as Groq\footnote{\url{https://groq.com/}} have shown promising progress and potential in creating specialized hardware (e.g., LPUs) tailored for LLMs. Building upon this, we propose and encourage efforts to design novel hardware and systems specifically optimized for 1-bit LLMs, leveraging the new computational paradigm introduced by BitNet~\cite{bitnet}.
