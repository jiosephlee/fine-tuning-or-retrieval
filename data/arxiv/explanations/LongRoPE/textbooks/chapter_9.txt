\title{A Textbook about the Paper: LongRoPE}

Chapter 9: Results: From 4k to 2,048k

# Within 256k: decreasing perplexity with longer contexts; outperforming baselines despite much longer windows

One of the central empirical claims of LongRoPE is that extending an LLM's context window need not degrade language modeling performance at intermediate long lengths; instead, with the right positional interpolation and modest fine-tuning, the model can continue to benefit from additional context. This was evaluated by measuring perplexity across a range of evaluation lengths up to 256k using long-text datasets (PG19 and Proof-pile). Perplexity was computed with a sliding evaluation window of size 256 tokens, which isolates the model's next-token predictive performance as the model is exposed to progressively larger histories.

Across both LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B, models extended with LongRoPE show an overall decreasing perplexity trend as evaluation length increases from the pretraining baseline (4k) up to 256k. A decreasing perplexity as more context is available indicates that the model can make better next-token predictions when given access to longer histories — i.e., it is effectively leveraging the extra context. Crucially, LongRoPE-extended models outperform state-of-the-art baselines (models extended by PI, NTK-based strategies, and YaRN, as well as several released long-context fine-tuned models) across these lengths, despite operating with much larger nominal windows. This demonstrates that the non-uniform, empirically searched rescale factors preserve essential positional information and provide a superior initialization for fine-tuning than uniform or hand-crafted interpolation schemes.

Two practical points underlie these gains. First, LongRoPE's search prioritizes preserving dimensions and early token positions that are most information-bearing, reducing the information loss that simple linear interpolation (PI) or coarse grouped strategies (YaRN) can introduce. Second, the progressive fine-tuning strategy (fine-tune at 128k then 256k, using only a small number of steps relative to training from scratch) makes it feasible to reach large windows while still preserving short- and mid-range performance. The combination produces models that are not just capable of reaching a larger nominal context size but can actually utilize it in language modeling tasks up to 256k.

# Beyond 2M: stable perplexity on Books3 with sliding 256k window; differences between LLaMA2 and Mistral

LongRoPE's most ambitious result is extending models to a 2,048k (2.048 million) context window. To evaluate behavior at such extreme lengths, the authors measured perplexity on Books3 using a sliding evaluation window of 256k tokens across long books (20 randomly selected books each exceeding 2,048k tokens). This evaluation isolates how well the models handle very long-range dependencies in real long-form text.

For LLaMA2-7B extended with LongRoPE (with the progressive 128k/256k fine-tuning schedule and a second non-uniform interpolation applied post-finetuning), perplexity remains stable and generally improves as more context within the 256k sliding windows is provided, with only marginal increases observed around the very largest evaluation points (1024k and 2048k). This behavior indicates that LLaMA2 with LongRoPE is successfully exploiting long-range context over multi-hundred-thousand-token spans, and that the secondary non-uniform interpolation preserves sufficient positional discriminability even when the model's nominal window is expanded by hundreds of times.

Mistral-7B extended with LongRoPE shows a different profile. While Mistral achieves strong perplexity at shorter extended lengths (up to a few hundred thousand tokens) — outperforming baselines at shorter windows — its perplexity increases more notably beyond 256k, and in the Books3 runs it exceeded a perplexity of 7 for very long windows. The paper attributes this difference partly to differences in fine-tuning setup: for Mistral the authors followed YaRN's training configuration using 16k training lengths when fine-tuning at 128k and 256k, which can limit how well the model adapts to subsequent, larger secondary extensions. Operationally this means that although LongRoPE can produce a working 2,048k nominal window for both LLaMA2 and Mistral, the realized long-range modeling quality depends on the base model and fine-tuning regime; LLaMA2 benefited more from the 256k fine-tuning schedule in the paper, whereas Mistral performed better when fine-tuned at 128k under the chosen training setup.

# Passkey retrieval: ≥90% accuracy for LLaMA2 up to 2,048k; Mistral strong up to ~1,800k

Perplexity measures next-token predictive quality, but generation tasks reveal different practical constraints of long-context models. The paper uses a passkey retrieval synthetic task to assess whether the model can find a small target (a random five-digit passkey) buried somewhere in a very long document. This task is a strong test of the model’s effective context window in a retrieval-like, generation-oriented setting: success requires locating and producing the exact target token sequence despite massive distractors.

LongRoPE-extended LLaMA2-7B (fine-tuned to 256k as described) maintains high retrieval accuracy — at or above 90% — across evaluation context lengths from 4k up to 2,048k. Maintaining such a high retrieval rate over a million-level context window is striking: it shows that LongRoPE preserves not only statistical prediction performance (perplexity) but also the model’s ability to synthesize and retrieve a precise item from an extremely large context. For Mistral-7B, LongRoPE-extended models achieve perfect (100%) retrieval up to roughly 1,800k, but accuracy drops to about 60% at 2,048k. This aligns with the earlier perplexity observations: Mistral's long-window modeling degrades more at the very largest windows under the used fine-tuning regimen, and that degradation undermines retrieval performance at the extreme endpoint.

The passkey results emphasize two insights. First, a working nominal context window must be judged by the model's practical ability to find and use relevant tokens distributed anywhere in that window, not merely by a single-measure perplexity. Second, the non-uniform rescaling plus progressive fine-tuning that LongRoPE employs enables retrieval robustness far beyond what earlier interpolation-only methods accomplished (where accuracy collapsed beyond 128k in the experiments).

# Short-context benchmarks: comparable performance; slight drops mitigated by short-context recovery

An important concern when massively extending context is preserving original competence on the shorter windows for which the models were trained. Positional interpolation that aggressively compresses original positions can crowd embeddings for tokens within the original window (e.g., 4k), causing degraded performance on standard benchmarks. The paper addresses this both empirically and methodologically.

Empirically, LongRoPE-2048k models achieve comparable results to their unmodified counterparts on standard short-context benchmarks evaluated in the Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard suite: ARC-Challenge (25-shot), HellaSwag (10-shot), MMLU (5-shot), and TruthfulQA (0-shot). In particular, LongRoPE models match or closely track baseline performance and even slightly improve on some tasks (for example, outperforming the original Mistral on TruthfulQA by +0.5% in the reported runs). LLaMA2-2048k models fine-tuned at 256k show modest degradation on some benchmarks compared to the base model, but the drops are small and within reasonable ranges.

To mitigate those modest short-length degradations, LongRoPE performs an additional targeted search to “recover” short-context performance. Concretely, after producing a 2,048k extended model the authors run the evolutionary search again with a reduced maximum allowed rescale factor (encouraging less interpolation) targeting short lengths like 4k and 8k. During inference the model dynamically selects the appropriate RoPE rescale factors: if the sequence length is below a configured short threshold (e.g., 8k), it uses the short-length rescale factors; otherwise it uses the long-length rescale factors. This recovery procedure substantially reduces perplexity at short lengths and restores competitive benchmark performance while retaining the long-window capabilities.

The result demonstrates that long-window modifications need not be a zero-sum tradeoff with short-window behavior; by searching independently for rescale strategies appropriate to short and long regimes and switching at inference time, one can retain strong performance across regimes.

# Effect of fine-tuning length (128k vs. 256k) on secondary extension quality

A core practical insight of LongRoPE is that aggressively large final extension ratios are best achieved via a progressive approach: search and extend non-uniformly to an intermediate length (e.g., 128k or 256k), fine-tune at that length (but only for a small number of steps), and then apply a second non-uniform interpolation (search) to reach the final massive window (2,048k). The choice of intermediate fine-tuning length affects the difficulty of the subsequent secondary extension and the final model quality.

For LLaMA2 in the paper, fine-tuning at 256k produced a better final 2,048k model than fine-tuning at 128k. The reason given is that the secondary extension ratio (the factor by which the fine-tuned model is further extended) is smaller when starting from 256k: extending from 256k to 2,048k is an $8\times$ secondary step, whereas extending from 128k would require a $16\times$ secondary extension. Smaller secondary jumps are empirically easier to manage with non-uniform interpolation and produce lower perplexity and stronger downstream behavior.

For Mistral-7B the pattern is different: the paper reports that Mistral performed better in the final extended regime when fine-tuned at 128k rather than 256k under the authors’ experimental setup. The explanation offered is that their fine-tuning configuration for Mistral (following YaRN’s setup) used 16k training sequence lengths for both 128k and 256k fine-tuning runs, and this affected Mistral’s ability to adapt to later larger secondary extensions. Thus, the optimal intermediate fine-tuning length interacts with practical choices in the fine-tuning pipeline: training sequence length, number of steps, and hardware limits. The general lesson is that a smaller secondary extension ratio (achieved by choosing a larger intermediate fine-tune length) tends to be beneficial, but training details and model-specific behavior can influence the best choice.

# Synthesis and practical implications

Taken together, the experimental results establish several actionable conclusions for practitioners aiming to extend LLM context windows:

1. Non-uniform positional interpolation matters. Searching for dimension-wise and token-position-wise rescale factors (rather than applying a single global scale or coarse groups) yields substantial perplexity and retrieval gains across large intermediate and extreme context lengths.

2. A progressive extension strategy is effective and efficient. Fine-tuning at an intermediate large length (e.g., 128k or 256k) for a modest number of steps, followed by a secondary non-uniform interpolation, enables reaching a 2,048k nominal window without fine-tuning directly at million-scale lengths.

3. Preserve short-window performance with targeted searches. Because interpolating for megascale windows can harm performance in the original short regime, running a separate search limited to smaller rescale factors and dynamically switching RoPE factors by input length at inference time recovers short-context competence.

4. Model- and training-sensitive choices matter. The best intermediate fine-tuning length and exact gains depend on the base model (LLaMA2 vs. Mistral) and on the fine-tuning regimen (training sequence length, number of steps). In practice, experimenting with $128\text{k}$ vs. $256\text{k}$ intermediate fine-tuning and with the training chunk length is important to reach the strongest final performance.

Overall, the empirical story in the paper is clear: with careful, searched non-uniform interpolation, a modest amount of targeted fine-tuning, and an inference-time mechanism to recover short-length behavior, pre-trained RoPE-based LLMs can be extended from their original 4k windows to working 2,048k windows that demonstrably improve long-context perplexity and retrieval, while preserving short-context competence.
