### How does LongRoPE mitigate attention dispersion at extreme lengths without changing the attention mechanism?
Question:
One challenge is that attention becomes dispersed as \(L'\) grows, hurting short-context performance. LongRoPE claims to preserve important positional information via non-uniform interpolation. Can you provide intuition or quantitative evidence (e.g., attention heatmaps or entropy) that shows reduced dispersion compared to PI/NTK/YaRN? Since you do not modify masks or sparsity, how exactly does adjusting \(\{\lambda_i\}\) counteract dispersion?
Answer:
Short answer: LongRoPE reduces “attention dispersion” by changing only the positional encodings (the RoPE rotation angles) in a way that preserves the phase differences that the model relied on during pretraining — not by changing attention weights or masks. Practically this is done by (a) using different rescale factors $\lambda_i$ per RoPE dimension and (b) leaving the earliest token positions un-rescaled. Both choices keep the position-induced phases for important frequency channels and important tokens from being crushed together by uniform downscaling, so the dot-product attention still produces sharp, discriminatory scores instead of “spreading” across many positions.

Why this works (intuitive + technical): RoPE encodes position by rotating components of query/key vectors by angles proportional to $n\theta_i$ (Eq. 1 in the paper). Attention between tokens depends on inner products of queries and keys, which in turn depend on relative phase differences created by those rotations. If you uniformly scale all rotation angles by $1/s$ (PI), many different positions map to much closer phases — nearby positions become nearly indistinguishable in the rotary-phase space, so query-key inner products become less peaked and attention “disperses.” LongRoPE breaks that harmful uniformity in two ways that preserve discriminative phase information:

1) Per-dimension (frequency) non-uniform scaling $\lambda_i$. Lower-index RoPE dimensions correspond to higher-frequency rotations (they encode fine-grained positional differences). By keeping $\lambda_i$ smaller for those dimensions (less interpolation / more extrapolation) and allowing larger $\lambda_i$ for lower-frequency channels, LongRoPE preserves the phase differences on the channels that matter most for distinguishing nearby tokens. In other words, the model’s fine-grained positional cues survive, so attention remains localized where needed.

2) Token-position threshold $\hat n$ (no-rescale for initial positions). Early tokens often receive large attention mass and are particularly important for maintaining short-context performance. By leaving positions $n < \hat n$ un-rescaled (i.e., using the original angles for those tokens), LongRoPE preserves the exact pretraining phase relationships for the tokens that most strongly influence attention. That prevents those key tokens from being “moved” into crowded phases and keeps their attention contributions sharp.

A compact mathematical view: RoPE applies rotations with angle proportional to $n/\beta^i$. LongRoPE applies instead $\cos\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i}\cdot \frac{n}{\beta^i}\right)$ and $\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i}\cdot \frac{n}{\beta^i}\right)$ for $n \ge \hat n$ (and keeps original $n/\beta^i$ for $n < \hat n$). By choosing $\lambda_i$ as a non-decreasing sequence across $i$ (in practice found by the evolutionary search), the high-frequency channels keep their angular spacing (small $\lambda_i$), while low-frequency channels can be compressed more without losing close-position discrimination. Since attention scores are inner products of Q,K that inherit these rotated phases, preserving phase differences on the right channels keeps attention peaks from flattening.

Quantitative / empirical evidence in the paper (what to look at):
- The paper shows that LongRoPE can extend $4\mathrm{k}\rightarrow32\mathrm{k}$ without fine-tuning while PI / NTK / YaRN suffer sharp perplexity increases after small extensions (see Fig. 3 / Fig.\ref{fig:non-ft} and Table~\ref{tbl:exp1}). This is a direct proxy for reduced dispersion: low perplexity means the model is still effectively using relevant context rather than getting “diffuse” signals.
- Ablations in Section 2.2: Table~\ref{tbl:exp1} reports substantial perplexity improvements when searching per-dimension $\lambda_i$ vs PI/NTK/YaRN, demonstrating that per-dimension non-uniformity reduces information loss (i.e., mitigates dispersion). Table~\ref{tbl:tokenposition} shows that leaving a small number of starting tokens un-interpolated improves performance at $8\mathrm{k}\!-\!16\mathrm{k}$, confirming the token-position non-uniformity point.
- The passkey retrieval experiment (Fig.~\ref{fig:passkey}) is a behavioural test of “can the model focus on one small piece of information inside a huge context?” LongRoPE maintains high retrieval accuracy ($\ge 90\%$ up to $2048\mathrm{k}$ for the LLaMA2 variant) while other methods drop to zero well before that. That is strong, task-level evidence that attention is not smeared across millions of tokens.
- Fine-tuning + second search: The progressive strategy (search → fine-tune at $128\mathrm{k}/256\mathrm{k}$ → re-search) keeps perplexity low even at $2048\mathrm{k}$ (Table~\ref{tbl:books3}), again indicating the model still concentrates relevant attention despite the massive extension.

About attention heatmaps / entropy: the paper does not present direct attention-entropy or attention-heatmap plots, but perplexity curves, targeted ablations (dimension-wise and position-wise search), and downstream retrieval accuracy together serve as quantitative evidence that LongRoPE reduces the harmful dispersion effect. Perplexity and passkey retrieval are sensitive to whether attention remains informative and focused; their improvements imply that the attention distribution is effectively less dispersed after LongRoPE’s interpolation.

Why this doesn’t require changing masks or sparsity: dispersion is fundamentally caused by destroying the phase differences that RoPE originally created. You can either change the attention mechanism to cope (sparsity, masks, different attention kernels) or you can restore useful positional structure so the original dot-product attention still works. LongRoPE takes the latter route: by re-scaling rotations in a non-uniform, structure-preserving way, it keeps the original attention computation valid and effective, so no mask/architecture changes are needed. The monotonicity constraint on $\lambda_i$ (and the evolutionary search that seeds with PI/NTK/YaRN) finds rescalings that preserve the model’s learned inductive biases while enabling huge extension ratios.

Short summary: adjusting $\{\lambda_i\}$ and optionally protecting the first $\hat n$ positions counteracts attention dispersion by preserving the phase differences that produce peaked query-key inner products. The paper’s ablations (per-dimension search and initial-token preservation), perplexity trends (Fig.\ref{fig:non-ft}, Table~\ref{tbl:exp1}, Table~\ref{tbl:books3}), and passkey retrieval results (Fig.~\ref{fig:passkey}) provide quantitative support that LongRoPE keeps attention focused better than PI/NTK/YaRN without modifying the attention mechanism.
