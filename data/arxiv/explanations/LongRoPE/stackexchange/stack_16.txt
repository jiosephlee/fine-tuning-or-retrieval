\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: LongRoPE}

### What is the definition of "information entropy" of positional embeddings used to justify non-uniformity?
Question:
The paper mentions "complex non-uniform information entropy" in the Transformer regarding positional embeddings. How is this entropy quantified? Is it the entropy of attention distributions conditioned on positions, the spectrum of positional encoding across layers, or something else? Could you share the exact metric and empirical plots that motivated the per-dimension and per-position non-uniform treatment?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper does not define or measure a formal Shannon-style “information entropy” of positional embeddings. The phrase “complex non-uniform information entropy” is used as an intuitive description of observed heterogeneity across RoPE dimensions and token positions. The authors quantify and justify the non-uniform treatment empirically via search-guided rescale factors and downstream loss/metric improvements (perplexity and passkey-retrieval accuracy), not by publishing an explicit entropy formula or a direct entropy plot of embeddings/attention.

What the paper actually does
- The paper frames the problem as finding per-dimension rescale factors $\lambda_i$ and a per-position threshold $\hat{n}$ (Eq. 1 / Eq. problem formulation). Those factors are searched by an evolutionary procedure that is evaluated with language-model loss (perplexity) on held-out long-context samples. The objective in Eq. (problem) is to minimize $\mathcal{L}$ (next-token loss / perplexity) for documents that exceed the target length.
- Empirical evidence motivating non-uniformity is presented as improvements in perplexity and downstream tasks when allowing dimension-wise and position-wise non-uniform rescaling, versus uniform PI, NTK, or YaRN strategies. The paper cites tables and figures (e.g., Table 1/2 comparisons, Table tokenposition, Fig. non-ft, passkey retrieval plots) that show: (a) searched $\lambda_i$ profiles that are non-uniform across RoPE dimensions, (b) gains when preserving the first $\hat{n}$ tokens from interpolation, and (c) overall perplexity / retrieval accuracy improvements. These empirical plots / tables are the motivation in place of an explicit “entropy” metric.

What the phrase likely means intuitively
- RoPE encodes positions via multiple frequency-like dimensions (rotation angles $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$). Different dimensions carry different granularity/frequency of position information. The authors observe that the importance (or “information content”) of those dimensions is not uniform: some dimensions are more sensitive/critical for model performance after extension. Likewise, early token positions tend to be disproportionately important (they get high attention mass), so treating them differently reduces catastrophic behaviour when extending. Calling this heterogeneity “complex non-uniform information entropy” is a qualitative way to say “the information carried by positional channels and by token indices is uneven and structured,” not a claim that they computed Shannon entropies.

Where to find the empirical evidence in the paper
- The search formulation and application are in Section 3 (Problem formulation and Eq. (problem)). The search procedure and monotonicity constraint are in Section 3.2 and Algorithm 1.
- Finding 1–3 in Section 2.2 summarize the experimental findings: dimension-wise non-uniform $\lambda_i$ improves perplexity (Table references in the text), keeping early tokens un-interpolated helps (Table tokenposition), and the combined strategy enables non-finetuned $8\times$ extension and progressive finetuned extension to 2048k (Fig. non-ft, Tables in Section 4).
- Evaluation metrics used throughout are perplexity (primary) and passkey retrieval accuracy (generation task). The search objective itself is minimizing perplexity on sampled long sequences.

If you want a formal entropy-style analysis (not in the paper)
- The paper doesn’t supply such a metric, but sensible formal diagnostics you could compute to quantify “information heterogeneity” are: (1) per-dimension variance or spectral power of positional vectors across positions, (2) singular-value spectrum / effective rank of the matrix of positional embeddings (positions × RoPE-dimensions), (3) Shannon entropy of the attention distribution conditioned on position indices, $H(\text{attention}\mid\text{position})$, to see which positions concentrate attention, or (4) mutual information estimates between position index and hidden activations/attention, e.g. $I(\text{position};\text{activations})$. Any of these can make the qualitative claim precise and could be plotted alongside the searched $\lambda_i$ to show correlation between “information content” and the rescale schedule the search found.

Practical pointers to reproduce the paper’s motivation plots
- Re-run the evolutionary search (Section 3.2) and save the best-found $\lambda_i$ vector and $\hat{n}$. Plot $\lambda_i$ versus RoPE dimension index to see the non-uniform profile the authors report.
- Plot perplexity vs evaluation context length for baseline PI/NTK/YaRN and for the searched scheme (this is what the paper uses as the main empirical justification; see Fig. non-ft and Tables in Section 4).
- For per-position evidence, run the ablation where you keep the first $\hat{n}$ tokens un-interpolated and plot the change in perplexity as $\hat{n}$ varies (the paper reports this in the tokenposition ablation).

Summary
The paper’s “information entropy” phrase is a descriptive, not formal, claim. The authors justify per-dimension and per-position non-uniformity by direct empirical optimization of rescale factors and by reporting perplexity and retrieval gains (the searched $\lambda_i$ profiles and ablation tables/figures are the concrete evidence). If you want a rigorous entropy-style measure, it is not provided in the paper but can be added with the diagnostics I suggested and compared against the searched $\lambda_i$ to make the intuition explicit.
