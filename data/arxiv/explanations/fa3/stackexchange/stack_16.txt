### Why is forward attention FLOPs computed as $4N^2 d H$, and why divide by 2 with causal masking?
Question:
In the benchmarking section, the forward FLOPs are given as $4\cdot \text{seqlen}^2\cdot\text{head dimension}\cdot\text{number of heads}$. Could someone derive this? My attempt: each of the two GEMMs ($\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ and $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$) costs about $2N^2 d$ per head, summing to $4N^2 d$ per head, then multiply by $H$. With causal masking, about half of the score matrix is not computed, hence divide by 2. Is this precisely correct when using block tiling and triangular masking, or is there a small deviation from exactly half because of tile edges?
Answer:
Your derivation is essentially correct; here is a concise, grounded derivation and the subtleties to watch for.

Cost per head (dense, multiply–add counted as 2 FLOPs)
For one head with sequence length $N$ and head dimension $d$ the two heavy matrix–matrix products are
1) $\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$: $\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$ times $\mathbf{K}^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}$ produces an $N\times N$ matrix. A dense GEMM of shape $N\times d$ by $d\times N$ costs about $2N^2 d$ FLOPs (one multiply and one add per inner product element).
2) $\mathbf{O}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$: $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ times $\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$ produces $N\times d$. That GEMM also costs about $2N^2 d$ FLOPs.

Summing these two dominant GEMMs gives $4N^2 d$ FLOPs per head. Multiplying by the number of heads $H$ yields the paper’s forward FLOPs formula
$$
\text{FLOPs}_{\text{fwd}} \approx 4\,N^2\,d\,H,
$$
which is exactly what the paper uses.

Why divide by 2 with causal masking
Causal masking means query position $i$ only attends to keys $j\le i$. The number of computed score entries becomes
$$
\sum_{i=1}^N i = \frac{N(N+1)}{2} = \frac{N^2}{2} + \frac{N}{2}.
$$
Asymptotically the quadratic term is halved, so the dominant GEMM work is roughly half: replace $N^2$ by $\frac{N^2}{2}$ and you get $\approx 2N^2 d H$. That’s why the paper divides by 2 for causal masking.

Practical caveats (tiling, masking, and lower-order terms)
The formula above is an asymptotic count under the usual assumptions: multiply–adds counted as 2 FLOPs, the softmax/scale/rowmax work is small compared to the GEMMs, and the GEMMs are implemented as dense operations only over the computed entries.

Two practical deviations you should be aware of:

1) Tile-edge effects. In a block/tile implementation (like FlashAttention variants), you operate on tiles of size $B_r\times B_c$. A triangular causal region does not align perfectly with tile boundaries: some tiles will be partially inside the causal triangle and require special handling (you must compute only a triangular subset inside the tile, or compute whole tiles and mask results). That means the actual number of multiply-adds can be slightly larger than the pure triangular count because of partially filled tiles or extra scalar work to prune results. The excess is $O(N\cdot B)$ (linear in $N$ times tile size) not $O(N^2)$, so asymptotically negligible, but it can matter for small or moderate $N$ or large tile sizes. In short: the "divide by 2" is correct asymptotically; exact FLOPs differ by lower-order terms due to tile edges and implementation choices.

2) Other operations are lower-order but present. Softmax (exponentials, reductions, scaling) and the rowmax/row-sum bookkeeping cost roughly $O(Nd)$ or $O(N^2)$ scalar operations with much smaller constant and throughput than Tensor Core GEMMs. The paper treats these as negligible when reporting TFLOPs because the matmuls dominate for large $N$ and typical $d$. Also note that in backward pass accounting the paper multiplies forward FLOPs by 2.5 because there are more GEMMs (5 vs 2) in the backward path.

Summary
The forward FLOPs formula $4N^2 d H$ comes directly from counting two dense GEMMs per head at $2N^2 d$ each. Causal masking halves the quadratic work asymptotically because only about $N(N+1)/2$ score entries are computed, hence the divide-by-2. Tile-based implementations introduce small, lower-order deviations (tile-edge partial tiles, masking overhead), but they do not change the asymptotic halving.
