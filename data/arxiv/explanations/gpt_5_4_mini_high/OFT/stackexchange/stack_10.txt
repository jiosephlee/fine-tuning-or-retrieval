\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix still preserve hyperspherical energy of the full neurons?
Question:
To reduce parameters, the paper replaces a full orthogonal matrix with a block-diagonal one, $\bm{R} = \mathrm{diag}(\bm{R}_1, \ldots, \bm{R}_r)$. I understand that such an $\bm{R}$ is still orthogonal if each block is orthogonal, but intuitively it feels like it only rotates coordinates inside each block and never mixes features across blocks. So why does this still preserve the hyperspherical energy of the whole neuron vectors exactly? Is the only effect of block structure to reduce expressivity, while the pairwise-angle preservation remains mathematically exact?
Answer:
Yes — in the paper, the hyperspherical energy is preserved exactly as long as the transformation applied to the whole layer is orthogonal, and a block-diagonal matrix with orthogonal blocks is still orthogonal as a matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.

The key point is that OFT does not rotate each neuron independently; it applies the same left-multiplication $\bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ to every neuron vector in the layer, where each neuron is a column of $\bm{W}^0$. If $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, then for any two neurons $\bm{w}_i$ and $\bm{w}_j$ we have
$$
(\bm{R}\bm{w}_i)^\top(\bm{R}\bm{w}_j)=\bm{w}_i^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}\bm{w}_j=\bm{w}_i^\top \bm{w}_j,
$$
and also $\|\bm{R}\bm{w}_i\|=\|\bm{w}_i\|$. So the normalized vectors satisfy
$$
\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i}=\bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}_i,
$$
and therefore pairwise angular relationships are unchanged:
$$
\|\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i}-\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_j}\|
=\|\bm{R}(\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j)\|
=\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|.
$$
Since the paper defines hyperspherical energy as a sum over terms of the form $\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$, every term in the sum is unchanged, so the whole energy is unchanged exactly.

The block-diagonal structure only restricts which orthogonal transformations you can represent. It reduces expressivity and parameter count, because you are only allowed to rotate within each block and not mix coordinates across blocks, but it does not weaken the geometric invariance. In other words, “block-diagonal” changes the set of admissible orthogonal maps; it does not stop an admissible map from being an isometry. That is why the paper says that “the resulting matrix $\bm{R}$ remains orthogonal, so there is no sacrifice in preserving hyperspherical energy.”

So the short answer is: the preservation is exact because hyperspherical energy is invariant under any orthogonal transformation, and a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix is still an orthogonal transformation. The only thing the block structure sacrifices is flexibility, not the invariance itself.
