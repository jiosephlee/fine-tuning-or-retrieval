\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does left-multiplying by an orthogonal matrix preserve pairwise neuron angles and hyperspherical energy?
Question:
The main OFT update is $\bm{W} = \bm{R} \bm{W}^0$ with $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I}$. The paper says this provably preserves the pairwise hyperspherical similarity between neurons in the same layer, and therefore preserves the hyperspherical energy. I think this should follow from orthogonal matrices preserving inner products and norms, but I am not fully seeing the derivation when the energy is defined as $\mathrm{HE}(\bm{W}) = \sum_{i \ne j} \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$. Can someone show explicitly why $\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0$ implies each pairwise term stays unchanged?
Answer:
Yes — the preservation is basically a consequence of the fact that an orthogonal matrix is an isometry of $\mathbb{R}^d$. In the paper’s setting, the weight matrix has columns $\bm{w}_i$ (the neurons), and OFT replaces each column by the same left multiplication $\bm{w}_i = \bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0$ with $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I}$. Since $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, it preserves lengths and inner products.

The first step is to check what happens after normalization. Because orthogonal matrices preserve norms, we have
$$
\|\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0\| = \|\bm{w}_i^0\|.
$$
So the normalized neuron becomes
$$
\hat{\bm{w}}_i
= \frac{\bm{w}_i}{\|\bm{w}_i\|}
= \frac{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0}{\|\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0\|}
= \frac{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0}{\|\bm{w}_i^0\|}
= \bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0.
$$
This is the identity you wrote down.

Now look at a pair of normalized neurons. Their distance after the transform is
$$
\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|
= \|\bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0 - \bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}_j^0\|
= \|\bm{R}(\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0 - \hat{\bm{w}}_j^0)\|.
$$
Since $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, it preserves Euclidean norm:
$$
\|\bm{R}\bm{x}\|^2
= (\bm{R}\bm{x})^\top (\bm{R}\bm{x})
= \bm{x}^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}\bm{x}
= \bm{x}^\top \bm{x}
= \|\bm{x}\|^2.
$$
Applying this with $\bm{x} = \hat{\bm{w}}_i^0 - \hat{\bm{w}}_j^0$ gives
$$
\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|
= \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0 - \hat{\bm{w}}_j^0\|.
$$
Therefore each summand in the hyperspherical energy is unchanged:
$$
\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}
=
\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0 - \hat{\bm{w}}_j^0\|^{-1}.
$$
Summing over all $i \neq j$ immediately gives
$$
\mathrm{HE}(\bm{W}) = \mathrm{HE}(\bm{W}^0).
$$

If you want the angle viewpoint, it is the same statement in cosine form. For unit vectors,
$$
\|\hat{\bm{u}} - \hat{\bm{v}}\|^2 = 2 - 2\hat{\bm{u}}^\top \hat{\bm{v}},
$$
so preserving distances is equivalent to preserving inner products, hence angles. Orthogonal matrices preserve inner products because
$$
(\bm{R}\bm{u})^\top(\bm{R}\bm{v}) = \bm{u}^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}\bm{v} = \bm{u}^\top \bm{v}.
$$
That is why the paper can say OFT “provably preserves pairwise hyperspherical similarity” and therefore preserves hyperspherical energy: the whole energy is built only from pairwise distances/angles on the sphere, and orthogonal transforms do not change those quantities.

So the short version is: left-multiplication by $\bm{R}$ rotates or reflects the entire set of neurons together, and rigid motions do not change pairwise angles or distances. Because hyperspherical energy depends only on those pairwise distances, it stays exactly the same.
