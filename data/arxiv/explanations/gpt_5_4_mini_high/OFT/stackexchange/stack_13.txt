\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does re-scaled OFT still count as preserving the important structure if it allows arbitrary positive per-neuron scaling?
Question:
The paper proposes a re-scaled OFT variant with forward pass $\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top \bm{x}$, where $\bm{D}$ is a positive diagonal matrix. I understand that scaling each neuron does not change hyperspherical energy because normalization removes magnitudes. But from the network's forward-pass perspective, changing magnitudes can still strongly affect activations and therefore generation. So I am confused about the interpretation: if the key preservation property is unchanged hyperspherical energy, why does adding unrestricted scaling not undermine the same stability argument?
Answer:
In the paper’s terminology, the “important structure” that OFT is trying to preserve is not the exact forward map of the network, but the angular geometry of the neurons, as measured by hyperspherical energy. That is why the authors can say that a positive diagonal scaling $\bm{D}$ does not break the preservation claim: hyperspherical energy is defined after normalizing each neuron, so the magnitudes introduced by $\bm{D}$ disappear when you form $\hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|$. In other words, if you replace $\bm{W}^0$ by $\bm{W}^0\bm{D}$ with $s_i>0$, then the directions of the neuron vectors are unchanged, only their radii are changed, and the pairwise cosine similarities remain the same.

That said, your concern is exactly right in a broader functional sense: changing magnitudes can absolutely change activations and therefore generation. The paper does not claim otherwise. Re-scaled OFT is presented as a more flexible extension, not as something with the same strict stability guarantee as vanilla OFT. The rigorous invariance result in the paper is about preserving the hyperspherical structure under orthogonal transformations; once you add arbitrary positive scaling, you are preserving only that angular structure, not the layer’s exact output distribution.

So the right way to read the paper is: orthogonal finetuning preserves the semantic structure they care about because they hypothesize that semantics live primarily in neuron directions, while magnitude is less important. The re-scaled variant keeps that angular geometry intact but gives the optimizer extra freedom to adjust gain. That extra freedom can indeed affect the function more strongly, which is why the authors treat it as an optional, more flexible variant rather than the core theoretical method. In fact, the paper even introduces COFT precisely to restore extra stability by constraining deviation from the pretrained weights. So the stability argument is not “scaling is harmless”; it is “scaling does not destroy the specific geometric invariant we use to proxy semantic preservation, but it may reduce functional stability, so use it as a flexibility knob rather than a guarantee.”
