\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### Why does the stabilization state $m_t$ not change the forward output or gradients in xLSTM?
Question:
For numerical stability, the paper defines a stabilizer state
$ m_t = \max(\log(f_t) + m_{t-1}, \log(i_t)) $
and then replaces the gates by
$ i'_t = \exp(\tilde{i}_t - m_t) $
and
$ f'_t = \exp(\log(f_t) + m_{t-1} - m_t) $.
The authors say that replacing $i_t, f_t$ by $i'_t, f'_t$ does not change either the network output or the derivatives with respect to the parameters. I can believe this is some kind of rescaling trick, but I do not see it formally. Is the idea that both $c_t$ and the normalizer get multiplied by the same accumulated factor, so the ratio stays unchanged? And if that is the argument, why do the gradients also remain exactly the same rather than just approximately the same?
Answer:
Yes — the stabilization is an exact rescaling trick, not an approximation.

The clean way to see it is to introduce the stabilized cell and normalizer states
$$
\bar c_t := e^{-m_t} c_t, \qquad \bar n_t := e^{-m_t} n_t.
$$
Using the paper’s definitions
$$
m_t=\max\bigl(\log(f_t)+m_{t-1},\log(i_t)\bigr),\qquad
i'_t=e^{\log(i_t)-m_t},\qquad
f'_t=e^{\log(f_t)+m_{t-1}-m_t},
$$
you get
$$
\bar c_t
= e^{-m_t}(f_t c_{t-1}+i_t z_t)
= f_t e^{m_{t-1}-m_t}\bar c_{t-1} + i_t e^{-m_t} z_t
= f'_t \bar c_{t-1} + i'_t z_t,
$$
and similarly
$$
\bar n_t
= e^{-m_t}(f_t n_{t-1}+i_t)
= f'_t \bar n_{t-1} + i'_t.
$$
So the stabilized recurrence is just the original recurrence written in a different scale. The hidden state is
$$
h_t = o_t \frac{\bar c_t}{\bar n_t} = o_t \frac{c_t}{n_t},
$$
because the same factor $e^{-m_t}$ multiplies both numerator and denominator and cancels exactly. That is the whole point of the stabilizer: it keeps the numbers from blowing up while preserving the normalized output.

The gradients are exactly unchanged for the same reason. The stabilized and unstabilized implementations compute the same function of the parameters, pointwise, not just approximately. If two formulas are algebraically identical, then their derivatives are identical wherever the derivative exists. Any dependence of $m_t$ on the gates does not introduce a new effect in the final output, because the $m_t$-terms cancel between $\bar c_t$ and $\bar n_t$. This is the same logic as the standard log-sum-exp/softmax stabilization: subtracting a common constant changes the numerics but not the function.

The only minor caveat is the usual one for $\max$: when the two arguments are exactly equal, the map is not differentiable. But that is a measure-zero case, and away from such ties the equality of outputs implies equality of gradients exactly.

So your intuition is right: the state and its normalizer are rescaled together, and because the model only uses their ratio, both the forward pass and the backpropagated gradients are unchanged.
