\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### Why is only the key projection scaled by $1/\sqrt{d}$ in mLSTM, but not the query projection?
Question:
In the mLSTM definition, the paper uses
$ \mathbf{k}_t = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \mathbf{W}_k \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{b}_k $,
while the query is simply
$ \mathbf{q}_t = \mathbf{W}_q \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{b}_q $.
In Transformer attention, I am used to seeing the dot product $\mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{k}$ scaled, not just the key. Is this choice equivalent in expectation, or is there a specific reason to scale only the keys here? I am wondering whether this is meant to keep the outer-product memory update $\mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top$ at a stable magnitude.
Answer:
In mLSTM the choice is not just a cosmetic rewrite of Transformer attention. In a Transformer, scaling $q^\top k$ by $1/\sqrt{d}$ is mainly about keeping the variance of the dot product under control. In mLSTM, however, the key vector does double duty: it is used both in the readout and in the recurrent memory update,
$$
\mathbf{C}_t = f_t \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + i_t \mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top,
\qquad
\mathbf{n}_t = f_t \mathbf{n}_{t-1} + i_t \mathbf{k}_t.
$$
So scaling $\mathbf{k}_t$ by $1/\sqrt{d}$ is a way of controlling the magnitude of the *stored state* itself, not just the final similarity score. This is exactly the kind of stability issue your intuition points to: it keeps the outer-product write $\mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top$ and the normalizer update $\mathbf{n}_t$ in a reasonable range.

By contrast, $\mathbf{q}_t$ only appears at read time,
$$
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t = \mathbf{C}_t \mathbf{q}_t \big/ \max\{|\mathbf{n}_t^\top \mathbf{q}_t|,1\},
$$
so scaling $\mathbf{q}_t$ would not stabilize the memory update at all. If the denominator were just $|\mathbf{n}_t^\top \mathbf{q}_t|$, then moving a constant factor from $\mathbf{k}_t$ to $\mathbf{q}_t$ would leave the readout ratio essentially unchanged. But here the model also has the lower bound $\max\{\cdot,1\}$, so the two choices are not strictly equivalent in all regimes.

So the clean interpretation, grounded in the paper, is: scaling the keys is a variance/stability convention tailored to the covariance-style memory update. It is more natural here than scaling the queries, because the keys participate in both writing and normalization of the recurrent memory, while the queries only read from it.
