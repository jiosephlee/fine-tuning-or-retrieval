\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Why does the paper say Mistral is fine-tuned to 128k/256k while using only 16k training sequences?
Question:
In the experimental setup, the paper says that for Mistral they follow YaRN and fine-tune the $128\text{k}$ and $256\text{k}$ models using $16\text{k}$ sequence length. Later, they also say this may hurt Mistral's ability to extend further.

This made me realize I may not understand what it means to fine-tune a model "to 256k." If the actual training examples are only $16\text{k}$ tokens long, in what sense has the model really adapted to a $256\text{k}$ context window?

Is the idea simply that the RoPE scaling is configured for $256\text{k}$, even though the model never sees sequences near that length during fine-tuning? If so, why would that help at all, and why is it still called a $256\text{k}$ fine-tuned model?
Answer:
Yes — in this paper, “fine-tuned to $256\mathrm{k}$” is shorthand for the **target context window at inference**, not the literal length of the fine-tuning examples.

For Mistral, they do **not** feed $256\mathrm{k}$-token sequences during fine-tuning. Instead, they fine-tune on **$16\mathrm{k}$ chunks** while using the RoPE scaling/interpolation setup intended for a $128\mathrm{k}$ or $256\mathrm{k}$ model. In other words, the model is trained with a *long-context positional parameterization*, but on shorter training sequences.

That makes sense because the “context window” is really a statement about how far the model can attend **when deployed**, whereas the fine-tuning sequence length is just the amount of text used per training example. You can think of it as learning the model to operate in a stretched positional coordinate system without having to actually process full $256\mathrm{k}$-token documents during training, which would be far too expensive and, as the paper says, scarce.

So the answer to your second question is basically yes: the main thing being configured for $256\mathrm{k}$ is the **RoPE rescaling / positional interpolation**, together with some weight adaptation from fine-tuning. But it is still called a $256\mathrm{k}$ fine-tuned model because the resulting checkpoint is intended to support a **$256\mathrm{k}$ inference window**.

The important caveat, which the paper explicitly points out, is that this is an imperfect approximation. Since Mistral only sees $16\mathrm{k}$ training sequences, it does not get direct supervision from genuinely very long contexts, and that may indeed hurt its ability to extend further. That is why the authors note that Mistral’s further extension beyond the fine-tuned window is weaker than LLaMA2’s in their experiments.

So the phrase “fine-tuned to $256\mathrm{k}$” should be read as “fine-tuned under the $256\mathrm{k}$ positional scaling regime,” not “trained on $256\mathrm{k}$-token examples.”
