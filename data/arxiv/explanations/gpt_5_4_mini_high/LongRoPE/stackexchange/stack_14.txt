\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Why does preserving the first-token positions help at 16k and 32k, but not clearly at 2048k?
Question:
In the ablation section, the paper says that the token-position non-uniformity (keeping the first $\hat{n}$ tokens un-interpolated) clearly helps at moderate lengths like $16\text{k}$ and $32\text{k}$, but does not show the same impact at $2048\text{k}$.

I find this surprising. If early tokens are important because they attract a lot of attention, I would expect preserving them to matter even more at extreme context lengths, not less.

Is the issue that when the sequence becomes extremely long, preserving only a tiny prefix is negligible compared to the global distortion across the rest of the sequence? Or does attention itself change qualitatively at million-token scales so that the "important sink tokens" intuition becomes less relevant?
Answer:
The paper’s own conclusion points to the first explanation: at $2048\text{k}$, preserving a small un-interpolated prefix is simply too small an effect to matter much.

What LongRoPE calls token-position non-uniformity is a very local correction: for the first $\hat n$ tokens, you keep the original RoPE exactly, and only after that do you apply the rescaled positions. That helps at $16\text{k}$ and $32\text{k}$ because the beginning of the sequence is still a noticeable part of the total context, and those early tokens can indeed act like attention “anchors” or “sinks,” as suggested by Streaming LLM and LM-Infinite. In that regime, leaving the first few positions untouched can improve the geometry enough to reduce perplexity.

At $2048\text{k}$, though, the situation is very different. Even the largest $\hat n$ values they search over are tiny compared with the full length; for example, $\hat n = 256$ is only about $0.0125\%$ of a $2048\text{k}$ sequence. So while those first tokens are still preserved, the model’s behavior is dominated by the enormous stretch applied to the remaining $>2$ million positions. In other words, the bottleneck is no longer “how should I treat the first few tokens?” but “how do I keep the whole positional space from collapsing under a $512\times$ extension?” The paper explicitly says this effect may disappear “due to the extremely long length” and that “preserving only the initial tokens without interpolation becomes non-useful.”

So I would not interpret this as attention changing qualitatively at million-token scales. The paper does not claim that. Rather, the simple sink-token intuition seems to be scale-dependent: it helps at moderate extensions, but at extreme lengths it gets washed out by the global distortion of the positional encoding. That is also why LongRoPE puts much more emphasis on the dimension-wise non-uniformity and on progressive extension/fine-tuning, instead of relying on the prefix trick alone.
