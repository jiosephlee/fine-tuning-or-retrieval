\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### How is the BOFT parameter count \(\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm\) derived?
Question:
The paper claims that BOFT\((m,b)\) uses \(\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm\) effective trainable parameters for a layer of size \(d \times n\). I want to understand where this count comes from. My guess is that each butterfly component contains \(d/(2b)\) blocks of size \(2b \times 2b\), but I am mixing up the number of free parameters in a full orthogonal matrix, in a skew-symmetric matrix, and in a Cayley parameterization. Could someone derive the formula step by step?
Answer:
The formula is counting the number of **free parameters**, not the number of nonzero entries in the final butterfly matrix.

The key point in the paper is that BOFT parameterizes each orthogonal factor with the same Cayley-style orthogonal parameterization used in OFT. For a block of size $b \times b$, the Cayley transform starts from a skew-symmetric matrix $Q$ with $Q^\top = -Q$. Such a matrix has only the strict upper-triangular entries as independent parameters, so the number of free parameters is

$$
\frac{b(b-1)}{2}.
$$

Now look at one BOFT factor. The paper’s block structure partitions the $d$-dimensional space into $d/b$ blocks of size $b$. Each block carries its own orthogonal transform, so one factor contributes

$$
\frac{d}{b}\cdot \frac{b(b-1)}{2}
= \frac{d(b-1)}{2}
$$

trainable parameters.

BOFT then uses a product of $m$ such orthogonal butterfly factors, and the factors are trained independently. Therefore the total number of effective trainable parameters is just

$$
m \cdot \frac{d(b-1)}{2}
= \frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm
$$

trainable parameters.

That is exactly the paper’s count.

So the intuition is: the butterfly layout changes **how** the orthogonal transforms are connected, but it does not change the local degrees of freedom of each orthogonal block. The count comes from “number of blocks $\times$ degrees of freedom per orthogonal block $\times$ number of factors.”

Your guess about $2b \times 2b$ blocks is close to the sparsity-pattern picture, but it is not the quantity being counted. The paper’s “effective trainable parameters” are the independent entries in the skew-symmetric Cayley parameter, and those are what lead to $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$.
