\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### How does the Cayley parameterization used in OFT/BOFT guarantee orthogonality in practice?
Question:
The paper parameterizes the orthogonal matrix as
\[
R = (I + Q)(I - Q)^{-1}, \quad Q = -Q^\top.
\]
I understand the usual algebra showing that \(R^\top R = I\), but I am confused about the practical details. Is \(I - Q\) always invertible for a real skew-symmetric \(Q\)? Also, does the Cayley transform represent every orthogonal matrix, or only those without eigenvalue \(-1\)? If it is not surjective onto the full orthogonal group, why is it still a good parameterization for finetuning?
Answer:
Yes. For a real skew-symmetric matrix $Q$ with $Q^\top=-Q$, the Cayley factor $I-Q$ is always invertible. A simple way to see this is to suppose $(I-Q)x=0$. Then $x=Qx$, so
$$
\|x\|^2 = x^\top x = x^\top Qx = 0,
$$
because $x^\top Qx=0$ for every real skew-symmetric $Q$. Hence $x=0$, so $I-Q$ has trivial kernel and is invertible. Spectrally, the same fact is that a real skew-symmetric matrix has purely imaginary eigenvalues, so $1$ is never an eigenvalue of $Q$. Therefore the denominator in
$$
R=(I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}
$$
is always well-defined.

On surjectivity, the Cayley transform is not onto all of $O(d)$. Its image is exactly the orthogonal matrices for which $R+I$ is invertible, equivalently those with no eigenvalue $-1$. On that set, the inverse is
$$
Q=(R-I)(R+I)^{-1},
$$
which is skew-symmetric whenever $R$ is orthogonal and $R+I$ exists. So the Cayley transform is a bijection between skew-symmetric matrices and a large open subset of $SO(d)$, but it misses orthogonal matrices with a $-1$ eigenvalue, including reflections and $\pi$-rotations.

That limitation is not a problem for OFT/BOFT, because the paper is not trying to parameterize the entire orthogonal group globally. It wants a smooth, trainable, exactly orthogonal family that starts at the identity. In the paper, the skew-symmetric parameter is initialized at $Q=0$, giving $R=I$ exactly, and the orthogonality constraint is then preserved by construction throughout finetuning. This is precisely the kind of local coordinate chart that is useful for optimization on a manifold: you get unconstrained parameters, exact orthogonality at every forward pass, and a natural bias toward small, stable deviations from the pretrained model.

So the practical picture is: orthogonality is guaranteed algebraically by the reparameterization, the map is not globally surjective, but it covers the part of the orthogonal manifold that matters most for finetuning around the identity. That trade-off is consistent with the paper’s broader theme that regularity and parameter efficiency can be more valuable than full expressivity.
