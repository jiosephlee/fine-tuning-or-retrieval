\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why is the orthogonal update applied on the left as \(R W^0\) instead of on the right as \(W^0 R\)?
Question:
In the paper, the finetuned weight is \(R W^0\), not \(W^0 R\). If \(W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}\), then left multiplication rotates the input-side coordinates, while right multiplication would mix output neurons. The paper motivates OFT by saying it preserves pairwise angles between neurons and identifies neurons with columns of \(W^0\), so I think left multiplication is deliberate. Can someone explain clearly what quantity would be preserved by \(W^0 R\) instead, and why \(R W^0\) is the natural choice here?
Answer:
The key point is that orthogonal multiplication preserves the geometry of whichever side it acts on. In the paper’s convention, the neurons are the columns of $W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$, so choosing

$$
W' = R W^0, \qquad R \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}, \ R^\top R = I
$$

means each column $w_i$ is transformed as $w_i' = R w_i$. Then for any two neurons,

$$
\langle w_i', w_j' \rangle = (R w_i)^\top (R w_j) = w_i^\top R^\top R w_j = \langle w_i, w_j \rangle.
$$

So left multiplication preserves the pairwise inner products, norms, and therefore angles between the columns, which is exactly the invariant the paper wants to keep.

By contrast, if you used right multiplication,

$$
W' = W^0 R,
$$

then the preserved geometry would be that of the rows, because

$$
W' W'^\top = W^0 R R^\top (W^0)^\top = W^0 (W^0)^\top.
$$

So $W^0 R$ preserves pairwise inner products between row vectors, not between columns. In other words, it would be the natural choice only if you were identifying the rows as the entities whose geometry you want to preserve. The paper does not do that: it explicitly treats the columns as neurons, and it motivates OFT as preserving the angles between those neurons.

There is also a simple dimensional reason why $R W^0$ is the natural form here. Since the columns of $W^0$ live in $\mathbb{R}^d$, the orthogonal transform should be $d \times d$ and act on that $d$-dimensional neuron vector. That is exactly what left multiplication does. Right multiplication would require an $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix and would rotate the other axis of the weight matrix instead, which is a different reparameterization.

So the short answer is: $W^0 R$ would preserve row geometry, while $R W^0$ preserves column geometry. Since OFT/BOFT is built around the idea that columns are the neurons and their pairwise angles should stay fixed, $R W^0$ is the natural choice.
