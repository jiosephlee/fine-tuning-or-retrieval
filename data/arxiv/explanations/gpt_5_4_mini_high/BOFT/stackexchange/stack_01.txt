\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why does multiplying a pretrained weight matrix by an orthogonal matrix preserve neuron angles?
Question:
I'm reading the BOFT/OFT paper, where a pretrained layer \(W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}\) is updated to \(R W^0\) with \(R\) orthogonal. The paper says this preserves the pairwise angles between neurons, where each neuron is a column of \(W^0\). I can see that for two columns \(w_i^0\) and \(w_j^0\),
\[
(Rw_i^0)^\top (Rw_j^0) = (w_i^0)^\top R^\top R w_j^0 = (w_i^0)^\top w_j^0,
\]
so dot products and norms are preserved. But I am still unsure whether the paper is talking about preserving the geometry of columns, rows, or activations, especially since the forward pass is written as \(z = (R W^0)^\top x\).
Answer:
The paper is talking about the geometry of the **columns** of $W^0$, not the rows or the activations themselves.

In their convention, a “neuron” in the layer is a column vector $w_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ of the pretrained weight matrix $W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$. When BOFT/OFT replaces $W^0$ by $R W^0$ with $R$ orthogonal, every column is transformed in exactly the same way:
$$
w_i^0 \mapsto R w_i^0.
$$
Because $R^\top R = I$, all inner products between columns are preserved:
$$
(Rw_i^0)^\top (Rw_j^0)= (w_i^0)^\top R^\top R w_j^0 = (w_i^0)^\top w_j^0.
$$
So norms are preserved as a special case, and therefore angles are preserved too. This is the standard fact that an orthogonal matrix is a rigid rotation/reflection of the ambient space: it does not distort lengths or angles.

The forward pass
$$
z = (R W^0)^\top x
$$
is just the same statement viewed from the input side:
$$
z = (W^0)^\top R^\top x.
$$
So instead of saying “the neurons were rotated,” you can equivalently say “the input is measured in a rotated basis.” The actual activations $z$ generally do change, because the model is being finetuned; what is preserved is the **pairwise geometry among the columns of the weight matrix**, not the output vector itself.

So the short answer is: the paper is preserving the geometry of the **weight vectors / neurons as columns**, and the forward-pass expression is consistent with that because left-multiplying by $R$ rotates all those column vectors in the same $d$-dimensional space. Rows are not what is being claimed, and activations are not preserved pointwise; only the column-wise relational structure is.
