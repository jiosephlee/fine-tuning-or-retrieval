\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Does left multiplication by an orthogonal matrix preserve only the spectral norm, or all singular values of \(W^0\)?
Question:
In the spectral properties section, the paper says OFT/BOFT preserve the spectral norm of the pretrained weight matrix because the finetuned weight is \(R W^0\) with \(R\) orthogonal. But for rectangular \(W^0\), left multiplication by an orthogonal matrix should leave the entire singular value spectrum unchanged, not just the largest singular value. Is the stronger statement true here? If yes, why does the paper emphasize spectral norm preservation specifically instead of saying that all singular values are preserved?
Answer:
Yes. In the setup of the paper, left multiplication by an exactly orthogonal matrix preserves the entire singular value spectrum of $W^0$, not just the largest singular value.

The key identity is simply
$$
(RW^0)^\top (RW^0) = (W^0)^\top R^\top R W^0 = (W^0)^\top W^0,
$$
because $R^\top R = I$. Since the singular values of a matrix are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $W^\top W$, this means that $RW^0$ and $W^0$ have exactly the same singular values. Equivalently, if
$$
W^0 = U\Sigma V^\top
$$
is an SVD, then
$$
RW^0 = (RU)\Sigma V^\top,
$$
and $RU$ is still orthogonal. So the singular values in $\Sigma$ are unchanged.

So the stronger statement is true for the paper’s OFT/BOFT reparameterization, assuming the orthogonality constraint is satisfied exactly. What changes is the set of singular vectors, not the singular values themselves. That is why the layer can still adapt its behavior even though its spectrum is preserved.

As for why the paper emphasizes the spectral norm specifically: that is the quantity most commonly discussed in the generalization/stability literature, and it is the one they explicitly connect to prior work on spectral normalization. Saying “the spectral norm is preserved” is a weaker but sufficient claim for their argument. In other words, preserving all singular values is mathematically stronger, but the paper only needs the top singular value to motivate why orthogonal finetuning tends to be stable.
