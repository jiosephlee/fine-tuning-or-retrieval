\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### How is 'multiplicative dropout' in BOFT analogous to standard dropout?
Question:
For LoRA, standard dropout is applied to the additive low-rank update. BOFT instead randomly chooses some butterfly components and some diagonal blocks within them, and replaces those blocks by identity matrices. I can see why this keeps the update multiplicative, but I do not understand the analogy with ordinary dropout. In standard dropout, activations or weights are zeroed and often rescaled so that the expectation is preserved. In BOFT, replacing a block with identity seems more like skipping part of the transformation than zeroing it. Is there an expectation-preserving interpretation here, or is this mainly a heuristic regularizer?
Answer:
The right intuition is that BOFT is not trying to mimic the algebra of standard dropout exactly; it is trying to mimic its regularizing effect in a multiplicative setting.

In LoRA, the learned part is additive, so “dropping” some contribution naturally means setting part of the low-rank update to zero. In BOFT, the learned part is a product of orthogonal factors, so the neutral “off” value is not $0$ but the identity matrix $I$. Replacing a butterfly component, or one of its diagonal blocks, by $I$ means “do not apply this learned sub-transformation.” That is the multiplicative analogue of zeroing a branch in an additive model.

So the analogy is really with *stochastic removal of update paths*: during training, some factors are randomly bypassed, which reduces co-adaptation among the butterfly components and acts as a regularizer. This is closer in spirit to DropPath/Stochastic Depth than to the usual activation dropout, although the paper calls it dropout because it plays the same anti-overfitting role.

As for expectation preservation, the paper does **not** present a standard inverted-dropout-style guarantee like “scale by $1/(1-p)$ so that the expectation matches.” It only says that conventional dropout does not naturally fit BOFT because the update is multiplicative, and therefore proposes replacing some blocks with identity matrices. So the interpretation is mainly heuristic regularization, not a formal expectation-preserving construction.

One extra intuition is that BOFT is initialized at identity anyway, so identity is the natural “no-op” state. Dropping a block to $I$ therefore keeps the finetuned model close to the pretrained model while still injecting stochasticity during training.
