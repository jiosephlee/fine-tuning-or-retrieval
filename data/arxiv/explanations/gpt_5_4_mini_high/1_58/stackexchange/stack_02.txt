\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### If BitNet b1.58 uses 8-bit activations and full-precision embeddings, in what sense is it a "1-bit LLM"?
Question:
The paper repeatedly calls BitNet b1.58 a "1-bit LLM", but later it says the model is trained with 1.58-bit weights and 8-bit activations, and it also mentions that embeddings remain full precision when discussing memory trends. Should I interpret "1-bit LLM" as referring only to the linear-layer weights, since those dominate the parameter count and compute? Or is there a more precise definition being used in low-bit LLM papers?
Answer:
Short answer: yes, you should read “$1$-bit LLM” as a shorthand for the **quantized weight matrices in the Transformer blocks**, not as a literal statement that *every* tensor in the system is stored at one bit.

The paper is actually more precise than the slogan. BitNet b1.58 is described as a Transformer that replaces $\texttt{nn.Linear}$ with $\texttt{BitLinear}$, and it is “trained from scratch, with $1.58$-bit weights and $8$-bit activations.” The $1.58$ comes from the fact that the weights are ternary, taking values in $\{-1,0,1\}$, and a ternary symbol carries $\log_2 3 \approx 1.58$ bits of information. So the model is not really “one bit” in the strict sense; the paper is using “$1$-bit LLM” as a family label for a model whose core linear weights are extremely low precision.

That also explains the apparent mismatch with the embeddings. Later the paper explicitly notes that “the embedding remains full precision” when discussing memory trends. So the model is not uniformly low-bit end to end. The embedding table stays FP16/BF16, and the activations are $8$-bit. But the paper’s main argument is that the expensive part of an LLM is the dense matrix multiplications in the linear layers, and those are where the big wins come from. In that sense, the headline bit-width refers to the dominant computation and parameter blocks, not to every parameter in the entire implementation.

So the cleanest interpretation is: BitNet b1.58 is a **ternary-weight Transformer with $8$-bit activations and some full-precision components such as embeddings**. The “$1$-bit LLM” phrase is rhetorical/approximate; the more exact description is “a $1.58$-bit weight LLM.” That is also why the paper repeatedly emphasizes that the model “retains the benefits of the original $1$-bit BitNet” while being “$1.58$ bits” in the weights.

In other words, your intuition is right: the label is mainly about the **linear-layer weights**, since they dominate both parameter storage and compute, but the paper’s more precise claim is about **ternary weights plus $8$-bit activations**, not a literally all-$1$-bit model.
