\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### Is the claim of Pareto improvement fair when comparing BitNet b1.58 3.9B against an FP16 3B model?
Question:
The paper says BitNet b1.58 3.9B is a Pareto improvement over a 3B FP16 LLaMA-like model because it is faster, uses less memory, and performs better. I understand the practical point, but I am unsure about the comparison methodology. Since the BitNet model has more parameters, should I think of this as "under a fixed deployment budget, I can afford a larger ternary model", rather than a direct apples-to-apples architecture comparison? I want to make sure I am interpreting the Pareto claim correctly.
Answer:
Yes — your interpretation is the right one.

The paper is not claiming that a $3.9$B BitNet is a fair apples-to-apples replacement for a $3$B FP16 model in the narrow sense of “same parameter count.” Rather, it is making a Pareto claim in the deployment sense: for the objectives they care about — quality, latency, memory, and energy — the BitNet point is better even though it has more parameters, because each parameter is vastly cheaper to store and compute.

That is exactly why the comparison can make sense. A ternary weight matrix is not just “smaller FP16 by a constant factor”; it changes the cost structure. In the paper, BitNet b1.58 uses ternary weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and 8-bit activations, so the model can have more parameters while still occupying less memory and running faster than a smaller FP16 model. The paper explicitly reports that BitNet b1.58 $3.9$B is faster, uses less GPU memory, and performs better than the FP16 $3$B baseline. In Pareto terms, it dominates that baseline on the measured axes.

So the clean way to read the result is: under a fixed deployment budget, you can afford a larger BitNet because the per-parameter cost is much lower, and that larger model can sit on a better quality-cost frontier. It is not “same-size architecture comparison”; that role is played elsewhere in the paper when they say BitNet b1.58 matches FP16 baselines starting from $3$B at the same training setup.

One caveat is that this is still an empirical, system-dependent claim: the latency and memory numbers depend on the kernels and hardware used in the paper’s evaluation. But conceptually, yes — “Pareto improvement” here means better quality for lower deployment cost, not equal parameter count.
