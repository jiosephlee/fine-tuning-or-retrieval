\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What does 'commit but do not wait' mean for WGMMA, and why is that useful here?
Question:
In the consumer algorithm, the paper repeatedly says to issue a WGMMA, then 'commit but do not wait'. Later it explicitly waits for the result before using it. I think the idea is that WGMMA is asynchronous on Hopper, but I am not sure what state the computation is in after 'commit'. Does this mean the instruction has been launched to Tensor Cores but the CUDA cores can continue executing unrelated instructions? If so, what kinds of operations are actually safe to overlap before the later wait, and how does the kernel avoid reading incomplete accumulator values?
Answer:
On Hopper, “commit but do not wait” means “the WGMMA has been issued into the GPU’s asynchronous Tensor Core pipeline, and we have recorded it as an outstanding operation, but we are not stalling the warpgroup for it to finish.” So yes, conceptually the matmul is now in flight on the Tensor Cores, and the consumer warps can keep executing other instructions while that result is still being produced.

The important subtlety is that “commit” does **not** mean “the accumulator is now ready.” It only means the operation has been launched and grouped for later synchronization. The values written by that WGMMA are still incomplete until the corresponding `wait` occurs, so they must not be read, reused, or overwritten before then. In the paper’s algorithm, that is handled by double buffering: the next score tile is computed into a separate register fragment, $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$, while the current tile $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}}$ is the one already safe to feed into softmax. The code explicitly waits only at the point where the new tile is about to be consumed, so incomplete accumulator values are never used.

What can be overlapped safely is any work that does not depend on the pending WGMMA result. In this paper, that is exactly the point of the 2-stage pipeline: while one WGMMA is running, the kernel does the softmax work for the previous tile, which is made up of comparatively low-throughput floating-point operations like max, exp, and reductions. The paper notes that these non-matmul operations run on different hardware units and are much slower in throughput than matmul, so hiding them under WGMMA improves utilization. In other words, the Tensor Cores are busy on one tile while the CUDA-side arithmetic and control flow are busy on another tile.

So the answer to “how does the kernel avoid reading incomplete accumulator values?” is: by explicit synchronization and staging. The kernel never reads a WGMMA output until after the `wait`, and it keeps the in-flight output in a separate register buffer from the already-completed one. That is why the pseudocode has “commit but do not wait” followed later by a wait right before the result is actually used.
