\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does the register-byte permutation after the first FP8 WGMMA still produce the correct $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{V}$ result?
Question:
One of the most confusing parts for me is the FP8 layout fix. The paper says the FP32 accumulator layout produced by the first FP8 WGMMA does not match the layout expected for operand A of the second FP8 WGMMA, so they use byte-permute instructions to reorder the accumulator entries. They then say this logically permutes columns of the $\mathbf{P}$ tile, and they compensate by writing out a matching row permutation of the $\mathbf{V}$ tile during the in-kernel transpose. I think this means they are effectively multiplying $(\mathbf{P} \Pi)(\Pi^\top \mathbf{V})$ for some permutation matrix $\Pi$, which equals $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$. Is that the right interpretation, or am I misunderstanding what is being permuted?
Answer:
Yes — that is essentially the right way to think about it.

What the paper is doing is not changing the mathematics of attention, but changing the *internal indexing convention* used by the Hopper FP8 WGMMA instructions. The first FP8 WGMMA leaves the softmax tile $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}$ in an FP32 accumulator layout that is inconvenient for the next WGMMA. The byte-permute reorders the accumulator entries so that, when interpreted as a matrix tile, it is as if the columns of $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}$ had been permuted by some permutation matrix $\Pi$.

Once you do that, you must feed the second WGMMA a $\mathbf{V}$ tile whose rows are permuted in the matching way, because the second GEMM contracts over the same inner dimension. In matrix form, that means the kernel is effectively computing
$$
(\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}\Pi)(\Pi^\top \mathbf{V}),
$$
which is exactly
$$
\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\Pi^\top \mathbf{V}
= \widetilde{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{V}.
$$
So your interpretation is correct up to the usual convention issue: “matching row permutation” on $\mathbf{V}$ means the inverse permutation on the linear-algebra side, i.e. $\Pi^\top$, because permutation matrices satisfy $\Pi^{-1}=\Pi^\top$.

The important subtlety is that the permute instruction is acting on the *register layout*, not on the abstract attention formula. The paper’s point is that the hardware wants the accumulator fragment and the operand-$A$ fragment in different byte orders, and by making the transpose of $\mathbf{V}$ produce the correspondingly reordered rows, the kernel preserves the exact product while avoiding extra shuffle instructions.

So in short: yes, it is a compensated permutation of the shared inner dimension, and that is why the final $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{V}$ result is still correct.
