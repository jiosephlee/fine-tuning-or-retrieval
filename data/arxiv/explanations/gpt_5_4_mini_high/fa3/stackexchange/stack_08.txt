\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Why does the 2-stage pipeline increase register pressure by roughly one extra $\mathbf{S}$ tile?
Question:
The paper says the extra buffering required by the 2-stage pipeline means keeping an additional score tile like $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ in registers, with extra storage on the order of $B_r \times B_c \times \mathrm{sizeof}(\mathrm{float})$ per threadblock. I do not fully understand where this quantity comes from. Is it because while softmax is consuming the current scores, the next scores from $\mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{K}_j^\top$ have already been produced and must remain live until the wait finishes? Also, are these accumulators really all stored in registers rather than shared memory?
Answer:
Yes — that is essentially the reason. The “extra $\mathbf{S}$ tile” is not an extra global-memory or shared-memory buffer; it is extra *live state* in the consumer warps’ registers caused by software pipelining.

In the 2-stage loop, the kernel issues the WGMMA for the next score block, $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}=\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^\top$, and does **not** wait for it immediately. Instead, it uses that time to run the second GEMM for the previous block, $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{cur}} \mathbf{V}_{j-1}$, and only then waits before softmaxing $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$. So for part of the iteration both tiles must coexist: $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}}$ is still needed for the current softmax/epilogue work, while $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ has already been produced by the asynchronous WGMMA and must stay live until the `wait` completes and softmax can consume it. That is exactly the same logic as double-buffering: overlap two stages, and you need two buffers.

The paper’s estimate $B_r \times B_c \times \mathrm{sizeof}(\mathrm{float})$ comes from the size of one score tile itself. A score tile has shape $B_r \times B_c$, and on Hopper the WGMMA score accumulator is in FP32, so one tile occupies about $B_r B_c$ floats, i.e. $4 B_r B_c$ bytes, spread across the threads of the warpgroup. The paper says “per threadblock” because that is the total CTA-wide register footprint, not because one thread stores the whole tile. In practice, the tile is partitioned into per-thread accumulator fragments, but the total live register demand is still roughly that of one extra $\mathbf{S}$ tile.

So, to your second question: yes, these accumulators are kept in registers rather than shared memory in this design. That is precisely why the paper flags register pressure as the trade-off. If $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ were spilled to shared memory, you would reduce register usage, but you would also lose the point of the overlap and add extra memory traffic/latency. The 2-stage pipeline works because the next score tile stays resident in registers long enough for the softmax stage to pick it up.
