\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### How is $\pi_\theta(y\mid x)$ computed for a full response in DPO?
Question:
The DPO loss uses terms like $\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$ and $\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$, where $y$ is a whole text completion rather than a single token. For an autoregressive language model, is this just the sum of token log-probabilities,
$$
\log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)=\sum_t \log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid x, y_{<t})?
$$
If so, does DPO implicitly prefer shorter responses because they involve fewer terms, or is length handled in some special way?
Answer:
Yes. In DPO, $\pi_\theta(y \mid x)$ is the probability of the entire completion $y$ given the prompt $x$, and for an autoregressive language model that is exactly the product of the token probabilities along the completion:
$$
\pi_\theta(y \mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^{|y|}\pi_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}),
$$
so
$$
\log \pi_\theta(y \mid x)=\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\log \pi_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}).
$$
That is the quantity appearing in Eq. $(\ref{eq:optimum_model})$ of the paper, and it is computed on the completion tokens only, not on the prompt.

As for length, DPO does not introduce any special length normalization. It works with the same sequence-level log-probabilities used in standard language-model training and RLHF. So there is no extra term saying “prefer shorter answers.” Instead, the objective compares the full-sequence log-probability ratio of the chosen versus rejected completion, relative to the reference model:
$$
\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w \mid x)}-\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l \mid x)}.
$$
Because this is a ratio against the reference policy, the model is not simply rewarded for being short; it is rewarded for making the preferred completion more likely than the dispreferred one, while staying close to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ through the implicit KL regularization described in the paper.

So the right intuition is: DPO scores whole responses as sequences, by summing token log-probabilities, and any length preference comes from the preference data and the learned policy, not from a built-in “shorter is better” bias in the objective. There is no separate length-handling trick in the paper beyond using sequence-level probabilities.
