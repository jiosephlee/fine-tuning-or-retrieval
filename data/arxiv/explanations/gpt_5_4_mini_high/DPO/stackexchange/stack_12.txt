\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why are reward functions that differ by a prompt-only term $f(x)$ considered equivalent?
Question:
In the theoretical section, the paper defines two reward functions as equivalent if
$$
r(x,y)-r'(x,y)=f(x)
$$
for some function depending only on the prompt. I can see that this would cancel in pairwise reward differences, but the paper also claims that such reward functions induce the same optimal policy under the KL-constrained RL objective. Could someone explain both claims carefully? I want to understand why adding a prompt-dependent constant changes neither the preference probabilities nor the optimal policy.
Answer:
The key idea is that, in this setting, the prompt $x$ is fixed when you compare two candidate completions $y_1$ and $y_2$. So any term that depends only on $x$ behaves like a constant offset for that prompt. Constants do not affect relative comparisons, and the RL objective only cares about differences that change which completion is better, not about an arbitrary baseline level.

For the preference model, this is immediate from the Bradley-Terry form used in the paper. The preference probability is modeled as

$$
p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x)=\sigma\!\bigl(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\bigr).
$$

If $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)-f(x)$, then for the same prompt $x$,

$$
r'(x,y_1)-r'(x,y_2)
= \bigl(r(x,y_1)-f(x)\bigr)-\bigl(r(x,y_2)-f(x)\bigr)
= r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2).
$$

So the argument of $\sigma$ is unchanged, which means the preference probabilities are identical. Intuitively, subtracting the same baseline from all answers to the same prompt cannot change which answer looks better; it only shifts all scores up or down together. This is exactly why the paper says the Bradley-Terry and Plackett-Luce models are only identifiable up to a prompt-dependent additive term.

The same logic explains the KL-constrained RL objective. The paper’s RL objective is

$$
\max_{\pi_\theta}\; \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{D},\, y\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta\, \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\right].
$$

If you replace $r$ by $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)-f(x)$, then the objective becomes

$$
\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[r(x,y)-f(x)] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}[\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}]
= \Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[r(x,y)] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}[\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}]\Bigr) - \mathbb{E}_x[f(x)].
$$

The extra term $\mathbb{E}_x[f(x)]$ does not depend on the policy $\pi_\theta$ at all, since it depends only on the prompt distribution. So it cannot change which policy maximizes the objective. It just shifts the objective value by a constant.

You can also see this directly from the closed-form optimal policy derived in the paper:

$$
\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right).
$$

If $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)-f(x)$, then

$$
\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}(r(x,y)-f(x))\right)
= e^{-f(x)/\beta}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right).
$$

The factor $e^{-f(x)/\beta}$ depends only on $x$, so it gets absorbed into the normalization constant $Z(x)$. After normalization, the policy is exactly the same. That is why the paper says all rewards in the same equivalence class induce the same optimal policy.

So the punchline is: for a fixed prompt, adding $f(x)$ is just changing the baseline score of all completions equally. Pairwise preference models only see relative differences, and the KL-constrained optimum depends on rewards only through those same relative differences, with any prompt-only offset canceled by normalization. This is the sense in which the reward is only defined up to a prompt-dependent constant.
