\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### What happens in DPO if the preference dataset was generated from a different reference policy than the one used for training?
Question:
The paper mentions that in practice preference datasets may come from some unavailable SFT model, and if that model is not available they fit a new $\pi_\text{ref}$ by maximum likelihood on the preferred completions. I am confused about why this is reasonable. Since the derivation uses the actual reference policy inside
$$
\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)},
$$
wouldn't using the wrong $\pi_\text{ref}$ change the implied reward and therefore the whole objective? Is this only an approximation to reduce distribution shift?
Answer:
Yes, it changes the objective, but that is exactly why the paper treats the choice of $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ as a practical surrogate rather than something mathematically exact.

In the DPO derivation, the reference policy is part of the KL-regularized RL problem itself. If the data were generated from some behavior policy $\pi_0$, then the corresponding optimal policy under the reward $r^*$ is
$$
\pi^*(y\mid x)\propto \pi_0(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r^*(x,y)\right).
$$
If you now replace $\pi_0$ by a different reference $\tilde{\pi}_{\text{ref}}$, you are no longer writing down the same policy/reward decomposition. You can still represent the same preference distribution, but the implied reward function becomes
$$
\tilde r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y\mid x)}{\tilde{\pi}_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta \log \tilde Z(x),
$$
which differs from the original reward by a prompt-dependent constant. Since Bradley-Terry only depends on reward differences, that constant cancels out in the pairwise preference likelihood. So the preference model itself survives the change.

What does change is the KL anchor: DPO is now optimizing “stay close to $\tilde{\pi}_{\text{ref}}$ while matching the preferences,” not “stay close to the original unseen SFT model.” That is why the paper says that fitting a new reference by maximum likelihood on the preferred completions “helps mitigate the distribution shift between the true reference distribution which is unavailable, and $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ used by DPO.” In other words, it is an approximation chosen for practicality and stability, not an exact recovery of the original training setup.

So the short answer is: using the “wrong” reference does change the implicit reward parameterization and the KL-regularized objective, but DPO is still well-defined. The hope is simply that a surrogate $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ close to the data-generating policy is close enough that the learned policy remains sensible. If the mismatch is large, then yes, you should expect bias; the paper does not claim otherwise.
