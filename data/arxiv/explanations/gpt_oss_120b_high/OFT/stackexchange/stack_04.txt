### What is the analogy between orthogonal finetuning and keeping only the phase spectrum in a Fourier transform?
Question:
The authors motivate OFT by noting that swapping magnitude while keeping phase still reconstructs an image’s semantics.
* How exactly does changing neuron directions correspond to modifying the phase spectrum?
* Is there a formal relationship between cosine similarities of neurons and Fourier phase components?
Answer:
**Answer**

The “phase‑only’’ intuition that the authors use for **orthogonal finetuning (OFT)** comes from the well‑known fact that a 2‑D Fourier transform separates an image into a *magnitude* (or power) spectrum and a *phase* spectrum,
$$
X(\boldsymbol{k})=|X(\boldsymbol{k})|\;e^{\,i\,\phi_{\boldsymbol k}}, 
$$
$$
\phi_{\boldsymbol k}=\\arg X(\boldsymbol k)\,. 
$$
If we replace $|X(\boldsymbol k)|$ by a random set of amplitudes but keep every $\phi_{\boldsymbol k}$ unchanged, the inverse transform still reproduces an image whose **semantic layout** – edges, shapes, object placement – is essentially identical to the original one.  The reason is that $\phi_{\boldsymbol k}$ encodes *where* each sinusoidal basis contributes its signal; the amplitudes only tell us *how much* it contributes.

#### Bottom line

1️⃣ A neuron's response decomposes into ~~a norm (**magnitude**)~~ And ~~an angle (**phase**)~~ describing how well input aligns with that neuron’s direction;

2️⃣ An orthogonal finetuning step multiplies all weight vectors by an orthogonal matrix – rotating every direction while keeping every norm untouched – which leaves _all pairwise cosine similarities_ (and therefore hyperspherical energy)

Consequently this analogy motivates OFT : preserving pairwise angles (“phase’’) protects semantic knowledge during finetuning,
while additional learnable scaling (the optional diagonal matrix in rescaled OFT)
plays the role of adjusting magnitudes when needed. No exact algebraic identity links cosine similarities directly to Fourier phases,
but both serve as invariant descriptors of orientation on their respective unit manifolds,
explaining why OFT can legitimately be interpreted as “keeping only the phase spectrum’’ during model adaptation.​
