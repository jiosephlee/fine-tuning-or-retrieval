### What exactly is hyperspherical energy and why is it defined as $\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j\|^{-1}$?
Question:
The paper defines hyperspherical energy (HE) of a weight matrix $\mathbf{W}=\{\mathbf{w}_1,\dots,\mathbf{w}_n\}$ as
$$\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}) = \sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j\|^{-1},$$
where $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i=\mathbf{w}_i/\|\mathbf{w}_i\|$ are normalized neurons.
I am confused about two things:
1) Why use inverse Euclidean distance rather than a more common similarity such as cosine similarity or squared distance?
2) What intuition does this sum capture about “uniformity” or “redundancy” among neurons on the unit hypersphere?
A concrete interpretation would help me understand why minimizing or preserving HE matters for diffusion models.
Answer:
