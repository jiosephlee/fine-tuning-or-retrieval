### How does an orthogonal transformation preserve the hyperspherical energy of a layer?
Question:
The authors claim that if $\mathbf{R}$ is orthogonal then $\text{HE}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0)=\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}^0)$ because pairwise angles stay unchanged.
Could someone provide a short proof or intuition showing that
$$\| \widehat{( \mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}_i )} - \widehat{( \mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}_j )} \| = \| \hat{\mathbf{w}}_i - \hat{\mathbf{w}}_j \|$$
and therefore HE remains invariant?
Answer:
**Answer**

Let $W=\{{w_1,\dots ,w_n}\}\subset \mathbb R^{d}$ be the set of column‑vectors (neurons) in a layer and denote their unit‑norm versions by  

$$
    \widehat w_i := \frac{w_i}{\|\,w_i\,\|},
    \qquad i=1,\dots ,n .
$$  

The hyperspherical energy (HE) used in the paper is  

$$
    \operatorname {HE}(W)
        =   \sum_{i\sneq j}\,
            \Bigl(\,\|\;\widehat w_i-\widehat w_j\,\|\Bigr)^{-1}.
$$  

Consider an orthogonal matrix $R∈O(d)$, i.e. $R^{⊤}R=I$. Orthogonal maps are exactly rotations or reflections of $\mathbb R^{d}$; they preserve Euclidean lengths and inner products:

$$
    \|Rv\|=\|v\|,
    \qquad
    (Rv)^{⊤}(Ru)=v^{⊤}u,
    \quad
    ∀\,v,u∈ℝ^{d}.
    \tag{1}
$$  

Because a scalar multiple commutes with a linear map, from (1) we obtain

$$
    \widecheck Rv
        =   \frac{Rv}{\|Rv\|}
        =   \frac{Rv}{\|v|}
        =   R\,\frac v{\|v|}
        =   R\,{\large{\scriptstyle{\!\!}}\;\check v},
        % where $\check v:=v/\|v∥$.
    \tag{2}
$$  

Now take two neurons $w_i,w_j$. Using (2),

$$
    \begin{aligned}
        \|\;\widecheck {Rw_i}-\,\widecheck {Rw_j}\;\|
            &=
            \Bigl|
                R\,{\large{\scriptstyle{\!\!}}\;\check w_i}
                -
                R\,{\large{\scriptstyle{\!\!}}\;\check w_j}
            \Bigr|
            \\[4pt]
            &=
            |\,R(\,\check w_i-\check w_j\,)\,|.
    \end{aligned}
$$  

Applying the norm‑preserving property of orthogonal matrices again yields

$$
    |\,R(\,\check w_i-\check w_j\,)\,|
        =
        |\;\check w_i-\check w_j\:|,

        (\text{i.e. }\;\|(Rw_i)-(Rw_j)\|
        =
        |\;\check w_i-\check w_j\:|)
    .
    \tag{3}
$$

Thus an orthogonal matrix does not alter hyperspherical energy.



### Intuition

Geometrically, normalising a weight vector places it on the unit sphere $S^{d-1}$. An orthogonal matrix acts as a rigid motion (rotation or reflection) of the whole space, hence it maps every point of the sphere onto another point while preserving *all* geodesic distances on the sphere. Since HE depends only on these pairwise distances, it remains unchanged. In other words, OFT learns a global change of coordinates for an entire layer; this re‑orientation reshapes the “coordinate axes” but leaves the relative angular configuration among neurons intact, which is exactly what the paper leverages to keep the pretrained semantic structure alive during finetuning.
