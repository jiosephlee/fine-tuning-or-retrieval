\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why do OFT/BOFT preserve the spectral norm of pretrained weights while LoRA does not?
Question:
The authors claim OFT (and BOFT) preserve spectral norm because they multiply by an orthogonal matrix on the left side ($\\bm W'= \\\bm R \, \\\bm W^0$). I understand that if $\\bm R$ is perfectly orthogonal then singular values stay unchanged.

However:
* In LoRA you add a low‑rank perturbation ($\\bm W'= \\\bm W^0 + \\\bm A \, \\\bm B^{\\top}$); why does this necessarily change singular values?
* Practically after training with Adam or SGD do these theoretical guarantees hold given numerical errors?
* Are there any empirical measurements in the paper confirming that spectral norms remain stable under BOFT?
Answer:
**Answer**

The difference between Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT / BOFT) and LoRA comes down to *how* the pretrained weight matrix is altered.  
OFT/BOFT multiply the frozen weight matrix $\displaystyle\bfsymb{W}^{0}$ on the left by an orthogonal matrix $\displaystyle\bfsymb{R}$; LoRA adds a low‑rank term $\displaystyle\bfsymb{\Delta}= \bfsymb{A}\,\bfsymb{B}^{\,\!\mathsf T}$.  
Because multiplication by an orthogonal matrix never changes any singular value, while an additive low‑rank perturbation generally does, OFT/BOFT keep the spectral norm of $\displaystyle\bfsymb{W}^{0}$ intact whereas LoRA does not.

---

### 1. Why an additive low‑rank update changes singular values

Write the SVD of the pretrained weight as  

$$
\bfsymb{W}^{0}= \bfsymb{U}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\bfsymb{V}^{\,\!\mathsf T},
\qquad
\sigma_{\max}(\bfsymb{W}^{0})=\max_i \Sigma_{ii}.
$$

LoRA replaces it by  

$$
\twidtilde{\bfsymb{W}}= \bfsymb{W}^{0}+ \bfsymb{\Delta}
      =\: \bfsymb{U}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\bfsymb{V}^{\,\!\mathsf T}+ 
          \bfsymb{A}\,\bfsymb{B}^{\,\!\mathsf T}.
$$

The perturbation $\displaystyle\bfsymb{\Delta}$ has rank at most  
$r=\operatorname{rank}(\bfsymb{A})=\operatorname{rank}(\bfsymb{B})$.  
From Weyl’s inequality for singular values we have  

$$
\bigl|\sigma_{\max}(\twidtilde{\bfsymb W})
      -\sigma_{\max}(\bfsymb W^{0})\bigr|
  	\: \le\: 
  	\norm{\twidtilde\bfsymb{\Delta}}_{2}
	= 	\norm{\bfsymb A\bfsymb B^{\,!\mathsf T}}_{2}
	\: \le\: 
	\norm{\bfsymb A}_{2}\,\norm{\bfsymb B}_{2}.
$$

Unless both $\displaystyle\bfsymb A=\boldsymbol 0$ and $\displaystyle\bfsymb B=\boldsymbol 0$, the right–hand side is positive, so in general
the largest singular value – i.e.\ the **spectral norm** – will move away from its original value.
Intuitively, adding a new rank‑$r$ component creates new directions in weight space that can amplify or attenuate some inputs, therefore altering the operator norm.

In contrast, an orthogonal left–multiplication  

$$
\twidehat{\bfsymb W}= 
	\bfsymb R\,\bfsymb W^{0}
	= (\,\bfsymb R\bfsym b U)\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,
	 	   	\bfsym b V^{\,!\mathsf T}
$$

creates a new factor $\displaystyle\twidtilde{\!\!U}= \,\bfsym b R\bfsymbol U.$  
Since both $\displaystyle\bfboldsymbol R,\;\,\boldsymbol U,\;\,\boldsymbol V$
are orthogonal,
$(\:{}\twidetilde U,\;\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\:{}\twidetilde V\:)=(
	\twidetilde U ,\:{}\boldsymbol {\Sigma},\:{}\boldsymbol V)$
is again an SVD of $\displaystyle\twidehat\bffun W$.  
All diagonal entries of $\displaystyle\vectsym Sigma$
– hence every singular value –
stay exactly the same,
and particularly  

$$
\sigma_{\max}(\twidehat\bffun W)= 
      	\sigma_{\max}(\btwslrawsym bW^0),
       		\vphantom{}
       		&&
\normtwoof{
    	\twidehat\bffun W}
	  =
	 	 	\normtwoof{
	    	 	 	\bffun W^0}.
$$

Thus OFT preserves the whole spectrum,
while LoRA inevitably perturbs it.

(and expand on them in Appendix A).

What follows are extensive experiments across vision transformers,
language models and diffusion models showing **greater training stability**

## Summary

* LoRA’s additive low‑rank term changes the operator norm because any non–zero perturbation shifts singular values according to Weyl’s inequality.
* OFT (and its butterfly variant BOFT) keep **all** singular values unchanged because they act solely through an ***exact*** orthogonal left multiplication.
* In practice those guarantees survive optimisation with Adam/SGD up to floating–point noise;
  implementations enforce skew–symmetry at each step so drift is negligible.
* The paper provides a rigorous theoretical justification and indirect empirical evidence via improved stability,
  although it does not include a dedicated figure tracking the spectral norm during finetuning.

Hence OFT/BOFT preserve the pretrained model’s spectral norm whereas LoRA does not—a property that contributes to their reported robustness and generalisation advantages.
