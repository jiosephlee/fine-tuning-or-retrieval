### Mandatory vs.\ free edges

An **edge** is simply a non‐zero element of one factor ${𝘽}_{𝚒}$.  
If ${𝘽}_{𝚒}$ were an arbitrary dense matrix it would contain ${𝙙}^{²}$ such elements.  
Orthogonal factors however cannot be arbitrarily sparse.  
The *minimal* sparse pattern that still makes ${𝘽}_{𝚒}$ an orthogonal matrix is a **signed permutation:**  
every row contains exactly one non‐zero entry, every column contains exactly one non‐zero entry,
and the value of each such entry has magnitude ${𝟭}$ ($±𝟭`).  

These ${𝟭}$ singletons form a *perfect matching* between the two levels.  
Because their location and sign are forced by ​the requirement ${𝘽}_{𝚒}^{⟂}{𝘽}_{𝚒}={𝕀}_{𝙙}$,
they do **not** contribute any learnable degrees‐of‐freedom.  
The authors therefore call them **mandatory edges**
(or simply “the ${𝟭}$ required edges”).  

All other non‐zero entries – those placed on top of the perfect matching – 
are *optional*. Their positions may be chosen arbitrarily,
subject later to the global dense‐connectivity requirement,
and their values are continuous parameters learned during finetuning.
These optional connections are what the paper terms **free edges**,   
and they correspond exactly to the trainable parameters of BOFT.
