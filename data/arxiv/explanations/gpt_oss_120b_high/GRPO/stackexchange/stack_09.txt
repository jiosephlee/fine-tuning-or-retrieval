Question:
"Equation (1) abstracts many methods under $\nabla_\theta \mathcal J_ {\mathcal A}(\theta)= \mathbb E_{(q,o)#sim#}{D} [ \frac{1}{|o|}\sum_t GC_{\mathcal A}(q,o,t,$\\ pi$_{rf}) \; \nabla_\theta \log \pi_ {\theta}(o_t|...) ]$. While I understand that $GC_{\mathcal A}$ encodes things like advantages or preference weights,
but I’m unsure how it looks concretely for
* Supervised fine-tuning,

* PPO,

Answer:
In the unified view introduced in § 4‑2 the whole family of fine‑training and reinforcement‑learning procedures can be written as  

$$
\nabla_{\!\theta}\,\mathcal J_{\!\mathcal A}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{(q,o)\sim\mathcal D}\!
		\Bigl[\;
			{\frac{1}{|o|}}\;\sum_{t=1}^{|o|}
				GC_{\!\mathcal A}(q,o,t,\pi_{rf})
				\,;\,
				\nabla_\theta\log \pi_\theta(o_t\,|\,
					q,\!o_{< t})
		\bigr]\!.
$$  

All methods differ only in the **gradient‑coefficient** \(GC_{\!\mathcal A}\); everything else—sampling distribution $\mathcal D$ and the log‑probability term—is shared. Below is a concise description of what \(GC_{\!\mathcal A}\) looks like for the four algorithms you asked about, together with an intuition for each form.

---

### Supervised fine‑training (SFT)

SFT maximises the likelihood of a human‑written answer $o^\star$.  
Because there is no external reward signal, every token receives exactly the same weight:

$$
GC_{\!\text{SFT}}(q,o^\star,t,\cdot)=1 .
$$  

The factor “$\frac{1}{|o|}$’’ already normalises over sequence length, so SFT simply pushes up the probability of every gold token equally—no advantage estimation, no importance‑sampling ratio.

---
