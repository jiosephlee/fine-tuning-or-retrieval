### Why does GRPO place a KL regularizer directly in the loss instead of adding a per-token KL penalty as done in PPO?
Question:
Equation (2) shows that GRPO adds "-$\beta \; \mathbb{D}_{KL}[\pi_{\theta}\parallel\pi_{ref}]" to its objective, whereas PPO’s reward definition (Equation (1)) incorporates a term $-\beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_t|\dots)}{\pi_{ref}(o_t|\dots)}$. I am unsure whether these two approaches are mathematically equivalent or whether moving the KL term outside of the advantage calculation changes gradients differently.
Specifically:
• What effect does applying an unbiased estimator of $\mathbb{D}_{KL}$ have on token-level updates?
• Does this simplification remove any bias introduced by GAE-based advantage estimates?
Clarification would help me see why GRPO can reduce memory usage.
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
GRPO does not *mathematically* replace the per‑token KL term used in PPO; it simply moves the same regulariser out of the *reward* computation and treats it as an *extra loss* that is added after the clipped‑surrogate part of the objective. Because GRPO does not need a value‑function critic, its baseline is obtained from the average reward of a **group** of sampled completions, which makes the whole computation cheaper in memory. The unbiased Monte‑Carlo estimator that they plug in for  

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\,\Vert\,\pi_{\text{ref}})
   = \mathbb{E}_{o\sim \pi_{\theta}}\!\Bigl[\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)}{\pi_{\theta}(o)}
        - \log \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)}{\pi_{\theta}(o)} - 1\Bigr]
$$  

gives an exact expected KL while requiring only the probabilities of the **single** sampled trajectory; it therefore does not introduce bias into the token‑level gradients. By removing the KL term from the advantage calculation GRPO also eliminates the small bias that arises when Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) mixes the KL‑penalised reward with bootstrapped value estimates.
