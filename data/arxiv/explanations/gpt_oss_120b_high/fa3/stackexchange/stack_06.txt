\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What are the FLOP count formulas used for benchmarking forward and backward passes of attention?
Question:
In Section 5.1 the authors compute FLOPs as $4 \cdot \text{seqlen}^2 \cdot \text{head dimension} \cdot \text{number of heads}$ for the forward pass and multiply by $2.5$ for backward pass. 

- Why is there a factor of 4 multiplying $\text{seqlen}^2$?
- How does this account for both $QK^\top$ and $PV$ matmuls?
- Where does the $2.5$ multiplier come from – is it based on exact operation counts or an empirical approximation?

Clarifying this will help me compare reported TFLOPs with raw operation counts.

Answer:
The numbers quoted in § 5.1 come directly from counting how many **multiply‑add** operations are performed by the dense matrix products that dominate an attention layer.  
In deep‑learning performance reporting a fused multiply‑add is usually counted as **two floating‑point operations** – one multiplication and one addition – even when a hardware instruction implements them jointly on Tensor Cores.  
With this convention every dense GEMM contributes a factor "$2$", and this is what creates the “$4$’’ in front of $\text{seqlen}^{2}$.

---

### Forward pass

For one head let  

* $Q,K,V\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$ ($N=$ sequence length, $d=$ head‑dimension),  
* $S=QK^{\top}\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}$,  
* $O=PV\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$ with $P=\operatorname{softmax}(S)$.

The two expensive kernels are  

1. **$QK^{\top}$** : an $N\times d$ times a $d\times N$ matrix multiplication.  
   For every output entry we do $d$ multiplications followed by $d-1\simeq d$ additions → roughly  
   $$
   2Nd\,(\times N)= 2\,N^{2}d
   $$ 
   floating‑point ops.

2. **$PV$** : an $N\times N$ times an $N\times d$ multiplication, which has exactly the same size pattern, therefore another  
   $$
   2\,N^{2}d
   $$ 
   flops.

Adding them gives  

$$
{\rm FLOP}_{\rm fwd}^{({\rm per\:head})}=  
(2+2)\,N^{2}d = 4\,{\rm seqlen}^{\,2}\times{\rm head\:dim}.
$$

Because an MHA contains “num‑heads’’ independent copies of this computation,
the total forward count becomes  

$$
{\rm FLOP}_{\rm fwd}=    
4\,{\rm seqlen}^{\,2}\times{\rm head\:dim}\times {\rm num\:heads},
$$

which is exactly the expression reported in the paper.  
(The authors ignore scalar work such as dividing by $\sqrt{d}$, adding biases,
or forming row‑wise maxima – those operations contribute far fewer arithmetic
operations compared with GEMMs.)

When causal masking is applied only roughly half of the entries of
$S=PV$ are actually needed, so they simply halve this quantity,
as mentioned in § 5.1.

### Backward pass

Back‑propagation through attention requires five dense GEMMs per head
(they follow directly from equations (6)–(10) in § 3):

| Gradient                                   | Operation | Shape                       |
|--------------------------------------------|-----------|-----------------------------|
| ${\partial L}/{\partial V}=P^{\top}\,\partial O$      | GEMM      | $N\times N \;\times\; N\times d$ |
| ${\partial L}/{\partial P}= \partial O\,V^{\!\top}$    | GEMM      | $N\times d \;\times\; d\times N$ |
| …                                          | …         | …                           |
| ${\partial L}/{\partial Q}= (\partial S)\,K        $      | GEMM      | $N\times N \;\times\; N\times d$ |
| ${\partial L}/{\partial K}= (\partial S)^{!\top} Q            $      | GEMM      | same size                   |

Each one costs again roughly $2\,N^{2}d$ flops,
so per head  

$$
{\rm FLOP}_{\rm bwd}^{({{\rm per\:head}})}=
5\times(2\,N^{2}d)=10\,{\rm seqlen}^{\,2}{\rm head\:dim}.
$$

Comparing with the forward count gives  

$$
\dfrac{{\rm FLOP}_{\rm bwd}}{{\rm FLOP}_{\rm fwd}}=
\dfrac{10}{4}=2.5 .
$$

Thus multiplying the forward‑pass flop estimate by **$2.5$**
is not an empirical fudge factor—it follows directly from counting
the five matrix multiplications required during back‑propagation versus
the two required during inference.
(The small additional work required for softmax’s Jacobian,
for scaling by $1/\sqrt{d}$,
or for residual connections has been omitted because it contributes less than
a few percent to the total arithmetic load.)

---

### Summary

* The prefactor **$4 = 0+0+0+0+$**, i.e. two dense GEMMs each contributing “$\displaystyle  *$, twice’’ (because each fused multiply–add counts as two FLOPs), yields the leading coefficient 4.
* These two GEMMs correspond precisely to **$QK^{⟂}$** and **$PV$.**
* The backward multiplier derives analytically:
  forward uses two GEMMs while backward needs five,
  so $\displaystyle {\rm flops}_{bwd}=({5}/{₂})·{\rm flops}_{fwd}=10/4=⟨[\dots]\!⟩$
  i.e., $\displaystyle {\frac{{10}}{{4}}}= {5}{/}{₂}$,
  giving the overall ratio $\,${${5}{/}{₂}}$\,$≈ $~\$ ”.*
  
In short,
the authors’ TFLOP/s figures are obtained by dividing wall‑clock time
by these analytically derived flop counts;
they therefore reflect pure arithmetic intensity centered on
the dominant matrix‑multiplication work rather than an empirical approximation.​
