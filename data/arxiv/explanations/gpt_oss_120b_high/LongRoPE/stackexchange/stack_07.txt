\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How is the size of the search space ($4 \times 10^{167}$) computed?
Question:
The paper states that for a $4 \times$ extension ratio there are roughly $400^{128/2}\times14 = 4 \times 10^{167}$ possible configurations.
* Where does the factor 400 come from – is it related to discretizing $\lambda_i$ between 1.0 and $s \times 1.25$ with step size 0.01?
* Why are there $(128/2)$ dimensions considered rather than all $d$ dimensions?
* What role does "14" play – is it the number of choices for $\hat n$?
A step‑by‑step breakdown would clarify how massive this combinatorial explosion really is.
Answer:
The quoted figure comes directly from the definition of the **search space** in Sec.​3 (“Searching the non‑uniform position interpolation”).  
For every RoPE frequency index \(i\) we have a rescaling factor \(\lambda_i;\) apart from those factors we also have one integer \(\hat n\) that tells how many leading token positions keep their original rotary angles.  
The total number of admissible configurations is therefore  

```math
|{\mathcal S}|
=\Bigl(\#\,{\text{choices for }}\,{\lambda_i}\Bigr)^{\,d/2}\times
   \Bigl(\#\,{\text{choices for }}\,{\hat n}\Bigr).
```  

Below each term is explained and then evaluated for the concrete example **extension ratio** \(s=4\times{}\) that leads to “\(4\times10^{167}\)”.

### How many values can a single \(\lambda_i\) take?

In Table II the authors state that  

* \(\lambda_i\) may vary from a minimum of **\(1.0{}\)** (pure extrapolation) up to **\(s\times1.25{}\)**,
* it is sampled **every \(0.01{}\)** unit.

When \(s=4,\)

```math
s\times1.25 = 5 .
```  

Hence the admissible interval is \([\,1.0,\;5.0\,]\).  
With step size \(\Delta = 0\.01,\)

```math
N_{\lambda}
=
\dfrac{5-1}{0\.01}+1
   \;\approx\: 401 .
```  

The paper rounds this count down to **\(400{}\)** because an off‑by‑one does not affect the order‑of‑magnitude estimate that follows (the product will be dominated by the exponential term).

Thus **the “400’’** comes exactly from discretising the range   
\( [\,1,\; s\times1.25\,]\) with step \(0\.01.\)

### Why \(d/2\) instead of \(d?\)

RoPE encodes a token position through alternating cosine– and sine–terms  

```math
[%
   {\cos}(n\,{\theta}_0),\: {\sin}(n\,{\theta}_0),%
   {\cos}(n\,{\theta}_1),\: {\sin}(n\,{\theta}_1),%
   \dots ,%
   {\cos}(n\,{\theta}_{d/2-1}),\: {\sin}(n\,{\theta}_{d/2-1})%
] .
```  

Every **pair** *(cos , sin)* shares exactly one rotation frequency
${\theta_i=\beta^{-i}}$; consequently a single scaling factor
$\lambda_i$ multiplies both members of the pair simultaneously.
Only the number of distinct frequencies matters,
which equals half the embedding dimension.
For LLaMA‑2‑7 B the head dimension used for RoPE is $d=128$, therefore there are  

$$d/2 = 64$$  

independent rescaling variables.
That explains why the exponent in the expression is written as **\(128/2.\)**

### What does “14’’ count?

The second source of non‑uniformity concerns token positions.
The algorithm keeps an initial segment of length $\hat n$ unchanged
and applies the searched factors only afterwards.
The admissible thresholds are explicitly listed in Sec.​3 :

```
{0,  1,  2,  4,  8, 12,
16, 20, 24, 28,
32, 64,
128,
256}
```  

Counting these entries gives exactly **14** possibilities.
Hence **the “14’’** multiplies the exponential term because every choice
of $\{\lambda_i\bigr)\}_{i=0}^{63}$ can be combined with any one of those fourteen thresholds.

### Putting everything together

For an extension ratio $s=4\times{}$

* each dimension contributes roughly $N_{\lambda}\approx400$ possibilities,
* there are $d/2 = 64$ independent dimensions,
* and there are $14$ choices for $\hat n.$

Consequently  

```math
|{\mathcal S}|
\simeq
   400^{64}\times14 .
```  

A quick logarithmic check shows why this evaluates to around $10^{167}$:

```
log10(400^64) = 64·log10(400)
               ≈ 64·(log10(4)+log10(100))
               ≈ 64·(0.60206+2)
               ≈ 166.53 .
log10(14)     ≈ 1.146 .
Total log10   ≈ 166.53+1.146 ≈ 167.68 .
```  

Hence  

$$|{\mathcal S}| \;\approx\: 10^{167.68} 
    \;\approx\: 4\times10^{167}. $$

Even if we had taken $401^{64}\times14$, the result would still lie in
the same order‑of‑magnitude region; therefore reporting “$4\times10^{167}$”
accurately captures the astronomic size of the configuration space.

### Why this matters

The estimate demonstrates that an exhaustive enumeration would require evaluating more ways than can be generated even by modern super‑computers within any realistic time budget.
That motivates LongRoPE’s **evolutionary search** together with two practical tricks (seeded initial population and a monotonicity constraint on the $\lambda_i)$); these reduce the *effective* number of candidates dramatically while preserving enough diversity to locate good solutions.

### Summary

*The “400’’ comes from discretising every rescale factor between $1.\!00$ and $s\times\:1.\!25$, i.e., between $1.\!00,\dots ,5.\!00,$*  
*Only half of the embedding dimensions are independent because each frequency appears twice (cosine & sine), giving an exponent $d/2 =128/2.$*  
*The multiplicative constant “14’’ equals the cardinality of the allowed values for $\hat n$, i.e., how many initial tokens stay untouched.*  

Multiplying these three ingredients yields  

$$|{\mathcal S}| \;\approx\: 400^{64}\cdot14 
                      \;\approx\: 4\xspace{}\cdot\xspace{}10^{167},$$

which quantifies precisely why sophisticated black‑box optimisation—not brute force—is indispensable for searching non‑uniform RoPE interpolations.</div>
