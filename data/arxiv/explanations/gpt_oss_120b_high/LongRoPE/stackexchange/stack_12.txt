\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### "Crowded" positions in linear PI – what does this mean geometrically?
Question:
"Linear positional interpolation reduces all rotation angles by factor $s$, making position information very 'crowded'" (Section 3). I am trying to visualize:
* In angular space $(\cos,n)$ versus $(\sin,n)$ what constitutes crowding—do many tokens map onto nearby points on the unit circle?
* How does crowding impact self‑attention softmax computations concretely – e.g., causing less discriminative dot products between queries/keys?
* Are there quantitative metrics (e.g., minimal angular separation statistics) that capture crowding severity?
Answer:
The “crowded‑positions’’ remark in Section 3 refers to what happens when the **linear positional interpolation (PI)** rule is applied to RoPE.  
RoPE represents a token at absolute position $n$ as a set of two‑dimensional rotations  

$$
\bigr[\cos (n\,\theta_{0}),\:;\:\sin (n\,\theta_{0}),\:;
      \cos (n\,\theta_{1}),\:;\:\sin (n\,\theta_{1}),\:;\:\dots\bigr],
      \qquad
      \theta_{i}= \beta^{-i},\:;
      \beta=\theta^{2/d},
$$  

so that each pair $(\cos ,\: \sinh)$ lives on its own unit circle (or “frequency plane’’).  
If we plot the points for one frequency plane we obtain equally spaced points around the circle, with an angular step  

$$
\vDelta_{\vartheta,i}= \frac{1}{\beta^{\,i}} .
\tag{1}
$$  

---

### Geometry of crowding under linear PI  

Linear PI rescales every rotary angle by the extension ratio $s>1$:  

$$
 n~{}\longrightarrow~{}\frac{n}{s},
        \qquad
        {\rm so~that}\qquad
        \vartheta_{i}(n)= \frac{n}{s\,\beta^{\,i}} .
\tag{2}
$$  

Equation (2) leaves the *maximum* angle attained at the farthest position unchanged (${\displaystyle 
        \vartheta _{i}(L')=\vartheta _{i}(L)}$
because $L'=sL$), but it shrinks the *step* between successive tokens from $\vDelta_{\vartheta,i}$ in (1) to  

$$
\vDelta_{\vartheta,i}^{\,{\rm PI}}=
        \frac{1}{s\,\beta^{\,i}}
        ~=~{\displaystyle\vDelta_{\vartheta,i}\over s}.
\tag{3}
$$  

Thus the set of points that previously occupied one full revolution now occupy only a ${{}_{{}\!}}\!{}{\displaystyle  {\ } }${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}$${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}${}{${$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}{$}$​fraction​of​it​.​In​other​words​many​more​token​indices​are​packed​into​the​same​arc​length​of​each​unit​circle – they become **geometrically crowded**. If you visualise the two‐dimensional planes side‐by‐side you would see long rows of points that are almost indistinguishable from one another, especially in higher‐frequency planes where $\beta^{\,i}$ is small and the step size was already tiny before scaling.  

---

### Consequence for self‑attention  

In RoPE‑augmented self‑attention each query–key inner product acquires an extra cosine factor per frequency plane:  

$$
q^\top k~=~
        (\text{content part})+
        {\sum}_{i=0}^{d/2-1}\!
        \cos\bigr(
                \vartheta _{i}(n_q)-%
                \vartheta _{i}( n_k )
              \bigr).
\tag{4}
$$  

When two positions are far apart their angle difference is large and the cosine term can become negative, sharply lowering the similarity. After linear PI the difference between two nearby indices is reduced by a factor $s$
[see (3)], so for most pairs we have  

$$
|\vartheta _{i}( n_q)-%
\vartheta _{i}( n_k )|
   ~{\approx }~
   {\displaystyle{| n_q-n_k |\over s\,%
                     \beta ^{{}\! i}}}
   ~{\ll }~ 1 ,
   ~~{\rm and }\quad%
   \cos (\cdot )~{\approx }~ 1 .
\tag{5}
$$  

Consequently the summed cosine term in (4) stays close to its maximum value $d/2$, irrespective of how far apart $ n_q,n_k $ actually are. The resulting query–key similarity distribution becomes **flatter**, so when the softmax is applied over all keys the attention scores no longer concentrate on nearby tokens but spread almost uniformly across a long span. The model therefore loses its ability to “focus’’ on truly relevant context – this is exactly what the authors refer to when they say that linear PI makes position information “very crowded’’ and harms performance at large extension ratios.

$$
