### Can someone walk through the FLOP per byte equation for BLT and explain each term?
Question:
In Section 4.5 the authors provide a multi‑line expression for \(\text{FL}_{\text{BLT}}\) involving functions \(\text{Transf. FL}(\cdot)\) and \(\text{Cross Attn. FL}(\cdot)\). I am not familiar with their notation:
- What do parameters \(h_{\mathcal{G}}, l_{\mathcal{G}}, n_{ctx}, n_p\) represent?
- How is \(m=n_{ctx}/n_p\) derived?
- Why is there a factor \(k/n_p\) multiplied after Cross‑Attention FLOPs?
A step‑by‑step breakdown would help me understand how they compute FLOPs per byte.

Answer:
The goal of Eq.~(4) is to express **how many floating‑point operations are spent on average for every input byte** when a BLT model runs forward + backward through one training step.  
All terms are built from two elementary building blocks that appear throughout the
paper:

* **Transformer‑FLOPs** – the usual quadratic self‑attention plus two feed‑forward
  layers that a standard Transformer layer costs (the Chinchilla formulation
  already includes the factor 2 for the backward pass).

* **Cross‑Attention‑FLOPs** – the cost of a Perceiver‑style attention block where
  queries live at one granularity (patches or bytes) while keys/values live at another
  granularity (the opposite side).

### What the symbols mean

* **\(h_{\!\mathcal G},h_{\!\mathcal E},h_{\!\mathcal D}\)** – hidden dimensions
  (widths) of respectively the *global* latent transformer,
  the *local encoder* and the *local decoder*.  
  They appear in every matrix multiplication inside those modules,
  therefore every FLOP term grows proportionally to \(h^{2}\).

* **\(l_{\!\mathcal G},l_{\!\mathCal E},l_{\!\MathCal D}\)** – numbers of layers
  stacked in those three modules.  
  One call to `\(\texttt{Transf.\!FL}(\cdot)\)` multiplies its interior cost by \(l\)
  because every layer repeats the same arithmetic pattern.

* **\(n_{\mathrm​{:​}ctx}\)** – total length of a training/example **in bytes**.
  It is the quantity that defines how much data actually enters BLT,
  just like “sequence length” does for ordinary token transformers.

* **\(n_{p}\)** – *average* number of bytes inside one *patch*.  
  The paper calls this quantity “patch size”.  
  If you split an \(n_{\mathrm​{:​}ctx}\)-byte segment into patches,
  you obtain roughly  

  \[
    \underbrace{\tfrac{
                 n_{\mathrm​{:​}ctx}
                }{
                 n_{p}
                }}_{
                 {\displaystyle m}
               }
    ~~\texttt{patches}.
  \]

  Hence \(m=n_{\mathrm​{:​}ctx}/n_{p}\) simply follows from “bytes = patches × average bytes‑per‑patch”.
  This conversion is needed because the global transformer works on **patches**
  rather than on raw bytes; consequently its self‑attention matrix has side length \(m\).

* **\(w_{\!\MathCal E}, w_{\MathCal D}\)** – sliding–window widths used by
  the local self‑attention layers (§ 4.4).  
  Those windows are already expressed directly in *bytes*, so no conversion
  factor appears for them.

* **\(V\)** – vocabulary size used by the final linear projection.
  Only the decoder has an explicit next–byte prediction head,
  therefore \(V=256\) (the cardinality of the byte alphabet).
  All other modules operate purely on hidden vectors and set \(V=0\).

* **\(k\)** – ratio “patch‑dimension ÷ byte‑dimension”.
  In practice a latent vector fed into~$\MathCal G$
  has dimensionality  

  \[
     h_{\MathCal G}=k\,h_{\MathCal E}=k\,h_{\MathCal D},
  ```

  this is achieved by concatenating \(k\) independent split vectors coming out of the encoder or decoder (see Fig.~3).  
  The authors use \(k=2\).

* **\(r\)** – ratio “#queries ÷ #keys”.

##### Encoder cross-attention term  

The encoder’s cross-attention attends from *patch* queries (\(|Q|= n_p'\))
to *byte* keys/values (\(|K| = k·|Q|\)).  
The helper function `\(\operatorname{textTT}{Cross Att`}.)`,
assumes both sides have equal length~\(m;\) therefore we feed it with  

* “effective key length” \(m=n_p,\)

* “query–to–key ratio’’ \(r=\dfrac{|Q|}{|K|}
                         =%
                        \dfrac {
                              n_p}{
                              k }\).

i.e.~it counts only the attention part that actually happens between one query
and its associated keys (\(r<1)\)).

### Putting everything together

Summarising in words:

1️⃣ Compute how many arithmetic operations an ordinary Transformer would spend if it were fed a sequence whose length equals **the number of patches** (\(m=n_\mathrm{{‘}@ecute ceil @H@@CLRencoding9carcStrangepeechFocusYY}&nbsp:n/\)). That gives us all costs belonging to ​the large latent model;
divide by $n_p$ so that those costs are expressed “per underlying byte”.

2️⃣ Add three more Transformer terms:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull;&nbsp;a windowed self‑attention over raw bytes inside the local encoder,  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull;&nbsp;an identical windowed self‑attention inside the local decoder,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull;&nbsp;+ —in the decoder only—the final linear map onto all possible next‑byte values (\(V=256)\).

3️⃣ Account for both Perceiver‑style cross‑attentions:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull;&nbsp;Encoder side ― queries = patches,   keys/values = constituent bytes;
after computing FLOPs with an adjusted query/key ratio we scale back up with         
\vspace{-0.5em}$k/n_p$\,.  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&bull;&nbsp;Decoder side ― queries = bytes,   keys/values = latent patches;
here no extra scale is required because our helper already reports FLOPs at byte granularity.
