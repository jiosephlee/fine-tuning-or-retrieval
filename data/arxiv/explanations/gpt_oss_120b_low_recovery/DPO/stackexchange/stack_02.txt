\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does the partition function Z(x) cancel out in the Bradley‑Terry preference model after reparameterization?
Question:
Equation 4 expresses the reward as $r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}+\beta \log Z(x)$. The Bradley‑Terry model depends only on the reward difference $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$. The authors claim that the $\log Z(x)$ terms cancel when plugging Eq. 4 into Eq. 1, yielding Eq. 6. I would like a concrete demonstration of this cancellation, especially since $Z(x)$ depends on $x$ but not on the specific $y$. How does the subtraction of the two rewards remove the $\log Z(x)$ term?
Answer:
In the Bradley–Terry (BT) model the probability that a human prefers completion \(y_{1}\) over \(y_{2}\) given a prompt \(x\) is  

\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)=\frac{\exp\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)}
{\exp\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)+\exp\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr)}
=\sigma\!\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr) .
\tag{1}
\]

Thus only **differences** of the latent reward \(r^{*}\) matter; any additive term that depends only on \(x\) disappears because it cancels in the subtraction.

From the KL‑constrained optimisation (Eq. 3 of the paper) the optimal policy for a given reward is  

\[
\pi_{r}(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\;\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\,
\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr),
\qquad 
Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr).
\tag{2}
\]

Taking logs and solving for the reward gives the **re‑parameterisation** used in the paper  

\[
r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_{r}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x).
\tag{3}
\]

Now substitute (3) for the ground‑truth reward \(r^{*}\) inside the BT expression (1).  
For the two completions we obtain  

\[
\begin{aligned}
r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})
&= \beta\Bigl[\log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{1}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1}\mid x)}
          -\log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{2}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{2}\mid x)}\Bigr]  \\
&\quad +\beta\bigl[\log Z(x)-\log Z(x)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

Because the partition function \(Z(x)\) is *identical* for both terms (it depends only on the prompt \(x\) and not on the particular completion), the two \(\log Z(x)\) contributions cancel exactly.  What remains is  

\[
r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})
= \beta \log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{1}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1}\mid x)}
   -\beta \log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{2}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{2}\mid x)} .
\tag{4}
\]

Plugging (4) back into the logistic function of the BT model yields Eq. 6 of the paper:

\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)
= \frac{1}{1+\exp\!\Bigl(
      \beta\log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{2}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{2}\mid x)}
     -\beta\log\frac{\pi^{*}(y_{1}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1}\mid x)}\Bigr)} .
\]

The key observation is that the BT model never uses the absolute value of a reward, only the *difference* between rewards for two alternatives.  Since the term \(\beta\log Z(x)\) is the same for every \(y\) at a given prompt, it drops out of the difference, and therefore disappears from the preference probability.  This is why the partition function, although present in the original reward parametrisation, does not appear in the final preference expression.
