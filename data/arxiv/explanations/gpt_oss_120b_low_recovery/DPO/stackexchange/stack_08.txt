\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does the gradient in Eq. 7 contain the term $\sigma(\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_l)-\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_w))$ rather than $1-\sigma(\cdot)$?
Question:
The loss in Eq. 5 is $-\log \sigma(\Delta)$ where $\Delta = \beta\log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_w)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w)}-\beta\log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_l)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l)}$. When differentiating $-\log \sigma(\Delta)$, I would expect the derivative to involve $-\sigma(-\Delta)=1-\sigma(\Delta)$. However, Eq. 7 shows $\sigma(\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_l)-\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_w))$, which equals $\sigma(-\Delta)$. Could someone clarify the sign convention and confirm the correct form of the gradient?
Answer:
In the DPO paper the loss that is actually optimized is  

\[
\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)=
-\mathbb E_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal D}\!
\Bigl[\,
\log\sigma\Bigl(
\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}
-
\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}
\Bigr)\Bigr].
\tag{5}
\]

If we introduce  

\[
\hat r_\theta(x,y)\;=\;\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)},
\]

then the inner argument of the logistic function is simply \(\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\).  
Thus (5) can be written as  

\[
\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)=
-\mathbb E\!\bigl[\log\sigma\bigl(\Delta\bigr)\bigr],
\qquad
\Delta\;=\;\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l).
\tag{6}
\]

---

### Derivative of the binary‑cross‑entropy term  

For a scalar variable \(z\)

\[
\frac{d}{dz}\bigl[-\log\sigma(z)\bigr]
= -\frac{1}{\sigma(z)}\,\sigma(z)\bigl(1-\sigma(z)\bigr)
= -\bigl(1-\sigma(z)\bigr).
\]

Applying the chain rule to (6) we obtain

\[
\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}}{\partial\theta}
= -\Bigl(1-\sigma(\Delta)\Bigr)\,
\frac{\partial\Delta}{\partial\theta}.
\tag{7}
\]

Now \(\Delta=\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\), so  

\[
\frac{\partial\Delta}{\partial\theta}
= \nabla_\theta\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\nabla_\theta\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)
= \beta\Bigl[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\Bigr].
\tag{8}
\]

Insert (8) into (7):

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}=
-\beta\,
\Bigl[1-\sigma(\Delta)\Bigr]\,
\Bigl[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\Bigr].
}
\tag{9}
\]

---

### Why the paper writes \(\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\)

Notice that  

\[
1-\sigma(\Delta) = 1-\sigma\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\bigr)
= \sigma\bigl(-\Delta\bigr)
= \sigma\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr).
\]

The logistic function satisfies \(\sigma(-z)=1-\sigma(z)\).  
Therefore the factor \(\bigl[1-\sigma(\Delta)\bigr]\) in (9) can be **equivalently** written as \(\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\).  

The authors chose the latter form because it makes the *weighting* interpretation clearer: the weight is large when the **implicit reward** assigns a higher value to the *dispreferred* completion \(y_l\) than to the preferred one \(y_w\). In that situation \(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\) is positive, \(\sigma(\cdot)\) approaches 1, and the gradient pushes the policy harder to correct the mistake. Conversely, when the model already ranks \(y_w\) above \(y_l\) the difference is negative, \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is near 0, and the example contributes little to the update.

Hence there is no sign error; the two expressions are mathematically identical, the paper simply rewrites \(1-\sigma(\Delta)\) as \(\sigma(-\Delta)\) to highlight the intuitive “higher‑weight‑when‑the‑reward‑estimate‑is‑wrong’’ interpretation. The final gradient shown in Eq. 7 of the paper is therefore correct.
