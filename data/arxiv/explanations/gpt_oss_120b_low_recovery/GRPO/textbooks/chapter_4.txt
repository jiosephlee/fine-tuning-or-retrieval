\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 4: **Pre‑training DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B**

In this chapter we describe in detail how the 7 billion‑parameter base model, **DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B**, was constructed.  The model is the result of a large‑scale continual pre‑training process that begins from a code‑oriented checkpoint, consumes a carefully balanced mixture of mathematical and non‑mathematical data, and follows a training schedule that has been tuned for stability and efficiency.  All the design decisions stem from the experiments reported in the DeepSeekMath paper, and the description below is intended to give a newcomer a clear, step‑by‑step understanding of how the model was built.

---

### Model Initialization from DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5

The starting point for DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B is **DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B**, a 7‑billion‑parameter language model that had already been trained on a large corpus of source‑code and natural‑language data.  The authors observed that initializing from a code‑trained checkpoint is advantageous for later mathematical reasoning: code training endows the model with strong token‑level handling of symbols, operators, and programmatic structures that frequently appear in mathematical expressions and algorithmic reasoning.  Consequently, the parameters of DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 serve as the initial weights \(\theta_{0}\) for the continual pre‑training phase.

---

### Data Mixture

The continual pre‑training data is a **heterogeneous mixture** designed to expose the model to a broad spectrum of mathematical content while preserving its coding abilities and general language competence.  The final token distribution, summed over the entire 500 B‑token training run, is:

| Data source | Approximate token share |
|-------------|------------------------|
| DeepSeekMath Corpus (web‑mined mathematical pages) | **56 %** |
| AlgebraicStack (mathematical code repositories) | **4 %** |
| arXiv papers (scientific articles) | **10 %** |
| Github code (general programming) | **20 %** |
| Natural‑language Common Crawl (English + Chinese) | **10 %** |

#### 1. DeepSeekMath Corpus  

The core of the mixture is the **DeepSeekMath Corpus**, a 120 B‑token collection of web pages filtered for mathematical relevance using a fastText classifier.  This corpus contains both English and Chinese mathematical text, which explains the multilingual improvements reported later in the paper.

#### 2. AlgebraicStack  

AlgebraicStack contributes high‑quality mathematical source code (e.g., symbolic‑computation scripts).  Although it represents only a small fraction of the total tokens, it supplies dense examples of formal mathematical notation in a programming context, which synergizes with the code‑pre‑training background.

#### 3. arXiv  

A modest 10 % of tokens are drawn from arXiv papers.  The authors note that arXiv data did not produce a noticeable gain on the downstream mathematics benchmarks, but it is retained to preserve exposure to formal scientific writing.

#### 4. Github Code  

The 20 % code share continues the model’s familiarity with programming constructs, libraries, and API usage.  This portion is essential to prevent catastrophic forgetting of coding abilities after the heavy math‑focused phase.

#### 5. Natural‑Language Common Crawl  

Finally, 10 % of the tokens are drawn from multilingual natural‑language web text (English and Chinese).  This component stabilizes the model’s general language understanding and ensures that the model does not become overly specialized to mathematical domains.

### Training Schedule, Optimizer Settings, and Learning‑Rate Schedule

The continual pre‑training follows the same **training recipe** that was used for the smaller 1.3 B‑parameter experiments (Section 2.3.1 of the paper) with a few adjustments to accommodate the larger model and the longer token budget.

| Hyper‑parameter | Value |
|-----------------|-------|
| Optimizer | AdamW |
| \(\beta_{1}\) | 0.9 |
| \(\beta_{2}\) | 0.95 |
| Weight decay | 0.1 |
| Maximum learning rate | \(4.2 \times 10^{-4}\) |
| Warm‑up steps | 2 000 |
| Learning‑rate decay | Multi‑step: after 80 % of total steps the LR is reduced to 31.6 % of the peak, and after 90 % it is reduced further to 10 % of the peak |
| Context length | 4 K tokens |
| Batch size (tokens per update) | 10 M tokens |

The **AdamW** optimizer is chosen for its robustness to large‑scale language‑model training.  The high \(\beta_{2}\) value (0.95) stabilizes second‑moment estimates, which is helpful when the data distribution shifts between very different sources (e.g., code versus mathematical prose).  Weight decay of 0.1 combats parameter drift that could otherwise degrade the model’s coding competence.

The **learning‑rate schedule** is a classic "warm‑up then cosine‑like decay" pattern, but the paper describes it in terms of discrete milestones: the peak LR is hit after the first 2 000 steps; then the LR is gradually decreased, first to roughly one‑third of the peak at 80 % of the total token count, and finally to one‑tenth at 90 %.  This schedule empirically balances rapid early learning (when the model is still adapting to the new math‑heavy data) with later fine‑tuning stability.

The **batch size** of 10 M tokens per gradient step ensures that each update aggregates gradients over a substantial amount of text, reducing variance and allowing the optimizer to make steady progress across the diverse data mixture.

### Scaling to 500 B Tokens: Hardware and Memory Considerations

Training a 7 B‑parameter model for **500 billion tokens** demands careful engineering of compute resources.  The DeepSeekMath team used the **HAI‑LLM** training framework, which is explicitly described as “efficient and lightweight” and is capable of scaling to large token counts without excessive memory overhead.

Key engineering points include:

1. **Model Parallelism** – The 7 B parameters are sharded across multiple GPUs, allowing each device to store only a fraction of the weights.  This sharding, combined with activation checkpointing, reduces per‑GPU memory consumption.

2. **Gradient Accumulation** – Although the logical batch size is 10 M tokens, the physical batch that fits on a single GPU may be far smaller (e.g., 1 M tokens).  Gradients are accumulated over several micro‑steps before the optimizer step, preserving the effective batch size while staying within memory limits.

3. **Mixed‑Precision Training** – All matrix multiplications are performed in 16‑bit floating point (FP16) with a dynamic loss‑scale to maintain numerical stability.  This halves the memory footprint of activations and weight updates compared with full‑precision (FP32) training.

4. **Efficient Data Pipelines** – The massive 120 B‑token DeepSeekMath Corpus is stored in a sharded, compressed format (e.g., Apache Arrow or TFRecord).  Data loading is parallelized across multiple workers, ensuring that the GPUs are never idle waiting for I/O.

5. **Checkpointing Strategy** – The model is saved periodically (e.g., every 10 B tokens) for both safety and for later analysis.  Because the training length is 500 B tokens, checkpoint frequency is tuned to balance storage cost and the ability to roll back if a training instability is detected.

6. **Memory‑Efficient RL Baseline (GRPO)** – Although not part of the base‑model pre‑training, the authors later introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) which avoids a separate value‑function network.  The same philosophy—reducing auxiliary model memory—guided the pre‑training infrastructure, allowing the full 7 B model to be trained on commodity clusters equipped with modern high‑bandwidth GPUs (e.g., A100 40 GB or H100 80 GB).

Overall, the **500 B‑token** schedule corresponds to roughly **35 epochs** over the 120 B‑token DeepSeekMath Corpus when considering the entire mixed dataset.  The training duration reported in the paper (not given in exact wall‑clock time) was sufficient to achieve convergence on the downstream mathematics benchmarks, as evidenced by the strong performance of DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B on GSM8K, MATH, and multilingual test sets.

### Summary

DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B is the product of a **continual pre‑training** pipeline that:

* Starts from a **code‑focused** checkpoint (DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5) to give the model an initial grasp of symbolic and algorithmic patterns.
* Exposes the model to a **balanced token mixture** dominated by a high‑quality, web‑mined mathematical corpus, while preserving coding ability (AlgebraicStack, Github code) and general language competence (Common Crawl NL, arXiv).
* Uses a **stable AdamW optimizer** with a carefully staged learning‑rate schedule, a large token‑wise batch (10 M), and a context window of 4 K tokens.
* Scales to **500 B tokens** through a combination of model sharding, mixed‑precision arithmetic, gradient accumulation, and an efficient data‑loading pipeline provided by the HAI‑LLM framework.

The resulting model, DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B, demonstrates that a **7 B‑parameter** architecture can match or surpass much larger closed‑source models on a suite of mathematical reasoning benchmarks, confirming that data quality and training recipe are at least as important as raw model size for this domain.
