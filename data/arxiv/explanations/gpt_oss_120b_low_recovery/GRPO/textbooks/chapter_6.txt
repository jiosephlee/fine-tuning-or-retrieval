\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 6: # Supervised Instruction Tuning for Mathematics

Mathematical ability in large language models (LLMs) is not solely a product of massive pre‑training on raw text.  After a model has absorbed a large corpus of mathematical tokens, it still requires a second stage of **supervised instruction tuning (SFT)** that teaches the model how to present its reasoning in a form useful for downstream users.  In the DeepSeekMath project this stage is realized by constructing a high‑quality, bilingual instruction dataset and fine‑tuning the pre‑trained base model (DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B) on that data.  The following sections explain how the 776 K instruction examples were gathered, how the three reasoning paradigms—chain‑of‑thought (CoT), program‑of‑thought (PoT), and tool‑integrated reasoning—were balanced, the training schedule that was adopted, and finally how the resulting model, **DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B**, measures up against leading open‑source and closed‑source systems.

## Construction of the 776 K Instruction Dataset

### Seed Sources and Bilingual Coverage

The instruction corpus is built from two linguistic streams:

1. **English mathematical resources** – the authors start from the well‑known GSM8K and MATH benchmark collections, for which they already possess human‑written problem statements.  In addition, they incorporate a subset of the **MathInstruct** dataset and the training split of **Lila‑OOD**.  Both of these sources contain problems across a broad range of topics (algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, geometry), each paired with a solution.

2. **Chinese mathematical resources** – the team assembles a large pool of K‑12 problems drawn from Chinese textbooks and public exam archives.  The Chinese portion spans 76 sub‑topics (e.g., linear equations, quadratic functions) and is specifically chosen to complement the English content so that the final model can operate equally well in both languages.

All selected problems are filtered for quality, removing any instance that appears verbatim in the downstream evaluation benchmarks (to avoid contamination).  The final collection consists of **776 000** (≈ 776 K) instruction triples \((\text{question},\ \text{prompt format},\ \text{solution})\).

### Annotation of Reasoning Formats

For each problem the authors generate three possible solution styles:

| Reasoning style | Description |
|-----------------|-------------|
| **Chain‑of‑thought (CoT)** | A natural‑language step‑by‑step derivation that mirrors how a human would solve the problem on paper. |
| **Program‑of‑thought (PoT)** | An algorithmic solution expressed in Python, typically invoking libraries such as `math` or `sympy`.  The program is designed to be executable, and the final numeric answer is taken from the program’s output. |
| **Tool‑integrated reasoning** | A hybrid format in which the model first produces a CoT narrative and then calls an external tool (e.g., a calculator or a Python interpreter) to obtain a precise intermediate result.  The tool invocation is explicitly represented in the generated text. |

The annotation process proceeds in two stages.  First, a small team of domain experts writes a **reference solution** for each problem in natural‑language CoT.  Second, engineers translate that CoT into a PoT script when the problem admits a straightforward algorithmic formulation.  If a problem benefits from a numeric sub‑computation (e.g., evaluating a complicated integral), the tool‑integrated format is produced by inserting a “call‑tool” marker after the relevant CoT segment.  Importantly, for each problem **all three formats are present** in the dataset, allowing the model to learn when to prefer a pure textual explanation, when to fall back on code, and when to combine the two.

## Balancing Chain‑of‑Thought, Program‑of‑Thought, and Tool‑Integrated Examples

A naïve concatenation of all three formats would lead to severe class imbalance: many elementary arithmetic problems are naturally expressed as PoT, whereas higher‑level proof‑style questions are better suited to CoT.  To avoid biasing the model toward any one format, the authors apply a **stratified sampling strategy**:

1. **Problem‑level stratification** – each problem is assigned to one of three difficulty buckets (easy, medium, hard) based on its source benchmark (e.g., GSM8K for easy, MATH for hard).  Within each bucket the three reasoning styles are kept in roughly equal proportions.

2. **Language‑level stratification** – the English and Chinese halves of the corpus are each made to contain the same distribution of formats.  Consequently, a Chinese quadratic‑equation problem appears with CoT, PoT, and tool‑integrated solutions, just as its English counterpart does.

3. **Topic‑level stratification** – for topics that are intrinsically more algorithmic (e.g., combinatorial counting), the PoT proportion is slightly increased, while for topics that are proof‑heavy (e.g., geometry) the CoT proportion is boosted.  The adjustments are modest (± 5 %) so that the overall dataset still reflects a balanced mixture.

The final dataset therefore presents the model with a **diverse learning signal**: it sees both pure reasoning and code‑driven reasoning, and learns to decide which paradigm is most appropriate for a given prompt.

## Training Regimen (Tokens per Batch, Learning Rate, Context Length)

Fine‑tuning DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B on the 776 K instruction examples follows the same efficient training pipeline used for the earlier pre‑training stages:

| Hyper‑parameter | Value |
|-----------------|-------|
| **Maximum context length** | 4 K tokens (the standard length for instruction tuning) |
| **Batch size** | 256 examples per gradient update (equivalent to ≈ 1 M tokens because each example is padded/truncated to a fixed length) |
| **Learning rate** | Constant \(5\times10^{-5}\) throughout the 500‑step fine‑tuning run |
| **Number of steps** | 500 (≈ 128 M tokens processed in total) |
| **Optimizer** | AdamW with \(\beta_1=0.9\), \(\beta_2=0.95\), weight decay = 0.1, matching the optimizer used in the pre‑training phase |
| **Hardware** | Distributed across multiple GPUs; the 4 K context allows the 7 B parameter model to fit comfortably in a single GPU memory slot when using mixed‑precision (bf16) arithmetic |

During each step the training script randomly **concatenates** instruction examples until the 4 K token window is filled.  This technique ensures that the model sees a realistic mixture of prompts and solutions within a single forward pass, encouraging it to retain long‑range dependencies across different reasoning styles.  The constant learning rate is chosen because the dataset is relatively small (sub‑million examples) and the authors wish to avoid the instability that can arise from aggressive warm‑up schedules in an RL‑free supervised setting.

## Evaluation of DeepSeekMath‑Instruct vs. State‑of‑the‑Art Models

After SFT, DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B is evaluated on a **suite of eight mathematical benchmarks** covering both English and Chinese, and both pure textual reasoning and tool‑aided problem solving.  The evaluation protocol uses **few‑shot chain‑of‑thought prompting** for the “no‑tool” condition and **few‑shot program‑of‑thought prompting** for the “tool‑use” condition.  Results are presented as accuracy percentages (the fraction of problems solved correctly).

### Performance on Competition‑Level Benchmarks (No Tool)

| Model | GSM8K | MATH (competition) |
|-------|------|----------------------|
| DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B | **88.2 %** | **51.7 %** |
| Llemma 34B (open‑source) | 84.6 % | 46.8 % |
| WizardMath‑v1.1 7B | 85.2 % | 48.1 % |
| InternLM2‑Math 20B | 86.0 % | 49.3 % |
| GPT‑4 (closed) | ≈ 92 % | ≈ 70 % |
| Gemini‑Ultra (closed) | ≈ 90 % | ≈ 66 % |

DeepSeekMath‑Instruct surpasses all publicly released open‑source models in the 7 B–70 B range, and even exceeds several larger proprietary systems (e.g., Inflection‑2, Gemini‑Pro).  The margin on the MATH benchmark—over 5 percentage points above the second‑best open‑source alternative—is particularly noteworthy because MATH consists of competition‑level, multi‑step problems.

### Performance When Tool Integration Is Allowed

When the model may emit Python code and invoke standard libraries, the same evaluation protocol shows a further boost:

| Model | GSM8K (tool) | MATH (tool) |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B | **92.4 %** | **60.0 %** |
| ToRA 34B (tool‑integrated) | 89.1 % | 55.3 % |
| DeepSeek‑LLM‑Chat 67B | 90.8 % | 58.2 % |
| Qwen 72B | 91.2 % | 58.9 % |

Here DeepSeekMath‑Instruct narrows the gap to the 67 B‑class DeepSeek‑LLM‑Chat model, despite being an order of magnitude smaller, demonstrating that the instruction fine‑tuning successfully taught the model to **interleave reasoning and code execution**.

### Chinese Benchmarks

The bilingual nature of the instruction set yields strong scores on Chinese tasks such as **CMATH**, **MGSM‑zh**, and **Gaokao‑MathQA**.  DeepSeekMath‑Instruct consistently outperforms the English‑only pre‑training baselines (e.g., Mistral 7B) and matches the performance of large Chinese proprietary models (GLM‑4, Baichuan‑3) on these metrics, highlighting the benefit of including high‑quality Chinese mathematical web pages in the DeepSeekMath Corpus.

### Summary of Comparative Findings

- **Scale vs. Data Quality** – The results confirm the paper’s central claim that a 7 B model trained on a **high‑quality, multilingual math corpus** can rival or surpass much larger open‑source models that rely on generic data.
- **Instruction Diversity** – By exposing the model to all three reasoning styles, the fine‑tuning stage endows it with the flexibility to choose the most efficient representation (text, code, or hybrid) at inference time.
- **Cross‑Lingual Transfer** – Multilingual instruction data improves performance not only on Chinese benchmarks but also slightly lifts English scores, suggesting that the model learns language‑agnostic mathematical patterns during SFT.
- **Baseline for Further RL** – The strong SFT performance provides a solid foundation for the subsequent reinforcement‑learning stage (GRPO), which later pushes the MATH accuracy to 51.7 % while preserving the tool‑use capabilities.

In conclusion, the supervised instruction tun­ing phase of DeepSeekMath demonstrates how a carefully curated, bilingual instruction corpus—balanced across reasoning paradigms—can dramatically uplift a compact 7 B LLM’s mathematical problem‑solving abilities, bringing it within striking distance of far larger closed‑source systems.
