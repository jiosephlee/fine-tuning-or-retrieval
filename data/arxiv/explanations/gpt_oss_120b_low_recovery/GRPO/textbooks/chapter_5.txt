\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 5: # Assessing Mathematical Capabilities of the Base Model  

In this chapter we examine the performance of **DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B**, the foundational model that underlies the full DeepSeekMath system.  DeepSeekMath‑Base is obtained by continuing the pre‑training of the code‑oriented model *DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B* on a massive 120 billion‑token mathematical corpus (the DeepSeekMath Corpus) together with additional code, natural‑language, and multilingual data.  The model is then evaluated on a diverse suite of benchmarks that probe its ability to reason step‑by‑step, to invoke external computation through Python, to generate formal proofs, and to perform general language understanding, reasoning, and programming tasks.  By analysing these results we can infer how large‑scale math‑focused pre‑training influences both specialized mathematical abilities and broader competencies.

---

## Few‑Shot Chain‑of‑Thought Evaluation  

### Evaluation protocol  

For all quantitative‑reasoning benchmarks we use the **few‑shot Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT)** prompting format introduced by Wei et al.  The model receives a small number (typically 4–8) of exemplars that contain both the problem statement and a detailed, step‑by‑step solution.  The prompt then appends a new test question, and the model is expected to continue the reasoning chain and produce the final answer.  Accuracy is measured by exact match of the final numeric answer after stripping formatting.

### Benchmarks  

| Benchmark | Language | Domain | Difficulty | Size |
|-----------|----------|--------|------------|------|
| **GSM8K** | English | Grade‑school arithmetic, algebra, counting | Easy‑moderate | 8 k test items |
| **MATH** | English | Competition‑level problems (AMC/AIME/IMO style) | Hard | 21 k test items |
| **CMATH** | Chinese | Similar to MATH, translated | Hard | 1 k test items |
| **MMLU‑STEM** | English | Multiple‑choice STEM questions from MMLU | Moderate‑hard | 1 k items |
| **Gaokao‑MathQA** | Chinese | Chinese high‑school exam multiple‑choice | Moderate | 1 k items |

All eight benchmarks (four English, four Chinese) are evaluated under the same few‑shot CoT regime.

### Results and interpretation  

Across the board, **DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B** outperforms every open‑source base model examined in the paper, including the strong **Mistral 7B** and the math‑trained **Llemma 34B**.  The most striking gaps appear on the **competition‑level MATH** benchmark: DeepSeekMath‑Base achieves an accuracy of **≈ 46 %**, surpassing the best publicly available 7‑parameter baseline by more than **10 percentage points** and even exceeding the closed‑source **Minerva 540B** (a model 77× larger) on this metric.  Similar gains are observed on GSM8K, where DeepSeekMath‑Base reaches **64.2 %** accuracy, and on the Chinese CMATH set, where multilingual pre‑training yields a clear advantage over English‑only corpora.

These outcomes demonstrate that **large‑scale, high‑quality math web data** can compensate for model size: a 7 B parameter model, when exposed to 120 B math tokens, matches or beats a 540 B model that was trained on a far smaller proportion of mathematically relevant text.  Moreover, the multilingual nature of the DeepSeekMath Corpus directly benefits performance on Chinese benchmarks, confirming that the corpus contains substantial non‑English mathematical content.

### Why chain‑of‑thought works here  

The CoT paradigm forces the model to generate an explicit reasoning trace rather than jumping directly to the answer.  The pre‑training on web pages that often contain **explanatory text, derivations, and step‑by‑step solutions** appears to have endowed the model with a latent “procedure‑generation” capability.  During fine‑tuning, the few‑shot exemplars activate this latent knowledge, allowing the model to decompose a novel problem into sub‑steps that mirror patterns seen during pre‑training.  The empirical superiority of DeepSeekMath‑Base therefore reflects both the **quantity** of math‑related tokens and the **quality** of the instructional style present in the source web pages.

## Program‑of‑Thought (PoT) Evaluation with Python Tool Use  

### Prompting setup  

Program‑of‑Thought (PoT) combines natural‑language reasoning with **explicit code generation**.  The model is prompted to solve a problem by writing a short Python program that may import standard libraries such as `math` or `sympy`.  After the model emits the program, an external Python interpreter executes it, and the returned numerical result is compared to the ground‑truth answer.  This evaluation isolates the model’s ability to **translate a mathematical problem into a correct algorithm** and to understand the semantics of the libraries used.

### Benchmarks  

The same quantitative benchmarks—**GSM8K** and **MATH**—are used, but the evaluation metric is the proportion of problems for which the executed program yields the correct answer.

### Performance  

DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B attains **higher accuracies** than the prior state‑of‑the‑art open‑source model **Llemma 34B** on both datasets.  The exact numbers (as reported in the paper) show improvements of several points on GSM8K and a noticeable lift on MATH, where code‑aided reasoning is particularly valuable because many competition problems require symbolic manipulation, case analysis, or iteration that are cumbersome to express purely in natural language.

### Analysis  

The **code‑first pre‑training** (starting from DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5) supplies the model with a strong foundation in programming syntax, library APIs, and the mapping between algorithmic constructs and textual description.  When this foundation is combined with the massive math‑text exposure, the model learns to **bridge the two modalities**: it can recognize that a sub‑task (e.g., solving a quadratic equation) is more reliably handled by calling `sympy.solve`, and it can correctly format inputs and parse outputs.  This synergy explains why DeepSeekMath‑Base exceeds purely language‑only baselines despite having the same parameter count.

## Informal‑to‑Formal Proof Generation on miniF2F / Isabelle  

### Task definition  

The **informal‑to‑formal** task asks the model to produce a **formal proof** in the Isabelle proof assistant given an informal problem statement, a formal statement of the theorem, and an informal natural‑language proof sketch.  The model must output a proof script that, when fed to Isabelle’s automated prover **Sledgehammer**, can be completed into a fully verified proof.  This task evaluates *auto‑formalization* capabilities rather than raw problem‑solving.

### Dataset  

The evaluation uses **miniF2F**, a benchmark of Olympiad‑level problems drawn from the **mini‑Formal‑2‑Formal (miniF2F)** suite, which pairs informal mathematics with Isabelle formalizations.

### Procedure  

- **Few‑shot prompting**: The model receives a handful of examples of informal → formal proof conversions.
- **Proof sketch generation**: The model first produces a high‑level sketch (a sequence of lemmas or proof steps).
- **Sledgehammer assistance**: The sketch is fed to Isabelle’s Sledgehammer, which attempts to fill in missing details automatically.
- **Success criteria**: A problem is counted as solved if the resulting Isabelle script verifies without error.

### Results  

DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B demonstrates **strong performance** on this task, outperforming the same model without math pre‑training and achieving competitive scores relative to larger specialist models.  Although the paper does not give an exact percentage, the narrative emphasizes that DeepSeekMath‑Base “demonstrates strong performance in proof auto‑formalization,” indicating that the model’s exposure to mathematically rigorous web text (definitions, theorem statements, proof sketches) has **transferred** to the formal proof domain.

### Why the model can do this  

1. **Exposure to formal‑style language**: A portion of the DeepSeekMath Corpus includes **LaTeX‑styled** mathematics, theorem statements, and proof outlines, which resemble the syntax used in proof assistants.
2. **Code‑training synergy**: Since the base model already knows programming constructs, it can more readily generate Isabelle commands.
3. **Chain‑of‑thought pre‑training**: Repeated patterns of “state hypothesis → apply lemma → conclude” seen in web pages are mirrored in the model’s generation of proof sketches, making the downstream auto‑formalization more natural.

## General Language, Reasoning, and Coding Benchmarks  

### Benchmarks considered  

| Category | Benchmark | Description |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Natural‑Language Understanding** | **MMLU** (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) | 57 multiple‑choice tasks covering humanities, STEM, and social sciences. |
| **Reasoning** | **BBH** (BIG‑Bench Hard) | 23 challenging tasks requiring multi‑step logical reasoning (e.g., logical deduction, planning). |
| **Coding** | **HumanEval** | Generation of Python functions that must pass unit tests. |
| **Coding** | **MBPP** (Multiple‑Bug‑Program Problems) | Short Python programs with tests, emphasizing problem‑solving via code. |

All benchmarks evaluate the model **zero‑shot** (no exemplars) unless otherwise noted, and the scores are *accuracy* (percentage of correct answers or passing tests).

### Observed impact of math pre‑training  

- **MMLU**: DeepSeekMath‑Base’s accuracy improves **substantially** over its predecessor *DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5*.  The paper reports a “significant enhancement” in MMLU scores, suggesting that the math‑focused corpus also enriches the model’s general knowledge and reasoning patterns, perhaps because many MMLU questions involve quantitative reasoning or logical deduction that benefits from the same chain‑of‑thought abilities.
  
- **BBH**: Similar gains are reported.  BBH tasks often require **stepwise inference** (e.g., “solve the puzzle by deducing the hidden rule”).  The large‑scale math data, which is replete with stepwise derivations, appears to provide transferable skills for these reasoning‑heavy tasks.

- **HumanEval & MBPP**: Despite the heavy emphasis on mathematics, the **coding** performance does **not degrade**.  DeepSeekMath‑Base maintains the strong code generation ability of its coder‑pre‑training foundation, achieving scores comparable to the original DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5.  This indicates that the additional math tokens **do not cause catastrophic forgetting** of coding knowledge; rather, the model retains its ability to emit syntactically correct code while gaining new mathematical insight.

### Synthesis  

The evidence demonstrates a **dual benefit** of math pre‑training: it **boosts mathematical reasoning** (both natural‑language and program‑aided) while simultaneously **preserving** or even **enhancing** broader language understanding and programming capabilities.  This counters the common concern that domain‑specific continued training would impair general abilities.

## Impact of Math Pre‑Training on Non‑Math Abilities  

### Why non‑math abilities improve  

1. **Shared reasoning structures**: Many tasks in MMLU, BBH, and coding require **logical sequencing**, variable binding, and result verification—structures that are also prevalent in mathematical derivations.
2. **Multilingual exposure**: The DeepSeekMath Corpus contains a substantial amount of **Chinese mathematical text**, which inadvertently improves the model’s Chinese language modeling, reflected in better scores on Chinese benchmarks (e.g., CMATH, Gaokao‑MathCloze).
3. **Regularization effect**: Continual pre‑training on a large, diverse corpus (math + code + natural language) acts as a regularizer, preventing over‑fitting to any single domain and thus yielding a more robust overall representation.

### Quantitative snapshot  

- **MMLU‑STEM**: DeepSeekMath‑Base exceeds other 7 B open‑source models by several points, narrowing the gap to much larger models.
- **BBH**: Gains are observed across most tasks, especially those that involve **numeric manipulation** or **algorithmic reasoning**.
- **HumanEval / MBPP**: Scores remain on par with the coder‑only baseline, confirming that the added math tokens do not sacrifice code generation fidelity.

### Limitations  

While math pre‑training is beneficial, the model still lags behind closed‑source giants (GPT‑4, Gemini‑Ultra) on **geometry‑heavy** reasoning and on **few‑shot adaptability**—areas where those systems leverage massive, diverse instruction data and possibly richer tool integration.  The authors attribute these gaps partly to **data selection bias** (e.g., insufficient triangulation examples) and to **model scale**, noting that a 7 B model cannot fully absorb all nuances of higher‑dimensional geometry or advanced theorem proving.

## Concluding Remarks on the Base Model Evaluation  

The comprehensive suite of experiments paints a clear picture:

1. **Step‑by‑step reasoning** (few‑shot CoT) improves dramatically thanks to a 120 B token math corpus that captures the style of human mathematical exposition.
2. **Program‑of‑thought** abilities are amplified by the combination of code‑first pre‑training and math‑focused data, enabling the model to generate correct Python scripts for non‑trivial quantitative problems.
3. **Formal proof auto‑formalization** becomes feasible, showing that informal web text can be a bridge to formal verification environments.
4. **General NLP and coding benchmarks** benefit from the same training regime, demonstrating that mathematical pre‑training is not a zero‑sum game but rather a **cross‑domain catalyst** for reasoning skills.
5. The **multilingual** component of the corpus yields superior performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks, highlighting the importance of diverse language sources in constructing domain‑specific corpora.

Overall, the results validate the authors’ central thesis: **high‑quality, large‑scale web‑derived mathematical data, when combined with a code‑oriented pre‑training foundation, can endow a modest‑size 7 B model with mathematical reasoning capabilities that rival far larger closed‑source systems.**  The subsequent sections of the paper build upon this strong base by applying instruction tuning and the novel **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** reinforcement‑learning algorithm, further pushing the limits of open‑source mathematical reasoning.
