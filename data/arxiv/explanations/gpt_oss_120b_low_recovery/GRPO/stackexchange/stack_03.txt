\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### What is the exact formulation of the group‑relative advantage \(\hat{A}_{i,t}\) in GRPO?
Question:
Equation (4) defines the GRPO objective, and Section 4.2 describes outcome‑ and process‑supervision. However, the paper only states that \(\hat{A}_{i,t}\) is "calculated based on relative rewards of the outputs inside each group". I would like to see the precise mathematical expression:
- For outcome supervision, is \(\hat{A}_{i,t}=\frac{r_i-\mu_{\mathbf r}}{\sigma_{\mathbf r}}\) for all \(t\) as implied?
- For process supervision, is \(\hat{A}_{i,t}=\sum_{j:\,index(j)\ge t}\widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}\)?
A clear definition would help me implement GRPO correctly.
Answer:
In GRPO the **advantage** of a token is not obtained from a learned value‑function (as in PPO) but from the *relative* rewards of the samples that belong to the same question‑group.  
For a given question \(q\) we draw a group of \(G\) continuations

\[
\{o_{1},o_{2},\dots ,o_{G}\}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\,\cdot\,|q),
\]

and we compute a reward for each sampled output.  
Two kinds of reward‑signals are considered in the paper:

---

### 1. Outcome‑supervision  

Only the final answer of an output is scored.  
Let the scalar reward for the \(i\)-th output be \(r_{i}\).  
All rewards of the group are normalised

\[
\widetilde{r}_{i}= \frac{r_{i}-\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf r)}{\operatorname{std}(\mathbf r)},
\qquad 
\mathbf r=\{r_{1},\dots ,r_{G}\}.
\]

The **group‑relative advantage** for every token \(t\) of output \(o_{i}\) is then defined as this normalised outcome reward, i.e.

\[
\boxed{\hat A_{i,t}= \widetilde{r}_{i}
      =\frac{r_{i}-\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf r)}
            {\operatorname{std}(\mathbf r)}},
\qquad t=1,\dots ,|o_{i}|.
\]

Thus the same \(\widetilde{r}_{i}\) is used for all tokens in the sequence, which matches the formulation you guessed.

---

### 2. Process‑supervision  

Here the reward model gives a score after each reasoning step (or after any predefined token index).  
Suppose output \(o_{i}\) contains \(K_{i}\) reasoning steps whose end‑token positions are
\(\operatorname{index}(1),\dots ,\operatorname{index}(K_{i})\).  
The step‑wise rewards are denoted
\(r_{i}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}\) and are normalised in the same way as above:

\[
\widetilde{r}_{i}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}=
\frac{r_{i}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}-\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf R)}
     {\operatorname{std}(\mathbf R)},
\qquad
\mathbf R = \bigl\{\{r_{1}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}\}_{j},
                \dots ,
                \{r_{G}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}\}_{j}\bigr\}.
\]

The advantage of a token \(t\) is the sum of the normalised step rewards that occur **at or after** that token:

\[
\boxed{\hat A_{i,t}= \sum_{\operatorname{index}(j)\ge t}
        \widetilde{r}_{i}^{\operatorname{index}(j)}} .
\]

In other words, a token receives credit from every future step’s reward, which makes the signal finer‑grained than the outcome case.

---

### Why these definitions matter  

Both definitions realise the same principle: the baseline is *the average performance of the group* rather than an external critic.  
Subtracting the group mean removes the global difficulty of the question, while dividing by the group standard deviation rescales the signal so that the advantage has a roughly unit variance. This “group‑relative” advantage is exactly what the authors refer to in §4.2 and is what appears inside the min‑clip term of the GRPO objective (Eq. (4)).  

Consequently, for **outcome supervision** the advantage is simply the normalised final reward, and for **process supervision** it is the cumulative sum of normalised step‑wise rewards as you wrote. Using these formulas you can implement GRPO directly from the sampled outputs and the reward model.
