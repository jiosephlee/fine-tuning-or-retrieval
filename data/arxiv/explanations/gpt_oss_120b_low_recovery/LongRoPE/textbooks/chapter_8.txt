\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 8: **Experimental Evaluation**

# Setup  

To assess the effectiveness of LongRoPE we conduct a broad suite of experiments on two widely used decoder‑only language models that employ Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE): LLaMA2‑7B \cite{llama2} and Mistral‑7B \cite{mistral}.  All experiments follow the same evaluation protocol so that differences can be attributed to the positional‑interpolation method rather than to incidental training choices.  

**Datasets.**  
Three families of datasets are used.  

* *Proof‑pile* \cite{pg19} and *PG‑19* \cite{pg19} are long‑document corpora (average length ≈ 100 k tokens) that are standard for measuring language‑model perplexity on extended contexts.  For the Proof‑pile tests we follow the sampling procedure of YaRN \cite{yarn} and draw ten documents each longer than 128 k tokens; for PG‑19 we evaluate on the entire test split of one hundred documents.  

* *Books3* \cite{pile} provides texts that are several million tokens long.  For the >2 M‑token evaluation we randomly select twenty books whose token count exceeds 2 048 k and evaluate with a sliding window of 256 k tokens.  

* *Passkey retrieval* \cite{passkey} is a synthetic generation task.  Each input consists of a long “distractor” document (length varies from 4 k to 2 048 k) that contains a randomly placed five‑digit number.  The prompt asks the model to output the hidden passkey; success is measured by exact string match.  Ten repetitions are performed per length, with the passkey uniformly sampled across the document.  

**Fine‑tuning hyper‑parameters.**  
Both base models are first fine‑tuned on a modest number of steps using the LongRoPE‑derived RoPE rescaling factors.  For LLaMA2 we use a learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$ with linear decay, a global batch size of 32, and a total of $1\,000$ steps split as $400$ steps at a 128 k context and $600$ steps at a 256 k context.  The 128 k stage runs on eight A100 GPUs, while the 256 k stage runs on sixteen A100 GPUs.  For Mistral we employ a constant learning rate of $1\times10^{-6}$, batch size 64, and the same $1\,000$‑step schedule; training is performed on four A100 GPUs.  All fine‑tuning data are drawn from publicly available long‑text corpora (RedPajama for LLaMA2 and the Together Computer Long‑Data Collections for Mistral) and are chunked to match the target context length.  

**Search configuration.**  
When extending to any target length $L'$ ≤ 256 k we invoke the evolutionary search described in Section \ref{sec:method}.  The population size $P$ is set to 64, with $N_1=N_2=16$ mutations and cross‑overs per generation, mutation probability $p=0.3$, and $\mathcal{T}=40$ generations.  The top‑32 individuals are retained as parents each generation.  Perplexity is computed on five randomly selected PG‑19 validation samples that are at least $L'$ tokens long.  For lengths exceeding 512 k the population and mutation sizes are halved, and perplexity is measured on three random Books3 validation samples.  

**Evaluation metrics.**  
* Perplexity ($\exp(\text{cross‑entropy})$) is the primary language‑modeling metric.  It is computed on a sliding window of 256 tokens and averaged over all windows that fully fit inside the evaluated document.  
* Passkey retrieval accuracy is simply the proportion of runs in which the model outputs the exact five‑digit passkey.  
* For the short‑context benchmark suite we report standard zero‑shot or few‑shot accuracy on ARC‑Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, and TruthfulQA following the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard protocol.  These scores indicate how much the very long‑context fine‑tuning harms performance on tasks that fit within the original 4 k window.  

# Perplexity on Proof‑pile and PG‑19 (up to 256 k)  

Tables \ref{tbl:proof-pile} and \ref{tbl:pg19} (reproduced from the paper) present perplexity results for LLaMA2‑7B and Mistral‑7B after applying LongRoPE, as well as for four strong baselines: PI \cite{pi}, NTK \cite{ntk}, YaRN \cite{yarn}, and LongLoRA \cite{longlora}.  The columns correspond to evaluation lengths $\{4\text{k}, 8\text{k}, 16\text{k}, 32\text{k}, 64\text{k}, 128\text{k}, 256\text{k}\}$.  

Across both datasets the LongRoPE‑extended models display a **monotonic decrease** in perplexity as the evaluation window grows, confirming that the models are indeed able to leverage additional context.  For example, LLaMA2‑7B with LongRoPE attains a perplexity of $11.3$ at 4 k tokens on PG‑19, which drops to $9.8$ at 256 k tokens.  By contrast, the PI baseline exhibits a sharp increase after 32 k tokens, reflecting the well‑known “crowding” effect when all dimensions are linearly compressed.  

The advantage of LongRoPE is most pronounced in the regime where the extension ratio exceeds $8\times$ (i.e., beyond 32 k).  At 128 k and 256 k tokens LongRoPE’s perplexity is **10–20 % lower** than the best competing method (YaRN), even though LongRoPE’s fine‑tuning involved only $1\,000$ steps and no additional data beyond the 128 k/256 k chunks.  This demonstrates that the non‑uniform rescaling of RoPE dimensions discovered by the evolutionary search provides a superior initialization for fine‑tuning, preserving more of the original positional information.  

# Perplexity on Books3 (beyond 2 M tokens)  

To stress‑test the method in the truly extreme regime we evaluate on Books3 documents that exceed two million tokens.  Evaluation proceeds with a $256\text{k}$ sliding window; each window’s perplexity is reported and then averaged across the twenty books.  

The LongRoPE‑2048k models (both LLaMA2 and Mistral) successfully maintain perplexities comparable to the best 256 k baselines while processing sequences three to eight times longer than any prior work.  For LLaMA2‑7B the perplexity rises only modestly from $9.8$ at 256 k to $10.5$ at 2 048 k, indicating that the additional $1\,792$ k tokens provide useful signal rather than noise.  Mistral‑7B shows a larger increase (from $8.7$ to $9.4$) but still outperforms all non‑fine‑tuned baselines at the same lengths.  

A notable observation from Table \ref{tbl:books3} is that the **fine‑tuning length matters**: extending LLaMA2 first to 256 k (rather than 128 k) yields a slightly better 2 048 k perplexity, because the secondary non‑uniform interpolation operates on a model that has already adapted to a larger set of rescaled angles.  Conversely, Mistral benefits more from a 128 k fine‑tune, likely because its training pipeline used a 16 k training length and therefore adapts more readily to a larger secondary scaling factor.  

# Passkey Retrieval  

The passkey retrieval task directly measures the *effective* context length that a model can attend to during generation.  Figure \ref{fig:passkey} plots retrieval accuracy versus evaluation length for all methods.  

Baseline models based on PI or NTK achieve near‑perfect accuracy up to roughly 64 k tokens, after which accuracy collapses to near 0 % because the hidden passkey is no longer represented faithfully in the crowded positional space.  YaRN extends the viable range to about 128 k but still deteriorates sharply beyond that point.  

LongRoPE‑2048k LLaMA2 (fine‑tuned at 256 k) maintains **$>90\%$ accuracy** across the entire 4 k–2 048 k span, confirming that the model can retrieve information from the farthest token positions.  The Mistral‑2048k variant (fine‑tuned at 128 k) shows perfect retrieval up to 1 800 k and a modest drop to 60 % at 2 048 k, which mirrors the slight perplexity increase observed in Table \ref{tbl:books3}.  These results demonstrate that LongRoPE effectively mitigates the “information loss” problem of traditional interpolation and enables truly long‑range reasoning.  

# Zero‑shot / Few‑shot Performance on Standard Benchmarks  

A crucial sanity check is whether the extensive positional modifications degrade performance on tasks that fit comfortably within the original 4 k window.  Table \ref{tbl:commonsense} reports accuracy on four widely used benchmarks, evaluated in the zero‑shot or few‑shot settings prescribed by the Open LLM Leaderboard.  

* On *ARC‑Challenge* and *MMLU* the LongRoPE‑LLaMA2‑2048k (ft = 256 k) scores $46.2$ % and $43.7$ % respectively, within $1$–$2$ % of the untuned LLaMA2 baseline, confirming negligible loss.  
* For *HellaSwag* the drop is slightly larger (from $79.1$ % to $77.4$ %), which aligns with the known sensitivity of commonsense reasoning to subtle changes in positional encoding.  
* Notably, *TruthfulQA* sees a **$+0.5$ % gain** for LongRoPE‑Mistral‑2048k over the original Mistral, suggesting that the richer context can sometimes improve factual consistency even when the prompt is short.  

Overall, the LongRoPE models retain competitive performance on standard benchmarks while offering orders of magnitude larger context windows.  

# Comparison with State‑of‑the‑Art Baselines  

Across every evaluation dimension—perplexity on long documents, passkey retrieval accuracy, and short‑context benchmark scores—LongRoPE outperforms the strongest publicly available baselines:  

* **PI** and **NTK** suffer from severe crowding when the extension ratio exceeds $4\times$, leading to runaway perplexities and zero passkey retrieval beyond 64 k.  
* **YaRN** ameliorates crowding by grouping dimensions, yet its hand‑crafted groups are sub‑optimal for the newer LLaMA2 and Mistral architectures; the method still collapses after 128 k.  
* **LongLoRA** and other fine‑tuning‑only approaches achieve up to 128 k windows but require orders of magnitude more training steps (often > 10 k) and still cannot reach the multi‑million token regime.  

LongRoPE’s evolutionary search discovers **dimension‑wise rescale factors $\lambda_i$** that respect the monotonicity constraint (lower‑frequency dimensions receive larger $\lambda_i$) while also exempting the first $\hat n$ tokens from any interpolation.  This two‑fold non‑uniformity yields an **8× extension without any fine‑tuning** (4 k → 32 k) and, after the progressive fine‑tuning pipeline (4 k → 256 k → 2 048 k), reaches a **512× total extension** with only $1\,000$ fine‑tuning steps.  

# Summary  

The experimental suite validates the central claims of the LongRoPE paper:

1. **Effective non‑uniform interpolation** dramatically lowers perplexity on documents up to 256 k tokens, outpacing all prior interpolation schemes.  
2. **Progressive extension** (256 k fine‑tune followed by a second search) scales the context window to 2 048 k tokens while keeping fine‑tuning costs modest.  
3. **Practical downstream utility** is demonstrated by the passkey retrieval task, where LongRoPE models retain high accuracy even when the hidden token lies over two million positions away from the prompt.  
4. **Minimal regression on short‑context tasks** confirms that the learned RoPE rescaling does not sacrifice performance on standard benchmarks.  

Collectively, these results establish LongRoPE as the most scalable and computationally efficient method for extending the context window of RoPE‑based LLMs beyond the million‑token barrier.
