### In the FLOPS estimation formula for Cross Attention, what does '$r$' represent physically?
Question:
In Section 3.5 ('FLOPS Estimation'), Equation (4) explicitly defines '$r$' as 'the ratio of queries to key/values' alongside '$k$, which is defined as 'the ratio of patch-dimension to byte-dimension'. In standard multi-head attention implementations where queries, keys, and values have equal dimensions and counts after projection, one would expect $r=1$. However, in BLT's cross-attention (described in Section 2.2), fixes are mentioned about maintaining multiple heads when patch representations ($h_{\mathcal{G}}$) are larger than byte representations ($h_{\mathcal{E}}$). Is '$r$' a variable that scales based on whether we are in the encoder or decoder context specifically because of this dimension mismatch? If I set up a BLT model where $h_{\mathcal{G}} = 4096$ and $h_{\mathcal{E}} = 512$, how would I calculate '$r$' explicitly in that specific scenario? Does this factor account for the overhead of concatenating heads later?
Answer:
In the FLOPS estimation formula for Cross Attention within the Byte Latent Transformer (BLT), the variable '$r$' represents the **ratio of queries to keys/values**. Physically, '$r$' accounts for the inherent mismatch in dimensions and counts between the coarse-grained patch representations and the fine-grained byte representations during the cross-attention operation.

To understand why '$r$' exists and how to calculate it, we must look at how BLT constructs its cross-attention layers, specifically in the local encoder. The goal of this layer is to pool a sequence of fine-grained byte representations ($h_{\mathcal{E}}$) into single, coarse-grained patch representations ($h_{\mathcal{G}}$). Because a single patch can contain multiple bytes (and in the standard implementation shown in Figure 1 of the paper, typically 2 bytes per head after splitting), you cannot simply run one multi-headed attention with equal numbers of query heads and key/value heads.

The paper defines '$k$' as the ratio of patch-dimension to byte-dimension, noting that $k=2$ in their architecture because $h_{\mathcal{G}} = 2 \times h_{\mathcal{E}}$. When computing cross-attention, if you have $n_p$ patches to process, you generate $n_p$ queries. However, since each patch's representation is built from multiple underlying byte streams (via the linear projection splitting), you effectively need multiple sets of Key and Value vectors per patch to cover all those constituent byte sequences. Consequently, the number of Keys/Values generated is roughly $n_p \times k$, while the number of Queries remains $n_p$.
