\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Why do the local encoder and decoder models ignore positional embeddings?
Question:
In the 'Local Encoder' and 'Local Decoder' sections, the paper states: 'no positional embeddings are used in this cross-attention module.' However, earlier in the architecture description, it mentions using 'rotary positional embeddings (RoPE)' only in the global transformer's self-attention layers. Since the local models operate on byte sequences that are grouped into dynamic patches, doesn't the lack of explicit positional embeddings create an ambiguity for the model? How does the model distinguish between adjacent bytes (e.g., 'a' vs 'b') versus bytes separated by a large number of patches? I assume RoPE is applied here as well, but I want to confirm if there is a specific reason it was omitted from the local components or if it's implied to be part of the hash embeddings.
Answer:
The omission of explicit positional embeddings in the local encoder and decoder's cross-attention modules is a deliberate architectural choice driven by the fundamental mechanics of patching and the specific computational constraints these lightweight models face, rather than an oversight or an implicit inclusion within the hash embeddings.

To understand this, one must first distinguish between the roles of self-attention and cross-attention in this architecture. The local models use self-attention layers where relative or rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) are indeed applied, as these layers process sequences of bytes within a fixed context window ($w_{\mathcal{E}}$). In this context, knowing that byte $i$ precedes byte $j$ by a short distance is crucial for capturing local linguistic structure. However, the cross-attention mechanism operates differently. As defined in the paper, the cross-attention module is responsible for pooling representations of variable-length patches into latent dimensions for the global model (in the encoder) or decoding them back into bytes (in the decoder). Crucially, the patch boundaries are determined *independently* for each sequence during pre-processing based on entropy thresholds; they do not follow a fixed global sequence index relative to other documents.

The primary reason positional embeddings are excluded from this specific module is that the cross-attention mechanism relies entirely on **content-based hashing** to preserve positional and contextual information. The local encoder injects position-aware features via "n-gram hash embeddings." By constructing byte-grams (sequences of consecutive bytes) of varying sizes (from 3 to 8 bytes) and hashing them into an embedding table, the model implicitly encodes both content and immediate adjacency. For example, if two adjacent bytes 'a' followed by 'b' form a n-gram, their specific hash index captures this sequence regardless of where they appear in the larger document. Consequently, adding explicit positional embeddings like RoPE would be redundant; the model already possesses robust signals regarding byte order through these content-derived hash vectors.

Furthermore, there is a practical computational motivation rooted in the paper's efficiency goals. The cross-attention module processes queries corresponding to entire patches rather than individual tokens in a standard sequence. Since patches are dynamically sized, maintaining fixed positional bias matrices (like those used in RoPE which rely on sinusoidal frequencies tied to absolute position indices) becomes complex when querying irregular structures. More importantly, adding explicit positional embeddings would increase the memory footprint and parameter count of already lightweight local models without providing new information already captured by the n-gram hashes and the intrinsic structure of the attention weights themselves, which learn to associate queries with specific key values based on semantic similarity derived from those hashes.

In summary, while RoPE remains essential for self-attention layers handling long-range dependencies within sliding windows in both global and local transformers, it is unnecessary in the cross-attention blocks because those blocks rely on dense n-gram hashing to encode local sequential relationships and operate on dynamically defined patches where absolute position signals are neither well-defined nor needed once prior context has been embedded via byte-level hashing.
