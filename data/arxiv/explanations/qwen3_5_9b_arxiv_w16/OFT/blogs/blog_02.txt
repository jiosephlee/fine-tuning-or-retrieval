\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# DreamBooth on Steroids: Generating Your Dog in a Spacesuit in Seconds

Large language models have revolutionized natural language processing, but text-to-image diffusion models have turned the same magic toward graphics. Models like Stable Diffusion can generate photorealistic images from simple text prompts, yet they often struggle when we need fine-grained control. If you try to generate an image of "your specific golden retriever wearing a spacesuit," standard methods might produce a generic dog or one that looks nothing like your pet. This is the challenge of subject-driven generation. While existing techniques like DreamBooth and LoRA offer solutions, they frequently suffer from model collapse or identity drift if trained for too long. In our latest research, we introduced Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), a method that mathematically guarantees the preservation of a model's semantic capabilities while allowing it to learn new subjects efficiently.

The core problem with finetuning massive diffusion models is balancing flexibility with stability. If you update too few parameters, the model fails to adapt to your specific subject; if you update too many, it forgets how to generate general concepts, leading to collapsed outputs. Traditional approaches often rely on updating neuron weights directly with tiny learning rates or adding low-rank noise matrices. While these methods work initially, empirical results show that they cannot guarantee the preservation of the pretrained model's generative performance over extended training periods. Our experiments reveal that standard finetuning methods often fail to maintain subject identity after a certain number of epochs, with LoRA specifically struggling to preserve fine details under prolonged training compared to our approach.

To solve this, we proposed a principled approach based on the geometry of neural network components. We found that the angle between neurons—a property known as hyperspherical similarity—encodes crucial semantic information about what an image looks like. By characterizing these relationships through hyperspherical energy, a measure of pairwise neuron uniformity on a unit hypersphere, we realized that preserving this energy during finetuning is key to maintaining pretraining quality. Our method, OFT, achieves this by applying an orthogonal transformation to the layer weights rather than changing their magnitudes arbitrarily. Mathematically, this means we rotate or reflect all neurons in a layer simultaneously without altering their pairwise angles, which provably keeps the hyperspherical energy constant while allowing the model to adapt its feature space for your specific dog in a spacesuit.

This geometric constraint provides immense stability compared to conventional finetuning methods dreamed up by previous researchers in DreamBooth and LoRA families of research because it prevents the model from drifting too far into uncharted territory where generalization breaks down.
