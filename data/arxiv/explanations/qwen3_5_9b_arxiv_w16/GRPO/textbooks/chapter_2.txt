Chapter 2: # Chapter 2: Large-Scale Mathematical Corpus Construction

Large-scale data curation stands as one of the most critical yet often overlooked components in training high-performance language models. For mathematical reasoning specifically, the challenge is compounded by a unique convergence of problems: extreme data scarcity relative to the model's hunger for tokens, and severe dataset bias toward specific sources like arXiv preprints. While autoregressive language models thrive on the sheer volume of internet text, pure web scraping yields an unacceptably high noise-to-signal ratio for mathematics. Most publicly available mathematical data is concentrated in curated repositories or academic preprint servers, which are insufficient in quantity to train models at the scale required to compete with proprietary systems. This section provides a comprehensive technical deep-dive into the construction of the DeepSeekMath Corpus, detailing how a meticulously engineered pipeline extracts billions of high-quality mathematical tokens from the noisy expanse of Common Crawl, thereby overcoming these fundamental limitations through iterative refinement and rigorous validation.

### The Problem of Dataset Bias and Data Scarcity in Math Training

To understand the necessity of a customized corpus for mathematical reasoning, one must first examine the landscape of existing public data and its inherent deficiencies. The prevailing hypothesis in the field regarding mathematical pre-training has been that access to current arXiv papers constitutes a near-panacea. However, empirical analysis reveals that training exclusively on arXiv papers yields diminishing returns and fails to provide significant improvements across diverse mathematical benchmarks compared to training on general internet text or more diverse web corpora. This suggests that while arXiv contains formal and rigorous content, it lacks the breadth and quantity necessary to robustly teach a model to reason about mathematics; furthermore, relying solely on such a homogenous source introduces a bias where models may overfit to specific academic writing styles that do not translate well to solving standardized problems found in datasets like GSM8K or MATH.

Simultaneously, there is a profound scarcity when it comes to raw, high-quality mathematical web content on the broader internet. Datasets like OpenWebMath represent a significant effort but remain limited in scale—totaling approximately 13.6 billion tokens—and generalize primarily English-centric content to around 68 billion tokens depending on versioning. These figures, while substantial compared to previous efforts, fall short of the hundreds of billions of tokens typically required to push open-source models toward the performance ceiling of GPT-4 or Gemini-Ultra without scaling law assistance via massive parameter counts (e.g., Minerva's 540B parameters). There exists a critical gap between these existing datasets and the vast potential hidden within publicly available web sources indexed by Common Crawl (CC). The CC scrapes almost all publicly accessible pages on the internet, meaning there are undoubtedly vast treasuries of math emagazines examples from university math questions - math relevant mark embedded within textbooks code repositories forum discussions that remain largely untapped by standard pre-training strategies which typically discard them as low-quality noise during initial filtering stages. Addressing this scarcity requires moving beyond simple inclusion lists and adopting an active learning approach where data quality is treated as a variable to be optimized rather than static ground truth.
