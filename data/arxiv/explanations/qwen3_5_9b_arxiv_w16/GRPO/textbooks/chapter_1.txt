Chapter 1: Chapter 1: The State of Mathematical Reasoning in Open-Source LLMs

Large language models (LLMs) have fundamentally transformed the landscape of artificial intelligence, offering unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding and generation. A particularly exciting frontier within this domain is quantitative reasoning, specifically the ability of models to solve complex mathematical problems. This capability has spurred significant advancements in both general quantitative reasoning benchmarks and specialized fields like geometry reasoning. Furthermore, these models have proven instrumental in assisting human experts, such as mathematicians known as "math bards," in deriving solutions to intricate problems. However, despite these strides, a significant disparity exists between the most capable commercially available models and those accessible to the open-source research community. Cutting-edge proprietary systems like GPT-4 and Gemini-Ultra represent the state-of-the-art in mathematical performance but are not publicly available. Consequently, currently accessible open-source models notoriously trail behind in performance, leaving a substantial gap that hinders reproducibility and limits the ability of researchers to push the boundaries of automated mathematical discovery without relying on black-box technologies. The primary contribution of this research addresses this critical imbalance by introducing a domain-specific language model designed to significantly outperform existing open-source alternatives while approaching the sophisticated reasoning levels of closed-source benchmarks.

The evolution of LLMs in quantitative reasoning reveals a clear trajectory from simple pattern matching to sophisticated, step-by-step logical deduction. Initially, models struggled with multi-step arithmetic, often failing due to a lack of persistent context or an inability to maintain coherent logical chains over long derivations. This challenge has been partially mitigated by techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting, which encourages models to generate intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. Recent advances have further incorporated program-of-thought and tool-integrated reasoning, allowing models to write and execute Python code for verification or to utilize external calculators for complex computations. These methods have yielded impressive results on standard benchmarks like GSM8K, which consists primarily of grade-school word problems requiring multi-step arithmetic operations aligned with U.S. state assessments. However, true mathematical reasoning is characterized not just by arithmetic fluency but by deep structural understanding applicable across diverse domains, from algebraic manipulation to competition-level problem solving requiring advanced concepts like combinatorics and differential equations.

Despite the rapid progress of public models, a widening gap separates them from proprietary counterparts like GPT-4 and Gemini-Ultra. These closed-source models leverage vast amounts of private data and extensive compute resources that remain inaccessible to the public sphere. As a result, open-source researchers face a difficult reality where their best-performing models considerably lag behind industry leaders.
