\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### (2) How OFT guarantees HE preservation exactly

The preservation is **exact**, not approximate. The key mathematical fact is that orthogonal transformations preserve inner products and norms. When you transform all neurons in a layer by the same orthogonal matrix $R$, the normalized neurons become $\hat{w}_i = Rw_i^0 / \|Rw_i^0\| = Rw_i^0 / \|w_i^0\|$ (since $R$ preserves norms). Then the pairwise distance between any two transformed neurons satisfies:

$$\|R\hat{w}_i^0 - R\hat{w}_j^0\| = \|R(\hat{w}_i^0 - \hat{w}_j^0)\| = \|\hat{w}_i^0 - \hat{w}_j^0\|$$

because orthogonal matrices preserve norms. Since hyperspherical energy is defined as $\text{HE}(W) = \sum_{i \neq j} \|\hat{w}_i - \hat{w}_j\|^{-1}$, every term in the sum is unchanged, and therefore $\text{HE}(W) = \text{HE}(W^0)$ *exactly*.

This is precisely why OFT uses orthogonal transformation rather than a soft regularizer. As the paper notes in the introduction, a naive approach would be to add a regularizer penalizing $\|\text{HE}(W) - \text{HE}(W^0)\|$, but "there is no guarantee that the hyperspherical energy difference can be well minimized." Instead, OFT exploits the invariance property: by construction, if $W = RW^0$ with $R^\top R = I$, the HE difference is provably zero. The orthogonality of $R$ is itself guaranteed exactly through the Cayley parameterization $R = (I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}$ with $Q$ skew-symmetric, which produces matrices in the special orthogonal group by construction. So the guarantee holds at every training step, not just at convergence.
