\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# Teaching Old Dogs New Tricks: How Orthogonal Finetuning Keeps AI Models from Forgetting

## The Finetuning Dilemma

Large text-to-image diffusion models, like Stable Diffusion, are incredibly good at generating photorealistic images from text prompts. However, guiding them to perform specific downstream tasks—such as generating images of a particular subject or following a structural control signal like an edge map—requires finetuning. The challenge is that finetuning often causes the model to "forget" its pretraining knowledge. Existing methods try to prevent this by keeping the updated weights close to the original ones using a small learning rate or low-rank updates like LoRA. But simply minimizing the Euclidean distance between the old and new weights isn't enough to preserve the complex semantic relationships the model learned during pretraining.

## It's All About Angles

The authors of the paper introduce a new way to think about model knowledge: hyperspherical energy. This concept characterizes the pairwise relationships between neurons by looking at the angles between them on a unit hypersphere. The authors found that these angles are crucial for storing semantic information. They demonstrate this with a toy experiment showing that the angular information of neurons can almost perfectly recover input images, while magnitude information is largely useless. If finetuning disrupts these angles, the model's core knowledge is destroyed.

## The OFT Solution: Rotating Without Breaking

To preserve these critical angles, the authors propose Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). The core idea is to update the model's weights using orthogonal transformations. An orthogonal transformation is essentially a rotation or reflection. Think of it like adjusting the angle of a camera: you can change the viewpoint, but the fundamental structure and relationships of the objects in the scene remain intact. By applying the same orthogonal transformation to all neurons in a layer, OFT provably preserves the hyperspherical energy, ensuring the model's pretrained knowledge is maintained.

Formally, instead of directly updating the weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$, OFT learns an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ and updates the weights as $\bm{W} = \bm{R} \bm{W}^0$, subject to the orthogonality constraint $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I}$. This is a multiplicative update, unlike the additive updates used in LoRA.

## Keeping Things Stable with COFT

While OFT is effective, sometimes you want to limit how much the model changes. For this, the authors introduce Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT). COFT adds an additional constraint that limits how far the orthogonal matrix can deviate from the identity matrix: $\|\bm{R} - \bm{I}\| \leq \epsilon$. This ensures the finetuned model stays within a small neighborhood of the pretrained model, acting as an explicit brake on drastic changes. This makes finetuning even more stable and easier to tune.

## Efficiency and Inference

You might think applying matrix transformations would be slow or parameter-heavy. OFT addresses this using a block-diagonal structure for the orthogonal matrix, which significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters. The authors use Cayley parameterization to efficiently generate these orthogonal matrices. Importantly, because the orthogonal matrix can simply be multiplied into the pretrained weights, OFT introduces zero additional computational overhead during inference. The model runs just as fast as the original.

## Results That Speak for Themselves

The authors apply OFT and COFT to two challenging tasks:
*   **Subject-driven generation:** Generating images of a specific subject in new contexts.
*   **Controllable generation:** Generating images that follow additional control signals like edge maps or segmentation maps.

In both tasks, OFT and COFT outperform existing methods like DreamBooth, LoRA, and ControlNet. They converge significantly faster—sometimes needing less than half the training epochs—and are much more stable. The generated images are higher fidelity, and the control is more accurate. For example, in subject-driven generation, OFT perfectly preserves the subject's identity while accurately following the text prompt, whereas LoRA often loses the subject identity and DreamBooth fails to follow the prompt.

## Conclusion

Orthogonal Finetuning offers a principled way to adapt powerful AI models to new tasks without causing them to forget what they already know. By focusing on preserving the angular relationships between neurons, OFT finds a sweet spot between flexibility and regularity. It's a simple yet powerful idea that leads to faster convergence, better stability, and superior image quality, all without adding any cost at inference time. This makes OFT a highly practical tool for anyone looking to customize large diffusion models.
