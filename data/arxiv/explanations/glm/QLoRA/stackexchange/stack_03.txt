\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### How do gradients flow through frozen 4-bit weights to update LoRA adapters?
Question:
The paper states that QLoRA "backpropagates gradients through a frozen, 4-bit quantized pretrained language model into Low Rank Adapters" and that computing $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ requires computing $\frac{\partial \mathbf{X}}{\partial \mathbf{W}}$ via dequantization from $\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}$ to $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$. But if the base weights $\mathbf{W}$ are frozen and stored in 4-bit, what exactly happens during the backward pass? Does the model dequantize the weights to BFloat16 on-the-fly during backpropagation to compute the gradients for the LoRA parameters? Does this mean the dequantized weights need to be stored in memory during the backward pass, or are they recomputed as needed? I'm struggling to understand the exact computational flow.
Answer:
In QLoRA, the frozen 4-bit weights are dequantized to BFloat16 on-the-fly whenever they are needed for computation, and this happens during both the forward and backward passes. The paper explicitly states that "whenever a QLoRA weight tensor is used, we dequantize the tensor to BFloat16, and then perform a matrix multiplication in 16-bit." The dequantized weights are not permanently stored in memory; they are recomputed as needed.

To understand why dequantization is necessary during the backward pass even though the base weights $\mathbf{W}$ are frozen, consider the forward computation for a QLoRA layer:

$$\mathbf{Y}^{\text{BF16}} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}} \mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}} + \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}} \mathbf{L}_1^{\text{BF16}} \mathbf{L}_2^{\text{BF16}}$$

where $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}} = \text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, c_2^{\text{k-bit}}, \mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}})$. During backpropagation, computing the gradient $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ for the LoRA adapters requires propagating gradients through this entire expression, which includes the term $\mathbf{X} \mathbf{W}$. To compute gradients with respect to the LoRA parameters and to propagate gradients to earlier layers (i.e., computing $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{X}} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{Y}} (\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{L}_1 \mathbf{L}_2)^T$), the dequantized $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$ must be available. As the paper notes, "the calculation of $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ entails the calculation of $\frac{\partial \mathbf{X}}{\partial \mathbf{W}}$ which proceeds via dequantization from storage $\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}$ to computation data type $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$."

Crucially, the paper does not compute or store $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{W}}$ since the base weights are frozen. The dequantization is purely a read operation: the 4-bit weights and their quantization constants are loaded, the BFloat16 weights are reconstructed, used for the necessary matrix multiplications, and then discarded. This is what makes QLoRA memory-efficient—the base model stays in 4-bit storage throughout training, and only transient BFloat16 values exist during computation.

The paper also employs gradient checkpointing, which further reduces memory by not storing intermediate activations from the forward pass. Instead, during the backward pass, the forward computation is partially re-run to recover the needed activations. This means the dequantization of $\mathbf{W}$ may happen multiple times for the same weights during a single training step—once during the forward pass, and again during the backward pass when recomputing activations under gradient checkpointing. This trade-off of recomputation for reduced memory is standard practice and is why the paper reports that "gradient checkpointing is important" for keeping memory usage manageable, noting that activation gradients can be more memory-intensive than all LoRA weights combined.

In summary, the computational flow during the backward pass is: the 4-bit weights are dequantized on-the-fly to BFloat16 as needed, used to compute the gradients for the LoRA adapters (and to propagate gradients to earlier layers), and then the dequantized values are discarded. No BFloat16 copy of the base weights is retained in memory. The memory savings come from keeping the base model in 4-bit NF4 storage and using gradient checkpointing to avoid storing intermediate activations, while the only persistent trainable parameters are the small LoRA adapters in BFloat16.
