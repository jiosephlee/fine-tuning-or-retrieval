Chapter 4: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Mathematics

# SFT Data Curation: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) represents a critical stage in the development of mathematical language models, serving as the bridge between a base model pre-trained on large-scale mathematical corpora and a model capable of following instructions to solve mathematical problems. After pre-training, the base model possesses substantial mathematical knowledge encoded in its parameters, but it lacks the ability to consistently produce well-structured solutions to posed problems in response to natural language instructions. The goal of SFT is to teach the model how to organize its reasoning and present solutions in formats that are both correct and human-readable.

The DeepSeekMath team constructed a mathematical instruction-tuning dataset comprising $776\text{K}$ training examples, each consisting of a mathematical problem paired with a solution. The solutions in this dataset are not all in the same format; rather, the team employed three distinct reasoning formats to annotate the solutions, each leveraging different capabilities of the model and addressing different aspects of mathematical problem-solving. These three formats are Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is a reasoning format where the model is asked to solve a mathematical problem by generating a step-by-step textual reasoning process that leads to the final answer. In this format, the solution consists of natural language explanations interspersed with mathematical expressions, equations, and calculations. The model writes out each intermediate step of the reasoning, explaining why each step follows from the previous one, until it arrives at the final answer. The advantage of CoT is that it closely mirrors how humans solve mathematical problems and how solutions are typically presented in textbooks and educational materials. It also makes the model's reasoning transparent and verifiable, since a human reader can follow each step and check its correctness. For the DeepSeekMath instruction-tuning dataset, CoT solutions were used as one of the primary annotation formats, particularly for problems where the reasoning process itself is as important as the final answer.

Program-of-Thought (PoT) is a reasoning format where the model is asked to solve a mathematical problem by writing a Python program that, when executed, produces the correct answer. In this format, the solution consists of Python code that encodes the mathematical reasoning needed to solve the problem, along with comments or print statements that output the final result. The advantage of PoT is that it offloads complex arithmetic computations, symbolic manipulations, and other error-prone calculations to the Python interpreter, which can execute them with perfect accuracy. This is particularly beneficial for problems involving large numbers, complex algebraic manipulations, or numerical computations that a language model might struggle to perform correctly through text-based reasoning alone. Libraries such as \textit{math} and \textit{sympy} can be utilized within the generated programs for intricate computations, enabling the model to handle a wider range of mathematical problems than would be possible through pure text-based reasoning.

Tool-Integrated Reasoning is a format that combines the strengths of both CoT and PoT. In this format, the model interleaves natural language reasoning with the execution of Python code, using the code as a tool to perform calculations that would be difficult or error-prone to do mentally. The model first reasons about the problem in natural language, then writes and executes Python code to perform a specific computation, observes the result, and continues reasoning based on that result. This process can be repeated multiple times, with the model writing and executing code at various points in its reasoning process as needed. Tool-Integrated Reasoning was introduced to enable models to solve complex mathematical problems that require both high-level reasoning and precise computation. The ToRA format was used for annotating solutions in this style.

The inclusion of all three reasoning formats in the instruction-tuning dataset ensures that the resulting model is versatile and can adapt its problem-solving approach to the nature of the problem at hand. Some problems are best solved through pure reasoning, others through computation, and many require a combination of both. By training on solutions in all three formats, the model learns when to use each approach and how to combine them effectively.

# English and Chinese Mathematical Datasets

The DeepSeekMath instruction-tuning dataset was designed to be bilingual, covering mathematical problems and solutions in both English and Chinese. This bilingual design reflects the multilingual nature of the DeepSeekMath Corpus used for pre-training and ensures that the model's mathematical reasoning capabilities are not limited to a single language. The inclusion of Chinese mathematical data is a notable departure from many previous works in the field, which typically focused exclusively on English mathematical data.

For the English mathematical datasets, the team drew from several sources to ensure coverage of diverse mathematical fields and complexity levels. GSM8K and MATH problems were annotated with tool-integrated solutions, providing the model with examples of how to combine natural language reasoning with code execution to solve these well-known benchmark problems. In addition, a subset of MathInstruct was adopted, along with the training set of Lila-OOD, where problems were solved with either CoT or PoT format solutions. The English collection was specifically designed to cover diverse fields of mathematics, including algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry. This breadth ensures that the model is exposed to a wide range of mathematical concepts and problem types, which is essential for developing general-purpose mathematical reasoning capabilities.

The Chinese mathematical datasets focused on K-12 mathematical problems, which are problems covering the kindergarten through twelfth grade educational levels. These problems spanned 76 sub-topics, including topics such as linear equations, providing comprehensive coverage of the Chinese mathematics curriculum. The solutions for the Chinese problems were annotated in both CoT and tool-integrated reasoning formats, giving the model examples of how to solve these problems using different approaches. The inclusion of Chinese K-12 problems is particularly valuable because it provides the model with exposure to mathematical problem-solving in a different educational and linguistic context, which can help the model develop more robust and generalizable reasoning skills.

The total number of training examples across both languages and all reasoning formats was $776\text{K}$. This size was chosen to provide sufficient data for the model to learn the mapping from problems to solutions across a variety of mathematical domains and reasoning styles, without being so large as to cause the model to overfit to the instruction-tuning data at the expense of its pre-trained knowledge. The careful curation of this dataset, with attention to language diversity, mathematical domain coverage, and reasoning format variety, is a key factor in the strong performance of the resulting DeepSeekMath-Instruct model.

# Training Configuration for DeepSeekMath-Instruct

The training of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B was based on the DeepSeekMath-Base model, which had been pre-trained on the DeepSeekMath Corpus and other data sources. The training configuration was designed to efficiently adapt the base model to follow instructions and produce solutions in the specified reasoning formats.

One notable aspect of the training configuration was the handling of training examples. Rather than training on individual examples separately, the training examples were randomly concatenated until reaching a maximum context length of $4\text{K}$ tokens. This approach of packing multiple examples into a single training sequence has several advantages. First, it improves training efficiency by reducing the amount of padding and wasted computation that would occur if each example were processed individually. Second, it exposes the model to a greater variety of problems within each training sequence, which can help the model learn to generalize across different problem types. Third, it allows the model to see transitions between different problems, which can help it learn to delineate between different problems and maintain coherent reasoning within each one.

The training was conducted for $500$ steps with a batch size of $256$. The use of a constant learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ was chosen for the training. A constant learning rate, as opposed to a learning rate schedule with warmup and decay, is often used in fine-tuning because the model is already pre-trained and only needs gentle adjustments to its parameters to adapt to the instruction-following task. The relatively low learning rate ensures that the model's pre-trained knowledge is not disrupted during fine-tuning, while still being high enough to allow meaningful adaptation to the instruction-tuning data.

The choice of $500$ steps with a batch size of $256$ means that the model was trained on a total of $128\text{K}$ sequences of up to $4\text{K}$ tokens each. Given that the total dataset contained $776\text{K}$ training examples, this means that the model was trained for less than one full epoch over the data, which helps prevent overfitting. The model sees a substantial portion of the training data but does not see any example too many times, which balances the need for learning with the need to maintain the model's pre-trained capabilities.

The training configuration reflects a careful balance between several competing considerations: the need to adequately learn from the instruction-tuning data, the need to preserve the pre-trained model's mathematical knowledge, and the need to train efficiently. The resulting DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B model demonstrates that this balance was achieved, as it shows strong performance across a wide range of mathematical benchmarks.

# Evaluation Without Tool Use

The evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B was conducted under two settings: one where tool use was disallowed and one where models were allowed to integrate natural language reasoning with program-based tool use. The evaluation without tool use assesses the model's ability to solve mathematical problems through pure reasoning, generating self-contained text solutions that include all necessary calculations and explanations.

Under the evaluation setting where tool use was disallowed, the model was evaluated on four quantitative reasoning benchmarks in both English and Chinese. The benchmarks included GSM8K, which contains grade-school level math word problems, and MATH, which contains competition-level mathematical problems. The evaluation also included Chinese benchmarks to assess the model's bilingual mathematical reasoning capabilities.

The evaluation without tool use is particularly challenging because the model must perform all calculations internally, without the assistance of a Python interpreter or other computational tools. This requires the model not only to reason correctly about the mathematical concepts involved in each problem but also to perform arithmetic and algebraic manipulations accurately through text-based generation. For complex problems, this can be a significant challenge, as the model must maintain accuracy over long chains of calculations.

DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrated strong performance in this evaluation setting. On the competition-level MATH dataset, the model surpassed all open-source models and the majority of proprietary models by at least $9\%$ absolute. This is a remarkable achievement for a $7\text{B}$ parameter model, particularly considering that it outperformed models that were substantially larger, such as Qwen $72\text{B}$, and models that had been specifically enhanced through math-focused reinforcement learning, such as WizardMath-v1.1 $7\text{B}$. The model's performance on MATH without tool use indicates that the instruction tuning process successfully taught the model to organize its mathematical reasoning in a clear, step-by-step manner and to perform calculations with a high degree of accuracy.

While DeepSeekMath-Instruct rivaled the Chinese proprietary models GLM-4 and Baichuan-3 on MATH, it still underperformed GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra, which are the most capable closed-source models. This gap is not surprising, given that GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra are substantially larger models trained on proprietary datasets with extensive resources. Nevertheless, the performance of DeepSeekMath-Instruct represents a significant step forward for open-source mathematical language models, demonstrating that a well-designed instruction-tuning pipeline can produce a model that approaches the performance of the best closed-source models.

# Evaluation With Tool Integration

The evaluation with tool integration assesses the model's ability to solve mathematical problems by combining natural language reasoning with the execution of Python code. In this setting, the model is allowed to write and execute Python programs as part of its solution process, using libraries such as \textit{math} and \textit{sympy} for computations that would be difficult or error-prone to perform through text-based reasoning alone.

The tool-integrated evaluation setting provides the model with a significant advantage over the no-tool setting, as it can offload complex computations to the Python interpreter. This is particularly beneficial for problems that involve large numbers, complex algebraic manipulations, or numerical computations that require high precision. By delegating these computations to code, the model can focus on the high-level reasoning needed to solve the problem, while the Python interpreter handles the computational details.

Under the evaluation setting where models were allowed to integrate natural language reasoning and program-based tool use for problem solving, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approached an accuracy of $60\%$ on the MATH dataset. This represents a substantial improvement over the no-tool setting and surpasses all existing open-source models. The improvement from tool integration highlights the value of the tool-integrated reasoning format in the instruction-tuning data. By training on solutions that interleave natural language reasoning with code execution, the model learned to effectively use Python as a tool for mathematical computation, significantly enhancing its problem-solving capabilities.

On the other benchmarks, the model was competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat $67\text{B}$, which was the prior state-of-the-art that is $10$ times larger than DeepSeekMath-Instruct in terms of parameter count. This is a particularly noteworthy result, as it demonstrates that a $7\text{B}$ model with the right training can match the performance of a much larger model on mathematical tasks when tool integration is allowed. The ability to use tools effectively can partially compensate for a smaller model size, as the model can rely on the Python interpreter for computations that would otherwise require a larger model to perform accurately.

The evaluation with tool integration also demonstrates the value of training on multiple reasoning formats. By including both CoT and PoT solutions in the training data, along with tool-integrated solutions, the model learned to flexibly choose the most appropriate approach for each problem. For problems that require primarily reasoning, the model can use a CoT-style approach. For problems that require primarily computation, the model can use a PoT-style approach. For complex problems that require both, the model can use a tool-integrated approach that combines the strengths of both.

# Benchmarking Against Open-Source and Proprietary Models

To provide a comprehensive assessment of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B's performance, the model was benchmarked against a wide range of both open-source and proprietary models. This extensive comparison allows for a clear understanding of where DeepSeekMath-Instruct stands relative to the state of the art and provides insights into the factors that contribute to strong mathematical reasoning performance.

The closed-source models against which DeepSeekMath-Instruct was compared included the GPT family, among which GPT-4 and GPT-4 Code Interpreter were the most capable. The comparison also included Gemini Ultra and Pro from Google, Inflection-2, Grok-1, and models recently released by Chinese companies including Baichuan-3 and the latest GLM-4 from the GLM family. These proprietary models are generally purpose-built and have undergone a series of alignment procedures, including pre-training on large-scale data, instruction tuning, and reinforcement learning from human feedback. They represent the state of the art in commercial AI systems and provide a high bar for comparison.

The open-source models included general models such as DeepSeek-LLM-Chat $67\text{B}$, Qwen $72\text{B}$, SeaLLM-v2 $7\text{B}$, and ChatGLM3 $6\text{B}$. The comparison also included models with specific enhancements in mathematics, such as InternLM2-Math $20\text{B}$, which builds on InternLM2 and underwent math training followed by instruction tuning. Math-Shepherd-Mistral $7\text{B}$, which applies PPO training to Mistral $7\text{B}$ with a process-supervised reward model, was also included. The WizardMath series, which improves mathematical reasoning in Mistral $7\text{B}$ and Llama-2 $70\text{B}$ using evolve-instruct and PPO training, was compared as well. Additional open-source models included MetaMath $70\text{B}$, ToRA $34\text{B}$, and MAmmoTH $70\text{B}$.

The benchmarking results reveal several important findings. First, DeepSeekMath-Instruct $7\text{B}$ demonstrates that a relatively small open-source model, when properly trained, can achieve performance that rivals or exceeds that of much larger models. This challenges the assumption that model size is the primary determinant of mathematical reasoning capability and highlights the importance of data quality and training methodology. Second, the comparison with math-specific models shows that the DeepSeekMath pipeline, which combines high-quality pre-training data, comprehensive instruction tuning with multiple reasoning formats, and effective reinforcement learning, can produce superior results even compared to models that have been specifically optimized for mathematics. Third, the gap between DeepSeekMath-Instruct and the best proprietary models, while still present, is relatively small, suggesting that the open-source community is making rapid progress toward closing the gap with commercial systems.

The benchmarking also reveals the value of bilingual training. DeepSeekMath-Instruct's strong performance on Chinese benchmarks, which stems from the inclusion of Chinese mathematical data in both the pre-training and instruction-tuning stages, demonstrates that bilingual training can enhance a model's mathematical reasoning capabilities without compromising its performance on English benchmarks. This finding has important implications for the development of mathematical language models, suggesting that future efforts should consider including data in multiple languages to develop more capable and generalizable models.

Overall, the benchmarking results position DeepSeekMath-Instruct $7\text{B}$ as a leading open-source model for mathematical reasoning, demonstrating the effectiveness of the DeepSeekMath pipeline and providing a strong foundation for further improvements through reinforcement learning.
