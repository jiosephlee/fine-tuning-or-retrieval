\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 3: Pre-Training DeepSeekMath-Base

The pre-training of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B represents a carefully designed process that leverages both the availability of high-quality mathematical data and the foundational strengths of a code-trained language model. This chapter walks through the initialization of the model, the data distribution and training configuration used during continual pre-training, and a comprehensive evaluation of the resulting model across mathematical reasoning, tool-integrated problem solving, formal theorem proving, and general natural language understanding tasks. Together, these elements illustrate how domain-specific pre-training, when grounded in a strong starting checkpoint and a well-curated corpus, can produce a base model that rivals or surpasses far larger models on mathematical benchmarks.

# Initialization from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, a code pre-training model, rather than from a general-purpose language model. This choice is motivated by a key empirical observation: starting from a code-trained model proves to be a better foundation for mathematical pre-training than starting from a general LLM. The rationale behind this finding is that code and mathematics share structural and logical commonalities—both require precise symbolic manipulation, multi-step reasoning, and adherence to formal rules of composition. By beginning from a model already exposed to large quantities of programming code, DeepSeekMath-Base inherits representational features that are well-suited to absorbing mathematical content during the subsequent continual training phase.

The paper provides additional evidence supporting this design decision through controlled experiments. In one set of experiments, a 1.3B-parameter model was trained first on 400 billion code tokens and then on 150 billion math tokens, and this two-stage approach was compared against training the same model first on 400 billion general tokens and then on 150 billion math tokens. The code-first approach led to stronger downstream mathematical reasoning performance, both with and without tool use. This suggests that the representations learned during code training provide a useful inductive bias for mathematical tasks. Importantly, the benefit of code training was observed not only for program-aided mathematical reasoning—where one might naturally expect code exposure to help—but also for chain-of-thought reasoning that does not involve any tool use at all. The paper interprets this as partial evidence that code training improves reasoning abilities, at least in the mathematical domain.

Beyond the two-stage training experiments, the paper also explored one-stage training in which code and math tokens were mixed together. This approach mitigated the catastrophic forgetting of coding ability that can arise when math training follows code training in separate stages, and it synergized coding and program-aided mathematical reasoning. However, the one-stage mixed approach compromised mathematical reasoning without tool use at the 1.3B scale, possibly because a model of that limited size lacks the capacity to fully assimilate both code and mathematical data simultaneously. Nevertheless, for the full-scale DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, the decision to initialize from the code-trained DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B and then perform continual training with a data mixture that retains a substantial code component proved to be an effective strategy, preserving coding capability while substantially boosting mathematical reasoning.

# Data Distribution and Training Configuration

The continual pre-training of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B proceeds over 500 billion tokens, drawing from a deliberately composed data mixture. The distribution is as follows: 56% of the training data comes from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, the 120 billion-token mathematical corpus extracted from Common Crawl through the iterative fastText-based data collection pipeline described earlier in the paper. An additional 4% comes from AlgebraicStack, a corpus of mathematical code. ArXiv papers constitute 10% of the data, while 20% consists of GitHub code. The remaining 10% is natural language data from Common Crawl, in both English and Chinese. This composition reflects a design philosophy that balances mathematical content with code and general natural language, ensuring that the model develops strong mathematical reasoning without losing its broader language understanding or programming skills.

The training configuration closely follows the settings established in the paper's corpus quality validation experiments, with two notable adjustments. The optimizer is AdamW, with hyperparameters $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.95$, and weight decay set to $0.1$. A multi-step learning rate schedule is employed: the learning rate ramps up over 2,000 warmup steps to a peak value, then decreases to 31.6% of that peak after 80% of the training process, and further decreases to 10.0% of the peak after 90% of training. For the full 7B model, the peak learning rate is set to $4.2 \times 10^{-4}$, and the batch size is 10 million tokens with a 4K context length. These choices reflect a training regime that is both efficient and stable, scaling up the batch size relative to the smaller validation experiments while modestly reducing the peak learning rate to accommodate the larger model.

The inclusion of code tokens (20% from GitHub) alongside the mathematical and natural language data serves an important function. As the paper's ablation studies reveal, math training that follows code training can degrade coding performance due to catastrophic forgetting. By including a significant code component in the continual training mixture, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B effectively maintains the coding capabilities inherited from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5. The multilingual nature of the DeepSeekMath Corpus—which, unlike prior math corpora that are English-centric, includes substantial Chinese mathematical content—also means that the resulting base model exhibits improved performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks, a benefit directly attributable to the data distribution choices made at this stage.

# Performance on Step-by-Step Mathematical Reasoning

The mathematical reasoning capabilities of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B are evaluated using few-shot chain-of-thought prompting across eight benchmarks in both English and Chinese. These benchmarks span a wide range of mathematical domains and difficulty levels. They include quantitative reasoning datasets such as GSM8K (grade-school level word problems), MATH (competition-level problems), and CMATH (Chinese grade-school mathematics), as well as multiple-choice problems such as MMLU-STEM (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics subjects from the MMLU benchmark) and Gaokao-MathQA (Chinese college entrance examination questions). Together, these benchmarks provide a thorough assessment of the model's ability to generate self-contained, step-by-step textual solutions to mathematical problems without relying on external tools.

The results demonstrate that DeepSeekMath-Base 7B leads in performance across all eight benchmarks among open-source base models. This includes comparisons with general-purpose models such as Mistral 7B and with models that have undergone dedicated math pre-training, such as Llemma 34B, which was trained on the Proof-Pile-2 corpus. The performance advantage is particularly striking on the competition-level MATH dataset, where DeepSeekMath-Base surpasses existing open-source base models by over 10% in absolute accuracy. This is a substantial margin, especially given that the model has only 7 billion parameters.

Perhaps the most notable result is that DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforms Minerva 540B, a closed-source base model that is 77 times larger. Minerva 540B is built on PaLM and was further trained on mathematical texts, making it a formidable baseline. The fact that a 7B model can surpass it underscores the paper's central thesis: the number of parameters is not the only key factor in mathematical reasoning capability. A smaller model pre-trained on high-quality, carefully selected data can achieve strong performance as well. The quality of the DeepSeekMath Corpus—with its iterative data collection pipeline, multilingual coverage, and decontamination procedures—plays a critical role in enabling this result.

The strong Chinese benchmark performance of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is also worth emphasizing. Because the DeepSeekMath Corpus includes high-quality non-English mathematical content, the model demonstrates superior performance on Chinese benchmarks such as CMATH, MGSM-zh, and the Gaokao-related tasks. This contrasts with existing mathematical corpora that are primarily English-centric and which, as the paper's corpus comparison experiments show, can exhibit limited improvement or even hinder performance on Chinese mathematical reasoning.

# Tool-Integrated Mathematical Problem Solving

In addition to evaluating self-contained textual reasoning, the paper assesses DeepSeekMath-Base 7B on program-aided mathematical reasoning using few-shot program-of-thought prompting. In this evaluation setting, models are prompted to solve each mathematical problem by writing a Python program. The program can import and use libraries such as `math` and `sympy` to perform intricate computations, such as symbolic manipulation or numerical evaluation. The execution result of the generated program is then taken as the answer to the problem. This evaluation paradigm tests a different and complementary skill: the model's ability to translate a mathematical problem into executable code, leverage external computational tools, and produce a correct numerical or symbolic answer through program execution.

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforms the prior state-of-the-art open-source model, Llemma 34B, on tool-integrated mathematical reasoning tasks on both GSM8K and MATH. This result is significant for several reasons. First, it demonstrates that the model's mathematical capabilities extend beyond natural language reasoning into the domain of program-aided problem solving. Second, it validates the decision to initialize from a code-trained model: the inherited coding ability from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, reinforced by the continued inclusion of code tokens during math pre-training, equips the model with the programming proficiency needed to write correct and effective Python programs for mathematical computation. Third, the result shows that the DeepSeekMath Corpus contains content that supports not only mathematical knowledge but also the intersection of mathematics and programming, which is essential for tool-integrated reasoning.

The synergy between code training and mathematical reasoning that the paper documents in its ablation studies provides further context for this result. The two-stage experiments showed that code training alone significantly enhances the ability to solve GSM8K and MATH problems using Python, and that subsequent math training yields further improvements. In the one-stage setting, mixing code and math tokens synergized program-aided mathematical reasoning. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, with its initialization from a code model and its training mixture retaining 20% GitHub code plus 4% AlgebraicStack, effectively captures this synergy at scale, resulting in strong tool-integrated performance.

# Formal Mathematics and Theorem Proving

Formal proof automation is an area of growing importance in artificial intelligence, as it offers the potential to ensure the accuracy and reliability of mathematical proofs and to enhance the efficiency of formal verification. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is evaluated on the informal-to-formal theorem proving task, which involves generating a formal proof given an informal statement, a formal counterpart of that statement, and an informal proof. This task, drawn from prior work on theorem proving, requires the model to bridge the gap between natural mathematical language and the precise, machine-checkable formal language of a proof assistant.

The evaluation is conducted on miniF2F, a benchmark designed for formal Olympiad-level mathematics. The proof assistant chosen for this evaluation is Isabelle. For each problem, the model generates a formal proof in Isabelle using few-shot prompting. Following the methodology of prior work, the model is leveraged to generate proof sketches—partial formal proofs that capture the high-level structure and key steps—and then the off-the-shelf automated prover Sledgehammer is used to fill in the missing details. This approach leverages the strengths of both the neural model, which can produce creative and high-level proof strategies, and the automated prover, which can rigorously verify and complete the low-level logical steps.

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B demonstrates strong performance in proof autoformalization on this benchmark. This result highlights the model's ability to reason about mathematical content at a level that goes beyond computing numerical answers or producing natural language explanations. Formal theorem proving requires an understanding of mathematical structure, logical dependencies, and the precise syntax and semantics of a formal proof system. The strong performance of DeepSeekMath-Base suggests that the pre-training data contains content relevant to formal mathematics, and that the model has internalized mathematical reasoning patterns that transfer to the formal domain.

It is worth noting that, despite this strong performance in autoformalization, the paper acknowledges in its conclusion that DeepSeekMath's capabilities in geometry and theorem proving are relatively weaker compared to closed models. For example, in the authors' dry runs, the model struggled with problems related to triangles and ellipses, which may indicate data selection bias in both pre-training and fine-tuning. This limitation underscores that while the DeepSeekMath Corpus is broad and high-quality, it may not uniformly cover all subdomains of mathematics, and certain areas—particularly those involving geometric reasoning and fully formalized theorem proving—remain challenging for the model.

# General Natural Language Understanding and Code Performance

To build a comprehensive profile of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B's capabilities beyond mathematics, the paper evaluates the model on a suite of general benchmarks. Natural language understanding is assessed on the Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU) benchmark, which encompasses 57 multiple-choice tasks covering diverse subjects across academic and professional domains. Reasoning is evaluated on BIG-Bench Hard (BBH), which consists of 23 challenging tasks that mostly require multi-step reasoning to solve. Coding capabilities are measured on HumanEval and MBPP, two widely used benchmarks for evaluating code generation models.

The results reveal that DeepSeekMath-Base 7B exhibits significant enhancements in performance on both MMLU and BBH over its precursor, DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. This is a notable finding: the continual training on mathematical data does not merely enhance the model's domain-specific mathematical abilities but also amplifies its general reasoning and language understanding capabilities. The paper interprets this as evidence that mathematical reasoning and general reasoning share common underlying skills—such as logical deduction, multi-step planning, and systematic problem decomposition—that are strengthened through exposure to mathematical content. This finding is consistent with the broader observation that math training serves as a form of general reasoning training, transferring benefits to tasks that are not explicitly mathematical.

On the coding benchmarks, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B effectively maintains the performance level of DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B on both HumanEval and MBPP. This is a direct consequence of the data distribution design: by including 20% GitHub code tokens in the continual training mixture, the model avoids the catastrophic forgetting of coding skills that would otherwise occur when training heavily on mathematical content. The preservation of coding ability is important not only for its own sake but also because it underpins the model's tool-integrated mathematical reasoning performance, as discussed earlier.

Overall, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B significantly outperforms the general-purpose model Mistral 7B on all three categories of benchmarks: natural language understanding (MMLU), reasoning (BBH), and coding (HumanEval and MBPP). This comprehensive advantage demonstrates that the pre-training strategy—initializing from a code model, training on a carefully composed mixture of mathematical, code, and natural language data, and leveraging a high-quality, multilingual mathematical corpus—produces a base model that is not only strong in mathematics but also well-rounded across general cognitive tasks. The model's broad capabilities make it a strong foundation for subsequent instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, as explored in later stages of the DeepSeekMath pipeline.
