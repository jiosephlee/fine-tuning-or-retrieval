\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### What does it mean for two reward functions to be in the same 'equivalence class' and why does it matter?
Question:
In Section 5.1, the paper defines that two reward functions $r(x,y)$ and $r'(x,y)$ are equivalent if $r(x,y) - r'(x,y) = f(x)$ for some function $f$. Lemma 1 says such rewards induce the same preference distribution, and Lemma 2 says they induce the same optimal policy. Why does adding any function of $x$ alone not change preferences? And why is this equivalence relation important for the DPO derivation — does it mean we have freedom in how we parameterize the reward?
Answer:
The equivalence relation defined in Section 5.1 captures a fundamental under-specification in preference-based learning: rewards are only meaningful up to a prompt-dependent shift. Two rewards $r(x,y)$ and $r'(x,y)$ are equivalent if they differ by some function $f(x)$ that depends only on the prompt $x$, not the response $y$.

**Why adding $f(x)$ doesn't change preferences.** The Bradley-Terry model defines preference probability as $p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma(r(x, y_1) - r(x, y_2))$, where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. If we substitute $r'(x,y) = r(x,y) - f(x)$, the difference becomes:

$$r'(x, y_1) - r'(x, y_2) = r(x, y_1) - f(x) - r(x, y_2) + f(x) = r(x, y_1) - r(x, y_2)$$

The $f(x)$ terms cancel because they appear identically in both rewards for the same prompt $x$. Since the preference probability depends only on the *difference* of rewards, any prompt-dependent offset is invisible to the preference model. This is Lemma 1.

**Why it doesn't change the optimal policy.** The optimal policy under the KL-constrained objective (Eq. 5) is:

$$\pi_r(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_\text{ref}(y \mid x) \exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y)\right)$$

where $Z(x) = \sum_y \pi_\text{ref}(y \mid x) \exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y)\right)$. If we replace $r$ with $r' = r - f(x)$, the new partition function becomes $Z'(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{f(x)}{\beta}\right) Z(x)$, and the $\exp\left(-\frac{f(x)}{\beta}\right)$ factor appears in both the numerator and denominator, so it cancels — yielding $\pi_{r'} = \pi_r$. This is Lemma 2.

$$r(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y \mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y \mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)$$
