\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does the register permutation {d0 d1 d4 d5 d2 d3 d6 d7} transform the FP32 accumulator for the second FP8 WGMMA?
Question:
In Section 3.3 of the FlashAttention-3 paper, they describe transforming the FP32 accumulator of the first FP8 WGMMA into a format suitable for the second FP8 WGMMA. The specific permutation is:
$$\{d0\ d1\ d4\ d5\ d2\ d3\ d6\ d7\}$$
replicated over every 8 bytes. They say this permutes the columns of the $\mathbf{P}$ tile (e.g., columns $0189$ become the first four columns), and that the in-kernel transpose of $\mathbf{V}$ writes a matching row permutation.

I'm completely lost on what $d0$ through $d7$ represent here. Are these individual bytes within a register? Register names? And how does permuting bytes in registers correspond to permuting columns of a matrix tile? The paper references figures (fig:rmem_accum and fig:rmem_operand) that I can't see — could someone explain what these layout fragments look like and why this specific permutation is needed?
Answer:
In the FlashAttention-3 paper, $d0$ through $d7$ refer to consecutive FP32 data elements held in a single thread's registers as part of the WGMMA accumulator fragment. When a warpgroup executes an FP8 WGMMA, the resulting FP32 accumulator matrix—here, the $\mathbf{P}$ tile after softmax—is distributed across the 128 threads of the warpgroup, with each thread holding a fragment of the output. The referenced figures (`fig:rmem_accum` and `fig:rmem_operand`) depict how these register-resident elements map to specific $(row, column)$ positions in the logical tile, for the accumulator layout and the operand-A layout respectively.

The core problem is a layout mismatch between these two formats. For FP16 WGMMA, the accumulator layout and the operand-A layout happen to be compatible, so you can chain $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ directly into $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$ with the result of the first GEMM feeding the second without rearrangement. For FP8 WGMMA, they are not compatible: the accumulator produced by the first WGMMA stores its FP32 entries in one arrangement of columns, while the second WGMMA expects its operand A in a different arrangement.

The permutation $\{d0\ d1\ d4\ d5\ d2\ d3\ d6\ d7\}$ reorders eight consecutive FP32 register elements. Although we cannot see the figures, we can infer the column mapping from the paper's statement that "columns $0189$ now become the first four columns." In the accumulator layout, these eight elements correspond to columns of $\mathbf{P}$ in the order $0, 1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11$—that is, the first group of four register slots holds
