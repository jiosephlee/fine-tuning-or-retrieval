\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What is the difference between pingpong scheduling and intra-warpgroup GEMM-softmax overlapping?
Question:
The FlashAttention-3 paper describes two overlapping strategies for hiding softmax computation behind GEMMs:

1. **Pingpong scheduling** (Section 3.1): Using synchronization barriers to schedule GEMMs of warpgroup 1 before warpgroup 2, so that softmax of warpgroup 1 overlaps with GEMMs of warpgroup 2.

2. **Intra-warpgroup overlapping** (Section 3.2, Algorithm 2): A 2-stage pipeline that overlaps the second WGMMA of iteration $j$ with softmax operations from iteration $j+1$ within a single warpgroup.

Are these two techniques meant to be used together, or are they alternatives? If they are complementary, how do they interact? Does the pingpong scheduling operate at the CTA level (between the two consumer warpgroups), while the intra-warpgroup overlapping operates within each consumer warpgroup independently? The paper says Algorithm 2 "functions as a replacement for the consumer path of Algorithm 1" — does this mean Algorithm 2 replaces the consumer warpgroup logic while pingpong scheduling is an additional layer on top?
Answer:
These two techniques are **complementary** and operate at different levels of the execution hierarchy, exactly as you surmise. Let me clarify how they fit together.

**Pingpong scheduling** is an inter-warpgroup (CTA-level) technique. In the warp-specialized design of Algorithm 1, the CTA contains two consumer warpgroups. Pingpong scheduling uses `bar.sync` barriers to force the GEMMs of warpgroup 1 to be scheduled before those of warpgroup 2, so that warpgroup 1's softmax — which runs on the multi-function unit, a separate hardware unit from the Tensor Cores — overlaps in time with warpgroup 2's GEMMs. The roles then swap. This is purely about coordinating the *two* consumer warpgroups relative to each other.

**Intra-warpgroup overlapping** (Algorithm 2) is an intra-warpgroup technique. It restructures the consumer loop so that within a single warpgroup, the second WGMMA of iteration $j$ (the $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{cur}} \mathbf{V}_{j-1}$ GEMM) is issued without immediately waiting for completion, allowing softmax computations from iteration $j+1$ to proceed concurrently. This works by pipelining across loop iterations with extra register buffers (e.g., $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$). The paper states that Algorithm 2 "functions as a replacement for the consumer path of Algorithm 1," meaning it replaces the consumer-side loop body — lines 16–23 of Algorithm 1 — with the 2-stage pipelined version.

The complete FlashAttention-3 FP16 algorithm uses **both**: each consumer warpgroup runs Algorithm 2 (intra-warpgroup overlapping), and the two consumer warpgroups are coordinated via pingpong scheduling (inter-warpgroup overlapping). So pingpong scheduling is indeed an additional layer on top of Algorithm 2, not an alternative to it.

Intuitively, there are two sources of overlap being exploited simultaneously. Within each warpgroup, Algorithm 2 hides softmax latency behind that same warpgroup's own WGMMA instructions by deferring the wait on the second WGMMA. Between warpgroups, pingpong scheduling further hides softmax latency of one warpgroup behind the other warpgroup's GEMMs. The ablation study in Section 3.2 confirms that both contribute: warp-specialization alone (with pingpong) brings throughput to roughly 570 TFLOPs, and adding the 2-stage intra-warpgroup pipelining increases it to 661 TFLOPs. The two techniques target different levels of the concurrency hierarchy and compose naturally.
