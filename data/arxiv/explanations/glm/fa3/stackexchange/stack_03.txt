\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does incoherent processing with Hadamard matrices reduce FP8 quantization error?
Question:
In Section 3.3 of the FlashAttention-3 paper, they describe "incoherent processing" as multiplying $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ with a random orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{M}$ before quantizing to FP8. They show that $(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M})(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M})^\top = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ since $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top = I$.

I understand the algebra, but I'm confused about the mechanism. They say this "spreads out" outliers because each entry of $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M}$ is a random sum of entries of $\mathbf{Q}$. But wouldn't a random sum of entries actually increase the magnitude of some entries (by the central limit theorem, the variance increases)? How does this help with FP8 quantization, which has limited dynamic range? 

Also, they mention choosing $\mathbf{M}$ as a product of random diagonal matrices of $\pm 1$ and a Hadamard matrix, which can be multiplied in $O(d \log d)$ time. How does this specific structure preserve orthogonality while being fast to compute?
Answer:
## How Incoherent Processing Reduces FP8 Quantization Error

Your intuition about the central limit theorem is partially right but misses the key point: the goal is not to reduce the variance of individual entries but to reduce the **ratio of the maximum entry magnitude to the typical entry magnitude**. This ratio is what determines quantization quality, and incoherent processing shrinks it dramatically.
