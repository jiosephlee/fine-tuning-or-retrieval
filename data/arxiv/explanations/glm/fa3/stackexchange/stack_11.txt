\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How are the backward pass FLOPs 2.5x the forward pass FLOPs in FlashAttention-3?
Question:
The FlashAttention-3 paper states that the backward pass FLOPs are 2.5 times the forward pass FLOPs, explaining that there are "2 matmuls in the forward pass and 5 matmuls in the backward pass, due to recomputation."

Looking at the backward pass equations in Section 2.1:
- $\mathbf{dV} = \mathbf{P}^\top \mathbf{dO}$ (1 matmul)
- $\mathbf{dP} = \mathbf{dO} \mathbf{V}^\top$ (1 matmul)
- $\mathbf{dQ} = \alpha \mathbf{dS} \mathbf{K}$ (1 matmul)
- $\mathbf{dK} = \alpha \mathbf{dS}^\top \mathbf{Q}$ (1 matmul)

That's only 4 matmuls. Where does the 5th matmul come from? Is it related to the recomputation of $\mathbf{S}$ or $\mathbf{P}$ during the backward pass (since FlashAttention doesn't store the full $N \times N$ attention matrix)? If so, which matrix is being recomputed and how does it add one more matmul?
Answer:
The 5th matmul comes from recomputing the attention scores $\mathbf{S} = \alpha \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ during the backward pass. This is exactly the recomputation the paper refers to.

Here is the key insight: FlashAttention avoids materializing the $N \times N$ matrices $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{P}$ in HBM memory (which is central to its efficiency advantage over standard attention, as described in Section 2.3 of the paper). Instead, it only stores the output $\mathbf{O}$ and the logsumexp values $L$ to HBM. In the forward pass, $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{P}$ are computed on-the-fly in blocks within SRAM/registers and immediately consumed, then discarded. This means that during the backward pass, $\mathbf{P}$ is not available and must be recomputed from $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{K}$.

Now let us count all the matmuls in the backward pass:

1. **Recompute** $\mathbf{S} = \alpha \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ and then $\mathbf{P} = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{S})$ — this requires **1 matmul** (the recomputation of $\mathbf{S}$, which is the same matmul as in the forward pass).
2. $\mathbf{dV} = \mathbf{P}^\top \mathbf{dO}$ — **1 matmul**
3. $\mathbf{dP} = \mathbf{dO}\mathbf{V}^\top$ — **1 matmul**
4. $\mathbf{dQ} = \alpha\,\mathbf{dS}\,\mathbf{K}$ — **1 matmul**
5. $\mathbf{dK} = \alpha\,\mathbf{dS}^\top \mathbf{Q}$ — **1 matmul**

The $\mathrm{dsoftmax}$ operation $\mathbf{dS} = \mathrm{dsoftmax}(\mathbf{dP})$ is element-wise (involving $\mathbf{P}$ but no matrix multiplication), so it does not add to the count.

That gives **5 matmuls** in the backward pass versus **2 matmuls** in the forward pass ($\mathbf{S} = \alpha \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ and $\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$), yielding the $5/2 = 2.5\times$ ratio used in the paper's FLOP accounting.

Intuitively, the recomputation strategy trades extra compute for memory savings: by not storing the large $N \times N$ attention matrix, FlashAttention saves substantial HBM bandwidth (which is the actual bottleneck), at the cost of redoing one matmul. This is a favorable trade-off on modern GPUs where compute throughput far exceeds memory bandwidth — the same philosophy that motivates gradient checkpointing in general, but applied here specifically to the attention scores matrix.
