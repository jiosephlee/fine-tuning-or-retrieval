\title{Blogs about the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

# From Benchmarks to Real-World Impact: What 1.2 PFLOPs/s Actually Means for Developers

When you see a benchmark number like 1.2 PFLOPs/s, it's easy to be impressed but hard to know what it means for your actual workflow. The recently released FlashAttention-3 paper reports exactly that—alongside up to 740 TFLOPs/s in FP16 precision on NVIDIA H100 GPUs. These are not just abstract achievements; they translate into real changes for developers building applications that rely on Transformers. Let's break down what these numbers mean in practice.

## The Numbers in Context

FlashAttention-3 achieves 1.5–2.0$\times$ speedup over FlashAttention-2 in the forward pass and 1.5–1.75$\times$ in the backward pass when using FP16. At its peak, the method reaches 740 TFLOPs/s, which corresponds to 75% of the theoretical maximum throughput on the H100 GPU. For context, FlashAttention-2 was only achieving 35% utilization on the same hardware. That gap represents a lot of wasted silicon, and FlashAttention-3 closes it through three key techniques: warp-specialized asynchronous execution, overlapping softmax with matrix multiplications, and hardware-accelerated FP8 computation.

In FP8 precision, the throughput nearly doubles to close to 1.2 PFLOPs/s. The paper also validates that FP8 FlashAttention-3 achieves 2.6$\times$ lower numerical error than a baseline FP8 attention implementation, thanks to block quantization and incoherent processing techniques that handle outlier features common in large language models.

## What This Means for Real Applications

The quadratic scaling of attention with sequence length has long been the bottleneck for Transformer-based applications. FlashAttention-3 directly addresses this, and the speedups matter most in scenarios involving long context windows.

**Coding assistants** that work with large codebases need to process thousands of tokens of context to provide useful suggestions. With FlashAttention-3, the attention computation becomes significantly less of a bottleneck. The 2$\times$ forward pass speedup means that models can attend over longer code files with lower latency, enabling more responsive developer tools.

**Long-document reasoning** applications—such as modeling over multiple long documents or maintaining conversation history over extended user interactions—benefit from the ability to scale to longer sequences without proportional increases in compute time. The paper specifically notes that scaling attention to longer context unlocks capabilities in modeling and reasoning over multiple long documents and files in large codebases.

**High-resolution video and audio processing** involves handling sequences that can stretch to tens of thousands of tokens. The throughput improvements from both the asynchronous overlap strategy and FP8 support make these previously expensive workloads more tractable. For applications in video understanding or audio processing, where sequence lengths can be enormous, the difference between 35% and 75% GPU utilization is substantial.

## Accuracy Matters Too

Speed means nothing if the output is wrong. The paper demonstrates that FP16 FlashAttention-3 achieves the same numerical error as FlashAttention-2 and is actually better than standard attention implementations, since intermediate results like softmax rescaling are kept in FP32. For FP8, the combination of block quantization (keeping one scaling scalar per block rather than per tensor) and incoherent processing (multiplying $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ with a random orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{M}$ before quantization) reduces numerical error by 2.6$\times$ compared to standard per-tensor quantization approaches.

The incoherent processing technique is particularly elegant. Since $\mathbf{M}$ is orthogonal, $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top = I$, so $(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M})(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M})^\top = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$. The attention output is unchanged, but outliers are spread out across entries, reducing quantization error. In practice, $\mathbf{M}$ is chosen as a product of random diagonal matrices of $\pm 1$ and a Hadamard matrix, which can be multiplied in $O(d \log d)$ time and fused with rotary embedding at no extra computation cost.

## Getting Your Hands on It

FlashAttention-3 is open-source with a permissive license and is available at the FlashAttention GitHub repository. The authors also plan to integrate it with PyTorch and Hugging Face libraries, making it accessible to the broadest possible audience of researchers and developers.

For developers currently using FlashAttention-2, the upgrade path is straightforward since FlashAttention-3 follows the same tiling strategy for parallelizing attention and fusing operations into a single GPU kernel. The key difference is that FlashAttention-3 takes advantage of Hopper-specific hardware features like the Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) for asynchronous memory copies and the warpgroup-wide WGMMA instruction for asynchronous matrix multiplications.

## Limitations to Keep in Mind

The paper is transparent about current limitations. The FP8 kernel does not yet include a persistent kernel design or load balancing strategy, which partly explains why FP8 FlashAttention-3 does not perform as well for small sequence lengths and causal masking compared to FP8 cuDNN kernels. The authors also note that optimizing for LLM inference and understanding the effects of low-precision attention in large-scale training remain future work.

## The Bottom Line

FlashAttention-3 represents a meaningful step forward not just in benchmark numbers but in practical capability. The jump from 35% to 75% GPU utilization means that developers can either train and run models faster on existing hardware or push to longer context windows that were previously impractical. The 2.6$\times$ accuracy improvement in FP8 means that lower precision is a more viable option for production use cases. And the open-source release means these benefits are available now, not locked behind a vendor's closed library. For anyone building applications that push the boundaries of sequence length—whether in code, text, audio, or video—FlashAttention-3 is worth integrating into your workflow.
