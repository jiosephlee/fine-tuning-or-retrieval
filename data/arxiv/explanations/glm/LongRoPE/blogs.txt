# Beyond 2 Million Tokens: How LongRoPE Shatters LLM Context Limits

## Why Context Window Size Matters

Large Language Models (LLMs) have transformed how we interact with information, but their utility is often bottlenecked by a critical limitation: context window size. For instance, while models like LLaMA2 start with a 4096-token limit, real-world applications often demand much more. AI agents need extensive context to track complex tasks over time, and analyzing lengthy documents—like legal contracts or research papers—requires models to "remember" thousands of tokens simultaneously. 

Extending this window allows for in-context learning with numerous examples and empowers LLM agents to maintain long-term coherence. However, pushing past the 128k token mark has been a persistent challenge due to three major obstacles: high fine-tuning costs, a scarcity of sufficiently long training texts, and the introduction of catastrophic values from new token positions.

## The Limits of Traditional Position Interpolation

To understand how LongRoPE breaks these limits, we first need to look at how models represent token order. Most modern LLMs use Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE). When extending the context window from an original length $L$ to a longer length $L'$, the extension ratio is $s = \frac{L'}{L}$. 

Existing methods like Position Interpolation (PI) attempt to linearly downscale the rotation angles by this ratio across all dimensions. Other methods, like NTK and YaRN, apply different interpolation strategies based on frequency dimensions. However, these approaches often rely on uniform or manually designed rules that fail to capture the complex non-uniformities in positional encoding. This leads to information loss and limits the context window to around 128k tokens. Furthermore, when scaled to millions of tokens, attention becomes dispersed, degrading the model's performance on shorter, original-length tasks.

## Enter LongRoPE

LongRoPE introduces a novel approach that extends the context window of pre-trained LLMs to an impressive 2048k tokens—over 2 million—while maintaining performance at original short context windows. This breakthrough relies on three key innovations.

### Exploiting Non-Uniformities in Positional Interpolation

Through empirical analysis, the researchers discovered that effective positional interpolation requires handling two forms of non-uniformities:

- **Varying RoPE dimensions**: Lower dimensions (higher frequency) benefit from less interpolation, while higher dimensions (lower frequency) can handle more.
- **Token positions**: The initial $\hat{n}$ tokens in a sequence receive large attention scores and should be extrapolated with less interpolation.

To capture these non-uniformities, LongRoPE introduces a set of rescale factors $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})$ for the rotation angle of the $i^{th}$ RoPE dimension. For initial tokens where $n < \hat{n}$, the original RoPE is used. For later tokens where $n \ge \hat{n}$, a non-uniform rescale factor $\frac{1}{\lambda_i}$ is applied. Finding the optimal factors is framed as an optimization problem to minimize the model's perplexity on long texts.

Because the search space expands exponentially with the extension ratio, LongRoPE uses an evolutionary search algorithm. It initializes the search population with existing methods (PI, NTK, and YaRN) and applies a monotonically non-decreasing constraint ($\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}$) to prune poor solutions efficiently. This search provides a better initialization for fine-tuning and enables an $8\times$ extension even without fine-tuning.

### Progressive Extension Strategy

Directly fine-tuning a model on 2-million-token texts is computationally prohibitive and limited by data scarcity. LongRoPE bypasses this with a progressive strategy:

- First, it searches for rescale factors to extend the pre-trained LLM to 256k tokens.
- It then fine-tunes the model in two stages: 400 steps using the 128k rescale factors, followed by 600 steps with the 256k factors.
- Finally, leveraging the $8\times$ non-fine-tuning extension capability, it conducts a second search on the 256k fine-tuned LLM to reach the 2048k context window.

This approach achieves the 2048k context window with only 1,000 fine-tuning steps at a maximum training length of 256k, avoiding the need for direct training on million-token sequences.

### Short Context Window Recovery

Extending to a $512\times$ ratio causes position embeddings in the original 4k context window to become crowded, hurting performance on shorter texts. To fix this, LongRoPE performs an additional evolutionary search to adjust the RoPE rescale factors specifically for short context lengths (4k and 8k). During inference, if the sequence length is less than 8k, the model dynamically applies these adjusted factors to recover its original short-context performance.

## Proving the Limits

Extensive experiments on LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B validate LongRoPE's effectiveness. The method maintains low perplexity from 4k all the way to 2048k. On passkey retrieval tasks—finding a hidden five-digit number in a sea of irrelevant text—LongRoPE maintains over 90% accuracy up to 2048k. Crucially, the models retain their accuracy on standard benchmarks designed for 4096-token contexts, proving that the extension does not degrade original capabilities.

By overcoming the traditional hurdles of data scarcity and massive computational costs, LongRoPE not only shatters the 2-million-token barrier but also paves the way for a new era of long-context LLM applications.



# The Secret Sauce of LongRoPE: Non-Uniform Positional Interpolation

## The Context Window Bottleneck

Large Language Models (LLMs) are powerful, but they often hit a wall when it comes to the context window—the maximum number of tokens they can process at once. For example, LLaMA2 originally had a 4096 token limit. Pushing beyond this limit usually requires fine-tuning on extremely long texts, which is expensive and computationally prohibitive, especially when scaling to millions of tokens. Furthermore, introducing new, untrained token positions leads to out-of-distribution issues that cause the model's performance to degrade. 

To break through this barrier, the LongRoPE paper introduces a method to extend LLM context windows to an impressive 2,048,000 tokens. But how does it achieve this? The first major technical innovation behind LongRoPE's success is its clever handling of non-uniformities in positional interpolation.

## A Quick Primer on RoPE and Interpolation

Transformer models need to know the order of tokens, which is typically handled by position embeddings. LongRoPE focuses on Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), widely used in modern LLMs. For a token at position $n$, its RoPE encoding uses rotary angles defined by $n\theta_i$, where $\theta_i = \theta^{-2i/d}$, $d$ is the embedding dimension, and the default base value $\theta$ is 10000.

When we want to extend the context window from an original length $L$ to a longer length $L'$, we use an extension ratio $s = \frac{L'}{L}$. To prevent the model from seeing completely new position indices that it wasn't trained on, methods like Position Interpolation (PI) linearly scale down the rotation angles by the ratio $s$. 

The problem with PI is that it makes the position information very "crowded." The model struggles to distinguish between closely positioned tokens. Other methods, like NTK and YaRN, attempt to distribute this scaling pressure differently across RoPE dimensions (frequencies) instead of applying a uniform linear scale. However, these methods rely on human-designed rules that don't fully capture the complex non-uniformities present in the Transformer architecture, leading to information loss.

## Discovering Two Key Non-Uniformities

Through empirical analysis, LongRoPE uncovers two critical forms of non-uniformities that existing methods miss:

1. **Dimension Non-Uniformity:** Different RoPE dimensions require different scaling factors. Lower RoPE dimensions (which have higher frequencies) and higher dimensions (lower frequencies) do not need to be scaled uniformly. Existing methods apply broad rules that fail to preserve the original RoPE information effectively across all dimensions.
2. **Token Position Non-Uniformity:** The initial tokens in an input sequence (the first $\hat{n}$ tokens) are heavily attended to by the model and are crucial for the attention layers. LongRoPE finds that these initial tokens should be extrapolated with less interpolation—in some cases, using the original RoPE without any scaling. The optimal number of these starting tokens, $\hat{n}$, depends on the target extension length.

## The Evolutionary Search Algorithm

To fully exploit these two non-uniformities, LongRoPE frames the problem as a search for the optimal rescale factors, denoted as $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})$, for each RoPE dimension $i$. For tokens at positions $n < \hat{n}$, the original RoPE is used. For positions $n \ge \hat{n}$, a specific rescale factor $\hat{\lambda}_i$ is applied.

The search space for these factors is massive. For a $4\times$ extension, there are $400^{128/2} \times 14$ possible choices. To navigate this efficiently, LongRoPE uses an evolutionary search algorithm guided by perplexity (a measure of how well the model predicts the text). The search incorporates two key optimizations to boost efficiency:

- **Optimized Initial Population:** Instead of starting the search randomly, the algorithm seeds the initial population with the known scaling configurations of PI, NTK, and YaRN. The rest of the initial population is generated by randomly mutating these known methods.
- **Monotonically Non-Decreasing Constraint:** To avoid evaluating poor solutions, the search enforces a monotonically non-decreasing constraint on the rescale factors: $\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}$. This means that as the RoPE dimension increases, the rescale factor must not decrease. This is based on the NTK theory, which suggests that lower dimensions with higher frequencies need less interpolation, while higher dimensions with lower frequencies can handle more.

## 8x Extension Without Fine-Tuning

By identifying and applying these non-uniform rescale factors, LongRoPE minimizes the information loss caused by positional interpolation. This provides a much better starting point for the model.

The results are striking. Without any fine-tuning, LongRoPE's evolutionary search can extend LLaMA2's context window from 4k to 32k—an $8\times$ extension. In contrast, existing methods like PI, NTK, and YaRN see perplexity spike after just a $2\times$ extension in non-fine-tuned scenarios.

## Conclusion

The secret sauce of LongRoPE lies in its deep understanding of non-uniformities in positional interpolation. By recognizing that different RoPE dimensions and different token positions require unique scaling strategies, and by using an evolutionary search to find the optimal configuration, LongRoPE achieves substantial context window extensions without the need for computationally expensive fine-tuning. This non-uniform interpolation serves as the foundational step that enables LongRoPE's progressive extension strategy to eventually reach a 2 million token context window.



# How to Train for 2 Million Tokens Without Actually Using 2 Million Tokens

Large language models are powerful, but they come with a frustrating limitation: their context window size. LLaMA2, for instance, was pre-trained with a 4096-token limit. Once you feed it more tokens than that, performance degrades because the model encounters position indices it has never seen during training. This is a serious bottleneck for applications like in-context learning with many examples or complex LLM agents that need to process long documents.

Recent work has pushed context windows to around 128k tokens through fine-tuning on longer texts. But going beyond that—say, to 2 million tokens—runs into three serious obstacles. First, when extending from 4k to over 1000k, more than 90% of position indices are new and untrained, introducing catastrophic values that make fine-tuning difficult to converge. Second, there simply aren't enough texts longer than a million tokens to fine-tune on, and training on such lengths would be prohibitively expensive in GPU hours. Third, when you spread attention across an enormous number of token positions, it gets dispersed and diluted, degrading performance even on the original short context window.

A recent paper from Microsoft called **LongRoPE** tackles all three of these challenges and achieves something remarkable: extending pre-trained LLM context windows to 2,048,000 tokens. The secret sauce is a clever combination of non-uniform positional interpolation and a progressive extension strategy that avoids the need for million-token training texts entirely.

## The Background: RoPE and Positional Interpolation

To understand how LongRoPE works, we need to talk about Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), the position encoding used in modern LLMs like LLaMA2 and Mistral. For a token at position $n$, its RoPE encoding involves a series of rotation angles:

$$[cos(n\theta_0), sin(n\theta_0), cos(n\theta_1), \cdot\cdot\cdot, cos(n\theta_{d/2-1}), sin(n\theta_{d/2-1})]$$

where $d$ is the embedding dimension and $\theta_i = \theta^{-2i/d}$ with a default base value of $\theta = 10000$.

When we want to extend the context window from length $L$ to $L'$, the extension ratio is $s = L'/L$. Positional interpolation methods work by downscaling the rotation frequencies so that new position indices map back into the range the model was originally trained on.

The simplest approach, **Position Interpolation (PI)**, just linearly divides all position indices by $s$. This works, but it makes position information very "crowded"—the model struggles to distinguish between closely positioned tokens, and performance suffers at high extension ratios.

Other methods like **NTK** and **YaRN** introduce some non-linearity by treating different RoPE dimensions differently. NTK scales lower (high-frequency) dimensions less and higher (low-frequency) dimensions more. YaRN divides RoPE dimensions into three frequency-based groups and applies different interpolation strategies to each. These methods help, but they still rely on human-designed rules that don't fully capture the complexity of how positional information behaves in transformers.

## Two Key Non-Uniformities

LongRoPE starts with a key insight: there are two forms of non-uniformity in positional interpolation that existing methods fail to fully exploit.

**Non-uniformity across RoPE dimensions.** Different dimensions of the RoPE embedding should be interpolated by different amounts, and the optimal factors depend on the target extension length. Through evolutionary search guided by perplexity, LongRoPE discovers that the optimal rescale factors for each dimension are non-uniform and differ significantly from the fixed rules used by PI, NTK, or YaRN.

**Non-uniformity across token positions.** The initial tokens in an input sequence receive large attention scores and are crucial to the attention layers. LongRoPE finds that these initial tokens should be extrapolated with less interpolation—in fact, the first $\hat{n}$ tokens should use the original RoPE embedding without any interpolation at all. The optimal value of $\hat{n}$ depends on the target extension length.

These two non-uniformities are captured through a set of rescale factors $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i, \hat{n})$, where $\hat{\lambda}_i$ is the rescale factor for the $i^{th}$ RoPE dimension and $\hat{n}$ is a token position threshold:

$$\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i, \hat{n}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } n < \hat{n} \\ \frac{1}{\lambda_i} & \text{if } n \geq \hat{n} \end{cases}$$

Finding the optimal set of rescale factors is a massive search problem. For a 4× extension, the search space contains roughly $4 \times 10^{167}$ possible configurations. LongRoPE uses an evolutionary search algorithm with two key optimizations to make this tractable:

- It seeds the initial population with known good solutions (PI, NTK, and YaRN configurations) rather than starting randomly.
- It enforces a monotonically non-decreasing constraint: $\lambda_i \leq \lambda_{i+1}$, based on the NTK theory that lower dimensions with higher frequencies need less interpolation. This dramatically reduces the number of candidates that need expensive perplexity evaluations.

The result of this search is powerful enough to achieve an 8× context extension **without any fine-tuning at all**—extending LLaMA2 from 4k to 32k while existing methods like PI, NTK, and YaRN see perplexity spike after just 2×.

## The Progressive Extension Strategy

Here's where things get really clever. An 8× no-fine-tuning extension is impressive, but 2048k requires a 512× extension from the original 4k. That's way beyond what the search alone can handle. Fine-tuning is necessary, but fine-tuning directly on 2048k-token texts is practically impossible—such texts barely exist, and the compute cost would be staggering.

LongRoPE solves this with a progressive, stepping-stone strategy:

**Step 1: Extend the pre-trained LLM to 256k with LongRoPE search.** Starting from LLaMA2's 4k context window, the search algorithm finds optimal rescale factors for target lengths of 128k and 256k (extension ratios of 32× and 64×, respectively).

**Step 2: Fine-tune to 256k.** Rather than jumping straight to 256k, the fine-tuning is itself progressive. First, the model is fine-tuned for 400 steps using the 128k rescale factors. Then, the rescale factors are swapped for the 256k configuration, and training continues for another 600 steps on the same checkpoint. This two-stage approach turns out to be more efficient than fine-tuning directly to 256k. Importantly, all of this training uses texts chunked to 128k or 256k lengths—nothing approaching 2 million tokens.

**Step 3: Extend the fine-tuned 256k model to 2048k with a second LongRoPE search.** This is the key leap. Because LongRoPE's non-uniform positional interpolation can achieve an 8× extension without fine-tuning, the already fine-tuned 256k model can be pushed to 2048k (an 8× jump from 256k) with just another search—no additional fine-tuning required.

The genius here is that the 512× total extension is decomposed into a 64× extension achieved through fine-tuning (at a manageable 256k length) and an 8× extension achieved through the search alone. The model never needs to see a 2-million-token document during training.

## Recovering Short Context Performance

There's one more problem to solve. When you extend the context window by 512×, the positional interpolation squeezes the original 4k positions into a much narrower region. This crowding degrades performance on short inputs—the model gets worse at the very thing it was originally good at.

LongRoPE addresses this by performing an additional evolutionary search to find a separate set of rescale factors specifically optimized for short context lengths (4k and 8k). The maximum allowed rescale factor is reduced to encourage less interpolation at these shorter lengths. During inference, the model dynamically switches between the short-context and long-context rescale factors depending on the input sequence length.

## Does It Actually Work?

The experimental results are compelling across multiple dimensions:

- **Perplexity on long documents:** LongRoPE-2048k models show a consistent decreasing perplexity trend from 4k to 256k on PG19 and Proof-pile datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines even within 256k. On the Books3 dataset with documents exceeding 2048k, LLaMA2 maintains reasonable perplexity across the full 2-million-token range.

- **Passkey retrieval:** LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k maintains over 90% accuracy in retrieving a hidden five-digit passkey from anywhere within the 4k to 2048k context window. Existing models drop to 0% accuracy beyond 128k.

- **Standard benchmarks:** On the Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA), LongRoPE-2048k models achieve results comparable to the original models within their 4096-token context window, with LongRoPE-Mistral even outperforming the original Mistral on TruthfulQA by 0.5%.

## Why This Matters

LongRoPE's progressive extension strategy is a practical demonstration that you don't need to train on million-token texts to handle million-token contexts. By decomposing the problem into a fine-tuneable intermediate step and a search-based final leap, the method sidesteps the fundamental scarcity of ultra-long training data and the prohibitive cost of training on it.

The approach is also notably lightweight. Models extended via LongRoPE retain their original architecture with only minor modifications to the positional embedding, meaning most pre-existing optimizations and tooling can be reused. The method works on any RoPE-based LLM, and the code is available at [https://github.com/microsoft/LongRoPE](https://github.com/microsoft/LongRoPE).

As LLMs continue to be applied to increasingly complex tasks—analyzing entire codebases, processing long legal documents, conducting deep research across many sources—the ability to work with context windows in the millions rather than thousands will become essential. LongRoPE shows a path to get there without waiting for impossibly long training datasets to materialize.



# The Balancing Act: Maintaining Short-Context Performance in Super-Sized LLMs

## The Hidden Cost of Going Long

When you extend an LLM's context window from 4k tokens to 2 million, you might expect the model to simply gain a new superpower: the ability to process book-length documents in a single pass. But there's a catch. Push a model's context window to 500× its original size, and something unsettling happens—it starts forgetting how to handle the short, everyday tasks it was originally good at.

This isn't a minor regression. It's a fundamental tension in positional interpolation, the technique used to stretch context windows beyond their training limits. As LongRoPE's authors put it plainly: when you apply a $512\times$ extension ratio, "positions within the original 4k context window become particularly crowded."

## Why Stretching Breaks Short Contexts

To understand why, we need to look at how positional interpolation actually works. Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) encodes each token's position as a rotation angle. When we extend the context window, we rescale these angles—essentially compressing them so that new, far-away positions map back into the range the model was originally trained on.

The problem is that this compression doesn't just affect the new positions. It affects *all* positions, including the original 4k ones. Imagine taking a ruler that measures 4 inches and relabeling it to measure 2,048 inches. The first 4 inches are still there, but now they're crammed into a tiny fraction of the ruler's total range. The model can no longer distinguish between closely positioned tokens as effectively, because the positional information that used to span the full range is now squeezed into a narrow sliver.

This is a known issue with positional interpolation, and it gets worse the further you stretch. The model's attention mechanism, already spread thin across millions of token positions, loses the fine-grained positional resolution it needs for shorter tasks.

## LongRoPE's Dynamic Solution

LongRoPE tackles this problem with an elegantly simple idea: use different positional embedding factors for different context lengths during inference.

The approach works as follows. After extending the model to 2048k tokens, the researchers perform an additional evolutionary search to find optimized RoPE rescale factors specifically for short context lengths (4k and 8k). These short-context factors are designed to encourage *less* interpolation—they reduce the maximum allowed rescale values, since shorter lengths don't need aggressive compression.

During inference, the model dynamically switches between these factor sets based on the actual input length. If the sequence is under 8k tokens, the model uses the short-context factors. For longer sequences, it falls back to the 2048k-optimized factors. This means the same model can handle a 2-million-token document and a 4,000-token prompt without compromise on either end.

## Does It Actually Work?

The paper provides clear evidence that this recovery mechanism is effective. The researchers evaluated LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k on standard LLM benchmarks within the original 4096-token context window, including:

- 25-shot ARC-Challenge
- 10-shot HellaSwag
- 5-shot MMLU
- 0-shot TruthfulQA

The results show that the extended models achieve accuracy comparable to the original models on these benchmarks. In one case, LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k even outperformed the original Mistral on TruthfulQA by +0.5%.

An ablation study confirms the value of this readjustment. When comparing perplexity on the Proof-pile dataset at 4k and 8k lengths, the recovered model shows significant improvement over the version without short-context readjustment. The average benchmark accuracy also improves measurably.

## Why This Matters

The short-context recovery feature is more than a nice-to-have. It addresses a practical concern that anyone deploying long-context LLMs would face. In real applications, a model that can ingest a million tokens is useless if it simultaneously degrades on the kinds of short prompts that make up the vast majority of real-world usage.

By dynamically adjusting positional embeddings based on input length, LongRoPE ensures that extending a context window is purely additive—you gain the ability to process longer sequences without losing anything you already had. This is the kind of design decision that makes the difference between a research curiosity and a practically deployable model.

The broader implication is that context window extension doesn't have to be a zero-sum game. With the right interpolation strategy—and the flexibility to use different strategies for different length regimes—we can have our long-context cake and eat our short-context performance too.
