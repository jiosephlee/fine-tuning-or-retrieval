\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Why does extending the context window to 2048k degrade performance on the original short context window?
Question:
The LongRoPE paper mentions that after extending to 2048k context window, there is a performance drop within the original context window (e.g., 4k-8k). They attribute this to positional interpolation forcing 'position embedding in higher dimensions within the original context window to reside in a much narrower region.' I don't understand this explanation. With a $512\times$ extension ratio, why would positions within the original 4k window become 'crowded'? Intuitively, I would think that positions 0-4096 would just map to smaller rotation angles, but why does this hurt performance? Can someone explain the mechanism behind this degradation more clearly?
Answer:
The key to understanding this degradation is to look at what positional interpolation does to the **rotation angles across different RoPE dimensions**, particularly the high (low-frequency) dimensions, within the original short context window.

In RoPE, each dimension $i$ rotates with frequency $\theta_i = \theta^{-2i/d}$. Lower dimensions (small $i$) have high frequencies, meaning their rotation angles change rapidly with position — they can distinguish adjacent tokens easily. Higher dimensions (large $i$) have low frequencies, so their rotation angles change slowly — they encode coarse, long-range position information. For the highest dimension with the default base $\theta = 10000$, the frequency is roughly $1/10000$, so even at position $n = 4096$, the rotation angle is only about $4096/10000 \approx 0.41$ radians — less than a quarter of a full circle.

Now apply Position Interpolation (PI) with extension ratio $s = 512$. PI divides every position index by $s$, so the rotation angle for dimension $i$ at position $n$ becomes $\frac{n}{s \cdot \theta^{2i/d}}$. For that highest dimension, the angle at position $4096$ shrinks to roughly $4096 / (512 \times 10000) \approx 0.0008$ radians. This is essentially zero — meaning **every token position from $0$ to $4096$ now produces nearly the identical rotation angle in the high dimensions**. The position embeddings for all tokens in the original window have been "crowded" into a tiny arc near angle $0$ on those dimensions' circles.

This matters because the model was pretrained expecting the *full* RoPE encoding to carry position information across all dimensions. The high (low-frequency) dimensions encoded coarse relative position, and the model learned to rely on that signal. After $512\times$ interpolation, those dimensions are effectively "frozen" near zero for any sequence within the original $4k$ window — they contribute almost no discriminative position information at all. The low (high-frequency) dimensions still distinguish positions fine (their angles were large to begin with, so even divided by $512$ they retain enough spread), but the model has lost the contribution from the high-dimensional part of the encoding it was trained to use. In the attention mechanism, where relative position is computed through the interaction of rotated query and key vectors across all dimensions, this effectively "turns off" position sensitivity in half the RoPE dimensions for short sequences.

So the crowding is not about positions $0$--$4096$ being mapped to smaller angles in general — it is specifically that the **high dimensions, which already had small angular spread within the original window, get squeezed into a negligibly narrow region where all positions become indistinguishable**. The model can no longer use those dimensions to tell tokens apart or to model relative distances, which degrades its language modeling performance on short contexts.

This is exactly why LongRoPE's recovery step works: it performs a *new* evolutionary search for rescale factors $\lambda_i$ targeted at $4k$ and $8k$ lengths, with a reduced maximum allowed $\lambda$ (encouraging less interpolation). This lets the high dimensions keep more of their original angular spread for short sequences, restoring the position information that $512\times$ interpolation had destroyed. During inference, the model dynamically swaps in these short-context rescale factors whenever the sequence length is under $8k$, while using the $2048k$ factors for longer inputs.
