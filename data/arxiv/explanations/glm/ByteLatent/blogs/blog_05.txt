\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

# From Llama 3 to BLT: How to 'Byte-ify' Your Existing Language Models

Tokenization has long been a foundational component of large language models (LLMs), serving as a heuristic pre-processing step that groups raw bytes into a static set of tokens. While this approach has driven the success of models like Llama 3, it introduces inherent biases and limitations. Tokenizers are sensitive to input noise, lack orthographic knowledge, and often struggle with multilingual equity. The Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) offers a compelling alternative: a tokenizer-free architecture that learns directly from raw bytes and dynamically groups them into "patches" based on entropy. 

However, training a new model architecture from scratch at scale is computationally prohibitive. To address this, the BLT paper introduces a practical method for transitioning existing models to this new architecture. By initializing a BLT model with pre-trained weights from a traditional token-based model like Llama 3, researchers can take a faster, more compute-efficient path to tokenizer-free AI.

## The BLT Architecture: A Quick Overview

To understand how to "byte-ify" an existing model, it helps to understand the three components of the BLT architecture:

- **Latent Global Transformer:** A large autoregressive transformer that operates on patch representations. This component consumes the bulk of the computational resources during both training and inference.
- **Local Encoder:** A lightweight transformer that maps input bytes into patch representations, using cross-attention to pool information.
- **Local Decoder:** Another lightweight transformer that decodes patch representations back into raw bytes.

The key insight is that the Latent Global Transformer performs the heavy lifting that is analogous to the main transformer body in a traditional LLM. This structural similarity opens the door to weight initialization from existing models.

## The 'Byte-ification' Process

Instead of training the entire BLT model from scratch, the researchers explore a workflow where the global transformer parameters of BLT are initialized using the pre-trained weights of a Llama 3.1 model. This approach effectively transforms a tokenizer-based model into a tokenizer-free one.

The training process relies on a differential learning rate strategy to preserve the knowledge captured in the pre-trained weights while allowing the new architecture to adapt:

- The global transformer, initialized from Llama 3.1, is updated using a learning rate that is $\frac{1}{10}$ of the standard rate.
- The local encoder and local decoder, which are unique to the BLT architecture and must learn to process raw bytes, are trained with the standard learning rate.

The models are trained for the optimal number of steps as determined for Llama 3.

## Performance and Trade-offs

The benefits of this initialization strategy are significant. A BLT model initialized from Llama 3.1 (referred to as "BLT from Llama 3.1") significantly outperforms both the baseline Llama 3 and a from-scratch BLT model, all trained with the same number of floating-point operations (flops). It even outperforms a BLT-Entropy model trained on a significantly larger dataset of 1 trillion tokens on the MMLU benchmark, suggesting that this is an effective method for reducing training compute.

When compared directly to the original Llama 3.1 model—which was trained on 15 trillion tokens—the byte-ified version experiences a slight performance decline on MMLU and HumanEval, with more significant drops on other tasks. This indicates that while the method is highly promising for reducing compute costs, further work is needed to fully leverage the pre-trained model and optimize the transition.

## The Path Forward

The ability to initialize a BLT model with existing pre-trained weights offers a practical bridge between the current tokenization-based paradigm and a future of tokenizer-free models. By reusing the heavy lifting already done by models like Llama 3.1, researchers can bypass the need to train the global transformer from scratch, focusing compute on learning the byte-level encoder and decoder modules. This "byte-ification" process represents a significant step toward adopting more robust, efficient, and adaptable language model architectures.
