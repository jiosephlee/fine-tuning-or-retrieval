\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

# Less Infrastructure, More Intelligence: How GSPO Could Simplify the AI Training Pipeline

When scaling reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs), the spotlight usually falls on algorithmic improvements, reward design, or model architecture. However, the underlying plumbing—the RL infrastructure—often dictates what is practically possible. A major infrastructural bottleneck arises from numerical precision discrepancies between training engines (like Megatron) and inference engines (like vLLM or SGLang). 

Recently introduced alongside the latest Qwen3 models, Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) is a new RL algorithm that not only improves training stability and performance but also has the potential to dramatically simplify this infrastructure overhead.

## The Hidden Cost of RL Training: Precision Discrepancies

In algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), policy updates rely on token-level importance ratios. For a token $y_t$ generated by the old policy $\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}$, the importance ratio is calculated as $w_{t}(\theta) = \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{t} | x, y_{<t}) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_{t} | x,y_{<t})}$. Because token-level probabilities are highly sensitive, even minute precision differences between the inference engine that generated the tokens and the training engine computing the gradients can introduce significant noise into the optimization process.

To mitigate this, practitioners typically have to use the training engine to recompute the likelihoods of sampled responses under the old policy $\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}$. This recomputation step is computationally expensive and severely complicates the training pipeline, especially in distributed environments.

## Enter GSPO: Sequence-Level Optimization

GSPO fundamentally rethinks how importance ratios are calculated. Instead of applying importance weights at the token level, GSPO defines the importance ratio based on sequence likelihood. 

Specifically, GSPO calculates the importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ for a response $y_i$ as:

$$s_{i}(\theta) = \left( \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_i | x) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \log \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_{i,t} | x,y_{i,<t})} \right)$$

By taking the geometric mean of token-level ratios, GSPO effectively averages out the variance. The length normalization ensures that the importance ratio remains within a unified numerical range regardless of response length.

## Streamlining the Infrastructure

Because GSPO operates on sequence-level likelihoods, it is much more tolerant of the minor numerical precision discrepancies between training and inference engines. The averaging effect of the sequence likelihood acts as a buffer against the extreme fluctuations that can occur at the individual token level.

This robustness has a profound implication for RL infrastructure: it makes it possible to directly use the likelihoods returned by the inference engine for optimization, thereby avoiding the need for recomputation with the training engine entirely.

By eliminating this recomputation step, GSPO removes a significant computational and logistical burden. This streamlining is particularly beneficial for:
- **Partial rollout**: Efficiently managing and updating policies without a full synchronous recomputation pass on the training side.
- **Multi-turn RL**: Reducing the overhead of calculating likelihoods for long, multi-turn interactions where inference and training engines might frequently drift.
- **Training-inference disaggregated frameworks**: Allowing a cleaner separation between generation and training, since the training engine no longer needs to redundantly "double-check" the inference engine's probability calculations.

## A Leaner Path to Intelligence

While the paper highlights GSPO's superior training stability and performance—particularly its ability to stabilize Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) RL training without complex workarounds like Routing Replay—its infrastructural benefits are equally compelling. 

By aligning the unit of optimization with the unit of reward (the sequence), GSPO not only fixes the theoretical misapplication of importance sampling found in token-level methods but also clears the way for leaner, simpler RL pipelines. As the AI community continues to scale RL to unlock deeper reasoning capabilities, algorithms like GSPO ensure that we spend less time fighting infrastructure overhead and more time scaling intelligence.
