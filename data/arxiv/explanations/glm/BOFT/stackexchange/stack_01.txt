\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why does preserving pairwise angles between neurons help maintain pretraining knowledge?
Question:
I'm reading the BOFT paper and they mention that OFT (Orthogonal Finetuning) transforms neurons by an orthogonal matrix to "preserve the pairwise angles between neurons." I understand that orthogonal matrices preserve dot products mathematically, but I'm confused about why this is important for finetuning. The paper says this preserves "semantic knowledge from pretraining" but doesn't elaborate. Could someone explain the intuition behind why preserving angles between neurons (i.e., columns of the weight matrix $W^0$) helps maintain pretraining knowledge? Is there a specific property of neural network weights that makes angular relationships particularly meaningful?
Answer:
The key insight is that in a pretrained neural network, the geometric arrangement of neurons in feature space encodes semantic relationships learned during pretraining. When we view each column (neuron) of the weight matrix $W^0$ as a direction in the input feature space, the forward pass $z = (W^0)^\top x$ computes how well the input $x$ aligns with each of these directions. The pairwise angles between these directions encode how the model represents the relationships between different concepts — neurons detecting semantically related features will have smaller angles between them, while neurons detecting unrelated features will be closer to orthogonal.

When OFT applies an orthogonal matrix $R$ to transform the weights as $RW^0$, the angle between any two neurons $w_i$ and $w_j$ (columns of $W^0$) is preserved because

$$\cos\theta'_{ij} = \frac{(Rw_i)^\top (Rw_j)}{\|Rw_i\|\|Rw_j\|} = \frac{w_i^\top R^\top R w_j}{\|w_i\|\|w_j\|} = \frac{w_i^\top w_j}{\|w_i\|\|w_j\|} = \cos\theta_{ij}.$$

This means the finetuned model operates in the same representational geometry as the pretrained model — the same concepts are still related in the same way, just viewed from a rotated (or reflected) perspective. You can think of this as rotating a coordinate system without stretching any axis: the underlying space and all relationships within it remain intact. In contrast, unconstrained finetuning (as in full finetuning or LoRA's additive update $W^0 + BA$) can arbitrarily distort these angles, potentially collapsing the carefully learned representational diversity — two neurons that were positioned to detect distinct concepts might drift toward similar directions, destroying semantic distinctions the model worked hard to learn during pretraining.

The paper connects this to work on hyperspherical energy and generalization (citing Liu et al., 2018, 2021), which studies how the distribution of neurons on the unit hypersphere — characterized precisely by their pairwise angles — relates to a model's generalization ability. A well-spread, diverse arrangement of neurons on the hypersphere tends to yield better generalization, and by preserving all pairwise angles, orthogonal finetuning maintains this distribution throughout adaptation. Additionally, the paper notes that orthogonal transformations preserve the spectral norm of $W^0$ (since singular values are unchanged by left-multiplication with an orthogonal matrix), which further benefits training stability and generalization, as established in the spectral normalization literature.

The bilinear interpretation the paper provides also helps build intuition: the finetuned forward pass $z_i = w_i^\top R x$ can be read as learning a new "similarity metric" $R$ through which the input is viewed, while the neurons $w_i$ themselves retain their pretrained relationships. This is analogous to distance metric learning, where you learn a transformation of the input space without changing the fundamental structure of the reference points. The orthogonal constraint ensures that this transformation is "gentle" — it can rotate and reflect but never stretch or compress the space, keeping the pretrained knowledge geometrically intact while still allowing meaningful adaptation to downstream tasks.
