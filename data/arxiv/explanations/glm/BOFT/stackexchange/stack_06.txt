\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### How orthogonality creates dependencies among edges

Orthogonality imposes that rows and columns are orthonormal, which couples entries that would otherwise be independent. The paper illustrates this with a $2 \times 2$ orthogonal matrix. Consider:

$$\bm{B} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$$

Orthogonality requires $a^2 + b^2 = 1$ (row 1 norm), $c^2 + d^2 = 1$ (row 2 norm), and $ac + bd = 0$ (rows orthogonal). If we set $a = 0$ (removing one edge), then $b^2 = 1$, so $b = \pm 1$. The orthogonality condition $ac + bd = 0$ becomes $bd = 0$, and since $b \neq 0$, we must have $d = 0$. Thus, removing the edge at position $(1,1)$ *forces* the removal of the edge at position $(2,2)$. The matrix collapses to a signed permutation matrix:

$$\bm{B} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \pm 1 \\ \pm 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

This is the "additional dependency among edges" the paper describes: one missing edge implies another missing edge. More generally, the orthogonality constraint means the sparsity pattern of each factor $\bm{B}_i$ cannot be chosen arbitrarily — specifying some entries as zero propagates constraints to other entries, potentially adding or removing edges to maintain orthonormality. This is why the paper notes that "for each feasible set of edge connections, the orthogonality may sometimes add or remove some edges," making the counting of free edges subtler than simply tallying non-zero entries in an unconstrained matrix.
