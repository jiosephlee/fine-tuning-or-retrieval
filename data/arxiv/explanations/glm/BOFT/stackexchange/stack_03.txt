\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### What does the information transmission framework's "free edges" concept mean?
Question:
The BOFT paper introduces an "information transmission" framework where generating a dense matrix $R = B_m B_{m-1} \cdots B_1$ is viewed as transmitting information on a grid with $d \times (m+1)$ nodes. They identify two desiderata: (i) dense connectivity and (ii) minimum free edges.

I'm confused about what exactly counts as a "free edge" versus a constrained one. The paper says: "for a $d \times d$ orthogonal with $d$ non-zero entries, these entries can only be $\pm 1$" and that these should not be counted as trainable. Why can these entries only be $\pm 1$? And how does the orthogonality constraint create "additional dependency among edges" such that "one missing edge could imply another missing edge"?
Answer:
## Understanding "Free Edges" in the BOFT Information Transmission Framework

The concept of "free edges" in the BOFT paper refers to edges (i.e., non-zero matrix entries) that correspond to *continuously trainable parameters*, as opposed to edges that are structurally forced by the orthogonality constraint and whose values are fixed to $\pm 1$.
