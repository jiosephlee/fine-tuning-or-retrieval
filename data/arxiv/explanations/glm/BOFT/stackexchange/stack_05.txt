\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### What makes an edge "free"

A *free edge* is any edge beyond these $d$ structurally necessary bijection edges. Free edges correspond to matrix entries that can be continuously varied (subject to orthogonality) and thus represent trainable parameters. The desideratum of "minimum free edges" asks: among all factorizations $\bm{R} = \bm{B}_m \cdots \bm{B}_1$ that achieve dense connectivity, which one uses the fewest such trainable entries? This is why the butterfly structure is attractive — it achieves dense connectivity with $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ total edges, of which only the "free" ones count toward the trainable parameter budget.
