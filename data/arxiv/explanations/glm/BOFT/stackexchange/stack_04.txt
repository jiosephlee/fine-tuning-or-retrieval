\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why a $d \times d$ orthogonal matrix with $d$ non-zero entries can only have $\pm 1$ values

The key insight is that an orthogonal matrix with exactly $d$ non-zero entries must have exactly one non-zero entry per row and per column — it is a *signed permutation matrix*. Here is why: orthogonality requires that every column has unit norm. If each column contains exactly one non-zero entry (which is forced when the total count is $d$ across a $d \times d$ matrix), then that single non-zero entry must have magnitude $1$ to satisfy $\|\text{column}_j\|_2 = 1$. The only freedom is the sign, giving values of $\pm 1$. Since sign is discrete (not a continuous parameter), the paper considers these entries as non-trainable. In the information transmission grid, these $d$ edges form a bijection between nodes in adjacent levels, which is the minimum needed for full rank — a necessary condition for orthogonality. They are "not free" because they carry no learnable continuous information; they are structurally determined.
