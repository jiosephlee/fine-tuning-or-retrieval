\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Summary

In short, "free edges" are the continuously trainable non-zero entries in the factorization matrices, excluding the $d$ per-level bijection edges whose values are locked to $\pm 1$ by orthogonality. The orthogonality constraint further entangles edges by creating algebraic dependencies among them, so that the sparsity pattern of an orthogonal matrix is not freely specifiable — setting one entry to zero can force others to zero (or to $\pm 1$), as the $2 \times 2$ example demonstrates. This interplay between structural necessity and algebraic dependency is what makes minimizing free edges a nontrivial design problem, one that the butterfly structure solves efficiently.
