\title{Blogs about the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

# Smaller, Faster, Just as Smart: How BitNet b1.58 Matches Full-Precision AI

As large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in size, their appetite for memory, compute, and energy has become a major bottleneck. Traditional models rely on 16-bit floating-point weights (FP16 or BF16), meaning a massive amount of the computation and memory transfer is spent on floating-point addition and multiplication. But what if we could drastically cut that down without losing any of the model's smarts?

Enter BitNet b1.58, a new 1-bit LLM variant where every single parameter is constrained to just three values: -1, 0, and 1. In the binary system, this ternary setup translates to 1.58 bits. The most exciting part? It matches the performance of full-precision FP16 models like LLaMA, starting at just 3 billion parameters, while being significantly faster and cheaper to run.

## The 1.58-Bit Innovation: Why Zero Matters

The original BitNet architecture introduced 1-bit weights, but BitNet b1.58 makes a crucial modification by adding the value 0 to the mix. While moving from binary $\{-1, 1\}$ to ternary $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ might seem like a small step, it has a profound impact on the model's capabilities.

The inclusion of 0 provides explicit support for feature filtering. When a weight is 0, the model effectively ignores that particular feature, simplifying the computation and improving the model's predictive strength. This gives BitNet b1.58 a stronger modeling capacity compared to the original 1-bit BitNet, allowing it to match full-precision baselines in both perplexity and end-task performance.

## Inside the Quantization Process

To achieve these ternary weights, BitNet b1.58 uses an \emph{absmean} quantization function. The process works by first scaling the weight matrix by its average absolute value. Then, each value is rounded to the nearest integer among the set $\{-1, 0, +1\}$.

Mathematically, the quantized weight $\widetilde{W}$ is computed as:

$$
\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)
$$

Here, the $\mathrm{RoundClip}$ function is defined as:

$$
\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x)))
$$

And the scaling factor $\gamma$ is the average of the absolute values of the weights:

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|
$$

For activations, the model uses an 8-bit quantization approach. Instead of scaling activations to a range of $[0, Q_b]$ before non-linear functions, BitNet b1.58 scales them to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ per token. This removes the need for zero-point quantization, making the implementation simpler and more efficient with negligible impact on performance.

## A LLaMA-like Backbone for Easy Adoption

To make the model easy to integrate into the open-source ecosystem, the authors designed BitNet b1.58 to closely resemble the popular LLaMA architecture. It replaces standard linear layers with \emph{BitLinear} layers and adopts several key LLaMA components:

- RMSNorm for normalization
- SwiGLU for the activation function
- Rotary embeddings
- Removal of all biases

This thoughtful design choice means BitNet b1.58 can be integrated into popular open-source software like Huggingface, vLLM, and llama.cpp with minimal effort.

## Proving It: Benchmark Results

The paper provides compelling evidence that 1.58-bit models can stand toe-to-toe with their FP16 counterparts. The researchers compared BitNet b1.58 against a reproduced FP16 LLaMA LLM, pre-training both on the RedPajama dataset for 100 billion tokens.

The results are striking. BitNet b1.58 matches the full-precision LLaMA LLM in terms of perplexity starting at a 3B model size. When scaled slightly to 3.9B parameters, BitNet b1.58 actually outperforms the LLaMA LLM 3B model in zero-shot accuracy across a range of language tasks, including ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, Hellaswag, Winogrande, PIQA, OpenbookQA, and BoolQ. Crucially, it achieves this while being 2.4 times faster and consuming 3.32 times less memory.

The efficiency gap only widens as the models scale up. At 70B parameters, BitNet b1.58 is 4.1 times faster than the FP16 LLaMA baseline. Because the model only uses integer addition for matrix multiplication—eliminating costly floating-point operations—it saves massive amounts of energy. The authors estimate that BitNet b1.58 reduces arithmetic operation energy consumption by a factor of 71.4 on 7nm chips.

## A New Scaling Law

BitNet b1.58 proves that we don't need to rely on heavy floating-point computations to achieve state-of-the-art LLM performance. By embracing 1.58-bit ternary weights and clever feature filtering, we can build models that are just as smart but dramatically faster, smaller, and more energy-efficient. This presents a new scaling law for LLMs, one where efficiency and performance grow hand-in-hand, opening the door for a new generation of hardware specifically optimized for 1-bit computation.
