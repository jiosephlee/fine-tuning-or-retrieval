\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What is the exact FLOPs accounting used for the forward and backward passes?
Question:
The paper uses 4 * seqlen^2 * head_dim * nheads FLOPs for the forward pass (halved for causal masks) and multiplies by 2.5 for the backward pass. Could someone derive the 4 factor for forward (is it precisely 2 GEMMs counting multiply-add as 2 FLOPs?), and the 2.5 factor for backward (from 5 matmuls vs 2 in forward)? Also, does the FLOPs counting include softmax exp, reductions, and rescalings, or are those excluded as non-GEMM FLOPs?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper’s forward FLOPs formula $4\cdot \text{seqlen}^2\cdot \text{head\_dim}\cdot \text{nheads}$ comes exactly from two dense matrix multiplies per head (each costing $2\cdot N^2\cdot d$ FLOPs if you count a multiply and an add as two FLOPs), and the backward multiplier $2.5$ is the simple ratio $5/2$ because a common backward implementation requires about five GEMMs vs two in the forward. The paper’s reported FLOPs focus on GEMM FLOPs; the softmax exponentials, row-reductions and scaling are lower-order non‑GEMM costs and are not included in that dominant FLOP count (they are discussed separately in the paper as non‑GEMM work that the algorithm hides/overlaps).

Why 4 in the forward (precise derivation). For one head with sequence length $N$ and head dimension $d$ the forward attention does these two dense matmuls:
1) compute the score matrix $\mathbf{S}=\alpha \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$. This is a multiply of an $N\times d$ by a $d\times N$ matrix, costing $2\cdot N\cdot N\cdot d = 2N^2 d$ FLOPs under the standard “multiply + add = 2 FLOPs” convention.
2) compute the output $\mathbf{O}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$. Here $\mathbf{P}$ is $N\times N$ and $\mathbf{V}$ is $N\times d$, so this GEMM also costs $2N^2 d$ FLOPs.

Adding them gives $2N^2 d + 2N^2 d = 4N^2 d$ FLOPs per head. For multi‑head attention multiply by $\text{nheads}$, giving the paper’s expression. For causal (upper‑triangular) masking about half of the $N^2$ entries are unused on average, so dividing by $2$ is the usual approximation used in the paper.

Why 2.5 for backward (intuitive derivation). The backward pass of attention is more GEMM‑heavy. A typical chain of gradient computations (and practical implementations that count GEMMs) involves (roughly):
- $\mathbf{dV} = \mathbf{P}^\top \mathbf{dO}$ — one GEMM,
- $\mathbf{dP} = \mathbf{dO}\,\mathbf{V}^\top$ — one GEMM,
- materializing/working with $\mathbf{dS} = \mathrm{dsoftmax}(\mathbf{dP})$ — the softmax Jacobian acting on $\mathbf{dP}$ can be implemented with reductions and a structured computation that in practice is implemented using additional GEMM-like reductions (e.g., computing the row-wise scalar terms $p^\top \mathbf{dP}$ and broadcasting/multiplying them), and to get $\mathbf{dQ}=\alpha \mathbf{dS}\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{dK}=\alpha \mathbf{dS}^\top\mathbf{Q}$ you need two more GEMMs.

Counting the GEMMs used by a common implementation (including the extra matmuls needed to realize the $(\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top)\mathbf{dP}$ structure efficiently or because of recomputation choices) yields about five GEMMs in total versus two GEMMs in the forward. So the backward GEMM FLOPs are about $5/2$ times the forward GEMM FLOPs; hence the paper multiplies the forward FLOPs by $2.5$ to report backward FLOPs. This is a practical accounting rule of thumb consistent with many implementations and with what the paper states.

What about softmax exp, reductions, rescalings? The paper’s FLOPs expression is the dominant GEMM FLOPs. The softmax-specific work (row-wise max subtraction, exponentials, row-wise sums, scalar rescalings, and the elementwise operations inside the softmax Jacobian) is important in wall‑clock time (and the paper explicitly discusses hiding/overlapping that work with GEMMs), but those operations are not included in the $4\cdot\ldots$ FLOP count. Two reasons the paper does this: (1) these are not large dense matmuls so their FLOP count is much smaller than the GEMM FLOPs for usual head dims and long sequences, and (2) the authors want a clean, comparable GEMM‑based FLOP metric where matmul FLOPs dominate. The paper explicitly calls out that non‑GEMM ops (e.g., exponentials) have far lower hardware throughput and motivates algorithmic work to overlap/hide them, which is why they are treated separately rather than folded into the $4\cdot N^2\cdot d$ term.

Caveats / practical notes. The $4\cdot N^2\cdot d$ and the $2.5$ multiplier are precise under the assumptions outlined above (multiply+add = 2 FLOPs, counting GEMMs as the dominant cost, and using the specific backward decomposition that uses $\sim 5$ GEMMs). Different implementations can change the constants: if you count fused multiply‑add (FMA) as a single FLOP the per‑GEMM FLOP count halves and the numeric factor would be expressed differently; if you avoid some recomputation or exploit other algebraic rearrangements you may reduce the number of GEMMs in the backward; and small sequence lengths or large non‑GEMM softmax cost may make the omitted softmax ops non‑negligible in practice. The paper uses the $4$ and $2.5$ accounting as a practical, implementation‑aware convention (and then separately discusses and optimizes the non‑GEMM softmax cost by overlapping it with GEMMs).
