\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 6: Supervised Instruction Tuning: DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B

# SFT dataset construction (776K examples): English and Chinese coverage
The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset that produces DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is a purpose-built, mixed-language collection of math problems and worked solutions designed to teach the base model to produce step-by-step reasoning and to write programs or tool-using solutions when appropriate. The entire SFT collection contains 776K training examples and intentionally spans both English and Chinese sources so the instruction-tuned model can improve mathematical reasoning across these languages.

For English material, the dataset draws primarily from well-known quantitative-reasoning benchmarks and curated instruction collections. Key sources include GSM8K and MATH, each augmented with tool-integrated solutions (programs and execution-guided answers), and a subset of MathInstruct together with the training split of Lila-OOD where problems are annotated with chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) style solutions. This English portion covers a broad spectrum of mathematical topics—algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, geometry—so that the model learns diverse reasoning patterns and solution formats.

For Chinese material, the SFT set emphasizes K–12 mathematics problems across 76 subtopics (for example, linear equations and other standard school topics). These Chinese examples are annotated in both CoT and tool-integrated reasoning formats. The multilingual choice reflects the paper’s observation that including high-quality non-English math content yields measurable improvements on Chinese benchmarks and avoids the English-centric limitation of many prior math corpora.

The SFT curation process therefore mixes established benchmark problems (used both as training sources and as targets for careful decontamination elsewhere in the pipeline) and human-annotated solutions in multiple formalities. The resulting set of 776K examples aims to provide coverage across difficulty, topic, and format so the supervised stage can meaningfully shape the model’s generation patterns before any reinforcement learning.

# Solution formats: CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated reasoning
The SFT dataset intentionally contains three complementary solution styles, each serving a distinct pedagogical purpose when training an instruction-following model for mathematical reasoning.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT). CoT solutions explicitly expose intermediate reasoning steps in natural language. A CoT example walks through the logical sequence—often algebraic manipulations, numerical reasoning, or stepwise argumentation—leading to the final answer. Training on CoT teaches the model to produce self-contained, human-like explanatory traces and improves its ability to solve multi-step problems without relying on external computation. CoT is the main format for problems where a text justification is the natural target.

Program-of-Thought (PoT). PoT solutions embed algorithmic procedures or short programs (commonly Python) that implement the reasoning. A PoT example shows the model how to convert a math problem into code that computes intermediate values and the final answer. PoT is particularly useful for teaching models to express precise, deterministic procedures and is directly applicable when later enabling tool-based execution (for example, running Python to evaluate expressions or symbolic computations).

Tool-integrated reasoning. For some problems the SFT data includes explicit examples where natural-language steps and program fragments are combined with instructions to call external computation libraries (e.g., Python libraries such as math or sympy). These examples demonstrate an integrated workflow: state the problem, outline the reasoning, write a small program fragment, call the tool to perform hard arithmetic or symbolic work, and present the computed result as the final answer. This format trains the model both to plan when to use tools and to format outputs compatible with program execution or tool APIs.

By including CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated examples, the SFT stage exposes the model to the entire spectrum of reasoning expression: purely textual chains, procedural code-oriented thinking, and hybrid patterns that rely on external computation. This multi-format exposure is crucial for the downstream evaluations that measure performance both when external tools are disallowed and when program execution is permitted.

# Training protocol: batching, context concatenation, hyperparameters
The instruction tuning procedure for DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is concise and deliberately straightforward so that the influence of the SFT dataset itself is clear.

Data preparation and context concatenation. Training examples are concatenated randomly until the concatenated tokens reach the model context limit. The maximum context length used during SFT is 4{,}096 tokens. Random concatenation of examples into a single context helps the model learn to handle long contexts and multiple instruction–response pairs within a session, which is relevant for multi-step or multi-part prompts.

Optimization hyperparameters and schedule. Starting from DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, the SFT stage performs 500 gradient-update steps. Each optimization step uses a batch size of 256 examples and a constant learning rate of 5\times 10^{-5}. The paper reports no additional complicated scheduling (e.g., warmups or decays) for this SFT run; the combination of modest step count, moderate batch size, and steady learning rate is chosen to align the base model to the SFT distribution without catastrophic overwriting.

Training targets. Each example in the batch contains an instruction-like prompt (the problem statement and any context) and a target continuation in one of the CoT/PoT/tool-integrated formats described above. The model is trained to maximize the likelihood of these target continuations (standard supervised cross-entropy on next-token prediction conditioned on the prompt and earlier generated tokens).

These design choices—context concatenation up to 4K tokens, 500 steps, batch 256, and learning rate 5e-5—create an instruction-tuning phase that is lightweight compared to large-scale pre-training but effective at teaching the model to prefer stepwise, tool-aware solution styles.

# Comparative evaluation under no-tool and tool-allowed settings
The paper evaluates DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B under two practical conditions: (1) no external tools allowed (the model must produce a self-contained chain-of-thought or other textual solution), and (2) tool use allowed (the model may write programs or otherwise rely on computation libraries; the program execution result is used to judge correctness when present).

No-tool setting. When external tools are disallowed, problems must be solved entirely within the model’s generated text. Under this setting, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates strong step-by-step reasoning ability. On the competition-level MATH dataset, the paper reports a notable lead over other open-source instruction-tuned models: DeepSeekMath-Instruct exceeds all open-source counterparts and many proprietary models by large margins—citing at least a 9 percentage-point absolute lead on MATH against several competing instruction-tuned or math-enhanced models. This result indicates the SFT dataset and protocol effectively push the model to produce more accurate multi-step textual reasoning.

Tool-allowed setting. When programmatic tool use is permitted and evaluated—i.e., the model can produce a Python program that is executed and the execution result judged—DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B performs even better. The paper reports that, in this setting, the model approaches an accuracy of 60% on MATH, surpassing all existing open-source models. Across other benchmarks where programmatic evaluation is used, DeepSeekMath-Instruct is competitive with much larger models (for example, DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B). The improvement under tool-allowed evaluation shows that SFT examples with PoT and tool-integration teach the model to produce correct, executable procedures that yield correct numerical answers when run.

Taken together, these evaluations show the SFT stage improved both pure text-based chains and programmatic solution styles. This dual capability is important because some competition-style math problems are best solved by careful textual reasoning, while others benefit from exact computation via code.

# Positioning vs. open- and closed-source instruction-tuned models
The paper situates DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B within the landscape of both open-source and closed-source instruction-tuned models, using the two evaluation regimes described above to characterize relative strengths.

Against open-source models. The instruction-tuned DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B surpasses all open-source instruction-tuned models reported in the paper on the competition-level MATH benchmark when tools are disallowed. It also outperforms many open models when programs are allowed—achieving a roughly 60% MATH accuracy that exceeds other open-source offerings and rivals much larger open models. The paper highlights that DeepSeekMath-Instruct bests models that either are larger (for example Qwen 72B) or that have had math-specific RL applied (for example, certain WizardMath variants), indicating that high-quality, multilingual SFT data plus the mathematics-aware pre-training strategy produced a strong instruction-tuned 7B model.

Against closed-source models. Compared with proprietary, large-scale systems, DeepSeekMath-Instruct still trails the very top-tier closed models. The paper reports that DeepSeekMath-Instruct is competitive with some commercial Chinese models (GLM-4, Baichuan-3) on MATH but remains behind the best closed models such as GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra. In other words, the SFT stage brings the open 7B model close to the performance of some larger closed models but not fully to the level of the strongest closed-source systems—there remains a gap, especially on geometry and theorem-proof style problems, where the paper notes weaknesses relative to the best closed models.

Conclusion of positioning. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates that carefully curated SFT data (776K mixed-language examples spanning CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated formats) and a concise, focused tuning protocol can materially elevate a math-specialized 7B model: it outperforms other open instruction-tuned models by significant margins on competition-level benchmarks, becomes competitive with much larger open models, and narrows the gap with several proprietary systems—though the top closed models remain ahead. The SFT stage thus delivers a strong, practical instruction-following math model that serves as a reliable base for further reinforcement learning and deployment in both no-tool and tool-enabled settings.
