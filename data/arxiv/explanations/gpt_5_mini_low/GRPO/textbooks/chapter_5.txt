\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 5: Pretraining the Base Model: DeepSeekMath-Base 7B

# Initialization from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B: why start with code
DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B rather than from a generic language model. The paper reports an empirical observation motivating this choice: models that have undergone substantial code training provide a stronger starting point for subsequent math specialization than general LLMs. Intuitively, code induces several inductive biases that are beneficial for mathematical reasoning. Code is highly structured, precise, and often requires multi-step decomposition of problems into deterministic operations; these characteristics resemble mathematical solution procedures. As a result, a model pretrained on code learns to (1) represent symbolic and structured content robustly, (2) plan multi-step procedures, and (3) generate syntactically constrained outputs. The authors therefore treat a code-pretrained checkpoint as a better prior for math continual training.

Empirically, the authors show that starting from a code-trained model yields improved performance on both program-aided and self-contained math tasks. In later ablations they also study code-vs-general-token curricula and find that code training benefits program-aided math reasoning and accelerates later math fine-tuning. These findings ground the initialization choice for DeepSeekMath-Base: using DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B gives a stronger foundation for math-specialized pretraining while helping the model retain coding ability after further continual training.

# 500B-token continual training mix: 56% DeepSeekMath, 4% AlgebraicStack, 10% arXiv, 20% GitHub code, 10% general CC
The continual pretraining recipe for DeepSeekMath-Base 7B runs for 500B tokens and mixes several data sources to build both deep mathematical reasoning and general capabilities. The reported data composition is: 56% tokens from the DeepSeekMath Corpus (the 120B-token math-focused web corpus constructed from Common Crawl), 4% from AlgebraicStack (math-related code/text), 10% from arXiv papers, 20% from GitHub code, and 10% from natural language Common Crawl (covering both English and Chinese). This mixture is purposefully balanced: the majority is domain-focused math text to drive mathematical reasoning, while a substantial fraction of code tokens preserves and strengthens programmatic skills, and a moderate inclusion of general web text maintains broad language coverage including multilingual content.

The role of each component is explicit in the paper. The DeepSeekMath Corpus provides large-scale, carefully selected mathematical web content (including multilingual pages) and is the chief driver of math ability. AlgebraicStack contributes algebraic examples and code-like mathematical artifacts. The arXiv fraction is included because prior works often include arXiv, though the authors later note that arXiv alone did not meaningfully improve math benchmarks in isolation. GitHub code (20%) reinforces the code inductive bias discussed earlier and helps retain coding performance. Finally, the 10% Common Crawl natural language tokens ensure the model does not overfit to math/code language and retains general understanding, including improved Chinese math performance due to multilingual tokens.

# Optimization and scaling setup differences vs. 1.3B study
DeepSeekMath-Base 7B uses the same general optimization framework as the 1.3B experiments (the DeepSeek-LLM training regime), but with adjustments scaled to a larger model and longer training. The paper follows AdamW with hyperparameters \(\beta_1=0.9\), \(\beta_2=0.95\), and weight decay \(=0.1\). The learning rate schedule is multi-step: the peak learning rate is reached after 2,000 warmup steps, then decreased to 31.6% of peak after 80% of total steps and to 10% of peak after 90% of steps. For the 1.3B experiments they used a peak learning rate of \(5.3\times10^{-4}\) and batch size 4M tokens with 4K context length. For the 7B continual training to 500B tokens, the authors set the maximum learning rate to \(4.2\times10^{-4}\) and increased the batch size to 10M tokens to reflect larger-scale training and throughput. Context length remains large to accommodate chain-of-thought style content.

These adjustments reflect practical scaling choices: slightly lower peak learning rate for stability on a larger model, larger batch size to accelerate token throughput, and retaining the same schedule shape to preserve stable convergence behavior observed in the smaller-scale study. The same decontamination and data filtering practices (10-gram exact matching to remove benchmark contamination) are carried forward into the 7B training to ensure fair evaluation.

# Evaluation without tools: CoT on GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, MMLU-STEM, Gaokao
A key evaluation axis for DeepSeekMath-Base is step-by-step reasoning without external tools, assessed via chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting on multiple English and Chinese benchmarks. The paper evaluates eight benchmarks including GSM8K and the competition-level MATH dataset in English, CMATH in Chinese, MMLU-STEM for STEM multiple-choice questions, and the Gaokao math benchmarks among others. On these assessments, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B leads among open-source base models across all eight selected benchmarks.

Notably, on MATH (a competition-level benchmark), DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforms other open-source base models by over 10 percentage points absolute and even surpasses Minerva 540B (a 540B-parameter closed-source model) despite the large parameter gap. On GSM8K, the base model achieves 64.2% (reported in the paper), demonstrating strong grade-school level quantitative reasoning. The multilingual composition of the pretraining data contributes to improved Chinese math performance (e.g., CMATH), where the model outperforms corpora trained primarily on English data. These results together show that a well-crafted data recipe and starting from a code-initialized checkpoint can produce a compact model with strong self-contained reasoning.

# Evaluation with tools: PoT on GSM8K and MATH
Beyond self-contained CoT reasoning, DeepSeekMath-Base is evaluated on program-of-thought (PoT) style tasks where the model is prompted to produce a Python program that computes the answer and that program is executed. PoT evaluation is performed on GSM8K and MATH with access to libraries such as math and sympy for complex computations. In this setting DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforms prior state-of-the-art open models; the paper reports that it surpasses Llama-derived Llemma 34B on program-aided math tasks.

These program-aided evaluations highlight two aspects of the model: first, the code pretraining and continued inclusion of GitHub tokens preserve and strengthen the model's capability to write correct, executable programs; second, the math-focused pretraining results in the model producing correct algorithmic reasoning and numeric computations when guided to delegate computation to an interpreter. The paper therefore shows that the mixed recipe supports both solution-writing (CoT) and programmatic problem-solving (PoT).

# Formal math: autoformalization on miniF2F with Isabelle
The authors also probe formal-mathematics capabilities via informal-to-formal autoformalization. The task is to transform an informal problem statement and its informal solution into a formal proof in a theorem prover. They evaluate on miniF2F using Isabelle as the target proof assistant. The experimental setup generates formal proof sketches with few-shot prompting; then the off-the-shelf automated prover Sledgehammer is used to fill in missing details.

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B demonstrates strong few-shot autoformalization performance. This indicates that the model can map informal narrative proofs to formal proof constructs sufficiently well for Isabelle and automated tactics to complete the proof. The result provides evidence that the DeepSeekMath pretraining enables representations and generation strategies compatible with formal proof systems, at least in the few-shot autoformalization setting evaluated.

# General abilities: MMLU, BBH, HumanEval, MBPP
While optimized for math, DeepSeekMath-Base retains and improves general reasoning, knowledge, and coding skills. The model is evaluated on MMLU (57 multiple-choice tasks spanning many subjects), BBH (23 challenging tasks requiring multi-step reasoning), and programming benchmarks HumanEval and MBPP. The paper reports that math pretraining improves MMLU and BBH performance relative to the precursor DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, demonstrating that math-specialized data can strengthen general reasoning abilities. For coding, because the continual training mix includes a substantial GitHub code fraction, DeepSeekMath-Base maintains the coding performance of the code-initialized model on HumanEval and MBPP.

Taken together, these evaluations show that the specialized math corpus does not come at the cost of general capabilities; instead, it refines reasoning that transfers across domains while code tokens preserve software-generation skills.

# Key result: small but well-trained model rivaling or beating much larger models (e.g., Minerva 540B)
A central claim of the paper is that DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, despite its modest parameter count, attains competitive or superior performance compared to much larger models when pretraining is carefully engineered. The base model surpasses other open-source base models across multiple math benchmarks and even outperforms Minerva 540B on datasets such as MATH in the self-contained CoT setting. This supports the paper's conclusion that parameter count is not the sole determinant of mathematical reasoning performance: quality and scale of domain-relevant pretraining data, initialization from a code model, and an informed continual training recipe can together enable a small model to reach performance levels previously achieved only by much larger models.

This result has practical implications emphasized in the paper: investing in domain-specific data collection and selection (the DeepSeekMath Corpus), choosing a code-rich initialization, and maintaining a mixed data recipe that balances math, code, formal papers, and general text can yield a compact model with state-of-the-art math reasoning. The remainder of the study proceeds to further boost these capabilities via instruction tuning and the GRPO reinforcement learning stage, but the DeepSeekMath-Base 7B experiments already demonstrate that a carefully engineered pretraining phase is foundational to achieving strong mathematical and general reasoning performance.
