\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### Could DPO be recovered as a limit of GRPO with specific group construction and reward choices?
Question:
You provide a unified paradigm and say these methods are direct or simplified RL. Conceptually, can DPO be seen as a special case of GRPO where groups are constructed as pairs of (preferred, dispreferred) responses, the advantage is proportional to the preference margin, and KL is handled by the DPO objective? Is there a precise derivation mapping the DPO gradient coefficient to a particular instantiation of \(GC_\mathcal{A}(\cdot)\) in Equation (6)?
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — up to some important caveats — DPO can be seen as a special / limiting instantiation of the unified paradigm in Equation (6) (and thus of GRPO viewed in that paradigm) if you (1) make the groups be size $G = 2$ consisting of a preferred and a dispreferred response sampled from the same question, (2) choose rewards that encode the pairwise preference margin, and (3) set the algorithmic processing of those pairwise rewards to reproduce the DPO pairwise coefficient. However this is an equivalence at the level of the gradient-coefficient construction in the unified framework, not an identity of the full algorithms (because DPO and GRPO differ in data sampling (offline vs online), how KL is applied, and objective details).

Explanation and mapping to the paper

The paper writes a unified form of the training gradient as
$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)
= \mathbb{E}_{(q,o)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{1}{|o|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o|}
GC_{\mathcal{A}}(q,o,t,\pi_{rf})\;\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_t\!\mid\!q,o_{<t})\right],
\tag{Equation (6)}
$$
and uses this to compare SFT, RFT, DPO, PPO, GRPO etc. Conceptually DPO is a pairwise, offline method that produces a per-token gradient coefficient which depends only on the pairwise preference signal between two completions for the same prompt. GRPO (as formulated in the paper) produces group-relative advantages by sampling $G$ completions per question, scoring them with a reward model, normalizing within the group, and using those normalized values as per-token advantages within the GRPO surrogate (Eq. (GRPO-objective) in the paper). So to recover DPO you can choose the following ingredients inside that unified form:

1. Data source (make it the DPO style offline pairs). Use the offline sampling regime: for each question $q$ you form a group with exactly two outputs, $o_{\text{pos}}$ (preferred) and $o_{\text{neg}}$ (dispreferred). This corresponds to $G = 2$ and offline sampling from the SFT policy (the paper already classifies DPO as offline in its table).

2. Reward function (turn pairwise preference into a margin). Let the reward-model / rule produce a scalar that encodes the pairwise preference margin. A minimal choice is to assign $r_{\text{pos}} = +m$ and $r_{\text{neg}} = -m$ (or equivalently $r_{\text{pos}} = m$ and $r_{\text{neg}} = 0$ then center/scale), where $m$ is proportional to the model’s confidence or the annotated preference margin. After the group normalization step used by GRPO (subtract mean and divide by std in the group), the two advantages become equal in magnitude and opposite in sign: $\hat A_{\text{pos},t} = +\widetilde r$, $\hat A_{\text{neg},t} = -\widetilde r$. This is exactly the pairwise signal DPO uses (a positive push on preferred token-probabilities and a symmetric negative push on dispreferred ones).

3. Algorithm / GC construction (match DPO’s pairwise coefficient). DPO’s per-token gradient coefficient is a deterministic function of the log-probability difference (or equivalently of the logit difference) between preferred and dispreferred completions and a temperature-like hyperparameter. In the unified form, if you set
$$
GC_{\mathcal{A}}(q,o_{\text{pos}},t,\pi_{rf}) \;=\; +f\big(\Delta\log\pi_\theta(q,\text{pair})\big)
\quad\text{and}\quad
GC_{\mathcal{A}}(q,o_{\text{neg}},t,\pi_{rf}) \;=\; -f\big(\Delta\log\pi_\theta(q,\text{pair})\big),
$$
with $\Delta\log\pi_\theta(q,\text{pair})$ the relevant log-probability difference and $f(\cdot)$ the sigmoid-like transform used in DPO, then the unified gradient matches the DPO pairwise gradient. Intuitively you can view the GRPO group normalization plus choosing a particular mapping from the normalized reward to a coefficient $f$ as reproducing DPO’s pairwise coefficient.

Why this is only a “recovery” and not a strict identity

- DPO is typically implemented as an offline pair-wise loss with a closed-form (or analytically simple) coefficient derived from the DPO objective (see the original DPO paper). Its coefficient depends explicitly on log probability ratios of the two outputs and a DPO temperature / KL scale. GRPO in the paper uses group-relative normalized rewards as advantages and a PPO-style clipped surrogate; to exactly reproduce the DPO coefficient you must replace GRPO’s advantage-processing and surrogate mechanics with the DPO-specific transformation $f(\cdot)$. In other words you can instantiate $GC_{\text{GRPO}}$ to equal $GC_{\text{DPO}}$, but that requires altering the GRPO advantage mapping and possibly removing the GRPO clipping/estimator differences.

- KL handling differs. The paper highlights that GRPO regularizes by adding a direct KL term between the policy and a reference model to the objective (using an unbiased estimator), whereas classic DPO encodes a preference-aware regularizer that can be interpreted as doing its own implicit KL control via the DPO loss derivation. To map exactly, you must set GRPO’s explicit KL coefficient and estimator so that the effective regularization on token probabilities matches the DPO design; otherwise you only get the pairwise gradient shape but a different regularization path.

- Online vs offline sampling. DPO is primarily an offline pairwise method (augmented SFT outputs); GRPO is presented as an online grouped method (sampling many outputs per prompt from the current policy). You can emulate DPO by running GRPO with $G=2$ and using offline-sampled pairs, but then you lose the memory / exploration advantages GRPO is designed to provide.

Concluding practical mapping

So a precise constructive way to see DPO inside the unified form of Eq. (6) is: take groups of size two (preferred, dispreferred) sampled offline, set the reward model outputs so that after group normalization the two advantages are $\pm a$ ($a$ proportional to the preference margin), and choose the algorithmic mapping from that advantage to the per-token gradient coefficient to be the DPO pairwise transform (i.e., a sigmoid-like function of the log-probability ratio and a DPO temperature). Under those choices $GC_{\mathcal{A}}$ in Equation (6) matches the DPO gradient coefficient. But remember this is a mapping at the level of the gradient coefficient; the full algorithms differ in sampling regime, surrogate objective details (clipping, estimator), and KL handling, so GRPO $\to$ DPO is achievable as a limiting / special instantiation inside the paper’s unified paradigm, not as a literal identity of the two algorithm pipelines.
