\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How comparable are the baseline training setups, and could data differences confound the results?
Question:
For Mistral you follow YaRN with 16k training length and Long-Data Collections, while for LLaMA2 you use RedPajama chunks at 128k/256k. Since data and training lengths differ across models and baselines (Together-32k, Code LLaMA, LongLoRA, etc.), how do we ensure a fair apples-to-apples comparison? Did you also run LongRoPE on the exact settings used by the strongest baselines to isolate the impact of positional scaling alone?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper attempts to make comparisons fair by following each model’s strongest prior fine-tuning recipe (so LongRoPE is evaluated on LLaMA2 and Mistral under the training regimes those communities used), and it contains targeted ablations that apply LongRoPE on the same fine-tuned checkpoints (e.g., LLaMA2-$256\mathrm{k}$) to isolate the effect of the positional re-scaling. That said, the authors acknowledge—and the results reflect—that differences in datasets, chunking length, and fine-tuning setup (e.g., Mistral’s use of $16\mathrm{k}$ training-length Long‑Data Collections vs. LLaMA2’s RedPajama $128\mathrm{k}/256\mathrm{k}$ chunks) can and do affect absolute performance, so comparisons should be interpreted with that caveat.

Explanation with paper grounding and intuition:

1) How the baselines were chosen and matched
The paper compares LongRoPE to several state-of-the-art open-source extended-context models (Together-$32\mathrm{k}$, Code LLaMA, LongLoRA-full-FT-$100\mathrm{k}$, YaRN-LLaMA, YaRN-Mistral). For each model family the authors generally follow the established fine-tuning/ training choices that prior art used: for LLaMA2 they fine-tune on RedPajama data chunked into $128\mathrm{k}$ then $256\mathrm{k}$ segments ($400 + 600$ steps as described), while for Mistral they follow YaRN’s setup using Together Computer’s Long‑Data Collections and $16\mathrm{k}$ sequence length (with the Mistral fine-tuning hyperparameters stated). This keeps LongRoPE’s LLaMA2 runs comparable to other LLaMA2 extended models and Mistral runs comparable to other Mistral extended models, rather than forcing all models onto one unfamiliar training recipe.

Intuition: forcing a model to be trained using someone else’s data/chunking can unfairly help or hurt it (different tokenization, domain, chunk size affect attention statistics). So the paper chooses the “best known” recipe per model family to give each baseline a reasonable advantage.

2) Where data/training differences could confound results (and the paper’s explicit note)
The paper explicitly points out that differences in training length and chunking affected outcomes. For example, in the Books3 results the authors note that Mistral’s perplexity behavior beyond $256\mathrm{k}$ is worse and they attribute part of that to following YaRN’s setting of $16\mathrm{k}$ training length for fine-tuning, which limits the model’s ability to further extend after fine-tuning. So the authors are transparent that data and training-length differences can influence performance and therefore that comparisons are not a perfectly controlled “all-else-equal” experiment.

Intuition: longer chunk lengths at fine-tuning expose the model to longer positional patterns and different attention distributions; if one baseline was trained with $16\mathrm{k}$ chunks and another with $128\mathrm{k}$, you can expect differences that are not purely caused by the positional interpolation algorithm.

3) Did the authors run LongRoPE under the exact settings of the strongest baselines to isolate positional scaling?
Yes in the sense that the paper contains controlled ablations where LongRoPE is applied on the same fine-tuned checkpoints and compared to other interpolation methods. Concretely, the paper:

- Performs a progressive/fine-tune workflow for LLaMA2 ($128\mathrm{k}$ then $256\mathrm{k}$ fine-tune) and reports comparisons to PI / NTK / YaRN applied to the same fine-tuned checkpoints (see “Effectiveness of the second positional interpolation” and main evaluation). This isolates the effect of the second non-uniform interpolation step versus other interpolation strategies on the same fine-tuned model.
- Runs non-fine-tuning comparisons (showing up to an $8\times$ extension without fine-tuning) by applying searched RoPE rescale factors directly to pre-trained models and comparing PI/NTK/YaRN under those same non-fine-tuned conditions.
- Includes ablations that compare (i) PI, (ii) searching only RoPE-dimension non-uniformity, and (iii) searching both RoPE-dimension and token-position non-uniformities on identical LLaMA2 targets ($16\mathrm{k}/32\mathrm{k}$ experiments and the $256\mathrm{k}\to 2048\mathrm{k}$ experiments) to show how much of the gain comes purely from the positional rescaling/search.

These controlled experiments demonstrate that much of LongRoPE’s improvement comes from the improved non-uniform positional rescaling itself (the search and the token-position thresholding), not purely from different datasets or extra fine-tuning compute.

4) Bottom line and recommended interpretation
The comparisons in the paper are pragmatically fair: each model is evaluated under reasonable, model-family-appropriate fine-tuning/data settings and LongRoPE is tested both without fine-tuning and on the same fine-tuned checkpoints used for direct baseline comparisons. That gives good evidence that LongRoPE’s positional scaling approach materially improves extended-context behavior.

However, the reader should keep in mind that absolute numbers are still affected by dataset/chunking and fine-tuning choices (the paper explicitly points this out for Mistral vs. LLaMA2). So treat the cross-model comparisons as indicative rather than perfectly controlled; the tightest evidence that LongRoPE’s positional scaling is the causal factor comes from the ablations where alternative interpolation methods are applied to the same checkpoints (the PI/YaRN comparisons on the LLaMA2-$256\mathrm{k}$ checkpoint, the non-finetuned $8\times$ tests, and the RoPE-dimension vs. token-position ablation).

If you want a stricter apples‑to‑apples experiment beyond what the paper reports, the most direct extension would be to re-run the strongest baseline(s) using the exact same fine-tuning data/chunk sizes and step counts the paper used for each LongRoPE variant; the paper’s ablations already go a long way toward isolating the positional-scaling effect, but full parity of datasets/hyperparameters across all models would remove the remaining confound.
