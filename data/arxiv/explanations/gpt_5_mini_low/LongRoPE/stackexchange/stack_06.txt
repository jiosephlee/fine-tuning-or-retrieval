\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How does per-dimension scaling interact with head-wise RoPE usage in multi-head attention?
Question:
RoPE is typically applied per head, and different heads may emphasize different frequency bands. LongRoPE searches one set of $\{\lambda_i\}$ per RoPE dimension index $i$. Is this shared across all heads, or do you allow head-specific scaling? If shared, could performance benefit from per-head $\lambda_{h,i}$, and did you attempt a reduced-parameter variant (e.g., a few groups across heads) to balance search cost and head specialization?
Answer:
Short answer: LongRoPE searches a single set of rescale factors indexed by RoPE dimension (the paper’s $\lambda_i$) and applies those same $\lambda_i$ across all attention heads. The paper does not report per-head $\lambda_{h,i}$ experiments or any head-grouped variant.

Why the paper uses shared per-dimension scaling and what that implies. RoPE in most LLMs is defined over the model embedding dimensions and then sliced into head-sized blocks for multi‑head attention. LongRoPE formulates the search over rescale factors per RoPE dimension (Eqns. (2) and (4) and the Problem Formulation), and enforces a monotonic constraint $\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}$. Because the same per-dimension $\lambda_i$ is used everywhere, every head that consumes a particular embedding dimension inherits that dimension’s rescaling. In practice different heads will still be affected differently because heads use different contiguous (or pre-specified) slices of the embedding space, so the global per-dimension $\lambda_i$ already produces head-dependent effective behavior without explicitly searching per head.

Would per-head $\lambda_{h,i}$ plausibly help? Intuitively yes: different attention heads specialize to different roles and different frequency bands, so allowing head-specific rescaling could let each head keep or compress the frequency content it needs (e.g., retrieval-like heads might want less interpolation on low-index/high-frequency dims, generation-coherence heads might prefer different tradeoffs). That could reduce information loss versus a single global $\lambda_i$, especially in models where heads are highly heterogeneous.

Why the paper didn’t pursue per-head lambdas (and the pragmatic cost). LongRoPE’s search space is already enormous even for per-dimension $\lambda_i$ (they discuss $4\times 10^{167}$ choices for modest settings and therefore use evolutionary search plus the monotonic constraint). Moving to per-head $\lambda_{h,i}$ would multiply the dimensionality by the number of heads, exploding the search space and evaluation cost. The paper therefore restricts to one $\lambda_i$ per RoPE dimension and focuses on search optimizations (monotonic constraint, seeded initial population) and on the token-position threshold $\hat n$ to keep the search tractable.

Practical reduced-parameter alternatives you could try (not reported in the paper, but consistent with its constraints and findings). A few low-cost designs would keep the spirit of LongRoPE while allowing some head specialization:

1) Head groups: partition heads into $G$ groups ($G \ll \text{num\_heads}$) and search a separate $\lambda_{g,i}$ per group. This multiplies parameters by $G$ instead of num_heads and balances expressivity vs search cost. It mirrors YaRN’s grouping idea but applied across heads rather than dimensions.

2) Low-rank/head-factorized scaling: represent per-head scaling as $\lambda_{h,i} = f_i \cdot g_h$, where $f_i$ is the per-dimension base (what LongRoPE already learns) and $g_h$ is a small per-head scalar (or small vector) learned with few parameters. You can fix $f_i$ from LongRoPE search and fine-tune only $g_h$ on short fine-tuning runs; this keeps search cheap.

3) Small additive/head bias terms: keep LongRoPE’s $\lambda_i$ and allow per-head offsets $\delta_{h,i}$ sampled from a tiny discrete set (e.g., $\{-0.02, 0, 0.02\}$) and search only over the offsets. That’s a constrained search that may let heads slightly specialize without exploding the search space.

4) Learnable fine-tuning rather than search: initialize with LongRoPE’s global $\lambda_i$ and fine‑tune a small set of per-head scalars (or group scalars) by gradient-based training on a small long-length dataset (the paper already fine-tunes for 400–1k steps for extension stages). This converts the combinatorial search into a low-cost continuous optimization.

How per-head variants could be evaluated (aligned to the paper’s methodology). Use the same perplexity-on-long-docs diagnostic (PG19 / Books3 sliding windows) and the passkey retrieval task. For search-based group methods, reuse the evolution algorithm but with smaller population or with the monotonic constraint applied within each group; for fine-tuned head scalars, run the short fine-tuning regimes LongRoPE already uses (e.g., 400–1,000 steps) so costs stay comparable.

Bottom line. LongRoPE explicitly optimizes one shared $\lambda_i$ per RoPE dimension and applies it across heads. Per-head or grouped variants are conceptually promising because heads are specialized, but the paper avoids them due to an already huge search space; practical compromises (head grouping, multiplicative head factors, tiny additive offsets, or light fine-tuning of per-head scalars) are promising directions to get head specialization with modest extra cost. These variants would be natural follow-ups to test whether head-wise specialization gives measurable gains beyond the gains LongRoPE already achieves by exploiting per-dimension and per-position non-uniformity.
