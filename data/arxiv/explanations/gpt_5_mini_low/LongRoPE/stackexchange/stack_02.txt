\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How does LongRoPE define interpolation vs. extrapolation when $\lambda_i \in [1, s\times1.25]$?
Question:
The text says PI uses a uniform scale $\lambda = s$ (interpolation), and that NTK mixes interpolation and extrapolation, while YaRN extrapolates for high-frequency dimensions. In LongRoPE's search space, $\lambda_i$ ranges from 1.0 to $s\times1.25$, with 1.0 called "direct extrapolation". But if the angle becomes $(n/\lambda_i)/\beta^i$, then $\lambda_i > 1$ reduces the angle (seems like interpolation), and $\lambda_i < 1$ would increase the angle (seems like extrapolation). Why is $\lambda_i = 1.0$ labeled extrapolation? Is there a sign or reciprocal convention I am missing (e.g., is $\hat\lambda_i = 1/\lambda_i$ the true scaling on the angle)?
Answer:
Short answer: There is indeed a reciprocal convention in the paper. The search parameter named "$\lambda_i$" in the search-table is not the multiplicative factor applied directly to the RoPE angle; the model uses $\hat\lambda_i = 1/\lambda_i$ (see Eq. (7) / Eq. (problem) in the manuscript). Concretely the angle used in the model is

$\cos\!\big(I(\hat\lambda_i,\hat n)\cdot n / \beta^i\big)$,

and $I(\cdot) = 1$ for early tokens and $I(\cdot) = 1/\lambda_i$ for tokens that are rescaled. So a searched $\lambda_i > 1$ produces an applied multiplier $< 1$ on the angle (angle shrinks → interpolation), while $\lambda_i = 1$ gives multiplier $= 1$ (angle unchanged → extrapolation / no interpolation).

Why the labeling in the paper then says "1.0 = direct extrapolation"? Because they define the search parameter so that $\lambda_i = 1$ means "do not downscale the rotary angle" (no interpolation) — i.e., keep the original RoPE angles beyond the pretrained length, which they call extrapolation. Larger $\lambda_i$ values correspond to stronger interpolation (angles are divided by $\lambda_i$). This matches the earlier PI description: PI linearly scales rotation angles by $s$, which in the paper's search-language corresponds to setting $\lambda_i = s$ so the model uses the multiplier $1/s$ on the original angle.

Put another way: the paper uses $\lambda_i$ as a convenient search variable, but the actual scaling applied to angles is $1/\lambda_i$. So there is no sign error — just a reciprocal convention. NTK/YaRN behaviors are consistent under this convention: dimensions with $\lambda_i$ close to $1$ are effectively extrapolated (angles unchanged), dimensions with larger $\lambda_i$ are interpolated (angles reduced).
