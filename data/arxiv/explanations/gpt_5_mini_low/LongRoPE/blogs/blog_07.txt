\title{Blogs about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

# Proof It Works: Perplexity, Passkey Retrieval, and Real Benchmarks

LongRoPE is a practical recipe for pushing RoPE-based LLMs from the usual 4k window into the millions of tokens. The core idea is to search for non-uniform rescale factors across RoPE dimensions and across token positions, use that to initialize and (lightly) fine-tune, and then apply a progressive extension strategy. The result: LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B models extended to a 2,048k (2048k) context window with strong empirical behavior across language modeling, synthetic retrieval, and standard short-window benchmarks. Below I summarize the most important experimental takeaways and explain why some design choices (notably the chosen fine-tuning length) matter.

## Key results at a glance

- LongRoPE extends LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B to a 2,048k context window (final extension ratio up to \(512\times\) from 4k).
- Perplexity: extended models retain low perplexity across increasingly long evaluation lengths, showing the models can leverage the extra context from 4k up to 2048k (evaluated using sliding windows).
- Passkey retrieval: in a synthetic retrieval task (find a five-digit passkey randomly placed in the context), LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k (fine-tuned to 256k) maintains \(\ge 90\%\) retrieval accuracy from 4k all the way to 2048k. LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k (fine-tuned to 128k) retains near-100% up to roughly 1.8M and drops to about 60% at 2048k in the reported runs.
- Standard benchmarks: when evaluated under the original short context window (e.g., 4k / typical few-shot setups), LongRoPE-2048k models achieve competitive scores on tasks such as ARC-Challenge (25-shot), HellaSwag (10-shot), MMLU (5-shot), and TruthfulQA (0-shot). In one reported case LongRoPE even slightly improves Mistral's TruthfulQA by +0.5%.

These outcomes show LongRoPE is not just an extreme-extension demo: it preserves (or closely preserves) both long-range and short-range capabilities.

## Perplexity behavior: smooth across long ranges

Perplexity was measured on long-document splits (Proof-pile, PG19 and Books3) using sliding-window evaluation. The salient observations:

- For windows up to 256k, LongRoPE-extended LLaMA2 and Mistral show an overall decreasing perplexity trend as evaluation context increases, indicating the model makes effective use of additional context.
- When pushed beyond 256k into the hundreds of thousands and up to 2,048k, LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k maintains reasonable perplexity, with only modest increases at the largest windows (e.g., small bumps around 1024k–2048k in some runs). Mistral shows better behavior at shorter long-context lengths in some settings but rises in perplexity beyond 256k in the reported experiments.
- A notable point: LongRoPE’s non-uniform RoPE rescaling—found by evolutionary search—preserves important lower-frequency/high-frequency structure in the original RoPE and provides a much better initialization for fine-tuning than simple linear interpolation (PI) or prior group rules (e.g., YaRN). That keeps perplexity far lower than prior interpolation-only or poorly initialized fine-tuning approaches.

In short, LongRoPE produces models that do not catastrophically blow up in perplexity as length grows to the million-token scale.

## Passkey retrieval: realistic stress-test of usable context

The passkey retrieval task hides a five-digit token (the “passkey”) somewhere uniformly at random in the long document and asks the model to retrieve it. This directly tests whether the model can locate and use sparse information distributed across a massive context.

- Existing extension methods typically drop to 0% accuracy well before very long ranges (many baselines degrade rapidly past 128k).
- LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k (ft = 256k) achieves ≥90% retrieval accuracy consistently from 4k to 2048k in the reported experiments.
- LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k (ft = 128k) keeps 100% up to roughly 1.8M, then declines to ~60% at 2,048k in the reported runs—this mirrors the perplexity trends seen for Mistral in the Books3 evaluation.

This demonstrates that LongRoPE models are not just “perplexity-friendly” but can effectively find and use discrete information buried deep in the context.

## Why fine-tuning length choices matter

LongRoPE uses a progressive extension strategy to reach 2048k without directly fine-tuning at that extreme length (which is costly and data-scarce). The practical recipe in the experiments was:

1. Search for non-uniform RoPE rescale factors for intermediate targets (e.g., 128k and 256k).
2. Fine-tune at 128k then 256k (in the LLaMA2 case: 400 steps at 128k, then 600 steps at 256k).
3. Run a second search on the fine-tuned 256k model to push to 2048k without additional fine-tuning.

Why this matters:

- Smaller-step fine-tuning (e.g., to 256k rather than attempting to fine-tune directly to 2,048k) stabilizes training and requires far less compute/data than trying to train at the target million-token window.
- The intermediate fine-tuning stage reduces the effective secondary extension ratio when going from 256k → 2048k (an \(8\times\) non-finetuned extension using the searched rescale factors), which empirically produces better final performance.
- Model-specific training choices matter. For example, Mistral’s fine-tuning in these experiments used a 16k training length (as in YaRN’s settings). That choice impacted how well Mistral generalized when extended further—explaining why Mistral performed better with ft=128k in the reported setup, whereas LLaMA2 benefited more from the 256k fine-tuning schedule.

Putting it simply: the intermediate fine-tuning length controls how much the model is asked to adapt in each stage; smaller, staged steps are easier to adapt and give better final results.

## Short-window recovery and dynamic RoPE usage

An important practical issue with large-extension schemes is preserving performance at original short windows. LongRoPE addresses this by running an additional (constrained) search to produce RoPE rescale factors tailored to short lengths (e.g., 4k and 8k). At inference time, the model dynamically switches to the appropriate rescale factors depending on sequence length, which restores short-window performance and avoids the “crowding” effect that hurts short-length behavior after massive extension ratios.

Empirically, this recovery step meaningfully improves perplexity at 4k and 8k and helps maintain competitive scores on standard benchmarks.

## Bottom line and practical implications

- LongRoPE is an effective, lightweight approach to push RoPE-based LLMs to extremely large context windows (2,048k) while retaining short-window performance.
- It combines a principled search for non-uniform RoPE rescaling with a progressive, resource-efficient fine-tuning strategy. This yields strong perplexity behavior, robust passkey retrieval, and competitive standard benchmark performance.
- The fine-tuning schedule and training-length choices matter in practice—staged fine-tuning (e.g., 128k → 256k) and the specifics of training sequence length influence final performance and should be selected based on resource constraints and the base model.

If you want to try this yourself, the paper reports that code and models will be released at https://github.com/microsoft/LongRoPE, and the method applies to any RoPE-based Transformer with only minor positional-embedding changes.
