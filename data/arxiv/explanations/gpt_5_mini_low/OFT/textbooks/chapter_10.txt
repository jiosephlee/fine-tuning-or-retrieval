\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

Chapter 10: Application II: Controllable Generation

# Tasks and datasets: C2I (COCO), S2I (ADE20K), L2F (CelebA-HQ)

Controllable generation in this chapter refers to finetuning a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model so that it respects an additional structural control signal at generation time. The work discussed uses three concrete tasks that are representative of common control modalities and diverse visual domains.

The first task is Canny edge to image (C2I) on the COCO dataset. Here the control signal consists of Canny edge maps extracted from COCO images. The model must synthesize photorealistic images that match a given text prompt while conforming to the edge layout. COCO provides complex scenes and object compositions, making this task a test of both geometric adherence and semantic hallucination from sparse edge information.

The second task is segmentation map to image (S2I) on ADE20K. The control signal is a semantic segmentation map indicating object and region categories. ADE20K contains diverse scene layouts and many semantic classes; therefore S2I tests the model’s ability to produce images whose semantic content (object presence and placement) aligns with the supplied segmentation while preserving photorealism and plausibility under an accompanying text prompt.

The third task is landmark to face (L2F) on CelebA-HQ. The control signal is a set of facial landmarks indicating keypoint positions that determine pose and relative facial geometry. This task emphasizes fine-grained spatial control: the generated face should exhibit a pose and configuration matching the landmark input while maintaining identity-agnostic realism and compatibility with the text prompt.

These three tasks cover sparse structural cues (C2I), dense semantic guidance (S2I), and precise geometric constraints (L2F). Using COCO, ADE20K, and CelebA-HQ as datasets anchors evaluation in widely-studied benchmarks with varying complexity and semantic content, enabling assessment of both control fidelity and image quality across domains.

# Training setup: finetuning SD v1.5; parameter counts vs adapters

All experiments in this chapter finetune Stable Diffusion v1.5 (SD v1.5) as the pretrained text-to-image diffusion backbone. The finetuning objective follows the same task formulations used by prior adapter-based methods: ControlNet and T2I-Adapter are representative baselines that add external modules and finetune those modules, while LoRA is a parameter-efficient adapter that adds low-rank additive updates to certain transformer weights. To ensure fair comparison, OFT/COFT are applied to the same layers targeted by LoRA when comparing to LoRA, and to the same overall evaluation protocol used for ControlNet and T2I-Adapter when comparing to those methods.

OFT (Orthogonal Finetuning) and COFT (Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning) differ in how many trainable parameters they introduce and in the nature of those parameters. ControlNet and T2I-Adapter typically add substantial module(s) with many parameters: these adapter modules increase inference-time complexity (ControlNet in particular). LoRA introduces additive low-rank matrices; the parameter count grows with the chosen low-rank dimension \(r'\) and with the matrix sizes it adapts. OFT parameterizes a layer-shared orthogonal transformation \(\bm{R}\) (often in a block-diagonal form) and may optionally add a small rescaling diagonal \(\bm{D}\); COFT adds an additional constraint \(\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le \epsilon\) to limit deviation from identity. In practice, OFT and COFT use far fewer trainable parameters than ControlNet and achieve a parameter count comparable to or smaller than LoRA for typical choices of rank and block settings. Importantly, unlike ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, OFT/COFT do not add inference-time overhead: the learned orthogonal transform can be folded into the pretrained weights, yielding identical inference speed to the original SD v1.5.

Training schedule and data: for the controllable generation experiments reported here, all methods finetune SD v1.5 on the respective dataset for a fixed number of epochs (20 epochs). OFT/COFT typically converge faster (see later sections) and are effective even with fewer trainable parameters and less training data (the chapter reports experiments where OFT converges well with only 5% of ADE20K), highlighting improved sample efficiency.

# Control consistency metrics: IoU/F1 (edges), mIoU/acc (segmentation), landmark L2 (faces)

To evaluate whether the generated images follow the input control signals, this work proposes a control consistency metric for each control modality. The core idea is straightforward: take a generated image, estimate the control signal from that image using an off-the-shelf detector or estimator, and compare the estimated control signal to the original input control. This yields a quantitative measure of how faithfully the model implements the control.

For C2I (edges): the control map is binary edges. After generating an image, an edge detector is applied to the generated image to produce a predicted edge map. The predicted map is compared to the input Canny edges using Intersection over Union (IoU) and F1 score. IoU measures the ratio of the intersection area of predicted and ground-truth edges over their union, and F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall computed from edge pixels. Higher IoU and F1 indicate stronger adherence to the edge layout.

For S2I (segmentation): the control map is a semantic segmentation. A pretrained semantic segmentation model is applied to the generated image to produce predicted class maps. These are compared to the input segmentation using mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), mean accuracy, and overall accuracy—standard segmentation evaluation metrics. Mean IoU averages per-class IoUs; mean accuracy averages per-class accuracies; overall accuracy counts total correct pixels divided by total pixels. Higher values indicate better semantic fidelity to the supplied segmentation.

For L2F (landmarks): the control signal is a set of facial landmark coordinates. A landmark detector is run on the generated face to produce predicted landmark positions. The average Euclidean distance (mean \( \ell_2 \) distance) between predicted landmarks and input landmark coordinates is computed. Formally, if the input landmark set is \( \{p_i\}_{i=1}^K \) and the predicted set is \( \{\hat p_i\}_{i=1}^K \), the metric is
\[
\text{mean-} \ell_2 = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K \| \hat p_i - p_i \|_2.
\]
Smaller mean-\( \ell_2 \) indicates more accurate pose and geometry alignment.

Using these metrics allows a quantitative assessment of control fidelity that is interpretable and comparable across methods. The chapter makes clear that the metric’s accuracy depends on the performance of the off-the-shelf estimator, but it provides an objective proxy for controllability.

# Convergence speed and stability: epoch-wise curves and qualitative progression

One of the salient empirical findings is that OFT and COFT converge substantially faster and more stably than alternative finetuning approaches. Convergence is evaluated both quantitatively with the control consistency metrics (e.g., landmark \( \ell_2 \) for L2F) plotted against training epochs and qualitatively by visualizing generated images across epochs to observe progressive adherence to controls.

For the L2F task, the mean-\( \ell_2 \) error between target and predicted landmarks is plotted as a function of finetuning epochs. OFT and COFT reach low landmark errors in far fewer epochs than LoRA, ControlNet, or T2I-Adapter. Concretely, the experiments show that LoRA requires about 20 epochs to reach the error level that OFT achieves around epoch 8. This faster convergence demonstrates that OFT’s search space—orthogonal rotations of neuron directions (possibly with small rescaling or constrained deviation)—is well-aligned with the kinds of semantic changes needed for controlling geometry and structure.

Stability refers to whether training exhibits sudden collapses, spikes, or abrupt emergence of behavior. OFT and COFT provide smooth and predictable training dynamics: control quality improves gradually and consistently across epochs. In contrast, methods like ControlNet sometimes display sudden emergence of control capability after several epochs—a brittle dynamic that can make hyperparameter selection and early stopping difficult. Similarly, LoRA may fail to preserve structure or converge slowly, with poorer stability in some control tasks.

Qualitative progression supports the quantitative curves: for L2F, generated faces gradually align pose and landmark positions with the control map epoch by epoch when using OFT/COFT. For C2I and S2I, OFT/COFT show steadily improved adherence to edge layouts or semantic boundaries over training. The experiments also report that OFT can achieve effective control even when trained with a reduced fraction of dataset images (e.g., 5% of ADE20K), indicating strong data efficiency.

Two complementary reasons explain the speed and stability advantages. First, OFT directly manipulates neuron directions (angles), which empirical analyses indicate are the dominant carrier of semantic information; rotating neuron directions is therefore an efficient way to change semantics in a controlled manner. Second, optimizing orthogonal transforms constrains the optimization to a smooth manifold (the orthogonal group), which reduces ill-conditioning and yields more predictable optimization trajectories than unconstrained large-dimensional additive updates.

# Quantitative results: stronger control and quality with fewer parameters

Across the three control tasks, OFT and COFT consistently outperform competing methods according to the control consistency metrics and also often produce higher-fidelity images. Quantitatively:

- On the C2I task evaluated on COCO with IoU and F1, OFT/COFT achieve higher IoU and F1 scores than ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and LoRA, indicating better alignment of generated edges to the input edge maps.
- On S2I (ADE20K), OFT/COFT obtain superior mean IoU, mean accuracy, and overall accuracy, showing that generated images better match the provided segmentation maps in terms of pixel-level class agreement.
- On L2F (CelebA-HQ), the mean-\( \ell_2 \) landmark error for OFT/COFT is lower than for the baselines, demonstrating more precise pose and facial geometry control.

The chapter emphasizes that these gains occur while using fewer trainable parameters than ControlNet and with parameter counts comparable to or smaller than LoRA. Moreover, OFT/COFT require fewer training epochs to reach competitive performance, which reduces total compute and data requirements. In particular, the COFT variant, which constrains orthogonal deviation by \(\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\| \le \epsilon\), often yields improved finetuning stability while maintaining or improving control metrics.

The experiments also show that adapter-based approaches (T2I-Adapter, ControlNet) tend to converge more slowly and often need more data or epochs to approach OFT’s final performance. LoRA exhibits variability: it may do relatively well on some tasks (e.g., S2I in some settings) but struggles on others (e.g., C2I and L2F), and its performance ceiling appears lower than OFT’s given typical hyperparameter choices.

# Qualitative comparisons: structure adherence, fidelity, and diversity

Qualitatively, OFT and COFT generate images that show better structural adherence to control inputs, higher photorealistic fidelity, and often more plausible geometric and semantic details than baselines. The reported visual comparisons illustrate several consistent patterns.

Structure adherence: In C2I, OFT/COFT produce images whose salient contours, object boundaries, and major shapes follow the input Canny edges closely, while baselines sometimes ignore or misinterpret edge structure, producing mismatched object placements or spurious content. In S2I, OFT/COFT produce images whose object regions and class boundaries align with the segmentation map; LoRA sometimes fails to generate images consistent with segmentation layouts, while ControlNet and T2I-Adapter perform reasonably but with less fine-grained texture quality or geometric reasoning.

Fidelity: OFT/COFT often produce more vivid textures, clearer object details, and overall more realistic images than adapter baselines. This is attributed to OFT preserving the pretrained model’s generalization ability via orthogonal transformations that keep hyperspherical neuron relationships intact, avoiding degradation of photorealistic priors during finetuning.

Diversity: OFT/COFT maintain diversity of outputs while respecting control signals. For subject-driven generation and controllable tasks alike, the method yields diverse variations consistent with prompts and controls. Even under strong geometric constraints (L2F), OFT/COFT manage to produce high-quality variations that follow the landmarks without collapsing to a single mode.

Stability and failure modes: The chapter reports that OFT/COFT finetuning trajectories are robust across different datasets and hyperparameter settings. ControlNet sometimes shows sudden onset of control capability (abrupt changes across epochs), making qualitative behavior less predictable. LoRA can fail qualitatively by losing identity-related details or by producing inconsistent structures (for example, converting clothing textures into unrelated patterns). OFT/COFT avoid such failures more often, which makes them easier to use in practice.

In summary, the qualitative comparisons corroborate the quantitative control metrics: OFT and COFT produce images that adhere more closely to control signals while preserving or improving image fidelity and maintaining diversity, all with fewer or comparable additional parameters and without adding inference overhead.

# Final remarks

Controllable generation via orthogonal finetuning demonstrates that constraining finetuning updates to orthogonal transformations (possibly with constrained deviation or small rescaling) is an effective strategy for integrating structural control signals into large pretrained text-to-image diffusion models. By preserving neuron pairwise angular relationships (hyperspherical energy), OFT/COFT maintain pretrained generative capability while providing a compact and stable mechanism to adapt models to edge, segmentation, and landmark controls. Empirical evaluation across COCO (C2I), ADE20K (S2I), and CelebA-HQ (L2F) shows faster convergence, stronger control consistency (IoU/F1, mIoU/accuracy, mean-\( \ell_2 \)), and better qualitative fidelity compared to ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and LoRA, all while keeping inference cost unchanged.
