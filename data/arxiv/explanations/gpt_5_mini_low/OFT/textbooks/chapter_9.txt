\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

Chapter 9: Application I: Subject-Driven Generation

# Task setup: few-shot subject images + text prompts; preserving identity and prompt fidelity

Subject-driven generation is the task of taking a small number of images of a particular subject (a person, pet, object, etc.) and producing new images that depict that same subject in novel contexts specified by text prompts. In practice, this is a few-shot problem: the finetuning procedure has access to just a handful of reference images of the subject and must adapt a large pretrained text-to-image diffusion model so that at sampling time the model can render that subject consistently while following arbitrary text guidance (for example, “a photo of {S} riding a bicycle in Paris”, where {S} is a learned token that denotes the subject).

Two requirements compete in this setup. First, identity (or subject) fidelity: generated images must retain the distinctive, instance-specific visual attributes present in the reference images (shape, color patterns, facial identity, clothing details, etc.). Second, prompt fidelity: the generated images must follow the new context or attributes specified by the text prompt (pose, background, objects, artistic style). A successful method must maintain both; changing the model so much that identity is lost (e.g., producing different animals or faces) or so little that prompts are ignored both fail the objective.

In the paper, subject-driven generation is framed within the DreamBooth setting. DreamBooth introduces a learned special token (a “customized token”) that represents the subject in text prompts. The finetuning objective used in DreamBooth—and adopted unchanged by OFT/COFT in the experiments—is a combination of reconstruction objectives on subject images and a class-specific prior-preservation loss that prevents overfitting the subject to degenerate outputs. The core practical question addressed by this work is: how to finetune the diffusion model so it acquires the subject-specific behavior while not destroying the pretrained model’s broad generative capabilities that are necessary to satisfy diverse prompts?

# Training protocol alignment with DreamBooth; layers finetuned with OFT/COFT

The OFT framework integrates directly into the DreamBooth finetuning pipeline by replacing the usual parameter update mechanism with an orthogonal layerwise transform. Concretely, rather than updating the pretrained weights \(\bm{W}^0\) of chosen layers by standard additive gradient steps, OFT learns a layer-shared orthogonal matrix \(\bm{R}\) and applies the transformed weights \(\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\) during forward passes. COFT (Constrained OFT) further constrains \(\bm{R}\) so that it remains close to the identity, enforcing \(\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq \epsilon\) in addition to orthogonality. Both OFT and COFT initialize \(\bm{R}=\bm{I}\) (or equivalently initialize the Cayley parameter \(\bm{Q}=\bm{0}\) used to produce \(\bm{R}\)), so training starts from exactly the pretrained weights and gradually adapts by rotating neuron directions.

To stay comparable to other adaptation methods (notably LoRA), OFT is applied to the same subset of network layers where LoRA would normally be used. In the experiments reported in the paper, OFT/COFT are applied to the attention weights (as LoRA commonly targets attention matrices in transformer-based diffusion U-Nets) so that comparisons are fair in both parameter count and layer scope. The Cayley parameterization is used to keep \(\bm{R}\) orthogonal efficiently; block-diagonal structure for \(\bm{R}\) (with \(r\) blocks) is employed to reduce parameter cost and to provide an additional regularization bias that reflects natural channel- or block-structure in convolutional/attention layers. This block-diagonal parameterization does not change the orthogonality property that underpins the method’s theoretical guarantees.

The training objective itself—losses, reconstruction + prior-preservation—is the same as DreamBooth’s. The only change is the optimization variables (orthogonal matrices \(\bm{R}\), optionally with a diagonal rescaling \(\bm{D}\) in the re-scaled OFT variant). COFT enforces the extra small-radius constraint via projection on the Cayley parameter (approximating \(\bm{R}\approx \bm{I}+2\bm{Q}\) and constraining \(\|\bm{Q}\|\leq \epsilon'\)), enabling explicit control over how far the finetuned weights may deviate from \(\bm{W}^0\).

# Evaluation metrics: DINO/CLIP-I (subject fidelity), CLIP-T (prompt fidelity), LPIPS diversity

To quantitatively evaluate subject-driven finetuning, the paper uses a set of metrics that separately probe subject fidelity, prompt fidelity, and sample diversity.

Subject fidelity is assessed by two feature-based similarity metrics: DINO and CLIP-I. DINO computes the similarity of ViT-S/16 self-supervised embeddings (DINO embeddings) between generated images and real reference images; CLIP-I uses CLIP image embeddings to compute average pairwise cosine similarity between generated and real images. High scores on DINO and CLIP-I indicate that the generated images match the subject appearance in the reference set.

Prompt fidelity is measured by CLIP-T: the average cosine similarity between the CLIP text embedding of the prompt and the CLIP image embedding of the generated images. This captures whether generated images actually reflect the textual instruction provided at generation time. Good prompt fidelity means the model changed the context or attributes according to the text while preserving the subject identity.

Diversity is important because a degenerate finetuned model might repeatedly output trivial near-identical reconstructions of the subject. The paper uses LPIPS, a perceptual distance measure, to assess diversity: average LPIPS cosine similarity among generated images for the same subject and prompt. Reasonable diversity indicates the model can produce different plausible variations rather than memorizing a single output.

These metrics together help determine whether OFT/COFT strike the desired balance: preserving the instance identity while remaining responsive to prompts and still producing varied outputs.

# Convergence and stability comparisons with DreamBooth and LoRA

One of the principal empirical claims of the paper is that OFT and COFT provide faster and more stable finetuning dynamics compared to DreamBooth’s vanilla finetuning and to LoRA. Several behaviors are emphasized.

First, convergence speed: OFT/COFT reach effective control (good subject and prompt fidelity) in far fewer training iterations. The paper reports that after 400 iterations OFT and COFT already produce good control results, whereas other methods require many more iterations. This faster convergence means fewer epochs and less compute are needed to get acceptable subject-driven generation.

Second, stability over longer training: standard DreamBooth finetuning can become unstable and eventually collapse if training runs too long; LoRA can fail to preserve subject identity under some settings. By contrast, both OFT and COFT maintain stable behavior even after many more iterations. In particular, COFT with a properly chosen \(\epsilon\) makes both the learning rate and the number of iterations much less sensitive hyperparameters because the model is explicitly constrained to remain within a small radius around the pretrained weights.

Third, failure modes: the paper highlights examples where LoRA begins to generate incorrect semantics (e.g., transforming clothing colors into fur textures) as training progresses, while OFT/COFT continue to preserve identity and correct prompt conditioning. This empirical stability is explained by OFT’s preservation of hyperspherical energy (pairwise neuron-angle structure): by applying layerwise orthogonal transforms at finetuning, the pairwise angular relationships among neurons are unchanged, which the authors hypothesize (and empirically observe) is crucial to maintaining the pretrained model’s semantic generation capability.

# Quantitative outcomes: fidelity/diversity improvements

In the DreamBooth-style subject-driven experiments using Stable Diffusion v1.5 as the base model, the paper reports that both OFT and COFT outperform DreamBooth and LoRA on subject-fidelity metrics while maintaining comparable or better prompt fidelity and diversity.

Specifically, on the DINO and CLIP-I metrics—both designed to measure how closely generated images match the subject reference images—OFT and COFT achieve a considerable margin over DreamBooth and LoRA. This indicates stronger preservation of subject-specific visual features. On CLIP-T, which measures text prompt adherence, OFT and COFT perform slightly better or comparably to baselines, demonstrating that improved subject fidelity does not come at the cost of ignoring prompts. For LPIPS diversity, OFT and COFT match or slightly improve over baselines, which indicates that OFT’s orthogonal constraint does not force the generator into narrow memorization modes.

The experimental protocol included repeating finetuning runs with 30 different random seeds for each method to reduce randomness; the reported improvements are said to be consistent across these runs. These quantitative gains are thus not isolated to a single lucky initialization but reflect more stable optimization behavior.

# Qualitative analyses: identity preservation and prompt adherence; robustness to longer training

Beyond the numerical metrics, the paper presents qualitative comparisons showing visual examples across methods. These qualitative observations align with the quantitative findings.

Identity preservation: OFT and COFT-produced images consistently retain distinctive subject attributes—face features, clothing patterns, or unique object marks—from the few-shot reference images, even when the generated images place the subject in markedly different contexts or poses. LoRA often fails to preserve subject identity in several showcased examples (for instance, replacing clothing texture with fur or altering facial traits), while DreamBooth sometimes preserves identity but struggles to follow the text prompt accurately.

Prompt adherence: OFT and COFT demonstrate accurate compliance with the text prompt. Where DreamBooth might preserve identity but ignore some prompt details, OFT/COFT show both identity preservation and alignment with prompt semantics. This confirms that the orthogonal adaptation mechanism is effective at altering neuron directions in a way that encodes new context instructions without breaking pretrained semantic structure.

Robustness to longer training: A particularly practical advantage of OFT/COFT is reduced sensitivity to the number of finetuning iterations. Because these methods initialize at the pretrained weights and only rotate neuron directions (with COFT additionally bounding how far those rotations can move from the identity), they avoid the common need in DreamBooth-style training to carefully stop finetuning at exactly the right iteration. The paper reports that DreamBooth and LoRA may start producing collapsed or semantically incorrect images when run long, whereas OFT/COFT remain stable and continue to produce high-quality outputs. This robustness eases hyperparameter selection (learning rate and epoch count) in practice and makes the finetuning process safer to overrun.

# Summary

In the subject-driven generation application, OFT and its constrained variant COFT fit into the DreamBooth finetuning framework by replacing standard additive weight updates with orthogonal, layer-shared multiplicative transforms applied to the pretrained weights. This preserves hyperspherical energy (pairwise neuron-angle relationships) by construction, a property the authors identify as empirically important for preserving the pretrained model’s semantic generation capabilities. Evaluated with DINO and CLIP-I for subject fidelity, CLIP-T for prompt fidelity, and LPIPS for diversity, OFT/COFT show consistent quantitative improvements over DreamBooth and LoRA, converge faster, and yield much more stable behavior across longer training. Qualitatively, OFT/COFT better maintain identity details while responding accurately to prompts and are less sensitive to the number of finetuning iterations, making them practical and reliable choices for few-shot subject-driven generation.
