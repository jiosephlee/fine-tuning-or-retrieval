\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### What matrix norm does COFT use for the constraint $\|\mathbf R-\mathbf I\|\le\epsilon$, and how does it control angle change?
Question:
COFT introduces $\|\mathbf R-\mathbf I\|\le\epsilon$ but does not state whether this is the spectral norm, Frobenius norm, or something else. Different norms bound different geometric quantities. For small $\epsilon$, can you relate this norm bound to a bound on the maximum principal angle between $\mathbf R$-rotated and original neuron directions? If the implementation constrains $\|\mathbf Q\|\le\epsilon'$ (post Neumann approximation), which norm is used for $\mathbf Q$, and how does it translate into a concrete bound on per-neuron angular deviations?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper’s math and the Neumann-series argument strongly point to the operator (spectral) norm being the intended meaning of "$\|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}\|\le\epsilon$". Interpreting the constraint that way gives a very simple and tight control on how much any single neuron direction (unit vector) can rotate: for any unit neuron vector $u$ the angle $\theta$ between $u$ and $Ru$ satisfies
$$
\|Ru-u\| \le \|R-I\|_{2} \le \epsilon
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\theta \le 2\arcsin\!\Big(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Big),
$$
and for small $\epsilon$ this is approximately $\theta \approx \epsilon$.

Why the operator norm is the natural reading here
- In Section 3.3 the authors write the Neumann expansion and explicitly say it converges “in the operator norm”; that is a direct clue that they are thinking in terms of the spectral/operator norm (largest singular value) when talking about matrix perturbation size.  
- The operator norm gives the tight uniform bound $\| (R-I)u\| \le \|R-I\|_{2}$ valid for every unit vector $u$, which directly controls worst-case per-neuron displacement and therefore the angle between a neuron and its rotated version. Frobenius or other norms do not give that per-vector worst-case bound without an extra dimension-dependent factor.

Derivation and intuition (concise)
For any two unit vectors $x$ and $y$, the chord length is related to the angle between them: $\|x-y\|=2\sin(\theta/2)$, where $\theta=\angle(x,y)$. Take $x=u$ and $y=Ru$ (both unit length because $R$ is orthogonal). Then
$$
2\sin\!\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)=\|Ru-u\|\le \|R-I\|_{2}\le \epsilon,
$$
so
$$
\theta \le 2\arcsin\!\Big(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Big).
$$
For small $\epsilon$, $\arcsin(\epsilon/2)\approx \epsilon/2$, hence $\theta\approx\epsilon$. This gives a direct and interpretable per-neuron angular bound.

What about the Cayley/Neumann approximation and the constraint on Q?
The paper parameterizes $R=(I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}$ with $Q$ skew-symmetric and then uses the Neumann-series approximation $R\approx I+2Q + O(Q^2)$ (they discuss convergence in operator norm). Moving the constraint inside the Cayley map they replace $\|R-I\|\le\epsilon$ by $\|Q\|\le\epsilon'$. Interpreting “$\|\cdot\|$” consistently as the operator norm again yields the simplest correspondence. Under the first-order approximation $R\approx I+2Q$ one gets $\|R-I\|_{2}\approx 2\|Q\|_{2}$, so the per-neuron angle bound becomes
$$
\theta \lesssim 2\arcsin\!\Big(\frac{2\|Q\|_{2}}{2}\Big)=2\arcsin(\|Q\|_{2}),
$$
and for small $\|Q\|_{2}$ this is $\theta\approx 2\|Q\|_{2}$. Thus constraining $\|Q\|_{2}\le\epsilon'$ roughly enforces per-neuron angular deviations $\lesssim 2\epsilon'$ (in radians) to first order.

If instead someone used Frobenius norm in the projection step
The paper does not explicitly say which norm the projected gradient step uses. If an implementation projects $Q$ by Frobenius norm (common in practice because projection is simple), you only have $\|Q\|_{2}\le\|Q\|_{F}$, so $\|Q\|_{F}\le\epsilon'$ implies $\|Q\|_{2}\le\epsilon'$ as well (so the same angle bounds still hold), but the reverse is not true: a spectral-norm ball is a strictly smaller (stronger) constraint than a Frobenius ball of the same radius. If the implementation instead enforces spectral-norm projection, then the geometric per-neuron angle guarantees above are exactly what you get.

Practical takeaway
Read the paper’s “operator norm / Neumann series” comment as evidence they intended the spectral/operator norm. With that choice you obtain the clean worst-case per-unit-vector angle bound $\theta\le 2\arcsin(\epsilon/2)$ for $\|R-I\|_{2}\le\epsilon$, and under the Cayley/Neumann approximation $\|Q\|_{2}\le\epsilon'$ gives roughly $\theta\lesssim 2\epsilon'$ for small $\epsilon'$. If you want a tight, interpretable per-neuron angular control in code, project $Q$ in the spectral norm (or pick $\epsilon'$ conservatively if you project in Frobenius norm).
