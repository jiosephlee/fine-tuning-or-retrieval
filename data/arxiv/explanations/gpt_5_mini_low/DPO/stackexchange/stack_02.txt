\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does the partition function cancel out when reparameterizing Bradley–Terry with the policy ratios?
Question:
In Eq. (6), the authors substitute the reward reparameterization

$$r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)$$

into the Bradley–Terry probability $\sigma(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2))$. They say the partition function terms cancel, yielding Eq. (7). I see that $\beta \log Z(x)$ appears in both $r(x,y_1)$ and $r(x,y_2)$, but could someone write out the algebra that shows the $\log Z(x)$ terms disappear inside the logistic function and that the result depends only on the log policy ratios?
Answer:
The cancellation is just a short algebraic consequence of (a) the reward reparameterization in Eq. (6) and (b) the Bradley–Terry model depending only on reward differences. Write the reparameterization for two completions $y_1,y_2$ (suppressing the dependence on $x$ where clear):

$$
r(x,y_i) = \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_i\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_i\mid x)} + \beta\log Z(x)\qquad (i=1,2).
$$

Take the difference that appears inside the logistic in the Bradley–Terry formula:
$$
r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2) 
= \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}
- \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}
+ \beta\log Z(x) - \beta\log Z(x).
$$
The two $\beta\log Z(x)$ terms cancel because they are identical (the partition function depends only on $x$, not on $y$). So
$$
r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)
= \beta\!\left[\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}
- \log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right]
= \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}
{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}.
$$

Now substitute this into the Bradley–Terry probability
$$
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\bigl(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\bigr)
=\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(r(x,y_2)-r(x,y_1)\bigr)}.
$$
Using the expression above gives the form in Eq. (7):
$$
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)
=\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\Bigl(\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}
{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}\Bigr)}
=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}
{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}\right)^{\!\beta}}.
$$

Intuitively, this happens because Bradley–Terry (and Plackett–Luce) depend only on differences of the score/reward; adding any function of $x$ (here $\beta\log Z(x)$) to all rewards shifts them equally and thus has no effect on pairwise comparisons. This is the same identifiability/shift invariance the paper discusses: reward functions are defined only up to adding an $x$-dependent offset, so the partition function may appear in a particular parameterization of $r$ but it cancels out in the preference probabilities.
