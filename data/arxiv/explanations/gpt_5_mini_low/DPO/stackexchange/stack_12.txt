\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### How does DPO handle ranked lists under Plackett–Luce, and what changes in the loss?
Question:
The paper mentions an extension from Bradley–Terry to Plackett–Luce in the appendix. If I have a ranked list $y_{(1)} \succ y_{(2)} \succ \cdots \succ y_{(k)}$, what is the exact DPO loss for that case? Does it factor into a sum of pairwise logistic terms with the same $\beta$ and $\log \pi/\pi_{\text{ref}}$ ratios, or do we need a different normalization to respect the PL likelihood? A concrete formula would help me implement it.
Answer:
Short answer: use the Plackett–Luce sequential-softmax form with the DPO reparameterization. If you write $s_j(x) = \beta\bigl(\log\pi_\theta(y_j\mid x) - \log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_j\mid x)\bigr)$, the negative log-likelihood (DPO loss) for the ranked list $y_{(1)} \succ y_{(2)} \succ \cdots \succ y_{(k)}$ is

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\{y_{(i)}\}_{i=1}^k)
= -\sum_{i=1}^k \log\frac{\exp(s_{(i)}(x))}{\sum_{j=i}^k \exp(s_{(j)}(x))}
= \sum_{i=1}^k \Biggl(\log\sum_{j=i}^k \exp\bigl(s_{(j)}(x)\bigr) - s_{(i)}(x)\Biggr).
\]

This follows from plugging the DPO reparameterization $r(x,y)=\beta\log\!\left(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\right)+\text{(prefix const.)}$ into the Plackett–Luce (PL) sequential-choice model. The prefix-dependent additive constant cancels in the PL ratios, so all terms are expressed purely in the $s_j$ logits above.

Remarks and intuition. Under PL a ranking probability is the product of sequential softmaxes: first pick the top item among all $k$, then pick the second among the remaining $k-1$, etc. Because DPO represents rewards as scaled log-probability ratios, each softmax in the PL model becomes a softmax over the $s_j$ scores. The resulting loss is therefore a sum of $k$ categorical-logits terms (one softmax per selection step). This is not equal to simply summing independent Bradley–Terry (pairwise logistic) losses over all pairs $y_{(a)}\succ y_{(b)}$: the PL likelihood induces a different normalization (the denominator at step $i$ sums only over the remaining items $j\ge i$), and while you can algebraically expand the PL loss into a weighted combination of pairwise comparisons, the weights are not uniform and the simple sum of pairwise logistic terms (as used for BT pairwise data) is not equivalent in general.

Implementation tip. For each ranked example compute $s_j$ as above, then for $i$ from $1$ to $k$ compute $\text{step\_loss}_i = -s_{(i)} + \operatorname{logsumexp}\bigl(s_{(i:k)}\bigr)$. Sum these and average over examples. Use stable logsumexp numerics. If your dataset contains only pairwise labels ($k=2$) this formula reduces to the DPO pairwise loss in the main paper:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{k=2} = -\log \sigma\bigl(s_{(1)}-s_{(2)}\bigr),
\]

since the sequential denominators become two-term softmaxes.
