\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Can someone clarify the equivalence classes of rewards and why adding an $f(x)$ term does not change the optimal policy?
Question:
The paper defines an equivalence relation over rewards where $r \sim r'$ if $r(x,y) - r'(x,y) = f(x)$. I understand this keeps Bradley–Terry probabilities the same since differences $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$ are invariant. But why does this also leave the KL-regularized optimal policy in Eq. (4) unchanged? Is it simply because $\exp((1/\beta)f(x))$ factors into the partition function $Z(x)$ and thus cancels? A short proof sketch for Lemma 2 would help.
Answer:
Short answer: because adding a term that depends only on the context prefix $x$ multiplies every un-normalized probability by the same factor, which cancels when you divide by the partition function. Equivalently, rewards are defined only up to an additive function of $x$ (a “potential”), so that the soft-max / Boltzmann form of the optimal policy is invariant under that addition.

Proof sketch (direct from the optimal-policy formula). The KL-regularized optimal policy for a reward $r$ is
$$
\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z_r(x)}\;\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big),
$$
with partition function $Z_r(x)=\sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\big)$. Now let $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)+f(x)$ for some function $f$ depending only on $x$. Plugging $r'$ into the formula gives
$$
\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)
= \frac{1}{Z_{r'}(x)}\;\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}(r(x,y)+f(x))\Big)
= \frac{1}{Z_{r'}(x)}\;\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big).
$$
Because $\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}f(x)\big)$ is independent of $y$, it factors out of the sum that defines the partition function:
$$
Z_{r'}(x)=\sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big)
=\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big)\,Z_r(x).
$$
Thus the common factor cancels and $\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)=\pi_r(y\mid x)$ for every $y$. So the optimal policy is unchanged by adding $f(x)$.

Intuition and broader connection: this is the same under-identifiability that appears in energy-based / Boltzmann models and in ranking models like Bradley–Terry or Plackett–Luce: only reward differences across actions matter for choices, and any context-only offset is a “gauge” (a potential) you can add without changing probabilities or the optimal policy. This is why the paper defines the equivalence class $r\sim r'$ when $r-r'=f(x)$ and why the DPO reparameterization can choose a canonical representative $r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ without loss of generality.
