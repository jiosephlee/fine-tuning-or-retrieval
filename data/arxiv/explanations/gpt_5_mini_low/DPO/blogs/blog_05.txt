\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

# DPO vs PPO: Who Wins on Summarization, Sentiment, and Dialogue?

## TL;DR
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple, RL-free method for training language models from pairwise human preferences. In the experiments from the paper, DPO:

- Achieves a strictly better reward–KL frontier than PPO in a controlled sentiment generation task (it gets higher reward for the same or lower KL to the reference policy).
- Matches or exceeds PPO-level performance on real preference datasets (Reddit TL;DR summarization and single-turn dialogue) according to GPT-4-based win-rate evaluations, and in a human validation study.
- Is more robust to sampling temperature at inference time than PPO.
- Is substantially simpler to implement and train—no reward model fitting phase, no reinforcement learning loop, and fewer moving parts.

Below I unpack how DPO works at a high level, summarize the experimental evidence, and note practical takeaways and caveats.

## Quick intuition: how DPO avoids RL
The standard RLHF pipeline fits a reward model r_\phi(x,y) from human comparisons (via a Bradley–Terry or Plackett–Luce model) and then runs RL (e.g., PPO) to optimize a policy \pi_\theta to maximize expected reward subject to a KL penalty to a reference policy \pi_ref. That RL step is expensive and often unstable.

DPO exploits an analytical property of the KL-constrained objective: the optimal policy for a given reward takes the log-linear form

\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x) \exp\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta} r(x,y)\right).

Rearranging this gives an implicit reward parameterization in terms of a policy:

\hat r_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)}.

Because human preference models depend only on reward differences, the partition function cancels and you can express the Bradley–Terry likelihood directly in terms of \pi_\theta and \pi_ref. This yields a simple maximum-likelihood loss:

\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\biggl[\log \sigma\Bigl(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\Bigr)\biggr].

In short: instead of fitting r_\phi then running RL, DPO directly fits the policy with a binary cross-entropy-style objective. The policy simultaneously represents the implicit reward.

## What the DPO update does (mechanistic view)
The DPO gradient pushes up the likelihood of the human-preferred completion y_w and pushes down the likelihood of the dispreferred y_l. Crucially, each pair is weighted by the sigmoid of the implicit reward gap,

\sigma\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l) - \hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr),

so examples where the current implicit reward ordering is most wrong get higher weight. That dynamic, per-example importance weight prevents naive ratio-based updates from causing model degeneration and explains much of DPO's stability in practice.

## Experimental summary

Tasks and evaluation
- Controlled sentiment generation (IMDb prefixes): synthetic preferences generated by a pre-trained sentiment classifier give access to a ground-truth reward. This lets the authors plot the reward vs sequence-level KL frontier for each algorithm.
- Summarization (Reddit TL;DR): human preference dataset from prior work. Evaluation is win rate against reference summaries using GPT-4 as a proxy evaluator (and a small human validation study).
- Single-turn dialogue (Anthropic Helpful & Harmless dataset): human preference dataset; evaluation is win rate against preferred responses using GPT-4.

Baselines included SFT, supervised fine-tuning on preferred responses (Preferred-FT), unlikelihood training, PPO (with a learned reward model), PPO-GT (PPO with ground-truth reward in the controlled experiment), and Best-of-N selection using a learned reward.

Key empirical findings
1. Reward–KL frontier (controlled sentiment)
   - DPO dominates PPO: for the same KL distance from the reference policy, DPO attains higher true reward. Across a hyperparameter sweep (PPO target KLs and DPO β values), DPO strictly improved the reward–KL tradeoff and even surpassed PPO-GT (PPO with access to ground-truth reward) in that controlled setting.

2. Summarization (Reddit TL;DR)
   - DPO achieved roughly a 61% win rate vs reference summaries at greedy sampling (temperature 0.0), compared to ~57% for PPO at its best temperature.
   - DPO outperformed Preferred-FT and Best-of-N baselines in maximum win rate in these experiments.
   - DPO was more robust to sampling temperature: PPO’s performance degraded at higher temperatures, while DPO maintained higher win rates across temperatures.

3. Single-turn dialogue (Anthropic HH)
   - DPO improved over Preferred-FT and was competitive with computationally intensive Best-of-128 sampling.
   - The PPO model provided by an external implementation did not outperform the base SFT model in the authors’ setup; DPO was the only computationally efficient method that improved over preferred completions in that dataset.

4. Generalization
   - A policy trained on Reddit TL;DR and evaluated on CNN/DailyMail articles still showed DPO outperforming PPO (evaluated by GPT-4 win rates), suggesting at least preliminary evidence that DPO generalization matches or exceeds PPO under some distribution shifts.

5. Human validation of GPT-4
   - The authors ran a human study to check GPT-4’s judgments and found GPT-4 agreement with humans was comparable to human–human agreement, justifying use of GPT-4 for large-scale evaluation. They also observed that evaluation prompt wording for GPT-4 affects win rates, and used a prompt that best matched human preferences.

## Practical advantages
- Simplicity: DPO is implemented as a straightforward supervised training objective (binary cross-entropy on preference pairs) — no reward model training loop, no RL algorithm complexity, and no need to sample trajectories during training.
- Stability: Empirically less brittle than PPO in these experiments, likely because it avoids high-variance policy gradients and the need for learned value baselines.
- Efficiency: Less computational overhead than RLHF pipelines (no repeated sampling and advantage/value updates).
- Robust inference: Better robustness to sampling temperature in generation.

## Limitations and open questions (from the paper)
- Scaling: experiments were up to 6B parameters; behavior at very large scales (state-of-the-art models) remains to be tested.
- Out-of-distribution generalization: preliminary results are promising, but a more systematic study of how DPO generalizes vs explicit reward + RL is needed.
- Reward over-optimization: how over-optimization manifests in the implicit reward parameterization is a topic for future work.
- Evaluation sensitivity: automated evaluators (like GPT-4) are prompt-sensitive; human evaluation remains the gold standard.

## Bottom line
On the tasks evaluated in the paper, DPO outperforms or matches PPO while being much simpler and cheaper to run. For practitioners who want to train language models from pairwise human preferences but want to avoid the complexity and instability of RLHF, DPO is an attractive, theoretically grounded alternative worth trying.
