# A Practical BOFT Playbook: Hyperparameters, Initialization, and Dropout

Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient way to adapt large pretrained models by multiplying a learnable orthogonal matrix into each layer's weights. BOFT composes a dense orthogonal transform from a product of sparse, structured factors (butterfly components), giving a tunable trade-off between expressivity and parameter count. This post gives concise, practical guidance for choosing BOFT hyperparameters, initializing the factors, using the multiplicative Dropout that the BOFT framework introduces, and integrating BOFT into existing finetuning pipelines.

## Quick BOFT recap (what you need to know)
- BOFT represents the left-multiplicative finetuning transform as an orthogonal matrix
  R(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{B}_{(d,i)}^b,
  where each \tilde{B}_{(d,i)}^b is a block-wise butterfly component.  
- Notation BOFT(m,b): m = number of butterfly components (depth), b = block size (even-valued building block). Typical constraints from the construction: d is the layer dimension and block factors tile d.
- Parameter count (effective trainable parameters) for a linear layer of width d: approximately (1/2)(b - 1) d m. In particular:
  - Butterfly regime: m \approx \log d and b = 2 gives \mathcal{O}(d\log d) parameters.
  - m = 1 recovers block-diagonal OFT with block size b.
  - m = 1 and b = d recovers full OFT (dense orthogonal).
- BOFT preserves the spectral norm of the pretrained weight matrix (left-multiplying by orthogonal R), which helps training stability.

With that foundation, here are actionable recommendations.

## Choosing m (depth) and b (block size)
BOFT gives a two-dimensional knob to control capacity vs parameter-efficiency:

- Intuition:
  - Larger m (more factors) and smaller b (smaller block size per factor) increase expressivity per parameter because repeated sparse factorization mixes coordinates more thoroughly.
  - Smaller m and larger b favor simplicity / regularity (fewer composition steps), approaching block-diagonal or full orthogonal cases.

- Practical guidance:
  - When your parameter budget is tight and you want a dense transform, favor the butterfly regime: set b = 2 and increase m toward \log d. This yields the classic butterfly factorization and achieves dense connectivity with \mathcal{O}(d\log d) parameters.
  - If you want an intermediate capacity between block-diagonal OFT and full orthogonal, choose moderate b (e.g., small multiples of 2) and increase m. Smaller b with larger m tends to be more expressive than the opposite for the same parameter budget.
  - If you want a cheap, low-capacity adaptation (very strong regularity), use m = 1 and choose b small; this is exactly block-diagonal OFT with block size b.
  - When compute is not restrictive and you desire maximal expressivity, BOFT(1,d) reproduces full OFT (dense orthogonal), but it costs \mathcal{O}(d^2) parameters.

- How to decide in practice:
  - Start by computing your layer width d and your parameter budget per layer.
  - Use the rough parameter formula (1/2)(b - 1) d m to find feasible (m,b) pairs that fit your budget.
  - Prefer configurations with smaller b and larger m for higher expressivity per parameter when you need a dense-style transform.
  - If you expect the downstream task to require only modest adaptation (e.g., domain shift or light style changes), start conservative (small m) and scale up if performance stalls.

Note: the paper frames expressivity formally: BOFT with smaller b and larger m generally approximates dense orthogonals more efficiently than plain block-diagonal OFT with the same block size.

## Identity initialization via Cayley transform
For stable finetuning you want R to be the identity at initialization so the pretrained model's behavior is unchanged by BOFT at the start.

- Cayley parameterization used in BOFT:
  - Parameterize each orthogonal factor by a skew-symmetric matrix Q (Q = -Q^T) and the Cayley transform
    R_factor = (I + Q) (I - Q)^{-1}.
  - To initialize R_factor = I, set the skew-symmetric Q to the zero matrix. This yields (I + 0)(I - 0)^{-1} = I.

- Practical tips:
  - Initialize the internal skew-symmetric parameters to zero for all butterfly component blocks; this yields exact identity initialization across the composed R(m,b).
  - Identity init means the forward pass initially uses the original pretrained weights exactly. This is convenient for small-step, stable finetuning and avoids the need for careful learning-rate scaling at the very start.
  - Keep in mind numerical stability when computing (I - Q)^{-1}. For the low-dimensional blocks used in BOFT (many are 2-by-2 or small blocks), Cayley inverses are cheap and stable; use robust linear solvers rather than naive matrix inverse where possible.

## Multiplicative Dropout (BOFT-style Dropout)
Traditional additive Dropout does not match BOFT's multiplicative update pattern (R multiplies weights). BOFT introduces a multiplicative Dropout tailored to block-wise, multiplicative orthogonal factors.

- Mechanism (as given in the paper):
  - BOFT is made of m orthogonal butterfly components. Each component is itself block-diagonal with block size b in a given stage.
  - Multiplicative Dropout randomly selects:
    - p1 fraction of the butterfly components (entire components), and
    - p2 fraction of the diagonal blocks inside each chosen component,
    and replaces those selected blocks with identity blocks for that training step.
  - Effectively you are turning some factor blocks off by setting them to identity multiplicatively, which reduces the effective adaptation strength and prevents overfitting.

- Practical guidelines:
  - Use multiplicative Dropout when you see overfitting or when the chosen (m,b) yields a relatively large parameter budget for the available training data.
  - Start with modest p1 and p2 (e.g., small percentages) and increase if overfitting persists. The paper does not give exact recommended values; tune p1/p2 on a held-out validation set.
  - Because multiplicative Dropout replaces blocks with identity, it is consistent with identity initialization and does not introduce distributional shifts at initialization.
  - Implement Dropout per training step (randomly sample blocks each iteration) similar to standard Dropout semantics.

## Integration tips for existing training pipelines
BOFT can be slotted into common finetuning setups with a few considerations.

- Where to apply:
  - BOFT applies to left-multiplicative updates of linear layers (weight matrices W^0). Typical choices: MLP/FFN linear layers, projection matrices in attention modules, or other dense layers where preserving pretraining geometry matters.

- Forward pass and inference:
  - During training, compute z = (R(m,b) \cdot W^0)^\top x. This requires composing the butterfly factors each forward pass (or applying them sequentially).
  - After finetuning, you can fold the learned R into the pretrained weights: replace W^0 with R(m,b) W^0 and discard BOFT parameters. There is no additional inference latency after folding.

- Compute / runtime:
  - BOFT increases training computation because R is a product of multiple factors; you will have extra matrix multiplies during training. The paper notes runtime overhead is slightly larger than OFT. Expect a modest training-time cost in exchange for parameter savings and possibly better generalization.
  - To reduce overhead, apply BOFT only to a subset of layers (e.g., large FFN layers or projection matrices), or use smaller m where necessary.

- Optimizer and learning-rate advice:
  - Because BOFT is initialized to identity, treat BOFT parameters like small-magnitude learnable transforms: start with conservative learning rates for these parameters. The paper follows the general OFT philosophy of small-step updates to preserve pretrained structure.
  - You can use the same optimizer used for the rest of finetuning; consider tuning separate learning rates for BOFT parameters vs other newly added parameters.

- Implementation checklist:
  - Ensure block sizes divide the layer dimension d and are compatible with the butterfly tiling strategy.
  - Parameterize block-wise skew-symmetric Q matrices and implement the Cayley transform robustly (prefer solving linear systems to explicit inversion).
  - Implement multiplicative Dropout by masking selected component blocks to identity each training step.
  - Provide a utility to fold the learned R into W^0 after training for zero-cost inference.

## Practical troubleshooting and heuristics
- If training diverges early: verify identity initialization (all Q blocks zero), reduce BOFT learning rate, or reduce the fraction of active blocks via multiplicative Dropout.
- If val performance is worse than simple LoRA or small adapters: your BOFT setup may be too expressive for the available data. Reduce m or increase Dropout, or test a larger b with smaller m to bias toward regularity.
- If you have ample compute but tight parameter budget: favor smaller b and increase m (but be mindful of training-time overhead).
- Folding for deployment: always validate that folded weights exactly reproduce behavior on a test batch before discarding BOFT parameters.

## Summary checklist
- Select (m,b) to meet a parameter budget using the formula \#params \approx (1/2)(b - 1) d m; prefer smaller b with larger m for dense connectivity and higher expressivity per parameter.
- Initialize all skew-symmetric parameters to zero and use the Cayley transform to get identity-initialized orthogonal factors.
- Use multiplicative Dropout by replacing randomly selected butterfly components / blocks with identity during training to reduce overfitting.
- Apply BOFT selectively to large weight matrices, fold the learned R into pretrained weights for inference, and be mindful of modest extra training cost.

BOFT offers a flexible, structured middle ground between block-diagonal OFT and fully dense orthogonal finetuning. With identity init and multiplicative Dropout in your toolbox, you can control the capacity and regularity of the finetuning transform and integrate BOFT into standard finetuning workflows while keeping inference overhead zero after folding.
