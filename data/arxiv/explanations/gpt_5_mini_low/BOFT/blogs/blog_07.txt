\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# One Method, Many Domains: BOFT Across Vision, Language, and Diffusion Models

## What BOFT is (short version)

BOFT (Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning) is a parameter-efficient way to finetune large pretrained models. Instead of adding low-rank updates (like LoRA) or fully finetuning all weights, BOFT multiplies the pretrained layer by a learned orthogonal matrix R. The trick is that R is not stored as a dense d×d matrix (which costs O(d^2) parameters). BOFT factorizes R into a product of sparse, orthogonal "butterfly" components so that a dense transform is composed from O(d log d) parameters (roughly speaking) while keeping desirable orthogonality properties.

Key technical points:
- The orthogonal update keeps the pretrained weight singular values unchanged (multiplying on the left by an orthogonal R preserves spectral norm).
- Each butterfly component is parameterized so it stays orthogonal (e.g., via Cayley transforms / 2×2 block rotations), and the full R is a product of these components.
- BOFT uses identity initialization (R starts as I) and a multiplicative dropout adapted to the multiplicative nature of the update.

## Why this matters across domains

The paper applies BOFT to three different classes of foundation models: large vision transformers, large language models (LLMs), and text-to-image diffusion models. The same core idea—learn a constrained, structured orthogonal transform per layer—works across these very different architectures. That’s notable: a single finetuning mechanism that preserves spectral properties and introduces a controlled inductive bias generalizes across modalities.

Two reasons this shared structure helps cross-domain generalization:

1. Preserving pretrained geometry. Multiplying by an orthogonal R preserves pairwise angles among neurons and the singular values of pretrained layers. That helps keep the useful pretraining geometry intact, which is a domain-agnostic benefit: vision, language, and diffusion stacks all rely on pretrained structure.

2. A useful inductive bias from butterfly structure. Butterfly matrices are the sparse building blocks behind many classic fast transforms (FFT, Hadamard, etc.). By representing R as a product of such sparse orthogonal factors, BOFT restricts the finetuning hypothesis class to structured orthogonal transforms. This structured bias is expressive enough to approximate many useful dense transforms, yet small enough to regularize finetuning and reduce overfitting. Because many modalities encode global-local mixing patterns similar to Fourier/Hadamard-like behavior, the butterfly prior can be helpful across domains.

## Empirical takeaways (plain language)

The paper reports an extensive empirical study showing BOFT is practical and competitive when adapting large foundation models. While the paper tests a variety of tasks, the headline observations are:

- BOFT achieves strong downstream performance while using far fewer learnable parameters than a full orthogonal matrix or full finetuning. With the butterfly factorization (e.g., m ≈ log d, block size b = 2), the parameter count scales like O(d log d) instead of O(d^2).
- BOFT subsumes the earlier Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a special case: with particular settings, BOFT reduces to block-diagonal OFT or to a full orthogonal OFT. In practice, intermediate settings give a smoother trade-off between expressivity and regularization.
- Across vision transformers, LLMs, and text-to-image diffusion models, BOFT finetuning is stable and generalizes well. The spectral preservation and structured parameterization help training stability compared to some other parameter-efficient schemes.
- Compared to block-diagonal OFT, BOFT can produce dense orthogonal transforms with far fewer parameters and without imposing rigid block sparsity that breaks expressivity. Compared to low-rank additive schemes (LoRA), BOFT offers different regularization (multiplicative, orthogonality) that can outperform or complement low-rank updates depending on task and capacity budget.

The paper emphasizes that BOFT is a practical middle ground: more expressive than block-diagonal OFT at the same block size, but far more parameter-efficient than naive dense OFT.

(Note: the paper presents detailed experiments across many datasets and model families; this summary keeps the focus on qualitative conclusions and cross-domain applicability rather than listing task-by-task numbers.)

## How BOFT fits with other PEFT approaches

There are two broad families of parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT): additive low-rank updates (LoRA family) and multiplicative reparameterizations (OFT family). BOFT belongs to the multiplicative, orthogonality-preserving family but borrows the parameter-efficiency idea from sparse factorization:

- LoRA: add a low-rank matrix ΔW = AB^T. Very parameter-efficient; operates additively.
- OFT: multiply pretrained weights W^0 by an orthogonal R, preserving angle structure; but dense R is costly.
- BOFT: keep OFT’s orthogonality and spectral preservation, but parameterize R as a product of sparse orthogonal butterfly factors so you can get a dense effective transform while using O(d log d) parameters.

So BOFT is complementary to low-rank methods; it trades a different inductive bias (orthogonality + butterfly sparsity) for parameter savings.

## Practical notes and limitations

- Identity initialization is used so finetuning starts from the pretrained model exactly (R = I). That mirrors best practice in other PEFT methods (e.g., zero-init LoRA) and helps stable adaptation.
- BOFT introduces some extra training-time computation because the learned R is a product of several factors (so each forward pass multiplies through those factors). That increases runtime during training compared to single-matrix updates; however, at inference time the learned R can be folded back into the pretrained weights so there is no extra inference cost.
- BOFT is not guaranteed to be the absolute minimal parameterization for transmitting information; the paper frames the design in terms of an "information transmission" graph and points out that other network topologies (beyond butterfly) might yield different trade-offs. Finding the optimal sparse factorization remains an open area.
- More expressive factorizations (more components, different block sizes) increase representational power but also raise the effective parameter count; BOFT provides knobs to tune that trade-off.

## Bottom line

BOFT is a single, conceptually simple finetuning recipe that works across vision transformers, LLMs, and diffusion models. It keeps the principal benefits of orthogonal finetuning (spectral and relational preservation), while drastically improving parameter efficiency by factorizing the orthogonal update into butterfly-structured sparse factors. That combination—structured multiplicative updates, identity initialization, and a cross-domain-friendly inductive bias—explains why BOFT is effective across modalities and architectures.

If you care about parameter budgets and stable adaptation of large pretrained models in multiple domains, BOFT is a compelling option to try.
