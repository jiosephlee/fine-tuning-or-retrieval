\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Real vs. complex transforms: Can BOFT recover the DFT exactly using only real orthogonal factors?
Question:
The FFT (DFT) is unitary and generally complex-valued. The paper claims butterfly structures can recover many classic fast transforms. When restricting BOFT to real orthogonal matrices, can we exactly recover the DFT (perhaps via a real-valued embedding of complex arithmetic), or only approximate it? If exact recovery is possible, what is the construction and parameterization required?
Answer:
Short answer: You cannot realize the DFT exactly as a $d\times d$ real orthogonal matrix when $d>2$, because the DFT is complex unitary with nonzero imaginary entries. However you can recover the DFT exactly in BOFT if you either (a) allow complex-valued butterfly factors, or (b) embed complex arithmetic into a $2d$-dimensional real space and realize the DFT as a product of real orthogonal butterfly factors acting on that doubled real vector. If you insist on staying in the original real $d$-dimensional space with only real orthogonal $d\times d$ factors, you can only approximate the complex DFT.

Why (intuitively). The DFT matrix $F_d$ is unitary over $\mathbb{C}$ and, except for trivial small $d$, has genuinely complex entries (nonzero imaginary parts). A real orthogonal matrix $Q$ satisfies $Q^T Q = I$ and has only real entries, so there is no equality $Q = F_d$ (over $\mathbb{C}$) unless $F_d$ happens to be real (it is not for general $d$). So exact equality in the same $d$-dimensional real space is impossible.

Exact recovery via complex BOFT. The paper already notes that butterfly structures (the Cooley–Tukey factorization) can recover classic fast transforms such as the FFT. If BOFT is allowed to use complex-valued butterfly components, you can parameterize each complex $2\times 2$ (or small) butterfly factor exactly with the complex twiddle factors $e^{-2\pi i k / n}$ that Cooley–Tukey prescribes. Multiplying those complex sparse factors reproduces the exact DFT matrix $F_d$. That is the standard FFT factorization expressed as a product of sparse complex matrices; BOFT with complex-valued factors is a direct fit.

Exact recovery by real embedding ($2d\times 2d$ real orthogonal BOFT). A constructive way to remain strictly in real arithmetic is to embed complex vectors into $\mathbb{R}^{2d}$ by mapping each complex number $a + i b$ to the real pair $(a, b)$. Complex-linear maps (including multiplication by unit-modulus complex numbers) map to real $2\times 2$ rotation blocks. Concretely, represent $F_d$ acting on $\mathbb{C}^d$ by a $2d\times 2d$ real matrix
$$
F_{\mathrm{real}}=\begin{pmatrix}
\operatorname{Re}(F_d) & -\operatorname{Im}(F_d)\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Im}(F_d) & \operatorname{Re}(F_d)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Because $F_d$ is unitary, $F_{\mathrm{real}}$ is real orthogonal. The Cooley–Tukey FFT factorization of $F_d$ into sparse complex factors can be converted blockwise into a product of sparse real orthogonal factors of size $2d$: replace each complex scalar multiplier $a+ib$ by the real $2\times 2$ rotation matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a & -b\\[2pt] b & a\end{pmatrix}$ and replace complex $2\times 2$ butterfly blocks by their corresponding $4\times 4$ real block forms (or, equivalently, keep the same logical butterfly connectivity but with $2\times 2$ real blocks). Using BOFT with block-size $b=2$ (meaning the atomic orthogonal blocks are $2\times 2$ rotations) and $m = O(\log d)$ stages you get a product of $O(\log d)$ sparse real orthogonal components whose product equals $F_{\mathrm{real}}$ exactly. In short, $\mathrm{BOFT}(m=\log d, b=2)$ on a $2d$-dimensional real layer can represent the DFT exactly by parameterizing the exact cosine/sine values (not learned approximations).

What parameters are required. For the complex BOFT you set the butterfly factors to the exact complex twiddle values that Cooley–Tukey prescribes (these are fixed deterministic scalars $e^{-2\pi i k / n}$). For the real-embedded BOFT you set each $2\times 2$ block to the rotation matrix corresponding to the twiddle phase: for twiddle $e^{i\theta}$ use
$$
R(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\[2pt]\sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The rest of each butterfly stage is the same sparse connectivity/permutation pattern as the FFT. Thus exact recovery requires deterministically setting those rotation parameters to the cosine/sine of the FFT twiddle angles (or equivalently initializing/forcing those entries rather than learning arbitrary entries). If you allow learning, BOFT can still learn to match these values (given enough capacity), but exact equality requires the precise twiddle parameters.

Practical remark and connection to the paper. The paper emphasizes that butterfly factorizations can recover classic fast transforms (FFT, Hadamard, etc.). That statement is consistent: the FFT is a butterfly factorization but intrinsically complex; the paper’s BOFT uses small orthogonal blocks ($2\times 2$ rotations) and permutations, so BOFT exactly recovers those transforms either by using complex-valued factors or by doing the real $2d$ embedding where each complex multiply is a $2\times 2$ real rotation. If you restrict yourself to $d$-dimensional real orthogonal BOFT factors only, you cannot represent the complex phases exactly and therefore can only approximate the DFT.

Summary. Exact DFT recovery with only real orthogonal $d\times d$ factors is impossible. Exact recovery is possible if you either permit complex-valued butterfly factors (direct FFT factorization) or embed into $\mathbb{R}^{2d}$ and implement the complex phases as $2\times 2$ real rotation blocks within the butterfly stages (BOFT with block size implementing $2\times 2$ rotations and $m = O(\log d)$ stages).
