\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Deriving the parameter count \(\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm\) for BOFT(m, b)
Question:
The paper states that BOFT(\(m,b\)) "takes \(\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm\) effective trainable parameters for finetuning a linear layer of size \(d\times n\)." Could someone provide a step-by-step derivation? Specifically: (1) For a single butterfly component with block size \(2b\times 2b\), how many free parameters does one orthogonal block have? (2) How many such blocks exist per component at width \(d\)? (3) How does orthogonality reduce the parameter count compared to counting nonzeros? (4) Why does multiplying by \(m\) components give the \(\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm\) figure?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper’s formula comes from counting the intrinsic degrees of freedom of orthogonal $2b$-by-$2b$ blocks, how many such blocks appear in a width-$d$ layer, and then multiplying by the number of butterfly components $m$. A careful bookkeeping of the paper’s naming convention (their reported BOFT$(m,b)$ uses $2b$-by-$2b$ orthogonal blocks internally) yields

$$
\text{total effective params} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}(b-1)\,d\,m.
$$

Derivation (step‑by‑step and intuitive):

1) Free parameters in one orthogonal $2b$-by-$2b$ block.  
An orthogonal matrix of size $K$ has exactly the dimension of the orthogonal group $O(K)$ as its degrees of freedom, namely $K(K-1)/2$. This is easy to see: an orthogonal matrix is determined by orthonormal column directions, and the constraints reduce the naive $K^2$ entries down to $K(K-1)/2$ independent parameters (equivalently the space of skew‑symmetric generators has that dimension, which is exactly what Cayley parameterization uses). For a block of size $2b$ we therefore have

$$
\text{DOF per block} \;=\; \frac{(2b)(2b-1)}{2} \;=\; b(2b-1).
$$

Intuition: a full $2b$-by-$2b$ dense matrix has $(2b)^2$ numbers, but the orthogonality constraints remove $2b$ constraints on column norms and $(2b)(2b-1)/2$ constraints on pairwise orthogonality; the remaining independent parameters equal the group dimension above.

2) Number of such blocks per butterfly component at width $d$.  
Each butterfly component (as defined in the paper’s block-butterfly construction) is block-diagonal with blocks of size $2b$-by-$2b$. Therefore the number of blocks in a $d$-by-$d$ component is

$$
\text{blocks per component} \;=\; \frac{d}{2b},
$$

assuming $d$ is a multiple of $2b$ (as in the paper’s radix-2 construction).

3) Why orthogonality reduces the parameter count versus counting nonzeros.  
If you counted nonzeros naively you would say each $2b$-by-$2b$ dense block contributes $(2b)^2$ parameters. Orthogonality couples those entries: they are not independent. Using Cayley/skew‑symmetric parameterization, each orthogonal block is generated from a skew‑symmetric matrix of size $2b$ whose number of independent entries is exactly $(2b)(2b-1)/2$. So orthogonality replaces the naive $(2b)^2$ free entries with the much smaller $b(2b-1)$. Intuitively, orthogonality enforces many linear and quadratic constraints among entries, drastically lowering the degrees of freedom.

4) Putting it together and the role of $m$ components.  
Multiply the DOF per block by the number of blocks per component to get the effective trainable parameters in one butterfly component:

$$
\text{params per component} \;=\; \frac{d}{2b}\cdot b(2b-1) \;=\; \frac{d(2b-1)}{2}.
$$

BOFT composes $m$ such components (each component is an orthogonal block-structured factor and you train the parameters of all $m$ factors), so total effective trainable parameters are

$$
\text{total} \;=\; m\cdot \frac{d(2b-1)}{2}.
$$

Now about the paper’s notation: the authors choose to report BOFT as BOFT$(m,b)$ where their internal blocks are size $2b$-by-$2b$ (they write “we denote BOFT with $\bm{R}(m,\tfrac{b}{2})$ as BOFT$(m,b)$”). If you rewrite the last expression in terms of the paper’s reported scalar $b$ (i.e., substitute $b_{\text{internal}} = b/2$), the factor $2b-1$ becomes $b-1$ and you obtain the stated formula

$$
\text{total} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}(b-1)\,d\,m,
$$

which is exactly the paper’s claimed effective trainable parameter count for BOFT$(m,b)$.

Compact recap: one $2b$-by-$2b$ orthogonal block has $\tfrac{(2b)(2b-1)}{2}$ degrees of freedom, there are $d/(2b)$ such blocks per component, orthogonality reduces free parameters from $(2b)^2$ to that group dimension, and multiplying by $m$ components (and applying the paper’s block-size naming convention) produces $\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$.
