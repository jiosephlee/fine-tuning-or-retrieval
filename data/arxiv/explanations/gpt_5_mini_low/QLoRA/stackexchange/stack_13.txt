\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### Clarifying the notation: is ‘BrainFloat16’ in the text a typo for ‘BFloat16’?
Question:
In the QLoRA definition and summary, the paper alternates between “BFloat16” and “BrainFloat” (e.g., “16-bit BrainFloat”). Is this just a typo or colloquial term for the standard BF16 format? There is no separate ‘BrainFloat’ format that differs from BF16 in this context, right?
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — “BrainFloat16” in the text is not a new, different numeric format; it’s the paper referring to the standard $BF16$ format. Treat instances of “BrainFloat” / “$16$-bit BrainFloat” in the paper as the usual $BFloat16$ computation data type.

Why: the paper repeatedly uses the abbreviation $BF16$ and also explicitly writes equations and descriptions where the computation dtype is $BF16$ (e.g., dequantization to $BF16$, and the statement that QLoRA “has one storage data type (usually $4$-bit NormalFloat) and a computation data type ($16$-bit BrainFloat)”). There is no description of any alternate $16$-bit float format in the paper, nor any different bit layout or exponent/mantissa specification; the contextual usage makes clear the authors mean the standard $BFloat16$ (the $16$-bit “brain/TPU” float commonly used for training/inference). In other words, “BrainFloat” is a colloquial synonym (or a minor wording inconsistency), not a different numeric representation.

One more clarification: this is distinct from the paper’s new $NF4$ storage datatype ($4$-bit NormalFloat) and from the double-quantized constants. $BF16$ / “BrainFloat” is only the higher-precision computation dtype you dequantize to when doing forward/backward math; $NF4$ is the low-bit storage format.
