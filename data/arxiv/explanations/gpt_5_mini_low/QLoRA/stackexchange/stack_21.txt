\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### Do the per-block scales $c_2$ use absolute max or some robust statistic, and how are they quantized?
Question:
For the first-level quantization of $\mathbf{W}$ into NF4, the paper mentions rescaling by absolute max per block, yielding scales $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$. Before double quantization, do they apply any clipping or robust estimation (like percentile-based scaling) to reduce sensitivity to single outliers? When quantizing $c_2$ to FP8 using blocks of 256, do they use absolute max again or a different procedure due to $c_2$ being strictly positive? They mention subtracting the mean to center around zero—does this imply a symmetric FP8 representation, and does mean subtraction introduce bias on dequant?
Answer:
Short answer: For the first-level quantization of the weight blocks the paper uses the per-block absolute maximum (absmax) to form the scale constants $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ (no percentile clipping or other robust estimator is described). Those $c_2$ values are then double-quantized in blocks of $256$ to an $8$-bit float representation after subtracting the block mean to center the (strictly positive) $c_2$ values. The mean subtraction is undone at dequantization, so it does not introduce a permanent bias if implemented correctly.

Explanation and how this follows from the paper
The paper describes two stages:

1) First-level (weight) quantization. Weight tensor blocks (they use blocksize $64$ for $\mathbf{W}$) are independently scaled by their absolute maximum and quantized into NF4. This is the standard "absmax per block" rescaling described in the Background and in the NF4 procedure. The paper does not describe any percentile-based clipping or robust-statistic replacement for the absmax, so you should assume absmax is used (and therefore the per-block scale $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ is the block absmax-derived constant).

2) Double quantization of the scales. The per-block scales $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ (positive numbers) are treated as the inputs to a second quantization step to save memory. The paper explicitly states they use $8$-bit floats with a blocksize of $256$ for this second quantization and that, because the $c_2$ are positive, they subtract the mean from $c_2$ before quantizing to “center the values around zero and make use of symmetric quantization.” In other words, they shift the $c_2$ block by its mean so the centered values are roughly zero-mean, quantize those centered values using a symmetric FP8 scheme, and store whatever ancillary scalar(s) are needed (the paper calls the remaining second-level constants $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$).

Why the mean subtraction and symmetric quantization?
The motivation given is practical: symmetric quantization around zero is more efficient (and simpler) for many quantizers. Since $c_2$ are strictly positive, directly applying a symmetric quantizer would waste half the representational range on negative numbers. Subtracting the mean recenters the block so the symmetric FP8 can be used more effectively. Using a coarser blocksize ($256$) for $c_2$ is chosen because the scales are less sensitive to fine-grained per-weight differences and no performance degradation was observed.

Does mean subtraction introduce bias on dequantization?
Not if the pipeline is implemented properly. The paper’s doubleDequant operation (see equations (6) and (7)) describes a two-step dequant: first dequantize the quantized $c_2$ back to (centered) FP32 using the stored second-level constants, then dequantize the weights using those recovered $c_2$. The mean that was subtracted before FP8 quantization must be added back when reconstructing the FP32 $c_2$. If you fail to add the mean back you will get biased (too small / shifted) scales and therefore biased weight reconstructions, but the paper’s description implies the mean is inverted as part of double dequantization. So there is no permanent bias introduced by mean-subtraction as long as the inverse operation (add mean back) is performed.

Additional intuitions and trade-offs
Using absmax per block for the weights is simple and fast but sensitive to single outliers; that sensitivity is why the paper uses relatively small weight blocks ($64$) and blocks the quantization so outliers are contained locally. Double-quantizing the scales with a larger block ($256$) is a memory/time trade-off: you save memory by compressing many small scales together, and subtracting the mean allows symmetric FP8 to represent the centered magnitudes efficiently. The overall double-quantization design is presented in the paper as reducing memory (they show the bit-per-parameter arithmetic) while not degrading performance empirically.

Bottom line: first-level weight scales use per-block absmax (no robust percentile clipping described); second-level quantization of those positive scales is done after subtracting the block mean and using symmetric FP8 quantization in blocks of $256$. The mean must be added back during dequantization, so no dequant bias is introduced when implemented correctly.
