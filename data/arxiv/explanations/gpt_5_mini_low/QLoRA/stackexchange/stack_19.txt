\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### How is bias handled: are bias terms stored/updated in 16-bit or quantized with NF4?
Question:
The paper focuses on quantizing weight matrices and adding LoRA adapters. What about bias vectors in linear layers and layernorm parameters? Are they kept in BF16/FP16 and updated, frozen, or quantized to NF4? If biases remain in higher precision while weights are NF4, could that explain part of the performance parity with 16-bit full finetuning?
Answer:
Short answer: QLoRA quantizes the pretrained linear weight matrices to $\text{NF4}$, but it does not quantize or update the pretrained bias vectors or LayerNorm parameters — those remain in the higher-precision computation format ($\text{BF16}/\text{FP16}$) as part of the frozen base model. During finetuning only the LoRA adapter parameters (stored/updated in $16$-bit) receive gradients and change.

Why this follows from the paper: the paper repeatedly describes QLoRA as “backpropagat[ing] gradients through a frozen, $4$-bit quantized pretrained language model into Low Rank Adapters (LoRA).” In the formal layer equation the authors show the forward pass as
$$
\mathbf{Y}^{\text{BF16}} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}}\ \text{doubleDequant}(c_1,c_2,\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}})\ +\ \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_1^{\text{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_2^{\text{BF16}},
$$
and they explicitly note “for parameter updates only the gradient with respect to the error for the adapters weights … are needed, and not for $4$-bit weights $\partial E/\partial\mathbf{W}$.” The base model is described as frozen (no weight updates), and the authors emphasize that the storage datatype is low-precision ($\text{NF4}$) while the computation datatype is $\text{BF16}$ (dequantize-to-$\text{BF16}$ for forward/backward). That design implies biases and LayerNorm parameters, being part of the pretrained (frozen) model parameters, are stored/handled as part of the frozen model and are not the trainable LoRA parameters. The paper does not describe a separate quantization of biases or LayerNorm gains/offsets to $\text{NF4}$, nor does it describe updating them during QLoRA finetuning.

Intuition and consequence: leaving small but important components (biases, LayerNorm scale/shift) in $16$-bit and frozen while quantizing the large weight matrices to $\text{NF4}$ is a natural engineering choice. Biases and LayerNorm parameters are few in number and often sensitive to low-precision noise; keeping them in higher precision avoids some quantization-induced errors while saving the bulk of memory by quantizing the giant weight tensors. Because the base model (including its biases/LayerNorms) remains frozen, any remaining performance parity with 16-bit full finetuning comes from two sources: (1) LoRA adapters, which are trained in $16$-bit and can compensate for quantization error by modifying the effective linear transforms, and (2) the fact that critical small parameters (biases / LayerNorm) remain higher precision and hence do not introduce additional numerical degradation. The paper’s claim that they “only compute weight gradients for the LoRA parameters” supports this interpretation.

If you need to be absolutely certain for a particular codebase or release, check the implementation (the authors released code/kernels): in common QLoRA/bitsandbytes implementations the large linear weights are stored in $4$-bit $\text{NF4}$ with dequantization to $\text{BF16}$ at runtime, while biases and LayerNorm parameters remain in $16$-bit and are not altered by QLoRA unless you explicitly choose a PEFT variant that updates them (e.g., BitFit).
