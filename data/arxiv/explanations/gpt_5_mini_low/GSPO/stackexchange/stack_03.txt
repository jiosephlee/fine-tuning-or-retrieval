\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Does GSPO’s gradient correspond to REINFORCE with a modified baseline or objective?
Question:
Ignoring clipping, the GSPO gradient is

$\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\left[s(y;\theta)\, \widehat{A}(y)\, \frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_{\theta}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})\right]$.

Can this be derived as REINFORCE on some transformed objective like $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\theta}}[\tilde{A}(y)]$ with a particular choice of $\tilde{A}$ or baseline? If yes, what is $\tilde{A}$ explicitly, and how does the $1/|y|$ interact with the baseline to keep the estimator unbiased (prior to clipping)?
Answer:
Short answer: No — GSPO’s gradient cannot be written as a plain REINFORCE gradient $ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y)] $ with a $\tilde{A}$ that is independent of $\theta$ (or with a simple transformed baseline). You can algebraically solve for a candidate $\tilde{A}$, but it depends on $\theta$ (through the importance ratio), so the equality does not reduce GSPO to standard REINFORCE without extra terms. Below I show the algebra, give the explicit ($\theta$‑dependent) expression, and explain why the $1/|y|$ and baseline issues prevent an unbiased REINFORCE interpretation.

Derivation and intuition grounded in the paper
The paper gives the unclipped GSPO gradient (writing $s(y;\theta) = \left(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\right)^{1/|y|}$ and $\hat{A}(y)$ for the group advantage):

$\nabla_\theta J_{\text{GSPO}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Big[ s(y;\theta)\, \hat{A}(y)\cdot \frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t|\cdot)\Big].$

Note that $\sum_{t} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t|\cdot) = \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)$, so this is

(1) $\nabla_\theta J_{\text{GSPO}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Big[ s(y;\theta)\, \hat{A}(y)\cdot \frac{1}{|y|}\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)\Big].$

Compare to REINFORCE: for any function $\tilde{A}(y)$ that does not depend on $\theta$, we have

$\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y)] = \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y)\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)]
= \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Big[ \Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\Big)\, \tilde{A}(y)\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)\Big].$

To make the REINFORCE-form integrand match (1), we would need

$\Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\Big)\, \tilde{A}(y)
= s(y;\theta)\, \hat{A}(y)\cdot \frac{1}{|y|}
= \Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\Big)^{1/|y|}\, \hat{A}(y)\cdot \frac{1}{|y|}.$

Solving algebraically for $\tilde{A}(y)$ yields

(2) $\tilde{A}(y) = \frac{1}{|y|}\, \hat{A}(y)\, \Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\Big)^{1/|y|-1}.$

This is the explicit expression you asked for. But notice the crucial point: $\tilde{A}(y)$ depends on $\theta$ through the factor $\left(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\right)^{1/|y|-1}$. That dependence breaks the usual REINFORCE identity, because when $\tilde{A}$ depends on $\theta$ the gradient of $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y,\theta)]$ is

$\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y,\theta)] = \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta] + \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \tilde{A}],$

so there is an extra $\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta \tilde{A}]$ term which is not present in the GSPO gradient (and in general is nonzero). Therefore GSPO is not equivalent to vanilla REINFORCE on a $\theta$‑independent advantage; the best you can do algebraically is (2), which makes $\tilde{A}$ $\theta$‑dependent and thus changes the differentiation.

Intuition: why the mismatch arises
REINFORCE works through the identity $\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[f(y)] = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[f(y)\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)]$ only when $f$ does not depend on $\theta$. GSPO inserts a per-sequence scale $s(y;\theta)$ which is a fractional power of the full importance weight, i.e., an atypical dependence on $\pi_\theta$. To convert that scale into the prefactor $\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}$ that REINFORCE produces when changing measure from $\pi_\theta$ to $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$, you must move a factor of $\left(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\right)^{\text{something}}$ into $\tilde{A}$; that necessarily makes $\tilde{A}$ depend on $\theta$. So GSPO’s gradient is the gradient of its own objective $J_{\text{GSPO}}(\theta)$ (as the paper shows), but not the gradient of $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y)]$ for any $\tilde{A}$ that is independent of $\theta$.

Role of the $1/|y|$ and baselines (unbiasedness prior to clipping)
The factor $1/|y|$ in the GSPO gradient is a per‑sequence length normalization that turns the sum of token log‑gradients into an average token gradient. Algebraically it is just a scalar factor that can be absorbed into any candidate advantage (see (2) above) — it does not change the core argument about $\theta$‑dependence. From the unbiasedness perspective (before clipping), subtracting a baseline $b(x)$ that depends only on the query $x$ (and not on the token sequence $y$) is safe: $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[b(x)\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)] = b(x)\, \nabla_\theta \int \pi_\theta(y)\, dy = 0$, so the estimator remains unbiased. However, any baseline that depends on $y$ or on $\theta$ in a way that correlates with $\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)$ will in general introduce bias unless the baseline’s $\theta$‑dependence is accounted for in the gradient (i.e., you include the extra $\nabla_\theta \tilde{A}$ term). In GSPO the group advantage $\hat{A}(y)$ is computed from the sampled group and does not depend on $\theta$, so subtracting a group mean baseline is valid and keeps the estimator unbiased (again, ignoring clipping). But trying to absorb the $\left(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\right)^{1/|y|-1}$ factor into a “baseline” that is independent of $\theta$ is impossible, because that factor is inherently $\theta$‑dependent.

Summary
You can write a formal equality by defining

$\tilde{A}(y,\theta) = \frac{1}{|y|}\, \hat{A}(y)\, \Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y)}\Big)^{1/|y|-1},$

and then GSPO’s integrand equals the REINFORCE-type term $\Big(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Big)\, \tilde{A}(y,\theta)\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y)$. But $\tilde{A}$ depends on $\theta$, so the gradient of $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[\tilde{A}(y,\theta)]$ contains extra terms and therefore GSPO is not REINFORCE on a $\theta$‑independent transformed objective. The $1/|y|$ is simply a length normalization you can fold into the advantage; unbiasedness (before clipping) is preserved only when baselines do not depend on the action sequence $y$ (or on $\theta$) in a way that would introduce extra gradient terms.
