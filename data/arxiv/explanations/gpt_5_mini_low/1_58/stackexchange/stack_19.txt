\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### Training from scratch with 1.58-bit weights: what optimizer and schedules were stable?
Question:
Quantized-from-scratch training can be unstable. What optimizer (AdamW?), weight decay, learning rate schedule, warmup, and regularization were used to reach parity at 100B and 2T tokens? Any special tricks (e.g., gradient clipping thresholds, loss scaling, EMA of weights before quantization, knowledge distillation, or auxiliary losses) needed specifically for the ternary setting?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper does not give an explicit training-hyperparameter table (optimizer, exact weight decay, LR schedule, warmup, gradient clipping, EMA, or distillation) for the runs that reached parity at $100\mathrm{B}/2\mathrm{T}$ tokens. It describes the model components and the quantization/activation design that enabled stable from‑scratch training, but it does not report the concrete optimizer and schedule numbers in the main text.

What the paper does report (relevant to stability)
The authors emphasize architectural and quantization choices rather than a novel optimizer/schedule. Concretely, BitNet b1.58 was trained from scratch with the following reported design decisions that affect stability:

- LLaMA-like backbone components: RMSNorm (not LayerNorm), SwiGLU feed‑forward, rotary embeddings, and removal of all biases. These choices change the numerical behaviour compared to a vanilla Transformer and were intentionally adopted to match open‑source LLaMA setups.
- Weight quantization: an absmean scaling followed by RoundClip to $\{-1,0,1\}$; i.e. $\widetilde W = \mathrm{RoundClip}\big(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1\big)$ with $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$. That scaling is applied per weight matrix and reduces dynamic range before ternarization.
- Activations: $8$-bit activations, scaled per token into the symmetric range $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ (they explicitly removed a per‑tensor zero point and token‑wise scaled activations).
- Trained on the RedPajama recipe for $100\mathrm{B}$ tokens for parity experiments, and a StableLM‑style $2\mathrm{T}$ tokens recipe for the long‑training run; reported results are matched to FP16 LLaMA baselines trained on the same data/token counts.

What the paper does NOT report
The manuscript does not state (at least in the provided text): optimizer family (Adam, AdamW, or variants), learning‑rate schedule shape (cosine, linear decay, polynomial), initial LR, weight‑decay constant, warmup length or schedule, gradient‑clipping thresholds, loss scaling strategy, use of EMA on weights, knowledge distillation, or auxiliary losses. There is also no explicit mention of special tricks that are uniquely required for the ternary setting (e.g., teacher distillation or per‑layer bespoke schedules). The experiments claim “trained from scratch” with the described quantization and $8$-bit activations and show parity, but the low‑level optimizer/hyperparameter table is absent.

How to proceed if you need the exact recipe
Because the paper omits those hyperparameters, the reliable ways to get them are: check the paper appendix / supplementary material (if available), the project code release / training scripts (authors often release an optimizer config), or contact the authors. The paper’s repeated reference to LLaMA‑alike components and to following existing data recipes (RedPajama, StableLM) suggests they likely used an optimizer and schedule similar to common LLaMA reproduction recipes, but that is an inference, not a statement from the paper.

Practical guidance (not claimed by the paper) — if you are attempting similar from‑scratch ternary training
If you need to reproduce or experiment and cannot get the authors’ exact config, use a conservative LLaMA‑style recipe as a starting point: AdamW with modest weight decay, a linear warmup then cosine or linear decay over training tokens, per‑step gradient clipping (e.g., $1.0$–$5.0$) if training is unstable, mixed‑precision loss scaling for numeric stability, and monitor training for divergence when applying the absmean ternarization. The paper’s stability seems to come from the combination of RMSNorm/SwiGLU/rotary embeddings and the absmean per‑matrix scaling before rounding; keep those exactly as reported. But remember these suggested hyperparameters are not given in the paper and so are only pragmatic defaults, not claims about what the authors used.

Summary
The paper documents the architectural/quantization ingredients that enabled stable from‑scratch training to parity (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, rotary embedding, removal of biases, absmean ternarization, and token‑wise symmetric activation scaling), and it reports token budgets/datasets ($100\mathrm{B}$ and $2\mathrm{T}$) and evaluation. It does not report the explicit optimizer, weight‑decay, LR schedule, warmup length, or other low‑level training hyperparameters or special tricks; for those you should consult the authors’ code or ask the authors directly.
