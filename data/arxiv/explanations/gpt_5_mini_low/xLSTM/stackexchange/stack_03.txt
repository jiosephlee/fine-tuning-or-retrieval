\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### Why does replacing $(i_t, f_t)$ with their stabilized versions $(i'_t, f'_t)$ not change outputs or gradients?
Question:
Equations (18–20) introduce a stabilizer state $m_t$ and define $i'_t = \exp(\log i_t - m_t)$ and $f'_t = \exp(\log f_t + m_{t-1} - m_t)$. The paper states (Appendix referenced) that using $(i'_t, f'_t)$ in place of $(i_t, f_t)$ leaves both the forward outputs and the loss gradients w.r.t. parameters unchanged. Could someone sketch the proof? Is it because $m_t$ factors out as a common rescaling over all accumulated terms in $c_t$ and $n_t$, which subsequently cancels in $\tilde{h}_t$ due to division by $n_t$ (and similarly in backprop)? A concise derivation would help.
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — the stabilizer just applies a time-dependent scalar rescaling to the internal accumulation states, and that scalar cancels wherever those states are used to produce the network outputs. Concretely, if you define scaled states
$$
\hat c_t := e^{m_t} c_t,\qquad \hat n_t := e^{m_t} n_t,
$$
then the stabilized recurrences (with $i'_t,f'_t$ as in the paper) transform into the original recurrences driven by the unstabilized gates $i_t,f_t$. Because the final normalized hidden $\tilde h_t = c_t/n_t$ equals $\hat c_t/\hat n_t$, the forward outputs are identical; and hence, by the chain rule, the loss gradients w.r.t. parameters are identical as well. Below is the brief derivation.

Derivation
Define the stabilizer and stabilized gates as in the paper:
$$
m_t=\max(\log f_t + m_{t-1},\; \log i_t),\qquad
i'_t=\exp(\log i_t-m_t)=e^{\tilde i_t-m_t},\qquad
f'_t=\exp(\log f_t+m_{t-1}-m_t).
$$
The sLSTM recurrences with the stabilized gates are
$$
c_t = f'_t\,c_{t-1}+ i'_t\,z_t,\qquad
n_t = f'_t\,n_{t-1}+ i'_t.
$$
Now multiply both equations by $e^{m_t}$ and define $\hat c_t:=e^{m_t}c_t$, $\hat n_t:=e^{m_t}n_t$. Using the definitions of $i'_t,f'_t$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat c_t &= e^{m_t}c_t
= e^{m_t}\big(f'_t c_{t-1} + i'_t z_t\big) \\
&= e^{m_t}\Big(e^{\log f_t + m_{t-1}-m_t} c_{t-1} + e^{\log i_t - m_t} z_t\Big) \\
&= e^{m_{t-1}} f_t\, c_{t-1} + e^{\log i_t} z_t
= f_t \,\hat c_{t-1} + i_t\, z_t.
\end{aligned}
$$
Exactly the same algebra gives
$$
\hat n_t = f_t\,\hat n_{t-1} + i_t.
$$
So the scaled pair $(\hat c_t,\hat n_t)$ obeys the original (unstabilized) update driven by $i_t,f_t$. The hidden pre-output is $\tilde h_t = c_t/n_t$, but
$$
\tilde h_t = \frac{c_t}{n_t} = \frac{e^{-m_t}\hat c_t}{e^{-m_t}\hat n_t} = \frac{\hat c_t}{\hat n_t},
$$
so $\tilde h_t$ computed with stabilized gates equals the $\tilde h_t$ you would get from the original recurrence on $\hat c,\hat n$. Therefore every network output that depends on $\tilde h_t$ (and later on $h_t=o_t\tilde h_t$) is unchanged by the stabilization.

Why gradients are unchanged
Since the network outputs (and hence the loss) are identical under the two implementations, the total derivative of the loss with respect to any model parameter is the same. More operationally, backpropagation through the stabilized computation is equivalent to backpropagation through the original recurrence on $\hat c,\hat n$ followed by a multiplication by $e^{m_t}$ at the forward pass and its reciprocal in the backward pass; these scalar multiplications cancel when producing gradients of the loss with respect to the gate preactivations and ultimately the parameters. Put differently, the forward mapping from parameters to final outputs is the same under both formulations, so their gradients (Jacobian of loss w.r.t. parameters) coincide.

Intuition
The stabilizer chooses a per-time scalar $e^{m_t}$ so that the exponentially large/small gate products are pulled into a single factor. That factor multiplies both the numerators and denominators of the normalized cell output $c_t/n_t$, hence it cancels. Stabilization thus prevents numerical overflow/underflow without changing the mathematical computation the network performs, nor the gradients coming from the loss.

This is the argument sketched in the paper’s appendix; the essential point is the exact algebraic cancellation shown above.
