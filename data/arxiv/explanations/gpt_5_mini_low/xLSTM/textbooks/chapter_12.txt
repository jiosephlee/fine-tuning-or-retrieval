\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

Chapter 12: Empirical Study III: xLSTM as a Large Language Model (300B Tokens)

# Setup: dataset, model sizes, and baselines
This study evaluates xLSTM when scaled to a large-language-model training regime. All models are trained on the SlimPajama corpus with a total of $300\text{B}$ tokens. The experiments span four model sizes: approximately $125\text{M}$, $350\text{M}$, $760\text{M}$, and $1.3\text{B}$ parameters. We compare xLSTM variants to three representative baselines: Llama (a Transformer), Mamba (a State Space Model, SSM), and RWKV-4 (a recurrent neural architecture). For xLSTM we report results for at least two block compositions: xLSTM[1:0] (all mLSTM blocks) and xLSTM[7:1] (majority mLSTM blocks with some sLSTM blocks). All models are trained with context length $2048$ during training; evaluation includes extrapolation to longer contexts unless otherwise stated.

The evaluation suite covers several complementary probes of model capability: sequence-length extrapolation, held-out validation perplexity for next-token prediction, performance on downstream common-sense reasoning tasks, per-domain next-token perplexity across the PALOMA dataset of $571$ text domains, and empirical scaling-law behavior. We also measure text generation time and decoding throughput to characterize inference trade-offs.

# Length extrapolation from $2048$ to $16384$ context
A critical question for any sequence model is how well it generalizes to longer contexts than seen during training. For this, the large ($1.3\text{B}$) checkpoints were trained at context length $2048$ and then evaluated at context lengths extended up to $16384$ ($16\text{k}$). xLSTM models retain low perplexity as the context is lengthened, whereas the alternative models degrade more rapidly.

Specifically, xLSTM exhibits robust extrapolation: perplexity remains near the levels attained at $2048$ context length even when the context is increased multiple-fold to $16384$. By contrast, the Transformer (Llama) and the SSM (Mamba) show larger increases in perplexity as the context grows, reflecting their more fragile extrapolation in this setting. RWKV-4 also degrades more quickly than xLSTM. These results indicate that the xLSTM memory structures and gating (in particular the mLSTM matrix memory and sLSTM mixing when present) support retention and retrieval of information across much longer horizons than encountered in training.

# Validation perplexity across model sizes
Validation perplexity on the SlimPajama held-out set is used as a primary language-modeling metric across the four model sizes. Both xLSTM variants evaluated—xLSTM[1:0] and xLSTM[7:1]—achieve the best validation perplexities among the compared methods at every model scale. This pattern holds consistently from the smallest ($125\text{M}$) to the largest ($1.3\text{B}$) models in the experiment.

The superiority of xLSTM in validation perplexity can be attributed to two architectural improvements over traditional LSTMs and to differences from the baselines: (i) exponential gating combined with normalization and stabilization, which enables better write/decay dynamics and revision of stored information, and (ii) the mLSTM matrix memory (in xLSTM[1:0]) that substantially increases storage capacity and retrieval fidelity for rare and associative information. The ablation studies reported elsewhere in the work attribute the largest gains to these two modifications.

# Downstream common-sense tasks
Beyond next-token perplexity, models are evaluated on a collection of downstream tasks that probe common-sense and reasoning abilities. Across model sizes and tasks, xLSTM generally leads in performance: the majority of tasks show xLSTM ahead of Llama, Mamba, and RWKV-4. The reported aggregate results indicate that the improved sequence modeling and memory behavior of xLSTM translate into better performance on reasoning-oriented downstream evaluations.

One notable exception is the ARC benchmark (a multiple-choice reasoning dataset). On ARC, Mamba sometimes attains the best scores, outperforming xLSTM on that specific task in certain model-size settings. Thus, while xLSTM is broadly superior across many common-sense tasks, particular datasets and reasoning formats may still favor alternative inductive biases present in other model families.

# PALOMA: per-domain next-token prediction across 571 domains
To examine domain-level generalization, models are evaluated for next-token prediction perplexity across the PALOMA suite of $571$ text domains, which encompass a very wide range of styles and topics (from major news outlets to specialized Reddit communities). Results show that xLSTM[1:0] is the strongest performer by a wide margin:

- xLSTM[1:0] has lower perplexity than Mamba on $568$ out of $571$ domains (approximately $99.5\%$).
- xLSTM[1:0] has lower perplexity than Llama on $486$ out of $571$ domains (about $85.1\%$).
- xLSTM[1:0] has lower perplexity than RWKV-4 on $570$ out of $571$ domains (about $99.8\%$).

These counts indicate that the mLSTM-rich variant (xLSTM[1:0]) not only attains better average perplexity but also systematically outperforms the baselines across nearly the entire range of evaluated domains. This domain-level dominance supports the claim that the matrix memory and gating mechanisms equip xLSTM to capture varied distributional properties and long-range dependencies that differ between domains.

# Scaling laws and extrapolation to larger models
Scaling behavior is analyzed in the sense of power-law relationships between model size and held-out loss, following the general framework established in prior scaling-law work. All considered model families (xLSTM, Mamba, Llama, RWKV-4) follow a power-law trend in validation loss versus parameter count, but with different vertical offsets. xLSTM displays the best offset among the compared methods: for a given parameter budget, xLSTM achieves lower loss than Mamba, which in turn outperforms Llama, and RWKV-4 is the least favorable of the group.

Because the slopes of these power laws are similar across families, the relative ordering persists as models get larger; thus the offset advantage of xLSTM is likely to hold at larger scales. In short, xLSTM follows the same predictable power-law scaling, but from a stronger starting point, suggesting that larger-scale xLSTM models would be competitive with or superior to large Transformers and SSMs when trained on sufficiently large corpora.

# Generation time and decoding throughput: linear scaling and constant-memory advantage
Inference experiments measure (i) single-sequence generation time and (ii) maximal decoding throughput for varying batch sizes and prefill lengths. These benchmarks highlight important practical trade-offs between autoregressive architectures.

Text generation time for xLSTM scales linearly with context length in wall-clock time, which is characteristic of recurrent and linear-context-complexity models. In contrast, Transformer-based models like Llama exhibit quadratic scaling in generation time with respect to context length when using naive attention recomputation, unless highly optimized caching or specialized attention kernels are used. In the experiments, generation runs use batch size $1$ and a prefill length of $16$; this prefill setting is favorable to the Transformer because it can amortize some attention computation via caches, yet xLSTM still shows linear scaling and competitive wall-clock times.

Beyond generation latency, xLSTM benefits from constant memory usage with respect to context length because its state does not store per-token keys and values in an ever-growing cache. This constant-memory property allows xLSTM to utilize much larger batch sizes during decoding than the Transformer before hitting memory limits, which in turn enables higher overall throughput. Empirically, xLSTM achieves the highest maximal throughput among the compared models in the reported experiments, particularly at large batch sizes where Transformer memory bounds become the bottleneck.

It is worth noting implementation caveats: the mLSTM CUDA kernels used are not yet as optimized as FlashAttention-style kernels, so measured absolute runtimes are not necessarily at the theoretical optimum for xLSTM. Nevertheless, the algorithmic complexity and constant-memory profile give xLSTM a clear practical advantage in high-throughput and long-context decoding scenarios.

# Summary
When trained at LLM scale ($300\text{B}$ tokens) across sizes from $125\text{M}$ to $1.3\text{B}$, xLSTM variants—especially the mLSTM-dominant xLSTM[1:0]—demonstrate robust long-context extrapolation, best-in-class validation perplexities, dominant per-domain performance across the PALOMA suite, and broadly strong results on downstream common-sense tasks (with isolated exceptions like ARC where Mamba sometimes leads). Scaling laws reveal a favorable offset for xLSTM, suggesting advantages will persist at larger parameter counts. Finally, xLSTM’s linear generation-time scaling and constant-memory decoding translate into superior decoding throughput in practice, making it an attractive architecture for long-context and production inference settings.
