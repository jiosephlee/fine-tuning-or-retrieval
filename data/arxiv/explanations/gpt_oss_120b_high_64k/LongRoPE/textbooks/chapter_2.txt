\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 2: 2. Positional Embeddings in Transformers – Focus on Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE)

Transformers process an input sequence purely by pairwise interactions between token representations.  
Because these interactions are *permutation‑invariant*, a model has no way to tell whether token A comes before token B unless an explicit notion of position is supplied.  Position information therefore constitutes an essential complement to the self‑attention mechanism.  The following sections develop a systematic view of how order is injected into Transformers, contrast the classic schemes with modern rotary encoding, and explain why Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) has become the de‑facto standard for many large language models (LLMs).

---

## Self‑Attention Mechanism Without Explicit Order Information  

In a vanilla multi‑head self‑attention layer each token vector $\mathbf{x}_n\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is projected into queries $\mathbf{q}_n$, keys $\mathbf{k}_n$, and values $\mathbf{v}_n$.  For every pair $(i,j)$ an attention score is computed as

\[
\alpha_{ij}= \frac{\mathbf{q}_i^{\top}\mathbf{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_\text{head}}}.
\]

The softmax over $j$ produces weights that are applied to $\mathbf{v}_j$.  Crucially, $\alpha_{ij}$ depends only on the *content* of $\mathbf{x}_i$ and $\mathbf{x}_j$; there is nothing in this formula that distinguishes “token 1 followed by token 2’’ from “token 2 followed by token 1”.  Consequently the raw self‑attention operation treats any permutation of the input tokens identically.  

To make attention sensitive to word order, *positional* vectors must be added or combined with $\mathbf{x}_n$ before projection into query/key/value spaces.  This step breaks permutation invariance and enables each head to attend differently based on where tokens appear in a document.



## Absolute Positional Embeddings (Sinusoidal) vs Learned Embeddings  

Early Transformer implementations introduced two families of absolute positional representations:

* **Fixed sinusoidal embeddings** – each position $n$ receives a deterministic vector built from sines and cosines at geometrically decreasing frequencies \cite{rope}.  For dimension $i$ ($0\le i<d/2$),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{2i}(n) &= \cos\!\bigl(n\,\theta_i\bigr),\\[0.2em]
p_{2i+1}(n)&= \sin\!\bigl(n\,\theta_i\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\qquad 
\theta_i = \theta^{-2i/d},
\]

where $\theta$ denotes a base constant (the default value used throughout this work is $10\,000$).  
This construction matches Equation (1) below and guarantees that every dimension carries a different oscillation period, allowing distant tokens to be distinguished even when their raw indices differ vastly.

* **Learned embeddings** – instead of fixing $\theta$, one can allocate a learnable lookup table $\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_\text{max}\times d}$ where $L_\text{max}$ denotes a maximum supported length during pretraining.  Each row $\mathbf{E}_n$ captures whatever pattern is most useful for downstream tasks—but it also restricts the model strictly below $L_\text{max}$ because unseen indices would yield undefined vectors during inference.



Both families share an *absolute* character: they attach a single vector solely based on a token’s index $n$, independent of surrounding tokens.



## Relative Positional Encodings and Their Limitations  

A complementary design direction augments *pairwise* interactions instead of assigning each token its own static vector.  
In short, an additional bias term $b_{ij}=g(i-j)$ depending only on the offset $(i-j)$ gets added inside the dot product prior to softmax so that attention scores become aware of *relative* distances between query and key positions.  

While such schemes naturally generalize across lengths—because any offset appears at multiple absolute positions—they nevertheless encounter a practical obstacle highlighted throughout this work: when extending an LLM well beyond its original training horizon many *new* offsets appear that were never observed during pretraining or fine‑tuning.  These unseen distances lead to **catastrophic values**, i.e., out‑of‑distribution behavior that hampers convergence during further training \cite{pi}.  The same phenomenon motivates our focus on learning *how* existing rotational angles should be reshaped when many fresh token indices become active.



## Derivation of Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE)

Rotary Position Encoding embeds coordinates directly into the *attention* computation rather than appending an extra vector onto token representations \cite{rope}.  
For a token situated at index $n$, its RoPE vector consists precisely of interleaved cosine–sine pairs :

\[
\underbrace{\bigl[\;\cos(n\theta_0),\,\sin(n\theta_0),\,\cos(n\theta_1),\,\ldots,\,
\cos(n\theta_{d/2-1}),\,\sin(n\theta_{d/2-1})\;\bigr]}_{\text{Equation (1)}}.
\tag{1}
\]

Key ingredients deserve special emphasis:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $d$ | Dimensionality of the model’s hidden state (often multiples of $64$). |
| $n$ | Zero‑based integer denoting a token’s sequential offset inside a document. |
| $\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}$ | Frequency associated with dimension $i$, obtained via a power law decay governed by base $\theta$. |
| $\beta = \theta^{2/d}$ | A compact notation for “frequency root” used later when rescaling angles (\S 5). |

The **rotational** viewpoint arises because applying Eq.\,(1) corresponds mathematically to rotating each query/key vector within its own two‑dimensional subspace $(2i,2i+1)$ by angle $n\,\theta_i$.  When two rotated vectors meet inside an inner product,

\[
(\operatorname{Rot}(n)\,\mathbf{q})^{\!\top}(\operatorname{Rot}(m)\,\mathbf{k})
= \mathbf{q}^{\!\top}\bigl[\operatorname{Rot}(m-n)\bigr]\, \mathbf{k},
\]

the resulting term depends **only** on their relative distance $(m-n)$—precisely what makes RoPE simultaneously an *absolute* encoding (it knows `$n$') yet yields *relative*-aware attention scores without extra bias terms.



### Why Rotations Matter for Long Sequences  

Each subspace rotates at its own speed:
- Low‐index dimensions ($i$ small) possess **high frequencies** ($\theta_i$ large).  
  Hence their angles vary rapidly with $n$, allowing fine discrimination among nearby tokens.
- High‐index dimensions ($i$ large) evolve **slowly**, acting like coarse “global” clocks capable of distinguishing far‑apart positions without becoming overly crowded.

When we attempt to extend context length dramatically—e.g., up through millions of tokens—the high‑frequency dimensions would otherwise wrap around many times, leading multiple distant positions onto nearly identical angular values (“crowding”).  Conversely low‐frequency dimensions remain well behaved even at extreme distances because their periods are billions long.


## Implementation Details in Common Libraries  

Most modern deep‑learning frameworks expose RoPE through a dedicated routine called `apply_rotary_pos_emb`.  The high‑level workflow follows three steps:

1. **Pre‑compute cosine/sine tables**  
   For every supported length $L_{\max}$ one builds two tensors,
   $
   C_{i,n}= \cos(n/\beta^{\,i}),\
   S_{i,n}= \sin(n/\beta^{\,i}),
   $
   where index $i$ runs over half‐the hidden dimension ($0,\dots,d/2{-}1$).  
   In practice these tables live either on CPU memory or GPU cache for fast lookup.
   
2. **Split queries/keys into even–odd halves**  
   Query matrix $\mathbf Q \in \mathbb R^{B\times L\times d}$ gets reshaped into $(B,L,d/2,2)$ where last axis stores $(x_{\text{even}},x_{\text{odd}})$.
   
3. **Elementwise rotation**  
   For each head,
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   q_{\text{even}}' &:= q_{\text{even}}\odot C - q_{\text{odd}}\odot S ,\\
   q_{\text{odd}}' &:= q_{\text{even}}\odot S + q_{\text{odd}}\odot C .
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   Identical operations are carried out on keys $\mathbf K$.  
   After this step one proceeds with ordinary scaled dot–product attention using $(q',k')$.
   
Because only elementwise multiplications replace addition or concatenation steps found in traditional absolute schemes,
the overall computational cost remains essentially unchanged.
Consequently models extended via LongRoPE retain **the original architecture** except for substituting these tiny rotation kernels—a point emphasized repeatedly throughout Section 3 (“Models extended via LongRoPE retain the original architecture with minor modifications”).

The HuggingFace Transformers library implements exactly this procedure under `modeling_xxx.py` (`apply_rotary_pos_emb`), making it straightforward for practitioners to swap into existing LLaMA 2 or Mistral checkpoints without rewriting larger parts of code.



## Strengths of RoPE for Long‑Range Dependencies  

The empirical studies presented later (§ 4–§ 6) reveal several concrete advantages stemming from Rotatory design:

* **Implicit Relative Awareness** – By folding position offsets directly inside query–key inner products,
MoE-free attention naturally distinguishes far apart tokens without auxiliary bias tables.
This eliminates extra parameters while preserving geometric meaning across arbitrary distances.
  
* **Frequency‑Adaptive Scaling** – High‑frequency dimensions capture short‐range minutiae whereas low frequencies encode very coarse trends.
When extending context windows one typically rescales rotation angles differently per dimension.
Theoretical analyses such as Neural Tangent Kernel theory (\S 5) argue precisely why lower frequencies tolerate larger rescaling factors ($\lambda_i = s^{\,i}$) while higher frequencies should remain nearly untouched ($\lambda_i\simeq1$).  

* **Reduced Catastrophic Values** – The main obstacle identified in Section 3 (“non‑uniformities”) concerns massive overflow caused by naïvely interpolating all dimensions uniformly (PI).  
LongRoPE remedies this by searching *non‑uniform* rescale factors $\lambda_i$ together with an initial untampered segment length $\hat n$.  By preserving early tokens unchanged—and allowing high‐frequency dimensions little scaling—the method mitigates crowding effects leading to smoother perplexity curves even up to $2048k$ tokens.
  
* **Compatibility With Fine‑Tuning** – Because rotary rotations sit inside attention rather than being concatenated externally,
they impose almost no architectural change.
Fine‑tuning procedures described (§ 5) can adjust only these scalars while keeping every other weight frozen,
making large extensions feasible with merely *thousand* gradient steps—a dramatic reduction compared with full‐model retraining at million‐token lengths.
  
* **Empirical Robustness Across Datasets** – Tables 4–7 demonstrate that LLaMA 2–7B and Mistral–7B equipped with LongRoPE consistently achieve lower perplexities than baselines such as plain PI or YaRN across diverse corpora (PG19, Proof Pile, Books3).  
Even under extreme evaluation lengths (> 2048k), passkey retrieval accuracy stays above $90$\% – evidence that rotary encoding sustains discriminative power throughout massive spans.

### Takeaway  

Rotary Position Encoding embeds positions through smooth trigonometric rotations whose frequencies vary systematically across dimensions.
This simple mathematical trick endows vanilla self‑attention heads with built‑in relative reasoning while preserving efficiency.
When combined with principled non‑uniform rescaling—searching suitable per‑dimension factors ($\lambda_i$) and optionally leaving early tokens untouched (\(\hat n>0\))—RoPE becomes powerful enough to stretch modern LLMs well past two million tokens without sacrificing performance at conventional short contexts.
The success reported throughout this study underscores why RoPE has emerged as the de facto positional backbone for today’s large language models.
