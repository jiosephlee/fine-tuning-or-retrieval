# The Unified Theory of AI Learning: Understanding SFT, DPO, and GRPO

The landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) training is often obscured by a thicket of acronyms. If you have spent any time following AI research, you have likely encountered Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and more recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). While these methods are often discussed as distinct techniques, the researchers behind DeepSeekMath propose a "Unified Paradigm" that reveals they are actually different variations of the same core mechanics.

By breaking these methods down into three fundamental components—Data Source, Reward Function, and Algorithm—we can simplify the complexity of AI alignment and reasoning.

## The Unified Paradigm

The researchers propose that the gradient used to update a model's parameters ($\theta$) can be generalized into a single framework. The gradient with respect to the parameter $\theta$ for any training method can be expressed as:

$$\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\underbrace{(q,o) \sim \mathcal{D}}_{Data \ Source}]\left( \frac{1}{|o|} \sum_{t=1}^{|o|}  \underbrace{GC_{\mathcal{A}}(q, o, t, \pi_{rf})}_{Gradient \ Coefficient}  \nabla_{\theta}\log \pi_{\theta}(o_t | q, o_{<t})\right)$$

This equation reveals that every training method is composed of three parts:
1. **Data Source ($\mathcal{D}$):** Where the training data comes from.
2. **Reward Function ($\pi_{rf}$):** The source of the signal that tells the model if an output is "good."
3. **Algorithm ($\mathcal{A}$):** How the data and reward are processed into a Gradient Coefficient ($GC$), which determines the magnitude and direction of the weight updates.

## Breaking Down the Methods

Using this unified lens, we can categorize the most popular training methods more clearly.

### Offline vs. Online Sampling
The first distinction is the Data Source. **Offline sampling** uses data generated by a static, pre-existing model (like the initial SFT model). **Online sampling** uses data generated by the model currently being trained. 
*   **RFT (Rejection Sampling Fine-tuning)** and **DPO** are offline methods.
*   **Online RFT** and **GRPO/PPO** are online methods.

The researchers found that online training significantly outperforms offline training in the later stages of development. This is because as the model evolves, the data it explores becomes increasingly different from the initial SFT data, making real-time exploration more valuable.

### Rule-based vs. Model-based Rewards
The second distinction is how we define "good" output.
*   **Rule-based rewards** rely on objective truths, such as whether a math answer is correct.
*   **Model-based rewards** involve a separate neural network (a reward model) that scores the quality or reasoning steps of an output.

This distinction explains the leap from Online RFT to GRPO. While Online RFT reinforces any correct answer equally, GRPO uses a reward model to provide a nuanced score. This allows for **differential reinforcement**, where the model can be penalized or rewarded based on the varying "quality" of the response rather than just a binary correct/incorrect signal.

## Why GRPO is a Game Changer

One of the standout contributions of the DeepSeekMath paper is **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. GRPO is a variant of PPO that solves a major bottleneck in reinforcement learning. 

Standard PPO requires a "critic" model—a second large model of comparable size to the one being trained—to estimate a baseline for rewards. This doubles the memory and computational requirements. GRPO eliminates the need for this critic model by instead estimating the baseline from the average rewards of a group of outputs sampled for the same question.

For a question $q$, GRPO samples a group of outputs $\{o_1, o_2, \cdots, o_G\}$. It then optimizes the policy by maximizing an objective that uses the relative rewards within that group to calculate the advantage $\hat{A}_{i,t}$. This makes RL much more efficient without sacrificing the performance gains of model-based rewards.

## Key Takeaways for the Future

By unifying these methods, the researchers provide a roadmap for building smarter AI:

*   **Process over Outcome:** The study shows that "Process Supervision" (rewarding each step of a reasoning chain) is more effective than "Outcome Supervision" (rewarding only the final answer). This is because it provides more fine-grained gradient coefficients.
*   **Robustness to Noise:** Since reward models are not perfect, future research should focus on "Weak-to-Strong" alignment—developing algorithms that are robust even when the reward signal is noisy or slightly incorrect.
*   **Boosting Robustness:** Interestingly, the researchers found that RL doesn't necessarily give the model new "fundamental capabilities," but rather makes the output distribution more robust. It helps the model ensure that the correct answer is more likely to appear in the top results (boosting $Maj@K$ rather than $Pass@K$).

By viewing SFT, DPO, and GRPO not as competing technologies but as different configurations of the same three pillars, we can better understand how to tune these models for complex tasks like mathematical reasoning.
