\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 4: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Mathematical Instruction

# SFT Data Curation: Combining CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated reasoning

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) serves as the critical bridge between a base language model, which is trained primarily to predict the next token in a sequence, and an instruction-following model capable of executing specific tasks like solving complex math problems. To transform the DeepSeekMath-Base model into the DeepSeekMath-Instruct model, the researchers curated a massive, high-quality mathematical instruction-tuning dataset. This corpus is intentionally diverse, encompassing 776,000 training examples that cover a wide array of mathematical fields and varying levels of complexity. The curation strategy is designed to ensure the model can handle different types of reasoning by utilizing three distinct formats: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning.

The dataset is further stratified by language to ensure the model remains proficient in both English and Chinese mathematical contexts. For the English portion of the dataset, the authors integrated several well-known sources, such as GSM8K and MATH. Specifically, they annotated GSM8K and MATH problems with tool-integrated solutions. They also incorporated a subset of MathInstruct and the training set from Lila-OOD, which provide examples solved via CoT or PoT. These sources ensure that the model is exposed to algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry. On the other hand, the Chinese mathematical dataset focuses on K-12 problems across 76 different sub-topics, such as linear equations. Each of these problems is paired with solutions annotated in both CoT and tool-integrated reasoning formats.

The intuition behind combining these three formats lies in diversifying the model's "problem-solving toolkit." By providing CoT examples, the model learns to break down a problem into a sequence of logical, linguistic steps. By providing PoT examples, it learns to translate logic into executable code. By providing tool-integrated reasoning, it learns to recognize when a problem is too complex for internal calculation and requires the assistance of an external computational engine. This multi-faceted curation ensures that the resulting model is not just memorizing answers but is learning various paradigms of mathematical decomposition.

# Instruction Tuning Techniques: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) vs. Program-of-Thought (PoT)

To understand how the model learns to reason, it is essential to distinguish between the two primary instruction-tuning techniques employed in this study: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Program-of-Thought (PoT). These techniques represent different ways of structured reasoning that the model must master during the SFT phase. Chain-of-Thought is a prompting and training strategy where the model is encouraged to generate a series of intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. In this format, the "thought process" is expressed in natural language, mimicking the step-by-step derivation a human might write on a piece of paper. The goal is to help the model maintain logical coherence over long sequences of operations.

In contrast, Program-of-Thought (PoT) shifts the medium of reasoning from natural language to computer code. Instead of describing the steps of a derivation in prose, the model is trained to write a program that, when executed, produces the correct answer. The intuition here is that while natural language is flexible, it can be prone to small errors in arithmetic or complex logic. Code, however, is a more rigid and precise medium. By training on PoT data, the model learns to structure its logic into a sequence of computational instructions. This allows the model to "delegate" the actual calculation to a computer while it focuses on the high-level logic of constructing the correct algorithm.

By including both CoT and PoT in the SFT data curation, DeepSeekMath-Instruct is trained to be versatile. It becomes capable of solving problems through pure linguistic reasoning when required, and through algorithmic construction when a more precise computational approach is necessary. This dual-modality training is crucial for high-level mathematics, where a problem might require a conceptual leap (best handled by CoT) followed by a tedious, error-prone calculation (best handled by PoT).

# Tool-Integrated Reasoning: Enabling models to use Python and external libraries

While CoT and PoT are powerful techniques, they both ultimately rely on the model's internal capabilities. Tool-integrated reasoning takes this a step further by enabling the model to interact with an external environment to solve problems. In the context of DeepSeekMath-Instruct, this specifically involves the model's ability to write and utilize Python programs to solve mathematical problems. This is particularly useful for problems involving intricate computations that are difficult to perform accurately using only "mental" reasoning or text generation.

In the tool-integrated reasoning format, the model is prompted to solve a problem by generating a Python script. The paper highlights that these scripts can utilize specific libraries, such as $math$ and $sympy$, which are standard for handling advanced mathematical functions and symbolic computations. Once the model generates the code, the code is executed by an external system, and the result of that execution is treated as the final answer. This creates a workflow where the model acts as a "reasoning engine" that writes the instructions, and the Python environment acts as the "calculator" that executes them.

The advantage of tool-integrated reasoning is that it significantly reduces the likelihood of "hallucinating" incorrect numbers during complex multi-step calculations. By leveraging Python, the model can solve problems that would otherwise be beyond the reach of even the most advanced text-only models. It allows the model to handle high-precision arithmetic, solve complex equations, and perform symbolic manipulations with 100% accuracy, provided the model can correctly identify which parts of the problem require code and how to structure that code.

# Evaluating DeepSeekMath-Instruct: Performance against proprietary and open-source models

The effectiveness of the SFT process is measured by comparing DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B against a wide range of both proprietary (closed-source) and open-source models. The evaluation is divided into two primary settings: one where tool use is disallowed (testing pure reasoning) and one where tool use is permitted (testing integrated reasoning). This allows the researchers to isolate the model's internal logic from its ability to coordinate with external software.

In the setting where tool use is disallowed, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates remarkable performance. It surpasses all currently available open-source models in the 7B to 70B parameter range. Most notably, it outperforms several large proprietary models, such as Inflection-2 and Gemini Pro, by at least $9\%$ absolute on the competition-level MATH benchmark. While it still trails slightly behind the top-tier models like GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra, its ability to rival much larger models on a 7B scale is a significant achievement. This suggests that high-quality SFT data and specific mathematical pre-training can compensate for a smaller number of parameters.

When the model is allowed to use tools, its performance increases even further. In the tool-integrated setting, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approaches an accuracy of $60\%$ on the MATH dataset. In this category, it surpasses all existing open-source models. Furthermore, on other mathematical benchmarks, it remains highly competitive with much larger models, such as DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, which is ten times its size. These results confirm that the combination of specialized pre-training, multi-format SFT, and tool integration allows a relatively small model to achieve state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning capabilities.
