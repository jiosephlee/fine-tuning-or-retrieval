\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 1: Introduction to Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models

# Challenges of mathematical reasoning: Structure and complexity

Mathematical reasoning represents one of the most significant hurdles in the development of large language models (LLMs). The primary difficulty lies in the complex and highly structured nature of mathematics. Unlike standard natural language processing, where a model might rely on the statistical probability of words appearing in a specific order, mathematical reasoning requires a deep adherence to logical structures and rules. The model must navigate through multi-step processes where each step depends on the accuracy of the previous one, making it susceptible to errors that can propagate throughout a solution.

Because of this inherent complexity, mathematical reasoning serves as a rigorous test for the cognitive capabilities of an AI. It requires the model to understand not just the surface-level vocabulary of a problem but the underlying logic that governs mathematical systems. For a student of artificial intelligence, it is important to understand that a model's ability to solve a math problem is a proxy for its ability to perform structured reasoning, as it must maintain internal consistency while manipulating symbols and concepts according to specific formal rules.

# Overview of benchmarks: MATH, GSM8K, CMATH, and Gaokao-Math

To measure the success of models in this domain, researchers rely on standardized benchmarks that evaluate a wide spectrum of mathematical difficulty. One of the primary benchmarks utilized in this research is GSM8K, which focuses on grade-school level quantitative reasoning. These problems typically require a few steps of reasoning and are designed to see if a model can handle basic word problems and arithmetic logic.

In contrast, the MATH dataset represents a much higher level of difficulty, characterized as "competition-level" mathematics. This benchmark includes problems from grade-school to college levels, covering diverse fields such as algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry. Because of its complexity, the MATH benchmark is a key metric for determining if a model can perform advanced, multi-step reasoning. Furthermore, the research incorporates Chinese mathematical benchmarks, specifically CMATH and Gaokao-Math (which includes Gaokao-MathCloze and Gaokao-MathQA). These benchmarks provide a comprehensive assessment of the model's abilities across different languages and regional educational standards, ensuring that the model's reasoning capabilities are robust and not limited to a single linguistic context.

# The gap between open-source and proprietary models (GPT-4, Gemini-Ultra)

A significant issue in the current landscape of artificial intelligence is the disparity between proprietary and open-source models. While cutting-edge models like GPT-4 and Gemini-Ultra have demonstrated impressive capabilities in solving complex mathematical problems and assisting humans in advanced reasoning, these models are not publicly available. This lack of accessibility prevents the broader research community from fully examining, reproducing, or building upon the underlying mechanisms of the most powerful systems currently in existence.

Conversely, currently accessible open-source models have historically trailed significantly behind the performance of these proprietary giants. This gap creates a barrier for researchers who wish to develop specialized, high-performing mathematical tools without relying on closed-source APIs. The objective of the DeepSeekMath project is specifically addressed by this problem: it seeks to create a domain-specific model that can significantly outperform existing open-source models and approach the performance levels of the top-tier proprietary models on academic benchmarks. By narrowing this gap, the project aims to provide the community with a high-performing, accessible tool for mathematical reasoning.

# Project Objectives: Scaling math pre-training and advancing RL techniques

The DeepSeekMath project is built upon two primary pillars: the massive scaling of math-specific pre-training data and the advancement of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. The first goal is to harness the vast potential of publicly available web data. The researchers developed a meticulously engineered data selection pipeline to extract 120B math-related tokens from Common Crawl. This corpus is significantly larger than many existing mathematical datasets, providing a much larger volume of high-quality data for the model to learn from during its pre-training phase. This scaling is designed to prove that a smaller model trained on high-quality, large-scale data can achieve performance levels comparable to much larger models.

The second major objective is the introduction of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO is a variant of the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, which is commonly used to fine-tune the reasoning abilities of LLMs. A key limitation of standard PPO is that it requires a "critic" model—a second model of comparable size to the policy model—which significantly increases the memory and computational resources required for training. GRPO addresses this by foregoing the critic model entirely. Instead, it estimates the baseline for rewards by using the average scores of a group of outputs produced in response to the same question. By optimizing the policy model using these group-relative scores, GRPO enhances the model's mathematical reasoning abilities while simultaneously making the training process much more efficient in terms of memory usage.
