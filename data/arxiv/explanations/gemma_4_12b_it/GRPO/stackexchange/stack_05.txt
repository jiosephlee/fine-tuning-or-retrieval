\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### In the 'Unified Paradigm', what is the specific difference between Online RFT and GRPO regarding the 'Gradient Coefficient'?
Question:
The paper provides a unified paradigm where the gradient is determined by a Gradient Coefficient ($GC$). According to the 'Observation about Gradient Coefficient' section, the authors state that GRPO uniquely adjusts its gradient coefficient based on the reward value provided by the reward model, whereas Online RFT does not penalize incorrect responses. 

If Online RFT only reinforces correct answers, and GRPO reinforces based on the magnitude of the reward, how does this specifically change the behavior of the model during training? Does Online RFT essentially treat all 'correct' answers as equally good, regardless of how the model arrived at them?
Answer:
To understand the difference between Online RFT and GRPO within the "Unified Paradigm" presented in the paper, we need to look at how the **Gradient Coefficient** ($GC$) acts as a scaling factor for the model's updates.

In the unified equation provided by the authors:
$$\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\dots]\left( \frac{1}{|o|} \sum_{t=1}^{|o|} GC_{\mathcal{A}}(q, o, t, \pi_{rf}) \nabla_{\theta}\log \pi_{\theta}(o_t | q, o_{<t})\right)$$
the $GC$ determines the magnitude and direction (reinforcement vs. penalty) of the gradient update for each token. The distinction between Online RFT and GRPO lies in how "rich" this coefficient is.

### The Difference in Gradient Coefficients
The fundamental difference is that **Online RFT uses a binary-style reinforcement signal**, while **GRPO uses a magnitude-based differential signal**.

In **Online RFT**, the reward signal is typically based on a "Rule" (the correctness of the final answer). Because it does not penalize incorrect responses, the $GC$ essentially acts as a filter: if an answer is correct, it receives a uniform reinforcement; if it is incorrect, it receives no update (or a neutral one). The paper notes that Online RFT "uniformly reinforces all responses with correct answers at the same level of intensity." This means the gradient coefficient does not distinguish between a perfectly reasoned correct answer and a "lucky" correct answer that might have had flawed intermediate steps.

In **GRPO**, the gradient coefficient is uniquely adjusted based on the reward value provided by a reward model (or a process reward model). Because GRPO calculates the advantage $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ by normalizing rewards within a group of samples, the $GC$ becomes a continuous variable. This allows for "differential reinforcement," where the model is pushed more strongly toward outputs that receive higher reward magnitudes and actively pushed away from outputs with lower rewards or incorrect results.

### Impact on Training Behavior
This difference significantly changes how the model "navigates" the optimization landscape:

1.  **Quality of Reasoning:** Because Online RFT treats all correct answers as equally good, the model's objective is simply to land on a correct final answer. It has no explicit gradient signal to prefer a "high-quality" reasoning path over a "low-quality" one, provided both end in the correct result. 
2.  **Active Penalization:** GRPO provides a negative gradient coefficient for incorrect or low-quality responses. By actively penalizing these trajectories, GRPO helps the model "prune" away bad reasoning paths. Online RFT, by contrast, only focuses on "adding" to the probability of correct answers without actively "subtracting" from the probability of incorrect ones.
3.  **Robustness:** The authors suggest that by altering both positive and negative gradient coefficients, GRPO is more efficient at shaping the output distribution. This is why GRPO outperforms Online RFT; it doesn't just tell the model what to do (correctness), it tells the model what *not* to do and provides a finer "ranking" of which correct answers are superior.

To answer your specific question: **Yes, Online RFT essentially treats all correct answers as equally good.** It lacks the mechanism to reward the "how" of the reasoning (the magnitude of the reward) and only recognizes the "what" (the correctness of the outcome). GRPO, by utilizing the reward model to produce varying magnitudes for the $GC$, allows the model to distinguish between different levels of quality among correct responses.
