\title{Blogs about the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

# The 1.58-Bit Revolution: Making AI Leaner Without Losing the Brains

The rapid evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) has brought us to a crossroads. While these models have demonstrated incredible capabilities across a wide range of natural language tasks, their sheer size presents significant hurdles. The high energy consumption, massive memory requirements, and the latency involved in running full-precision models (like those using FP16 or BF16) create economic and environmental challenges that make widespread deployment difficult.

To combat this, the industry often relies on post-training quantization—reducing the precision of weights after a model is already trained. However, this approach is often sub-optimal. A new research direction is emerging that suggests we can change the paradigm entirely by training models from the ground up using significantly fewer bits. This is the era of 1-bit LLMs, specifically the **BitNet b1.58** architecture.

## What is BitNet b1.58?

The core innovation of BitNet b1.58 is that it restricts every single weight (parameter) in the model to a ternary system. Instead of the complex floating-point numbers used in standard Transformers, every weight in BitNet b1.58 can only take on three values: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. 

The name "1.58 bits" comes from the information theory behind this choice. Since there are three possible states for each weight, the number of bits required to represent each weight is $\log_2(3) \approx 1.58$. By moving to this ternary representation, the model replaces the heavy lifting of floating-point multiplication with simple integer addition. Because power is often the fundamental limit to compute performance on modern chips, saving on energy through simpler arithmetic directly translates into faster computation.

## The Mechanics: From nn.Linear to BitLinear

BitNet b1.58 replaces the standard `nn.Linear` layers found in architectures like LLaMA with a specialized `BitLinear` layer. During training, the model is constrained using an *absmean* quantization function. To turn a standard weight matrix $W$ into a ternary matrix $\widetilde{W}$, the researchers use the following process:

First, they calculate a scaling factor $\gamma$, which is the average absolute value of the weight matrix:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$$

Then, they scale the weights and round them to the nearest value in the $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ set:
$$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$$

Where the $\mathrm{RoundClip}$ function is defined as:
$$\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x)))$$

While the weights are ternary, the activations are kept at 8-bit precision. Furthermore, to stay compatible with the current open-source ecosystem, BitNet b1.58 adopts LLaMA-alike components, including RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and rotary embeddings (RoPE), while removing all biases.

## Performance and Efficiency Gains

The results of this research are striking. When compared to a full-precision FP16 LLaMA model trained on the same data, BitNet b1.58 achieves a "Pareto improvement"—it matches or exceeds the performance of the baseline while being significantly more efficient. 

Key findings include:
*   **Performance Parity:** BitNet b1.58 begins to match the perplexity and end-task performance of full-precision LLaMA models starting at the 3B parameter size. 
*   **Speed and Memory:** A 3.9B BitNet b1.58 model is 2.4 times faster and uses 3.32 times less memory than a standard 3B LLaMA model, while actually performing better on language tasks. 
*   **Energy Savings:** Because matrix multiplication in BitNet b1.58 relies on INT8 addition rather than FP16 multiplication, it saves roughly 71.4 times the arithmetic energy consumption on 7nm chips.
*   **Throughput:** At the 70B scale, BitNet b1.58 can support up to 11 times the batch size of a standard LLaMA 70B model, resulting in nearly 9 times higher throughput.

## A New Scaling Law for the Future

Perhaps the most profound contribution of this paper is the definition of a new scaling law. The research shows that we can achieve the efficiency of a smaller full-precision model by using a larger 1.58-bit model. For example:
*   A 13B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient (in terms of latency, memory, and energy) than a 3B FP16 model.
*   A 30B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 7B FP16 model.
*   A 70B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 13B FP16 model.

Looking ahead, this 1-bit paradigm opens several doors. It makes Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models easier to deploy by reducing memory footprints and communication overhead. It also paves the way for native support of long sequences by reducing activation bits, and it makes it much easier to run high-performance LLMs on edge and mobile devices where CPU power and memory are limited. Finally, this shift creates a massive opportunity for hardware designers to build specialized chips optimized specifically for 1-bit computation.
