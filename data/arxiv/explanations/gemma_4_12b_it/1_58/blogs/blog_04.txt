\title{Blogs about the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

# Breaking the Scaling Laws: How BitNet b1.58 Outperforms the Giants

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has brought us to a crossroads where the sheer scale of these models—while impressive—poses significant hurdles. As models grow larger, the costs associated with training, memory usage, and inference energy consumption become increasingly prohibitive. While industry standard practices often rely on post-training quantization to shrink models for deployment, these methods are often sub-optimal. A new paradigm is emerging: 1-bit LLMs. Specifically, the introduction of BitNet b1.58 suggests that we can maintain high-performance capabilities while radically optimizing the underlying computation.

## The Ternary Revolution: What is 1.58-bit?

At the heart of BitNet b1.58 is a fundamental shift in how model weights are represented. Traditional LLMs typically use 16-bit floating-point values (FP16 or BF16). In contrast, BitNet b1.58 constrains every single parameter in the model to a ternary value: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. 

While "1-bit" might imply a binary system, the inclusion of the $0$ value results in a representation that occupies 1.58 bits in a binary system. This isn't just a compression trick; it changes the fundamental math of the model. Because weights are restricted to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, the massive matrix multiplications that define the Transformer architecture are transformed into integer addition operations. By removing the need for floating-point multiplication, BitNet b1.58 significantly reduces the energy cost of computation and allows for much faster execution on power-constrained hardware.

## Architecture and Training

BitNet b1.58 is not just a quantized version of an existing model; it is designed to be trained from scratch. To ensure compatibility with the current ecosystem, the researchers adopted "LLaMA-alike" components, including RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and rotary embeddings (RoPE). 

To achieve the ternary weights, the model employs an *absmean* quantization function. During training, the weight matrix $W$ is scaled by its average absolute value $\gamma$ and then rounded to the nearest integer in the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. The formula used is:

$$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$$

where $\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$. This explicit support for the $0$ value is crucial, as it provides the model with a mechanism for feature filtering, which enhances its modeling capabilities compared to strictly binary models.

## Data-Driven Performance: A Pareto Improvement

The research provides a compelling data-driven comparison between BitNet b1.58 and full-precision LLaMA models. When trained on the same number of tokens (100 billion), BitNet b1.58 demonstrates that it can match the performance of its much heavier counterparts while being significantly more efficient.

A key finding is that BitNet b1.58 starts to match full-precision LLaMA performance at the 3B parameter size. For example, a BitNet b1.58 model with 3.9B parameters actually outperforms a 3B LLaMA model while being 2.4 times faster and consuming 3.32 times less GPU memory. This represents a "Pareto improvement," where the model achieves better results with fewer resources.

The efficiency gains become even more pronounced as we scale up:
*   **Latency and Memory:** At the 70B parameter scale, BitNet b1.58 is 4.1 times faster than the LLaMA baseline.
*   **Throughput:** On a dual A100 GPU setup, BitNet b1.58 70B can support up to 11 times the batch size of LLaMA 70B, leading to an 8.9 times higher throughput.
*   **Energy Efficiency:** Because it replaces floating-point multiplications with integer additions, BitNet b1.58 saves approximately 71.4 times the arithmetic operations energy consumption for matrix multiplications on 7nm chips.

## Redefining the Scaling Laws

Perhaps the most profound takeaway from this research is the emergence of a new scaling law. The efficiency of BitNet b1.58 allows for a shift in how we view model equivalence. Based on the research findings, we can establish the following efficiencies:

*   A 13B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient (in terms of latency, memory, and energy) than a 3B FP16 LLM.
*   A 30B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 7B FP16 LLM.
*   A 70B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 13B FP16 LLM.

These findings suggest that the future of LLMs may not lie in making models larger with more precision, but in utilizing 1.58-bit architectures to achieve higher performance at a fraction of the cost. This opens the door for native long-sequence support, more efficient Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, and the deployment of high-performing LLMs on edge and mobile devices.
