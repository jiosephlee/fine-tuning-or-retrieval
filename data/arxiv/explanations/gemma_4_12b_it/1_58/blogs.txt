# The 1.58-Bit Revolution: Making AI Leaner Without Losing the Brains

The rapid evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) has brought us to a crossroads. While these models have demonstrated incredible capabilities across a wide range of natural language tasks, their sheer size presents significant hurdles. The high energy consumption, massive memory requirements, and the latency involved in running full-precision models (like those using FP16 or BF16) create economic and environmental challenges that make widespread deployment difficult.

To combat this, the industry often relies on post-training quantization—reducing the precision of weights after a model is already trained. However, this approach is often sub-optimal. A new research direction is emerging that suggests we can change the paradigm entirely by training models from the ground up using significantly fewer bits. This is the era of 1-bit LLMs, specifically the **BitNet b1.58** architecture.

## What is BitNet b1.58?

The core innovation of BitNet b1.58 is that it restricts every single weight (parameter) in the model to a ternary system. Instead of the complex floating-point numbers used in standard Transformers, every weight in BitNet b1.58 can only take on three values: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. 

The name "1.58 bits" comes from the information theory behind this choice. Since there are three possible states for each weight, the number of bits required to represent each weight is $\log_2(3) \approx 1.58$. By moving to this ternary representation, the model replaces the heavy lifting of floating-point multiplication with simple integer addition. Because power is often the fundamental limit to compute performance on modern chips, saving on energy through simpler arithmetic directly translates into faster computation.

## The Mechanics: From nn.Linear to BitLinear

BitNet b1.58 replaces the standard `nn.Linear` layers found in architectures like LLaMA with a specialized `BitLinear` layer. During training, the model is constrained using an *absmean* quantization function. To turn a standard weight matrix $W$ into a ternary matrix $\widetilde{W}$, the researchers use the following process:

First, they calculate a scaling factor $\gamma$, which is the average absolute value of the weight matrix:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$$

Then, they scale the weights and round them to the nearest value in the $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ set:
$$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$$

Where the $\mathrm{RoundClip}$ function is defined as:
$$\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x)))$$

While the weights are ternary, the activations are kept at 8-bit precision. Furthermore, to stay compatible with the current open-source ecosystem, BitNet b1.58 adopts LLaMA-alike components, including RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and rotary embeddings (RoPE), while removing all biases.

## Performance and Efficiency Gains

The results of this research are striking. When compared to a full-precision FP16 LLaMA model trained on the same data, BitNet b1.58 achieves a "Pareto improvement"—it matches or exceeds the performance of the baseline while being significantly more efficient. 

Key findings include:
*   **Performance Parity:** BitNet b1.58 begins to match the perplexity and end-task performance of full-precision LLaMA models starting at the 3B parameter size. 
*   **Speed and Memory:** A 3.9B BitNet b1.58 model is 2.4 times faster and uses 3.32 times less memory than a standard 3B LLaMA model, while actually performing better on language tasks. 
*   **Energy Savings:** Because matrix multiplication in BitNet b1.58 relies on INT8 addition rather than FP16 multiplication, it saves roughly 71.4 times the arithmetic energy consumption on 7nm chips.
*   **Throughput:** At the 70B scale, BitNet b1.58 can support up to 11 times the batch size of a standard LLaMA 70B model, resulting in nearly 9 times higher throughput.

## A New Scaling Law for the Future

Perhaps the most profound contribution of this paper is the definition of a new scaling law. The research shows that we can achieve the efficiency of a smaller full-precision model by using a larger 1.58-bit model. For example:
*   A 13B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient (in terms of latency, memory, and energy) than a 3B FP16 model.
*   A 30B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 7B FP16 model.
*   A 70B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 13B FP16 model.

Looking ahead, this 1-bit paradigm opens several doors. It makes Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models easier to deploy by reducing memory footprints and communication overhead. It also paves the way for native support of long sequences by reducing activation bits, and it makes it much easier to run high-performance LLMs on edge and mobile devices where CPU power and memory are limited. Finally, this shift creates a massive opportunity for hardware designers to build specialized chips optimized specifically for 1-bit computation.



# Goodbye Floating Point: Why 1-bit LLMs are a Hardware Game Changer

As Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to scale in size and complexity, we are hitting a physical wall. The current paradigm of training and deploying these models relies heavily on 16-bit floating-point (FP16 or BF16) precision. While this has enabled the current era of AI, the sheer cost of floating-point multiplication—both in terms of energy consumption and hardware latency—is becoming a significant bottleneck for widespread deployment. A new research paper, "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits," proposes a fundamental shift in this architecture: moving from complex floating-point arithmetic to simple ternary integer addition.

## The Shift to Ternary Weights

The core innovation presented in the paper is **BitNet b1.58**. Unlike standard Transformers that use high-precision weights, BitNet b1.58 constrains every single parameter in the model to ternary values: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. Because these three values can be represented using approximately 1.58 bits in a binary system, the model is dubbed a "1.58-bit LLM."

This isn't just a compression trick; it is a change in the fundamental computation paradigm. In a standard LLM, the bulk of the computation is performed via matrix multiplication, which requires intensive floating-point multiplication. By restricting weights to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, the operation simplifies from floating-point multiplication to integer addition. Since integer addition is significantly less power-intensive than floating-point multiplication, this shift allows for massive energy savings and faster computation, as power consumption is the primary limit on chip performance.

## How BitNet b1.58 Works

To achieve this without sacrificing model performance, the authors use a specific quantization function. To constrain the weights, they first scale the weight matrix by its average absolute value ($\gamma$) and then round the result to the nearest integer among $\{-1, 0, +1\}$. The math works as follows:

$$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$$

Where the rounding function is defined as:
$$\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x)))$$

And the scaling factor is:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$$

One of the most important technical details is the inclusion of the value $0$. While a pure 1-bit model (using only $\{-1, 1\}$) exists, the addition of $0$ provides the model with "feature filtering" capabilities. This allows the network to effectively ignore certain inputs, which the authors found significantly improves modeling performance. Despite this drastic reduction in precision, BitNet b1.58 can match full-precision (FP16) baselines in terms of perplexity and end-task performance starting at a model size of 3B parameters.

## Hardware Implications and Efficiency

The move to 1-bit LLMs creates a "Pareto improvement" over existing state-of-the-art models—meaning we can achieve the same or better performance while using fewer resources. The paper highlights several key metrics that make this a game-changer for hardware engineering:

*   **Energy Efficiency:** On 7nm chips, BitNet b1.58 saves approximately 71.4 times the arithmetic operation energy consumption for matrix multiplications compared to FP16 models.
*   **Memory Footprint:** Because the weights are ternary, the memory footprint is drastically reduced. For instance, BitNet b1.58 uses 3.55 times less GPU memory than an FP16 baseline at the 3B size.
*   **Throughput and Latency:** The reduced memory and simplified math lead to significant speedups. A 70B BitNet b1.58 model is 4.1 times faster than its FP16 counterpart.

These efficiencies enable a new scaling law. For example, a 13B BitNet b1.58 model can be more efficient in terms of latency, memory, and energy than a 3B FP16 LLM. This suggests that we can run much larger models on the same hardware or, conversely, run smaller models with much higher throughput.

## The Future: AI-Specific Hardware

The most profound implication of BitNet b1.58 is the opening of a new door for hardware design. Currently, most AI hardware is optimized to accelerate floating-point operations. However, the BitNet paradigm paves the way for designing specific hardware—similar to the path taken by companies like Groq with their LPUs—that is optimized specifically for 1-bit arithmetic.

By designing chips that excel at integer addition rather than floating-point multiplication, we can drastically lower the energy barrier for AI. This will be particularly transformative for edge and mobile devices, where power and memory are strictly limited, and for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, where reducing the memory footprint and inter-chip communication overhead is critical for deployment.



# AI in Your Pocket: Bringing High-Performance LLMs to Mobile and Edge Devices

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized how we interact with technology. However, there is a significant bottleneck: these models are massive. To run them effectively, we currently rely on high-powered data centers and cloud computing because the memory and energy requirements are too demanding for most consumer hardware. This dependency creates hurdles for privacy, latency, and accessibility. A new research paper introduces a potential game-changer in this space: **BitNet b1.58**, a model architecture that paves the way for high-performance AI to run locally on mobile and edge devices.

## The Shift to 1.58-Bit Weights

Most modern LLMs, such as the LLaMA family, utilize 16-bit floating-point values (FP16 or BF16) for their weights. While precise, this requires significant computational power because the "heavy lifting" of these models involves constant matrix multiplication of these floating-point numbers. 

BitNet b1.58 changes this fundamental paradigm. In this architecture, every single weight in the model is ternary, meaning it can only take on three values: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. The researchers refer to this as a "1.58-bit" model because the inclusion of the zero value (added to the original 1-bit BitNet) results in 1.58 bits in a binary system. By restricting weights to these three values, the model replaces complex floating-point multiplications with simple integer additions. This shift is crucial because integer addition is orders of magnitude more energy-efficient than floating-point multiplication, which is often the primary limit on chip performance.

## Matching Performance with Massive Efficiency

A common concern with "quantizing" or simplifying a model is that it will lose its intelligence. However, BitNet b1.58 is designed to match the performance of full-precision models. The research demonstrates that starting from a model size of 3B parameters, BitNet b1.58 matches the perplexity and end-task performance of full-precision LLaMA models when trained on the same amount of data.

The efficiency gains, however, are where the model truly shines for consumer electronics:
* **Speed:** A 70B parameter BitNet b1.58 model is 4.1 times faster than its full-precision baseline.
* **Memory:** At the 3B scale, the model uses 3.55 times less GPU memory.
* **Energy:** For matrix multiplications on 7nm chips, the researchers estimated a 71.4 times reduction in arithmetic operations energy consumption.
* **Throughput:** For a 70B model, the 1.58-bit architecture can support up to 11 times the batch size, resulting in nearly 9 times higher throughput.

## Why This Matters for Your Smartphone

These statistics aren't just academic; they represent a fundamental shift in what is possible for "Edge AI"—running intelligence directly on your phone, laptop, or IoT device. 

Mobile devices are strictly limited by two factors: battery life and thermal limits. Because BitNet b1.58 drastically reduces energy consumption and memory overhead, it allows for much more powerful models to be deployed locally. This means your smartphone could potentially handle complex reasoning tasks without sending your data to a cloud server. This not only improves privacy but also ensures that the AI remains functional even without an internet connection.

Furthermore, BitNet b1.58 is particularly "CPU-friendly." Most mobile devices rely on CPUs as their primary processors, and the move toward integer addition makes these models much easier to execute on standard mobile hardware compared to traditional FP16 models.

## Enabling Long Sequences and Future Scaling

One of the biggest hurdles for local AI is the "KV cache," which consumes massive amounts of memory as the conversation gets longer. Because BitNet b1.58 also works to reduce activation precision (from 16 bits down to 8 bits), it provides a path toward supporting longer context lengths on devices with limited RAM. In some cases, this could effectively double the amount of context a device can handle given the same amount of resources.

By defining a new scaling law where efficiency increases as the model grows, BitNet b1.58 suggests a future where we don't have to choose between a "smart" model and a "portable" one. We are moving toward an era where high-performance AI can truly live in your pocket.



# Breaking the Scaling Laws: How BitNet b1.58 Outperforms the Giants

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has brought us to a crossroads where the sheer scale of these models—while impressive—poses significant hurdles. As models grow larger, the costs associated with training, memory usage, and inference energy consumption become increasingly prohibitive. While industry standard practices often rely on post-training quantization to shrink models for deployment, these methods are often sub-optimal. A new paradigm is emerging: 1-bit LLMs. Specifically, the introduction of BitNet b1.58 suggests that we can maintain high-performance capabilities while radically optimizing the underlying computation.

## The Ternary Revolution: What is 1.58-bit?

At the heart of BitNet b1.58 is a fundamental shift in how model weights are represented. Traditional LLMs typically use 16-bit floating-point values (FP16 or BF16). In contrast, BitNet b1.58 constrains every single parameter in the model to a ternary value: $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. 

While "1-bit" might imply a binary system, the inclusion of the $0$ value results in a representation that occupies 1.58 bits in a binary system. This isn't just a compression trick; it changes the fundamental math of the model. Because weights are restricted to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, the massive matrix multiplications that define the Transformer architecture are transformed into integer addition operations. By removing the need for floating-point multiplication, BitNet b1.58 significantly reduces the energy cost of computation and allows for much faster execution on power-constrained hardware.

## Architecture and Training

BitNet b1.58 is not just a quantized version of an existing model; it is designed to be trained from scratch. To ensure compatibility with the current ecosystem, the researchers adopted "LLaMA-alike" components, including RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and rotary embeddings (RoPE). 

To achieve the ternary weights, the model employs an *absmean* quantization function. During training, the weight matrix $W$ is scaled by its average absolute value $\gamma$ and then rounded to the nearest integer in the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. The formula used is:

$$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$$

where $\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$. This explicit support for the $0$ value is crucial, as it provides the model with a mechanism for feature filtering, which enhances its modeling capabilities compared to strictly binary models.

## Data-Driven Performance: A Pareto Improvement

The research provides a compelling data-driven comparison between BitNet b1.58 and full-precision LLaMA models. When trained on the same number of tokens (100 billion), BitNet b1.58 demonstrates that it can match the performance of its much heavier counterparts while being significantly more efficient.

A key finding is that BitNet b1.58 starts to match full-precision LLaMA performance at the 3B parameter size. For example, a BitNet b1.58 model with 3.9B parameters actually outperforms a 3B LLaMA model while being 2.4 times faster and consuming 3.32 times less GPU memory. This represents a "Pareto improvement," where the model achieves better results with fewer resources.

The efficiency gains become even more pronounced as we scale up:
*   **Latency and Memory:** At the 70B parameter scale, BitNet b1.58 is 4.1 times faster than the LLaMA baseline.
*   **Throughput:** On a dual A100 GPU setup, BitNet b1.58 70B can support up to 11 times the batch size of LLaMA 70B, leading to an 8.9 times higher throughput.
*   **Energy Efficiency:** Because it replaces floating-point multiplications with integer additions, BitNet b1.58 saves approximately 71.4 times the arithmetic operations energy consumption for matrix multiplications on 7nm chips.

## Redefining the Scaling Laws

Perhaps the most profound takeaway from this research is the emergence of a new scaling law. The efficiency of BitNet b1.58 allows for a shift in how we view model equivalence. Based on the research findings, we can establish the following efficiencies:

*   A 13B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient (in terms of latency, memory, and energy) than a 3B FP16 LLM.
*   A 30B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 7B FP16 LLM.
*   A 70B BitNet b1.58 model is more efficient than a 13B FP16 LLM.

These findings suggest that the future of LLMs may not lie in making models larger with more precision, but in utilizing 1.58-bit architectures to achieve higher performance at a fraction of the cost. This opens the door for native long-sequence support, more efficient Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, and the deployment of high-performing LLMs on edge and mobile devices.
