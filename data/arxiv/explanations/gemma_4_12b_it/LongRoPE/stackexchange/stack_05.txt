\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Does 'Short Context Window Recovery' involve updating the model weights or just the RoPE calculation?
Question:
In Section 3.3, the authors describe 'readjusting' the RoPE rescale factors to recover performance on 4k and 8k contexts after extending the model to 2048k. During inference, if the sequence length is less than 8k, the paper says they 'update RoPE with the searched rescale factors.' Does this mean the model's weights remain frozen and we are only changing the rotation angles calculated on the fly, or does this recovery step involve a new round of gradient descent?
Answer:
The "Short Context Window Recovery" described in the paper does not involve updating the model's weights through gradient descent; rather, it involves identifying and applying optimal hyperparameters for the RoPE calculation on the fly.

To understand why, it is helpful to look at how LongRoPE handles positional embeddings. The core mechanism relies on rescaling the rotation angles of the RoPE embedding using a set of factors, $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$. Specifically, for a token at position $n$ and a RoPE dimension $i$, the rotation angle is modified by a factor that depends on whether the position is below or above a certain threshold $\hat{n}$. As shown in Equation 3, the term $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i},\hat{n})$ determines the rescaling: it is $1$ for positions $n < \hat{n}$ and $1/\lambda_i$ for positions $n \geq \hat{n}$.

The "recovery" process is an offline optimization step. The authors noticed that extending the context window to 2048k causes the positional embeddings in the original 4k-8k range to become "crowded," which degrades performance. To solve this, they use an evolutionary search algorithm to find the specific values of $\lambda_i$ and $\hat{n}$ that minimize perplexity for those shorter lengths. During this search, the model's weights remain frozen; the algorithm simply iterates through different combinations of rescale factors, calculates the perplexity for each, and selects the best-performing set.

Once these optimal factors are found, they are stored as hyperparameters. During inference, the model performs a dynamic check on the input sequence length. If the sequence length is shorter than 8k, the system "updates" the RoPE calculation by plugging in these pre-searched "recovered" $\lambda$ factors instead of the factors used for the 2048k window. 

In summary, the weights of the Transformer remain fixed during this recovery step. The adjustment happens entirely within the positional embedding logic—specifically, by changing the rotation angles calculated on the fly before they are used in the attention mechanism. This is conceptually similar to how other positional interpolation methods (like PI or NTK) operate: they modify the "map" of positions that the model sees, rather than retraining the model's internal knowledge.
