\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Why does the k-major layout constraint specifically affect the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix in FP8 WGMMA?
Question:
In Section 4.3, the authors explain that for FP8 WGMMA, only the k-major format is supported for input operands in shared memory. They mention that this necessitates an in-kernel transpose for the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix. I'm confused about why this is a problem for $\mathbf{V}$ specifically. Since the attention output is $\mathbf{O} = \text{softmax}(\alpha \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top)\mathbf{V}$, doesn't the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix naturally align with the row-wise operations of the softmax? Why does the k-major requirement for the Tensor Cores force us to transpose it?
Answer:
The confusion arises from the distinction between how we conceptually view the global matrix multiplication $\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$ and how the hardware (specifically the Hopper Tensor Cores via the WGMMA instruction) processes that multiplication in a tiled fashion.

To understand why $\mathbf{V}$ specifically requires a transpose in FP8, we have to look at the definitions provided in Section 4.3 regarding $mn$-major and $k$-major layouts. The authors define $k$-major as being contiguous in the inner dimension $K$ of a GEMM operation where $A$ is an $M \times K$ matrix and $B$ is an $N \times K$ matrix. In the context of the attention mechanism, there are two primary GEMMs performed in the forward pass:

1.  The Score GEMM: $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$. Here, the query $\mathbf{Q}$ and keys $\mathbf{K}$ have a head dimension $d$. The "inner dimension" being contracted is $d$.
2.  The Value GEMM: $\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$. Here, $\mathbf{P}$ is the softmax output (a tile of size $B_r \times B_c$) and $\mathbf{V}$ is a tile of the value matrix. Because the attention is tiled over the sequence length, the "inner dimension" being contracted for this specific tile multiplication is the sequence block size $B_c$.

The paper notes that $\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}$, and $\mathbf{V}$ are typically stored contiguously in the head dimension $d$. This means they are naturally $mn$-major (contiguous in the outer dimension). For the first GEMM ($\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$), this is actually an advantage: because the contraction dimension is $d$, and the matrices are already contiguous in $d$, they already satisfy the $k$-major requirement for the hardware. No transpose is needed for $\mathbf{Q}$ or $\mathbf{K}$ to perform the score calculation.

However, for the second GEMM ($\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$), the hardware is contracting over the dimension $B_c$ (the sequence block size). Since $\mathbf{V}$ is stored contiguously in the head dimension $d$, it is *not* contiguous in the sequence dimension $B_c$. Because the FP8 WGMMA instruction strictly requires the $k$-major format (contiguity in the inner contraction dimension), the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix fails this requirement for the second GEMM. 

Your intuition that $\mathbf{V}$ "naturally aligns" with the row-wise operations of the softmax is correct in a global, mathematical sense—every row of $\mathbf{P}$ is multiplied by the full matrix $\mathbf{V}$. But the Tensor Cores do not see the full row at once; they see a block of size $B_c$. For that specific hardware operation, the "inner dimension" is no longer the head dimension $d$, but the sequence dimension $B_c$. Because the layout of $\mathbf{V}$ is fixed to the head dimension $d$, it becomes "misaligned" with the hardware's requirement for the second multiplication, necessitating the in-kernel transpose.
