\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does the 'pingpong' scheduling avoid stalls between softmax and GEMM operations?
Question:
The paper describes a 'pingpong' scheduling where warpgroup 1 performs softmax while warpgroup 2 performs GEMMs. The authors state that the exponential function (required for softmax) has much lower throughput than matmul operations. Is the goal here to keep the Tensor Cores busy 100% of the time? If warpgroup 1 is doing softmax, is the hardware's Tensor Core unit completely idle, or is it still processing the instructions from warpgroup 2?
Answer:
The "pingpong" scheduling in FlashAttention-3 is designed to maximize hardware utilization by exploiting the fact that different types of operations on a GPU are executed by different physical hardware units. To understand how it avoids stalls, it is helpful to look at the hardware architecture of the Hopper H100 GPU and the specific throughput bottlenecks the authors identify.

The core issue in previous versions (like FlashAttention-2) is that the exponential function required for softmax is executed on a "multi-function unit," while matrix multiplications (GEMMs) are executed on "Tensor Cores." The paper highlights a massive disparity in throughput between these units: the H100 can perform 989 TFLOPS of FP16 matmuls but only 3.9 TFLOPS of special functions. In a synchronous execution model, when the GPU reaches the softmax stage, the Tensor Cores sit idle because the execution flow is blocked until the "slow" multi-function unit completes the exponential calculations.

The goal of the pingpong scheduling is indeed to keep the Tensor Cores busy as close to 100% of the time as possible. By using warp-specialization, the authors divide the warpgroups into different roles. In the pingpong scheme, while warpgroup 1 is occupied with the softmax computation (using the multi-function unit), warpgroup 2 is actively issuing WGMMA instructions to perform GEMMs (using the Tensor Cores). 

To answer your specific question regarding whether the Tensor Cores are idle: no, the Tensor Core unit is not idle while warpgroup 1 is doing the softmax. Because the WGMMA instructions on the Hopper architecture are asynchronous, warpgroup 2 can issue the GEMM operations and then proceed with other logic, while the Tensor Cores process those instructions in the background. By using synchronization barriers ($\texttt{bar.sync}$) to carefully sequence these operations, the authors ensure that the "slow" work of warpgroup 1 (softmax) happens at the exact same time as the "fast" work of warpgroup 2 (GEMM).

Essentially, the pingpong scheduling transforms a sequential bottleneck into a parallel overlap. Instead of the GPU timeline looking like $\text{GEMM}_1 \rightarrow \text{Softmax}_1 \rightarrow \text{GEMM}_2 \rightarrow \text{Softmax}_2$, where the Tensor Cores are wasted during the $\text{Softmax}$ steps, the pingpong approach interleaves them so that the Tensor Cores are processing $\text{GEMM}_2$ while the multi-function unit is busy with $\text{Softmax}_1$. This effectively "hides" the high-latency, low-throughput softmax operations behind the high-throughput GEMM operations, significantly increasing the overall TFLOPs/s achieved by the kernel.
