\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# One Tool, Many Worlds: Adapting LLMs, Vision, and Diffusion Models with BOFT

The rapid rise of foundation models like GPT-3 and Stable Diffusion has demonstrated the incredible power of large-scale pretraining. However, because training these models from scratch is prohibitively expensive, the research community has shifted focus toward efficient adaptation. We need ways to take these "giant" models and specialize them for downstream tasks—like medical imaging, specific linguistic styles, or controlled image generation—without retraining every single parameter.

While several Parameter-Efficient Finetuning (PEFT) methods exist, such as LoRA (low-rank adaptation) and prompt tuning, researchers are increasingly looking for principled ways to preserve the knowledge already stored in a model. This is where Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its more efficient successor, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), come into play.

## The Foundation: Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)

To understand BOFT, we first have to look at Orthogonal Finetuning. The core idea of OFT is to preserve the relational information of a model's weight matrices—specifically, the pairwise angles between neurons. The authors argue that by preserving these angles, the model can retain the semantic knowledge gained during pretraining while still learning new task-specific behaviors.

In practice, OFT achieves this by applying a learnable orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ to the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$. The forward pass is modified from $\bm{z}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$ to $\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$. Because $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, it doesn't change the spectral norm of the weights, which helps maintain training stability and prevents the model from "forgetting" too much.

However, OFT faces a major hurdle: high dimensionality. A dense $d$-dimensional orthogonal matrix requires $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters. Even using a block-diagonal structure to save space can be limiting, as it introduces undesirable inductive biases and restricts the expressiveness of the transformation.

## The Solution: Butterfly Factorization

The researchers address this efficiency problem by reimagining the orthogonal matrix as an information transmission problem. They ask: *Can we construct a dense orthogonal matrix using only a small number of parameters?*

The answer lies in the "butterfly structure." You might recognize this from the Cooley-Tukey fast Fourier transform (FFT) algorithm. In a butterfly structure, information from different nodes is exchanged efficiently across a grid-like graph. By adopting this, the authors propose **Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)**.

BOFT represents the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ as a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices:
$$\bm{R}(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$$

This factorization has several massive advantages:
*   **Extreme Parameter Efficiency:** While a standard dense orthogonal matrix scales at $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$, BOFT scales at $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$.
*   **Improved Expressivity:** Unlike block-diagonal matrices, which are very restricted, the butterfly structure can recover many classic linear transforms (like the Discrete Fourier Transform or Hadamard Transform).
*   **Generalizability:** Because the butterfly structure is a common motif in many efficient linear transforms, it provides a "smooth" interpolation between a simple identity matrix and a full orthogonal group.

## Cross-Domain Success: One Tool, Many Worlds

One of the most compelling aspects of this research is the breadth of its application. While previous studies on orthogonal finetuning were largely limited to controlling text-to-image diffusion models, the authors tested BOFT across three vastly different domains:

1.  **Large Language Models (LLMs):** Testing the method's ability to adapt to various natural language tasks.
2.  **Computer Vision:** Applying BOFT to large vision transformers to see how it handles spatial data.
3.  **Text-to-Image Diffusion:** Validating that it maintains the high-quality generative capabilities of models like Stable Diffusion.

The results were consistent: BOFT outperformed current state-of-the-art methods by a considerable margin. It proved to be a "universal" adaptation tool, providing a stable and parameter-efficient way to fine-tune the world's most powerful models without adding inference latency. By merging the learned matrices back into the original model after training, BOFT offers the power of sophisticated orthogonal transformations with the efficiency of modern PEFT techniques.
