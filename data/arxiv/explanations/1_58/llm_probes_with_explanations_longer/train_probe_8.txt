In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the authors attribute the main cause of BitNet b1.58's increasing speed-up with larger model sizes to the computation whose time cost grows with model size, which they identify as nn.Linear. This is sensible because Transformer blocks devote most of their per-token latency to generalized matrix multiplications (GEMMs) inside the attention projections and the MLP, i.e., the nn.Linear layers. The arithmetic cost of a linear layer with input dimension \(k\), output dimension \(n\), and batch/sequence aggregation \(m\) scales as \(O(m n k)\) (more precisely \(\approx 2mnk\) FLOPs in FP16/BF16), so as hidden sizes and number of layers grow, the fraction of time spent on these GEMMs dominates other components (e.g., embeddings, softmax, normalization). BitNet b1.58 replaces nn.Linear with BitLinear, where weights are ternary \(\{-1,0,1\}\), turning multiplications into integer additions/subtractions and skipping work on zeros, so the per-op cost drops from FP16 multiply-adds to INT8 additions. Because the absolute number of GEMM operations scales with model size while the relative cost of non-linearities and embeddings does not, the benefit from cheaper linear algebra compounds as models get larger.

Moreover, the system-level bottlenecks tied to nn.Linear—weight bandwidth and on-chip accumulation—also scale with model width and depth, and BitNet b1.58 alleviates both. Ternary weights stored in \(\approx 1.58\) bits reduce DRAM traffic and SRAM footprint by a factor proportional to the parameter count, shrinking the memory-bound portion of latency; and integer accumulation with 8-bit activations lowers energy and cycle counts per MAC. The paper’s measurements reflect this: as the model size increases (e.g., up to 70B), the speed-up grows to \(4.1\times\), consistent with nn.Linear’s scaling and with the observation that the embedding’s full-precision cost becomes a smaller proportion at scale. In short, because nn.Linear’s time cost grows with model size while BitNet b1.58 makes that exact computation drastically cheaper (\(\)INT8 additions instead of FP16 multiply-adds, plus effective sparsity from zeros), the overall speed-up necessarily increases as models become larger.
