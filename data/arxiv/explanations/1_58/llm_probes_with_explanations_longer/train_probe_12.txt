In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', interpreting ternary weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ as actions on inputs, BitNet b$1.58$ matrix multiplications effectively reduce to add, subtract, or skip. This follows directly from the BitLinear design where every weight is quantized to $\tilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,0,1\}$ via the absmean scaling-and-rounding scheme, $\tilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)$. When activations are quantized to integers in a symmetric range $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ per token (removing any zero-point offset), each inner product term in a matrix multiply, $\tilde{W}_{ij}\,x_j$, collapses to one of three cases: $x_j$ if $\tilde{W}_{ij}=1$, $-x_j$ if $\tilde{W}_{ij}=-1$, or $0$ if $\tilde{W}_{ij}=0$. Consequently, a row-wise dot product becomes $\sum_j \tilde{W}_{ij}x_j = \sum_{j:\tilde{W}_{ij}=1} x_j - \sum_{j:\tilde{W}_{ij}=-1} x_j$, where entries with $\tilde{W}_{ij}=0$ are skipped. This replaces multiplication with sign selection and accumulation, implementable entirely with integer addition and inexpensive negation in two’s complement.

This reduction is consistent with the paper’s claims that BitNet’s matrix multiplication “only involves integer addition” and that BitNet b$1.58$ “requires almost no multiplication operations.” Because activations are symmetric around zero, subtraction can be realized as addition of a negated operand, and negation itself is a constant-time integer operation. The explicit zero in the ternary alphabet enables feature filtering—terms with $\tilde{W}_{ij}=0$ incur no arithmetic at all—further lowering the number of additions needed. Together, these properties explain why the arithmetic energy is dominated by INT8 additions and why the model achieves large speedups and energy savings: the fundamental compute transforms from general multiply-accumulate to select-sign-and-accumulate, i.e., add, subtract, or skip.
