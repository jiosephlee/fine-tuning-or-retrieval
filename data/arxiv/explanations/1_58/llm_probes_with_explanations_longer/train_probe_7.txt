According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', BitNet's dramatic energy savings for matrix computations are primarily explained by the elimination of the expensive floating-point operation known as multiplication. In standard Transformer layers, the dominant cost is dense matrix multiplication in modules like nn.Linear, which perform FP16/BF16 multiply-accumulate operations: for a layer output y = W x, each element requires a sequence of floating-point multiplications and additions. BitNet b1.58 constrains weights to the ternary set W_{ij} ∈ {−1, 0, 1} and quantizes activations to 8-bit integers, so the product W x reduces to a pattern of integer additions, subtractions, and skips: y_i = ∑_j W_{ij} x_j, where W_{ij} = 1 implies an addition, W_{ij} = −1 implies a subtraction (sign flip + addition), and W_{ij} = 0 implies no operation. This removes floating-point multiplications entirely and replaces the FP16/BF16 MACs with predominantly INT8 additions, which the paper notes are vastly cheaper in energy on modern process nodes.

The paper’s energy analysis focuses on matrix multiplication as the dominant arithmetic, and reports that BitNet b1.58’s matmul is composed mainly of INT8 additions, whereas the FP16 baseline consists of both FP16 additions and FP16 multiplications. Using published energy models for 7 nm chips, the authors estimate a 71.4× reduction in arithmetic energy for matrix multiplication, which is consistent with the well-known gap E_{mul}^{FP} ≫ E_{add}^{INT}. Since compute power is a fundamental limiter on chip throughput, reducing per-operation energy also translates into speedups. Moreover, the architecture and quantization choices (absmean scaling to ternary weights, activations scaled per token to [−Q_b, Q_b] to avoid zero-points) ensure the computation stays in the integer domain without costly scaling multipliers, preserving the elimination of floating-point multiplications while maintaining accuracy. Thus, because LLM cost is dominated by matrix multiplies and BitNet b1.58 re-expresses these multiplies as low-energy integer additions and sign flips, the removal of floating-point multiplication is the primary driver of its dramatic energy savings.
