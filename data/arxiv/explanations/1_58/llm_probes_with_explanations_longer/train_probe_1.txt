According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', when optimizing a BitNet b1.58 accelerator, a hardware designer should deprioritize floating-point multiplication. This follows directly from the computation paradigm of BitNet b1.58: all weights are ternary in the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and activations are quantized to 8 bits, so the core matrix multiplication in Transformer layers reduces to integer accumulation with sign and zero handling rather than real-valued multiply-accumulate. The paper explicitly notes that BitNet’s matrix multiplication “only involves integer addition,” and that the model “requires almost no multiplication operations,” in sharp contrast to FP16/BF16 baselines whose bulk compute is floating-point additions and multiplications. As a result, the arithmetic pipeline best suited to BitNet b1.58 centers on high-throughput INT8 addition, bitwise sign application, and efficient data movement, not FP16 multipliers; optimizing floating-point multiply units yields little benefit because the algorithmic workload doesn’t exercise them.

The energy and performance results further justify deprioritizing floating-point multiplication. Using the energy model in prior work, the paper shows that BitNet b1.58’s matrix multiplication—dominated by INT8 additions—reduces arithmetic energy by $71.4\times$ on 7nm compared to FP16 add/mul pipelines. End-to-end measurements indicate growing latency and energy advantages as model size scales, because the fraction of time spent in $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ grows with size and BitNet replaces FP16 multiply-accumulate with cheaper integer accumulations. Therefore, an accelerator targeting BitNet b1.58 should allocate silicon and power budgets to fast integer add trees, low-latency SRAM and bandwidth for 8-bit activations and 1.58-bit weights, and efficient clipping/rounding and per-token scaling, while deprioritizing FP16/BF16 multiplication units that are largely idle under this workload.
