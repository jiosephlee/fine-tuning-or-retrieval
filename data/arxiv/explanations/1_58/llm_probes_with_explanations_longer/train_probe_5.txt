According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the fundamental hardware constraint that links reduced energy consumption to the possibility of faster compute throughput is power. The paper explicitly notes that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” and then argues that if each arithmetic operation consumes less energy—as is the case when replacing FP16 additions and multiplications with mostly INT8 additions in BitNet b1.58—then one can execute more operations per unit time under the same power budget. Formally, if the chip is bounded by a maximum allowable power \(P_{\max}\) (set by thermal and delivery constraints), the achievable operation rate \(R\) satisfies \(P \approx E_{\mathrm{op}} \cdot R\), so \(R \approx P_{\max}/E_{\mathrm{op}}\). Lowering the per-operation energy \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\) (e.g., by avoiding FP16 multiplies) directly increases \(R\) without exceeding \(P_{\max}\), enabling higher throughput. The paper’s energy model citations and estimate (a 71.4× arithmetic energy reduction for matrix multiplications on 7nm) make this relationship concrete: with dramatically smaller \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\), BitNet b1.58 can sustain more integer additions per second within the same power envelope.

This power-bound perspective extends beyond ALU energy to memory movement, which the paper identifies as another major contributor. Moving fewer bits (ternary weights and 8-bit activations) reduces DRAM-to-SRAM energy and thus average power, mitigating bandwidth and thermal constraints that otherwise force frequency throttling or limit parallelism. Using the standard dynamic power relation \(P \propto \alpha C V^2 f\), designs that allow lower switching activity \(\alpha\) and smaller effective capacitance \(C\) per operation—achieved by simpler integer datapaths and narrower memories—permit higher frequency \(f\) or more concurrent units at the same \(P_{\max}\). That is why the paper observes increasing speedups at larger model sizes: as the fraction of time in \(\mathrm{nn.Linear}\) grows, the benefit of lower \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\) for matrix multiplies translates more directly into higher sustained throughput within fixed power limits.
