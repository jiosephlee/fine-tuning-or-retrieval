According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', to achieve the performance parity claimed for BitNet b1.58, the implicitly preferred training approach over post-training quantization is quantization-aware training. The paper explicitly argues that post-training quantization (PTQ) is “sub-optimal,” especially at ultra-low precisions, and then describes BitNet b1.58 as “trained from scratch, with 1.58-bit weights and 8-bit activations.” That training setup means the model optimizes under a ternary constraint throughout learning, using a quantization operator on weights of the form
\[
\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon},-1,1\right),\quad \gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|,
\]
and activation scaling to the per-token range \([{-}Q_b,Q_b]\). By making the forward pass and loss depend on \(\widetilde{W}\) and quantized activations, the optimization adapts normalization, gating (SwiGLU), and weight distributions to the ternary bins \(\{-1,0,1\}\). This is the essence of quantization-aware training: learn with quantization “in the loop” so the network co-designs its statistics (e.g., RMSNorm scales and the absmean \(\gamma\)) to minimize the task loss under quantization, rather than quantizing a full-precision optimum post hoc.

This choice is necessary to reach the paper’s parity claims at 3B and above because ultra-low-bit bins induce large quantization error if applied after training. In PTQ, the model optimizes a full-precision objective \(L(W)\) and only later incurs the rounding error \(\|W-\widetilde{W}\|\), which at 1–2 bits can severely distort learned features and attention weights. In contrast, BitNet b1.58’s recipe encourages weight magnitudes and sparsity patterns (via the explicit zero bin) to align with \(\widetilde{W}\), effectively minimizing \(L(\widetilde{W})\) during training. The absmean scaling \(\gamma\) reduces per-matrix quantization error by normalizing weight distributions before rounding, and per-token activation scaling eliminates zero-point issues, both of which are learned around the quantized regime. The reported results—matching FP16 perplexity and zero-shot accuracy starting at 3B, while maintaining 1.58-bit constraints—are exactly the outcomes one expects when using quantization-aware training rather than PTQ at such low precisions.
