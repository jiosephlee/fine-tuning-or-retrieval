According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', after applying the scale, rounding, and clipping pipeline, small-magnitude weights near 0 are most likely mapped to zero. This follows directly from the defined absmean quantization rule: weights are first normalized by the average absolute magnitude, then rounded to the nearest integer in the ternary set, and finally clipped to the range. Concretely, the paper sets
\[
\widetilde{W} \;=\; \mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon},\, -1,\, 1\right),\quad
\gamma \;=\; \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|,
\]
with
\[
\mathrm{RoundClip}(x,a,b) \;=\; \max\!\big(a,\, \min(b,\, \mathrm{round}(x))\big).
\]
Under standard rounding to the nearest integer, any scaled entry \(x = W_{ij}/(\gamma+\epsilon)\) with \(|x| < \tfrac{1}{2}\) rounds to \(0\). Therefore, all weights satisfying \(|W_{ij}| < \tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon)\) are mapped to \(0\), and the clip operation leaves that value unchanged. Since \(\gamma\) is the mean absolute value, it is natural that a substantial fraction of weights fall below this half-mean threshold—especially in heavy-tailed or approximately Laplacian/Gaussian-like distributions typical in trained neural networks—so small-magnitude weights concentrate in the basin that quantizes to zero.

Beyond the thresholding geometry, the inclusion of \(0\) in the codebook explicitly encourages feature filtering: components whose contribution is weak relative to the layer’s average scale are suppressed. Because the normalization uses a global per-matrix scale \(\gamma\), the rounding boundary \(|W_{ij}| < \tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon)\) adapts to the weight distribution in each layer, preferentially zeroing out low-energy coefficients. This makes the ternary mapping both noise-robust and efficient: values near \(0\) that would otherwise introduce negligible or noisy signal are pruned to \(0\), while only weights with \(|W_{ij}| \gtrsim \tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon)\) survive as \(\pm 1\), yielding the intended sparse, sign-preserving representation.
