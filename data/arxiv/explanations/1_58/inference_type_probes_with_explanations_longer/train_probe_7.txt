According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', if BitNet b1.58 were reverted to have weights only in $\{-1, +1\}$ (removing $0$), the most likely outcome for modeling capability compared to BitNet b1.58 would be worse performance. The paper explicitly motivates the ternary design by noting that the inclusion of $0$ gives “explicit support for feature filtering,” which increases modeling capacity in low-bit regimes. Concretely, with ternary weights $\widetilde{W}_{ij} \in \{-1,0,1\}$ and 8-bit activations, a linear layer computes $y = \widetilde{W}x$ where entries with $\widetilde{W}_{ij}=0$ remove harmful or noisy contributions, effectively performing learned sparsification at the connection level. In contrast, a binary-only layer with $\widehat{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,1\}$ forces every connection to contribute either additively or subtractively, increasing interference and limiting the network’s ability to gate features. Under the paper’s absmean quantizer,
$\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon},-1,1\right)$ with $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$,
small-magnitude real weights are naturally mapped to $0$, approximating magnitude pruning and preserving salient signals while suppressing weak ones. This additional degree of freedom—being able to “not use” a connection—has long been observed to improve ternary/binary networks, and the authors’ empirical results show it is pivotal for closing the gap to FP16 at 3B+ scales.

Moreover, removing $0$ would degrade optimization dynamics and signal-to-noise characteristics in the quantized Transformer. With $\widetilde{W}\in\{-1,0,1\}$, the effective fan-in of each neuron adapts during training because many connections become zero, reducing variance in $y_i=\sum_j \widetilde{W}_{ij}x_j$ and stabilizing gradients through RMSNorm and SwiGLU blocks; this is especially important when activations are quantized to $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ and zero-point quantization is eliminated. In a strictly binary setting, $y_i=\sum_j s_{ij}x_j$ with $s_{ij}\in\{-1,1\}$ has no mechanism to suppress weak or misaligned features, increasing destructive interference and gradient noise, which is exacerbated as model width grows and the proportion of nn.Linear dominates the compute. The paper’s results and discussion consistently attribute BitNet b1.58’s ability to match FP16 perplexity and end-task accuracy (starting at 3B) to this ternary gating effect, implying that reverting to pure binary weights would most likely worsen performance due to reduced representational flexibility and poorer feature selection.
