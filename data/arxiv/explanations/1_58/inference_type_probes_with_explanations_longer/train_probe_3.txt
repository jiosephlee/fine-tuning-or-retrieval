In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the ability to set weights to zero is analogous to the common model compression concept sparsity. BitNet b1.58 constrains every parameter to the ternary set \(\{-1,0,1\}\) via the absmean quantization
\[
\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon},-1,1\right),\quad \gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|,
\]
so any scaled weight whose magnitude falls below \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) will round to \(0\). This systematically produces many exact zeros wherever the underlying full-precision \(W\) has small-magnitude entries, which is precisely what sparsity aims to achieve in compression and pruning: reduce the \(\ell_0\) pseudo-norm \(\|W\|_0\) by zeroing unimportant connections. The paper even highlights an algorithmic benefit of these zeros as “explicit support for feature filtering”—zero weights act as masks that disable certain feature channels or connections, just as sparse models selectively remove edges to improve generalization and efficiency. In other words, including \(0\) makes BitNet b1.58 a quantized-but-sparse parameterization, functionally equivalent to sparse compression where unnecessary parameters are pruned.

This sparsity analogy extends to systems and compute. When many entries of \(\widetilde{W}\) are \(0\), matrix multiplication becomes an accumulation over only the nonzero ternary entries, replacing most FP16 multiplies with cheap integer additions and skips:
\[
Y = \widetilde{W}X \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; Y_i = \sum_{j:\,\widetilde{W}_{ij}\neq 0} \widetilde{W}_{ij}\, X_j, \quad \widetilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
This is the core lever behind the reported latency and energy gains: arithmetic is dominated by INT8 additions rather than FP16 adds and multiplies, and zero entries incur no work. From a memory perspective, zeros are compressible with sparse formats or simple run-length schemes, lowering DRAM bandwidth and on-chip footprint—exactly the traditional benefit of sparse compression. The paper’s measurements—3.55× lower GPU memory, up to 4.1× speedups, and 71.4× arithmetic energy savings—are consistent with a sparse ternary regime where the proportion of costly operations scales with \(\| \widetilde{W}\|_0\) rather than the dense size \(nm\). Thus, setting weights to zero in BitNet b1.58 not only mirrors the conceptual role of sparsity in compression, but also realizes its practical advantages in modeling, compute, and systems efficiency.
