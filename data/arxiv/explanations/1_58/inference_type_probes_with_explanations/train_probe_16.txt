In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the added $\epsilon$ in $\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon}$ guards against division by zero. This follows from the definition $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, which can become $0$ if a weight matrix (or a submatrix under blockwise processing) is all zeros—an event made plausible by the ternary constraint $\{-1,0,1\}$ and the paper’s emphasis on “feature filtering” via explicit zeros—so without $\epsilon$ the scale factor would be ill-defined; moreover, when $\gamma$ is merely very small (e.g., many weights at $0$ and few at $\pm 1$), adding a small $\epsilon$ also prevents excessively large rescaling that would push $\frac{W}{\gamma}$ to magnitudes that systematically saturate $\mathrm{RoundClip}(\cdot,-1,1)$ and destabilize the straight-through optimization, thereby serving both as a safeguard against the $\gamma=0$ case and as a numerical stabilizer for near-degenerate weight distributions induced by ternarization.
