According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the on-chip memory component that could therefore be reduced in size to lower hardware cost while maintaining throughput is SRAM. This follows from the paper’s discussion that moving weights from DRAM to an on-chip accelerator memory (explicitly exemplified as SRAM) is a major inference cost and that industry often enlarges SRAM to sustain throughput, which “introduces significantly higher costs than DRAM”; BitNet b1.58’s ternary weights reduce parameter storage from 16-bit to 1.58-bit, yielding roughly a \(16 / 1.58 \approx 10\times\) decrease in capacity and bandwidth demands for weights, while activations are quantized to 8-bit, further cutting on-chip traffic, so a much smaller SRAM can still feed the compute without becoming the bandwidth bottleneck, thereby maintaining (and even improving) throughput while lowering hardware cost.
