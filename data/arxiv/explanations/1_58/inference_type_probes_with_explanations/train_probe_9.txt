According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', if BitNet b1.58 reverted to only $\{-1, 1\}$ weights (no $0$), the most directly lost modeling capability would be feature filtering. This follows from the paper’s central motivation for adding the zero level: by allowing weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$, BitLinear layers can set $\widetilde{W}_{ij}=0$ for specific connections after the absmean quantization $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)$ with $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, which provides an explicit, learned hard mask that “turns off” unhelpful input dimensions or output channels, i.e., a form of sparsity-based feature selection; without the $0$ option, every path contributes either additively or subtractively ($\pm 1$) and cannot be silenced, so each output is an unavoidable signed sum of activations, increasing crosstalk and preventing the model from suppressing noisy or irrelevant features. In practice, this ternary “off switch” acts like a discrete gate within projections (e.g., in attention and SwiGLU blocks), improving representational precision by selectively blocking information flow, which the authors cite as the reason b1.58 has stronger modeling capacity than the original 1-bit BitNet; removing $0$ would therefore most directly eliminate this explicit feature filtering mechanism.
