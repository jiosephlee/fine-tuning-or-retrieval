In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', if activations were scaled to $[0, Q_b]$ instead of $[-Q_b, Q_b]$, the extra quantization complication that would reappear is zero-point quantization. This follows from their explicit design choice: they scale activations per token to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ “to get rid of the zero-point quantization,” which is only possible with symmetric ranges around zero where the integer representation of zero can be $0$; by contrast, scaling to a non-symmetric, non-zero-centered interval like $[0, Q_b]$ requires an affine mapping $x_q = \mathrm{round}(x/s + z)$ with a non-zero zero-point $z$ to represent $x=0$ in the quantized domain, reintroducing the need to store and apply $z$ and to compensate for it during matrix multiplications (e.g., subtracting $z$-dependent terms), thereby complicating both implementation and hardware kernels relative to the simpler symmetric case where $z=0$ and $x_q = \mathrm{round}(x/s)$.
