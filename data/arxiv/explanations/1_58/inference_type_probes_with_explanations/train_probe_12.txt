In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the conceptual role of $\\gamma$ in the weight quantization formula is to act as a scale factor. This follows directly from the definition $\\widetilde{W} = \\mathrm{RoundClip}\\big(\\tfrac{W}{\\gamma + \\epsilon}, -1, 1\\big)$ with $\\gamma = \\tfrac{1}{nm} \\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$, where dividing by the layer-wise average absolute weight rescales the weight matrix to have unit mean magnitude before rounding to $\\{-1,0,1\\}$; in effect, $\\gamma$ normalizes the dynamic range so that the ternary grid points correspond to meaningful relative magnitudes across layers, preventing uniform saturation at $\\pm 1$ when weights are large and avoiding collapse to $0$ when weights are small, while preserving the sign structure and promoting explicit feature filtering via the zero state. By anchoring the rounding thresholds around a standardized scale (with $\\epsilon$ for numerical stability), the abs-mean normalization makes the ternarization stable and consistent across different layers and training steps, ensuring that $\\mathrm{RoundClip}(\\cdot)$ operates on a roughly comparable scale and thus $\\gamma$ serves as the critical scaling factor that maps full-precision weights onto the ternary lattice with minimal distortion.
