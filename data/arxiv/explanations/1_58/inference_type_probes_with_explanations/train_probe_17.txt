In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', interpreting ternary weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ as actions on inputs, BitNet b$1.58$ matrix multiplications effectively reduce to add, subtract, or skip. This follows because each BitLinear layer replaces real-valued weights with ternary $\widetilde{W}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n\times m}$ via the absmean quantizer $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)$, and activations are kept in low-precision integer form, so the core operation $y_i=\sum_j \widetilde{W}_{ij} x_j$ involves only terms where $\widetilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,0,1\}$: multiplying by $+1$ corresponds to adding $x_j$, by $-1$ to subtracting $x_j$, and by $0$ to skipping $x_j$ entirely; thus the dot product becomes a signed accumulation with conditional inclusion, implementable with integer additions and sign flips rather than general multiplications. The paper emphasizes that BitNet’s matrix multiplications “only involve integer addition” and “require almost no multiplication,” with energy/cost dominated by INT8 additions; empirically, kernels use 2-bit implementations and show large speedups, consistent with the fact that $\{-1,0,1\}$ weights reduce multiply-accumulate to add/subtract/skip at the arithmetic level.
