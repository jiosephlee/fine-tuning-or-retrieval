In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the logarithmic expression (in base 2) that corresponds to the 1.58 bits claimed for a ternary weight is $\log_2(3)$. This follows because BitNet b1.58 constrains each weight to the ternary set $\{-1,0,1\}$, giving exactly three discrete states per weight, and the minimal number of bits needed to uniquely encode $k$ equiprobable states is the information-theoretic quantity $\log_2(k)$; thus a ternary weight has an information content of $\log_2(3)\approx 1.585$ bits, which the paper rounds to 1.58 bits, reflecting that adding the extra “0” state to the original 1-bit (binary $\{-1,+1\}$) BitNet increases the representational cardinality from $2$ to $3$ and hence the effective bit budget per weight from $\log_2(2)=1$ to $\log_2(3)\approx 1.58$.
