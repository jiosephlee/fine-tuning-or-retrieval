According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', to achieve the performance parity claimed for BitNet b1.58, the implicitly preferred training approach over post-training quantization is quantization-aware training. This follows because the authors expressly deem post-training quantization “sub-optimal” for LLMs and instead train BitNet b1.58 from scratch with quantized weights and activations, embedding the ternary constraint directly into the forward path via an absmean quantizer, i.e., they scale each weight matrix by its average absolute value and apply a ternary rounding-and-clipping operator, $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)$ with $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, while maintaining 8-bit activations scaled to $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ per token to avoid zero-points; such training-time quantization (a form of QAT) lets the optimizer adapt weight distributions and feature usage to the ternary set $\{-1,0,1\}$, including exploiting the introduced $0$ state for explicit feature filtering, rather than incurring the distributional mismatch and accuracy loss typical of after-the-fact quantization. Empirically, this co-designed, quantization-aware training pipeline is what enables BitNet b1.58 to match FP16/BF16 perplexity and end-task performance from the 3B scale onward while delivering large gains in latency, memory, and energy, whereas the paper notes that industry-standard post-training quantization, even at higher bit-widths like 4 bits, tends to be inferior.
