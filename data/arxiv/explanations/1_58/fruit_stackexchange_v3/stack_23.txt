### For the 2T-token training comparison against StableLM-3B, how were data recipe and evaluation matched?
Question:
You compare a BitNet b1.58 model trained for 2T tokens to StableLM-3B's reported numbers on a benchmark (Winogrande, PIQA, SciQ, LAMBADA, ARC-easy). Were tokenizer, data filtering, and sampling policies identical? Since StableLM reports both accuracy and normalized accuracy for some tasks, you average them—does StableLM do the same? Could you share exact prompts and evaluation harness versions for a strictly apples-to-apples comparison?
Answer:
Short answer: we matched StableLM-3B at the level reported in the paper but did not (and do not claim to have) reproduced every low-level preprocessing and evaluation artifact of their release. Concretely, our $2\mathrm{T}$-token <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> run used the StableLM-3B data recipe and the lm-evaluation-harness pipeline for scoring, and the StableLM numbers were taken from their technical report. Beyond that the paper does not claim identical tokenizer, filtering, sampling seeds/policies, or a specific lm-eval commit/prompts, so a perfectly strict “apples-to-apples” <|endoftext|> was not established in the writeup.

What the paper states and what we matched
The Methods/Results sections say we trained the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> model “following the data recipe of StableLM-3B” and that evaluation followed the pipeline from lm-evaluation-harness. The $2\mathrm{T}$-token StableLM-3B numbers are quoted directly from StableLM’s technical report. For the five-task benchmark (Winogrande, PIQA, SciQ, LAMBADA, ARC-easy) the paper therefore matches at two explicit levels: the training token budget ($2\mathrm{T}$) and the <|endoftext|> data recipe, and it uses lm-eval for zero-shot scoring of the same benchmark <|endoftext|>

What was not (explicitly) matched in the paper
The paper does not report any claim of reproducing or matching the following low-level artifacts, and these are not documented in the manuscript: the exact tokenizer/vocabulary (for example whether we used the identical tokenizer model and byte-pair tokenization parameters as StableLM-3B), the precise data filtering scripts or thresholds they used when constructing their <|endoftext|> sampling policies (random seeds, order or chunking of data, duplication removal specifics, upsampling/downsampling of sources), nor an explicit lm-evaluation-harness commit hash or seed for deterministic evaluation. In short, we followed StableLM’s <|endoftext|> recipe but did not (and did not report) a bit-for-bit replication of their preprocessing and evaluation harness.

On averaging accuracy and “normalized accuracy”
StableLM’s report provides both plain accuracy and, for some tasks, a normalized-accuracy <|endoftext|> In our paper we state: “For tasks measured with accuracy and <|endoftext|> accuracy, we take the average of the two.” That averaging is our evaluation choice to produce one <|endoftext|> per task for Table 2; it is not necessarily what StableLM did in their internal aggregation. The StableLM technical report numbers we quote were taken as reported (we did not re-compute StableLM’s scores ourselves). So if StableLM averaged or combined metrics differently, that would create a mismatch; the paper’s averaging rule is our post-processing applied to the reported values or to our own lm-eval outputs (the wording indicates we applied this averaging when reporting our numbers).
