### How are activations quantized to 8-bit 'per token' and how is the scale handled across layers and KV cache?
Question:
The paper states activations are 8-bit and 'scaled to [-Q_b, Q_b] per token' to avoid zero-point quantization. Does 'per token' mean a separate symmetric scale per sequence position (i.e., per row in the batch*time dimension), or per token per channel? How is the scale tracked and applied across consecutive layers, especially around non-linearities (SwiGLU) and attention softmax? What precision is used to store the KV cache, and is the same per-token scaling applied to cached keys/values across timesteps?
Answer:
Short answer: In this paper “per token” means one symmetric (zero-centered) scale scalar per token position (i.e., per row of the $\text{batch}\times\text{time}\times\text{hidden}$ matrix), not a separate scale per channel. The implementation implication is that every token’s entire hidden vector is quantized with the same symmetric scale value; that scale is carried alongside the 8-bit integers and combined (multiplicatively) with weight scales when you do subsequent linear layers. Nonlinearities and softmax are evaluated in higher precision (or after applying the appropriate scale), and the KV cache is stored in the 8-bit form together with the per-token scale for that timestep so cached keys/values can be re‑scaled correctly when reused.

Explanation and reasoning grounded in the paper
The paper explicitly says “the activations are all scaled to $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ per token to get rid of the zero-point quantization.” That wording most naturally reads as: for each token position you compute a single symmetric scaling factor and map the full hidden vector of that token into the symmetric 8-bit range. This avoids having to store a zero-point (an additive offset), which complicates integer arithmetic and storage. Using one scale per token is cheap (one float per token) and is exactly what “per token” is used to mean in similar quantization schemes.

How scales are tracked and used across layers
When an activation vector $x$ (floating) is quantized per token you have
$x_{int8} = \mathrm{Quantize}\left(\frac{x}{s_x}\right)$ in the integer domain and you store $s_x$ (a scalar) alongside the int8 vector. For a linear layer with (possibly quantized) weights $W$, the matrix multiply in real math is
$y = W x$.
If $x$ is stored as int8 with scale $s_x$ and $W$ is represented with its own scale $s_W$ (or as low-bit weights with an implicit scale), then the accumulated integer product yields an integer accumulator $A$ such that
$y_{\text{float}} = s_W \, s_x \, A$
so the output scale $s_y = s_W \, s_x$ (possibly times any rescaling introduced by the accumulator normalization) — equivalently you attach a new per-token scale for $y$. In practice you either (a) keep the output in quantized form and store $s_y$ per token, or (b) dequantize to float for downstream ops and recompute scales as needed. The paper’s description (8-bit activations, per-token symmetric scale) implies they keep a scale per token and propagate/compute the correct combined scale when composing layers.

What happens around nonlinearities (SwiGLU) and softmax
Nonlinearities pose two choices: dequantize, apply the nonlinearity in float, then requantize (storing a new per-token scale); or implement the nonlinearity in integer with scale bookkeeping. The paper does not give a low-level microkernel description, but its text emphasizes simplicity (“more convenient and simple for both implementation and system-level optimization”) and that they do not scale activations before nonlinear functions to $[0,Q_b]$. That implies the practical route is to keep symmetric int8 + per-token scale, dequantize to float (or to a higher internal accumulator precision like FP16/BF16) when applying nonlinear functions such as SwiGLU, then requantize the result back to int8 with a freshly computed per-token scale. For attention softmax, numerical stability and the need for exponentials/normalization strongly favour performing softmax in floating precision: you dequantize the attention logits (apply the stored scales), run softmax in float, and then (if you need to) quantize the resulting attention weights/outputs back to int8 with a new per-token scale. The paper’s discussion of accuracy and system simplicity is consistent with doing nonlinearities and softmax in higher precision rather than doing complex integer approximations.

KV cache precision and scaling across timesteps
The paper explicitly notes that reducing activations from 16 bits to 8 bits helps KV cache memory and that these 8-bit activations “allow the context length to be doubled given the same resources.” That suggests the KV cache is stored in its compressed (8-bit) form with the same per-token symmetric scale stored for each cached timestep. When you later reuse cached keys/values for a new attention query, you either dequantize using the stored per-token scale(s) (restore float keys/values for the attention), or you incorporate the scale into the attention matmul by multiplying accumulators by the stored scales. Concretely: if $K_t$ and $V_t$ were quantized with scale $s_t$ at timestep $t$, the system must keep $s_t$ so when computing e.g. $Q\cdot K^{T}$ you account for $s_Q \, s_t$ in the dot-products. The paper mentions potential for further lossless compression, but the described approach is to store int8 + per-token scale for cache entries.

What the paper does and what is left unspecified
The paper gives the macro design: 8-bit activations, symmetric per-token scaling, and keeping nonlinearities/attention consistent with that choice. It deliberately emphasizes system-level convenience and avoids zero-point arithmetic. It does not publish microkernel-level details such as: whether they fuse multiply–accumulates to delay dequantization, what accumulator bit width is used in each kernel, or whether some nonlinearities are approximated in integer in some optimized runtimes. Those are implementation choices compatible with the paper’s design: storing one float scale per token, propagating scales multiplicatively through linear layers, dequantizing for nonlinear ops and for softmax, and saving cached $K/V$ in int8 plus per-token scales for later use.

Summary
The phrase “per token” = one symmetric scale per token position (one scalar per row of hidden activations). That scale is stored and combined with weight scales to produce output scales when you do linear layers; nonlinearity and softmax are handled in higher precision after applying the scale (then requantized if you want to keep 8-bit activations); the KV cache is kept in the 8-bit + per-token-scale format so cached entries can be rescaled correctly when reused. The paper intentionally chooses symmetric per-token scaling to avoid zero-point complexity and to simplify system-level kernels, but the exact low-level fusion/accumulator choices are left to implementation.
