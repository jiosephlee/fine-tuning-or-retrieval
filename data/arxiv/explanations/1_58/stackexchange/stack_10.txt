\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits}

### What is Q_b for 8-bit activations and does per-token symmetric scaling impact cross-token operations?
Question:
When scaling activations to [-Q_b, Q_b], is Q_b set to 127 for signed int8? If each token uses its own symmetric scale, how does this interact with operations that mix tokens, like attention softmax across a sequence? Do you rescale to a common dynamic range before attention scores are computed, or are scores computed in higher precision after dequantization?
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — for symmetric signed $int8$ you take $Q_b = 127$. And you do not try to run softmax across differently scaled tokens in raw $int8$: when tokens with per-token symmetric scales must be mixed (e.g. $QK^T$ and softmax across the sequence) the usual approach is to bring the values to a common numeric domain (effectively apply the per-token scale factors into the accumulation or dequantize to higher precision) and then compute the dot-products / softmax in higher precision ($FP16/FP32$) rather than directly mixing differently scaled $int8$ integers.

Why $Q_b = 127$
The paper says activations are scaled to the symmetric range $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ “to get rid of the zero-point quantization.” For an $8$-bit signed integer dtype ($int8$) the representable symmetric range is $[-128, 127]$, but symmetric quantization normally uses the positive maximum as $Q_b = 127$ so that zero maps exactly to integer $0$ and the range is symmetric in magnitude. So when they say $8$-bit activations scaled to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ the practical choice is $Q_b = 127$.

Why you must handle per-token scales before cross-token ops
The paper quantizes activations “per token” (per-token symmetric scale). That means the integer values for token $i$ implicitly represent $ \text{real values} = (scale_i)\cdot(\text{int8\_values}_i)$. If you then mix activations from different tokens in the same operation (for example computing attention scores $QK^T$ where $Q$ and $K$ come from different tokens or computing softmax over sequence positions), you cannot correctly accumulate integer values directly unless all terms share the same scale. Two correct implementation strategies exist:

1) Fold the per-token scales into accumulators / weights and do integer matmul with a common scale factor. If you want to keep integer kernels you can multiply the $int8$ inputs by their per-token scale factors (or equivalently multiply the accumulator after integer matmul by the product of scales) so that all contributions are in a common real-domain scale before applying nonlinearities. In practice this often means computing integer matmul to $int32$ accumulators and then multiplying the $int32$ accumulator by a floating-point scaling factor before applying softmax. That is numerically equivalent to dequantizing the activations for the matmul but lets you preserve an integer kernel for much of the work.

2) Dequantize per-token activations to $FP16/FP32$ before any cross-token mixing and compute $QK^T$ and softmax in $FP16/FP32$. This is simpler and very common: convert each token chunk to floating point by multiplying $int8$ by its per-token scale, then run the attention computations in floating point. Softmax and normalization are sensitive to dynamic range and numerical stability, so running them in $FP16/FP32$ after dequantization is the safer and usual choice.

Which the paper implies / what is practical
The paper states activations are $8$-bit and “scaled to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ per token” to remove zero-point for simplicity and system-level optimization, but it does not claim softmax or attention are performed directly in $int8$ across different token scales. Given standard practice and the paper’s emphasis on compatibility with existing toolkits (FasterTransformer, vLLM, etc.), the pragmatic implementation is to apply per-token scale factors into the computation so that attention score computation and softmax happen in higher precision (or at least with a single common scale applied to the accumulators). In short, you either rescale (via multiplying accumulators) to a common dynamic range before computing softmax, or you dequantize to $FP16/FP32$ and compute attention scores in floating point. Doing the softmax in higher precision after dequantization is the more robust and commonly used choice.

Practical note
If you want to maximize integer throughput, you can keep matmuls in low-precision ($int8 \rightarrow int32$ accumulators) and postpone floating conversions to after accumulation, but you must account for per-token scales consistently (multiply the accumulator by $scale_Q \cdot scale_K$). For simplicity and numerical safety—especially for softmax and normalization—dequantizing to $FP16/FP32$ before attention is the easiest and safest route.

Summary: $Q_b = 127$ for signed $int8$; per-token symmetric scales must be reconciled before cross-token operations, and the standard solution is to fold scales into accumulators or dequantize and compute attention/softmax in higher precision.
