\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits}

### Where do multiplications remain in BitNet b1.58, and what is the exact compute pathway for a ternary matmul?
Question:
The paper says 'almost no multiplication operations' and that most compute becomes INT8 additions. For a linear layer \(y = \widetilde{W}x\) with \(\widetilde{W} \in \{-1,0,1\}^{m\times n}\), is the computation implemented as conditional adds/subtracts of \(x\), plus a final rescale by \(\gamma\)? If weights are encoded using a 2-bit kernel from Ladder, how is the multiply-by-\(-1\) implemented efficiently? Where precisely do any remaining multiplications occur (e.g., scaling by \(\gamma\), activation rescaling, layernorm, softmax), and in what precision?
Answer:
Short answer: the vast majority of per-layer multiply operations disappear inside the ternary matmul and are replaced by integer additions and a few cheap integer subtractions/bit-ops. The remaining true multiplications are the small per-matrix (or per-block) rescale(s) introduced by the quantization (the $\gamma$), and the usual FP math in non-linear/normalization primitives (RMSNorm, SwiGLU internal FP ops, softmax). In practice those scalar rescalings are done in the model floating precision (FP16/BF16 in this paper) while the ternary matmul itself is implemented with integer accumulation (INT32/INT8 pipeline). Below I explain the exact compute pathway and how “multiply by $-1$” is implemented efficiently.

How a ternary linear is computed (intuitive + exact pathway)
For a linear layer with quantized weight matrix $\widetilde W\in\{-1,0,1\}^{m\times n}$ and input vector $x$, the product
$$ y=\widetilde W x $$
can be seen elementwise as adding or subtracting selected entries of $x$ (or skipping them). Concretely you can split $\widetilde W$ into two binary mask matrices $P$ and $N$ where $P_{ij}=1$ iff $\widetilde W_{ij}=+1$ and $N_{ij}=1$ iff $\widetilde W_{ij}=-1$. Then
$$ y = (P\odot x)\mathbf{1} - (N\odot x)\mathbf{1} $$
which in implementation becomes: compute sum of selected inputs for the +1 positions, compute sum of selected inputs for the -1 positions, then subtract. No per-element FP multiply is needed.

Typical optimized kernel implementation does exactly that (or an equivalent bit-packed trick): bitpack/encode the two masks (2 bits per weight or one bit per sign and one bit for zero), use masked integer adds (or popcount-style accumulation) to compute the two partial sums, then do one integer subtraction per output entry. So the per-weight "multiplication" reduces to either an integer add (if $+1$) or an integer add that later contributes to the negative sum (if $-1$), or nothing (if $0$).

How “multiply by -1” is implemented
Multiply-by $-1$ is handled without a per-element multiply: weights with value $-1$ are routed into the negative accumulator instead of the positive one. At the output we compute positive_sum $-$ negative_sum. On typical hardware this is implemented either as (a) two masked integer accumulation loops plus one integer subtraction per output, or (b) a single accumulation where sign bits flip the add/subtract direction (e.g., conditional add/subtract using integer ALU), or (c) bitwise tricks for binary-packed kernels (xnor/popcount style for bipolar) when applicable. The Ladder 2-bit kernel integration mentioned in the paper is a packed/optimized 2-bit implementation that exposes exactly these mask and accumulate operations; it doesn’t introduce FP multiplies per weight.

Where multiplications remain and in what precision
The paper is explicit that almost all matrix multiplication work becomes INT additions. The remaining multiplications are:

1. The quantization scaling by $\gamma$ used to normalize raw weights before rounding:
   - During quantization/training this $\gamma$ is computed as $\displaystyle \gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$. That computation is done in floating precision (training uses FP16/BF16 internals or FP32 for stable statistics), and the application of the scale is a floating multiply when mapping back to real-valued outputs (see next point).
2. Dequantization / final rescale after integer accumulation:
   - After the INT accumulation (often done in INT32 to avoid overflow), the accumulated integer outputs are typically converted back to a floating representation and multiplied by the learned/global scale $\gamma$ (or a per-block scale) to restore the proper magnitude. That scalar multiply is a true FP multiply; in this paper the model training and baseline are FP16/BF16, so this rescale is done in FP16/BF16 (or often promoted to FP32 for numerical stability then cast back).
3. Activations’ per-token scaling:
   - BitNet b1.58 scales activations to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ per token (to avoid zero-points). Any small scale factor associated with that can be applied as an integer scale in inference or as an FP multiply depending on the implementation; the paper treats activations as 8-bit, so the kernel pipeline usually performs integer requantization + an eventual FP multiply when converting back for primitives that need FP inputs.
4. Nonlinearities and normalization:
   - Components such as RMSNorm, SwiGLU internal projections (if they are kept in low-bit they still require some FP ops for gating), softmax, and other normalization/attention scalings are performed in FP16/BF16 (the paper follows LLaMA-like components and retains those in floating precision). Softmax especially requires exponentials and divides (FP operations).
5. Any remaining per-layer bias terms (paper removes biases, but if present) and embedding layers:
   - The paper removes biases and leaves embeddings in full precision; embeddings and token projection layers that remain FP will involve FP multiplies.

Precision summary and typical accumulation flow
In a deployed, optimized inference kernel the sequence is usually:
- Inputs: activations quantized to INT8 (paper uses 8-bit activations).
- Weights: ternary encoded as 2-bit (packed) kernels (e.g., Ladder 2-bit kernel).
- Multiply-replace: perform integer accumulation (INT32 accumulators) by masked adds (no per-weight FP multiplies).
- Requantize / dequantize: convert INT32 accumulator to FP16/BF16 and multiply by $\gamma$ (FP16/BF16 multiply) or perform integer rescaling if the system supports it exactly.
- Downstream: pass floats to RMSNorm / softmax / SwiGLU which run in FP16/BF16.

Why energy and FLOP claims hold
Because per-weight multiplies are removed and replaced by integer add/subtract and bit-manipulation, the dominant arithmetic inside the big \emph{nn.Linear} blocks becomes integer add/subtract and a few integer subtractions at the final stage. The only remaining FP multiplies are low in count (one per output element for rescale $\gamma$, and the usual FP ops in small modules). That explains the large per-matrix energy savings claimed in the paper.

Practical notes
The exact numeric choices (accumulator width INT32, perform dequantization in FP16 vs FP32, layout of 2-bit encoding) are implementation details and the paper integrates the 2-bit Ladder kernel and FasterTransformer to realize the integer-accumulate + FP rescale pipeline. In practice many optimized kernels keep accumulation as INT32, do a single FP multiply by $\gamma$ for each output row or block (done in FP16/BF16), and keep the rest of the transformer primitives in FP16/BF16.

In short: ternary matmul = conditional integer adds (plus one integer subtraction per output), multiply-by-$-1$ is implemented as routing to negative accumulation (no per-element FP multiply), and the only remaining FP multiplies are small-count scalings (quantization/dequantization by $\gamma$) and the usual FP math in RMSNorm/softmax/SwiGLU, typically in FP16/BF16.
