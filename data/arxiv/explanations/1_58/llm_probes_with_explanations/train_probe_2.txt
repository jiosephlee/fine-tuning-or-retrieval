According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', to achieve parity with FP16 baselines, BitNet b1.58 is assumed to have the same model size and token count. This assumption is built into both the claims and the experimental design: the abstract states that BitNet b1.58 “matches the full-precision (i.e., FP16 or BF16) Transformer LLM with the same model size and training tokens,” and the Results section enforces this by pretraining both BitNet b1.58 and reproduced FP16 LLaMA models on the same RedPajama corpus for \(100\,\mathrm{B}\) tokens and comparing models of matched parameter counts (e.g., \(3\mathrm{B}\), \(3.9\mathrm{B}\), \(7\mathrm{B}\), \(13\mathrm{B}\), \(70\mathrm{B}\)); the paper further notes that performance parity in perplexity and end-task accuracy emerges “starting from a \(3\mathrm{B}\) size, when using the same configuration (e.g., model size, training tokens, etc.),” thereby explicitly tying parity to equal capacity and data budgets so that any differences are attributable to quantization rather than scaling, while the cost advantages (latency, memory, energy) come from the ternary weights and 8-bit activations rather than altered training resources.
