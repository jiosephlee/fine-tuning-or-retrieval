In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the hardware constraint cited as the link that allows energy savings to become execution speed improvements is the power limit. This follows directly from the authors’ statement that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” so when BitNet b1.58 replaces FP16 multiplications and additions with mainly INT8 additions, it greatly lowers the energy per arithmetic operation; under a fixed power budget \(P\), the achievable operation rate satisfies \(\text{ops/s} = P / E_{\text{op}}\), hence reducing \(E_{\text{op}}\) (energy per op) translates linearly into higher throughput and lower latency, which explains why the measured speedups grow with model size as more of the workload becomes power-limited matrix multiplication.
