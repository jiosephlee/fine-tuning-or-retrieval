According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the fundamental hardware constraint that links reduced energy consumption to the possibility of faster compute throughput is power. The paper explicitly notes that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” and shows that BitNet b1.58 replaces FP16 multiply-accumulates with mostly INT8 additions and drastically cuts DRAM-to-SRAM traffic by shrinking weights to ternary values, leading to much lower energy per arithmetic and memory operation; under a fixed power budget \(P\), the attainable throughput scales as \(\text{throughput} \approx P / E_{\text{op}}\), so when the energy per operation \(E_{\text{op}}\) drops (e.g., by 71.4× for matrix multiplications on 7nm, per the paper’s energy model), the system can execute proportionally more operations per unit time without exceeding the same power limit, yielding higher latency/throughput gains; additionally, reducing memory bandwidth energy further relaxes the same power ceiling, which is why b1.58’s energy savings translate into faster inference on power-limited hardware.
