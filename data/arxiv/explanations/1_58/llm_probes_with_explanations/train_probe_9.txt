According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the experimental practice reported to ensure a fair comparison between BitNet b1.58 and the FP16 LLaMA baseline is using the same pretraining data. This is sensible because the Results section explicitly states that “to ensure a fair comparison, we pre-trained the models on the RedPajama dataset for 100 billion tokens,” meaning both BitNet b1.58 and the reproduced FP16 LLaMA were trained from scratch on the same corpus with the same token budget (\(10^{11}\) tokens), which controls for data-driven performance variance; the paper further emphasizes matching other training configuration factors (e.g., model size and number of training tokens) and evaluates both models under identical zero-shot and perplexity protocols, so any observed differences in perplexity, end-task accuracy, latency, memory, and energy can be attributed to the weight precision and architectural choices rather than differences in the data distribution.
