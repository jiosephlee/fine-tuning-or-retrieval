According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in $1.58$ Bits', when optimizing a BitNet b$1.58$ accelerator, a hardware designer should deprioritize floating-point multiplication. This is sensible because BitNet b$1.58$ constrains every weight to the ternary set $\{-1,0,1\}$ via an absmean quantization $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}\big(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1\big)$ with $\gamma=\tfrac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, so matrix “multiplication” with these weights reduces to additions and simple sign/zero operations: multiplying an activation by $-1$, $0$, or $1$ amounts to negation, zeroing, or pass-through, eliminating the need for general floating-point multiply units in the dominant nn.Linear kernels; the paper explicitly notes BitNet’s matrix multiplication “only involves integer addition,” “requires almost no multiplication operations,” uses 8-bit activations, and that the majority of BitNet b$1.58$ arithmetic is INT8 addition rather than FP16 add/mul, yielding a $71.4\times$ reduction in arithmetic energy for matmul on 7nm chips; furthermore, as model size grows the fraction of cost from nn.Linear increases while full-precision components (e.g., embeddings) occupy a smaller share, so prioritizing high-throughput integer addition, ternary weight handling, and memory bandwidth over floating-point multiplication aligns with both the computation pattern and the observed latency/energy advantages.
