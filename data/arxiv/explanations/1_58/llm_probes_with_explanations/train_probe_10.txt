According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the dominant factor explaining why BitNet's relative inference speed-up improves with larger model sizes is the dominance of nn.Linear layers. This follows from two observations repeatedly emphasized in the paper: (1) inference time in Transformers is dominated by matrix multiplications in nn.Linear, whose compute and bandwidth costs scale with model width (e.g., per-token cost grows roughly as \(O(d^2)\) for the large projection matrices), and whose fraction of total runtime increases with size as fixed-cost components (like embeddings and some non-linearities) become comparatively negligible; and (2) BitNet b1.58 replaces FP16 multiply–accumulate in these nn.Linear ops with mostly INT8 additions, cutting per-op energy by \(\sim 71.4\times\) and latency substantially, so the larger the share of nn.Linear, the larger the aggregate gain. Concretely, if total latency can be approximated as \(T \approx \alpha C_{\text{linear}} + \beta C_{\text{other}}\) with \(C_{\text{linear}}\) growing superlinearly in width while \(C_{\text{other}}\) is relatively size-insensitive, then BitNet scales down \(\alpha\) by a large factor while leaving \(\beta\) nearly unchanged, yielding a speed-up \(S = \frac{\alpha C_{\text{linear}} + \beta C_{\text{other}}}{\alpha' C_{\text{linear}} + \beta C_{\text{other}}}\) that increases as \(C_{\text{linear}}\) dominates for larger models; the paper’s measurements (e.g., 70B achieving \(4.1\times\) speed-up versus smaller models) and remarks that “the time cost for nn.Linear grows with the model size” and that embeddings remain full precision (thus shrinking as a fraction of cost) directly support this mechanism.
