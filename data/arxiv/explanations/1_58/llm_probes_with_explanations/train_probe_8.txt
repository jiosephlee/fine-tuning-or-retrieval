In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the authors attribute the main cause of BitNet b1.58's increasing speed-up with larger model sizes to the computation whose time cost grows with model size, which they identify as nn.Linear. This is sensible because the dominant operation in Transformers is matrix multiplication in the linear layers, i.e., computing \(y = W x\) with \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\), which scales in time roughly with \(O(mn)\) per layer and becomes an ever-larger fraction of end-to-end latency as width/depth (and thus parameter count) increase; BitNet b1.58 targets exactly this hotspot by replacing FP16/BF16 multiply-accumulate in \(\texttt{nn.Linear}\) with ternary weights \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and primarily INT8 additions, eliminating most multiplications and drastically reducing memory bandwidth for weight fetches, so the per-layer cost shrinks both in arithmetic (71.4× lower matmul energy on 7nm per their model) and in DRAM-to-SRAM transfer. As the model scales, fixed-cost components (e.g., embeddings that remain full precision) contribute a smaller proportion of total runtime, while the proportion due to \(\texttt{nn.Linear}\) grows; because BitNet’s advantage is concentrated in \(\texttt{nn.Linear}\), the overall speed-up increases with model size, empirically reflected in their 70B model being \(4.1\times\) faster than FP16 and the trend that larger models see larger latency and memory gains.
