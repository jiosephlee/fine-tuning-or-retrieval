In the paper “The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits”, interpreting ternary weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ as actions on inputs, BitNet b$1.58$ matrix multiplications effectively reduce to add, subtract, or skip. This follows because each quantized weight $\widetilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,0,1\}$ (obtained via the absmean scaling and $\mathrm{RoundClip}$) multiplies an activation $x_j$ to yield $\widetilde{W}_{ij}x_j\in\{x_j,0,-x_j\}$, so the matrix-vector product $(\widetilde{W}x)_i=\sum_j \widetilde{W}_{ij}x_j$ becomes a sum of either adding $x_j$, subtracting $x_j$, or skipping $x_j$ when the weight is $1$, $-1$, or $0$, respectively. Because activations are quantized to a symmetric range $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ without zero-points and stored in 8 bits, these operations can be implemented as INT8 additions with cheap sign flips, eliminating FP16 multiplications; indeed, the paper explicitly notes that BitNet’s matrix multiplication “only involves integer addition,” embodying a computation paradigm with “almost no multiplication operations.” This structural property directly underlies the reported gains in latency, memory bandwidth (fewer bits moved), and energy (INT8 adds versus FP16 mul/add), and explains why ternary weights naturally correspond to add/subtract/skip actions during accumulation.
