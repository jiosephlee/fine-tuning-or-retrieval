Chapter Title: Why Eliminating Floating-Point Multiplication Drives Energy Savings in 1.58-bit LLMs

# Introduction

Large Language Models increasingly rely on massive matrix multiplications performed throughout Transformer architectures. In conventional full-precision implementations, these operations are dominated by floating-point additions and multiplications executed at high precision (e.g., FP16 or BF16). The paper “The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits” introduces BitNet b1.58, a 1.58-bit variant of BitNet whose weights are ternary and activations are 8-bit. A central insight of this work is that BitNet’s dramatic energy savings for matrix computations are primarily explained by the elimination of the expensive floating-point operation known as multiplication. This chapter develops the intuition behind that conclusion, shows how BitNet b1.58’s design enables addition-only matrix multiplication, and connects the resulting arithmetic changes to the measured trends in energy, latency, memory, and throughput reported in the paper.

# Floating-Point Multiplication as the Energy Bottleneck in LLMs

Transformer LLMs built with full-precision layers are dominated by matrix multiplications inside linear projections. In conventional models (e.g., LLaMA variants in FP16), the bulk of the computation cost comes from floating-point addition and especially floating-point multiplication. While both additions and multiplications occur in matrix multiplication, the multiplication operations are a major contributor to energy consumption at the chip level. The paper emphasizes that “the major computation cost comes from the floating-point addition and multiplication operations,” and contrasts this with BitNet’s implementation where matrix multiplication “only involves integer addition.”

The distinction matters because energy is the fundamental limiter of compute performance in many chips. When arithmetic operations require more energy, they constrain throughput and increase latency. The paper reports that for full-precision baselines, matrix multiplication consists of both FP16 addition and FP16 multiplication, whereas in BitNet b1.58 the vast majority of arithmetic is INT8 addition. According to the energy model cited in the paper, this shift results in a 71.4 times reduction in arithmetic operations energy consumption for matrix multiplication on 7nm chips. Eliminating floating-point multiplications removes the single most expensive arithmetic operation in the stack, thereby dominating the observed energy savings.

# BitNet b1.58’s Computation Paradigm: Addition-Only Matrix Multiplication

BitNet b1.58 replaces standard linear layers with BitLinear layers and constrains every parameter (weight) to be ternary. Specifically, weights take values in the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and activations are quantized to 8 bits. With ternary weights and integer activations, matrix multiplications can be implemented using integer additions and negligible multiplications. In essence, the contribution of a weight to a product is either to add, subtract, or cancel the corresponding activation element. This transformation collapses multiplication into signed addition and zeroing operations, enabling the model to avoid expensive floating-point multiplication altogether during the bulk of computation.

The paper characterizes this as “almost no multiplication operations for matrix multiplication,” emphasizing a new computation paradigm that is “highly optimized.” Because the computations are integer and addition-based, they are vastly cheaper in energy compared to FP16 arithmetic. This change in the arithmetic profile directly explains the dramatic energy savings, faster inference, and reduced memory bandwidth demands observed in BitNet b1.58 across model sizes.

# Quantization Enabling Ternary Weights

BitNet b1.58 achieves ternary weights via an absmean quantization function that scales and rounds full-precision weights to the nearest integer in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. The procedure is:

\[
\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right),
\]

where

\[
\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max\left(a, \min\left(b, \mathrm{round}(x)\right)\right),
\]

and

\[
\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\]

This scaling by $\gamma$ (the average absolute value of the weight matrix) normalizes the weights so that rounding to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ preserves as much of the structure as possible. The inclusion of $0$ explicitly supports feature filtering, improving modeling capacity relative to purely binary $\{-1, 1\}$ weights. With activations quantized to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ per token and weights constrained to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, the resulting matrix multiplication reduces to sign-controlled integer addition and zeroing, eliminating the need for floating-point multiplications in the core compute.

This quantization scheme is central to realizing the addition-based arithmetic profile. By construction, when $\widetilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1, 0, 1\}$, the product $\widetilde{W}_{ij}\cdot a_j$ is either $a_j$, $-a_j$, or $0$, so summing across $j$ becomes an accumulation of integer values with signs, rather than a sequence of floating-point multiplications.

# Energy Implications and Scaling Trends

The paper presents several empirical and modeled results that connect the arithmetic change to energy, latency, and throughput:

1. Energy composition: In BitNet b1.58, “the majority … is INT8 addition calculation,” whereas the FP16 baseline “consists of both FP16 addition and FP16 multiplication.” Under the cited energy model, BitNet b1.58 “saves 71.4 times arithmetic operations energy consumption for matrix multiplication on 7nm chips.”

2. End-to-end energy trends: As model size scales, BitNet b1.58 becomes increasingly more efficient in energy consumption compared to FP16 LLaMA baselines. The reason given is that the percentage of time and energy spent in $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ grows with model size. Since BitNet b1.58 eliminates floating-point multiplication in these layers, the relative advantage compounds with scale.

3. Latency: The paper reports that BitNet b1.58 achieves speed-ups that grow with model size, reaching 4.1 times faster than the FP16 LLaMA baseline at 70B parameters. Eliminating floating-point multiplication not only reduces energy but also alleviates power constraints that limit clock speeds and throughput, translating energy savings into latency improvements.

4. Throughput: With 70B parameters on two 80GB A100 cards, BitNet b1.58 supports up to 11 times the batch size of the FP16 baseline and yields an 8.9 times higher throughput. The arithmetic simplification reduces per-token compute cost and memory bandwidth requirements, allowing larger batch sizes within the same memory envelope.

These results consistently point back to the central change: removing floating-point multiplication from the matrix multiplication core of the Transformer shifts the arithmetic to low-energy integer additions, and this change dominates both energy savings and performance gains.

# Memory and Bandwidth Effects Reinforcing Energy Savings

While the elimination of floating-point multiplication is the primary driver of energy savings for matrix computations, BitNet b1.58’s quantized representation also reduces memory footprints and bandwidth demands. Compared to full-precision models, the paper notes that 1-bit LLMs (and by extension 1.58-bit) have much lower memory footprint from both capacity and bandwidth standpoints. This reduces the cost and time of loading weights from DRAM into on-chip memory (e.g., SRAM) and accelerates inference.

The paper highlights that transferring parameters from DRAM to accelerator memory can be expensive and that attempts to enlarge SRAM incur higher costs than DRAM. Because BitNet b1.58 uses ternary weights and 8-bit activations, it substantially compresses the data that needs to be moved. The latency benefits reported, particularly at larger scales, are intertwined with this reduction in memory traffic and its energy cost. However, these memory and bandwidth reductions reinforce—rather than replace—the dominant effect: when the arithmetic core no longer executes floating-point multiplications, the energy consumed per matrix operation plummets.

# From Arithmetic Changes to System-Level Gains

The paper repeatedly connects arithmetic-level savings to system-level improvements. Since chip-level compute is power-limited, reducing energy per operation allows higher throughput and lower latency under the same power envelope. The reported speed-ups, memory savings, and throughput improvements all align with this perspective:

- Speed-ups increase with model size because $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ layers dominate both time and energy more heavily at scale, and these are precisely the layers where floating-point multiplication is eliminated.
- Memory consumption drops significantly due to ternary weights and 8-bit activations; combined with an addition-only compute path, this reduces both compute and data movement energy.
- Throughput rises dramatically because cheaper per-token arithmetic and bandwidth allow larger batch sizes and faster execution without exceeding device limits.

The paper synthesizes these observations into a “new scaling law” for 1.58-bit LLMs, illustrating equivalences where larger BitNet b1.58 models are more efficient than much smaller FP16 baselines in latency, memory, and energy. This scaling law is materially enabled by the change in arithmetic: shifting from FP16 addition and multiplication to predominantly INT8 addition changes the core cost structure of inference.

# Conclusion

BitNet b1.58 achieves its dramatic energy savings for matrix computations primarily by eliminating floating-point multiplication from the core of Transformer inference. By constraining weights to $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and using 8-bit activations, matrix multiplication reduces to sign-controlled integer additions and zeroing operations. The paper’s energy model and empirical results show that this arithmetic transformation yields up to 71.4 times savings in matrix multiplication energy on 7nm chips, with increasing advantages at larger model scales. These energy savings translate directly into faster inference, lower memory footprints, higher throughput, and more efficient deployment across hardware configurations. At its core, the efficiency of BitNet b1.58 rests on replacing the most energy-intensive arithmetic operation in full-precision LLMs—floating-point multiplication—with cheap integer addition.
