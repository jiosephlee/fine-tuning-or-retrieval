\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### What are the "catastrophic values" at unseen positions, and can you quantify them?
Question:
The introduction mentions that untrained new position indices introduce catastrophic values and out-of-distribution issues. Do you observe exploding/vanishing attention logits or anomalous layernorm/activation statistics at long positions pre-interpolation? Any histograms or gradients showing how interpolation reduces these pathologies?
Answer:
Short answer: in this paper “catastrophic values” means out‑of‑distribution positional encodings and the downstream attention/activation patterns they induce (not numeric infinities). The authors do not show internal histograms or gradient plots in the paper; they quantify the failure modes and the improvement from LongRoPE using external task metrics (perplexity curves, passkey‑retrieval accuracy, and fine‑tuning convergence behaviour). The paper’s empirical evidence for the pathology is the large spikes in perplexity and loss / failures to converge when naive interpolation (or no interpolation) is used, and the much smoother perplexity and high retrieval accuracy after LongRoPE’s non‑uniform rescaling.

Why those “catastrophic values” happen (intuitively + a concise technical description). RoPE encodes position by rotating query/key subspaces, so for dimension $i$ the phase for token position $n$ is
$$
\phi_i(n)=n\cdot \omega_i
$$
where $\omega_i$ is the per‑dimension frequency (the paper writes the RoPE vector as $[ \cos(n\omega_0),\sin(n\omega_0),\dots]$). New, unseen positions (large $n$) are out of the distribution seen during pretraining: either the raw phases $\phi_i(n)$ are far outside the trained range, or a naive interpolation rescales them and causes many tokens to map to very similar phases. After a uniform linear interpolation by factor $\lambda$ the phase increment between adjacent tokens becomes
$$
\Delta\phi'_i=\frac{\omega_i}{\lambda}\,,
$$
so the number of tokens per full $2\pi$ rotation scales as
$$
N'_{\text{cycle},i}=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta\phi'_i}=\frac{2\pi\lambda}{\omega_i}.
$$
Large $\lambda$ (e.g. PI with a huge extension ratio) therefore “stretches” the phase space: many positions collapse into almost the same phase (aliasing / crowding). This does not blow up cos/sin numerically (they stay in $[-1,1]$), but it does change the relative phases that the model learned to rely on, which in turn makes query/key inner products $L_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j/\sqrt{d_k}$ have a distribution very different from training. The downstream effects are (a) attention patterns that are effectively random or overly diffuse (softmax inputs are out of the training distribution), (b) gradients and updates that behave poorly because the model’s implicit positional manifold has been distorted, and (c) fine‑tuning either fails to converge or needs many more steps.

What the paper measures instead of internal histograms. The authors show that naive interpolation or existing non‑uniform heuristics cause perplexity spikes and degraded passkey retrieval beyond modest extensions, and that their searched non‑uniform $\lambda_i$ and initial‑token threshold $\hat n$ significantly reduce perplexity and allow $8\times$ non‑fine‑tuned extension and progressive fine‑tuning up to 2048k. Those external metrics are the paper’s quantitative proxy for the “catastrophic values” problem. The paper does not include plots of attention‑logit histograms, layernorm mean/variance vs position, activation norms, or gradient‑norm traces.

If you want to measure/visualize these pathologies yourself (recommended diagnostics). The following statistics will expose the kinds of OOD effects discussed in the paper:

- Compute attention‑logit histograms per query position. For layer $\ell$, head $h$ and query position $i$ form the logits
  $$
  L_{ij}=\frac{q_{i}^{(\ell,h)\top} k_{j}^{(\ell,h)}}{\sqrt{d_k}}
  $$
  and plot the distribution of $L_{ij}$ across $j$ for $i$ in the trained range vs $i$ in the new (unseen) positions. Large shifts in mean/variance or multi‑modal structure indicate OOD behaviour.

- Plot softmax entropy per query:
  $$
  H(i)=-\sum_j p_{ij}\log p_{ij},\qquad p_{ij}=\mathrm{softmax}_j(L_{ij}).
  $$
  Increased entropy for far positions signals attention dispersion (crowding) while near‑zero entropy signals saturation (one token dominates).

- Track attention concentration (top‑k mass) versus position:
  $$
  M_{k}(i)=\sum_{j\in\text{top-}k(i)} p_{ij}.
  $$
  A fall in $M_k$ with position indicates attention is becoming uniformly spread.

- Measure layernorm pre‑activation statistics per token (mean and variance of the vector before LayerNorm) and activation norm $\|h^{(\ell)}_i\|$ as functions of position. Large systematic shifts indicate distributional mismatch.

- Compute per‑layer and per‑parameter gradient norms during a backward pass from a long sequence and compare for PI vs LongRoPE. Plot $\|\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}\|$ vs token‑position or step to see whether gradients vanish/spike.

- Quantify distribution shift with a divergence metric (e.g. KL or Wasserstein) between the training‑range logits histogram and the new‑position histogram.

How interpolation should (and in the paper does) reduce these effects. By choosing non‑uniform $\lambda_i$ and keeping the first $\hat n$ positions unrescaled, LongRoPE preserves higher‑importance frequencies and early token phases so that $\Delta\phi'_i$ is not uniformly tiny across all dimensions. Intuitively this keeps the Q/K relative phases in the “manifold” the model was trained on, so the distributions of $L_{ij}$, softmax entropies, and layer activation statistics stay much closer to in‑distribution. The paper’s quantitative evidence of that is the dramatically reduced perplexity spikes, stable fine‑tuning with far fewer steps, and high passkey‑retrieval accuracy up to million‑scale contexts.

Summary / practical note. The paper calls them “catastrophic values” to describe OOD positional encodings and the downstream distributional shift that makes fine‑tuning hard; it does not document internal histograms/gradient traces. If you want to prove the mechanism empirically, run the diagnostics above on a checkpoint pre‑ and post‑interpolation (PI, NTK, YaRN, LongRoPE) and you will typically see (i) a marked shift in $L_{ij}$ histograms and increased softmax entropy for naive PI at long positions, and (ii) LongRoPE restoring those distributions closer to the pretrained range — which matches the paper’s external metrics (perplexity and retrieval).
