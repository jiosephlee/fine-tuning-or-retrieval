\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Why does the paper call \(\lambda=1.0\) "direct extrapolation" in the search space?
Question:
In the search-space description, \(\lambda_i\) ranges from 1.0 to \(1.25\,s\), and 1.0 is described as "direct extrapolation". But with your unified form \(\cos\big(n/(\lambda_i\beta^i)\big)\), \(\lambda_i=1\) looks like "no scaling" (neither interpolation nor extrapolation). Shouldn’t extrapolation correspond to \(\lambda_i<1\) (i.e., increasing effective angle per step)? Is this a terminology issue, or is there a different definition of "extrapolation" being used?
Answer:
Short answer: it’s a terminology/semantics choice in the paper. The authors call $\lambda=1.0$ “direct extrapolation” because $\lambda=1$ means “use the original (pre‑trained) RoPE formula without any rescaling” and thus you are literally extrapolating that learned positional mapping to larger token indices. The paper’s “interpolation” means “compress the angles” (i.e. $\lambda>1$), not “any change away from the original” — so “direct extrapolation” = no change ($\lambda=1$).

Why that matches the math. The original RoPE rotation angle for dimension $i$ is
$$
\phi_i(n)=\frac{n}{\beta^i},
$$
and the paper’s rescaled angle is
$$
\phi'_i(n)=\frac{n}{\lambda_i\,\beta^i}.
$$
The per‑token phase increment is $\Delta\phi_i=1/(\lambda_i\beta^i)$. If $\lambda_i>1$ you reduce the per‑token increment (angles are compressed) — that is interpolation (tokens get “closer” in angle space); if $\lambda_i=1$ you keep the pretrained increment exactly (no rescaling) — that is the authors’ “direct extrapolation” of the pretrained RoPE to new, larger $n$. Formally $\lambda_i<1$ would increase per‑token increments (an “expansive” change), but the paper does not explore that regime; its search space starts at $\lambda_i=1$ and only allows $\lambda_i\ge1$.

This usage is consistent with prior work cited in the paper (e.g. YaRN/NTK discussions), where “extrapolation” often meant “leave some dimensions unscaled ($\lambda=1$) so the pretrained mapping is applied directly,” while “interpolation” meant scaling angles down to fit new positions into the pretrained range. Also note the paper sometimes writes the rescale as a factor $1/\lambda_i$ in places (so watch the notation); it’s the same operation but can look inverted depending on where they define $\hat\lambda_i=1/\lambda_i$.

So the confusion comes from two different intuitive readings of “extrapolation”: one could imagine “making angles larger per step” (which would be $\lambda<1$), but the authors use “extrapolation” to mean “apply the original (unscaled) RoPE formula to new positions” (which is $\lambda=1$).
