\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How should one switch between 8k-tuned and 2048k-tuned schedules during inference without artifacts?
Question:
You propose readjusting RoPE for short contexts and say that during inference, if the sequence length is less than 8k, you update RoPE with the short-length rescale factors. If a generation starts short and later exceeds 8k, should we switch \(\lambda_i\) mid-stream? Would that cause inconsistencies in QK dot products for already cached keys/values? What is the recommended implementation for streaming servers?
Answer:
Short answer: don’t flip the rescale schedule blindly in the middle of generation. Switching the per-dimension scale factors $\lambda_i$ while you keep previously cached keys (and their rotations) will break the $Q\cdot K$ geometry and produce artifacts. If you need to change schedules at runtime, either (a) pick the final schedule at session start and keep it fixed, or (b) make the cache rotatable (store the pre‑RoPE key projections or update cached keys in place by applying the delta rotation) so that all cached keys are represented under the same RoPE mapping that your new queries will use.

Why switching causes artifacts (intuition + maths). RoPE applies an orthogonal $2\times 2$ rotation to each complex pair of dimensions so a cached key at position $n$ typically looks like
$$
K_n^{\text{old}} = R\big(\phi^{\text{old}}_i(n)\big)\,k'_n,
$$
where $k'_n$ is the key projection before RoPE and $R(\phi)$ is the 2D rotation by angle $\phi$ (and $\phi^{\text{old}}_i(n)$ encodes the old $\lambda_i$ mapping). A newly computed query at time $t$ under a new schedule is
$$
Q_t^{\text{new}} = R\big(\phi^{\text{new}}_i(t)\big)\,q'_t.
$$
Their dot product becomes
$$
Q_t^{\text{new}}\cdot K_n^{\text{old}} = q'_t{}^{\!T}\,R\big(\phi^{\text{new}}_i(t)-\phi^{\text{old}}_i(n)\big)\,k'_n,
$$
but valid positional encoding relies on the relative phase difference being the intended function of $(t-n)$. If $\phi^{\text{new}}_i$ and $\phi^{\text{old}}_i$ come from different $\lambda$ schedules, $\phi^{\text{new}}_i(t)-\phi^{\text{old}}_i(n)$ is not the correct relative-angle and attention will interpret wrong distances — producing visible artifacts or broken generation. In short: Q and K must be in the same RoPE coordinate frame.

Practical, safe implementation choices for streaming servers. The paper says it “dynamically adjusts the corresponding RoPE rescale factors” for short vs long contexts, but it doesn’t spell out the streaming implementation. Here are safe options you can implement:

1) Fixed schedule for the session (simplest). Decide up‑front which $\{\lambda_i\}$ you will use for the whole generation session (for example the 2048k schedule if you might grow that large) and never switch. This is lowest-risk and cheapest at runtime.

2) Cache pre‑RoPE projections so you can switch on the fly. At encode time store $k'_n=W_k x_n$ (the key linear projection before applying RoPE) and store $v_n$ as usual. When attending, compute rotated keys on the fly with the currently active $\{\lambda_i\}$:
$$
K_n(\text{active}) = R\big(\phi^{\text{active}}_i(n)\big)\,k'_n.
$$
Because you stored $k'_n$ you can change $\phi$ later without recomputing $W_k x_n$. Memory cost is essentially the same as storing rotated keys; compute cost is the per-step rotation, which is cheap and vectorizable.

3) Update cached rotated keys in place via a delta rotation (if you only stored rotated keys). If you only have $K_n^{\text{old}} = R(\phi^{\text{old}}(n))k'_n$ cached, you can update it to the new frame cheaply by applying the rotation
$$
K_n^{\text{new}} = R\big(\Delta\phi_i(n)\big)\,K_n^{\text{old}},\quad \Delta\phi_i(n)=\phi^{\text{new}}_i(n)-\phi^{\text{old}}_i(n).
$$
On the 2‑D block $[x,y]$ this is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}x'\\ y'\end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix}\cos\Delta\phi & -\sin\Delta\phi\\[4pt]\sin\Delta\phi & \cos\Delta\phi\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.
$$
This still touches every cached token (cost $O(\text{seq}\times\text{head\_dim})$), but avoids re-running the projection $W_k$ or the whole model.

Notes and tradeoffs. Values $V$ are not affected by RoPE (RoPE is applied to Q and K projections), so you don’t have to touch cached Vs. If your LongRoPE schedules are per-dimension ($\lambda_i$) and position-thresholded ($\hat n$) as in the paper, the delta rotations are dimension- and position-wise; implement them vectorized over sequence and RoPE pairs to be efficient. If you need minimal latency and expect many sessions that stay short, using the short schedule fixed is fine; if you must support arbitrary growth while keeping short‑context fidelity, caching raw projections (option 2) is the most flexible production design.

Bottom line. Yes — switching $\lambda_i$ mid-stream without re-encoding/re-rotating cached keys will produce QK inconsistencies and visible artifacts. The recommended server choices are to either choose and lock a schedule at session start, or store pre‑RoPE projections (or apply a delta rotation to cached rotated keys when switching) so that all cached keys are brought into the same RoPE frame before you continue generating.
