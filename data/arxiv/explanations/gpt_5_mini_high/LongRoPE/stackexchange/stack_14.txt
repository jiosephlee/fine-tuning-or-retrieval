\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Clarifying the indicator function in Eq. (3): is it equivalent to dividing by \(\lambda_i\) for \(n\ge\hat{n}\)?
Question:
Eq. (3) defines \(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i,\hat{n})\) so that the angle is \(\cos(\mathbb{I}\cdot n/\beta^i)\), with \(\mathbb{I}=1\) for \(n<\hat{n}\) and \(\mathbb{I}=1/\lambda_i\) for \(n\ge\hat{n}\). This appears to match your earlier \(\cos\big(n/(\lambda_i\beta^i)\big)\) form. Could you confirm this is the intended behavior and that there is no additional \(\beta\) or \(\lambda\) exponent mixing (e.g., the "\(\lambda(\beta)^i\)" notation in Eq. (2))?
Answer:
Short answer: yes — Eq.(3) implements exactly that. For positions $n\ge\hat n$ the indicator multiplies the original RoPE angle by $1/\lambda_i$, so the angle becomes
$$
\frac{1}{\lambda_i}\cdot\frac{n}{\beta^i} \;=\; \frac{n}{\lambda_i\,\beta^i},
$$
and for $n<\hat n$ you keep the original angle $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$.

To make the mapping explicit: the paper's RoPE angle per dimension is $n\theta_i = n/\beta^i$. Eq.(3) writes
$$
\cos\!\Big(\mathbb{I}(\hat\lambda_i,\hat n)\cdot\frac{n}{\beta^i}\Big),
$$
with $\mathbb{I}=1$ when $n<\hat n$ and $\mathbb{I}=1/\lambda_i$ when $n\ge\hat n$. Thus algebraically it is
$$
\cos\!\begin{cases}\dfrac{n}{\beta^i}&(n<\hat n),\\[6pt]\dfrac{n}{\lambda_i\beta^i}&(n\ge\hat n).\end{cases}
$$
The slightly awkward notation in Eq.(2) — written as $\lambda(\beta)^i$ — is intended as multiplication with the per-dimension frequency term, i.e. the denominator is $\lambda\cdot\beta^i$ (or, when $\lambda$ varies by dimension, $\lambda_i\cdot\beta^i$). There is no extra exponent mixing: $\lambda$ is a multiplicative rescaling of the denominator (not an exponent of $\beta$ or vice versa).

One small source of notational confusion in the paper: during search they parameterize by $\lambda_i$ (the scale $>1$) but sometimes use a hat to denote the reciprocal; concretely the applied factor in the angle is $1/\lambda_i$. So your interpretation that Eq.(3) recovers the form $\cos\!\big(n/(\lambda_i\beta^i)\big)$ for $n\ge\hat n$ is correct.
