\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### In LongRoPE's unified RoPE formula, what exactly is being scaled: positions or frequencies?
Question:
In the paper "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens", Eq. (1) writes RoPE as \(\cos(n\theta_i), \sin(n\theta_i)\) with \(\theta_i = \theta^{-2i/d}\), and then the unified interpolation (Eq. (2)) looks like \(\cos\big(n/(\lambda (\beta)^i)\big)\) with \(\beta = \theta^{2/d}\). Later, the indicator-based Eq. (3) multiplies the angle by \(\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i,\hat{n})\) to get either \(n/\beta^i\) or \(n/(\lambda_i\beta^i)\). I’m confused about the notational consistency: are we scaling the frequencies \(\theta_i\) or the positions \(n\)? Are Eqs. (1)–(3) mathematically equivalent under \(\beta = \theta^{2/d}\), and is \(\lambda\) a per-dimension value (\(\lambda_i\)) or a global one?
Answer:
Short answer: LongRoPE is always scaling the RoPE *angle* (the argument of the sine/cosine). Because that angle is the product $n\theta_i$, you can equivalently view the change as scaling the position $n$ or scaling the frequency $\theta_i$. Equations (1)–(3) are consistent with each other under the paper's notation ($\beta=\theta^{2/d}$); the difference is whether the paper writes the multiplicative factor on $n$ or on $\theta_i$, and whether that factor is a single global scalar or a per-dimension value.

A bit of algebra from the paper makes this explicit. The paper defines
$\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}=\beta^{-i}$, so the original rotary angle is $n\theta_i=\dfrac{n}{\beta^i}$. The unified interpolation in Eq. (2)
is written as
$\cos\!\big(\dfrac{n}{\lambda\beta^i}\big)$. But
$$
\cos\!\big(\tfrac{n}{\lambda\beta^i}\big)
= \cos\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\cdot\tfrac{n}{\beta^i}\Big)
= \cos\!\big(n\cdot(\theta_i/\lambda)\big)
= \cos\!\big((n/\lambda)\cdot\theta_i\big).
$$
So dividing the angle by $\lambda$ is algebraically identical to replacing $n$ with $n/\lambda$ (scaling positions) or replacing $\theta_i$ with $\theta_i/\lambda$ (scaling frequencies). That is why the paper sometimes talks about “downscaling rotation frequencies” and sometimes about “position interpolation” — both viewpoints describe the same multiplicative change to the angle.

Eq. (3) is the position-aware, per-dimension generalization LongRoPE uses. It applies an indicator
$\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i,\hat{n})$ which equals $1$ for early tokens ($n<\hat{n}$) and $1/\lambda_i$ for later tokens ($n\ge\hat{n}$). Thus for $n\ge\hat{n}$ the angle becomes
$$
\mathbb{I}\cdot\frac{n}{\beta^i}=\frac{1}{\lambda_i}\cdot\frac{n}{\beta^i}=\frac{n}{\lambda_i\beta^i},
$$
exactly the form used in Eq. (2) but with a per-dimension $\lambda_i$ (and with an exemption for the first $\hat{n}$ tokens). Note the paper chooses to *search* the parameter $\lambda_i$ (they allow $\lambda_i\ge 1$ in the search space) and they sometimes denote the actually-applied multiplicative factor as $\hat{\lambda}_i=1/\lambda_i$ — that is the source of the apparent notation flip.

So: (1) mathematically the paper is altering the angle $n\theta_i$; you may think of that as scaling positions or frequencies interchangeably, (2) Eqs. (1)–(3) are consistent under $\beta=\theta^{2/d}$ when you interpret $\lambda$ as the divisor of the angle, and (3) $\lambda$ in the unified Eq. (2) can be a global placeholder (PI uses $\lambda=s$ globally), while LongRoPE generalizes to per-dimension $\lambda_i$ (with the early-token exception $\hat{n}$) and searches those $\lambda_i$ (subject to the monotonicity constraint $\lambda_i\le\lambda_{i+1}$).
