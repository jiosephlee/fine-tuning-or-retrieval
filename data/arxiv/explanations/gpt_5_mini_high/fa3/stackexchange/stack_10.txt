\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How are per-block FP8 scales folded into the computation at no extra cost?
Question:
With block quantization, each $\mathbf{Q}$ block of shape $B_r\times d$ has its scale $s_Q$ and each $\mathbf{K}$ block of shape $B_c\times d$ has its scale $s_K$. One can write $\mathbf{Q}_{\text{fp}} \approx s_Q\,\mathbf{Q}_8$ and $\mathbf{K}_{\text{fp}} \approx s_K\,\mathbf{K}_8$. Then $\mathbf{S}_i^{(j)} = \mathbf{Q}_i\mathbf{K}_j^\top \approx (s_Q s_K)\, (\mathbf{Q}_{8,i}\mathbf{K}_{8,j}^\top)$. How exactly is the factor $s_Q s_K$ incorporated into the row-wise softmax with the running $m_i$ and $\ell_i$ without introducing extra flops? Do you fold it as an additive shift via $\log s_Q + \log s_K$ inside log-sum-exp, or multiply the partial probabilities before the second GEMM? A short, explicit formula for the updates would be very helpful.
Answer:
Short answer: the per-block FP8 scales enter exactly as a scalar multiplier on the block of scores and are handled by the usual numerically‑stable, incremental softmax merge used in FlashAttention. Concretely you treat the block score matrix as
$$S^{(j)} = (s_{Q,i} s_{K,j}) \cdot (Q_{8,i} K_{8,j}^\top)$$
and run the same running‑max / running‑sum update from the paper. In practice FlashAttention‑3 avoids paying a new per‑element cost for that scalar either by (a) doing the tiny dequantization/scaling work when the block is loaded (fused with the producer work such as rotary embedding / the TMA load) so the consumer sees $$S^{(j)}$$ already scaled, or (b) by performing only very small per‑row scalar ops (row maxima scaling / rescale of $O$ which are negligible compared to the GEMMs) that are overlapped with other work. You cannot replace the multiplicative score factor by a simple additive log shift inside log‑sum‑exp (nor by a post‑hoc multiplication of unscaled probabilities) because softmax(exp) is non‑linear in the scores.

Explicit formulas (same structure as Alg.1 / Alg.2 in the paper, with the block scale $s := s_{Q,i} s_{K,j}$ shown explicitly). Suppose after processing blocks $0..j-1$ you have per‑row running maximum $m^{old}$ and running (unnormalized) sum $\ell^{old}$ and partial output $O^{old}$. For block $j$ compute the raw FP32 GEMM result
$$Y := Q_{8,i} K_{8,j}^\top$$
and let $s := s_{Q,i} s_{K,j}$. Then the consumer updates are exactly

$$m_{\text{block}} = \mathrm{rowmax}(s \cdot Y) = s\cdot\mathrm{rowmax}(Y)$$,

$$m = \max(m^{old},\, m_{\text{block}})$$,

$$\widetilde{P}^{(j)} = \exp\!\big(s\cdot Y - m\big)\quad(\text{rowwise})$$,

$$\ell = \exp(m^{old}-m)\,\ell^{old} \;+\; \mathrm{rowsum}(\widetilde{P}^{(j)})$$,

$$O = \exp(m^{old}-m)\,O^{old} \;+\; \widetilde{P}^{(j)}\,V_{j}$$.

At the end you produce the normalized block output and log normalizer via

$$O_i = O / \ell,\qquad L_i = m + \log(\ell).$$

These are exactly the operations in the pseudocode (lines labelled softmax_start..softmax_end and the RS‑GEMM update) with the scalar $s$ multiplying $Y$ inside the exponent/rowmax. That is the mathematically correct and stable way to include the per‑block scale.

Why not do additive log shift? Because scaling the scores by $s$ acts inside the exponent: $\exp(s\cdot x)\neq\exp(x+\log s)$ (indeed $\exp(x+\log s)=s\exp(x)$ while $\exp(sx)$ is generally different). So you cannot replace score multiplication by an additive shift to the score vector. Likewise you cannot compute softmax on the unscaled block and then just multiply those partial probabilities by $s$ and expect the same final softmax.

Why no extra cost in FlashAttention‑3? The paper’s implementation makes the scaling cheap by doing it outside the hot GEMM/softmax path: the block quantization / dequantization is fused with producer work (rotary embedding or the TMA load/transposes), so when the consumer runs the softmax/GEMM pipeline it already sees properly scaled FP32 scores (or the tiny per‑row scalar adjustments are overlapped). Thus the attention kernel does not add a large extra per‑element multiply just to account for $s_{Q} s_{K}$.

In short: you must multiply the block scores by the block scale and then run the same running‑max / running‑sum softmax updates above. FlashAttention‑3 implements that without an extra expensive pass by fusing the dequantization/scale with producer work or by overlapping the small scalar work with GEMMs, not by substituting an additive log trick or multiplying probabilities later.
