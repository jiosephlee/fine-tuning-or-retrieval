\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### What exactly is counted in the reported GPU memory numbers—weights only, or also KV cache and temporary activations?
Question:
For the runtime memory comparisons, do the numbers include: quantized weights (plus any per-tensor scales), KV caches at the stated batch size and context length, intermediate activations during prefill, and workspace buffers from FasterTransformer? Are optimizer states excluded (inference-only), and were the measurements taken at batch size 1 decode or at the maximum batch that fits?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper’s “runtime GPU memory” numbers are the total GPU memory used by the FasterTransformer inference runs reported in the paper, so they should include the quantized model storage (as represented by the $2$‑bit/ternary kernels and their small per‑tensor scales), the KV caches for the batch$\times$context used in each measurement, the temporary activation buffers and any scratch/workspace allocations FasterTransformer needs. Optimizer states are not included (these experiments are inference‑only). For the throughput experiment the authors explicitly increased batch size until the GPU memory limit with sequence length $512$, so that measurement is “maximum batch that fits”; for the other latency/memory comparisons the paper reports time per output token (the decode cost) but does not explicitly document the exact batch/sequence settings in the text, so those particular memory numbers correspond to whatever inference configuration was used with FasterTransformer (most likely a decode scenario), but the paper does not give a fine‑grained breakdown.

Why this is the correct reading: the paper says the benchmarks were measured with the FasterTransformer codebase and with a $2$‑bit kernel integrated (so the reported memory is the end‑to‑end memory allocation of that runtime). The paper also discusses KV cache memory and reduced activation precision ($8$‑bit activations, embeddings kept full precision), which shows the authors considered KV $+$ activations in their memory accounting. Because these are inference runs, optimizer/state tensors are naturally excluded. If you need an exact per‑component breakdown (weights vs. per‑tensor scales vs. KV cache vs. workspace), the paper does not publish that detailed split; the most direct way to get it is to reproduce the FasterTransformer run they used (they cite the codebases) or ask the authors for the exact measurement script/config.
