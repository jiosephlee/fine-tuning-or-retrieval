\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### How is SwiGLU implemented with 8-bit activations—do you dequantize around SiLU or keep everything integer?
Question:
In LLaMA-like MLPs, SwiGLU applies two linear projections followed by a SiLU gate and elementwise multiplication. With int8 activations, are the tensors entering SiLU upcast to FP16/BF16 for the nonlinear and then requantized, or are these kept purely in integer arithmetic? Did you observe any accuracy degradation specific to the SwiGLU gate under symmetric int8 activation quantization?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper does not explicitly say that they compute SiLU purely in integer or that they upcast to FP16/BF16 around the nonlinearity. What it does state is that activations are quantized to symmetric int8 per token (scaled to $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ to remove zero point) and that SwiGLU is used; it also reports that BitNet b1.58 matches FP16 baselines (from 3B up) and that “the majority of BitNet b1.58 is INT8 addition calculation.” The authors do not report any SwiGLU-specific accuracy drop under this symmetric int8 activation scheme, so whatever handling they used for SiLU did not cause a measurable loss at the model level.

Why this is ambiguous and what it means in practice. SwiGLU implements two linear projections and a gate: if $A=XW_a$ and $B=XW_b$ then the MLP output is
$$
Y = A \odot \mathrm{SiLU}(B),\qquad \mathrm{SiLU}(x)=x\cdot\sigma(x).
$$
When $A,B$ are stored as int8 with a scale $s$ (so the real value is $\hat v = s\cdot v_q$), evaluating $\mathrm{SiLU}(B)$ requires a nonlinear map on $\hat B$. There are two common practical strategies (both consistent with “8-bit activations” in the paper):

1) Dequantize around the nonlinearity: convert the int8 $B$ to FP16/BF16, compute $\mathrm{SiLU}(\hat B)$ in floating, then requantize the result back to int8. This is simple, low-risk for accuracy, and commonly used in PTQ/inference engineering; it does introduce some extra FP work and temporary memory overhead.

2) Integer-only SiLU: keep everything in integers by approximating the SiLU mapping with a small LUT, piecewise-linear/polynomial approximation, or integer sigmoid approximation, using the known symmetric per-token scale so the LUT/domain is small and symmetric. This preserves the full INT8 flow and maximizes runtime/energy benefits, but requires careful scale management and calibration because nonlinearities (and the subsequent elementwise gating) amplify quantization error if the approximation or scale is poor.

Which route the authors used is not specified. The paper’s decision to quantify activations to $[-Q_b,Q_b]$ per token (no zero point) is important: symmetry around zero makes integer LUTs or symmetric polynomial approximations much easier and less biased, and it reduces one common source of quantization error for gating nonlinearities. Their reported results (parity with FP16 at reasonable scales) imply their chosen solution—whether dequantize/compute/requantize or a carefully calibrated integer SiLU—worked well in practice, and they do not report SwiGLU-specific degradation.

If you are reproducing this work, the pragmatic path is to start by dequantizing around SiLU (FP16/BF16 compute for the nonlinear) because it is straightforward and preserves accuracy; then, if you need maximal INT8 efficiency for production/hardware, replace the float SiLU with a calibrated integer LUT or a small integer polynomial and verify no PPL/accuracy regression. To isolate the effect of the gate, run an ablation that quantizes only the MLP (or only the SiLU path) and compare perplexity—the paper does not provide such a microanalysis, but their overall results indicate no glaring SwiGLU failure under their symmetric int8 activation recipe.
