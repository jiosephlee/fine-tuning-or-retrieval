\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### How exactly are the \(G\) samples per question generated in GRPO, and how sensitive is performance to decoding settings?
Question:
You mention using naive nucleus sampling during exploration. What specific decoding parameters did you use for GRPO sampling (temperature, top-p, max length, frequency/presence penalties)? Did you study sensitivity to these choices or compare against beam search or tree-search decoders?
Answer:
Short answer: in the paper GRPO draws a group of independent samples from the old policy using “naive nucleus sampling”, then scores those samples with the reward model and computes group-normalized advantages. The only concrete decoding-related numbers the paper reports are the group size $G=64$ and a max generation length of $1024$; temperature, the exact top-$p$, and frequency/presence penalties are not reported, and the authors did not present a systematic ablation comparing different decoding hyperparameters or comparing sampling vs beam/tree search. They explicitly call exploration/decoding choices a direction for future work (e.g., tree-search based decoders).

How the $G$ samples are generated and used (in paper terms). For each question $q$ the algorithm samples a set $\{o_1,\dots,o_G\}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot\mid q)$ with naive nucleus (top-$p$) sampling. Each $o_i$ is scored by the reward model $r_\varphi$ producing $r_i$. For outcome supervision the paper normalizes the group rewards
$$
\widetilde r_i=\frac{r_i-\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf r)}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf r)}
$$
and sets the per-token advantage for every token in $o_i$ to $\hat A_{i,t}=\widetilde r_i$. For process supervision the reward model scores intermediate reasoning steps and the token advantage is the sum of future normalized step rewards, i.e. $\hat A_{i,t}=\sum_{index(j)\ge t}\widetilde r_i^{index(j)}$. These advantages are then plugged into the GRPO surrogate objective (the paper’s Equation (GRPO-obj)) and the policy is updated once per exploration stage. Concretely reported RL training settings include policy LR $1\times 10^{-6}$, reward-model LR $2\times 10^{-5}$, KL coefficient $0.04$, batch size $1024$, $G=64$, and max length $1024$.

Why decoding choices matter (intuition grounded in the paper). GRPO’s baseline/advantage computation is intra-group: it normalizes rewards inside each sampled group. That means the distribution of the sampled candidates in the group (their diversity and quality) directly controls the magnitude and sign of the normalized advantages. If sampling is too conservative (very low temperature / tiny top-$p$ or deterministic beam), group outputs will be similar and reward variance will be small, so normalized advantages collapse toward zero and the update signal becomes weak. If sampling is too wild (very high temperature / huge top-$p$), many low-quality samples will inflate variance and can produce noisy, possibly misleading reward signals that destabilize training and can amplify reward-model errors. Because the paper uses reward-model scores and normalizes by group mean/std, decoding affects both exploration (what behaviors the policy sees) and the stability/scale of the gradient signal. This connects to the paper’s observation that their RL improved Maj@K rather than Pass@K: the sampling distribution determines whether correct answers are pushed into the top mass of the model or are merely present among many noisy samples.

What the paper did not do (so far). The authors did not report the concrete top-$p$, temperature, or frequency/presence-penalty values and did not include an ablation of decoding hyperparameters. They also did not report comparisons to beam search, diverse-beam, or tree-search based decoders in the experiments — instead they pointed out such advanced sampling strategies (e.g., tree-search methods) and efficient inference as promising directions for future work.

Practical advice if you want to test sensitivity. Run small controlled ablations before full RL: vary temperature and top-$p$ (for example try a grid such as temperature in $\{0.3,0.7,1.0\}$ and top-$p$ in $\{0.8,0.9,0.95\}$), vary group size $G$ (e.g. $8, 16, 32, 64$) and compare sampling vs (diverse) beam search and a tree-search method. Track not only final benchmark accuracy (Maj@K, Pass@K) but also: reward-model score vs ground-truth correctness correlation, group reward mean and std, fraction of unique outputs per group, KL drift to the reference model, and training stability. Expect trade-offs: larger $G$ and more exploratory decoding increase signal diversity and reduce variance of the baseline estimate, but cost more compute and may raise noisy-label problems for the reward model; beams give high-likelihood candidates (less diversity) so can reduce training signal unless you use diverse beam variants or combine beam + sampling; tree-search (Tree-of-Thoughts / MCTS) often produces higher-quality diverse reasoning trajectories and is a promising direction to boost the exploration-quality tradeoff (the paper explicitly mentions exploring such methods).

Summary. The paper’s GRPO exploration used naive nucleus sampling with $G=64$ and max length $1024$ and did not publish the temperature/top-$p$/penalty values or a decoding-sensitivity study nor comparisons to beam/tree search. Conceptually, decoding choices should have a large effect because GRPO’s group-normalized advantages depend on sample diversity and quality; exploring this systematically (varying temperature/top-$p$/penalties, $G$, and alternative decoders) is exactly the promising follow-up the authors note.
