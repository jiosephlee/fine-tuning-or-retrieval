\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### What exactly is the per-token KL regularization in GRPO, and why is the estimator called unbiased and positive?
Question:
GRPO adds a KL penalty between the trained policy and a reference policy directly in the loss using the estimator \(\mathbb{D}_{KL}[\pi_{\theta} \Vert \pi_{ref}] = \frac{\pi_{ref}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})} - \log\frac{\pi_{ref}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})} - 1\). Under which expectation is this estimator unbiased (e.g., \(\mathbb{E}_{o_{i,t}\sim \pi_{\theta}}\))? How is it aggregated across tokens and samples? The expression is always nonnegative because \(x - \log x - 1 \ge 0\) for \(x>0\), but could you clarify in what precise sense it is unbiased for the true KL?
Answer:
Short answer: the quantity GRPO writes down is a per-token Monte‑Carlo estimator of the conditional KL $D_{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid \text{context})\Vert \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid \text{context})\big)$. It is unbiased when the random next token is drawn from the policy whose KL you want to measure (i.e. $o_{i,t}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{i,<t})$). The paper computes that estimator per token and then averages it across tokens, outputs and questions; because the estimator is the function $g(x)=x-\log x-1$ with $x=\pi_{\text{ref}}/\pi_\theta$, each single-sample value is nonnegative and the expectation (under $\pi_\theta$) equals the true per‑token KL. Below I give the short algebra and then explain how GRPO aggregates it and the practical caveats about sampling from $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$.

Algebraic identity (why the estimator is unbiased). Fix a context $(q,o_{<t})$ and write $x(o)=\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)/\pi_\theta(o)$ for short. The estimator used in the paper is $g(x)=x-\log x-1$. Taking expectation over the next token sampled from the current policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{o\sim\pi_\theta}\big[g(x(o))\big]
&= \mathbb{E}_{o\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)}{\pi_\theta(o)}\right]
- \mathbb{E}_{o\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[\log\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)}{\pi_\theta(o)}\right] - 1 \\
&= \sum_o \pi_{\text{ref}}(o) \;-\; \big(-D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot)\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot))\big) \;-\; 1 \\
&= D_{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\big).
\end{aligned}
$$
The first term equals 1 because $\sum_o \pi_{\text{ref}}(o)=1$, and the second term is the negative of the usual KL definition, so everything collapses to the per‑token KL. This proves that $g(x)$ is an unbiased Monte‑Carlo estimator of the conditional (per‑token) KL when the token is drawn from $\pi_\theta$.

Why it is nonnegative per sample. The scalar function $g(x)=x-\log x-1$ satisfies $g(1)=0$ and $g''(x)=1/x^2>0$, so it is convex and has its global minimum at $x=1$. Thus $g(x)\ge 0$ for all $x>0$; so every single-sample estimate is nonnegative (and zero only when $\pi_{\text{ref}}(o)=\pi_\theta(o)$ for the sampled token).

How GRPO aggregates these per‑token estimates. In the paper the KL term appears inside the token-level sum in the GRPO objective (equation (6)). Concretely, for a sampled output $o_i = (o_{i,1},\ldots,o_{i,|o_i|})$ GRPO evaluates the estimator at each token
$$
\widehat{D}_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}
- \log\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})} - 1
$$,
and then the implementation averages these across tokens (the paper uses the factor $1/|o_i|$ inside the inner sum) and across the $G$ sampled outputs and across the batch of questions. Thus the implemented KL penalty in expectation equals the dataset/question average of the per‑token KLs (or, if you omit the $1/|o|$ normalization and sum tokens, the sum of per‑token KLs equals the full‑sequence KL). Symbolically, the expected aggregated penalty is
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q}\,\mathbb{E}_{o\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[\frac{1}{|o|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o|} D_{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\big)\right]
$$,
which is what the Monte‑Carlo averages converge to.

Practical caveat about sampling and "unbiased" in training. The unbiasedness proved above requires the random token to be drawn from the same $\pi_\theta$ whose KL you are estimating. In GRPO (as in PPO) training you sample trajectories from the current/old policy and then perform several gradient steps on the collected batch. In the algorithm the authors set $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\leftarrow\pi_\theta$ just before sampling, so at the point of data collection the per‑token $g(x)$ values are unbiased for that sampled policy. When you then do multiple optimization epochs using the same batch the policy moves and the estimator computed on the fixed samples is no longer an exact Monte‑Carlo sample from the updated policy; this is the standard PPO/importance‑sampling tradeoff and is controlled by small updates and clipping. In short: the estimator is exactly unbiased under expectation over tokens drawn from the policy you are estimating ($\mathbb{E}_{o_{i,t}\sim\pi_\theta}$), and GRPO’s data collection step is arranged so this condition holds at sampling time; during inner‑loop updates the estimate becomes an approximation but the same practical assumptions used in PPO apply.

Implication. Using $g(x)$ gives you a per‑token penalty that is (1) guaranteed nonnegative on each sample, (2) a Monte‑Carlo unbiased estimator of the conditional KL under $\pi_\theta$, and (3) straightforward to plug into a token‑wise objective and average across tokens/outputs/questions to get an unbiased estimate (at sampling time) of the expected KL regularizer that GRPO wants to impose.
