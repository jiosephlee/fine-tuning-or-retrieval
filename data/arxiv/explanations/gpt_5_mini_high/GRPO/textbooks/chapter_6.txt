\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 6: Mathematical Instruction Tuning: DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B

# SFT data formats: CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated reasoning
The supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage for DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is built around three distinct solution formats that appear throughout the instruction-tuning dataset: chain-of-thought (CoT), program-of-thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning. These three formats represent different ways of exposing a model to not just final answers but the intermediate structure of mathematical problem solving.

Chain-of-thought (CoT) presents solutions as natural-language, step-by-step reasoning. In the context of the DeepSeekMath work, CoT examples give the model explicit verbalized reasoning traces that lead from the problem statement to the final numeric or symbolic conclusion. The paper follows prior work on CoT as a format for teaching a model how to decompose multi-step problems into intermediate steps and conclusions.

Program-of-thought (PoT) provides solutions in the form of short executable programs (for example, Python snippets) that implement the logical steps of the solution. PoT focuses on expressing the reasoning process as a procedure: calculations and manipulations are encoded in code rather than natural-language steps. The DeepSeekMath SFT collection explicitly contains PoT-style examples alongside CoT examples so the model learns to express reasoning either verbally or programmatically.

Tool-integrated reasoning mixes natural-language reasoning with explicit calls to programmatic tools (for example, writing code that will be run to obtain intermediate numeric results). The DeepSeekMath authors annotated many problems in a tool-integrated format (notably GSM8K and MATH) so the model can learn how to interleave human-readable reasoning and tool use. Practically, tool-integrated examples show the model both how to structure the reasoning and how to produce code or tool calls whose execution yields part of the solution.

Together, these three SFT formats teach the model three complementary skills: verbalized multi-step reasoning (CoT), procedural/programmatic expression of reasoning (PoT), and disciplined interaction with computational tools (tool-integrated reasoning). The instruction-tuning dataset intentionally mixes these formats so the model can generalize across purely textual reasoning and program-aided reasoning scenarios.

# English sources: GSM8K, MATH (tool-integrated), MathInstruct subset, Lila-OOD
The English portion of the instruction-tuning dataset draws from several canonical math problem collections. The SFT curation explicitly annotates GSM8K and MATH problems with tool-integrated solutions. In addition, the authors include a subset of MathInstruct and the training set of Lila-OOD; problems from these sources are represented with either CoT or PoT solutions. Collectively these English sources cover a spectrum of difficulty and mathematical domains.

GSM8K provides grade-school quantitative reasoning problems that are well suited to CoT-style teaching because many solutions require a handful of arithmetic and algebraic steps. MATH contributes competition-level problems that demand longer, more intricate reasoning chains; for these the dataset includes tool-integrated solutions so models learn how to combine human-style reasoning with programmatic verification or computation. MathInstruct and Lila-OOD add additional breadth and varied solution styles (CoT and PoT), which helps the instruction set cover fields such as algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry, as reported by the authors.

By annotating GSM8K and MATH with tool-integrated solutions and including examples from MathInstruct and Lila-OOD in CoT/PoT formats, the English SFT collection gives the model both a wide curriculum of problem types and multiple ways of expressing solutions. This mixture of source datasets and formats is a deliberate design choice in the construction of the $776{,}000$-example instruction set.

# Chinese K-12 coverage across 76 sub-topics
The instruction-tuning dataset is multilingual: alongside English material, the authors collected Chinese K–12 mathematical problems spanning $76$ sub-topics (examples include linear equations and other standard school topics). Each Chinese problem in the corpus is annotated with solutions in both chain-of-thought and tool-integrated formats.

Including Chinese K–12 material serves two purposes reported in the paper. First, it broadens the model’s exposure to mathematical expression and pedagogy in a second language, giving the model practice in solving and explaining problems phrased in Chinese. Second, the multilingual SFT collection complements the multilingual pre-training corpus and contributes to the model’s improved performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks that the paper evaluates (e.g., CMATH and Gaokao-style tasks).

The Chinese portion therefore supplies a dense, curriculum-like set of problems across many elementary and secondary school topics, with both natural-language and programmatic solution styles. This helps the model learn to produce stepwise explanations and tool-integrated solutions in Chinese as well as in English.

# Training recipe: concatenation to 4K, 500 steps, batch size 256, LR $5\times10^{-5}$
The SFT training recipe used to produce DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is concise and tightly specified. The SFT procedure starts from DeepSeekMath-Base 7B and consumes the curated instruction dataset by randomly concatenating training examples until the context window is filled. The maximum context length used during SFT is $4{,}096$ tokens; examples are concatenated at random to populate those contexts so the model sees variable sequences of problems and solutions together.

Training runs for $500$ optimization steps with a batch size of $256$ and a constant learning rate of $5\times10^{-5}$. These hyperparameters define the SFT pass that yields DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B from the base model: random concatenation into $4{,}096$-token contexts, $500$ steps of fine-tuning on the entire mixed-format $776{,}000$ example set, batch size $256$, and learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$.

The practical effect of random concatenation into long contexts is that the model repeatedly sees heterogeneous adjacent items (different problems, formats, and languages) within the same training context. The paper reports that after applying this SFT recipe—mixing CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated examples, English and Chinese—the resulting DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B displays markedly improved mathematical reasoning both without tool use and with tool use (details in the evaluation sections below).

# Evaluation without tools: strong step-by-step reasoning
One of the primary evaluation settings in the paper forbids tool use: the model must generate self-contained, step-by-step solutions in natural language and produce the final answer without invoking external computation. Under these conditions, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates strong chain-of-thought reasoning.

Concretely, on the competition-level MATH benchmark (a large, challenging dataset of contest-style problems), the paper reports that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B surpasses all open-source models and the majority of proprietary models (the authors cite Inflection-2 and Gemini Pro as examples) by at least $9\%$ absolute accuracy. The authors also state that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B beats all other $7$B instruction-tuned models and is comparable with $70$B open-source instruction-tuned models—evidence that a carefully curated, multi-format SFT set can elevate a moderate-size model’s reasoning performance to the level of much larger models.

Although DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is very competitive, the paper is explicit that it still underperforms the strongest closed models: GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra remain ahead on the no-tools chain-of-thought evaluation. Nevertheless, the SFT results demonstrate that instruction tuning with CoT-style data produces a very capable step-by-step mathematical reasoner at the $7$B scale.

# Evaluation with tools: approaching $60\%$ on MATH
The second core evaluation setting permits program-based tool use: models may produce code or other tool calls as part of their solution, and the outputs of those programs are executed to obtain numeric results. In this setting—typical of program-of-thought or tool-integrated evaluation—DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B attains substantially higher accuracy on difficult problems.

The paper reports that when models are allowed to integrate natural-language reasoning with program-based tool use for problem solving, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approaches an accuracy of $60\%$ on the MATH benchmark, surpassing all existing open-source models in that tool-enabled evaluation. On other benchmarks in the paper’s suite, the instruction-tuned model is competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat $67$B—the previously leading open-source chat-capable model that is roughly ten times larger in parameter count.

These improvements are grounded in the SFT design: GSM8K and MATH examples were annotated with tool-integrated solutions, and the training corpus includes PoT examples that teach the model to write small executable programs. The SFT stage therefore directly trains the model to combine natural-language reasoning with code and tool calls, enabling the model to benefit from execution and improve accuracy on problems where raw symbolic or numeric computation is essential.

# Comparisons vs open-source 7B–70B and major closed models
DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is evaluated against a broad set of contemporary models, both open-source and closed. The paper lists closed-source baselines including GPT-4 (and GPT-4 Code Interpreter), Gemini Ultra and Gemini Pro, Inflection-2, Grok-1, Baichuan-3, and GLM-4. Open-source baselines include DeepSeek-LLM-Chat $67$B, Qwen $72$B, SeaLLM-v2 $7$B, ChatGLM3 $6$B, InternLM2-Math $20$B, Math-Shepherd-Mistral $7$B, the WizardMath series, MetaMath $70$B, ToRA $34$B, and MAmmoTH $70$B.

Against this field, the paper’s headline claims are twofold. First, DeepSeekMath-Instruct $7$B outperforms all other $7$B-level instruction-fine-tuned models and is comparable with some $70$B open-source instruction-tuned models—demonstrating that a careful combination of pre-training and a high-quality SFT corpus can shrink the gap between model sizes. Second, on the MATH benchmark under no-tool evaluation, DeepSeekMath-Instruct $7$B surpasses all open-source models and most proprietary models by at least $9\%$ absolute accuracy. In the tool-enabled evaluation mode, the model approaches $60\%$ on MATH and again leads the open-source field.

The authors do not claim parity with the very top closed models: GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra remain stronger in the evaluated settings. Still, the comparisons documented in the paper make a clear point: a curated, mixed-format instruction dataset plus targeted SFT can yield a $7$B model whose mathematical instruction-following and problem-solving ability rivals much larger open models.

# Implications: data diversity and instruction formats matter
The empirical outcomes reported for DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B carry several practical implications for constructing mathematical instruction-tuning datasets.

First, variety in instruction formats—CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated reasoning—is important. The SFT collection contains all three formats, and the resulting model performs strongly both when forced to reason purely in natural language and when permitted to write and execute programs. This dual success indicates that exposing the model to multiple modalities of solution expression helps it generalize to different problem-solving regimes.

Second, multilingual coverage matters. Including Chinese K–12 problems across $76$ sub-topics complements English sources and supports competency on Chinese benchmarks. The paper’s broader pre-training and SFT design explicitly aimed for multilingual math capability, and the evaluation suite shows improvements in both English and Chinese tasks.

Third, careful curation of canonical English datasets (GSM8K, MATH, MathInstruct, Lila-OOD) and explicit annotation in tool-integrated and PoT formats enables a model to leverage execution-based solving when appropriate. The SFT annotations of GSM8K and MATH in tool-integrated form appear directly tied to the model’s strong program-aided performance (approaching $60\%$ on MATH).

In short, the DeepSeekMath-Instruct results suggest that high-quality instruction tuning for mathematical competence is not just a matter of scale but of the right mixture of problem sources, multilingual coverage, and complementary solution formats. For practitioners building instruction-tuning corpora, the paper’s evidence favors assembling a diverse curriculum that teaches natural-language reasoning, programmatic solution expression, and disciplined tool use within the same SFT set.

# Concluding summary
DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is the supervised fine-tuned successor to DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, produced by SFT on a $776{,}000$-example dataset that mixes chain-of-thought, program-of-thought, and tool-integrated reasoning examples across English and Chinese sources. The recipe—random concatenation into $4{,}096$-token contexts, $500$ training steps, batch size $256$, and learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$—yields a model that demonstrates strong step-by-step reasoning without tools and substantially improved program-aided performance with tools (approaching $60\%$ on MATH). Across the evaluated benchmarks, DeepSeekMath-Instruct $7$B outperforms other $7$B instruction-tuned models, surpasses open-source models at larger scales, and approaches the capabilities of much larger open-source systems, while still trailing the very best closed models. The empirical story in the paper underscores a clear takeaway: carefully curated, format-diverse, and multilingual instruction data produce a compact model that is unusually capable at mathematical instruction-following and problem solving.
