\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 5: Training DeepSeekMath-Base 7B

# Initialization from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B
DeepSeekMath-Base was not trained from scratch: the authors initialize the model from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, a 7B-parameter checkpoint that had already gone through substantial code-focused pretraining. Empirically, the team found that beginning from a code-pretrained model yields better downstream mathematical reasoning than starting from a general-language model. This choice is reported as an explicit design decision in the paper: the code-pretrained starting point gave stronger final results after the mathematics-focused continual pretraining stage.

Why might a code-pretrained initialization help? The paper presents empirical evidence (in later ablations) that code training improves both program-aided mathematical reasoning and chain-of-thought reasoning without tool use. Code corpora expose the model to long, structured, precise token sequences, disciplined variable naming, stepwise logical transformations, and many examples of translating intent into executable operations. While the paper does not claim a formal proof of transfer, the observed gains motivate the pragmatic choice to initialize from a code model when the target is improved mathematical reasoning and program-of-thought behaviors.

# Data mixture: DeepSeekMath, AlgebraicStack, arXiv, GitHub code, general CC
The 500B-token continual pretraining run uses a deliberately engineered data mixture. Sampling proportions across the run are: $56\%$ from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, $4\%$ from AlgebraicStack, $10\%$ from arXiv, $20\%$ from GitHub code, and the remaining $10\%$ from general Common Crawl (both English and Chinese). The DeepSeekMath Corpus itself is a large, filtered Common Crawl extraction containing roughly $120\text{B}$ math-related tokens assembled with an iterative fastText-based retrieval pipeline, human annotation, and decontamination against held-out benchmarks. Because the training run totals $500\text{B}$ tokens, sampling $56\%$ from the DeepSeekMath Corpus implies multiple passes (re-sampling) over that corpus during training.

Each component plays a complementary role. The DeepSeekMath Corpus contributes domain-specific mathematical text and worked solutions across multiple languages (notably English and Chinese), providing the high-density mathematical material the model needs. AlgebraicStack supplies mathematical code snippets and algebraic manipulations (it is explicitly a source of mathematical code), useful for learning how to express symbolic manipulations in code-like form. GitHub code maintains and strengthens the model’s practical ability to write and reason in executable Python, which is central for program-of-thought evaluation. ArXiv provides formal scientific prose and mathematical exposition (though the paper later reports that arXiv-only pretraining did not, by itself, yield consistent gains), and the general Common Crawl samples preserve broad-language coverage and multilingual context. Importantly, the DeepSeekMath corpus underwent decontamination to avoid benchmark leakage: whole-page and substring-level filtering rules (e.g., matching substrings against evaluation benchmarks) were applied to reduce contamination of test sets such as GSM8K and MATH.

# Training settings: 500B tokens, batch size, context length, learning rate
The continual pretraining run is large: the model is trained for $500\text{B}$ tokens. The training largely follows the authors’ standard DeepSeek LLM practice: optimization with AdamW using $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, and weight decay $=0.1$, together with a multi-step learning-rate schedule. The schedule warms up to peak over $2{,}000$ steps, then decays so that the learning rate is $31.6\%$ of the peak after $80\%$ of training and $10\%$ of the peak after $90\%$ of training. For this specific $7\text{B}$ continual run the peak learning rate was set to $4.2\times 10^{-4}$. The batch-size provisioning for the run was large: an effective batch of $10\text{M}$ tokens, and the model used a $4\text{K}$-token context window. These settings are intended to be stable for long continual runs while allowing the model to absorb a dense, specialized corpus without destabilizing previously learned capabilities.

Practically, a few implementation-level choices matter: the team used efficient training tooling in other experiments (HAI-LLM) and adopted the same general optimization recipe for the $7\text{B}$ run. The combination of a conservative optimizer configuration, a multi-step decay schedule, and a high effective batch size is intended to make steady progress over hundreds of billions of tokens while avoiding catastrophic updates that would harm the already-useful code-pretrained initialization.

# CoT reasoning results on GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, MMLU-STEM, Gaokao-MathQA
Evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Base focuses on the model’s ability to produce self-contained, step-by-step reasoning using few-shot chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting. The authors evaluate the model across a set of English and Chinese mathematics benchmarks that range from elementary quantitative problems (e.g., GSM8K) to competition-level problems (MATH), and also include multiple-choice and national-exam-style problems (MMLU-STEM, Gaokao-MathQA, and Chinese CMATH).

Two headline numbers reported for the base model are: $64.2\%$ on GSM8K and $36.2\%$ on the competition-level MATH dataset (under the evaluation protocol that solicits chain-of-thought, self-contained solutions without tool use). These results place the $7\text{B}$ DeepSeekMath-Base well ahead of other open-source base models (e.g., the general Mistral $7\text{B}$ and the larger Llama-like models that had been math-trained on smaller corpora). On MATH in particular, the paper reports that DeepSeekMath-Base exceeds existing open-source base models by over an absolute $10\%$ and even outperforms Minerva (a closed-source model) despite Minerva having many more parameters.

The model’s gains are not limited to English: because the DeepSeekMath Corpus includes multilingual math content (notably Chinese), the base model shows stronger performance on Chinese mathematics benchmarks compared to prior open-source models that used English-centric math pretraining. The evaluation therefore demonstrates that concentrated, high-quality math data plus a code-informed initialization can substantially lift chain-of-thought mathematical reasoning at modest parameter scale.

# Program-of-thought with Python (math, sympy) on GSM8K and MATH
Beyond natural-language CoT, the authors evaluate DeepSeekMath-Base in a program-of-thought (PoT) setting: the model is prompted to produce a Python program that, when executed, yields the answer. The PoT prompts permit the use of standard numeric and symbolic libraries such as Python’s built-in math and the SymPy library; the evaluated output is the program’s execution result rather than the generated text. This evaluation tests both the model’s ability to decompose a problem into a correct computational procedure and to emit syntactically correct, executable code.

In this setting DeepSeekMath-Base $7\text{B}$ outperforms prior state-of-the-art open-source PoT-capable models (the paper cites Llemma $34\text{B}$ as a comparison point). That result highlights two interactions in the training design: first, initializing from a code-pretrained model preserves and strengthens the capability to emit runnable code; second, training on a large, math-focused corpus teaches the model how to translate math problems to programmatic calculation patterns (including symbolic reasoning enabled by SymPy). Evaluating by execution reduces the chance of spurious-looking but incorrect derivations; the executed-program evaluation therefore provides a robust measure of whether the model actually implemented the intended computation.

# Formal proof autoformalization on miniF2F using Isabelle and Sledgehammer
The paper also examines DeepSeekMath-Base on informal-to-formal theorem proving: the task is to convert an informal proof and statement into a formal proof in a proof assistant. The authors use the miniF2F benchmark (a compact formal-competition-style suite) and target Isabelle as the proof assistant. The workflow is few-shot prompting to produce a formal proof sketch; then Sledgehammer (an automated prover integrated with Isabelle) is used to fill in lower-level details and complete the proof.

DeepSeekMath-Base demonstrates strong few-shot autoformalization performance under this protocol. While the paper does not report a single aggregate number in the excerpt, it emphasizes that the base model’s mathematical pretraining yields meaningful gains on informal-to-formal tasks compared with baselines. The result is notable because formalization requires precise symbolic output and adherence to the strict syntax and logical steps accepted by automated provers; improvements there indicate the training mixture improved the model’s ability to generate structure-preserving mathematical content, not only informal explanations.

# General reasoning and coding: MMLU, BBH, HumanEval, MBPP
The authors evaluated the base model on several general reasoning and coding benchmarks to measure collateral effects of math-focused continual pretraining. On natural-language knowledge and reasoning, they use MMLU and BIG-Bench Hard (BBH); on coding, they use HumanEval and MBPP. The findings are consistent and important: math pretraining improved general reasoning benchmarks (MMLU and BBH) relative to the code-pretrained starting point, indicating that targeted math content can broaden general reasoning abilities beyond pure mathematics. At the same time, because the training mixture retained a large share of GitHub code tokens ($20\%$ of sampling), the model preserved its coding performance: DeepSeekMath-Base maintains performance comparable to the initial DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 on coding benchmarks.

Overall, DeepSeekMath-Base $7\text{B}$ significantly outperforms the general Mistral $7\text{B}$ across these reasoning and coding evaluations, showing that a focused continual pretraining run can lift both domain-specialized and general-purpose capabilities simultaneously when the mixture is chosen carefully.

# Key findings: surpassing Minerva 540B; cross-lingual strengths
The central takeaway of the DeepSeekMath-Base pretraining experiment is that a relatively small model ($7\text{B}$ parameters) can achieve high levels of mathematical reasoning when it is (1) initialized from a code-pretrained checkpoint, (2) trained on a large, high-quality math corpus mined from the web, and (3) exposed to a balanced mixture that preserves code fluency and general language coverage. Concretely, the base model’s $64.2\%$ on GSM8K and $36.2\%$ on MATH place it ahead of other open-source base models and, strikingly, ahead of Minerva (a closed-source model with many more parameters). The multilingual construction of the DeepSeekMath Corpus gives the model improved performance on Chinese mathematics benchmarks compared to prior English-centric efforts.

These results support two practical lessons emphasized in the paper. First, data quality and domain focus can compensate for parameter scale: careful corpus engineering (the DeepSeekMath pipeline) and targeted continual pretraining can yield large gains even with a $7\text{B}$ model. Second, code-centric pretraining is a valuable foundation for mathematical reasoning: it enables stronger program-of-thought behavior and appears to synergize with math-focused text to lift both formal and informal mathematical capabilities. For a student reading the research, this case study underlines the importance of initialization, corpus composition, and stable optimization when trying to push reasoning performance on specialized tasks.
