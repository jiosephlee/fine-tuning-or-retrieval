\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Where exactly is the byte-level language modeling loss applied in BLT?
Question:
Since the latent transformer operates on patch representations and the local decoder maps patch representations to byte distributions with a 256-sized output projection, am I correct that the next-byte cross-entropy loss is applied only at the local decoder's output? Does the global transformer ever directly predict bytes (e.g., via auxiliary losses), or is it only trained through gradients flowing back from the decoder?
Answer:
Short answer: Yes — the next-byte cross-entropy is applied at the local decoder's 256‑way output only. The global latent transformer does not emit byte logits or receive its own auxiliary next‑byte loss in BLT; it is learned indirectly by backpropagation through the decoder (except for the separate small entropy LM used only to decide patch boundaries, which is trained on its own).

Why this is the case (intuitively and technically). BLT is a hierarchical / latent design: the local encoder turns raw bytes into patch representations $p_j$, the global transformer $\mathcal{G}$ autoregressively maps those to output patch representations $o_j$, and the local decoder $\mathcal{D}$ expands the $o_j$ back into a sequence of byte logits. Because bytes are the supervised objects, the training loss is the usual next‑byte cross‑entropy computed on the decoder's softmax over the 256 byte classes. In notation this is the paper's cross‑entropy objective $\mathcal{L}_{CE}(\mathbf{x})$ (used in the BPB formula)
$$
\text{BPB}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{CE}(\mathbf{x})}{\ln(2)\cdot n_{\text{bytes}}},
$$
and $\mathcal{L}_{CE}(\mathbf{x})=-\sum_i \log p_{\mathcal{D}}(x_i\mid x_{<i},\,o_{1:\text{patchs}})$, where $p_{\mathcal{D}}$ is produced by the decoder's 256‑dim output projection (the paper explicitly accounts for this with the decoder flop term using $V=256$). The decoder cross‑attention uses linear transforms of the patch outputs (e.g. $K,V = \mathcal{D}_C(o_j)$ in the paper), so gradients from the byte CE flow into those transforms and then back through the global transformer and the encoder.

Two practical clarifications from the paper. First, the small byte LM used for entropy patching (the entropy model $p_e$ and its entropies $H(x_i)$) is trained separately to estimate where to cut patches; that model does predict bytes and has its own objective, but it is not the main BLT global transformer and is only used to decide segmentation. Second, the paper’s “BLT from Llama 3” experiment initializes the global transformer from a pretrained tokenized model and updates it slowly (smaller LR), but even there the supervision still comes from the decoder’s byte CE — there is no separate auxiliary next‑byte loss attached to the global outputs in the BLT design.

So your reading is correct: the next‑byte cross‑entropy is applied at the local decoder outputs, and the global transformer is trained only indirectly via gradients propagated from that decoder (plus any separate entropy‑model training or initialization tricks described in the paper).
