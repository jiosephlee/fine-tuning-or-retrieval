\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Is there a principled relationship between DPO’s \(\beta\) and the inference-time sampling temperature?
Question:
Both \(\beta\) in DPO and temperature at inference rescale logits in different places: \(\beta\) scales the implicit reward/log-ratio during training, while sampling temperature rescales the model logits at decode time. Is there any theoretical relationship between these two scalings? For example, if \(\beta\) is set too low or too high, can an appropriate choice of sampling temperature compensate at inference, or do they affect qualitatively different trade-offs (e.g., preference alignment vs. diversity)?
Answer:
Short answer: no — there is no general, principled one-to-one substitution of DPO’s training-scale parameter $\beta$ by the decoding temperature. They both “rescale logits” but at different places and with different consequences: $\beta$ is a training-time hyperparameter that changes the trained policy (the balance between reward and KL-to-reference), whereas the sampling temperature only reshapes the already-trained policy at decode time (changing stochasticity/diversity). Except in special, degenerate cases (e.g. a uniform reference policy) you cannot recover the effect of a different $\beta$ simply by changing temperature.

Why, mathematically. The paper shows the KL-constrained optimal policy has the Boltzmann form
$$
\pi_r(y\mid x) \;=\; \frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x,y)\Big),
$$
and DPO parameterizes the implicit reward as
$$
\hat r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\text{const}.
$$
At inference you usually apply a temperature $T$ by sampling from
$$
\pi_{T}(y\mid x)\;\propto\;\pi_\theta(y\mid x)^{1/T} \;=\; \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{T}\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\Big).
$$
If the trained model exactly equals the Boltzmann solution $\pi_\theta=\pi_r$, then applying temperature gives
$$
\pi_{T}(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)^{1/T}\,\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta T} r(x,y)\Big).
$$
This is not of the same canonical form $\propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y)\exp(r/\tilde\beta)$ unless the extra factor $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y)^{1/T-1}$ is harmless — for example if $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is uniform (or constant across the candidate outputs), in which case temperature simply rescales the effective $\beta$ by $T$ (so $\tilde\beta=\beta T$). In the general case the temperature injects an extra $ \big(\tfrac{1}{T}-1\big)\log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y)$ term, so you change more than just the reward/KL tradeoff.

Intuition / consequences. $\beta$ is part of the training objective and therefore changes which policy the optimizer converges to: it directly controls the reward vs KL trade-off (smaller $\beta$ $\to$ more reward-seeking, larger $\beta$ $\to$ more conservative w.r.t. $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$). The sampling temperature only reshapes the already-learned distribution pointwise at decode time; it cannot change the learned ordering of probabilities (for $T>0$ the map $p\mapsto p^{1/T}$ is strictly monotone so rankings and argmax are preserved). Thus if a mis-set $\beta$ caused the model to learn the “wrong” ranking of candidate completions, no choice of sampling temperature will recover the intended ranking.

Practical guidance consistent with the paper. Set and tune $\beta$ during training to achieve the desired alignment vs. deviation from the reference (or to hit a target KL frontier). After training, use decoding temperature to trade off determinism vs. diversity when sampling from the fixed policy. The paper’s experiments also note DPO is comparatively robust to sampling temperature (empirically the trained DPO policies retained good win-rates across a range of temperatures), but that robustness does not imply that temperature can replace proper choice of $\beta$.

Summary. $\beta$ and temperature are related in that both appear as “scale” parameters in exponential/softmax expressions, but they act at different stages (training vs. decoding) and are not generally interchangeable. Only in special cases (notably a flat reference $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$) does a change in temperature correspond to a simple rescaling of the effective $\beta$. Otherwise they control qualitatively different trade-offs (preference alignment / KL penalty vs. sampling diversity).
