\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### How are EOS and variable-length completions handled when computing \(\log \pi(y\mid x)\) for DPO?
Question:
When computing sequence log-likelihoods for \(y_w\) and \(y_l\), do we include the EOS token probability as part of \(\pi(y\mid x)\)? If \(y_w\) and \(y_l\) have different lengths, is there any normalization or padding strategy, or does DPO simply sum the log-probabilities over the exact tokenization of each completion? Could differences in EOS handling systematically bias the preference loss (e.g., preferring shorter sequences due to fewer EOS penalties)?
Answer:
Short answer: treat $\pi(y\mid x)$ as the ordinary sequence-level likelihood of the completion (the product of the autoregressive token probabilities) and compute $\log\pi(y\mid x)$ as the sum of the token log‑probabilities for the exact tokenization of the completion (including an EOS token if the completion is terminated by one). DPO’s loss uses those sequence log‑likelihoods directly (no extra length normalization or padding correction in the objective), so inconsistent handling of EOS or tokenization can produce systematic biases; however many length effects partially cancel because DPO always uses the ratio against the reference policy $\pi_{\rm ref}$.

Why this is the right interpretation. The paper writes the DPO objective in terms of full sequence probabilities (for example,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\rm ref})
= -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\Big[\log\sigma\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_w\mid x)}
-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_l\mid x)}\Big)\Big],
$$
)
and an autoregressive policy defines $\pi(y\mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^T \pi(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})$, so
$$
\log\pi(y\mid x)=\sum_{t=1}^T \log\pi(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}).
$$
If your completion includes an explicit end‑of‑sequence token, that token is part of $y$ and its log‑probability belongs in the sum. In batched implementations you should pad for vectorization but mask out pad positions so they do not contribute to the summed log‑prob.

Does length / EOS produce bias? It can, in principle. Because the DPO objective uses full sequence log‑likelihoods (not a per‑token average), longer sequences accumulate more log terms and usually have much smaller absolute sequence probability, so naive comparisons of raw sequence log‑probs can reflect length differences. The important mitigating factor in DPO is that the loss uses the log ratio with a reference policy $\pi_{\rm ref}$: the implicit reward is $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)}$, so any length‑dependent behavior that both $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{\rm ref}$ share will largely cancel out. The paper therefore recommends using the same reference policy that generated the data (typically the SFT model) or fitting $\pi_{\rm ref}$ on the preferred completions so that reference/model biases are aligned. If $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{\rm ref}$ have different length biases (or if your dataset was collected with a different tokenization/EOS convention than you use at training time), then DPO updates can indeed systematically prefer shorter (or longer) completions.

Practical recommendations grounded in the paper. Evaluate $\log\pi(y\mid x)$ as the sum of token log‑probs for the exact tokenization of $y$, and make sure to (a) use the same tokenizer and EOS convention for $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{\rm ref}$, (b) include the EOS token when the completion is a terminated sequence (or consistently omit it for all completions, but consistency is the key), and (c) mask pads when summing in batches. Do not apply a length normalization (e.g. average log‑prob per token) if you want to preserve the theoretical correspondence and guarantees in the paper; that would change the implicit reward parameterization $r=\beta\log(\pi/\pi_{\rm ref})$ and so break the derivation. If you observe undesired length bias in practice, diagnose it (compare average lengths of preferred vs dispreferred completions, ablate including/excluding EOS, check $\pi_{\rm ref}$ fit) and address it by fixing tokenization/EOS mismatch or by engineering the dataset (e.g. collect comparisons that control for length) or by explicitly adding a length term to the model’s implicit reward with the conscious understanding that you are changing the objective.

In short: include EOS consistently, sum the exact token log‑probs (mask pads), and rely on the $\pi_\theta/\pi_{\rm ref}$ ratio to cancel many length effects—but watch out for mismatches between model and reference or between generation and evaluation conventions, because those are the real source of systematic length/EOS bias.
