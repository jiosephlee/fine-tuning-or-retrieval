\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### Recommended initialization for exponential forget/input gates to control timescales?
Question:
With exponential gates, $i_t = e^{\tilde{i}_t}$ and optionally $f_t = e^{\tilde{f}_t}$, the effective timescale depends on the typical magnitude of $f_t$. What initialization strategy ensures stable, useful timescales at the start of training?
- For example, if we target an average forget factor $\bar{f}$ corresponding to a half-life $H$ (so $\bar{f}^H \approx 1/2$), what bias on $\tilde{f}_t$ achieves that given the input distribution and layer norm?
- Do you prefer initializing $\tilde{f}_t$ negatively so that $f_t<1$ initially, and then letting training increase it when needed?
Answer:
Short answer: pick the forget-bias so that the typical pre-activation equals the log of your desired per-step multiplier, and (because you usually use pre-LayerNorm) this is simply
$b_f \approx \ln(\bar{f})$, where for a half-life $H$ you want $\bar{f} = 2^{-1/H}$, so
$b_f = - \ln 2 / H$. In practice you should initialize $b_f$ slightly negative (so $f_t < 1$ initially); training can then push it toward $1$ or above if amplification is useful.

Why and how this follows from the paper. In xLSTM the forget gate can be exponential so $f_t = \exp(\tilde{f}_t)$ (see the sLSTM/mLSTM definitions, eqs. in the paper). Over $H$ steps a memory term is multiplied roughly by $\prod_{k=1}^H f_{t+k}$ so a first-order, “typical” decay is controlled by the sum of logs:
$E\left[\log \prod_{k=1}^H f_{t+k}\right] \approx H \cdot E[\tilde{f}]$. If you want that typical multiplicative factor after $H$ steps equals $1/2$, set $\exp(H \cdot E[\tilde{f}]) = 1/2$, hence
$E[\tilde{f}] = \ln(2^{-1/H}) = - \ln 2 / H$.
If your gate pre-activation has the affine form $\tilde{f}_t = w_f^T x_t + r_f h_{t-1} + b_f$ and the input vector is zero-mean (which is the case when you use pre-LayerNorm residual backbones as in the paper), then $E[\tilde{f}_t] \approx b_f$ and you can directly set
$b_f = - \ln 2 / H$.

Small-but-important corrections. If the pre-activation has non-negligible variance $\sigma^2$ (for example because weights produce spread around the bias), then $f_t$ is log-normal and the mean and median differ: $\operatorname{median}(f_t)=\exp(\text{mean})=\exp(b_f)$ while $E[f_t]=\exp(b_f + \sigma^2/2)$. Which should you match? the dynamics of multiplicative memory are governed by sums of $\log f$ (so the median of the product follows $\exp(H\cdot\text{mean})$), therefore matching the mean of $\tilde{f}$ (i.e. the median of $f$) is the natural choice and gives the simple formula above. If instead you specifically want the expected value $E[f_t]$ to equal $\bar{f}$, set
$b_f = \ln(\bar{f}) - \sigma^2/2$.
In practice with pre-LayerNorm and small initial weights $\sigma^2$ is small, so the uncorrected bias $b_f = - \ln 2 / H$ is a good practical default.

Input-gate and practical defaults. For the exponential input gate $i_t = \exp(\tilde{i}_t)$ you can use the same idea: choose $b_i = \ln(\text{desired per-step write strength})$. A conservative choice is to make $i$ small initially (e.g. $i \approx 0.01\text{--}0.1$), so $b_i \approx \ln(0.01\ldots0.1)$ ($\approx -4.6\ldots -2.3$). Small $i$ and slightly negative $f$ together give stable, slowly-updating memories; the xLSTM stabilizer $m_t$ (eqs. in the paper) prevents numerical overflow but does not remove the need for sensible biasing.

Should you bias $\tilde{f}$ negative at init? Yes. Initializing $\tilde{f}$ negative (so $f_t < 1$) is the safer and usually preferable choice: it produces decaying memory that avoids runaway amplification and numerical growth early in training, and lets gradient descent increase the timescale or even push $f>1$ if the model needs amplification. The paper explicitly warns that forget initialization must be chosen carefully; the formulas above give a principled way to do it.

A short worked example: if you want half-life $H = 512$ tokens then $b_f = - \ln 2 / 512 \approx -0.00135$, so $f \approx \exp(b_f) \approx 0.99865$ (very close to $1$). If your gate pre-activation variance is, say, $\sigma^2 = 0.01$, and you care about mean $f$, subtract $\sigma^2/2 \approx 0.005$ from $b_f$ (so $b_f \approx -0.00635$).

Summary: set $b_f$ so that $E[\tilde{f}] = \ln(\bar{f})$ and use $\bar{f} = 2^{-1/H}$ for a target half-life $H$. With pre-LayerNorm this is just $b_f = - \ln 2 / H$ (with optional small variance correction). Initialize $b_f$ slightly negative ($f<1$) and $b_i$ so $i$ is small; training will adjust timescales as needed.
