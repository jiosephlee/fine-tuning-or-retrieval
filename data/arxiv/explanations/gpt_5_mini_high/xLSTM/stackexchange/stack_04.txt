\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### Is the sLSTM still a "constant error carousel" after introducing normalization c_t / n_t?
Question:
Classic LSTM preserves gradients through the additive update of $c_t$ (the "constant error carousel"). For sLSTM, the hidden state is $h_t = o_t (c_t / n_t)$ with $c_t = f_t c_{t-1} + i_t z_t$ and $n_t = f_t n_{t-1} + i_t$.
Does the division by $n_t$ preserve the favorable gradient flow properties of LSTM? In particular:
1) If I backprop through $c_t/n_t$, under what conditions on $i_t$ and $f_t$ do gradients avoid vanishing or exploding across long time spans?
2) Is there an analogous "constant error carousel" interpretation when we consider both $c_t$ and $n_t$ evolving together?
A short gradient analysis (even in a linearized setting) would be very helpful.
Answer:
Short answer: sLSTM preserves the additive “carousel’’ inside the two-dimensional state $(c_t,n_t)$, so there is still an additive path for gradients (the same product-of-forget-gates factor appears). However the readout $h_t = o_t (c_t / n_t)$ multiplies that path by normalization factors (roughly $1/n_t$ or $c_t/n_t^2$), so whether gradients stay constant, vanish or explode over long spans depends on how the forgets $f_t$ and the accumulated normalizer $n_t$ scale together. In particular, with constant gates you get three clear regimes: exponential decay if $0<f<1$, polynomial $(1/t)$ decay for $f=1$ and constant $i>0$, and potentially constant (bounded) gradients if $f>1$ and numerator and denominator grow at the same exponential rate.

Derivation (linearized / gate-fixed view). Work with a scalar loss that depends on a final $h_T$ (you can insert a multiplier $dL/dh_T$ as needed). From the sLSTM forward rules

$$
c_t = f_t c_{t-1} + i_t z_t,\qquad
n_t = f_t n_{t-1} + i_t,\qquad
h_t = o_t \frac{c_t}{n_t},
$$

the direct partials at time $T$ are

$$
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial c_T} = \frac{o_T}{n_T},\qquad
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial n_T} = -\,\frac{o_T c_T}{n_T^2}.
$$

The internal additive recurrence implies (for $k<T$)

$$
\frac{\partial c_T}{\partial c_k} = \prod_{u=k+1}^T f_u,\qquad
\frac{\partial n_T}{\partial n_k} = \prod_{u=k+1}^T f_u,
$$

(and cross derivatives like $\partial c_T/\partial n_k$ are zero). By the chain rule therefore

$$
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial c_k}
= \frac{o_T}{n_T}\,\prod_{u=k+1}^T f_u,
\qquad
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial n_k}
= -\frac{o_T c_T}{n_T^2}\,\prod_{u=k+1}^T f_u.
$$

Two points follow immediately. First, the familiar product-of-forgets factor $\prod f_u$ — the hallmark of the LSTM constant-error carousel — is still present. Second, the division by $n_T$ inserts extra prefactors ($1/n_T$ or $c_T/n_T^2$) that multiply the carousel factor and can attenuate or amplify gradients depending on the scaling of $n_T$ (and $c_T$).

Simple constant-gate examples (illustrate the regimes). Take constant $f_t = f$ and $i_t = i$, and ignore $o_T$ (assume $0<o_T\le 1$ constant) and inputs for clarity. Then

$$
n_T = f^T n_0 + i\frac{1-f^T}{1-f}\quad(f\neq1),\qquad
n_T = n_0 + iT\quad(f=1).
$$

Hence for the earliest scalar memory $c_0$,

$$
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial c_0} = \frac{f^T}{n_T}\times o_T.
$$

As $T\to\infty$:

- If $0<f<1$, then $f^T\to0$ while $n_T\to i/(1-f)$ (finite) so the gradient vanishes exponentially (as in standard LSTM when forgets are $<1$).

- If $f=1$ and $i>0$, then $n_T\sim iT$ and the gradient decays only polynomially: $\partial h_T/\partial c_0 \sim O(1/T)$. Intuitively sLSTM behaves like a running average in this case, and the weight of a single past contribution is $O(1/T)$.

- If $f>1$ and $i>0$, then both numerator and denominator scale like $f^T$ and the ratio can converge to a nonzero constant (so the gradient remains bounded away from zero). In other words, exponential growth in $c_T$ is canceled by commensurate growth in $n_T$, preventing explosion and allowing a non-vanishing gradient.

Intuition and “carousel’’ interpretation. The two quantities $(c_t,n_t)$ form a two-dimensional additive memory that obeys the same multiplicative forget dynamics: $s_t := [c_t; n_t]$ satisfies $s_t = f_t s_{t-1} + i_t [z_t;1]$. That additive recurrence is the true “constant error carousel’’: gradients back to $s_k$ carry the $\prod f$ factor and therefore benefit from the LSTM trick. The extra normalization $c_t/n_t$ is a readout that rescales this carousel. So there is an analogous carousel for the internal state vector $s_t$, but the observable gradient to earlier scalar memories (and to model parameters via that readout) is multiplied by the readout Jacobian; this is why you see the extra $1/n_T$ or $c_T/n_T^2$ factors in the formulas above.

Practical implications (from the paper). Because $i_t$ and $f_t$ are input- and state-dependent and the paper allows exponential gating (so $f_t$ can exceed 1), the network can learn gate patterns that keep the effective factor $(\prod f)/n_T$ in a regime where important gradients are preserved. The stabilization trick introduced in the paper (the $m_t$ rescaling) is only a numerical device that does not change outputs or parameter derivatives (see the appendix), so it does not alter the algebraic gradient factors above. The authors also note in Limitations that forget-gate initialization matters: in practice one must choose/learn gate behavior to avoid undesired polynomial or exponential decay.

Takeaway. sLSTM keeps the additive gradient path (so it is not worse than LSTM in principle), but the normalization $c_t/n_t$ means the error seen at the hidden/output layer is a normalized summary of the two-dimensional carousel. If you want a literal constant error carousel for the scalar $h_t$ you need the combined factor $(\prod f)/n_T$ to stay $O(1)$ — achievable if gates are set so numerator and denominator grow together (e.g. $f>1$ with matching $i$) or if $f$ is tuned to keep $n_T$ bounded — otherwise you get polynomial $(1/T)$ or exponential decay as in the standard regimes above.
