\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### How is sLSTM "multi-head" mixing defined and combined—concatenation, summation, or something else?
Question:
The paper mentions that sLSTM can have multiple heads with memory mixing within each head but not across heads.
- Are the outputs of different sLSTM heads concatenated (like Transformer heads) or summed/averaged? 
- Are the recurrent matrices $R_z, R_i, R_f, R_o$ block-diagonal across heads to prohibit cross-head mixing?
- At what stage (if any) do different heads communicate—only via subsequent feedforward/residual layers?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper does not spell out a single, formal implementation detail for how sLSTM heads are merged, but it is explicit about the semantic constraint: memory mixing (the recurrent hidden–hidden interactions implemented by the R matrices) is allowed inside each head and forbidden across heads. The simplest and most natural way to implement that is to give each head its own recurrent parameters (or equivalently make the R matrices block‑diagonal) and to combine the per‑head outputs later via a linear projection over the concatenated head outputs (exactly the same recombination pattern used in multi‑head attention). In the xLSTM design heads therefore do not communicate via the recurrent/memory mixing; they only interact through the surrounding linear/pointwise layers (input projection, the up/down projections in the residual block, output projection and subsequent residual/feed‑forward layers).

Why this is the correct reading and what it means practically. The paper states that sLSTM “can have multiple heads with memory mixing within each head but not across heads.” That phrasing implies an architectural partition of the cell state and of the hidden state into head‑wise groups. If there are $H$ heads and each head has a hidden vector of size $d_h$, the global hidden state at time $t$ can be written as
$$
\mathbf{h}_t = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{h}_t^{(1)} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{h}_t^{(H)}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{H d_h}.
$$
To forbid recurrent mixing across heads you implement the per‑gate recurrent maps so they only see their head’s part of $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$ — equivalently the recurrent matrix for a gate (say $R_z$) is block‑diagonal,
$$
\mathbf{R}_z = \mathrm{diag}\!\big(\mathbf{R}_z^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{R}_z^{(H)}\big),
$$
or you store separate per‑head matrices $\mathbf{R}_z^{(k)}$ and never multiply a head’s past hidden vector by another head’s recurrent block. That enforces “no memory mixing across heads” at the recurrence level.

How head outputs are merged is not stated verbatim in the paper, but the xLSTM block structure strongly suggests the same pattern used in multi‑head attention: compute per‑head hidden/outputs independently, concatenate them to form a joint vector, and then apply a linear projection that maps back to the block’s (original) model dimension. Concretely, if each head produces $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_h}$, form the concatenation
$$
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t^{\mathrm{cat}}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t^{(1)}\\\vdots\\\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t^{(H)}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{H d_h},
$$
and then apply an output projection $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times H d_h}$ to obtain the block output $\mathbf{y}_t=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_t^{\mathrm{cat}}+\mathbf{b}$. This keeps per‑head information separate through the recurrence (no cross‑head contamination) but lets later linear layers recombine head features.

Why concatenation + linear projection is the natural choice. Concatenation preserves the full per‑head information and gives the downstream projection full flexibility to recombine signals, which is important because the paper’s goal is to let residual/FFN layers do high‑dimensional summarization (the sLSTM is used inside a post up‑projection residual block). Summation/averaging would immediately mix head signals and reduce capacity; it would also implicitly allow cross‑head interaction at the moment of summation, which would contradict the paper’s stated “no mixing across heads” at the memory/recurrent level unless the summation is performed only after all recurrence/gating is finished (which would then still be a form of late mixing — i.e., through the output projection or residual path rather than the R matrices).

Where heads can communicate. Following the paper’s description, different heads do not communicate via the recurrent/memory mixing (the R matrices) but they do communicate via (a) shared input projections (all heads typically see the same input embedding or its linear transforms), (b) the output projection / up/down projections inside the xLSTM residual block, and (c) subsequent residual/feed‑forward layers in the stacked architecture. In other words, head isolation is strictly about the internal recurrence; higher layers and linear projections are the channels for cross‑head interaction.

Practical verification. The paper provides code at the linked repository; if you need the exact implementation details (concatenation vs. sum, precise parameter shapes, whether they use explicit block‑diagonal matrices or separate per‑head matrices) check the sLSTM implementation in the repo. Conceptually: treat heads as independent recurrent submodules (separate R matrices) whose outputs are recombined by the block’s linear projections and the residual stack.
