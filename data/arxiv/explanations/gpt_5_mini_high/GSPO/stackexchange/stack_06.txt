\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Why define $s_{i,t}(\theta)$ with a stop-gradient trick in GSPO-token instead of using $s_i$ directly?
Question:
GSPO-token sets $s_{i,t}(\theta) = \operatorname{sg}[s_i(\theta)] \cdot \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\operatorname{sg}[\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})]}$, which is numerically equal to $s_i(\theta)$, but yields a gradient proportional to $s_i(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})$. Could you show the autograd steps that motivate this construction, and explain why simply multiplying $s_i(\theta)$ by per-token advantages would not produce the desired token-level gradient?
Answer:
Short answer: the stop-gradient (detach) is needed so that the sequence-level importance score $s_i(\theta)$ acts purely as a multiplicative constant for each token's likelihood-gradient, rather than itself contributing gradients that mix all tokens together. With the detaches the autograd chain gives exactly the desired per-token term $s_i(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)$. Without detaching you get extra terms coming from $\nabla_\theta s_i(\theta)$ and so per-token advantages get averaged / leaked across tokens instead of weighting each token's own log-probability gradient.

Below are the simple chain-rule steps that make this precise.

1) Gradient produced by the GSPO-token construction (with stop-grad)
Write $\bar s_i \equiv \operatorname{sg}[s_i(\theta)]$ and $\bar\pi_{i,t} \equiv \operatorname{sg}[\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})]$. Then
$$
s_{i,t}(\theta)=\bar s_i\cdot\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)}{\bar\pi_{i,t}}.
$$
Because $\bar s_i$ and $\bar\pi_{i,t}$ are treated as constants by autograd,
$$
\nabla_\theta s_{i,t}(\theta)
= \bar s_i\cdot\frac{1}{\bar\pi_{i,t}}\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)
= \bar s_i\cdot\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)}{\bar\pi_{i,t}}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot).
$$
Numerically $\bar\pi_{i,t}=\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)$ and $\bar s_i=s_i(\theta)$, so
$$
\nabla_\theta s_{i,t}(\theta)=s_i(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot).
$$
Multiplying by the per-token advantage $\widehat A_{i,t}$ and averaging over tokens gives exactly the gradient reported in the paper:
$$
\nabla_\theta\mathcal J_{\text{GSPO-token}}
=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i s_i(\theta)\cdot\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}
\widehat A_{i,t}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)\right].
$$
So the detach trick enforces that $s_i(\theta)$ is used as a multiplicative weight but does not itself contribute derivative terms.

2) What goes wrong if you use $s_i(\theta)$ without stop-grad
If you naively put the sequence weight $s_i(\theta)$ directly into a tokenized objective, e.g.
$$
\tilde J=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} s_i(\theta)\,\widehat A_{i,t}\right],
$$
then the gradient flows through $s_i(\theta)$. Using $\nabla_\theta s_i(\theta)=s_i(\theta)\cdot\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t'}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t'}\mid\cdot)$ we get
$$
\nabla_\theta\tilde J
=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i
s_i(\theta)\left(\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}\widehat A_{i,t}\right)
\cdot\left(\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t'}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t'}\mid\cdot)\right)\right].
$$
Two bad things happen: (i) each token's gradient is multiplied by the average advantage across the whole sequence $\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t\widehat A_{i,t}$, so individual token advantages $\widehat A_{i,t}$ are not applied to their own token gradients, and (ii) each token-gradient receives contributions coming from all other tokens through $\sum_{t'}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t'})$ (cross-token leakage). This is exactly the unwanted mixing the detach was designed to avoid.

3) Intuition and why the paper’s form is the right hack
We want the sequence-level importance $s_i(\theta)$ to reweight token-level likelihood gradients, but we do not want the dependence of $s_i(\theta)$ on those same token probabilities to create extra gradient paths that change the credit assignment. The stop-gradient construction yields a numerically identical importance factor (so clipping and objective values behave the same) while forcing autograd to produce the clean per-token term $s_i(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)$. In short, detach isolates the multiplicative role of $s_i$ from its derivative, preventing averaging/leakage of per-token advantages across the sequence.

This is a standard pattern when you need a quantity to act as a scalar multiplier in the forward pass but do not want gradients to flow back through it.
