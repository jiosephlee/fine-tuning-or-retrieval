\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

# GSPO: The New Game Changer in LLM Alignment

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become the engine driving the rapid scaling of large language models (LLMs), enabling them to tackle complex reasoning tasks. However, this scaling has hit a hard wall. Current state-of-the-art algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are plagued by severe instability. When training massive models, these algorithms often suffer from "catastrophic and irreversible model collapse," a phenomenon where the model's capabilities disappear permanently. Researchers have identified that this instability stems not from poor engineering, but from a fundamental flaw in how importance sampling is applied. This gap is now being filled by Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), an algorithm that stabilizes training with remarkable efficiency.

The core problem with existing methods like GRPO lies in the granularity of its optimization objective. In RL training, we generate responses from an old policy to evaluate and improve them against a new policy. To measure success, we typically compare the probability of specific tokens within those responses. However, GRPO applies importance ratios at the token level. It calculates how much more likely each individual token was under the new policy compared to the old one and weighs the gradient updates based on these micro-level probabilities. The paper argues that this approach is theoretically misapplied because importance sampling should average over many samples to correct for distributional mismatch, yet GRPO relies on single-token probabilities which introduce high-variance noise. This noise accumulates over long sequences, destabilizing training and leading to collapse.

GSPO addresses this by shifting the unit of optimization from the token to the sequence. Instead of weighing individual tokens, GSPO defines an importance ratio based on the likelihood of the entire response $y$. It uses a geometric mean to calculate this sequence-level ratio:

$s_{i}(\theta) = \left( \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_i | x) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}}$

By aligning the unit of optimization (the sequence) with the unit of reward (the entire response), GSPO eliminates the accumulation of unstable noise found in token-level approaches. The graphical equivalent might be struggling with a flickering screen caused by pixel-level interference; GSPO essentially replaces that cracked filter with one that smooths out visual artifacts across an entire image at once, resulting in a sharp and stable presentation. This shift makesGradient estimation rewards highly converged even when dealing with long-form generation tasks where token probabilities can be notoriously elusive.

The practical impact of this theoretical shift is profound, particularly regarding training stability and efficiency. Experiments conducted using GSPO demonstrate drastically improved performance compared to GRPO across standard benchmarks like AIME'24 and LiveCodeBench. Crucially, GSPO achieves superior sample efficiency; it reaches higher accuracy and benchmark scores while consuming fewer compute resources than GRPO for equivalent tasks. In another significant finding observed in the research, GSPO allows for continuous performance improvement as training time progresses without hitting diminishing returns or hitting ceiling limits associated with catastrophic collapse seen in previous algorithms.

Perhaps most notably, GSPO offers a breakthrough for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, which integrate different specialized layers within a single network architecture but only activate subsets during inference. Training MoE models under RL is notoriously difficult due to "expert-activation volatility," where different experts are activated across gradient updates even for identical prompts, rendering token-level metrics meaningless or useless guidance signals. To fix this volatility under GRPO, researchers had to employ a complex stabilization strategy called Routing Replay, which caches and reuses routing modes during training to artificially stabilize importance ratios but slows down training speeds due to increased memory overheads and communication costs. GSPO completely obviates this need by focusing only on sequence likelihood rather than individual token likelihoods, making it inherently insensitive to expert activation volatility.
