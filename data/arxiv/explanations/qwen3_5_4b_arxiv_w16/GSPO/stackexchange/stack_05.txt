Answer:
The GSPO-token variant is numerically equivalent to the original GRPO algorithm **only under the specific condition that all advantages within a single response are equalized**, i.e., $\widehat{A}_{i,t} = \widehat{A}_i$ for every token $t$ in sequence $i$.

The equivalence arises from the specific construction of the importance ratio in GSPO-token. The formula provided in the paper,
$$
s_{i,t}(\theta) = \mathrm{sg} \left[ s_{i}(\theta) \right] \cdot \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) }{ \mathrm{sg} \left[ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) \right] },
$$
divides the per-token policy probability by a "stopped gradient" version of itself ($\mathrm{sg}$ or `detach`). This operation isolates the numerical value of the ratio from its gradient flow during optimization. Consequently, since the denominator perfectly mirrors the numerator for optimization purposes but acts as a constant scalar (equal to 1.0) during backpropagation, $s_{i,t}(\theta)$ extracts to the exact numerical value of $s_i(\theta)$.

When this mechanism is combined with per-token importance ratios in the objective function (as GRPO does), and then aggregated over tokens using a uniform advantage term ($\widehat{A}_{i,t} = \widehat{A}_i$), the resulting theoretical gradient matches that of GSPO exactly. However, this numerical identity does not imply functional equivalence if advantages are not equalized. If distinct advantages ($\widehat{A}_{i,t}$) are assigned to specific tokens—as intended in scenarios like multi-turn RL or custom token weighting—GSPO-token reverts to being distinct from standard GSPO and differs from GRPO's behavior because GSPO-token applies sequence-level likelihood normalization ($s_i(\theta)$) rather than token-dependent normalization ($w_{i,t}(\theta)$). Even if GSPO-token and Standard GSPO coincide mathematically under equalized advantages, neither is functionally identical to GRPO because GSPO utilizes a geometric mean-based sequence importance ratio, whereas GRPO utilizes a direct product of individual token likelihood ratios; these two mathematical formulations yield different weighting for longer sequences despite converging to the same scalar when applied uniformly across tokens. Thus, GSPO-token serves as a flexible interface that can theoretically replicate GRPO's constraints and numerics but alters them whenever granular advantage adjustments are required.
