### In Gradient Analysis: Why do SLO weights get averaged out while GRPO weights accumulate flaw?
Question:
Looking at Equation ~15 (Gradient Analysis), there is a critical difference in how gradients are weighted between GRPO and GSPO.

In GRPO (Equation ~18), each token gradient term is multiplied by its full importance weight: $w_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, ...) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (...) }$. The paper says these weights are not negligible and can vary widely ($[0, 1+\varepsilon]$ or $[1-\\varepsilon, +\\infty)$), leading to accumulated errors.

In GSPO (Equation ~20), after deriving the gradient by differentiating the geometric mean optimality condition. The final form shows each token gradient term being multiplied effectively by 1 (or rather assigned equal treatmenet within the sum before averaging). 

Could you walk through *exactly* where this cancellation happens? If we take $W = P_\text{new}/P_\text{old}$ and look at $d/d\\theta \\log(W)^{1/L}$, doesn't mathematically become $(1/L) W \\cdot d/d\\theta W$? Where does that pesky W go?
Answer:
The apparent contradiction you are observing stems from a fundamental mathematical property of the Geometric Mean versus the Arithmetic Mean, specifically regarding how they handle products and quotients during differentiation. While your intuition that $d/d\theta \log(W)^{1/L} = \frac{1}{L} W \frac{dW}{d\theta}$ might seem correct for independent sample weights, the critical distinction lies in how $W$ (the importance ratio) is defined and how the chain rule applies when differentiating the logarithm of a product of dependent probabilities.

In GSPO, the importance weight $s_i(\theta)$ is defined as the geometric average of token-level probabilities:
$$ s_i(\theta) = \left( \prod_{t=1}^{|y_i|} w_{i,t}^{new} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}} = \exp\left( \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \log w_{i,t} \right) $$
When you differentiate this geometric mean with respect to $\theta$, you are differentiating an exponential function where the exponent is a sum of logarithms. By the chain rule:
$$ \nabla_\theta s_i(\theta) = s_i(\theta) \cdot \nabla_\theta \left( \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \log w_{i,t} \right) $$
Since $\log$ and summation are linear operations, the gradient passes through as:
$$ \nabla_\theta s_i(\theta) = s_i(\theta) \cdot \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} (\nabla_\theta w_{i,t}) / w_{i,t} $$
Note that $(\nabla_\theta w_{i,t}) / w_{i,t}$ is equivalent to $\nabla_\theta (\log w_{i,t})$. Thus, we arrive at Equation 20 in the paper (via Eq. 5 in the derived sequence):
$$ s_i(\theta) \cdot (\nabla_\theta w_{i,t}) / w_{i,t} = w_{i,t}(new)/w_{i,t}(old) * (w_{i,t}(new)/w_{i,t}(old)) * (\dots) $$
Wait, let's clarify the specific cancellation you asked about regarding $W$. If we look at the term inside the expectation in Equation 20 after differentiating:
$$ s_i(\theta) (\hat{A}_i)^T .\_g\rho_s $$
The derivation shows that for GSPO-token (Equation 23), which explicitly connects to your question about individual token gradients, we have:
$$ s[i] * [\text{sg}[W]/\text{sg}[W]] $$
where $W = P_\text{new}/P_\text{old}$ and `sg` stops gradients on its numerical value but passes it through. The term $\text{sg}[W]$ evaluates to a scalar value (often close to the training target weight), while $W$ contains the full gradient signal needed. The factor `sg[W]/sg[W]` simplifies to exactly **1** numerically because they represent identical floating-point values at that instant.
