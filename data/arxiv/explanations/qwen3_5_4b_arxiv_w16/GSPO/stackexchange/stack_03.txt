\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### What is the precise role of 'Routing Replay' in GRPO-MoE, and why does GSPO make it obsolete?
Question:
Section 5.4 discusses MoE training challenges. It states that in GRPO trained with MoE models, experts become 'activated' differently at each gradient update step because their selection depends on tokens generated by an old policy versus a new policy. To fix this noise in importance ratios ($w_{i,t}$), they use 'Routing Replay,' which caches expert activations from one step to force consistency.

The paper claims GSPO eliminates this need because it only relies on sequence likelihood ($\pi(y|x)$). I understand series species usually depend on all previous tokens.

My confusion lies in: Since MoE routing typically rejects paths if activation conditions (like gating scores) aren't met early on, wouldn't a new policy with different expert weights produce a different routing trajectory anyway? If so, how does measuring just $P(y|x)$ avoid capturing these routing fluctuations that vary between steps? isn't there still a hidden dependency on intermediate states?
Answer:
The precise role of 'Routing Replay' in GRPO-MoE is to stabilize training by artificially coupling the routing trances of the old and new policies, thereby rendering token-level importance ratios consistent. In Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, expert activation is stochastic and depends on specific routing layers; as model parameters update after a gradient step, the gating mechanism produces different expert selection patterns for identical tokens. In GRPO, this mismatch between the expert configuration used to generate the reference response (old policy) and the one used to compute the likelihood ratio (new policy) introduces massive variance into the importance weights $w_{i,t}(\theta)$. Routing Replay addresses this by caching the expert activation set from the old policy step and replaying it during the computation of gradients for the new policy, forcing $\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} | \dots)$ and $\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_{i,t} | \dots)$ to share the same active network.

GSPO obviates this strategy because it eliminates sensitivity to per-token or expert-level routing fluctuations entirely. While MoE models do exhibit volatility in which experts are activated for a given token, these internal routing variations do not necessarily alter the aggregate sequence likelihood $s_i(\theta) = (\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_i|x)})^{1/|y_i|}$ significantly enough to cause catastrophic instability. Routing Replay serves no functional purpose in GSPO because GSPO aggregates token probabilities over the entire sequence history before applying importance weighting; rather than relying on noisy, token-by-token consistency of expert paths, GSPO relies on macro-level sequence coherence where such fine-grained routing noise averages out. Consequently, GSPO computes importance ratios directly without caching activation states, avoiding both the computational overhead of replay and potential capacity constraints imposed by artificially locking onto outdated routing modes.
