\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### How does shifting from token-level clipping to sequence-level clipping address model collapse in GRPO?
Question:
The paper argues that GRPO suffers from model collapse due to high-variance noise in token-level importance weights which accumulates over response length. It claims that GSPO stabilizes this by 'performing sequence-level clipping.'

Specifically, in Equation ~10, GSPO clips the scalar $s_i(\theta)$ derived from the entire response's likelihood ratio. In contrast, GRPO clips $w_{i,t}(\theta)$ for every single token.

Can you explain mathematically why clipping at the token level is inherently unstable for long sequences while sequence-level clipping remains stable? Is it merely that noise averages out over a sequence in GSPO but not in GRPO, or does the geometric mean property of $s_i$ drastically reduce the magnitude of fluctuations compared to a direct product of potentially clipped tokens?
Answer:
The shift from token-level clipping in GRPO to sequence-level clipping in GSPO addresses model collapse by fundamentally changing how the variance of importance weights is propagated through long sequences and how it interacts with the clipping mechanism. In GRPO, the importance weight at token $t$ is a direct ratio of conditional probabilities, $w_{i,t}(\theta) = \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) / \pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})$. Because these probabilities are conditioned on increasingly specific histories as the sequence grows, the denominator can become extremely small for rare tokens in long responses, causing $w_{i,t}$ to explode. When such an exploding weight is subsequently clipped (e.g., bounded by $1+\varepsilon$), the gradient update relies on the extreme tail of the unclipped distribution. Over a sequence of length $|y|$, this leads to the accumulation of high-variance noise because each token's weight represents a distinct probabilistic event that is poorly estimated from a single sample in an off-policy setting.

Mathematically, GSPO stabilizes this by defining the importance ratio as the geometric mean of the per-token weights:
$$ s_i(\theta) = \left( \frac{\pi_\theta(y_i | x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \log w_{i,t}(\theta) \right). $$
The stability arises from two key properties inherent in this geometric mean formulation. First, exponentiation converts multiplicative fluctuations into additive ones in their logarithms; averaging over the log-likelihoods across all tokens smooths out outlier noise that would otherwise dominate if multiplied directly. Second, and crucially for stability, clipping at the geometric mean level provides a sharp threshold that acts as an early gatekeeper. If any single token in a sequence deviates sufficiently from the previous policy to cause $s_i(\theta)$ to exceed $1+\varepsilon$, the entire response is discarded before its per-token components are individually evaluated and clipped. This eliminates the "noise averaging" fallacy often assumed with multiplication; instead of hoping random noise cancels out via multiplication (which is statistically ineffective for rare events), GSPO ensures that only responses whose *entire* trajectory remains consistent with the behavior policy contribute to gradients.

By performing sequence-level clipping on $s_i(\theta)$ rather than on each $w_{i,t}(\theta)$, GSPO alters the optimization landscape: gradients are now weighted uniformly across all tokens within a valid response (as seen in Equation ~4 where all terms receive equal weighting despite differing log-likelihoods), preventing unstable large weights from disproportionately influencing learning dynamics specific to certain positions. This aligns with the principle that clamping deviating samples reduces variance more effectively than clamping underlying unstable components after they have drifted far from their expected distributions. In MoE models specifically, where expert routing changes drastically between policies lead to invalidating conditional probabilities for individual tokens (as highlighted in Section ~6), GSPO's reliance on aggregate sequence likelihood insulates training from these routing instabilities without requiring auxiliary strategies like routing replay. Thus, GSPO replaces gradient noise accumulation with geometric smoothing and strict global consistency checks to prevent irreversible model collapse during long-sequence RL training.
