Chapter 3: # Chapter 3: Prompt Engineering and Rule Extraction Strategies

In the landscape of large language model (LLM) applications for tabular learning, the ability to reliably translate unstructured reasoning into structured data transformations is paramount. Conventional approaches often treat the LLM as a black-box predictor, feeding serialized table rows directly into the model to generate a probability distribution for the target class. However, this end-to-end black-box utilization incurs prohibitive computational costs at inference time and suffers from scalability issues when dealing with high-dimensional tabular data. To overcome these limitations, frameworks like \textsf{FeatLLM} reposition the LLM as an intelligent feature engineer. This chapter delves into the sophisticated prompt engineering techniques and structured reasoning strategies employed to extract high-quality decision rules from LLMs. By meticulously designing prompts that enforce a specific reasoning trajectory—separating prior knowledge retrieval from data-specific inference—and by utilizing domain-specific priming, we can guide models to generate interpretable binary features. These features are subsequently parsed into executable Python code, enabling fast, low-latency downstream classification without requiring repeated LLM invocations for every sample. We will explore how Chain-of-Thought mechanisms are adapted for tabular contexts, how multi-step reasoning filters out spurious correlations found in noisy data, and how feature descriptions serve as critical priors to enhance rule accuracy in few-shot regimes.

# Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Adaptation for Tabular Tasks

The integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has proven to be a transformative technique in disentangling complex logical tasks from simple pattern matching. In the context of tabular learning, where the goal is not merely prediction but the discovery of underlying causal relationships between features and targets, adapting CoT requires a precise structural decomposition of the problem-solving process. Traditional CoT prompts encourage models to "think inside the hat" by generating intermediate steps before arriving at a final answer; however, when applied blindly to tabular data serialization, this can lead to noisy outputs or convergence on spurious correlations present in small datasets. The \textsf{FeatLLM} framework addresses this by explicitly引导幼儿's inference process into two distinct stages within a single prompt interaction. This adaptation transforms generic reasoning into a targeted feature extraction protocol tailored for tabular domains.

The first stage, or Step 1 of the reasoning instruction, relies entirely on prior knowledge without referencing any few-shot examples contained in the prompt's input demonstrations. During this phase, the LLM acts as an expert consultant hired only its encyclopedic training data rather than the specific (and potentially limited) training examples provided for the task. It is tasked with inferring general causal relationships or tendencies between feature types and task descriptions based solely on its pre-trained weights and external knowledge bases. For instance, if predicting chronic heart failure, Step 1 might reason about general medical knowledge linking age and blood pressure without needing to see specific patient records yet. This decoupling ensures that the model leverages robust domain knowledge rather than overfitting to rare or idiosyncratic patterns in current few-shot samples at this stage of generation. The constraints here are strict: no reference to example demonstrations $({\bf x}^i)$ is permitted during this logical deduction phase.

The second stage builds upon the findings of Step 1 but integrates concrete evidence from supported preparation steps and example demonstrations derived from previous knowledge steps. Here, the LLM synthesizes its general insights with specific instance patterns detected in the few labeled examples provided via natural language serialization ($\text{Serialize}(\mathbf{x}^i,\mathbf{y}^i,F)$). This hybrid approach allows the model to refine its abstract hypotheses derived from prior knowledge by validating them against actual data trends observed in current samples while maintaining logical consistency with general domain principles defined earlier in Step 1. By structuring instructions this way—specifically enabling causal relationship extraction followed by decisive rule derivation—the framework mitigates two common pitfalls: identifying irrelevant columns based solely on noise in tiny datasets and failing to utilize strong external priors that would otherwise be overlooked due to context window saturation or attention limitations during step-one inference alone).
