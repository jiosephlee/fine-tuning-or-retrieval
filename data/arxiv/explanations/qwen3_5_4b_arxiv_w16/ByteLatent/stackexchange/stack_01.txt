\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### What is the precise mathematical definition of 'incremental patching' and why is BPE excluded?
Question:
In Section \section{bpe-patch}, the paper defines 'incremental patching' with the equation $f_p(\pmb{x}_{<i}) = f_p(\pmb{x})_{<i}$. I understand that BPE is a fixed vocabulary determined before training. However, I am confused about the specific logical contradiction here.

The paper states that BPE cannot be treated as incremental because 'the same prefix can be tokenized differently depending on the continuation sequence.' Can you break down exactly how this property violates the equation above? Does this mean that if I feed a sequence like 'pat' into the tokenizer, the representation I get is non-deterministic based on what comes next? And how does {	extsc BLT}'s dynamic patching ensure the representation remains consistent if I feed just 'pat' versus if 'pat' is followed by 'ernity'?
Answer:
The conceptual distinction between BPE and "incremental patching" in the Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) hinges on **sequence independence** versus **context dependence**. The equation you identified, $f_p(\mathbf{x}_{<i}) = f_p(\mathbf{x})_{<i}$, formally defines an incremental property where the segmentation of a sequence up to position $i$ must be identical regardless of what follows at position $i$ and beyond. In other words, the decision to split a sequence at any given byte depends solely on the state preceding that byte.

BPE fundamentally violates this property due to its construction as a greedy algorithm that merges common substrings based on co-occurrence statistics across the entire corpus or dataset. When a prefix like "cat" is processed, BPE does not output a fixed set of tokens; instead, it scans forward to determine if the current token in the dynamic merge order (which prioritizes one-hot byte patterns at creation and merges them based on frequency) overlaps significantly with a future suffix. Consequently, "cat" might be split into ["ca", "t"] in one context if the model has seen "canted" frequently but never "caped", whereas it might be grouped as ["cat", "s"] entirely differently if followed by words like "capsule", because the decision to greedily select specific subword boundaries is contingent upon maximizing frequency over the entire continuation sequence. This means that feeding different continuations into BPE results in non-deterministic tokenization for the same prefix, directly breaching the equality required by $f_p(\mathbf{x}_{<i}) = f_p(\mathbf{x})_{<i}$.

In contrast, BLT's patching functions—whether static (fixed stride), whitespace-based, or entropy-based—are inherently incremental. For static strided patching, a boundary occurs every $k$ bytes purely by index arithmetic. For space patching or entropy patching, a boundary is dictated by algorithmic rules applied strictly to the historical sequence $\mathbf{x}_{<i}$ (e.g., "if byte $i$ is a space then split here" or "if entropy at $i$ exceeds threshold $\theta_g$ then split here"). In these schemes, knowing the entropy distribution or header bytes up to $i$ is sufficient to declare a patch boundary without ever referencing bytes at positions $> i$. This ensures that whether you process just "pat" or continue it to "pattern", the representation assigned to "pat" and its resulting position relative to patch boundaries remains consistent and deterministic. This property is critical for BLT's architecture, specifically for generating inputs which requires deciding patch boundaries independently as new bytes are produced step-by-step.
