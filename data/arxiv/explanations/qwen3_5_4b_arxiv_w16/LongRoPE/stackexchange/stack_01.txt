### What is the precise mathematical definition of the rescale factor lambda in the LongRoPE unified equation?
Question:
In Section \ref{sec:analysis}, the paper unifies Positional Interpolation (PI), NTK, and YaRN methods into Equation \ref{eq:unified_rope} using a variable $\lambda$.

The paper states that PI uses linear interpolation ($\lambda=s$) and NTK uses unequal interpolation ($\lambda=s^i$). However, later in Section \ref{sec:method}, Equation \ref{eq:problem} introduces a specific check function $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_i, \hat{n})$. This function sets the rescale factor to $1$ for initial tokens ($n < \hat{n}$) and to $1/\hat{\lambda}_i$ for subsequent tokens.

I am confused about how these relate. Is $\lambda$ in the unified equation actually representing $1/\hat{\lambda}$, or is there a consistency error in the paper where the text uses one notation ($s$ or $s^i$) while the math uses another ($1/\hat{\lambda}_i$)? Answer:

There is no inconsistency in the paper; rather, it utilizes different notations to distinguish between the theoretical interpolation factor applied during search and the effective scaling factor applied to the rotation angles during inference.
