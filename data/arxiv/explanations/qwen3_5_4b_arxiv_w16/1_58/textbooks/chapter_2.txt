The landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) has recently shifted from a domain dominated by floating-point precision, specifically FP16 or BF16, toward a paradigm of ultra-low-bit quantization. While previous research had focused on 4-bit quantization techniques to reduce memory footprints without catastrophic performance loss, the emergence of architectures like BitNet has introduced a more radical approach: compressing model parameters entirely into a ternary space consisting of the values {-1, 0, 1}. In this chapter, we will dissect the anatomy of **BitNet b1.58**, exploring why this specific ternary representation—named because it occupies 1.58 bits in binary entropy terms—is transformative. We will move beyond superficial descriptions of quantization to understand the mathematical implications of introducing a zero state into weight matrices, how this fundamentally alters matrix multiplication operations from floating-point arithmetic to integer addition, and the specific design architectures that enable this shift to match full-precision performance while drastically reducing latency and energy costs.

### Ternary vs. Binary Weights: The Significance of the Zero State

In the traditional realm of deep learning, weights are typically treated as continuous values stored in high-precision formats like FP32 or FP16. Even in 4-bit or 2-bit quantized models, the non-zero states are usually dense; for instance, a simple binary representation (±1) essentially acts as a threshold unit or a sigmoid approximation with limited capacity for representing zero signals explicitly as zero rather than negative contributions. The critical innovation of BitNet b1.58 lies in its explicit inclusion of **0** as one of the three discrete states for weights. This zero state is not merely an optimization trick but serves as a fundamental mechanism for "feature filtering." When a weight is zero during matrix multiplication ($W \times X$), that particular dimension contributes nothing to the output activation regardless of the input $X$. This is mathematically elegant and practically beneficial because it effectively turns off irrelevant channels or pathway connections without requiring complex conditional logic mechanisms found in more complex logic gates.

By allowing weights to be {-1, 0, +1}, BitNet b1.58 introduces structural sparsity at the architectural level rather than relying solely on post-hoc pruning during inference. This explicit sparsity improves modeling capability because it provides a sharper binary decision boundary compared to binary weights that can only represent positive and negative correlations ambiguously between them. In crude terms, binary weights can represent phases (positive or negative) but struggle with magnitude and silence (NULL). Ternary weights add magnitude control (strictly -1 or +1) while adding silence control (0). However, the text notes that simply adding 0 brings us to what is technically termed "1-bit" in physics terms (analogous to negative voltage), but in information theory classified at **1.58 bits** per parameter because they require $\log_2(3) \approx 1.58$ bits to store uniquely. This tiny increase in storage overhead compared to true 1-bit storage yields exponential gains in model expressivity and task performance compared to non-zero binary systems like those explored by early Shader-Transformer papers or simple sign-magnitude approximations delivering performance even below full-precision LLMs: FFH library demonstrates superior performance!
