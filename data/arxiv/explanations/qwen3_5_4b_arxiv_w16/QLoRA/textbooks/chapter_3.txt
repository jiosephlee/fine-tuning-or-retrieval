Chapter 3: # Chapter 3: Validating Performance with Precision Recovery

The primary objective of \textsc{QLoRA} is not merely to compress existing models for inference, but to enable the training of Language Models that remain comparable to 16-bit precision methods in terms of task performance. In prior work, attempts to finetune quantized models often resulted in a significant performance drop compared to their 16-bit counterparts. This chapter presents a comprehensive validation proving that \textsc{QLoRA} effectively bridges this gap. By integrating \emph{k-bit NormalFloat} (NF4) quantization with Low-rank Adapter (LoRA) training, we demonstrate that the information loss inherent in aggressive 4-bit storage can be almost entirely recovered through careful backpropagation of gradients into the adapter weights. This section details our experimental design, presenting empirical evidence that NF4-QLoRA matches full 16-bit finetuning across small and massive models, while also establishing that the choice of data type (\emph{i.e.,} NF4 versus standard FP4) plays a critical role in this recovery process.

## Experimental Setup

To rigorously validate the performance capabilities of \textsc{QLoRA}, we established a robust experimental framework designed to compare quantized parameter-efficient finetuning against both full-model finetuning and standard LoRA approaches across a spectrum of architectures and scales. Our evaluation strategy involves three distinct model architectures: encoder-only models (RoBERTa), encoder-decoder models (T5), and decoder-only models (LLaMA family). We selected LLaMA, LLaMA-2, and OPT as our primary benchmark families due to their dominance in the research community and varying hardware efficiency profiles. The scope of our comparison includes three key baselines: full 16-bit finetuning, which serves as the gold-standard upper bound for performance; standard LoRA finetuning with optimized hyperparameters but measured against full finetuning; and \textsc{QLoRA} utilizing our proposed NF4 data type.

Our primary datasets are GLUE for language modeling capabilities on RoBERTa, Super-NaturalInstructions for instruction following on T5, and Multi-Modal Language Understanding (MMLU) for benchmarking reasoning on LLaMA models finetuned on FLAN v2 or Alpaca datasets. To ensure statistical validity, we conducted hyperparameter searches where necessary. We observed that default LoRA hyperparameters are underspecified for large-scale baselines; therefore, we optimized learning rates between $10^{-6}$ and $5\cdot 10^{-5}$ and batch sizes between 8 and 128 robustly for our baseline 16-bit full-finetuned models. For instance, achieving robust baselines required balancing these parameters to avoid premature convergence or optimization instability inherent in massive models. We explicitly note that Paged Optimizers were used during training to prevent memory spikes during gradient checkpointing when processing mini-batches with long sequence lengths, particularly on 24GB and 48GB consumer GPUs where batch sizes were constrained by hardware limits rather than model size alone. These computational constraints allowed us to train more than 1,000 models across different structures and scales, providing a granular analysis of where precision recovery succeeds or partially fails under real-world resource constraints.

## Case Study: Small-Scale Parity with RoBERTa and T5

Our first line of inquiry focused on whether NF4-QLoRA could achieve parity with full finetuning even in small model regimes where resources are less constrained but automation is easier to test. We evaluated RoBERTa-large (355M parameters) and T5-small variants (approximately 800M parameters) using the GLUE dataset from the Representation Empirical Evaluation using Automatic Metrics benchmark suite, as well as Super-NaturalInstructions for instruction following tasks. The central hypothesis was that if NF4 recovers the precision loss properly during backpropagation through adapters, small architectures should exhibit identical or near-identical performance to their fully trained counterparts up to several decimal places on accuracy metrics.
