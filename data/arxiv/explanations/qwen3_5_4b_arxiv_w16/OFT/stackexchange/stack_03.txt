Answer:
The conversion of the global constraint $\|\bm{R} - \bm{I}\| \leq \epsilon$ into a local constraint on the skew-symmetric matrix $\bm{Q}$ is achieved by approximating the Cayley transform using a first-order Neumann series expansion, which is valid under the assumption that $\bm{Q}$ is sufficiently small (small norm).

The exact relationship defined by the Cayley transform is $\bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}$. When analyzing this expression, if the norm of $\bm{Q}$ (let's say under the operator norm) is small, specifically $(\|\bm{Q}\| < 1)$, the inverse term $(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}$ can be approximated.
