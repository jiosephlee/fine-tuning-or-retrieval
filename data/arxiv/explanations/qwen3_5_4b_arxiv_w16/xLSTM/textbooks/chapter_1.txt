Chapter 1: # From LSTMs to Transformers: The Design Crisis of Sequential Models

## The Triumph of LSTMs: Solving the Vanishing Gradient Problem

In the history of deep learning, few architectural breakthroughs rival the impact of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. Introduced in the early 1990s to address a fundamental flaw plaguing recurrent neural networks (RNNs), the vanishing gradient problem, LSTMs have stood the test of time as a cornerstone of sequential modeling. Before the advent of modern large-scale architectures, RNNs struggled to learn long-range dependencies; information propagated through time steps via backpropagation through time often decayed exponentially, causing gradients to vanish until no meaningful weights could be updated for historical data. This limitation rendered RNNs ineffective for tasks requiring the retention and utilization of distant context, such as language generation or sequence classification.

The LSTM successfully overcame this barrier by rethinking memory dynamics. The core insight, formalized in Equation~\eqref{eq:lstm_idea}, was to decouple the flow of information from an additive update to a cell state, denoted as $c_t$. This mechanism allows gradients to flow unimpeded through the constant error carousel structure simply by adding terms rather than multiplying them. By introducing three primary gates—the input gate $i_t$, the forget gate $f_t$, and the output gate $o_t$—the LSTM gained sophisticated control over what information enters, what is retained or discarded from memory, and what is exposed as the current hidden state output $h_t$. Since their introduction, LSTMs have dominated numerous applied domains where sequence data is paramount. They achieved并取得 remarkable success in generating natural text\citep{Graves:13,Karpathy:15blog}, rendering handwritings\citep{Graves:13}, and enabling state-of-the-art sequence-to-sequence translation systems\citep{Sutskever:14nips}. Their utility extends beyond natural language, proving their efficacy in evaluating computer programs\citep{Zaremba:14arxiv}, generating image captions\citep{Karpathy:15}, and creating source code\citep{Karpathy:15blog}. In fields outside linguistics, LSTMs excel at modeling complex physical processes, such as rainfall-runoff modeling\citep{Kratzert:18,Kratzert:19} and hydrological predictions for flood warnings\citep{Nearing:24}. In reinforcement learning, they serve as robust agents; perhaps most notably in gaming, where LSTM-based agents like AlphaStar \citep{Vinyals:17short} defeated human elites in StarCraft II and OpenAI Five \citep{OpenAI:19} dominated Dota 2 using similar reasoning encoded in their hidden states.

The enduring success of LSTMs lies not just in their ability to handle long sequences but in their capacity for abstraction. Analyses have shown that LSTM neurons behave like specialized semantic processors; they can extract number and syntax neurons \citep{Lakretz:19}, linguistic structures \citep{Bau:19}, and even sentiment indicators \citep{Radford:17arxiv}. These capabilities positioned LSTMs as a formidable competitor before 2017. However, despite their versatility across diverse application domains, a fundamental architectural limitation emerged—one that would catalyze a paradigm shift toward Transformer-based models that would ultimately outpace LSTMs when scaling parameters to billions.

## The Architecture of LSTM: Gating Mechanisms and the Constant Error Carousel

To understand why LSTMs succeeded where standard RNNs failed, one must examine their internal mechanics closely. The architecture relies on three coupled equations defining the hidden state $h_t$, the cell state update $c_t$, and several gate activations based on current inputs $\mathbf{x}_t$ and previous hidden states $h_{t-1}$. As depicted in Equation~\eqref{eq:lstm_idea}, the memory update rule is additive rather than multiplicative across time steps. Specifically, to preserve critical information over long sequences, several gates control alternative storage flows within each memory cell at time step $t$. These gates function as "attractors" for specific tensor patterns or "repulsors" for other patterns when computing backpropagation gradients through time steps.

The cell state update equation is given by $c_t = f_t c_{t-1} + i_t z_t$. Here, $c_{t-1}$ represents the persistent memory from the previous time step. The forget gate $f_t$ fixes this file by determining how much previous information needs to be retained within the constant error carousel mechanism by modulating its influence on future outputs with sigmoid activation functions $\sigma$. Conversely, $i_t$ controls how much new information from input $\mathbf{x}_t$ is added via cell inputs $z_t$. These gates allow the network to revise its memory decisions dynamically at every timestep based on current context. Simultaneously, if multiple scalar memory cells are used\citep{Greff:06book}, they can mix cell outputs at foundations (such as hidden states) through recurrent weight matrices $\mathbf{R}$ mixing cell states across memory cells\citep{Greff:06book,mLSTM:mixing:Detailed}. This recurrence creates re-weighted outputs between neurons within layers that permit interactions with higher dimensionality contexts than single cells could support alone.
