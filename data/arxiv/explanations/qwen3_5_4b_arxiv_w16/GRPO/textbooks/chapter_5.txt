\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 5: # Supervising Policies with Process Rewards versus Outcome Rewards

The evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) toward sophisticated mathematical reasoning has largely relied on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants. While early successes were driven by outcome-based evaluations—judging a model's performance solely on whether its final answer matched the ground truth—a critical bottleneck emerged in handling complex, multi-step logical derivations. In mathematical tasks, simply arriving at the correct answer does not guarantee that the reasoning path was sound; conversely, a flawed intermediate step can sometimes be corrected before reaching the end, or conversely, a perfect sequence might diverge slightly but recover. The DeepSeekMath paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that fundamentally shifts the paradigm of reward application. By moving beyond static outcome supervision to dynamic process supervision, GRPO utilizes granular feedback loops that assign rewards to individual reasoning steps rather than just the terminal token. This chapter delves deep into the mechanistic distinction between these two paradigms, analyzing how normalizing rewards across sampled groups and aggregating them temporally transforms the optimization landscape for mathematical agents. We will explore the theoretical formulation of outcome supervision, derive the mechanics of process supervision through step-wise normalization and summation, and empirically justify why this shift is indispensable for scaling reasoning capabilities in open language models.

## Conceptual Framework: Outcome Supervision RL ($r_{final}$)

Outcome Supervision Reinforcement Learning represents the traditional baseline for evaluating generative models in mathematics and other logic-intensive domains. In this framework, the reward signal is strictly tied to the final output token of a generated response. For every input query $q$, a model generates a sequence of tokens $o = (o_1, o_2, ..., o_T)$ representing its solution attempt. An external evaluator—whether a binary oracle checking against ground truth labels or a probabilistic reward model—assesses only the entire sequence once completion occurs. If the final answer is correct, a positive reward $r > 0$ is assigned; if incorrect or empty, a penalty $r < 0$ or zero is issued. Mathematically, this results in a scalar reward vector $\mathbf{r} = \{r\}$ where $|\mathbf{r}| = 1$. Consequently, when computing gradients to update the policy parameters $\theta$, all internal decisions made during tokens $t=1$ through $t=T-1$ are implicitly weighted equally by their proximity to this single endpoint event.

While simple and computationally cheap to implement via pre-computed labels or lightweight classifiers, pure outcome supervision suffers from severe gradient dilution issues in long-horizon tasks like solving algebraic equations or geometric proofs. Because the reward is concentrated entirely at time step $T$, tokens generated earlier in the chain receive vanishingly small updates unless they happen to be part of a very short sequence. This creates an incentive structure where models learn to skip steps ("hallucinate" shortcuts) to reach an answer faster rather than adhering to rigorous deductive logic. Furthermore, outcome signals fail to distinguish between two distinct paths: one where an error was made early but recovered later, and another where errors were avoided throughout but led to redundancy at the end. Both yield identical outcomes ($Correct$), thus providing no guidance on *how* to improve intermediate reasoning quality. In the context of DeepSeekMath's exploration using GRPO, reliance on outcome-only rewards proved insufficient for achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks requiring deep syntactic manipulation of symbols and variables; it optimized for "getting lucky" with long chains rather than mastering consistent derivation patterns.

## Refinement: Process Supervision RL ($r_{steps}$): Summing Normalized Rewards Across Steps

To overcome the limitations inherent in terminal-only evaluation, DeepSeekMath introduces Process Supervision RL (PS), which decomposes the monolithic task into atomic units: individual reasoning steps. Instead of assigning a single scalar value to an entire output history, PS employs a Process Reward Model (PRM) that evaluates specific segments of text corresponding to discrete logical operations or structural milestones within the problem-solving workflow. A "step" is formally defined as ending at token indices determined by semantic boundaries—for instance, completing an equation setup (`...`), deriving an intermediate variable (`=`), stating a lemma (`Therefore`), or concluding with units (`units`).
