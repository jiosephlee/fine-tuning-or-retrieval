\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 3: # Chapter 3: Base Model Pre-Training and Instruction Tuning Strategies

The journey toward achieving elite mathematical reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) does not begin with the final instruction-tuned model but rather with strategic decisions made during the foundational stages of training. As established in the DeepSeekMath paper, the path to high-performance reasoning requires careful orchestration of initialization, data curation, and the specific instructional formats used to guide the model's internal logic. This chapter delves into the architectural and pedagogical choices that define DeepSeekMath-Base and DeepSeekMath-Instruct, detailing how these components work in concert to elevate a standard transformer into a specialized mathematical agent. We will examine why a code-based foundation was deemed critical, how massive amounts of public data were synthesized into a coherent curriculum, and how distinct reasoning paradigms—from simple chain-of-thought to complex tool integration—were embedded via supervised fine-tuning.

## Initialization

The decision to initialize the DeepSeekMath model architecture with **DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5** rather than a generic large language model (such as Llama or ChatGLM) represents a pivotal theoretical stance in modern mathematical AI research: that syntactic competence in programming is intrinsically linked to logical competence in mathematics. The authors posit that mathematical problems often rely heavily on symbolic manipulation, algebraic substitution, and structural decomposition—skills that are naturally aligned with computer science syntax. Consequently, starting from a code-specialized model provides a richer semantic landscape for interpreting mathematical variables and operations compared to a general-purpose text model.

This initialization strategy addresses the long-standing question of whether training on code improves reasoning abilities. The paper provides empirical evidence suggesting an affirmative answer for mathematical domains. By inheriting the linguistic patterns associated with pseudocode, variable definitions, and algorithmic flow from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, the initial weights possess a latent bias toward structured thinking. This is not merely incidental; experiments described in the paper indicate that this code-centric origin allows the model to grasp abstract mathematical structures more rapidly during subsequent math-specific training phases. Unlike general models that might treat math symbols purely as visual tokens within sentences, a code-initialized model recognizes expressions like $f(x)^2$ or summation operators $\sum$ as functional constructs requiring operational precision. This foundational advantage creates a smoother learning trajectory when introducing rigorous mathematical constraints later in the pipeline.

## Composition

Following initialization comes the construction of **DeepSeekMath Corpus**, a meticulously engineered dataset comprising 120 billion math-related tokens sourced from Common Crawl (CC). The composition of this corpus is unique because it prioritizes quantity derived from public web data over curated but scarce datasets like arXiv papers alone or OpenWebMath subsets. The training mix for **DeepSeekMath-Base 7B** was distributed strategically across five distinct sources to balance breadth and depth: 56% came from the newly constructed DeepSeekMath Corpus (primarily web pages), 4% from AlgebraicStack (mathematical code), 10% from arXiv papers, 20% from GitHub repositories (natural language code), and 10% from general natural language data drawn from Common Crawl in both English and Chinese.

This specific ratio reflects an intentional design philosophy: heavy reliance on web scraping combined with targeted refinement rather than exclusive dependence on academic archives. The authors observed that while arXiv papers are common in other models, they did not yield proportional improvements across all benchmarks compared to their sheer volume or accessibility issues regarding parsing noise versus actual problem-solving utility found on educational websites. Instead, the overwhelming majority of tokens (the ~66% split between DeepSeekMath Corpus and Code/Generic CC) ensured exposure to millions of diverse problem types ranging from elementary arithmetic to university-level calculus presented in varied contexts—textbook exercises, forum discussions, and exam banks—that might be absent in formal academic literature alone. Furthermore, unlike many competitors who restrict their math pre-training to English-only data sourced primarily from Western institutions or arXiv, this composition explicitly includes high-quality non-English content via Common Crawl scans of Chinese educational sites (like Baidu Zhidao or Zhihu), ensuring multilingual readiness without sacrificing quality through indiscriminate scraping filters applied post-hoc by other researchers who missed these opportunities earlier.
