\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 7: # Deep Dive into Pre-Training Experiments & Ablation Studies

While the primary narrative of DeepSeekMath focuses on achieving state-of-the-art performance on mathematical benchmarks through massive scale and novel reinforcement learning, the path to this success involved rigorous ablation studies designed to validate critical design decisions. Before committing to the final architecture and training regimen, the researchers needed to answer fundamental questions regarding the composition of pre-training data. Specifically, they sought empirical evidence to support the hypothesis that code training serves as a foundational prerequisite for enhancing mathematical reasoning. They also aimed to determine whether arXiv papers, a staple of academic data curation, were providing genuine cognitive benefits or acting as noise that diluted model performance. Finally, they investigated the stability of their multi-modal training strategy, asking whether mixing code and math tokens in a single pass could prevent the catastrophic forgetting of coding capabilities—a common risk in domain-specific fine-tuning. The following sections detail these experiments, offering deep insights into the trade-offs inherent in large-scale language modeling and confirming that code training acts as a catalyst for mathematical proficiency while questioning the efficacy of pure arXiv ingestion.

## Does Code Training Improve Mathematical Reasoning?

The central question driving this investigation was whether initializing or continuing training with code tokens genuinely enhances a model's ability to perform abstract mathematical reasoning. This hypothesis posits that code represents a formality system closer to symbolic logic than natural language, thereby equipping models with latent structures useful for manipulating variables and executing calculations mentally. To test this rigorously without confounding factors like data quantity differences, the authors designed controlled experiments using DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B as the baseline backbone model. They established two distinct experimental arms: one involving a sequential two-stage training process and another employing a concurrent one-stage mixture approach. In the two-stage setup, the model underwent 400 billion (B) tokens of pure code training followed by 150B tokens dedicated exclusively to mathematics. Conversely, in the one-stage configuration, the model was trained simultaneously on a mixture of 400B code tokens and 150B math tokens within a single continuous pass.

The results presented in Table \ref{tab:code-math} reveal compelling evidence supporting the hypothesis that code training improves mathematical reasoning capabilities across both paradigms. Under the two-stage protocol where code precedes math training, even without any explicit mathematical exposure during the first phase, mere code training resulted in significant enhancements on program-aided tasks such as GSM8K and MATH when executed via Python scripts (Program-of-Thought). When followed by subsequent math training, these gains compounded into superior performance compared to baselines lacking prior code exposure. This suggests that code training primes the model's internal representations with structural priors necessary for handling symbolic manipulation before it encounters explicit mathematical problems. Furthermore, when comparing this against a control group trained on general corpus tokens instead of code tokens for the initial 400B steps—while keeping all other parameters identical—the latter failed to yield comparable improvements in program-aided reasoning. This isolation confirms that it is specifically the nature of *code* data that drives these reasoning gains rather than simply increased exposure to technical vocabulary or syntax found elsewhere in general corpora.

Interestingly, extending this analysis to non-tool-assisted problems reveals nuances depending on whether tasks are solved sequentially or iteratively within mixed sessions. In scenarios requiring purely textual logical deduction (without generating executable Python scripts), two-stage training proved highly effective; here too, preliminary code exposure boosted efficiency during subsequent math phases and led to optimal results by mid-training stages. However, when attempting one-stage mixing—where 400B codes and 150B maths were interleaved—the performance on non-tool-assisted math problems actually degraded slightly compared to isolated math-only trajectories initially reported in earlier sections (though ultimately outperforming general baselines). The authors interpret this degradation as indicative of saturation limits at smaller scales; specifically, DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B appears insufficiently scaled to fully assimilate both dense streams of syntactic logic (code) and semantic abstraction (mathematics) simultaneously without interference or dilution effects during early convergence phases. Thus, while code training undeniably fuels mathematical reasoning when sequenced appropriately or sufficiently separated temporally from competing objectives during weight updates, merging them
