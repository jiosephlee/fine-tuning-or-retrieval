### How does preserving hyperspherical energy guarantee semantic preservation in text‑to‑image diffusion models?
Question:
In the paper, hyperspherical energy (HE) is defined as \(\text{HE}(W)=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{w}_i-\hat{w}_j\|^{-1}\) where \(\hat{w}_i = w_i/\|w_i\|\). The authors claim that keeping \(\text{HE}(W)\) unchanged during finetuning preserves the pretrained model's generative semantics. I am unclear on:

1. Intuitively, why should the sum of inverse pairwise distances among normalized neurons capture "semantic" information?
2. Does preserving HE imply that all pairwise cosine similarities are unchanged? If so, how does this translate into preserving visual quality?
3. Are there any theoretical results linking HE invariance to invariance of the diffusion model's output distribution?

Any explanation or sketch proof would help me understand this core hypothesis.
Answer:
**Answer**

The paper’s claim that “preserving hyperspherical energy (HE) preserves the pretrained generative semantics” rests on two facts that are well known in the theory of neural‑network symmetries:

1. **The geometry of the weight vectors – not their absolute scale – encodes most of the knowledge that a network has acquired**;  
2. **An orthogonal transformation of all weight vectors in a layer leaves every pairwise inner product (hence every cosine similarity) unchanged and can be absorbed into a change of basis of the hidden activations**.  

Because HE is exactly invariant under such a global orthogonal change, keeping HE fixed forces the finetuned weights to stay inside this equivalence class and therefore prevents the loss of the semantic information that lives in the angular relationships among neurons.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. Why does the sum of inverse pairwise distances capture “semantic” information?

For a weight matrix $\,\mathbf W=[\mathbf w_{1},\dots ,\mathbf w_{n}]\,\in\,\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ let  

$$
    \hat{\mathbf w}_{i}= \frac{\mathbf w_{i}}{\|\mathbf w_{i}\|},
    \qquad 
    \text{HE}(\mathbf W)=\sum_{i\neq j}\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf w}_{j}\bigr\|^{-1}.
$$

Since all vectors have been normalised, $\,\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf w}_{j}\bigr\|^{2}=2(1-\cos\angle(\mathbf w_{i},\mathbf w_{j}))$. Hence each term $\,\|\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf w}_{j}\|^{-1}$ is a monotone function of the cosine similarity  
$c_{ij}=\,\cos(\angle(\!\widehat{\!\!\bfw_i},\,!\widehat{\!\!\bfw_j}))$. The whole sum therefore measures how *pairwise* aligned or repelled the weight directions are on the unit hypersphere.

Empirically it has been observed that neurons whose directions are close tend to respond to semantically related features (e.g. edges oriented similarly or parts belonging to the same object class), whereas neurons that are far apart tend to encode unrelated concepts [17‑19]. Consequently, a representation such as **hyperspherical similarity** – a collection of all pairwise angles – works as a fingerprint of what the layer “knows”. By aggregating these angles into a single scalar quantity (the energy), one obtains a compact structural statistic that reflects whether this fingerprint has been distorted.

Intuitively, if you imagine each normalized neuron as a point on a globe, HE tells you how densely those points cluster in certain regions versus being evenly spread. A large change in this clustering pattern would correspond to moving many neurons from one semantic region to another, which would inevitably alter what concepts can be expressed by the layer.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2\. Does preserving HE imply that every cosine similarity stays unchanged?

Not exactly – equality of the scalar $\text{HE}(\!\bfW)$ *does not* mathematically force every individual distance $\,\|\hat{\bfw}_i-\hat{\bfw}_j\rVert$ (or equivalently every cosine $c_{ij}$) to be identical. Two different configurations of points can happen to have the same total energy while having different pairwise angles.

What *does* guarantee both **energy preservation** *and* **pairwise‑angle preservation** is an **exact orthogonal transformation** applied uniformly to all neurons:

$$
    \widetilde{\bfW}= {\bf R}\,\bfW^{0},
    \qquad 
    {\bf R}^{\,T}{\bf R}= {\bf I}.
$$

Because  
$$
    \langle\twidetilde{{w}}_i,\twiddle{{w}}_j\rangle
      = 
      \langle {\bf R}{w}_i^0,\:{R}{w}_j^0\rangle
      = 
      {w}_i^{0,T}{w}_j^0,
$$  
the full matrix of cosines,
$$
    C=[c_{ij}]_{i,j},
    ~~c_{ij}=~%
    \cos(
        {\widehat{{w}}}^{\,0}_ i ,
        {\widehat{{w}}}^{\,0}_ j
        ),
$$  
is invariant under any such ${R}$. Consequently every term inside the energy sum stays unchanged, so HE stays exactly constant.

The authors therefore do **not** rely on plain HE regularisation alone; they *parameterise* finetuning as learning an orthogonal matrix ${R}$. By construction this ensures both that HE remains identical and that all pairwise cosine similarities are preserved. The magnitude of each neuron may still be altered (e.g. by an additional diagonal scaling matrix in “re‑scaled OFT”), but scaling disappears after normalisation and thus does not affect HE either.

How does this translate into visual quality? In diffusion models the denoising network computes a score  
$s_{\theta}({x}_t,t)= -\,\varepsilon_{\theta}(x_t,t)$  
by repeatedly passing hidden activations through linear layers followed by pointwise non‑linearities (GELU, SiLU etc.). The linear part at layer $k$ is precisely  

$$
    h^{(k)}=( {\bf W}^{(k)} )^\top h^{(k-1)} .
$$

If we replace every ${W}^{(k)}$ by an orthogonal transform  

$$
    {\widetilde{W}}^{(k)}={R}^{(k)}\,W_{{0}}^{(k)},
``` 

and simultaneously apply its inverse to incoming activations  

$$
   {\tilde h}^{(k-1)}=(R ^{(k-1)})^\top h ^{(k-1)},
```  

the computation yields exactly  

$$
   ({R}^{(k)}W_{{0}} ^ {( k ) })^\top(R ^ {( k-1) })^\top h ^ {( k-1 )}
   ~=~ 
   {W}_{{0}} ^ {( k )T }h ^ {( k-1)}
``` 

so it produces exactly the same value binding forward propagation identically.\footnote{All subsequent nonlinearities are element‑wise hence equivariant under linear basis changes;}
therefore **the entire forward pass—and consequently its predicted noise distribution—remains unchanged**, yielding indistinguishable image samples despite finetuning.

### Summary

*The sum over inverse distances among normalised neurons – hyperspherical energy – encodes how those neurons are arranged on a unit sphere; this arrangement determines which directions encode which visual concepts.*  
*If finetuning moves all weights by a common orthogonal matrix, every pairwise cosine similarity is exactly preserved; consequently HE stays unchanged.*  
*Because deep linear–nonlinear networks are equivariant under such global rotations, replacing each layer’s weights by an orthogonal transform yields an ​identical​ denoising function for diffusion models, so their generated‑image distribution does **not** change.*

Hence preserving hyperspherical energy via Orthogonal Finetuning guarantees that finite‑task adaptation cannot destroy pretrained knowledge encoded in angular relationships—and empirically manifests as unchanged fidelity, diversity and semantic consistency in text‑to‑image generation.
