\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### Why does NF4 outperform FP4/Int4 despite all being 4‑bit representations?
Question:
Empirically NF4 yields lower perplexity on language modeling benchmarks compared to FP4 and Int4.

* Is the improvement solely due to matching the assumed normal distribution of pretrained weights, or are there other factors such as better handling of outliers/zero values?
* Do experiments exist showing NF4’s advantage when weight distributions deviate from normality (e.g., heavy tails)?
* Could you provide an intuitive explanation linking information theory optimality to observed downstream task gains?

Answer:
**Short answer:**  
NF‑4 (the *NormalFloat* 4‑bit format) beats plain FP‑4 and Int‑4 because it places its four‑bit codes where the pretrained weights are most likely to be found – i.e. around the centre of a zero‑mean Gaussian – and it reserves a dedicated code for the exact value $0$. This “distribution‑aware” placement dramatically reduces the average quantisation error on the bulk of the parameters while keeping the same block‑wise scaling that all three formats use for outliers. The smaller distortion propagates through the frozen base model into the LoRA adapters, so after finetuning the model’s predictions (perplexity, downstream accuracy) stay much closer to those obtained with full‑precision weights.  

Below I unpack why this happens, what evidence the QLoRA paper provides, and how an information‐theoretic view explains the empirical gains.

### 1. What makes NF 4 different?

All three formats start from the same *block‐wise* quantisation pipeline: a block of $B$ weight elements is rescaled by a per‐block constant $c=\dfrac{2^{k-1}}{\operatorname{absmax}}$ (with $k=4$ here), then rounded into a low‐bit code word and later de‐quantised by dividing by $c$.  
The only freedom left is how we map the four bits $\{0,\dots ,15\}$ onto numerical values inside the interval $[-\,1,+\,1]$.

* **Int 4** uses a uniform integer lattice $\{-8,\dots ,7\}$; after rescaling each bin has equal width.  
* **FP 4** uses a uniform *floating‐point* lattice whose exponent is fixed but whose mantissa steps are also uniform in linear space.  
* **NF 4** starts from a *quantile* discretisation of a standard normal distribution $N(0,\,1)$. Concretely it computes the $2^{k}+1$ quantiles  

$$

          + Q_{N(0,\,1)}\,\bigl(\tfrac{i+\,1}{2^{k}+\,1}\bigr)\Bigr),
          \qquad i=0,\dots ,\,2^{k}-1,
$$
