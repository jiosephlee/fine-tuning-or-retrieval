### Why does BOFT$(m,b)$ have exactly $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d m$ effective trainable parameters?
Question:
In Section 4 it is stated that BOFT$(m,b)$ uses $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d m$ parameters for a layer of size $d \times n$. I understand each $2b \times 2b$ block requires $(b-1)b$ degrees of freedom when represented via a skew‑symmetric matrix, but I cannot derive the full expression accounting for all $m$ components and their block placements. Could someone provide a step‑by‑step derivation linking block size $b$, number of factors $m$, and total parameter count?
Answer:
In BOFT the trainable part of a finetuning layer is the orthogonal matrix  

$$
R(m,b)=
\rule[-6pt]{0pt}{18pt}
{\underbrace{\widetilde B^{\,b}_{(d,m)}
               \
               \widetilde B^{\,b}_{(d,m-1)}
               \cdots 
               \widetilde B^{\,b}_{(d,1)}}_{\displaystyle m~{\rm factors}}}
         \;\in\;\mathbb R^{d\times d}.
$$  

It multiplies the frozen pretrained weight $W^{0}\in \mathbb R^{d\times n}$.  
All factors $\widetilde B ^{\,b}_{(d,i)}$ $(i=1,\dots ,m)$ are **orthogonal butterfly components**.  
Below we count how many independent scalar variables survive after all constraints have been taken into account; this yields the number quoted in Sec.~4,

$$
\tfrac12 (\,b-0{})\, d m .
$$
