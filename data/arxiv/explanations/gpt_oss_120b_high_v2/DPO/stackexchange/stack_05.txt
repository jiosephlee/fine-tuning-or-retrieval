### How should one choose the KL‑penalty coefficient $\beta$ in practice, and what is its effect on the trade‑off between reward maximization and staying close to the reference policy?
Question:
The paper shows experiments with several values of $\beta$ (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 1, 5). Intuitively I understand larger $\beta$ loosens the KL constraint, but I am unsure how to reason about an appropriate magnitude for a given dataset or model size. Is there any guideline—perhaps relating $\beta$ to typical log‑probability scales of large language models—that helps set $\beta$ without extensive hyperparameter sweeps?
Answer:
**Answer**

In DPO the coefficient $\beta$ appears twice – once in the *optimal‑policy* formula that underlies all RLHF methods  

$$
\pi^{*}(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\textup{ref}}(y\mid x)\,
     \exp!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr)
$$  
(1)

and again inside the loss that DPO actually optimises  

$$
\mathcal L_{\textup{DPO}}(\theta)=-
     {\mathbb E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}
     {\log }\sigma!\Bigl(
        {\beta }\, 
        {\log }\frac{\displaystyle 
               \pi_\theta(y_w|x)}
                 {\displaystyle 
               \pi_{\textup{ref}}(y_w|x)}
        -
        {\beta }\, 
        {\log }\frac{\displaystyle 
               \pi_\theta(y_l|x)}
                 {\displaystyle 
               \pi_{\textup{ref}}(y_l|x)}
     \Bigr)
$$  
(2)

Equation (2) makes it clear what $\beta$ does:

* it **scales the log‑odds** $\log\dfrac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\textup{ref}}}$;
* it therefore **controls how strongly a single preference pair pushes the policy away from the reference**;
