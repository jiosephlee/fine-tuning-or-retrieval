\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does DPO’s gradient include the term $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$ as a weighting factor?
Question:
The gradient in Eq. (7) contains a factor $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$, which the authors say gives higher weight when the reward estimate is wrong. How exactly does this term arise from differentiating the loss in Eq. (6), and why does it help prevent model degeneration compared to an unweighted "increase‑likelihood of $y_w$, decrease‑likelihood of $y_l$" update?
Answer:
**Answer**

In Direct Preference Optimization the training objective is a binary‑cross‑entropy loss written in terms of the *implicit* reward  

$$
\hat r_{\!\theta}(x,y)\;\stackrel{\rm def}{=}\;\beta\,\log
      \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\,|\,x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\,|\,x)},
      \qquad (\text{Eq.\,(6)})
$$  

and a pair of generations $(y_{w},y_{l})$ such that human annotators prefer $y_{w}\succ y_{l}$:  

$$
\mathcal L_{\rm DPO}(\theta)
=
-\!\mathbb E_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})}\!
      \Bigl[
         \log\sigma
         \bigl(
            \hat r_{\!\theta}(x,y_{w})
           -%
            \hat r_{\!\theta}(x,y_{l})
         \bigr)
      \Bigr].
      \tag{6}
$$  

---

### Where does the sigmoid‑weight appear?

Let us differentiate one datum’s contribution  

$\ell(\theta)= -\log\sigma(\Delta)$  

with  

$\Delta:=\hat r_{\!\theta}(x,y_{w})-\hat r_{\!\theta}(x,y_{l})$.  

The derivative of the logistic function is  

$\sigma'(z)=\sigma(z)[1-\sigma(z)]$; therefore  

$$
\nabla_{\!\theta}\ell
=
-\frac{\sigma'(\Delta)}{\sigma(\Delta)}\,\nabla_{\!\theta}\Delta
=
-[\,1-\sigma(\Delta)\,]\,
\nabla_{\!\theta}\Delta .
$$  

Because only the policy parameters appear inside the implicit reward,
the gradient of the difference splits as  

$$
\nabla_{\!\theta}\Delta
=
\nabla_{\!\theta}\bigl[\beta\,\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}|x)\bigr]
-
