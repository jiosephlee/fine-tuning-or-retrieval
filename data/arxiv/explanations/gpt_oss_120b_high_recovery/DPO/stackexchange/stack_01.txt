### Why does the partition function $Z(x)$ disappear in the DPO preference model derivation?
Question:
In Section 3 the authors start from the optimal policy under a KL‐constrained objective
$$\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\bigr),$$
and then re‑parameterize the reward as
$$r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta \log Z(x).$$
When they plug this into the Bradley‑Terry model they claim that $Z(x)$ cancels out, yielding Eq.(5).
Can someone show the algebraic steps that make $Z(x)$ disappear? I'm especially confused about why the term $\log Z(x)$ vanishes after taking the difference $r^*(x,y_1)-r^*(x,y_2)$.

Answer:
In the DPO derivation the **partition function**

$$
Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\,
      \exp!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr)
$$

appears only because the optimal policy under the KL‑constrained objective must be a proper probability distribution:

$$
\pi_{r}(y|x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\,
      \exp!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Bigr).\tag{3}
$$

If we solve (3) for the reward we obtain

$$
r(x,y)=
      \beta\,\log\frac{\pi_{r}(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}
      +\beta\,\log Z(x).\tag{4}
$$

The **key observation** is that $Z(x)$ depends **only on the prompt $x$**; it does **not** depend on the particular completion $y$.  
When a preference model such as Bradley–Terry is used, only **differences of rewards** for two completions matter:

$$
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}|x)
     = \sigma\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr),
     \qquad 
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}\,. \tag{5}

$$
\sigma[( r(y_0)+c)-( r(y_+)+c)]
