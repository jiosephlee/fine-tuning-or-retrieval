\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Can DPO be extended beyond binary preferences to multi‑ranking or listwise preferences?
Question:
The paper mentions extensions to Plackett–Luce models in Appendix C but focuses on Bradley–Terry (pairwise) data. If I have human annotations that rank three or more completions per prompt, how would the loss change? Would we still use a binary cross entropy over log‑ratio terms, or is there a natural listwise loss derived from Eq.(5)?

Answer:
**Answer**

The Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective is derived by inserting the optimal‑policy form of the KL‑constrained RLHF problem into a Bradley–Terry (BT) preference model.  In its basic incarnation (Eq.~(5) of the paper) the human data consists of a single binary judgement $y_{w}\succ y_{l}\mid x$ and the loss is  

$$
\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)
=
-\,
\E_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})\sim \mathcal D}
\Bigl[
   \log\sigma
   (
      \beta\,\log\dfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{w}\mid x)}
      -
      \beta\,\log\dfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{l}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{l}\mid x)}
   )
   .
   ]
$$

where $\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})$.  Because the BT model depends only on *differences* of rewards, the partition function cancels out and we obtain a binary‑cross‑entropy over a log‑ratio term.

---

### From pairwise BT to listwise Plackett–Luce

The BT model is a special case of the **Plackett–Luce (PL)** family.  For a full ranking  
$R=(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\succ\dotsc\succ y_{k})$ generated from a latent reward $r^{*}(x,y)$, the PL probability is  

$$
p^{*}(R\,|\,x)=
    \prod_{j=1}^{k}
    \frac{\exp r^{*}(x,y_{j})}
         {\displaystyle
          \sum_{i=j}^{k}\exp r^{*}(x,y_{i})
         } .
    {\qquad}{(PL)}
$$

Appendix C of the paper shows that exactly the same cancellation that occurs for BT also happens for PL once we substitute the re‑parameterisation  

$$
r(x,y)=
    \beta\,%
    \log\dfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y\,|\,x)}%
                {\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\,|\,x)}
    .
    {\qquad}{(6)}
$$

Plugging $(6)$ into $(PL)$ yields a **listwise likelihood that depends only on the policy**:

$$
p_{\theta,\mathrm{ref}}(R\,|\,x)
=
    \prod_{j=1}^{k}
    \frac{\exp s_{j}}
         {\displaystyle
          \sum_{i=j}^{k}\exp s_{i}},
    %
    {\qquad}
    s_{j}:=
        \beta\,%
        \log\dfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{j}\mid x)}%
                    {\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{j}\mid x)} .
    {\qquad}{(7)}
$$

Observe that when $k=2$ this product collapses to  

$p_{\theta,\mathrm{ref}}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)=
\sigma(s_{1}-s_{2})$,  

which is precisely the term inside Eq.~(5).  Thus Eq.~(5) is just the special case of a **listwise cross‑entropy** loss derived from a PL model.
