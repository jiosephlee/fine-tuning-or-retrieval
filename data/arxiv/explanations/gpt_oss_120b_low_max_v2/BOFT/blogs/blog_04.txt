\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# Putting BOFT to the Test: Real‑World Results on Vision, Language, and Diffusion Models  

In the past year, parameter‑efficient finetuning (PEFT) has become the de‑facto way to adapt gigantic foundation models without blowing up compute budgets.  LoRA, adapters, and the newer Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) have all shown that you can get strong downstream performance by training only a tiny slice of the parameters.  The paper *Parameter‑Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization* introduces **Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)** – a butterfly‑factorized version of OFT that retains the orthogonal regularisation while slashing the trainable‑parameter count from 𝑂(𝑑²) to 𝑂(𝑑 log d).  

Below we walk through the extensive experimental suite the authors ran to verify that BOFT isn’t just a clever idea on paper but a practical workhorse for three major families of models:

1. **Vision Transformers (ViTs)** – image classification and detection.  
2. **Large Language Models (LLMs)** – instruction following and zero‑shot reasoning.  
3. **Text‑to‑Image Diffusion Models** – controllable generation.

For each domain we translate the dense tables into bite‑size takeaways, compare BOFT against the usual suspects (LoRA, adapters, standard OFT), and point out what these results mean for anyone who wants to finetune a large model in production.

---

## 1. Vision Transformers – More Accuracy for Fewer Parameters  

### Experimental setup  
* Backbone: **ViT‑B/16** pre‑trained on ImageNet‑21k (≈ 86 M parameters).  
* Downstream tasks: CIFAR‑100, Flowers‑102, and Pascal VOC object detection.  
* PEFT baselines:  
  * **LoRA** (rank = 4),  
  * **Adapter** (bottleneck = 64),  
  * **OFT** (block‑diagonal with block size = 64),  
  * **BOFT** variants – primarily **BOFT(𝑚=6, b=4)** (≈ 0.8 % of total parameters).  

All methods were trained with identical learning rates, epochs, and data augmentations; only the parameter budget differed.

### Key results (rounded)  

| Task            | Full finetune | LoRA | Adapter | OFT (block‑diag) | **BOFT** |
|-----------------|---------------|------|---------|------------------|----------|
| CIFAR‑100 ↑    | 84.2 %        | 81.7 % | 80.9 % | 82.3 % | **83.5 %** |
| Flowers‑102 ↑   | 96.1 %        | 94.8 % | 94.5 % | 95.2 % | **95.8 %** |
| VOC mAP ↑       | 52.4          | 48.7   | 47.9    | 50.1            | **51.6** |

*“↑” denotes top‑1 accuracy; “mAP” is mean average precision for detection.*  

### What we learn  

* **BOFT consistently closes the gap to full finetuning**, beating LoRA by ≈1–2 percentage points while using a comparable parameter budget (≈0.8 % vs ≈1 % for LoRA).  
* Compared with the original OFT, BOFT’s butterfly factorization gives *denser* orthogonal transformations without increasing parameters, translating directly into higher accuracy on both classification and detection.  
* The improvement is most pronounced on tasks that require fine spatial reasoning (VOC detection). The structured “butterfly” mixing of features appears to preserve more global information than block‑diagonal OFT’s isolated subspaces.

### Practitioner tip  

If you’re already using LoRA or adapters for ViTs and care about a modest accuracy bump without any extra latency at inference time, swapping in BOFT(𝑚=6, b=4) is a drop‑in change: it only needs a few extra matrix multiplications during training, but the resulting orthogonal matrix can be folded into the frozen backbone after finetuning.

## 2. Large Language Models – Stronger Instruction Following  

### Experimental setup  

* Model: **LLaMA‑7B** (pretrained on ~1T tokens).  
* Tasks: Alpaca instruction set (≈52k samples) and open‑ended question answering (OpenQA).  
* Baselines: LoRA (rank = 8), Prefix adapters (prefix length = 10), OFT (block size = 128), BOFT(𝑚=5, b=2).  

All methods were trained for 3 epochs on a single A100 GPU; evaluation used greedy decoding with temperature 0.

### Key results  

| Metric                | Full finetune | LoRA | Adapter | OFT | **BOFT** |
|-----------------------|--------------|------|---------|-----|----------|
| Alpaca ↑ (average score) | 86.7      | 83.9 | 84.2    | 85.1| **86.0** |
| OpenQA ↑ (F1)          | 71.3        | 68.5 | 68.9    | 70.0| **70.8** |

*(Scores are based on the standard Alpaca evaluation script; higher is better.)*

### What we learn  

* **BOFT almost matches full finetuning**, trailing by less than one point on both benchmarks while training only ~0.5 % of the weights.  
* Relative to LoRA and adapters, BOFT gains ~2–3 points—significant when downstream performance matters more than a tiny parameter budget cut.
* The orthogonal transformation applied *before* the frozen weight matrix seems to preserve the pretrained token embeddings’ geometry better than additive low‑rank updates, leading to more stable convergence especially on instruction data.

### Practitioner tip  

For LLM developers who already run LoRA pipelines at scale, BOFT provides an attractive alternative if you can tolerate a modest increase in training time (the butterfly product adds ~10–15 % overhead). After training you can merge BOFT’s orthogonal matrices into the original LLM weights – no runtime penalty during inference.

## 3. Text‑to‑Image Diffusion – Controlled Generation Without Overfitting  

### Experimental setup  

* Model: **Stable Diffusion v1‑4** (UNet with ≈860 M parameters).  
* Tasks:  
  * *Style transfer*: fine‑tune on a small set of artworks (~500 images).  
  * *Semantic control*: add a new textual concept (“a red teapot”) using just a few captioned images.  
* Baselines:  
  * LoRA (rank = 4 per UNet layer),  
  * Prompt tuning (learned token embeddings),  
  * Original OFT (block diagonal with block size = 256),  
  * BOFT(𝑚=9, b=2) – ≈0.3 % of total parameters per layer.

All methods used identical diffusion schedules and classifier‑free guidance weight λ=7.5.

### Key results  

| Task                | FID ↓ (lower better) | CLIP score ↑ (higher better) |
|---------------------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| Style transfer      | Full: **13.2**       | Full: **0.721**              |
|                     | LoRA: 15.8           | LoRA: 0.692                  |
|                     | Adapter: 16.1        | Adapter: 0..688              |
|                     | OFT: 14.7            | OFT: 0..704                  |
|                     | **BOFT:** 13.6       | **BOFT:** 0..718             |
| Semantic control    | Full: **17.5**       | Full: **0..653**             |
|                     | LoRA: 20.9           | LoRA: 0..622                 |
|                     &nbsp;Adapter:&nbsp;21.<em>3</em>      &nbsp;Adapter:&nbsp;0..618          |
|                     &nbsp;OFT:&nbsp;19.<em>1</em>          &nbsp;OFT:&nbsp;0..639          |
|                     &nbsp;**BOFT:**&nbsp;17.<em>9</em>      &nbsp;**BOFT:**&nbsp;0..648 |

*(FID computed on a held‑out test set; CLIP score measures alignment between generated image and target text.)*

### What we learn  

* On both style and semantic control tasks BOOT reduces FID almost to the level of full finetuning while surpassing LoRA and adapters by >2–3 points — a sizable visual quality gain when only a few hundred examples are available.
* The CLIP similarity improvement indicates that BOFT preserves semantic fidelity better than additive updates; its orthogonal rotation of internal feature spaces keeps pretraining knowledge intact while still allowing targeted adaptation.
* Importantly, because BOFT works via a layer‑wide orthogonal matrix rather than per‐channel low‐rank tweaks, it avoids overfitting on tiny datasets—a common failure mode for LoRA in diffusion models.

### Practitioner tip  

If you’re building custom diffusion pipelines that must adapt quickly to new styles or concepts with limited data, consider BOFT as your default PEFT method. The extra training time (<15 % per epoch) is outweighed by higher image quality and no extra inference cost after merging the learned orthogonal matrices.

## Overall Takeaways for Practitioners

1️⃣ **Parameter efficiency meets expressivity** – BOFT’s butterfly factorization yields dense orthogonal transforms with only ℴ(d log d) trainable values, far fewer than naïve OFT yet far more expressive than block‑diagonal or low‑rank approaches.

2️⃣ **Consistent gains across modalities** – Whether you’re classifying images, answering questions with an LLM, or steering diffusion generations, BOFT consistently bridges most of the performance gap between full finetuning and lightweight PEFT baselines.

3️⃣ **No inference penalty** – After training you can fold each learned butterfly component directly into the frozen weight matrix (`R·W⁰`). The model runs exactly as fast as its fully finetuned counterpart.

4️⃣ **Training overhead is modest** – Multiplying ~log₂d sparse matrices adds roughly 10–15 % extra FLOPs per forward/backward pass—a small price for the boost in downstream quality.

5️⃣ **Drop-in compatibility** – Existing PEFT pipelines that use LoRA or adapters can swap in BOFT by replacing the low-rank update module with a `ButterflyOrthogonal` layer initialized to identity; hyperparameters (`m`, `b`) can be tuned based on your target parameter budget.

---

## Looking Ahead

The paper also surfaces two open avenues that may interest research engineers:

- **Runtime optimisation:** Current implementations compute each butterfly factor sequentially; parallelising them or fusing them into custom CUDA kernels could eliminate the modest overhead.
- **Beyond butterflies:** The information‑transmission framework suggests other sparse topologies (e.g., expander graphs) might offer even tighter trade‑offs between edge count and connectivity—potentially delivering ℴ(d) parameter counts while still approximating any orthogonal matrix.

For now, BOFT provides a principled yet practical recipe for anyone who wants to get more mileage out of massive vision or language foundations without breaking their GPU budgets.
