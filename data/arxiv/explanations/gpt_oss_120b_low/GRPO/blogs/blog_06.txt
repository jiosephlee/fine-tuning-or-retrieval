\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# Beyond Numbers: Evaluating DeepSeekMath on Formal Proofs and Multilingual Benchmarks  

Large language models have made headlines for cracking quantitative‑reasoning tests, but the real test of a “math‑savvy” model lies in two less‑glamorous corners: formal theorem proving and non‑English mathematics. In this post we dive into how **DeepSeekMath‑7B**—the newest open‑source model from the DeepSeek team—behaves when asked to generate Isabelle proofs for miniF2F problems **and** when it tackles Chinese curricula such as **CMATH**, **Gaokao‑MathCloze**, and **Gaokao‑MathQA**.  

---

## 1. Why Formal Proofs Matter  

Most benchmark suites (GSM8K, MATH) evaluate a model’s ability to produce *natural‑language* step‑by‑step solutions. While useful, this format leaves a large gap between a model’s answer and a mathematically rigorous proof. Formal proof assistants (Isabelle, Lean, Coq) require the entire logical chain to be encoded in a machine‑checkable language—no hidden heuristics or ambiguous wording allowed.  

Successfully auto‑formalizing informal statements is a proxy for:

* **Logical precision** – the model must understand definitions, quantifiers, and inference rules at a fine granularity.  
* **Tool integration** – generating a proof sketch that a backend prover (e.g., Isabelle’s `Sledgehammer`) can complete demonstrates effective collaboration between LLM and theorem‑proving software.  
* **Future AI tutors** – a system that can translate textbook problems into formal proofs could automatically verify student work or generate graded feedback.

### 1.1 DeepSeekMath on miniF2F  

The paper evaluates DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B on the *miniF2F* benchmark (informal Olympiad‑level problems converted to Isabelle statements). The workflow is:

1. **Few‑shot prompting** → the model outputs a **proof sketch** in informal language.
2. The sketch is fed to Isabelle’s `Sledgehammer`, which attempts to fill missing details automatically.
3. Success is measured by whether the resulting Isabelle script checks out.

**Result:** DeepSeekMath‑Base achieves strong auto‑formalization scores, surpassing prior open models of comparable size (e.g., Mistral 7B). Although it does not reach the performance of massive closed models like Gemini‑Ultra, it shows that a 7 B parameter model can already produce usable proof sketches when combined with a classical prover.

The key takeaways:

| Model | Params | MiniF2F success (few‑shot) |
|-------|--------|---------------------------|
| DeepSeekMath‑Base | 7 B | Competitive with state‑of‑the‑art 7 B baselines |
| Mistral‑7B | 7 B | Lower |
| LLaMA‑2 70 B (closed) | 70 B | Higher (as expected) |

This suggests that **pre‑training on 120 B math tokens** plus code pre‑training gives the model enough exposure to formal language patterns and proof techniques to be useful in an interactive theorem proving pipeline.

---

## 2. Multilingual Mathematics: From English Benchmarks to Chinese Exams  

Most publicly released math corpora are English‑centric, which creates an implicit bias: models excel on U.S./European contests but stumble on problems framed in other languages or educational systems. DeepSeekMath deliberately breaks this pattern.

### 2.1 The DeepSeekMath Corpus is Multilingual  

* Over **90 %** of the 120 B token corpus comes from Common Crawl pages filtered for math content via an iterative fastText classifier.
* The classifier’s second iteration discovers many Chinese math portals (e.g., zhihu.com topics, Chinese university lecture notes).  
* Human annotation cleans false positives and ensures high precision across languages.

The result is a corpus where **English and Chinese tokens are both dominant**—unlike OpenWebMath or MathPile, which are > 95 % English.

### 2.2 Benchmark Suite  

| Benchmark | Language | Format | What it tests |
|-----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **CMATH** | Chinese | Grade‑school quantitative reasoning | Basic arithmetic, algebra |
| **Gaokao‑MathCloze** | Chinese | Fill‑in‑the‑blank exam style | Symbolic manipulation, word problems |
| **Gaokao‑MathQA** | Chinese | Multiple choice (high school) | Conceptual understanding, geometry (limited) |
| **MATH / GSM8K** | English | Competition‐level / elementary reasoning | Chain‐of‐thought, program‐of‐thought |

All are evaluated with *self-contained* solutions (no external tools) unless explicitly using Python for program‐of‐thought prompts.

### 2.3 Performance Highlights  

DeepSeekMath shines on Chinese benchmarks:

| Model | Params | CMATH (↑) | Gaokao–Cloze (↑) | Gaokao–QA (↑) |
|-------|--------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| DeepSeekMath‑Base | 7 B | **84.6 %** → after RL **88.8 %** |
| DeepSeekMath‑Instruct | 7 B | 84.6 % → RL 88.8 % |
| Minerva 540 B (closed) | 540 B | ~80 % (English focus) |
| LLaMA 13 B (open) | 13 B | ~70 % |

The jump from the base model to the RL version (+~4 points) demonstrates that **reinforcement learning on English instruction data still transfers to Chinese tasks**, thanks to the multilingual pre-training foundation.

### 2.4 Why Does Multilingual Pre-Training Help?  

1. **Vocabulary Coverage:** Chinese characters appear frequently during pre-training, so tokenization does not suffer from excessive subword fragmentation—a common issue when fine-tuning an English-only model on Chinese math data.
2. **Cross-Lingual Reasoning Patterns:** Many mathematical concepts have identical logical structures across languages (e.g., solving a quadratic equation). Exposure to both languages helps the model learn language-agnostic reasoning templates.
3. **Data Diversity:** The corpus includes textbooks, forum discussions, and Q&A sites from both cultures, providing varied phrasing (“求解方程” vs “solve for x”) that improves robustness.

## 3. Implications for AI–Assisted Education  

### 3.1 Tutoring Across Borders  

A single open model that can solve U.S./European contest problems *and* Chinese high school exams reduces the need for country-specific fine-tuning pipelines. Schools could deploy one inference service that automatically generates step‐by‐step solutions in the student’s native language.

### 3.2 Formal Verification as a Grading Tool  

Imagine an online homework platform where every student answer is turned into an Isabelle sketch by DeepSeekMath, then fed to `Sledgehammer`. A passing proof guarantees correctness without manual grading—particularly valuable for advanced courses where human grading is bottlenecked.

### 3.3 Accessibility & Open Research  

Because DeepSeekMath is released under an open license, researchers can:

* Extend the multilingual corpus with underrepresented languages (e.g., Arabic or Hindi math webpages).  
* Build better reward models for RL that specifically target formal proof quality or multilingual reasoning consistency.  
* Explore hybrid pipelines that combine chain-of-thought reasoning with program-of-thought execution for subjects like calculus where symbolic computation is essential.

---

## 4. Takeaways & Future Directions  

1. **Formal reasoning is no longer exclusive to massive closed models** – DeepSeekMath shows that with carefully curated web data and code pre-training, a 7 B model can generate useful Isabelle sketches.
2. **Multilingual math pre-training matters** – High performance on CMATH and Gaokao benchmarks demonstrates that non-English data improves both language understanding and mathematical reasoning.
3. **Reinforcement learning (GRPO) amplifies these gains** – Even when RL only sees English instruction data, it pushes up Chinese benchmark scores, highlighting the transferability enabled by shared logical structures.
4. **Open avenues:**  
   * Scaling up the multilingual corpus further (adding Korean, Vietnamese, etc.).  
   * Training stronger reward models that evaluate *proof quality* rather than just final answer correctness.  
   * Integrating tree-search or speculative decoding during RL sampling to improve Maj@K performance (the current RL boost mostly reshapes answer distribution).

DeepSeekMath marks an important milestone: open models can now approach GPT‑4 level performance not merely on numeric quizzes but also on formal verification tasks and multilingual curricula. As more researchers adopt its data pipeline and its GRPO RL algorithm, we can expect increasingly capable math assistants that speak any student’s language—and can even converse with theorem provers directly.
