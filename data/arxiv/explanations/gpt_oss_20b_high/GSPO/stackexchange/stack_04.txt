### From (9) → first line of (10)

Because the sampling distribution is built from  
the **old** policy \(\pi_{\!\theta_{\rm old}}\), none of the terms in the
expectation depend on \(\theta\).
Consequently we may exchange differentiation
with the outer expectation:
  
```
⊡          ⊙                     ⊙   ⊙            ⊙  
--------
∂ /∂θ        E[g(x,y ; θ)]      g(x,y ; θ)
‑            -------             ----------     
⊢         ←                    ←-------  
```

This is often called the *“same‑expectation”* or “likelihood‑ratio” trick
because it relies on the fact that  

\[
\E_{\state }\![f(\state;\,\theta)]
=\E_{\state }\Bigl[\frac{\pi_\!\theta (\state )}{q(\state )}f'(\state;\,\theta)\Bigr]
,\qquad q=\hbox{sampling dist}.
\]

Applying it gives

\[
\nabla_\theta J=
\E[\,
    {}\,{}\,{}\partial_\theta 
    (\tfrac{\pi_\!\beta(y)}{\pi_{\alpha}(y)})
    \;\widehat A\,]
    =
\E[\;
   s_{i} (\theta)\,\widehat A _i   
   \nabla_\theta 
   \log s_{i} (\theta )]
.\tag{first line of (10)}
\]

The key point here is that the whole **gradient falls** onto
the logarithm of \(s_{i}(\theta)\);  
all other factors—including both rewards and any clipping – are simply
treated as constants during this operation.

### Sandwiched intuition

* **Why does all this averaging replace a token‑wise weight?**  
Because each token’s likelihood depends on *the same* parameters,
while every token contributes likewise through its own probability;
therefore pulling their influence out together yields a clean,
length‑normalised average

```math
ϵ\dots =
⌝♏͠┴ °
    /fy ℒ gₚʰ ...
```

* **What would happen if we differentiated token‑by‑token instead?**  
We would obtain exactly GRPO's update – where every token carries its own weight `wᵢₜ`. Those individual weights are highly unbalanced when sequences are long, causing severe variance; having one single factor `sᵢ` fixes this variance problem while still preserving off‑policy correction at sequence level.



--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Bottom line

Between equations (9) and (10):

1. We **pull gradients inside** an expectation thanks to independence of routes from `π_𝜃`.
2. We rewrite `sᵢ` using its exponential form so that its logarithmic derivative collapses neatly into a sum over tokens divided by sequence length.
3. Because denominators carry no `𝜃`, their contribution vanishes—only numerators remain.
4. The result is exactly what appears in Equation (10): an average over tokens’ log‑likelihood gradients multiplied by a single scalar importance weight `sᵢ` raised to `⁻¹⁄⁣`.

If you keep those four steps handy,
the leap from eq (9) → eq (10) disappears – it's just standard calculus applied carefully within an RL surrogate loss at sequence level.
