# GSOP vs GRP0 – The Reinforcement Learning Face‑Off

Large language models learn their best skills through reinforcement learning (RL).  
Scales up models past tens of billions of parameters, however—the studio set‐up hinges on a single feature: *training stability*.  
Enter **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRP0)**—an elegant attempt that eliminated costly value networks—followed by **Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSOP)**, a redesign that finally tames the crashes humans were afraid would suck us off‑track forever.
This post breaks down why GRP0 blows up while GSOP steadies the ship—and what you can take away when you build your own RL pipeline.

## What Tailoring Rewards Another Way Did Not Solve  

### Recap: How GRP0 Works  

In classic “on‑policy” PPO every decision gets an *importance weight*:  

```text
wᵢ,ₜ(θ) = π_θ(yᵢₜ│x,yᵢ,<ₜ) /
            π_old(yᵢₜ│x,yᵢ,<ₜ).
```  

A group G responses `y₁…y_G` come from an *old* policy.
The mean/std estimate over this group replaces a value network because the reward belongs entirely to a *whole sentence*, not one word at a time:

```textmod
Â̂ᵢ = [ r(x,yᵢ) − mean(r()) ] / std(r())
```

Each response’s loss blends every span’s weighted contribution,

```
J_GRP₀ ∝ Σᵢ Σₜ  min(
           wᵢ,ₜ·Â̂ᵢ ,
           clamp(wᵢ,ₜ , 1−ε , 1+ε )·Â̂ᵢ ).
```

The hope was that clipping keeps spicy out-of‑policy edits tamed.


### Where It Boo‑ked Up  

* **Per‑token weight misuse** – Importance sampling would normally re‑weight expectations taken over an **entire batch**, not one single look­ahead sample.
  In practice `wᵢ,ₜ` is calculated from one lucky coin flip and inserted into an *average rating*\*.  
  Any large fluctuation propagates immediately into gradients—especially bad when sentences roll up hundreds of words!

* **Unit mismatch** – Rewards belong to whole responses, yet gradients try adjusting every individual token according to a locally noisy factor.
  That misalignment makes the learner swing wildly and eventually “collapse” catastrophically (no going back).

* **High variances explode with longer sequences** – Since longer prompts involve more multiplicative terms,
the aggregate penalty hits a cliff if any single `wᵢ,ₜ` leaves its tiny window (`[1−ε , 1+ε]`).   
So even a well‑focused model suffers volatile uphill/downhill steps after only a few thousand updates.

### Defining the Ratio Correctly  

Instead of shortening edits just one word at a time,
GSOP springs back onto *the full likelihood*:  

```textmod
sᵢ(θ)= [ π_θ(yᵢ│x)/π_old(yᵢ│x)]^(1/|yᵢ|)
       = exp(
         [ Σₜ log π_θ(yᵢₜ│x,yᵢ,<ₜ)
           −Σₜ log π_old(...) ] / |yᵢ|
       ).
```

That exponent alone “averages” log‑probabilities, meaning every utterance gets **one smooth adjustment factor**, free from stray spikes generated anywhere inside it.


### Why Slice Once Is Better Than Many Small Cuts  

* Every letter benefits whatever glimpse accrues across its sentence —
  you’re no longer holding your breath looking ahead secantly.
* Clipping envelops → whole response → a single probability band `[z⟶+]`.
  With normalised `sᵢ` values typically close to `exp(v)` where \(v≈10^{-4}\),
the clipping window can safely sit at \(ε≈10^{-4}\), ten times narrower than GPT‑style grad windows.
* The “weight” term becomes uniform across all symbols:
  
```math
∇J_GSOP ∝ Σᵢ sᵢ · Â̂ᵢ · Σₜ ∇logπ_θ(yᵢₜ│⋯)
```
where the scalar `sᵢ` multiplies an average log‑gradient that contains every fresh isotope—and vanishes any spurious singular spikes.

## Side‑by–Side View  

| Feature | **GRP0** | **GSOP** |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Importance Ratio | Token‐wise \(w^\mathbf{i}_t(\theta)\) | Sequence‐wise \(s^\mathbf{i}(\theta)\) |
| Clipping Bound |\([\,1-\varepsilon\, ,\, 1+\varepsilon\,]\), ε≈0.2–0.27 | Same form but ε≈3e‑4–4e‑4 |
| Gradient Weighting | Token weights vary wildly (`w^t`) → noisy updates | All tokens share one mild scale (`s`) → smooth |
| Reward Alignment | Reward–advantage split per sentence versus per‐token update → unit mismatch | Reward + advance + weighting operate uniformly at sentence level |
| Practical Impact | Frequent “model collapse”, needs extra tricks such as Routing Replay for MoE models | Stably convergent even on giant sparse MoE architectures; eliminates Routing Replay |

> **Takeaway:** By correcting how we weigh predictions against rewards—moving from per‑token trickery back up onto full sequences—GSOP fixes the root cause that made even moderately sized WP teachers drift out total control.

## If You’re Building an RK8 Hook – Quick Cheat Sheet

- Always normalize rewards across responses rather than give every symbol its own estimate unless truly needed.
- Compute *sequence*-level ratios (`π_theta / π_old`) before breaking down gradients—that’s half your cost savings!
- Keep clipping ε tiny (~10⁻⁴); larger tubes only hurt GV flows.
- If you run Mixture-of-experts → skip.routing-replay entirely.
