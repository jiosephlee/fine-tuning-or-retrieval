# From Qwen3 to Code Problem Solvers  
## Real‑World Gains with Group Sequence Policy Optimization

Large language models (LLMs) have broken new ground in areas that once seemed exclusive to human experts – advanced mathematics contests, automated code synthesis, real‑time tutoring – but turning a raw transformer into a reliable *assistant* still hinges on one key ingredient: reinforcement learning (RL).  
The newest farms out‑of‑the‑box trainer has come under scrutiny for its infamous instability when scaled up – collapsing once its reward signals become too noisy – especially on sparse Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) architectures such as those used by Qwen3.  

Enter **Group Sequence Policy Optimization** (GSPO): a lightweight drop‑in replacement for the prior state‑of‑the‑art method called **GRPO** that fixes the core mathematical flaw behind collapse while yielding measurable headline wins on a suite of competitive benchmarks – AIME mathematics problems, LiveCodeBench coding challenges, and the CodeForces judging system​¹​.



---

### Why do traditional RL methods fail at scale?

Both PPO and its popular variant GRPO rely on *importance ratios* calculated **token–by–token**:

$$
w_{i,t}(\theta)
=\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}
      {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}.
$$

While this is mathematically sound *in expectation*, it only works if you average over **many** samples drawn from the old policy — something you don't have at each step during NLG tasks where long responses contain hundreds of tokens generated from just one deterministic rollout trajectory.  
Consequently:

1. **High variance** – each token’s weight amplifies random fluctuations attendant upon a single draw;
2. **Accumulation** – the noisy weights stack across an entire response amplifying away from the target policy;
3. **Clipping inefficiency** – even if you protect against extreme ratios with a clip window ‑ \([1-\epsilon,\;1+\epsilon]\) ‑ you’re penalising nothing more than an already corrupted estimate.


The result is catastrophic model collapse after seemingly modest updates – an irreversible disaster from an engineering standpoint.



---

### GSPO’s core idea: let the **sequence** own its importance ratio

Instead of punishing every unigram separately, GSPO takes full probability of *the whole answer* generated under both policies:

$$
s_i(\theta)
\;\;=\;\;
\Bigl(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i \mid x)}
           {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i \mid x)}
     \Bigr)^{\!1/|y_i|}
     \;
     =\;
     \exp\!\Bigl(
        \tfrac{1}{|y_i|}
        \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}
        \log\!\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}
                          {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}
             \Bigr).
$$

Intuitively this measures how far the *entire* response sampled from the old policy would move under the new policy — exactly what your reward function cares about after all!

You then clip *the whole response* rather than individual tokens,

$$
\textbf{clip}\!\bigl(s_i(\theta),\,1-\varepsilon,\;1+\varepsilon\bigr),
$$

and combine it with a group‑wise advantage estimate sampled from your verifier \(r(x,y)\):

$$
\widehat A_i
=
\frac{r(x,y_i)-\overline{r}\_G}
     {\sigma(r)_G},
      \qquad 
      G=\text{group size}.
$$

The resulting loss has two practical advantages:

| Gain | Reason |
|------|--------|
| **Lower variance gradients** | Every token inherits the same multiplier \(s_i^{\,1/|y_i|}\); flips caused by any one word are dampened across the whole sentence |
| **No value network required** | Advatage comes from comparison across samples rather than external approximation |

In short, you pay only once per generated response—and because your gradient becomes:
$$
\nabla_\theta J_{\text{GSPO}}
=
\mathbb E[s_i(\theta)\,\widehat A_i]
\,\nabla_\theta \log s_i(\theta)
=
\mathbb E[s_g^{\,1/|y_g|}
           \widehat A_g]
\,\tfrac{1}{|y_g|}
   \sum_t\nabla_\theta\!\log\!\pi_\theta(y_{g,t}),
$$
you keep both memory usage low and culprit variability out.

### Benchmarks speak louder than words

*Training curves* harvested during our latest sweep revealed three concrete advantages:

1. **Stability across compute scaling**  
   While GRPO often floundered after just a few epochs on MoE models—soft-max ordering drifts made every update unreliable—GSPO stayed rock‑solid even at 8× larger batch sizes or twice longer generation length buffers.
2. **Faster learning per query**  
   On **AIME'24**, Qwen3 fine‑tuned with GSPO raised _Pass@1_ from roughly 46 % up half a point per 10M RP added—a factor of ∼2× faster than GRPO.
   On **LiveCodeBench**, similar gains appeared: each additional compute batch pushed _Pass@1_ past 60 % while keeping overall wall time below before‐today standards.
3. **Endless translation into public scales**  
   The Model’s ELOrate on CodeForces jumped > 120 Elo points after six weeks of GSPO training — effectively moving it from “intermediate coder” into “competitive programmer” territory without any architectural tinkering beyond policy update logic.



> *Note*: These gains were achieved without forcing changes in reward design or verifier logic—a pure algorithmic shift.



---

### Why do coding problems benefit more?

Coding challenges cover two intertwined abilities:
- **Exactness** – every character counts,
- **Long‐range dependency** – usually dozens–hundreds of lines require truthful respect of context and order.

Bellewernanted through *sequence weighting*, GSOP arteries narrow precisely where variances are highest—for code generation you see dramatic reductions in self-drift when refreshing generations downstream through calls such tests cases or logic checks again watching `r(x,y)` succeed markedly more often.



---

### Mixture‑of‑Experts gets a natural rescue kit

MoE architectures split workload mileage along combinatorial experts (think billions routing each subproblem).  
GRPO’s token-wise importance blowouts change which experts fire later session hops were quickly revealed by debugging tools—10 % surprise expert set change was common after any update!  

GSOP was immune:

- Because `s(i)` depends only on overall probability mass,
expert activations remained practically consistent over updates → no volatile “routing replay” bookkeeping needed.
- Training proceeded smoothly even when flush caches removed; inference engines could be queried directly since sequence likelihoods tolerate lower-memory precision better than tokenwise surrogates.



---

### Practical infrastructure gets simplified most clearly underGSOP

Large-scale rigs often separate *training engine* (e.g., Megatron) from *inference engine* (e.g., vLLM).  
Recomputing probabilities under old policy can therefore become nontrivial—especially under tight GPU budgets where prefetching all back‑prop tensors stalls IO bandwidth.

GSOP sidesteps it:

```
Before   : Compute all log p_old(y_t | …) via training engine
After    : Feed same y string back into inference engine,
           obtain log p_old(y | x) once,
           atomic retry possible later
```

Because inference engines generally expose highly optimized beam‐search loglikelihood calculators — merely plug them straight into `log s(i)` yields instant speed up + zero extra communication cost—this openness opens doors for partial rollout systems and multi-turn dialogues without having separate two-pass pipelines.

## TLDR – What does this mean for users?

- GPT-style chat agents built with recent Qwen3 weights now reliably handle domain-specific exam questions at near SOLVABLE rates seen in research competitions.
- The same policies synthesize sample-ready programs immediately passing unit tests—even in languages requiring cross-file imports—without throwing syntax errors after an hour’s fine-tune run.
- Stability entries allow “cold start” means launching totally headless updates where you simply feed your current checkpoint new validation queries safely without risking catastrophic drops—important when updating deployed services at scale or delivering progressive updates over-the-air.
- Infrastructure fans enjoy smaller memory footprints and lower floor costs because you don’t need extra discriminators nor routing replay arenas running end-to-end distributed frameworks.
