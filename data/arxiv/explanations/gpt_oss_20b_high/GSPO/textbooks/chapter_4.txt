\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

Chapter 4: **Group Relative Policy Optimisation (GRP)**  

Reinforcement learning has emerged as a vital tool for pushing language models towards higher‑level reasoning capabilities.\footnote{See \citet{o1,dpsk-r1,qwq32b,qwen3} for early applications of RL to large language models.}  
A major obstacle encountered during large‑scale RL is maintaining stable updates when optimisation moves far away from data collected under an older policy.\footnote{\citet{grpo} emphasises instability arising after relatively few training iterations when using standard Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO).}\markboth{}{Ch.~\ref{sec:intro}}\par  

In classical PPO one keeps a running estimate of state‑value functions in order to reduce variance when estimating advantages.\footnote{Eq.~(1.) defines $\mathcal J_{\text{PPO}}$ below and highlights dependence upon $\widehat A_t$, estimated typically via an auxiliary network.\citep{ppo}}\par  

To eliminate this auxiliary network while retaining a tunable scale factor,\citet{grpo} proposed **Group Relative Policy Optimisation**.\footnote{\citet{grpo} exhibited significant empirical reductions in wall‑clock time compared with vanilla PPO at little loss in performance.\citep[cf.~Sec.~2]{grpo}}\par  

Below we describe this algorithm rigorously and illuminate why it constitutes a valid principled adaptation of importance sampling concepts specifically suited for language‑generation tasks.



----------------------------------------------------------------------
### Generating \(G\) Replies per Query  

Let $x$ denote a user query sampled i.i.d.\ from $\mathcal D$ during reinforcement learning.\footnote{\citet{o1}}  
Training proceeds lazily over batches drawn from $\pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}(\cdot\,|\,x)$, where $\theta_{\rm old}$ are frozen parameters taken prior to the current gradient iteration.\footnote{\citet[Ch.~4]{grpo}}  
For every query $x$, the learner generates **\(G\)** candidate responses $\bigl\{ y_1,\dots ,y_G\bigr\}$ independently according}\footnote{\citet[§4]{grpo}}  
to the categorical distribution produced by $\pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}$, resulting in  
$$
   y_i=\bigl(y_{i,1},\,y_{i,2},\,\dots ,\,y_{i,\lvert y_i\rvert}\bigr)
   \;\sim\;
   \pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}\!\bigl(\cdot\,|\,x\bigr), 
   \qquad i=1,\dots ,G .
$$ 
The set $\bigl\{\lvert y_1\rvert,\dots ,\lvert y_G\rvert\bigr\}$ is usually heterogeneous since different samples discover different termination points according to their own predicted termination token distribution.



----------------------------------------------------------------------
### Token–Density Weight \(w_{\,i,t}(\!\theta)\)

Each reply $y_i$ consists of tokens indexed sequentially $t=1,\dots ,\lvert y_i\rvert$.  
For every position $t$ Gregsa defines an **importance‑ratio** comparing how much more probable it has become under the *current* parameter vector $\theta$ versus under its *old* counterpart:
$$
   w_{\,i,t}\!(\theta)
   =
   \frac{
      \pi_{\theta}\!\bigl(
          y_{i,t}\mid x , y_{i,<t}
      \bigr)
   }{
      \pi_{\theta_{\rm old}}\!\bigl(
          y_{i,t}\mid x , y_{i,<t}
      \bigr)
   } .
$$
This quantity reflects how sharply two policies disagree when selecting **that** particular next symbol whilst conditioned on identical past contexts $y_{i,<t}$.\footnote{\citet[Eq.~2]{grpo}}  
Because sampling happens once per exact subsequent symbol its denominator equals one independent draw – therefore $w_{\,i,t}$ does **not** benefit from averaging over many samples, which breaks the foundations established by classic Importance Sampling theory.



----------------------------------------------------------------------
### Advantage Estimate \(\widehat A_i\)

Stability demands normalising raw rewards before feeding them into gradient updates.\footnote{
“In evaluating several alternatives belonging to parallel groups one ought naturally remove scale effects.” 
– c’mon – pseudonym rule.” }\citep[Sec.~4]{grpo}\\
For every key response $y_i$, its scalar reward is reported:\,
$$
   r(x,y_i)\;\in~[0,\,+1]
$$ 
by a verifier network independent of $\theta$.  
Because absolute rewards fluctuate dramatically across dissimilar queries ($r$ may differ widely for unrelated question families), rewards are reshaped *inside* generations:
$$
   \widehat A_i
   :=
     \frac{
         r(x,y_i)- 
         {\color{gray}{ {\rm mean}\!\bigl(
              r(x,y_1),\,r(x,y_2),\,\dots ,\,r(x,y_G)
         )}}
     }{
         {\color{gray}{ {\rm std}\!\bigl(
                r(x,y_1),\,r(x,y_2),\,…\,r(x,y_G)
          )}}
     } .
$$
Every token belonging interchangeably sized‐demand retains the same salience:
$$
   \widehat A _{{ i },t}
   =
   {\color{red}{\widehat A _ i}}
,\qquad t=1,\dots ,{\lvert y _ i\rvert}.
$$
