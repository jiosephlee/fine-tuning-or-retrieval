\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

Chapter 3: # Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT): Principle Inheritance  

Large‑scale foundation models encode a wealth of knowledge during their vast pretraining regimes. Adapting these models efficiently requires preserving much of that learned structure while still allowing flexibility for downstream tasks. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) achieves precisely this balance by replacing the usual additive update rule with a **multiplicative change** driven by an ***orthogonal*** transformation applied uniformly across all neurons in a given layer. This section explains how OFT’s reparameterization maintains pretraining knowledge, why an identity initialization makes sense, and how the optimisation proceeds under strict orthogonality constraints.



## Reparameterising \( \protect{\mathbf{W}^{\text{new}}=\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^{\text{old}}}\)

In standard fine‑tuning one learns an incremental update \( \Delta \mathbf{W}\) such that \( \mathbf{W}^{\text{new}}=\mathbf{W}^{\text{old}}+\Delta \mathbf{W}\).  This additive perturbation changes both the length and direction of every column vector in \( \mathbf{W}\), thereby potentially distorting relationships that were established during pretraining (e.g., similarity relations between output units).

OFT eschews this approach in favour of a **multiplicative** reparameterization
\[
  \boxed{\;\mathbf{W}^{\text{new}}=\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^{\text{old}}\;}
  \tag{1}
\]
where \( \mathbf{R}\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\) is a learnable **orthogonal** matrix (\(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^\top=\mathbf{I}_d\)).  Since every column vector is left‑multiplied by the same rotation,
any inner product between two columns \(w_{i}^{(0)}\) and \(w_{j}^{(0)}\) satisfies
\[
  \langle\,\mathbf{R}w_{i}^{(0)},\,\mathbf{R}w_{j}^{(0)}\,\rangle
  =w_{i}^{(0)\top}\underbrace{\bigl(\mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{R}\bigr)}_{\!=\!\mathbf I_d}\!w_{j}^{(0)}
  =w_{i}^{(0)\top} w_{j}^{(0)} .
  \tag{2}
\]
Thus pairwise angles – a key geometric quantity capturing semantic relationships among neurons – are literally *preserved* throughout fine‑tuning, no matter how large the parameters change.  At the same time, if the full rank transformation \( \mathbf R \) has full Gamut coverage then it can realize any re‑orientation of the neuron space; hence OFT offers substantial expressivity while still honouring prior geometry.



## Zero / Identity Initialization Rationale

Optimisation algorithms tend to converge more stably when starting near a sensible point in parameter space—especially when hard algebraic constraints are involved.  For OFT this translates into two intertwined design choices:

* **Zero‑weight updates** – analogous to initializing LoRA’s low‑rank matrices with zeros – would amount essentially to keeping every row or column unchanged until gradients accumulate enough strength to move it away from identity or zero.
* **Identity transformation** – for our multiplicative rule it simply means choosing an initial \( \mathbf R^{(0)}=\mathbf I_d \).

Because
\( \boxed{\;\overline{\mathbf W}^{\,0}
   = (\overline{\phi })(\overline{\psi }) }
      s.t.\;
      (\overline{\phi })= (\overline{\psi })=I_d ,
    }
        the revised forward pass exactly reproduces the pretrained behaviour before gradient descent kicks in:
\( {\bf z}=({I_d}{\,W^{{init}}\!})^\top {\bf x}=({ {W^{init}}})^{\top}{\,x} .
   \)
Consequently any change comes solely from learning \( \Delta \!\mathcal Q(t)\)-informed rotations rather than arbitrary large deviations.



### Why Identity Trumps Zero

If one were to start from a zero‑rotating matrix (\(\mathbf R^{(0)}=\bfzero_d\)), early iterations would output almost no signal—rendering training ineffective until numerical regularisation forces non‑zero entries elsewhere.  An identity matrix guarantees that even before any gradient steps every neuron remains functional and has clearly defined direction with respect to its pretrained counterpart.



## Training Dynamics Under Strict Orthogonality Constraints

The crux of OFT’s training routine lies in updating \( \mathbf R \) **while guaranteeing**

1. **Exact preservation** of its orthogonality (\(\|\!\cdot\,|_F=1,\;\!\|\cdot||_2=1\f)) at *every* iteration;
2. A relatively small set of latent parameters that drives these transformations.


### Cayley Transform Parameterisation

Let us write any real square matrix \(Q=(Q_1,\dots ,Q_d)\) satisfying \(Q=-Q^\top\).  The map
\( Q~{\longmapsto}~ {\bf R}
    :=(\boldsymbol I+Q)(\boldsymbol I-Q)^{-1}
    ~~~~(\star)
    \)
is known as the Cayley transform  – it bijectively maps skew‑symmetric matrices onto special orthogonal matrices excluding their eigenvalue equalities ±1 if $\boldsymbol I-Q$ remains nonsingular.\footnote{
In practice gradients are taken with respect reasond just-in-time reformatting whether $\textstyle(\boldsymbol I-Q)$ remain invertible during optimization – typical optimisers such as Adam damp large steps avoiding near‑singular regime.
}
Keyly this representation carries only \(d(d-1)/2\) trainable variables (\(Q_-\!matricesemantics), whereas naïvely parametrising every entry in *R* would require \(d^2.\)

### Why Strict Orthogonality Is Beneficial

Preserving exact metrics not only conserves pretraining geometry but also keeps spectral norms unchanged, guaranteeing bounded outputs across layers—a boon for convergence stability especially when applying vanilla optimisers like Adam without additional clipping or normalisation tricks.\footnote{
Recent works have shown spectral norm maintenance mitigates exploding gradients over transformer depth—a feature automatically satisfied here since Fourier-like rotations do not alter singular values.
}


## Take‑aways

1 *Reparameterisation*: Instead of adding noise we'd rotate the entire neuron space via an orthogonal operator—pairwise geometry’s ghost remains unaltered while still having plentiful degrees‐of‑freedom for downstream adaptation.
2 *Identity start*: Thereby we literally begin fine‑tuning from an identical pretrained state—early epochs do not unknowingly depart telescoping past converged patterns.
3 *Strictness through Cayley*: The skew‑symmetric latent variable confers efficient optimisation under hard constraints—ensuring consistent behaviour across epochs without ever leaving the special‐orthogonal manifold.


These principles make OFT preferable whenever preserving hierarchical learned structure outweighs raw parameter count savings—a tension later alleviated further by butterfly factorisation—yet at this stage they highlight why simple multiplicative rotations already carry an impressive blend of expressivity and stability.
