Question:
"Section 6 hints usefulness of fast network topologies beyond Cooley‑Tukey for improved factorisations.

Answer:
**Answer**

BOFT replaces the block‐diagonal OFT weights with a *product* of many sparse orthogonal factors whose sparsity pattern follows the classic *butterfly* network used in Cooley–Tukey FFTs.  
Section 5/6 of the paper explains why this choice looks appealing—the butterfly has only  
\(O(d\log d)\) non‑zero entries while still guaranteeing that every input dimension can influence every output dimension after the cascade of \(\log_2 d\) stages, much like an FFT builds a dense \(d\times d\) transform from \(\log_2 d\) sparse stages.
