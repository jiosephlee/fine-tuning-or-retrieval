# Evolution From Vanilla RNNs → Gated Units → Transformers  

The earliest formulation for predicting a symbolic sequence was the vanilla
Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), which updates a hidden state \(h_{t}\)
only through an affine transformation followed by an element‑wise non‑linearity:
\(h_{t}=g(\mathbf{W}_{h}\mathbf{x}_{t}+R_{h}h_{t-1})\).
Practitioners quickly realized that such networks could learn very short
dependencies (the “vanishing gradient” problem), limiting their usefulness
for language modeling where longer dependencies routinely appear
(see~\citep{Hochreiter}).  
A direct remedy was introduced by Hochreiter & Schmidhuber:
the **Long Short‑Term Memory** (LSTM), whose single scalar memory cell
\(c_{t}\in\mathbb{R}\) is updated through an additive *constant error carousel*
\
\(c_{t}=f_{t}\,c_{t-1}+i_{t}\,z_{t}\;\text{and}\;
 h_{t}=o_{t}\,\psi(c_{t})\),
with a **forget gate** \(f_{t}=σ(\tilde{f}_{t})\), an **input gate**
\(i_{t}=σ(\tilde{i}_{t})\), an **output gate**
\(o_{t}=σ(\tilde{o}_{t})\), and a non‑linear cell input \(z_{t}=\varphi(\tilde{z}_{t})\).
Through this design each timestep can *carry* its error signal backward
unmodified across arbitrarily long horizons,
save for weight matrices acting multiplicatively on gate inputs.
Because every unit handled an independent scalar value,
the model could compute very deep stackings—upwards hundreds of layers—
without instruction overflow.
Figuratively speaking, the gates manage how much new information enters
the cell (\(i_{t}\)), how much past information shears toward oblivion (\(f_{t}\)),
and how much “visible” signal emerges (\(o_{t}\))!  
These innovations resolved an immediate practical bottleneck,
leveraging recurrent cores deep downstream into early GPT‑0 series
(the precursor of modern large language models)~\citep{Graves}.  
Nevertheless, even with massive model sizes transformers rose during
2017~\citep{Vaswani}, because self‑attention offers *parallelizable*
token–token interactions inherently decoupled across spatial positions.
Consequently transformer blocks could efficiently process sequences whose
length exceeded tens of thousands tokens—a realm still outstripped by classic RNNs,
due only primarily to their serial computation path:
every subsequent hidden state depends deleteriously on its immediate predecessor
via symmetric recurrence matrices.\footnote{
Linear recurrence layers introduced later~\citep{Orvieto} underscored 
that even simpler gates can be aggressively parallelized when coupled 
with adequate signal cancellation mechanisms.}
Thus it remains obvious why sequence modeling research open-endedly pursued two complementary axes:
improve *expressive power* without sacrificing computational efficiency—
an endeavour addressed directly below.



# Why Large Language Models Historically Relied Upon Recurrent Cores  

Prior to self‑attention breakthroughs,
most next‑token predictors emerged from **recurrent cores**.
They possessed two decisive advantages precisely tailored toward language:
firstly they feature an **internal state buffer** capable of utterances;
for instance, number neurons~\citep{Lakretz}, linguistic neurons~\citep{Bau},
or sentiment units~\citep{Radford} were discovered serendipitously in single-cell behavior,
underscoring tiny transformer‐free memories embedding endogamous knowledge.
Secondly recurrent layers permit explicit tracking structures such 
as updating or revising what has been stored—the `memorization’ process signals outflows via `forget' channels similarly exploited during program evaluation problems~\citep{Zaremba}.
In reinforcement learning pipelines (AlphaStar diameters),
these very abilities caused variance reductions worth massive physical simulations more efficiently than blockwise attention
in those low data regimes.\footnote{
Despite being serial,
the interleaving nature allows us explicit control over token ordering,
essential under RL’s horizon extensions where time steps represent agent/environment interactions rather than raw token indices.\result}
Consequently most benchmark “large” language models up through GPT‑3 epitomized favorite recurrent skinned designs prior costcurves tallied immersion studies resisted replacing faster yet complicated attention layers until ~2022 advancement throughput curves took hold.
