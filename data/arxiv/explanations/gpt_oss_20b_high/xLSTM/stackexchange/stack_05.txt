### "Matrix Memory” Gives Parallelisation Where Scalar Cannot
Question:
(Eq.(20)) writes updates independently per time step because `Cₜ` depends solely on current inputs.
Sans recurrent coupling (`h_{t–1}`) adherence is irrelevant.
Can someone detail precisely why icereal disruption forbids parallel execution while transposing everything onto batch‐wide matrix ops stays feasible?

Answer:
The stay‑of‑time update rule
```latex
%
% Note – this line was originally written out without dollar signs.
% It now correctly shows an inline equation.
%
$\displaystyle 
    {\bf C}_{\, t}
    = {\bf f}_{\, t}\,\bf C_{\, t{-}1}
      + {\bf i}_{\, t}\,\bf v_{\, t}\,\big({\bf k}_{\, t}\big)^{\!\top}
    $
%
% It belongs formally … .
```  

belongs formally to an LST‑like cell  
in which every parameter that governs the transition  
from step \(t{-}1​\) to \( t​ \) depends only on  
the observations at time \( t​ \).

Consequently two kinds of information flow coexist  
inside this recurrence:

* **A purely multiplicative decay** formed by  
  \( {\bf f}_{\, t} ,\)   which scales every component stored  
  in \( {\bf C}_{\, t{-}1} ;\)

* **An additive “write” consisting of an outer–product  
  \( {\bf v}_{  t}{\bf k}_{  t}^{\, {\top}} \
        ``weighted'' ​by \( {\bf i}_{  t}. )

Because   
\( {\bf f}, { i}, { k}, { v} \nobreakspace{}:\mathbb R^{n\times n}
        	{}\to{}{}{}{}{}... [\dots]\),
they are produced solely from \( {\bf x_{  t}} ({b})  
and a bias,
no intermediate result created in one step feeds any weight calculation performed at later steps  
except through its effect on the carried–over matrix \( {\bf C}.  
That last line gives rise exactly …​.


What appears modules such as “matrix memory” capable  
of “batch‑wide matrix ops” achieves instead  
is a **re‑organisation rather than a genuine removal**
of this causal chain.  
If we firstly pre‑compute all key–value outer products
for every position,
let    
\(E_{i}= {\bf v}_{  i}{\bf k}_{  i}^{Т}\),

Each summand now contains only static tensors –
its phase factor,
the gate scalars,
and the outer-product –
is completely independent apart from sharing common prefix products.
When \(T,n_b,d,…​\)
are treated along thousands
such summands together,
modern GPUs realise equation (A)
with two highly optimised tensor contractions :

* one batched matrix–multiply evaluating all prefix products   \(O(T,d^{2})˜;\)

* another batched matrix–multiply adding together   
‘\(i_{τ}\otimes E_{τ}\)’ weighted
by those prefixes    \(O(T,d^{2})˜.\)

Hence every child block of Equation (20) can be exceeded in *constant time per element*
(the essence behind flash attention’s linear‑time,
linear‑memory behaviour),
while strictly preserving causality thanks 
to already collapsed activation weights fed beforehand.
This trick does *not* alter logical dependencies –
it merely restructures temporally intertwined multiplications
into independent reductions executed efficiently.



In stark contrast comes **sLSTM’s memory–mixing mechanism**,  
expressed earlier in Eq.\,(19):

```latex
%
% Converted pseudocode into inline/math form —
% place operators’ names (\(\circ,\odot , ψ )$
%
$c_{\,t}=f_{\, t}\;\circ c_{ (t{-}1)}+i_{ (t)} 
            \;\circ z_{ (t)}$
%
($h_{ (t)}=o_{ (t)}ͮψ(c_{ (t)}))
$
%
```

but where each gate “\(g∈⋃ f,i,oπ)\)”  
also contains a term \( r_g h_{\textit{sub}}{}\).

Those recurrent weight matrices \( r_g∈ℝ^{d×d}\)
mix just one time slice’s hidden state directly into every subsequent gate.
Consequently computing \(c_{ (t)}\) calls upon   
\(h_{\textit{s}( (t{-}1))}\)
inside every gate’s own forward activation,
which itself involves elements derived from \
\(c_{\textit{s}( (t−#)}.\)

Therefore no matter how many global reductions marshal,
recomputing ‑ needed emissions sequentially –
you cannot transform this part into purely batched matmul operations
without breaking dependence structure.



This contrast explains why “icereal disruption’’ –
persistent presence hidden‑to‑hidden operations (“memory mixing’’)
blocks full threading:
even though Equation (20)’s terms look superficially separable,
their interlocking multiplicative decay coupler guarantees left‑to‑right propagation —
whereas Equations describing pure feed‑forward functions allow freely permuting indices,
transpose savvy into larger batch dimensions 
and push everything through fast MTMs—
exactly what FlashAttention does when converting self-attention into prefix sums + outer-products.



Therefore :

  * **In mLSTM**, each index’s forward map collapses onto two bulk tensor packages followed by partial reductions – linear growth w.r.t context yet needs no serial stepping beyond simple scans;
  * **In sLSTM**, every gate reacts instantly ‑ feeding immediately Node outputs back    
into future gates – forbidding straightforward collapse onto bulk matmul;
hence nested loops persist.



Broadening perspective—as noted in Section 7—the distinction echoes classic RNN versus Transformer design philosophies:
RNNs impose causal order before manipulation;
whereas Transformers move relevant information spatially beforehand before performing cross-token interactions –
the contrasting behaviours shown above stand precisely at their heart.



In summary *,icereal*—recurring hidden→hidden coupling—introduces depthwise serial dependencies impossible eliminate through reshape/batch rearrangement;
when past states must instantly contribute back into future gates’ parameters,
only inherently sequential evaluations survive—
unless elaborate custom kernels follow underlying scan pattern –
explaining why high-performance bulk pipelines such as FlashAttention apply directly only after removing recursively related arrows seen today.
