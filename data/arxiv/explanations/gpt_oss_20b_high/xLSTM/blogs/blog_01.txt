# Reinventing the Classic LSTM: Meet xmLSTM’s Modern Twist

## Why revisit an L‑shaped wonder?

The long short–term memory (LSTM) came out almost two decades ago as a masterful trick against vanishing gradients—a deep learning staple until self‑attention burst onto the scene a few years later. Transformers now dominate large language models because their attention mechanism scales linearly (or quasi–linearly) with context length while keeping weights strictly tabulated between tokens. Yet a fundamental power remains locked inside every well‑behaved RNN that a nervous mind gets out of its head—state tracking—and nobody has found a way to make this trait survive the rigorously tuned billions‑parameter arena without paying a huge price in speed or precision loss.\n\nThe goal of **x‑Longest Short‑Term Memory (x\(lstm\))** was simple yet bold:
> “Can we let a single architectural family reach Transformer‑level performance simply by adding two well‑placed ideas?”

Their answer pivots around three classic weaknesses of vanilla LSTMs—memory revision inability, scalar storage limits and inevitably serial processing—and shows how tiny changes bring those deficits under control while preserving orthogonal benefits such as easy differentiation through layers and interpretability (`number neurons`, `linguistic neurons`, …).

## Two revolutionary ingredients

### Exponential Gating + Normalization “Stabilized”

Traditional gates are sigmoid‐bounded → close‐to 0 limits gradient flow before reaching new information.
Instead take gates directly out of exponentials,
```math
i(t)=e^{\,\tilde i(t)} ,\quad
f(t)\in\{e^{\,\tilde f(t)},\;\sigma(\tilde f(t))\}
```
so both forget/insert power grows multiplicatively rather than additively.
\nChange trick lies next—the “normalizer” `n(t)` counts expected intensity ahead:
```math
n(t)=f(t)n(t−1)+i(t);
```
With it the hidden becomes
```math
h(t)=o(t)\,\frac{c(t)}{\,n(t)} .
```
Because raw exponentials explode numerically,
a lightweight “stabilizer” tracks log f/f sum (`mₜ`) ensuring `e^{…}` never runs off‐road.
What does this buy?
– In extreme contexts older memories die out gracefully yet recover when newer data sits tighter inside window,
– Eqlickingly unwashed keeps gradients stable,
and an additional diagonal gate mix preserves exactly *which* student stores what effectively inter‑cellally during multi‑heads setups — introduced next below along with head‐specific “memory mixing”.

### From Scalars To Matrices – Rememory Structural Upgrade

To escape scalar compression propose elevating cell state `c` into a full \(d\times d\) weight matrix \(C\).
You are now storing outer products \(v_kk_k^T\) (key · value), just like fast weight popularizers:
```math
C(t)=f(t)\,C(t−1)+i(t)\,v_kk_k^T .
```
That’s nothing more exotic than computing an accumulated covariance slowly weighted toward recent items.
Retrieval follows:
```math
ĥ(t)=\frac{C(t)\!\cdot q}{\,\max(|n^Tq|,\!1)} ,
```
∈ means dot product division gives us how close current query aligns against past keys—
exactly the notion behind attention scores—but all done once per token thanks to absence of hidden dependencies.
More salient benefits appear immediately:
* Fully **parallelizable**, analogous to FlashAttention/GLA.
* No additional trainable parameters are needed beyond usual Wₚ/Wₖ/Wᵥ…,
so increased capacity comes “for free”.
* Stronger homogeneous storage makes our tiny network resilient even against rare token spikes observed empirically during large vocab experiments.*

Putting them together yields two families:
* **s³ (L): scalar ↔ mixed heads, strong memory shuffling**
* **m³ (M)** , full matrix multiplication bolting out compute locality versus prefix modelling

Those families then hairbrush into residual block backbones—the core architecture behind many competitive large language models (`preLayerNorm`, up-projection tricks).

## Architectural sales pitch

{x\(lstm\)} embraces modern practice without discarding proven tricks:
```text
[Token] → PreLayerNorm → Block → Residual -> … -> Block -> Final Softmax
         ↘            ↑
       Gate conv / Inv ⊙ proj…
```
A few architectural choices differ per block type:
* `s³ (L)` blocks use *post-up-projection*: last non-linearity returns unchanged dimensionality while using inner mixing gates first,
safe for sparse heads.
* `m³ (M)` blocks leverage *pre-up-projection*: occupy higher dimensional space entirely so background similarity comparisons cascade naturally—
the intuition behind many recent Linear Transformers such as Performer/Hyena.
Final residual stacking assures gradient flows through an entire stack after dozens/hundreds of layers—a necessity for any billion-plus parameter model surface area work online!
