Chapter 3: 3️⃣ Bytes Versus Tokens  

Large language models today rely heavily on an intermediate representation called a *token*. In most transformer‑based systems this token is chosen by applying a deterministic pre‑processing step—usually involving Byte‑Pair Encoding (BPE)—to raw text written in UTF‑8. Tokens are members of a fixed vocabulary \( V_{\text{tok}} \), whose elements are themselves contiguous byte sequences drawn from an utterance during training an unsupervised learning phase performed outside the encoder/decoder network itself. In contrast, *patches*—the core units exploited by Byte Latent Transformers (BLT)—are groups of bytes constructed dynamically during preprocessing **without** ever consulting an external dictionary. A correctly formulated chapter begins by contrasting these two design paradigms and then delves into why certain algorithmic properties—that is intrinsic to tokenization—create inefficiencies for autoregressive language modeling during inference; finally it demonstrates how BLT circumvents these limitations through an “incrementally designed” attachable grouping mechanism.
