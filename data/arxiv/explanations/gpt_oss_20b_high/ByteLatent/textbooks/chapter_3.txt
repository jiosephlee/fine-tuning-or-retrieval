Chapter 3: Conceptual Foundations of Patching  

Large language models routinely rely on a pre‑tokenised representation of text before any neural computation takes place.  The existence of a fixed vocabulary imposes two intertwined constraints—each element ingested by the Transformer carries an identical cost regardless of its complexity, and the final projection layer must accommodate every symbol that ever appears during training.  When one wishes instead to learn directly from raw Unicode bytes—the “exact” material reality that modern software ultimately manipulates—these constraints become glaringly inefficient.  The core insight behind Byte‑Latent Transformers is that relevance–aware computation can hide itself simply within how sequences are partitioned into higher‑level units called **patches** rather than one‑size‑fits‑all tokens​.  Patches begin life as arbitrary contiguous groups of bytes whose lengths adapt locally according to signal predictability while remaining transparent enough for sequential processing during both training and inference​¹​&nbsp;&nbsp;(Section 2 introduces several concrete strategies for this end‑to‑end partitioning).

# Formal Definition of a Patch‑Patching Function  

Let \(\mathbf{x}=\{x_{i}\}\) denote an input sequence where \(x_{i}\in \mathcal V=\{0,\dots ,255\}\) is an individual byte and \(i=1,\dots ,n\) indexes position.  
A **patch‑function**  
\(f_{p}\colon \mathcal V^{n}\rightarrow {\mathbb N}\times {\mathbb N}^{*}\)
produces a sequence \(\mathbf p=\{p_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\, m<n,\)
with each \(p_{j}=\{x_{s},\,x_{s+1},\dots ,x_{t}\}\subseteq \mathbf x.\)
Formally this corresponds to assigning every byte a binary flag
\(b_{i}\in\{0,1\}\), where \(b_{i}=1\) signals the inception
of a fresh patch beginning at position \(i.\)  
Thus
\(b_{i}=f^{b}((x_1,\dots ,x_n),\, i)\)
for some deterministic function \(f^{b},\)
and 
\(m=\sum_{i=1}^{n} b_i.\)  
The average patch size,
\[
\nu_\text{avg}= \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m}|p_j|}{m},
\]
directly dictates how many “outer” Transformer steps must run
for any length‑\(n'\) portion \( \tilde{\mathbf x}\subseteq \mathbf x.\)  
Since computation grows linearithmically w.r.t additions conducted per *patch*, longer patches directly reduce Compute FLOPs while simultaneously demanding richer representations provided by the local encoder pathway discussed later.​²​

### # Byte Preprocessing Pipeline  

At the very beginning every language‑model instance first receives an unstructured stream of raw UTF‑8 bytes $\pmb{x}=\{x_1,x_2,\dots ,x_n\}$ that represents a document or a prompt plus its target continuation. BLT eschews the conventional fixed‑vocabulary tokenizer that would collapse this stream into subword tokens prior to learning any language signal. Instead it records every individual byte verbatim while postponing any grouping decision until an *entropy‑aware* segmentation module processes the incoming sequence during data loading or during generation itself.\

The goal of this segmentation step is twofold:</li>
1) **Dynamic consolidation** – contiguous runs whose next‑byte statistics are highly predictable (low Shannon entropy) are aggregated into one *patch*, while runs where predicting the immediate next byte requires significant uncertainty are split so that every ensuing model step focuses computation where it matters most.\</li>
2) **Uniform processing budget** – regardless of how blurry or crisp a particular segment looks, every capitalized character still consumes at least one *global* computation step once that segment has been mapped into its compact representation.\  

The practical realization employs one lightweight auto‑regressive byte language model trained **separately** on almost identical data mixtures (§5). Its per‑byte entropy estimate predicts whether a new byte starts a fresh conceptual context:
$$
H(x_t)=\sum _{v\in{\cal V}}p_e(x_t=v\,|\,{\bf x}_{<t})\,\log p_e(x_t=v\,|\,{\bf x}_{<t})
$$
where $p_e$ denotes the probability mass function learned by this auxiliary network over the entire 256‑byte vocabulary ${\cal V}$.
Depending on a chosen global threshold $\theta_g$ and an optionally monotonic relative rise criterion $\theta_r$, every position satisfying  
$$
H(x_t) > \theta_g    \qquad{\rm OR}\qquad H(x_t)-H(x_{t-1}) > \theta_r 
$$
is declared a boundary between two adjacent patches.
The resulting list $\pmb p=\{p_1,p_2,\dots ,p_m\}$ captures arbitrary lengths ($m<n$), enabling later work units — namely *local encoders*, *latent transformers* and *local decoders* — to operate directly on $\pmb p$ without any fixed vocabulary lookup tables.\

Once established during pre‑training timeouts (where the whole batch and its entropies are available), these boundaries determine all subsequent inputs fed into BLT modules upon both training backward passes and real‑time inference generation rounds.



### # Local Encoder Role  

The **Local Encoder** $\mathcal E$ translates extremely fine‑grained byte observations into compact *latent* vectors suitable for processing by the large **Global Latent Transformer** ($\mathcal G$).  
Its structure mirrors a standard causal transformer stack ($l_{\mathcal E}\ll l_{\mathcal G}$), punctuated between each self‑attention layer by an additional *cross‑attention* module that acts as an adaptive pooling operator constrained exclusively over bytes belonging to a particular output patch \(p_j\).\

#### Byte Embedding & N‑gram Hash Augmentation  
Each incoming byte value $b_k$ is emitted through an embedding matrix $E^x:\{0,\dots ,255\}\mapsto{\Bbb R}^{h_{\mathcal E}}$, yielding vector \(x_k=E^x(b_k)\).
To equip these vectors with richer contextual senses than plain symbols alone would allow,
the model queries hashes over short contiguous windows.
For every position \(k\)
the component n‑grams  
\(g^{(n)}_k=\{b_{k-n+1},...,b_k\}\),  
for \(n=3,\dots ,8,\) terminate immediately when \(k<n.\)
Each such gram feeds through a single hash function:
$$
{\rm Hash}(g^{(n)}_k)= {\rm RollPolyHash}\!\bigl(g^{(n)}_k\bigr)\;\bmod |E_n^{hash}| ,
$$
thereby folding potentially astronomic combinatorics into ordinary lookup buckets 
\(E_n^{hash}\in{\Bbb R}^{|E_n^{hash}|\times h_{\mathcal E}}\).
The resulting hash vector simply adds componentwise:
$$
e_k=x_k+\sum_{n=3}^{8}
   E_n^{hash}\bigl({\rm Hash}(g^{(n)}_k)\bigr),
$$
which is finally normalized before being injected back into the encoder stack.\  

This dual strategy furnishes “intrinsic” lexical signals – including locality revealed through n‑gram realisations – respecting typographic constraints without ever exposing non–byte symbols.



#### Cross‑Attention Pooling Mechanism  
After each self‑attention/FF layer pair,
$\mathcal E$ executes a dedicated masked attention operation whose key/value set consists solely of hidden states belonging sooner indicated “byte window” belonging to active patch \(p_j.\)
Assuming channel dimensions $(h_{\mathcal G},h_{\mathcal E})$, its schema unfolds:
1. Initialize queries \(q_j=W_q P^\circ_j,\)
   where \(P^\circ_j:=G( f_{\rm pool}(h^{(j)})):\)).
   Here \(f_{\rm pool}\) implements simple sum or mean pooling over all hidden states linked 
   with the indices belonging to Internet-coded patch \(j.\)
2.Four projection transforms generate keys/values:
   \
   $$ K_\ell=W_k h_\ell,\quad V_\ell=W_vh_\ell,$$
   where $h_\ell$ runs through indices constituting that precise patch.
3.The resultant attentional map is computed,
   cropped strictly limited ⟨organised⟩ so it never references hidden states outside $j$: 
   \
   $$A^\circ_{\ell,j}
      ={\rm softmax}\!\left(
        {q_j K_\ell^\top}/{\sqrt d_q}
                   \right)V_\ell,$$
4.The query update becomes:
   \
   $$P^\circ_{\ell,j}=P^\circ_{\ell-1,j}+A^\circ_{\ell,j},$$  
and this process repeats depthwise across all $(l_{\mathcal E}-1)$ cascaded incarnations.
Finally aggregates `$P_j$` formats a single dense vector per-patch residing inside dimension $h_{\mathcal G}$,
which aligns directly with corresponding positions expected from $\mathcal G.$  

Because both query/value projections learnable weights operate within distinct dimension spaces,
only keys/values remain “short”, computationally inexpensive even when many patches coexist.
Crucially, in contrast to purely local smoothing operators such Gauss kernels,
this mechanism remains fully differentiable throughout learning whilst preserving exact positional locality through masking.



### # Global Latent Transformer Responsibilities  

BLT’s heavy weight lies largely within **Global Latent Transformer** (**G**) — an autoregressive transformer running over *patch embeddings* ($m=n_p$ total).
This unit receives complete panelization $\pmb P=\{P_1,P_2,…,P_m\}$ produced ad hoc by $\mathcal E.$  
It behaves analogously to token-level transformers except it terminates locality checkwise at the newer artful abstraction level.
Self‐attention uses a block **causal mask**
which permits every future relative offset up until present document index;
the mask prohibits “future” information within any single document principle making large panels easier parallelisable than unconstrained long sequences seen under pure token settings.\

Parameter setting leverages Llama 3 skeletons ­– identical heads counts, hidden sizes £(e.g.$h_{\mathcal G}=4096$), SwiGLU activation functions, rotary positional encodings limited exclusively inside self‐attention subsystems (§5), guaranteeing compatibility between bitwise data feed‐in procedure sans further titanium overheads.

The blueprint summarized here illustrates precisely why byte granularities coupled with dynamically assigned ‘latency units’ prove advantageous against static token systems seen historically:
• They remove opaque memorisation biases caused simply imposing discrete subword cutoffs.\  
• They let computations grow correlated tightly with intrinsic conditional complexity measured locally without incurring high latency overheads.\
