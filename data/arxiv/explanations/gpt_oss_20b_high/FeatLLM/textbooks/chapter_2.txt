Chapter 2: Background and Related Work

The growing interest in few‑shot learning for tabular data arises from practical constraints such as expensive labeling pipelines or scarce labeled examples that plague many real‑world scenarios—from medical diagnostics to financial risk assessment. A parallel line of research seeks to leverage the already abundant inductive biases embedded inside large language models (LLMs), which are pretrained on vast amounts of text and therefore encode rich world knowledge that might be useful for structured‐data problems. This chapter surveys both tracks—classic few‑shot tabular approaches followed by recently proposed language‑interfaced solutions—before positioning the *FeatLLM* framework within that landscape.

## Few‑shot learning fundamentals – transfer priors vs unlabeled augmentation

In its simplest form, few‑shot learning aims to fit highly expressive models on an exceptionally small training set \(k \ll N\). Confronted with this scarcity it is natural for a learner to pick up spurious patterns that do not generalise beyond the observed examples (\cite{bartlett2021deep}). Two complementary strategies have emerged to mitigate this danger:

* **Transfer priors** inject external prior knowledge into the learner so that its inductive bias aligns better with latent structure present even in tiny datasets. An example is unsupervised pre‑training on a large collection of unrelated tabular records followed by fine‑tuning on the target task (\cite{somepalli2021saint}). Pre‑training objectives such as reconstructing masked entries (\cite{arik2021tabnet}) or contrasting augmentations (\cite{bahri2022scarf}) force a model often to learn representations that capture global characteristics rather than memorising idiosyncratic correlations present only in a few labels.
* **Unlabeled augmentation** leverages external pets—existing datasets without annotations—to enlarge the effective sample space via synthetic generation or semi‑supervised training procedures (\cite{yoon2020vime}). “Synthetic‐distribution” mixups (~\cite{zhu2023xtab}) and auxiliary diffusion models (~\cite{zhang2023generative}) explore the manifold induced by unlabelled records so that downstream classifiers see smoother decision boundaries despite lacking abundant labels.\footnote{\textbf{Note}: Although several pioneering frameworks experiment with hybrid likelihood avenues—combining brief supervised runs with deep generative priors—the primary goal remains avoiding overfitting while preserving robust inductive structure inherited from cross-domain corpora rather than simply increasing signal noise.\xspace}

These paradigms manifest differently in practice depending on whether one has access solely to supervised signals or may bring along millions of unlabelled structured documents.\textit{}

## “TabPFN”, “SCARF”, “STUNT” and other prototypical approaches

A handful of research efforts crystallised around these general ideas:
* **TabPFN** trains a Bayesian neural network on *synthetic* tabular samples drawn from a mixture of distributions devised on holistic column types (\(C_{\text{id}}), C_{\text{num}}, C_{\text{text}}\). Once trained offline its evaluation procedure simply concatenates both training and test records into one prefixed input sequence keying‐in every feature value together (\(x^{\text{train}}, x^{\text{test}}\)). This has two advantages—no further optimisation at test time and an innate form‐weak prior over possible table layouts \(\rightarrow\) effective generalisation even under brutal sparsity.\footnote{\textbf{SP}: Indeed this purely Bayesian paradigm tightly couples prediction confidence propagation to known covariate dimensions while honouring deterministic training budgets.\xspace}
  
* **SCARF** adopts a contrastive objective positing two ``proxy’’ representations per record built through stochastic augmentations over categorical/granular columns.\(\mathrm{s}\) While unsure if fully supervised measurements were considered present beforehand \cite{s}, its distinguishes itself by liberating representations under absolutely unlabeled conditions—to minimise non‑linear entanglement between features thereby stabilising downstream classifiers.\footnote{\textbf{SP}: When applied naturally under little labelled data conditions it shortens intra‑task covariance shape divergence dramatically.\xspace}
  
* **STUNT** implements an implicit curriculum whereby a child network learns gradually increasing complexity deadlines using distributions curated from broadly distributed domains—its use case being clinical predictions where described `missingness patterns' strongly articulate selection bias.\(\mathrm{s}\)

Beyond these three frames exist hybrids such as *TransTab*, which leverages inter‑column semantics learned through transformer attention over column names alone (\cite{wang2022transtab}), contributing another source outside typical statistical frameworks; likewise *Lift* introduces multi‑task prompts indexing over pretrained object detectors fed into GLM backbones (\cite{dinh2022lift}), suggesting encoding raw numeric patterns via linguistic tokens directly supplied within prompts.\(\mathrm{s}\)
These exemplars share an idealized picture—the label space arrives later via sufficiency transformation functions applied either during training stage modifications or entirely inside probability spaces generated either online or offline.
