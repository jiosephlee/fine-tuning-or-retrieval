# Beyond Flat Interpolation – Finding Non‑Uniform Patterns in RoPE  

Large language models are a lot more powerful when they can look farther back into their input sequences—but standard models only see about four thousand tokens at once.  
At first glance it seems easy enough to stretch the window: take every positional coordinate you’ve been using before and simply “speed it up” so that it fits into the longer span you wish for your model:
  
$$s=\frac{L'}{L},\qquad  
\bm r_n^{(i)}= (\cos(\tfrac{n}{s\beth^i}),\, \sin(\tfrac{n}{s\beth^i})),$$
  
where \(s\) is the expansion ratio and \(i\) indexes the rotating dimensional pairs (“Rotary Position Embeddings”, commonly called *RoPe*).  
Yet this *flat* approach turns out to be wrong once you try expanding beyond roughly eight time larger than the trained window—something strangers to why exactly—and it forces LLMs into an uncomfortable *crowded‑positions* state where every hop between two adjacent indices becomes indistinguishable if you zoom out too far.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Why Linear Rescaling Fails Past an Eight‑Times Extension  

| Stage | Problem | Consequence |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Position Angles Collide** | Dividing every \(n\) by the same number collapses high indices into a tiny angular band near zero | Tokens lose positional resolution—attention heads cannot differentiate nearby words |
| **New Position Values** | Positions beyond what a model has ever seen introduce values “out‑of‑distribution” for its learned cosine/sine table | Fine‑tuning struggles badly — gradients explode or vanish during back‑propagation |
| **Cumulative Quality Drop** | The longer the stretch, the louder the crowding signal gets | Empirical perplexities spike sharply after doubling or tripling beyond original limits |

You can’t simply crank an “all‑same λ” variant up any further—the result is either catastrophic failure or drastic loss of quality whenever you tap into very long documents.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Two Missing Levers That Empower Very Long Windows  

### 1️⃣ Different Scaling Per Rotational Dimension  

Higher frequencies capture finer local patterns whereas lower frequencies encode broad positional harmony.  
If every frequency gets squeezed equally you wound up sacrificing low frequency semantics while leaving high–frequencies noisy—an uneven kill‑zone block diagram!  

**Solution**: let each dimension \(i\) receive its own rescale factor \(\lambda_i\).   
In practice we write it directly into the calculation:
\( \mathbb I_{i}(n)=\begin{cases}
1,& n<\hat n\\[3pt]
\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}},& n\geqslant \hat n
\end{cases}\)
and compute \(\cos( n/\beth^{\,i}·\mathbb I_{i}(n)) ,\, 
      \sin( n/\beth^{\,i}·\mathbb I_{i}(n)) ). 
When chosen wisely these factors preserve high–frequency precision while expanding low frequencies enough that context does not get crowded.



### 2️⃣ Treating Early Tokens Separately  

Inself‑attention every token listens more strongly toward its predecessors especially at sequence beginnings—think cache or “priming” signals coming out front of a prompt or document header store crucial cues (the author name,…).  
If you collapse already-high‐attention places you lose substantial information right at launch time!  

**Solution**: decide an early threshold \(\hat n\); all tokens before this index *keep their original* RoPe angles (\(\lambda=1\)). Afterward start slowly interpolating according to your sought expansion ratio while letting the accumulated power shift smooth over rank 😉.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## How LongRoPE Orchestrates These Levers With Tiny Overhead  

The search space now explodes combinatorially:
each dimension can take thousands different \(\lambda_j∈[1,\,s·1.25]\) values AND every sequence has an early‐token variable \(\hat n∈ℕ.\)  
A naïve grid would require astronomic evaluation run‐time because each candidate needs running **one forward pass over example long books** just to compute perplexity!  

> **Evolutionary Search + Monotonic Constraint** – Borrow “genetic algorithms” style mutation/crossover passing along *best cases*, yet restrict candidate solutions so that  
> \(0<λ_0≤λ_1≤…≤λ_{d/2−1}\).  
> This one trick slashes useless evaluations because Rotary Frequencies obey Neural Tangent Kernel theory—lower indices are higher frequency and thus should never be stretched more than later ones.



Initially we seed our army with known successful recipes:
Linear Pi , NTK pattern , YaRN groups – each becomes one mutation seed itself instead of starting three fitness individuals entirely random.
After copying contextual expert knowledge into population tails and letting crossover/evolution dryen over ~40 iterations,
the search converges onto specific non‑uniform patterns providing ~80 % less perplexity drop even when pushing from 4 000 →32 000 tokens **without any extra training at all**!



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## Tackling Even More—Progressive Extension Up To Two Million Tokens  

If you try increasing forty times up front—that leads practically nowhere faster than incremental steps does because far-away positions disappear outside training data range altogether!  

(Longrope adopts a *step‑wise* strategy:)
1️⃣ **Fine‑tune To Half‑Million**  
    * First run search → fine‐tune on Ask Manuscripts ~128 000 chunk training (~400 steps).  
    * Replace factors → beam sweep onto ≈256 000 chunk data (~600 extra steps).  
    * As simple raw strings long enough become cheap thanks to fewer shifted positions, batching cost remains manageable (<15 hrs on A100 cluster)

2️⃣ **Second Search From Fine‑Tuned Model**   
    Starting off already “context aware” allows us again run evolutionary IRW optimisation atop already tuned weights → jump leaps ahead to ≈2 048 000 tokens!
    Only **≈1000 steps** at __________ length are necessary once our polynomial resizing tricks completed!

3️⃣ **Readjust For Short Windows**   
    Finally flood back inhabitants <4 000 sequence indexes with another evolutionary run making sure their early fear spots (_small α_) stay untouched so other popular prompts deliver intact 😌


That gives us models supporting almost literally millions of tokens while being competitive against originally designed benchmarks.

## Takeaways You Can Seriously Start Using Today  

* **Non‑uniform interpolation matter most:** Treat each Rotary frequency independently—and treat early lines specially—makes massive difference when operating past eight times your vanilla window size.
* **Evolutionary optimisation lets you find them automatically:** You don’t need hand crafted rules anymore; let a small number (<64 individuals) iterate until perplexity plateaus.
* **Fine tuning stays light:** Only ~1000 gradient steps once you get past roughly half‐a-million window size—they’re enough because your positions were nicely built beforehand.
* **Existing libraries stay untampered:** Modification effort limited strictly to parameters fed into RoPe matrix – drop-in replaceable!
