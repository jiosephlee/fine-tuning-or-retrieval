### Why does simple linear positional interpolation cause ‘crowding’ when extending beyond a few times its original window size?
Question:
PI scales every rotary angle uniformly by λ=s where s=L′/L.
Apparently this makes nearby token positions “crowded” especially under large extensions (> 4×).
Could someone describe mathematically why many distinct positions collapse into almost identical angle vectors?
And practically how this crowding translates into poorer perplexity / attention discrimination observed experimentally?
Answer:
**Short answer**

When a pre‑trained model uses Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), every token gets its own *angle vector*

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta}_n=\bigl[\cos(n\,\theta_0),\
      \sin(n\,\theta_0),\
      \dots,\
      \cos\tfrac{n}{\,\beta^{d/2-1}},\
      \sin\tfrac{n}{\,\beta^{d/2-1}}\bigr],
$$

with  
\( {\displaystyle
  \beta=\vartheta^{\,{2}/{d}}
}
.\)  
The geometric interpretation is that each pair
$(\cos,\sin)$ rotates round the unit circle
with an angular speed proportional
to \( {\displaystyle
  {\vartheta}^{-\;\tfrac{2i}{d}}
}
;\)
higher–index pairs rotate more slowly
(low frequencies).
