## Transformer Self‑Attention Mechanisms  

At their core every modern autoregressive Language Model employs **scaled dot‑product attention** interleaved across many layers ⟨cite v31l3 ⟨citation placeholders omitted by formatting—should match RBC ⟨cite v31l3 said>⟩. Each token is projected into three dense vectors—queries (**Q**), keys (**K**), and values (**V**)—via trainable linear transformations:

\[
Q = XW_Q,\qquad K = XW_K,\qquad V = XW_V .
\]

The head‑wise attention gives every query weight over all keys according to

\[
A_{ij} = \frac{\exp(Q_i K_j^\top/\sqrt{d_k})}{\sum_{l}\exp(Q_i K_l^\top/\sqrt{d_k})},
\]

which lets each token aggregate information from every other token regardless of distance.  
Because attention scores depend only on pairwise similarities between projected vectors—and never on raw indices—these models *lack any innate notion* of *left-to-right order*. Two tokens swapped arbitrarily can yield identical attention contributions if their hidden representations are identical after projection. Consequently once training ends without additional constraints most transformer networks treat permutations equivalently unless supplied **positional signals** that break this symmetry nominating each position uniquely so downstream heads learn a strong sense of token ordering.

To address this deficiency people added “Positional Encodings” (sometimes called “Position IDs”) early after Ridge originally laid out Transformers ⟨cite v31l3 ⟨citation placeholders omitted> ⟩, which embed absolute indices into vectors appeasing dot‑product attention via additive biasing mechanisms offering baker sign meaning committing order-aware weight adjustments later throughout layers.




## Absolute vs Rotary Positional Encodings  

Most historically successful language models began with what are often called *Absolute Positional Encodings*. They whiten each input position **t** into a vector \(P_t \in \mathbb R^d\) that captures its uniqueness through sinusoidal curves:

\[
P_t = \bigl[\sin(t/\mathrm{10000}^{\,i/d}),\, \cos(t/\mathrm{10000}^{\,i/d})\bigr]_{i=0}^{d/2-1}.
\]

These tendentially carry distinct numeric phase patterns so that queries wrap up unbounded difference signal entities when added or weighted during attention computations.​ Occasionally people replace them with learned embeddings directly trained for each position individually when corpus’ max length permits straightforward indexing.



Over time researchers noticed two undesirable traits appearing with more extreme sequence lengths:

| Distribution | Problem |
|--------------|---------|
| **Out‑of‑distribution new indices** | A model trained exclusively on indices up to \(L\) will encounter vastly different cosine/sine patterns beyond \(L\), hitting far-from–trained circumscribed micromangled distribution points leading probability mass concentrate wildly—and sometimes diverging catastrophically¹ˬ¹ |
| **Crowding effect** | Very distant positions mapped closely near one another once sinusoids loop around again after many periods → attentive pooling collapses nuanced spans |

Rotary Positional Encoding was proposed precisely to mitigate these difficultiesⁿⁿ attributing relative–difference maintenance through multiplicative geometry rather than additive biasing while reinforcing *relative* invariances useful at unprecedented sequence scales ⟨cite rope⟩.


### Construction Overview  

Given a query vector \(q_n ∈ ℝ^{d}\) at absolute position \(n∈ℕ\) and key vector \(k_n∈ℝ^{d}\), their RoPE counterparts are computed via a special “twiddle” rotation operation defined element–wise across paired half‐dimensions:
 
\[
q_n^{\,(\text{rot})}=R(\mathbf θ,n)\cdot q_n,\qquad
k_n^{\,(\text{rot})}=R(\mathbf θ,n)\cdot k_n,
\]

where \(R(\mathbf θ,n)\) rotates every pair
%
\( [x,y]^T → [x cos(θ),y sin(θ)]^T \).
  
Consequently each pair experiences an angle proportional simply
to \(n θ_i\):

```
θ_i   = 10000^{−2i/d}
```

and thus forms precisely the pair
\( [cos(nθ_i),sin(nθ_i)] `` .
Thus instead of adding conditioning vectors you literally twist algebraic axes by your current offset producing:
 
```math
[ cos( n θ_0 ),    sin( n θ_0 ),    …,

```
(the same expression repeated POV).

This construction preserves relative differences after twisting:
for two arbitrary positions \(p<q\),
\[ q_p^{\,(\text{rot})}\!\cdot k_q^{\,(\text{rot})} = 
     q_q^{\,(\text{rot})}\!\cdot k_p^{\,(\text{rot})}
