## Why Attention Is Expensive  

All transformer heads share three linear projections ‑ queries (𝐐), keys (𝐊), values (𝐕). For a single head the core operation is  

\[
\mathbf S = \alpha\,\mathbf Q\,\mathbf K^{\!\top}\;, \qquad   
\mathbf P = \operatorname {softmax}\!\bigl(\mathbf S\bigr)\;, \qquad   
\mathbf O = \mathbf P\,\mathbf V ,
\]

with   
$\alpha=1/\sqrt d$.  
The cost grows quadratically with sequence length $N$: $\mathcal O(N^{\,2}\!d)$ floating‐point ops are needed just to sample every $(q_k,v_k)$ pair once the model cares about it.

On modern discrete GPUs this quadratic cost translates largely into **memory traffic**—every element of $𝐐$, $𝐊$, $𝐕$ must be streamed off‐chip into shared buffers again whenever it becomes part of another chunk’s product matrix $𝐒$. Even after moving data suitably once per kernel launch you still hit tens–of–GiB sweeps every forward pass, scrubbing entire tensors down again during backpropagation.

## First‐Generation Fast Attention – where did it falter?  

The original FlashAttention trick fused everything into one pass that reads $𝐐$, streams $𝐊,\!𝐕$ tiles entirely in register/memory hop stride, does an “on‐chip” reduction so you never materialize $𝐒$ or $𝑃$ globally, then emits $𝐎$ stably—all zero extra writes except finally dumping $𝐎$ itself… Great idea – Davidson reduced pressure significantly compared against naïve NumPy computes – yet it was built assuming an AMPERE backbone:\-

| Attribute | Original FlashAttn | Projection toward Hopper |
|-----------|--------------------|---------------------------|
| Tensor Core support | Used natural logical WMMA kernels | HQ device exposes fully asynchronous WGMMA (warp­group matrix multiply accumulate) |
| Data movement | Naïve global ↔ shared streaming | Hopper provides a dedicated Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) capable of fully asynchronous async copies |
| Low precision | Only BF16 /FP32 paths were tested | Hopper brings fixed‐point FP 8 support |

Because all ops were organized under a restrictive “synchronous” model nobody could hide latency between a slow matmul fragment and its sparse partner routine such as the exp() kernel required for softmax exponentiation.
