Chapter 4: # Standard Attention vs Earlier FlashAttentions

Understanding how conventional multi‑head self‑attention has been accelerated on modern graphics processors requires tracing two distinct design philosophies that evolved during recent years.  
The first philosophy—what practitioners have traditionally called **“standard” attention**—employs three consecutive SIMD matrix multiplications separated by stages whose outputs are written back to global device memory  
(\texttt{HBM}).  
The second philosophy—the lineage denoted **FlashAttention** (original prototype) followed closely in its successor **FlashAttention‑2**—introduces locality‑aware tiling together with an intertwined computation–communication schedule that eliminates almost all intermediate writes until a single output tensor is produced.



## Standard kernel flow  

In its elementary form an attention head receives three tensors,
$\mathbf{Q},\,\mathbf{K},\,\mathbf{V}\in{\mathbb R}^{N\times d}$,
and produces 
the per‑token representations 
$\mathbf{O}\in{\mathbb R}^{N\times d}$ according to  

$$
\mathbf{S}\;\leftarrow\;\frac1{\sqrt d}\,\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{K}^{\!\top},
   \qquad 
   \mathbf{P}\;\leftarrow\;\operatorname*{
      {\rm softmax}}\!\bigl(\mathbf{S}\bigr),
   \qquad 
   \mathbf{O}\;\leftarrow\;\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}.
   $$

On GPUs this procedure is usually split into separate CUDA kernels recorded directly with CUBLAS calls:
1. A **generalized–matrix multiply** (GEMM) generates $\mathbf S$.  
2. Since ${\rm softmax}$ involves an exponential followed by normalisation,
the vendor‐supplied routine rewrites every element,
shifts it by $\operatorname*{
      {\rm rowmax}}(\!\mathbf S)$,
calculates entry‑wise $z_{ij}=e^{s_{ij}-m_{i}}$,   
computes per‑row sums $l_{i}=\,{\sum}_{j}z_{ij}$,
and stores both the shifted scores $z_{ij}$ (forming an intermediate score matrix)
and their sums/utilisation factors those vectors scalar‐wise inverted later;
3. A second GEMM multiplies these probabilistic weights against $\mathbf V$, yielding $\mathbf O$.  

Because each of these stages touches nearly $N^2d$ elements—
the sheer size of $\mathbf S$ being $(N\times N)$ entries even when $d=64$
—and relies heavily upon DRAM bandwidth,
the *standard* route incurs significant latency stalls.
Every round trip through global device memory wipes out tenths—or often exceeding eighty percent—
of processor utilisation powerfully acquired when dense arithmetic units saturate their request queues.



## FlashAttention principle  

The driving insight behind **FlashAttention** was that many computations performed operations that never need fully materialized tensors once globally passed.
Instead they can be carried out locally whenever possible.
To realise this idea it introduces **blocked local‐softmax reduction** executed entirely in *shared* device RAM (`SMEM`) arranged cyclically so successive layers reuse buffers without introducing costly synchronisations.

During execution a threadblock handles one chunk  
$\!\boldsymbol Q_{\!i}\!\in\nolimits{\mathbb R}^{\,B_{\!r}\times d}$  
of query tokens while iterating through every chunk  
$\boldsymbol K_{\!j},\,\boldsymbol V_{\!j}
   \!\in\nolimits{\mathbb R}^{\,B_{\!c}\times d}$  
of keys/value pairs stored temporarily in registered SMEM slots.
For each pair $(i,j)$,
FlashAttention performs a **single packed GEMM**

$f_{ij}=    {\boldsymbol Q}_{i}{\,}{\,}{\,}
              {\boldsymbol K}_{j}^{\,!\top}
     \;\bigl(\equiv {\bf SS{-}GEMM}{b}_{ij}\bigr),$

after which it extracts per‑row maxima ($m_{i}$)
and computes exponentials locally (`exp(z)`),
obtains running partial sums ($l_{i}$),
and immediately accumulates the weighted contribution
      
$$
C_{ij}=Z_{ij}\,\boldsymbol V_{\!j},   \qquad (\equiv {\bf RS{-}GEMM}{b}_{ij}),
$$

into an **intermediate accumulation buffer**
representing truncated fragments of the desired output tensor
$\boldsymbol O_{\!i}$.
Only *after* every chunk has been processed does:
|–|It normalises accumulated weights across rows using accumulated maxima/sums|
|--|It writes out *one contiguous segment* $\boldsymbol O_{\!i}$ together with its corresponding log‑scale term $L_{i}=m_{i}+{\log(l_{i})}$|
into global device RAM once per CTA.
Thus every expensive DRAM round trip occurs **exactly twice** per input tensor –
during whole‐score computation entering SMEM buffers –
and never again during intra‑kernel updates,
dramatically slimming communication volume compared against vanilla split‑kernel pipelines.
