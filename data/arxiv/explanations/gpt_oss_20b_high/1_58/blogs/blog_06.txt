# Beyond Business Value—What’s Next for One‑Bit LLMs  

Large Language Models have become indispensable tools for everything from question answering to automated writing assistants—yet their silicon bill keeps climbing even faster than their capabilities grow.  
A growing body of research now shows that *one‑bit*—or even *ternary*—weights can preserve almost all performance while slashing compute costs dramatically.  
The most recent milestone comes from **BitNet b¹·⁵⁸**, an architecture where every parameter takes one of three values \<− 1, 0, + 1\>. It achieves perplexity comparable to a full‑precision FP16 model **starting at only three billion parameters** while delivering its outputs twice as fast on GPUs and using roughly one‑third as much VRAM.



-----------------------------------------------------------------------

## How one‑bit came into mainstream attention  

Traditional LLMs store their weights in floating point format (FP32/FP16).  
Because every transformer layer relies almost entirely on matrix multiplications, the average bandwidth demand comes largely from moving dense tensors into DRAM/SRAM and executing many expensive *floating‑point multiply–add* pairs.



### Why ternary works  

A single weight now lives in a tiny three‑state set `{− 1, 0, + 1}` (“ternary”).  
During inference it behaves like an *int8 addition* operator over very sparse matrices—no multipliers are required except when both operands are non‑zero—and fits perfectly into standard integer units found on GPUs, CPUs and custom ASICs.



### From “BitNet” → “BitNet b¹·⁵⁸”  

* **Quantisation** – Every weight `W` undergoes an *average‑absolute* normaliser (`γ`) followed by rounding and clipping:
  
```latex
\widetilde{W}=   
   \operatorname{RoundClip}\!\Bigl(\frac{W}{\,γ+ε\,}, -\,|\,,+|\,\\Bigl),
where 
   γ=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
```

* **Activations** – Remain eight‑bit integers (`INT8`) instead of `FP16`.  
No extra offset (“zero‑point”) handling is needed thanks to symmetrical scaling across tokens.



### Tenets borrowed from popular open‑source models  

The blockstack mirrors an LLaMA backbone — RMSNorm instead of LayerNorm, SwiGLU activation mixer, rotary embeddings—and drops any learnable biases entirely—all purely integer operations.

## Where do these savings translate into real opportunities?

### Mixture‑of‑Experts becomes physically feasible

MoE layers replace all-but-a-slice matrices with conditional experts spinning off “fast kernels.”  
Historically MoE has been *memory hungry* – hundreds of billions terabytes spilled across dozens or hundreds of GPUs—due precisely to classical floating point tensors moving off chip during gating steps.

Because *every parameter counts fewer bits*, the data footprint shrinks → fewer inter‑chip transfers → a single TPU‑like device can host thousands more experts whereas before they'd have lived split over many chips.



### Truly longer context windows

Presentation versus inference spectra both dominated now by KV cache sizes written back into DRAM/SRAM per generated token (≈ 48 k × sequence length bytes @ FP32).

With `INT8` activations Tile width doubles without a change in compute load – you get **74 tokens -> ≈ 148 tokens** simply because every word needs only one byte instead of two (or four).  
Pools furthermore hint toward still deeper compression down toward quaternary bits (`Q4`), enabling yet larger contexts without modifying current accelerator pipelines.



### Edge & mobile friendliness

Traditional mobile AI accelerators target sub‑gigabyte footprints while polling simultaneously background OS processes + deep learning inference simultaneously—a daunting combination when using quartierish parameter storage (~160 GB per DL multi-modal kernel).

* One‐bit cores embarrass themselves comfortably under designated CPU gears (`TMUL` isn’t required).
* The sheer paucity (*≈ 300 MB*) lets an entire fine‐tuned GPT-like policy fit comfortably into mobile SoC caches plus DRAM which normally satellites wallet fill only once during startup.


### Dedicated ASIC dreams materialise immediately

If you look past perhaps millisecond-level accelerator forces behind training some transformer matrix multiplication engine boasting horizons <10 million FLOPs/s/Watt—then crafting silicon specialized toward integer pass‐through circuitry alone provides up-close match elimination benefits traditionally beloved only in data science clouds:
* Eliminated multipliers altogether—masterfully cheap `ADD` NAND/OR gates.
* SRAM arrays optimized specifically around sparse ternaries give both gigantic hit rates & data density.
* Off‐chip I/O bandwidth becomes largely irrelevant once embedding outputs shrink dramatically.


> The Oracle team behind Groq recently released an LPUs framework tailored around activation packing—that's already proof-of-concept stage under industrial lens—and builds precisely what this line does electrically.
