# From Theory to Numbers—BitNet b¹⁵⁸ Matches Full Precision at Three Billion Parameters  

Large‑scale language models have become a double‑edged sword: their ability to master language grows dramatically when you add parameters, yet every extra megabyte increases deployment costs linearly – longer inference latency, higher GPU memory usage and larger carbon footprints all shoot straight upward.



In this post I walk through an empirical milestone proved by **BitNet b¹⁵⁸** – a transformer that stores every weight as one of three possible signs \(\{-1,\!0,\!+1\}\) instead of a floating‑point value – and demonstrate how once the model reaches roughly **3 billion parameters** it performs exactly on par with its full‑precision counterpart while consuming far less compute.



---------------------------------------------------------------------



## Why Low‑bit Training Is Needed  
Post‑training quantisation techniques batch existing models down to four or even two bits per weight *after* they have already been trained in full float16 precision. Because they never change the optimisation dynamics halfway through training, those methods often lose a few percentage points on downstream benchmarks.



By contrast **BitNet** trains directly in a low‑bit regime ([Balon et al., 2024]). All operations during learning take place already over finely carved integer ranges so no runtime penalty is paid when switching back–and–forth between training and inference.



---------------------------------------------------------------------



## How BitNet b¹⁵⁸ Works  
| Component | What It Does | Why It Matters |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Ternary weights** |\(\{-১,\!0,\!+১\}\)| Gives each weight an effective log₂(₃) ≈ **1·58 bits** – drawing a line between pure binary models and classic half‑precision nets |
| **AbsMean QUANT**<br>\(\tilde W=\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\bigl(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon},\,{-１},\,+１\bigr)\)<br>\(\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{i,j}|W_{ij}|\)<br>\(\mathrm{RoundClip}(x,a,b)=\max(a,\min(b,\mathrm{round}(x)))\)| Normalises then forces every real weight into one of the three discrete levels without exploding gradients |
| **8‑bit activations** | Scaled per-token into \([-8,+8]\) rather than higher precision | Keeps nonlinear paths lightweight while maintaining enough dynamic range |
| **LLaMA-alike backbone** | RMSNorm ≤ SwiGLU ↦ Rotary pos enc | Matching community standards means any HuggingFace pipeline sits straight‐in or runs under llama.cpp without extra wrappers |

Because all matrix multiplications become pure INT8 additions (no costly floating‑point multiply), inference curves collapse sharply both in execution time *and* energy consumption.



---------------------------------------------------------------------



## Training Pipeline  
Both BitNet b¹⁵⁸ models and their Float16 siblings were trained scratch on an identical corpus (**RedPajama**): **100 billion tokens**, ordered by token length‐boiled variety so that every tokenizer state is seen once during each epoch.



The authors kept every hyperparameter identical except for linear layers – replaced with **BitLinear**, keeping batch size provisionally constant across widths so that comparisons truly reflect *weight storage* rather than subtle scheduling tricks.

## Zero‑Shot Task Performance  
Using the OpenAI–style evaluation harness over seven challenging benchmarks…

* ARC‑Easy / Challenge  
* Hellaswag / OpenbookQA  
* Winogrande / PIQA / BoolQ  

the quantitative gap shrunk steadily beyond three billions:

* At **≈ 3 G**, accuracy / normalized score diff < 0·5% compared with Float16.  
* At **≈ 4 G**, pointwise performance flips––the ternary side shines slightly ahead even while using far fewer bits.



When plotted together those curves sit one another almost forever after passing the tipping point.

## Efficiency Side‑Effects  
The “real” value proposition shows up below reallocating denominators hide behind half tabs.


### Speed versus Memory  
For matching‐size models,

* Linux shows that benchmarks run **≈ 2½× faster** on A100 GPUs thanks mainly to skipping multiplications during attention dot–products.
* GPU RAM consumed falls proportionally likewise (**≈ ¾–⅔** compared against Float16).

At **70 billion parameters**, speedups cone upwards … nearly a **4× acceleration**, while remaining comfortably inside single chip budgets.



### Energy Economics  
Energy modelling based on FLOPs measured at chip level yields near astounding savings:

> For any token length ≥512,
> *Roughly **70× less arithmetic‐unit power** is screamed away ‑ merely involving cheap INT8 adds instead of expensive FP ops.*



Even when extra tapes such as KV cache use are counted—the overall picture indicates an energy footprint comfortably below half that attained by mere mixed‑precision Tensor cores.



### Throughput Scalability  
Pairing two NVidia A100s via pipeline parallelism stresses both architectures equally joyfully.
Scaling batch size until GPU memory saturates yields raw throughput gains —

> **8–9× higher total output token rate** ‑ largely due entirely to miniaturised per sample kernel time.

## Implications Beyond Numbers  
The original coincidences were astronomical:
    
#### Mixture–of–Experts Orchestration  
All storage inefficiencies vanish when experts can slot into a single device without shuffling large floating-point matrices across high latency links—a nightmare today solved here.


#### Long Context Parsing  
Halving bit-width gives double KV cache capacity; consequently a model could effortlessly understand dynamical windows twice its current range using identical silicon budget.


#### Edge & Mobile Demise   
On CPUs where FPU units dwarf dedicated adders therefore dry shift money away from GPUs,
a ternary tensor network moves gold tips instantly onto headsets or embedded assistants without sacrificing conversational nuance.


#### Custom Hardware Callout    
Groq’s linear logic processors note precisely how fast plain Integer kernels run without sign updates,
all crucial facts enabling “LLPU” chip designs targeted directly at these sweet spots.
