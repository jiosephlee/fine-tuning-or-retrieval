**Short answer**

Equation (6) computes hyperspherical energy from *normalised* neuron
weight vectors—$\widehat{\boldsymbol{w}}_i={ {\boldsymbol{w}}_i}/{||{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_i||}$.   
The measure is **not** computed on activations and it does not depend
on the magnitude of a weight vector at all; it only looks at the
direction that each neuron learns inside its parameter space and judge how uniformly these directions are spread on the unit hypersphere.

### Why only weights, not activations?

A learning objective that depends on *activations* would need an input‐specific,
runtime computation at every training step,
while working out a clean regulariser for such quantities is difficult  
and introduces extra variance tied to
the data distribution.\ |newline|
Weights are static parameters that fully determine how activations 
will be produced.
Because hyperspherical structure captures most semantic capability living
in *angles*, governing this structure without touching magnitudes turns
out sufficient for preserving pre‑training knowledge during finetuning 
—exactly what OFT aims at.



---

### Bottom line

* Yes – you first normalise each neuron’s weight vector before plugging it into Eq.(6).
* It measures how **well‑spread** those direction vectors are on a sphere;
  it ignores magnitudes entirely.
* It focuses purely on angle information,
  which empirical evidence shows carries much semantic load in diffusion models.
* Because an orthogonal transform preserves these angles,
  keeping hyp­erspectral energy constant guarantees finetuning
  with OFT will not drastically rese‑wire the network’s internal geometry.
