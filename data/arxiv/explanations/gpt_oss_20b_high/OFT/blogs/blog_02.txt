# “I Came From Here”: Subject‑Driven Generation Made Simple With OFT  

For anyone who’s ever wished a custom face‑portrait could be generated from just a handful of personal photos – without spending weeks tweaking hyper‑parameters – this post is your quick guide into one of the most elegant tricks for steering diffusion models today: **Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)** for *subject‑driven generation*.  

We’ll walk through why *direction matters*, how the orthogonal trick works under the hood, and why it outperforms the long‑standing DreamBooth or LoRA baselines when you have only two or three images at hand.



---------------------------------------------------------------------  

## 1. The Problem Space  

Diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion now generate stunning photorealism from text prompts alone.  
But they treat *“who”* the image is about like another prompt – loosing surface‑level identity cues when fed new photos.

*Subject‑driven generation* tries to build a model that starts from an existing large diffusion network and *focuses* it on *one* person or object using only a few reference images – usually < 5 images – while still letting arbitrary text conditions steer composition, style or lighting.



### Key Challenges

| Challenge | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| Few‑shot data | Typical fine‑tuning needs thousands of samples; here we have only a few tens at most |
| Identity preservation | Neural networks usually throw away subtle cues unless carefully regularised |
| Stability & speed | Long training runs risk “catastrophic forgetting” – forgetting global visual priors |

Historically people tried:

| Method | Core idea |
|--------|-----------|
| **DreamBooth** | Adds a *new* token + small loss terms; finetunes at tiny learning rates |
| **LoRA** | Inserts low‑rank adapters in attention matrices – still updates magnitudes heavily |

Both work but have drawbacks:
* DreamBooth needs careful tuning (learning rate, epoch count) and often collapses after many iterations when the subject details are fragile.
* LoRA touches magnitude and direction simultaneously; learning rates must be tiny otherwise you lose all pre‑trained knowledge.



---------------------------------------------------------------------  

## 2. A Fresh Angle on Finetuning – Orthogonal Finetuning  

Instead of sprinkling tiny updates on every weight, OFT *rotates* all neurons inside each layer by an orthogonal transformation that preserves their pairwise angles – thereby retaining the diffusion model’s semantic geometry.



### Hyperspherical Energy Revisited  

For a weight matrix \(W = [w_1,\dots,w_n] \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\) define unit vectors \( \hat w_i = w_i/\lVert w_i\rVert \).  
The **hyperspherical energy** is

\[
\mathrm{HE}(W) \;=\;
\sum_{\substack{i,j=1\\i\neq j}}^{n}
\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\|^{-1}.
\]

Intuitively it measures how spread out neuron directions are on the unit sphere – smaller energy → more geometrically coherent feature space.



### What Does “Preserving Energy” Mean?

From algebra,

\[
W' = R\,W,\qquad R^\top R = I,
\]

for an orthogonal matrix \(R\) satisfies

\[
\mathrm{HE}(W') = \mathrm{HE}(W).
\]

So *any rotation or reflection* is free: hypotheses encoded in neuron relations survive untouched while every neuron direction can be nudged towards better alignment with your subject’s few exemplars.



### The Model–Agnostic Beam

The forward pass with OFT becomes

```text
z = (R · W⁰)ᵀ x                       (1)
```

where \(W⁰\) is the pre‑trained block tensor and \(R\) is learned per-layer but **shared across neurons** inside that block.  
Because all neurons share one rotation matrix per layer ∈ ℝᵈ×ᵈ , parameter overhead stays tiny (~ O(d)).



#### Why Efficiency Pays Off

Compare parameter counts:

| Method | Trainable Parameters per block | Notes |
|--------|---------------------------------|-------|
| **LoRA** | \(r'(\!d+n)\) | Rank–\(r'\) low‑rank modifier |
| **OFT** | \(d(d/r-1)/2\times r_{\text{blocks}}\) | Block–diagonal \(R\), roughly O(d) |

With a typical fully connected block \(128\times128\),
LoRA needs **2048** trainable values while OFT needs **≈9×10²**, saving ~60 % memory & compute.

## 3. Subject‑Driven Pipeline Using OFT  

Below is distilled workflow people usually follow when adapting Stable Diffusion v1.5 for personalized portrait generation with just two photos:

1. **Collect Data**  
   2–5 frontal/sketchy views of the target subject (high‑res medical aiming minimal crop).

2. **Preprocess**  
   Resize/center‑crop to consistent dimensions; optionally augment via small rotations if you wish extra data seeds—it doesn't hurt because training data remains tiny.

3. **Initialize Finetune Module**  
   Build kernel block(s) (`R`) per large self‑attention module of Stable Diffusion’s UNet; set `Q` (skew sector in Cayley transform) = zeros ≡ > \(R = I\).

4. **Loss Functions**  
   Same as DreamBooth:
    * Reconstruction loss between generated image conditioned on reference prompt & text (“<subject> portrait”) and real subjects.
    * Prior‑preservation term relative to pseudo tokens isolating background semantics.
   
5. **Training Loop**  
   ```text
   for epoch in range(epochs):
       zero grads
       y_hat = diffusion_forward(x_noisy,
                                 R·W⁰,
                                 prompt)
       loss = recon(y_hat,obj)+prior(y_hat)
       loss.backward()
       optimizer.step()            # gradient updates only on Q blocks
       clip_grad_norm_(optimizer.param_groups[0], eps)
   ```

6. **Testing / Inference**  
   After training you simply pipe `R·W⁰` into Stable Diffusion exactly as before – no extra runtime passes or conditioning modules needed!


### Hyper‐parameters that matter & how sensitive they are

| Setting | Typical Value | Effect |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| Batch size | 2–4 samples per GPU | Small batches suffice because dataset ends early |
| Learning rate | 1e‑4 – 5e‑4 | Larger rates give faster converge but risk drift outwards (`ε` below). For standard OFT `ε=∞`. |
| Training epochs/steps | 100–200 steps / ~ 5–10 minutes on an RTX 3090 GPU | Convergence is remarkably fast compared to DreamBooth (~2k steps). |
| Number of blocks per layer (`r_blocks`) | 8–16 (denominator scaling ~ d/r_blocks ) | Trade–off between parameter size vs expressive power.|

If you add an explicit radius constraint—**Constrained OFT (COFT)**—you supply an extra ε bounding how far `R` can rotate from identity:

```text
∥R−I∥ ≤ ε .
```

In practice setting ε≈0.05–0.1 yields superstable trainings without harming control quality.

## 4.Deploying Only Two Photos? Fine!  

Why does OFT work so well on such scarce data?

1. **Geometry over Magnitude** – Since we rotate all neurons together instead of letting every weight wiggle independently, information encoded through directions remains largely intact while adapting directions toward the subject’s embedding space.
2. **Large Degrees Raised Safely** – Orthogonality guarantees we never alter clear numeric magnitude—staying within the same unit hypersphere means no drastic scaling collapse which easily destroys color/texture priors required for photorealism.
3. **Fast Convergence** – Because every update operates on high‑level directions already well calibrated across layers, less fine tuning suffices—observed heatmaps show identity/style fidelity turning solid at ≈ 200 steps whereas DreamBooth keeps wobbling until > 800 steps.

## 5.Cutting‐Edge Comparisons

Below distillation summarizes key quantitative heads‐up from our experiments (Stable Diffusion v1.5).

### Metric Definitions

* **DINO / CLIP‐I**  
  Average intra‑class cosine similarity between generated images and real reference subjects → higher better → stronger identity match.
* **CLIP‐T**  
  Cosine similarity between generated image embedding and text prompt embedding → higher better → content accuracy.
* **LPIPS**  
  Perceptual distance among random draws from same seed/subject → lower better → higher diversity.

## 6.Bonus Note → Why No Extra Overhead at Inference?

Because final `W = R·W⁰` equals simply rotating pretrained weights once after finetuning completes—no run‐time interpolation or additional network modules are invoked during sampling! This keeps FPS exactly how Stable Diffusion originally ran.



---------------------------------------------------------------------  

## Final Takeaway

Orthogonal Finetuning leans into geometry that natural diffusion has already mastered:

* A single learnable rotation per major weight block replaces messy low‐rank tricks;
* Hyperspherical energy guarantees all pre‑trained semantic relationships survive intact;
* Training converges orders faster than classic dreambooth or LoRA loops;
* Identity fidelity stays robust even when you squeeze three photos into the fine tune stage;
* And inference costs stay unchanged shaping even light-weight deployment needs.
