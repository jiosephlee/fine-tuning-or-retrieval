## 3. Parameter Efficiency – The Block Diagonal Conundrum

### Current status

Without extra tricks, an unrestricted orthogonal matrix needs  
\( d(d−1)/2 \) trainable parameters—far more than LoRA’s low‑rank updates `r'`.  
Using block‑diagonal matrices reduces this cost quadratically by factoring into \( r \) smaller rotations.
Yet two side effects surface:

1. **Bias toward axis‑aligned transformations:** Small blocks rotate only within their subspace; this reduces expressivity compared to full rotations.
2. **Block size selection challenges:** Choosing \( r \) demands empirical tuning—no principled guideline exists in the paper beyond simple examples (e.g., \( r=8 \)).


### Research directions aligned with existing insights

- **Shared block patterns** : As noted, sharing identical blocks considerably cuts parameters (\(\mathcal O(d^2/r^2)\)). A systematic study could reveal whether sharing yields acceptable expressiveness across diverse diffusion layers.
- **Sparse rotational patterns** : Employ “block diagonal” plus random sparse off-diagonal elements—this retains group closure while allowing limited cross–channel interactions.
- **Re‐scaled OFT extension** : Incorporate learned diagonal scalings (`D`) alongside rotations (`R`). According to Section 4 (“Re‐scaled Orthogonal Finetuning”), this adds negligible parameters yet gives extra degrees of freedom without touching hyperspherical energy.
