\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### What Is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) At Its Core?
Question:
I understand that traditional reinforcement learning from human feedback trains an independent reward model followed by policy optimisation with an RL algorithm such as PPO. In contrast the authors claim that DPO optimises a language‑model policy directly using only a binary cross‑entropy loss over human preference pairs. Can someone explain intuitively why this works – why isn’t an explicit reward worth learning needed?
Answer:
**Short answer**  
DPO replaces the two‑step “learn a reward → then act” cycle with one analytic mapping from any *reward* that explains the observed preferences to its *optimal* policy. Once we know such a closed‑form mapping, we can replace “reward‑model training” with a single binary‑cross‑entropy loss that directly pushes a language‑model’s probabilities so that the preferred token sequences become more likely than the dis‑preferred ones, while keeping the model close to its reference distribution through an implicit KL term. Because this mapping already accounts for the KL constraint, we never need an independent reward network or any reinforcement–learning sampling loop.



---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. What does RLHF actually do?



In standard reinforcement‑learning–from‑human‐feedback (RLHF)

1. **Reward model** \(r_{\phi}(x,y)\) is fitted from comparison data  
   $
      p^*(y_1\succ y_2 | x)=
      \sigma(r_{\phi}(x,y_1)-r_{\phi}(x,y_2))
   $
   where $\sigma$ is the logistic function (Bradley–Terry).

2. **Policy** $\displaystyle {\rm PI}_{{}_{θ}}\!(·|x)$ starts at an SFT model ${}^{𝜋}_{𝑅𝑒𝑓}$.
   It is fine‑tuned with a *constrained* objective
   $
      {\rm max}_{π_{θ}}
            {\mathbb E}_{y∼π_{θ}}\!
            [
               r_{𝜙}(𝑥,𝑦)
               -
               β~ {\rm KL}[π_{θ}\Vert π_{𝑅𝑒𝑓}]
            ] .
   $
   Because decoding is discrete, this is usually optimized by PPO or REINFORCE.



---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.The key analytic observation



For any “true” reward $r^{𝜌}(𝑥,𝑦)$ and reference ${}^{𝜋}_{𝑅𝑒𝑓}$,
the solution of  
the KL–constrained maximization has a neat **closed form**
$
   {${}^{∗}_{𝜌}π}(y|x)=
     Z^{-1}(𝑥)
     · {}^{𝜋}_{𝑅𝑒𝑓}(y|x)
     ·e^{ r^{𝜌}(𝑥,𝐲)/β } .
$
where  
$
   Z(ℵ)=Σ_y {}^{∗}_{ℵ}π_regressed(u)\!,
$
is just a normalisation constant.



Because *preference models* depend only on differences of rewards,
the partition function disappears when we plug $(†)$ into—
say—Bradley–Terry:
$
p^*( y₁\succeq y₂ | x )
=
σ(
 β~
 ln[
 {\displaystyle {}^{∗}π(r)(y₁|}
                     {}
                    {}
                   {}^{∗}π(r)(reg,(assumed))}
]-
 β~
 ln[
 {\displaystyle {}^{∗}π(r)(y₂|}
                    {}
                   {}
                     {}^{∗}π(r)({}^{Rᶠ})
] ).
$
Thus *any* optimal policy must satisfy $(†′)$.
Equation $(†′)$ expresses human preferences **solely**
in terms of—internally learned policy—
without separate reward network.

### 3.From equations to an objective



If now let our *parameterised* language‑model be $\displaystyle {\rm PI}_{{}_{θ}}\!(·|⋯)$,
we simply replace ideal solution in *(†′)*,
with our current estimate:
$
p_{θ}( y₁\succeq y₂ | x )
=
σ(
 β~
 ln[
          {{PI}_{{}_{θ}}\!(y₁|⋯}
          /{}^{PI}_{{}_{Rᶠ}}\!(⋱ }
]
-
 β~
 ln[
          {{PI}_{{}_{θ}}\!(⋱}|⋱}
          /{}^{Pi}\!
 ]
).
$
Maximising likelihood over empirical comparison data
is thus equivalent to minimising
$
L_DPO({}_{θ})
=
-ℰ_{(⋯)}
    ln σ(
             β~(\ln{{PI}_{{}_{θ}}\!(⋯}/GI)-...)
          ).
$
Equation $(†′)$ constitutes nothing more than binary cross‐entropy—
it causes $p_{θ}$’s own log‑ratio against ${}^{PI}_{{}^{Rᶠ}}\!(⋱)$
to behave as its own intrinsic “reward”.




---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4 Why it emulates reinforcement learning





If conceiving $(†′)$,
note maximising $(†′)$ triggers precisely ordinary policy gradient updates—
increase likelihood under preferred samples,
decrease likelihood under inferior ones,
scaled automatically via sigmoid acting as unstated baseline thus stabilising gradients.



---------------------------------------------------------------------



###5.Stabilisation via per-example weighting



The sigmoid weight ceases updates once confidence grows,
preventing collapse onto tiny token set—
exactly analogous PPO clipping.



---------------------------------------------------------------------



###6.What does *not* happen?



Adding sheer shifts $\forall f(x)$ leaves ordering invariant,
hence preference specifications stay equivalent.

## Bottom line



DPO works because:



<blockquote>
<ul>
<li><strong>Closed-form optimal policy:</strong>
$\displaystyle PI^*_r=\dots$. </li>
<li><strong>Explicit mapping:</strong>
Policy ↔ Preference expressed solely through log-ratio.</li>
<li><strong>Maximum likelihood training:</strong>
Binary CE converges directly onto optimal distribution.</li>
</ul></blockquote>

It bypasses fitting separate rewards/reinforcing samplings yet solves the same optimization target transparently.
