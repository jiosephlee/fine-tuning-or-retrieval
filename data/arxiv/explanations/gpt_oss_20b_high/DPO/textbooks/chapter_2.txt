\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

Chapter 2: Safety / helpfulness / controllability gaps  

Generative modeling trained solely via next‑token prediction learns representations naturally suited for generating plausible continuations irrespective of user intent or ethical considerations. In practice this manifests through several undesirable phenomena:\begin{itemize}\item *Hallucination*: generated facts often conflict strongly without evidence.\item *Unsafe content*: offensive remarks can surface inadvertently.\item *Misuse*: prompts asking how to execute an illicit activity will be given instructions.\end{itemize}\textemdash{} These deficiencies underscore the enterprise-risk premise posed by **complete autonomy** during generation—a consequence shape-large scale decisions require careful mitigation.\footnote{Safety researchers note even close-to trace-instructions processed through reinforcement learning exhibit catastrophic drift when rewards are miscalibrated ``if you train towards luck-yielding patterns'' \citep{ouyang2022training}.}\textemdash{}  

A practical guide thus calls for methods that can *steer* a high-capability engine toward user-derived goals while respecting hard constraints such as principle-based declassification (“never reveal personal identifying information”). Achieving both precision control over sentiment generation and subtle adherence to correctness requires training signals beyond casual debugging audits.\footnote{$\mathcal X$ denotes prompts representing user contexts}; ensuring outputs preserve benefits while rejecting anomalies remains unmatched without external supervision.\textemdash{}
