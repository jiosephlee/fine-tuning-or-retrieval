\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

Chapter 1: Unsupervised pre‑training gains emergent skills  

Large language models trained purely on massive corpora of natural language records exhibit remarkable breadth of knowledge and an impressive ability to perform many reasoning tasks \citep{brown2020language,chowdhery2022palm,touvron2023llama,bubeck2023sparks}. Their objective during pre‑training is simple—inverse-marginal likelihood estimation—that predicts a token conditioned on its left context without any extra supervision regarding meaning or intent. Despite this unconstrained objective, these models capture latent world knowledge embedded sparsely across diverse internet documents and additionally acquire useful inductive biases that allow them to generalise zero‑shot or few‑shot over downstream problems such as translation or code generation.\footnote{Because these acquisitions arise only from statistical observation rather than explicit pedagogical signals, their behavior remains opaque: philosophers remark that “highly capable” stands against “highly intelligible” \citep{bubeck2023sparks}.}  

While useful, this breadth comes at a cost—the distribution of responses is unfiltered by safety or usefulness constraints that humans would impose when interacting with an assistant. Consequently large language models retrieve both valuable insights **and** harmful or misleading patterns encoded in their training data.\footnote{The data includes quotes from self‑promoted experts who claim epistemic certainty while being wrong \citep{ouyang2022training}.} An uncontrolled model therefore risks replicating such biases unless architecture or therapy intervenes.\textemdash{}
