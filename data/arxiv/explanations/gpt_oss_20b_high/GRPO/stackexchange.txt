### "Arxiv Papers Deleterious Influence on Math Benchmarks"
Question:
"Authors claim models trained exclusively on arXiv scraping perform worse or show little gain.
Given arXiv contains clean TeX mathematics,
basic intuition suggests extra scientific material should help.
Could sparsity/noise issues arising from macro stripping
(ArXiV‐RedPajama workflow) explain reduced performance?
Is there evidence arXiv examples match task distribution poorly,
either stylistically or linguistically?”
Answer:
**Short answer**

In *DeepSeekMath* the authors explicitly tested whether training on ArXiV‑derived data helps mathematical reasoning and found it does **not** – in fact it can hurt performance compared with a corpus obtained directly from Common Crawl. The lack of benefit is consistently observed across the main quantitative‑reasoning benchmarks (GSM‑8K, MATH) as well as the multiple‑choice tests (MMLU‑STEM). The authors attribute this mainly to a **distribution mismatch** (style and content of ArXiV papers versus the kinds of problems used for evaluation) rather than an artefact of macro stripping or sparsity per se.
