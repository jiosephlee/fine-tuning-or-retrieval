Chapter 2: Motivation Behind DeepSeekMath  

# Bridging performance gap while staying open‑source  
The most immediate driver behind DeepSeekMath was the observation that world‑class closed‑source language models—most notably GPT‑4 and Gemini Ultra—exhibit remarkable proficiency on rigorous mathematics benchmarks whereas publicly available alternatives lag far behind despite often matching or exceeding them in size or compute budget gaps remain wide even among recent large‑scale open‑source projects such as Llama‑2 70 B or InternLM 20 B [3–5].  
The motivation was therefore dual‑folded: first, eliminate this divide so researchers worldwide could experiment freely with state‑of‑the‑art reasoning capabilities; second, expose an exemplary framework that associates corpus engineering, specialized pre‐training, instruction tuning, and efficient reinforcement learning into a coherent pipeline for others desiring comparable outcomes without proprietary barriers.

To accomplish this goal three pillars were established explicitly—(i) create the largest ever curated arithmetic corpus extracted purely from public web data, (ii) demonstrate how prior exposure to programming functionally enhances generalizable problem solving even when architecture limits prevent full attunement between algebraic logic and decomposition strategies, and finally (iii) design an optimisation objective that discards heavy auxiliary components yet preserves their expressive benefits.*

These steps together produced an open‐source seven billion parameter system whose self‑contained chain‐of‐thought accuracy reaches **51 %** on competition‐level MATH—an achievement only slightly below GPT‑4’s performance—and surpasses all administrators possessing substantially larger modalities.*
