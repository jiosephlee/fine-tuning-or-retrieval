\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 3: # Corpus Validation Experiments  

In order to demonstrate concretely how the scale and quality of pre‑training data influence mathematical reasoning, we carried out a controlled study in which an independent small language model was pre‑trained separately on each of four widely used mathematics corpora. The results confirmed that when held at equal token budgets and following an identical training schedule, models built upon the DeepSeekMath corpus consistently outperform those based on MathPile, OpenWebMath, and Proof‑Pile‑2 across all evaluated benchmarks. This experiment therefore underscores the robust size–quality trade‑off that underlies modern large‑language‑model development for mathematics.\n\n# Experimental regime\n\nThe experiment employed a compact transformer architecture with approximately \(0.83\text{ B}\) parameters—chosen as an accessible baseline that matches research expectations while remaining computationally tractable for repeated pre‑training runs. Each instance of this architecture was initialized from a general language backbone and then exposed solely to one of four distinct mathematics corpora: DeepSeekMath (120 B tokens), MathPile (8.9 B tokens), OpenWebMath (13.6 B tokens), and Proof‑Pile‑2 (28 B tokens when accounting for arXiv contributions). To isolate data quality effects rather than sheer volume variations, every training run processed exactly \(150\text{ B}\) tokens of its respective corpus through identical schedules spanning \(\sim50{\,}\rm{b}\) steps—a figure chosen such that all corpora underwent roughly equivalent epochs relative to their sizes.\n\nA uniform tokenizer was applied throughout so that token granularity remained constant across runs; optimizer hyperparameters followed standard practice for large language pre‑training—AdamW with \(\beta_1=0.9\), \(\beta_2=0.95\), weight decay \(=0.1\), batch size \(4\,\rm{M}\) tokens, maximum context length \(4{\,}\rm{k}\) tokens—and temperature settings were kept identical when sampling prompts later.\n\nAfter completing pre‑training, each model continued through a short supervised instruction fine–tuning step using one thousand carefully crafted chain‐of‐thought examples drawn from an external validation set so as not to alter domain biases severely.\n\n# Competing baselines\n\nFour benchmark data families represent contemporary choices for numeric reasoning pre‑training:\n\n* **DeepSeekMath**—derived from Common Crawl via an iterative fastText selection pipeline based on OpenWebMath seeds and subsequently refined through human annotation;\n* **OpenWebMath**—a publicly released Common Crawl subset filtered for mathematical content comprising ~13 b tokens;\n* **Proof‑Pile‑2**—a multilingual aggregate incorporating OpenWebMath together with AlgebraicStack code snippets (~10 b tokens) and arXiv papers (~28 b tokens) weighted deliberately toward academic source material;\n* **MathPile**—an eight‐layered mix primarily dominated by arXiv (~9 b tokens after heavy cleaning).\n\nEach baseline harmonized its sampling strategy so that exploration coverage matched its corpus volume during pre‑training.\n\n# Evaluation metrics\n\nEvaluation adopted a purely semantic metric designed around *chain‐of‐thought* (CoT) prompting in zero–shot style:\n\n* For each benchmark problem we provided three few‐shot demonstrations consisting solely of CoT solutions;\n* Candidates produced by each rule‐run were decoded using nucleus sampling (\(p=0.\!95\)) over a \(5{\,}\rm{k}\)-token horizon;\n* Accuracy was computed over entire logical reasoning chains—the final answer produced after explicit derivations had been generated—and matched against ground truth labels.\n\nBenchmarks spanned four primary domains:\n1️⃣ arithmetic/word problems (GSM8K),\n2️⃣ multi‐step STEM challenges (MATH),\n3️⃣ symbolic reasoning tasks in Chinese (MGSM‑zh), and\n4️⃣ college mathematics practice sets (CMATH). *All* accuracy figures are presented as percentages rounded to one decimal place.\n\n# Results – linguistic reach & multilingual uplift\n\nWhen comparing variety across languages, five distinct observations emerged:\n• Models pretrained on **DeepSeekMath** achieved noticeably higher scores on Chinese datasets relative to MathPile even though all runs intervened through identical mixed–language prompts.\n• On all **English** tests scores rose consistently by at least ten percentage points per ten‐fold increase in token budget when using DeepSeekMath versus OpenWebMath.\n• The breadth of mathematical concepts captured in DeepSeekMath—ranging from elementary Algebraic identities to advanced calculus inequalities—translates into richer *latent representations*, reflected by steadier gains even when facing unseen *unstructured code fragments*. For example the GSM8K accuracy exceeded 90 % once Fine-Grained Decoding was introduced after reinforcement steps.\n• Comparative plots revealed a steep positive curvature when plotting accuracy against cumulative training token consumption: **DeepSeekMath** moved team ahead after only five epochs while other corpora plateaued early.\n• Finally, cross-lingual elasticity was most pronounced for proofs requiring symbolic manipulation; **DeepSeekMath** exhibited up to \(+4\,\%\) transfer shift over MGSM–zh compared with its peers.\n\n# Takeaway – Size × Quality attack\n\nThe most salient conclusion emerging from these controlled experiments is that data scale alone does not guarantee superior reasoning proficiency without corresponding editorial rigor and multilingual breadth.\nWhen trained under strictly equivalent resource budgets, models ingesting **120 billion** high-quality Common Crawl snippets outperformed those ingesting almost four times more multisetarized content derived from academic repositories merely because each token contributed *semantically rich*, *well-aligned*, and *well-dissected” mathematical knowledge.*\nThis pattern indicates an actionable recipe: prioritizing curated web data extraction pipelines can economically replace astronomical amounts of replicated arXiv parsing while delivering immediate benefits across both English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks—a clear *size × quality attack.*
