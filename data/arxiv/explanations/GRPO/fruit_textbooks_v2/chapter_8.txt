\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 8: Supervised Fine-Tuning for Mathematical Instruction Following

# English datasets: GSM8K, MATH (with tool-integrated solutions), MathInstruct subsets, Lila-OOD

The English portion of the <|endoftext|> fine-tuning (SFT) collection in DeepSeekMath-Instruct is built to cover a wide range of quantitative-reasoning problems and to expose the model to multiple solution formats. It includes explicit annotations for two canonical math benchmarks: GSM8K and MATH. For both GSM8K and MATH the team produced tool-integrated solutions — that is, solution exemplars that combine natural-language reasoning steps with programmatic computation (for example, writing a short program or code fragment that performs the algebra or numeric computation part of the solution). These tool-integrated exemplars teach the model how to interleave human-readable reasoning and deterministic program execution, which is crucial when evaluation allows tool use.

Beyond GSM8K and MATH, the collection draws on a curated <|endoftext|> of MathInstruct and on the training portion of Lila-OOD. From MathInstruct the authors selected examples that illustrate chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) styles; the Lila-OOD <|endoftext|> contributes additional diverse problems and solution styles. The goal of including these diverse English sources is twofold: first, to provide many examples of CoT-style step-by-step reasoning so the model learns how to produce detailed, human-readable derivations; second, to provide PoT and tool-integrated exemplars so the model learns to generate executable reasoning when that is desired.

Combining GSM8K, MATH (with tool-integrated annotations), MathInstruct subsets, and Lila-OOD yields a training mixture that emphasizes both natural-language multi-step reasoning and the disciplined, programmatic elements of solutions. This bilingual approach in formats (CoT, PoT, tool-integrated) is intended to make the model simultaneously good at producing full textual solutions and at emitting executable code or tool calls when appropriate.

# Chinese datasets: K-12 coverage across 76 subtopics with CoT and tool-integrated formats

DeepSeekMath-Instruct <|endoftext|> includes a large Chinese component to improve performance on non-English mathematical benchmarks. The Chinese SFT collection focuses on K–12 problems and spans a broad set of elementary topics: the paper states coverage across 76 subtopics (for example, linear equations and related basic algebraic concepts). Each Chinese problem is paired with solutions in two <|endoftext|> formats: chain-of-thought (CoT) style natural-language derivations and tool-integrated solutions suitable for programmatic execution.

Providing both formats in Chinese serves the same pedagogical purpose as in English. The CoT annotations teach the model how to express a full human-style reasoning trace in Chinese, while the tool-integrated solutions teach the model how to offload heavy numeric or symbolic work to code or libraries while still describing the <|endoftext|> reasoning in Chinese. Because earlier math-pretraining corpora and many prior efforts were heavily English-centric, inclusion of high-quality Chinese SFT data yields measurable gains on Chinese benchmarks (as reported elsewhere in the paper), and helps the final instruction-tuned model generalize across languages.

# Formatting and concatenation, context length, batch, learning rate

The SFT examples are arranged into training sequences by randomly concatenating multiple examples until a maximum context length is reached. Concretely, training examples were randomly concatenated until the sequence length reached $4\mathrm{K}$ tokens. This concatenation strategy exposes the model to multi-example contexts during fine-tuning, increasing robustness to longer-context prompts and reducing mismatches between pretraining context behavior and instruction-tuning contexts.

Training was performed for $500$ steps with a batch size of $256$ and a constant learning rate of $5\times 10^{-5}$. These hyperparameters define a relatively short but intensive fine-tuning pass starting from the DeepSeekMath-Base model. The random-concatenation + long-context recipe means that each training update often contains several problems and responses, enabling the model to learn transitions between examples and to practice producing complete explanations within a large context window.

The SFT dataset size is substantial: the paper reports a total of $776\mathrm{K}$ annotated training examples across English and Chinese, covering CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated solution styles. The mixture of formats and languages, together with the concatenation and long-context training setup, is thus explicitly designed to teach the model both how to generate detailed reasoning traces and how to interleave programmatic <|endoftext|> when appropriate.

# Performance without tools and with tools vs open and closed models

Evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B was carried out under two different usage regimes to isolate different capabilities:

- A no-tool setting, where the model must produce a full step-by-step textual solution without invoking external computation; and
- A tool-integrated setting, where the model may combine natural-language reasoning with programmatic computation (the same style the SFT exemplars included).

Under the no-tool evaluation, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates strong step-by-step reasoning performance across the considered English and Chinese benchmarks. On the competition-level MATH dataset, the instruction-tuned model surpasses all open-source contemporaries and most proprietary models by a substantial margin: the paper states an advantage of at least $9\%$ absolute on MATH compared to other open-source instruction-tuned models and many proprietary systems. In particular, DeepSeekMath-Instruct outperforms larger open or specialized models (for example, Qwen 72B, and some math-focused 70B instruction-tuned systems) in the no-tool CoT setting.

When allowed to use tool-integrated reasoning, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B becomes even more competitive. The paper reports that under tool-enabled evaluation the model “approaches an accuracy of $60\%$ on MATH,” a level that surpasses all existing open-source models at the time of reporting. On other benchmarks the model is competitive with larger instruction-tuned systems; for example, it is competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B despite being much smaller.

The authors benchmark DeepSeekMath-Instruct against a broad set of models. Closed-source models used for comparison include GPT-4, GPT-4 Code Interpreter, Gemini Ultra/Pro, Inflection-2, Grok-1, and some recent Chinese proprietary models (GLM-4 and Baichuan-3). Open-source comparisons include general chat models and math-enhanced instruction models across a range of sizes (DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, Qwen 72B, SeaLLM-v2 7B, ChatGLM3 6B, InternLM2-Math 20B, Math-Shepherd-Mistral 7B, WizardMath <|endoftext|> MetaMath 70B, ToRA 34B, MAmmoTH 70B, and others). Against this field, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B performs extremely well for a $7$B-class model: it exceeds all open-source alternatives on MATH in the no-tool evaluation and approaches top-tier performance under tool-integrated evaluation.

# Observations on capability gains and remaining gaps <|endoftext|> vs GPT-4, Gemini-Ultra)

The <|endoftext|> fine-tuning phase yields clear capability gains. The combination of <|endoftext|> SFT data, CoT/PoT/tool-integrated formats, and long-context concatenation produces a model that is both better at producing careful step-by-step solutions and better at writing executable programmatic solutions than prior open-source 7B models. The paper highlights that these gains make DeepSeekMath-Instruct comparable in many respects with much larger instruction-tuned models (tens of billions of parameters) and able to outperform a broad set of open-source <|endoftext|> on both English and Chinese math benchmarks.

Despite these advances, the authors explicitly note remaining limitations. First, DeepSeekMath-Instruct still underperforms the strongest closed-source systems (GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra) on several axes. In particular, GPT-4 exhibits stronger few-shot capability (the ability to improve with a handful of demonstrations), while DeepSeekMath-Instruct shows similar zero-shot and few-shot performance, indicating room to improve few-shot <|endoftext|> Second, certain specialized domains—geometry and formal theorem proving—remain relatively weaker compared to the closed models. The paper notes specific failure modes seen in dry runs, such as difficulty with problems involving triangles and ellipses, suggesting that the SFT and pretraining data distribution may have gaps in some <|endoftext|> subtopics. Finally, because the instruction tuning and RL in the project target a specific set of tasks (dominantly GSM8K and MATH in the RL phase), the model’s improvements in some cases reflect sharpening of output distributions (making correct answers more likely in top-$k$ sampling) rather than a wholesale increase in fundamental problem-solving competence; this observation is discussed more fully in the RL analysis in the paper.

In summary, the SFT stage for DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B creates a small but powerful instruction-tuned model by combining a large <|endoftext|> SFT corpus ($776\mathrm{K}$ examples), mixed solution formats (CoT, PoT, tool-integrated), long-context concatenation ($4\mathrm{K}$ tokens), and concise optimization (500 steps, batch size $256$, learning rate $5\times 10^{-5}$). The result is an instruction-following model that substantially closes the gap with larger open-source models and outperforms them on demanding benchmarks, while still leaving headroom relative to the best closed-source systems and in some specialized math subdomains.
