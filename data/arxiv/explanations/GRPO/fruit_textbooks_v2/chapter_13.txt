# Online vs offline sampling: advantages of online data in later stages

A central distinction among these methods is whether the sampled outputs used for training are obtained offline (from a fixed SFT policy) or online (from the evolving policy). Offline methods like RFT and DPO sample once from the SFT model and train on that static dataset of outputs. Online methods (Online RFT, PPO, <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> sample from the current policy during training.

Intuitively, online sampling becomes more advantageous as training progresses. Early on, the policy and the SFT model are similar, so the online samples are not very different from the offline ones and the gain is small. Later, however, as the policy improves and departs from the SFT distribution, online sampling discovers novel outputs and failure modes that are more informative for continued improvement. The paper’s experiments reflect this: Online RFT overtakes offline RFT in later training stages. This empirical behavior supports the idea that online data <|endoftext|> to the <|endoftext|> weaknesses and offers richer, time-varying signals that drive further progress.

Online sampling is not a free lunch: it requires exploration (sampling many outputs per prompt) and careful stability mechanisms <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> penalties or <|endoftext|> to avoid runaway policy drift.
