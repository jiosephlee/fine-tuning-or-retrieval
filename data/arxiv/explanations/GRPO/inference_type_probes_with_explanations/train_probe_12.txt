In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', in everyday terms, GRPO's group-relative baseline most resembles the classroom grading scheme called grading on a curve. This is because, instead of learning a separate value function as a baseline like PPO, GRPO forms a group of \(G\) sampled responses for the same question and computes each response’s advantage by normalizing its reward relative to the group, explicitly setting \(\hat{A}_{i,t}=\widetilde{r}_i=\frac{r_i-\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})}\) for outcome supervision (and an analogous step-accumulated form for process supervision), so that the mean group reward acts as the baseline and the dispersion scales the signal. Consequently, the policy update (Eq. (3)) reinforces or penalizes responses based on how well they do compared to their peers’ average—not on an absolute scale—precisely mirroring “curved” grading where scores are shifted and scaled relative to the cohort’s mean and variance, thereby removing the need for a separate critic while providing variance reduction through within-group normalization.
