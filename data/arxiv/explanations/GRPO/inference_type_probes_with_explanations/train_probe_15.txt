In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors explain that because their pre-training corpus includes multilingual content rather than being English-only as in prior work, restricting the corpus to English would likely lead to worse performance on Chinese benchmarks. This follows directly from their corpus design and ablations: they explicitly construct a multilingual DeepSeekMath Corpus with substantial non-English (notably Chinese) math text and report that models trained on it improve mathematical reasoning in both English and Chinese, whereas prior corpora that are “primarily English-centric” show “limited improvement and may even hinder performance in Chinese,” and they further note that DeepSeekMath-Base is superior on Chinese benchmarks “likely because” they include high-quality non-English data; since Chinese mathematical text uses different token distributions, terminology, symbol conventions, and even script coverage that an English-only corpus would underrepresent, an English-only restriction would reduce exposure to these patterns, degrade tokenization efficiency for Chinese, and thus impair the learned likelihoods \(p(o \mid q)\) for Chinese problems, which is consistent with their empirical findings on MGSM-zh, CMATH, and Gaokao benchmarks.
