In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', GRPO was applied using only a subset of English instruction tuning data yet improved a Chinese benchmark ($\text{CMATH}$), demonstrating cross-lingual transfer. This is sensible because (i) the base model is pretrained on a multilingual math corpus with substantial Chinese content, so the latent mathematical representations and tokenization already support Chinese inputs; (ii) the GRPO update mainly reshapes the model’s reasoning policy rather than teaching new language-specific facts—its objective adds a KL regularizer $-\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}]$ and uses group-relative advantages $\hat A_{i,t}$ computed from comparative rewards within each question, which encourages globally more reliable step-by-step reasoning patterns that are largely language-agnostic for math; (iii) empirically, the authors show RL improves $\mathrm{Maj@K}$ but not $\mathrm{Pass@K}$, indicating it makes the output distribution more robust by boosting correct traces into the top-$K$, a distributional effect that transfers across languages given shared mathematical structure; and (iv) KL anchoring to the instruction-tuned reference preserves existing Chinese ability while GRPO’s comparative signal (normalized within-group rewards) exploits the same correctness criteria (final answers or step-level rewards) that apply equally to Chinese and English problems, allowing the English-supervised RL to enhance reasoning reliability on $\text{CMATH}$ without language-specific supervision.
