According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', initializing the model from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint such as DeepSeek-Coder would most likely lead to decreased math performance. The paper explicitly reports that “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and backs this with controlled ablations showing that code-first pretraining (e.g., a two-stage schedule of code for \(400\text{B}\) tokens \(\rightarrow\) math for \(150\text{B}\) tokens) improves mathematical reasoning both with and without tool use relative to starting from general-language tokens, while also enhancing subsequent math training efficiency; moreover, mixing code and math in one-stage training further preserves program-aided reasoning, whereas the general-first alternative underperforms in both CoT and PoT settings for the same model family. Empirically, DeepSeekMath-Base is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B and then math-trained for \(500\text{B}\) tokens, yielding large gains on GSM8K, MATH, and other reasoning benchmarks (and even improving MMLU and BBH), consistent with the paper’s broader observation that code pretraining supplies algorithmic priors and tool-use competence that translate into stronger mathematical reasoning; by contrast, the authors find arXiv-only or general-text-first pretraining does not produce comparable benefits, so replacing the code-initialized checkpoint with a general LLM would plausibly remove these advantages and degrade math performance.
