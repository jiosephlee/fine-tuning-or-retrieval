According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', based on the authors' initialization choice, the expected outcome if DeepSeekMath had been initialized from a general LLM instead of a code-trained model is lower math performance. This follows from both their design and ablation evidence: they explicitly initialize DeepSeekMath-Base 7B from a code-pretrained model (DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B) because they “notice that starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and their controlled studies show code pretraining substantially boosts mathematical reasoning with and without tools relative to pretraining on general tokens; in a two-stage setup, “Code Training for 400B Tokens → Math Training for 150B Tokens” outperforms “General Training for 400B Tokens → Math Training for 150B Tokens” on benchmarks such as GSM8K and MATH, and in one-stage training, mixing code with math tokens improves program-aided math and mitigates catastrophic forgetting, whereas replacing code with general tokens does not confer the same gains. They attribute this to code pretraining imparting algorithmic and step-structured priors that align with chain-of-thought and program-of-thought reasoning, yielding higher scores (e.g., Deep residually higher results on GSM8K/MATH and improved MMLU/BBH) and better sample efficiency; therefore, absent these code-derived inductive biases, the model initialized from a general LLM would be expected to attain lower math accuracy (e.g., lower \( \%\) on MATH and GSM8K) after the same math-focused training.
