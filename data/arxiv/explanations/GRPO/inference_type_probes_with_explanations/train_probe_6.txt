According to the paper “DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models,” if the DeepSeekMath Corpus had been English-only, the authors would most likely expect the model’s performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks to be worse. This follows directly from their observation that the DeepSeekMath Corpus is explicitly multilingual—with English and Chinese as the two most represented languages—and that training on this multilingual corpus improves Chinese mathematical reasoning, whereas existing English-centric corpora “show limited improvement and may even hinder performance in Chinese mathematical reasoning.” Moreover, they attribute DeepSeekMath-Base’s superior Chinese performance to including high-quality non-English data, and their 7B continual training mixture allocates substantial multilingual content (e.g., \(56\%\) from the DeepSeekMath Corpus plus an additional \(10\%\) natural language from Common Crawl in both English and Chinese), reinforcing the conclusion that removing Chinese (i.e., making the corpus English-only) would deprive the model of language-specific mathematical patterns and thus degrade scores on Chinese benchmarks.
