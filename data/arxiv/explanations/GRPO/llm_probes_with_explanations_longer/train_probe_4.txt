According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the iterative domain-expansion step marks a domain as math-related when over 10% of its pages are collected; the implicit assumption underlying using that heuristic to add other pages from the same domain to the seed corpus is domain homogeneity. This is sensible given their definition of a domain as the set of web pages sharing the same base URL and their empirical observation that certain sites (e.g., “mathoverflow.net”) have topical coherence. If the initial fastText-based recall pulls at least 10% of a domain’s pages, it indicates that the conditional probability that a randomly sampled page from that domain is mathematical, \(P(\text{math} \mid \text{domain})\), is significantly higher than the general web prior \(P(\text{math})\). Choosing a threshold like \(10\%\) balances precision and recall: it filters out general-purpose or heterogeneous domains where math content is sparse, while retaining domains whose content is sufficiently concentrated so that the marginal gain from harvesting additional pages is positive. Moreover, they do not rely on homogeneity alone; after flagging domains, they manually annotate math-specific URL patterns (e.g., “/questions”), and only pages linked to those patterns but not yet collected are added, which operationalizes a subdomain-level homogeneity assumption and further reduces noise.

The results in the paper support the effectiveness of this heuristic, and thus the plausibility of the homogeneity assumption in practice. After iteratively expanding the seed corpus across domains using the 10% rule and URL-level annotation, they obtain 35.5M mathematical web pages (120B tokens), and by the fourth iteration, nearly \(98\%\) of data had already been captured in the third iteration, indicating convergence and that the domain-driven bootstrapping was efficiently covering math-rich sites. Downstream, models trained on the resulting corpus show a steeper learning curve and higher performance than those trained on alternative corpora, with DeepSeekMath-Base 7B surpassing open-source baselines and even approaching closed-source models on MATH. These empirical gains imply that the domain-level topical coherence assumption—i.e., that pages within a math-flagged domain have a high \(P(\text{math} \mid \text{domain})\) and similar distributional characteristics—holds sufficiently well to improve data quality and diversity when combined with URL pattern filtering and decontamination.
