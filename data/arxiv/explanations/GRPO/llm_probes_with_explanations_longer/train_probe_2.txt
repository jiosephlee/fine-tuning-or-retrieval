According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', based on the authors' initialization choice, the expected outcome if DeepSeekMath had been initialized from a general LLM instead of a code-trained model is lower math performance. The authors explicitly state that DeepSeekMath-Base is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B because “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and their ablations in the “Lessons Learnt in Pre-Training” section support this with targeted experiments. In two-stage training, pretraining on code for 400B tokens followed by math for 150B tokens yields stronger gains than replacing the first stage with general-language tokens. In particular, code pretraining boosts both program-aided math reasoning (via Python execution with libraries such as sympy) and math reasoning without tools; general-language pretraining does not produce the same improvements and can even compromise the subsequent math gains. The paper also observes that after math training, the model’s general reasoning (MMLU, BBH) improves relative to the non-code baseline, indicating that code initialization confers broadly useful inductive biases for structured reasoning.

This outcome is sensible given the structural alignment between code and mathematical reasoning. Code pretraining exposes the model to algorithmic patterns, symbolic manipulation, and precise syntax, which are closer to the compositional, step-by-step nature of mathematical problem solving than typical natural language distributions. Concretely, exposure to program-of-thought formats makes the model better at mapping problems into executable procedures, improving its performance when evaluated under PoT prompts and tool-integrated settings. Even without tools, the learned biases toward deterministic, verifiable step sequences aid chain-of-thought reasoning. From an optimization perspective, beginning with weights tuned on code likely places the parameters closer to a basin that encodes algorithmic primitives and discrete operations; subsequent math training then fine-tunes these primitives into domain-specific skills. In contrast, initializing from a general LLM tuned on unstructured text yields a representation further from such algorithmic basins, reducing sample efficiency and leading to a lower asymptote under the same math-training budget.
