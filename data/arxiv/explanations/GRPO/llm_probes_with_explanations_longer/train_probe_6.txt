In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors claim that the prior initialization choice that causally improved downstream mathematical reasoning compared to starting from a general LLM was a code-trained model. This is supported by both the design decision and controlled ablations: DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is explicitly initialized from a code-trained checkpoint (DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B), with the authors noting that “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM.” They then isolate the effect via two-stage training comparisons—first training on code tokens versus general tokens for \(400\mathrm{B}\) tokens, followed by math training for \(150\mathrm{B}\) tokens—and show that the code-initialized path yields stronger mathematical reasoning, both with natural-language chain-of-thought and with program-aided solutions. In particular, code-first pretraining already improves program-aided performance on GSM8K and MATH, and further math training compounds these gains, whereas the general-token-first control does not produce comparable improvements. This constitutes a causal probe: holding math data and downstream evaluation constant, replacing the initial general pretraining with code pretraining leads to better math reasoning outcomes.

Mechanistically, the paper’s evidence aligns with how code pretraining shapes the model’s inductive biases. Code distribution emphasizes precise syntax, compositional structures, and execution-grounded reasoning, which are the same skills exploited by program-of-thought (\(\mathrm{PoT}\)) prompting and tool-integrated reasoning. The authors show that mixing code and math tokens in a single stage can further enhance program-aided math while mitigating catastrophic forgetting, and that even without tools, code-first pretraining increases the efficiency of subsequent math training. Formally, if we denote performance after stage \(s\) as \(P_s\) and the data modality as \(D\in\{\mathrm{code},\mathrm{general},\mathrm{math}\}\), their experiments imply \(P_{\mathrm{code}\rightarrow \mathrm{math}} > P_{\mathrm{general}\rightarrow \mathrm{math}}\) on both chain-of-thought and \(\mathrm{PoT}\) settings, with larger margins on tool-integrated tasks. This pattern is consistent with code pretraining providing a better initialization in parameter space—i.e., parameters \(\theta_{\mathrm{code}}\) are closer, in terms of optimization geometry, to the manifold of policies that support multi-step symbolic reasoning—so that subsequent math training with loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{math}}(\theta)\) converges to stronger minima than when starting from \(\theta_{\mathrm{general}}\).
