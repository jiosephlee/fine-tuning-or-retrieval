According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors analyze the role of arXiv papers in their pretraining experiments and describe the experiments carried out to measure their effect on mathematical benchmarks; based on that discussion, removing arXiv entirely from the pretraining mix would most likely result in no notable change. The paper runs targeted ablations where models are trained on arXiv-only corpora and finds “no notable improvements or even deterioration” across a range of math benchmarks. Concretely, DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B trained for $150\mathrm{B}$ tokens on arXiv corpora (both MathPile, where $>85\%$ tokens are arXiv, and ArXiv-RedPajama at $28.0\mathrm{B}$ tokens) fails to improve GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, or miniF2F; similarly, DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B trained for $40\mathrm{B}$ arXiv tokens shows the same ineffectiveness. In contrast, the gains in the paper are attributed to the $120\mathrm{B}$-token Common Crawl–derived DeepSeekMath Corpus, selected via a fastText pipeline and refined iteratively, and to code-first initialization, both of which demonstrably push performance. Given that the arXiv component neither lifted results in isolation nor showed targeted benefits in their evaluation suite, its removal is unlikely to change outcomes materially.

Moreover, the paper’s broader empirical narrative emphasizes that data source quality and diversity—not parameter count or conventional sources like arXiv—drive improvements. The DeepSeekMath Corpus exhibits stronger learning curves and multilingual coverage (notably improving Chinese benchmarks) than English-centric datasets, while arXiv is predominantly English and formatted for scholarly exposition rather than step-by-step problem-solving or tool-integrated reasoning. Even within the final 7B training mix, arXiv constitutes only $10\%$ of tokens, whereas $56\%$ come from the curated web math corpus and $20\%$ from code, both shown to be effective. The authors do note caveats (e.g., potential benefits at larger scales or in specific tasks not studied), but given their controlled experiments and the observed lack of impact from arXiv on GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and miniF2F, the most reasonable inference is that dropping arXiv would most likely yield no notable change in the reported mathematical reasoning performance.
