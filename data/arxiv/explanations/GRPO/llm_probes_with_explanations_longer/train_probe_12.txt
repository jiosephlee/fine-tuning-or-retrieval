In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors mention a plausible scenario where arXiv tokens might still benefit mathematical reasoning: when models are trained at larger model scale. This caveat follows directly from their negative results at the 1.3B and 7B scales and their explicit statement of limitations: they found that training on arXiv-only corpora did not help and sometimes hurt performance across GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and miniF2F, but they note they have not tested whether “the benefits of arXiv papers would manifest themselves at a larger model scale.” That is sensible because leveraging arXiv’s long-form, formal mathematical discourse, proofs, and LaTeX structure likely requires more capacity and longer-context reasoning than smaller models possess. As model size \(N\) increases and context length \(L\) grows, larger models are typically better at modeling long-range dependencies, synthesizing multi-step proofs, and extracting signal from noisier or more heterogeneous sources—capabilities directly relevant to arXiv content.

Moreover, the paper’s broader findings reinforce this interpretation. They show that data quality and domain specificity matter at scale, and that starting from code-trained models improves mathematical reasoning—both observations consistent with the idea that sufficient capacity is needed to exploit structure-rich domains. ArXiv corpora contain high-level mathematical exposition with domain shifts (formal notation, theorem-proof style, LaTeX artifacts) and long documents that may exceed the 4K context used in many of their experiments; smaller models trained for \(T\) tokens on such data may underfit or fail to align with downstream reasoning tasks. In contrast, larger models with better optimization, longer contexts, and improved preprocessing could learn more abstract proof patterns and formal-to-informal mappings from arXiv, turning what is ineffective at small \(N\) into a net positive. Thus, given their experimental scope and stated limitations, it is reasonable to expect that arXiv tokens might yield benefits once model scale and training setup are sufficiently increased.
