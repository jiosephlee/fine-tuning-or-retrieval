### How does GRPO handle length bias given the $\frac{1}{|o|}$ factor and sequence-level rewards?
Question:
In Eq. (3), the objective averages per-token terms with a $\frac{1}{|o_i|}$ factor, while the outcome reward is a sequence-level score applied to each token equally. Does this induce a bias towards shorter or longer responses? How does this interact with the separate KL regularizer, which may also depend on sequence length?
Answer:
In Eq. (3) the GRPO objective is written as an expectation over groups of sampled outputs, but every sequence–level term—both the reward and the KL penalty—is wrapped into a per‐token average by the factor $1/|o|$. Concretely, if you are doing “outcome supervision,” each sampled output $o$ carries a single scalar reward $r(o)$ (after group‐mean and group‐std normalization). That same scalar $r(o)$ is used as the advantage $\hat A_{t}$ for every token $t \in \{1,\dots,|o|\}$. Thus the inner sum over tokens

$$
\frac1{|o|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o|}\hat A_{t}
\;=\;\frac1{|o|}\bigl(|o|\cdot\hat A\bigr)\;=\;\hat A
$$

so the total reward contribution is exactly $\hat A$, independent of $|o|$. Likewise, the KL penalty is introduced per token and then averaged,

$$
-\beta\,\frac1{|o|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o|}D_{KL}\bigl[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\;\|\;\pi_{\rm ref}(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\bigr],
$$

which again collapses to a single scalar KL term, independent of sequence length.

Because both reward and KL‐regularizer are “evenly spread” across tokens and then renormalized by $1/|o|$, a short sequence does not get a smaller gradient nor a long sequence a larger one, *by virtue of its length alone*. All that remains is the *per‐sequence* reward signal coming from your reward model or your process‐level rewards. If your reward model itself actively favors shorter answers (say, by giving longer answers lower scores), GRPO will learn that. But GRPO’s machinery—its $1/|o|$ factor—does *not* introduce any additional bias toward shorter or longer outputs.

In practice this is exactly the same trick that standard PPO uses when you have a reward only at the end of an episode: you divide the *sum* of per‐time‐step rewards by the episode length to get an *average* reward per time step, thereby preventing your agent from “game‐playing” length alone. GRPO simply adopts that same per‐token averaging for both the scalar reward and the KL term, so the update magnitude per sequence is neutral to $|o|$.
