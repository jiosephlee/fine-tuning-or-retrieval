### Does DeepSeekMath-Instruct concatenate multiple SFT examples per 4K context without separators?
Question:
The SFT training concatenates examples up to a 4K context. Are special separators, BOS/EOS tokens, or role tags used between examples to prevent cross-example attention leakage? If not, could there be unintended conditioning from the previous example's solution into the next prompt, and is that desirable or harmful?
Answer:
In the DeepSeekMath paper (see “Training and Evaluating DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B”), the authors state simply that “training examples are randomly concatenated until reaching a maximum context length of $4\,\mathrm{K}$ tokens.”  There is no mention of inserting extra special tokens—no bespoke separator marker, no “Instruction:”/“Response:” tags, and no custom BOS/EOS beyond whatever your tokenizer normally provides (in most GPT-style toolkits you automatically get an  or equivalent).

What this means in practice is:

1.  Each SFT example (an English or Chinese prompt paired with its CoT/PoT solution) is serialized end-to-end and then laid out back-to-back in the $4\,\mathrm{K}$ window.  
2.  The only hard break between them is the model’s built-in end-of-text token.  There is no manually-injected “====” line or role-tag to isolate one example from the next.
