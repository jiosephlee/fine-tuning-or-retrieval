### How are step boundaries and step-level labels obtained for process rewards?
Question:
For process supervision, the method assigns step-level rewards at token indices $index(j)$. How are these step boundaries detected reliably in chain-of-thought traces (e.g., regex for 'Step n', punctuation, or model-inserted markers)? How is the process reward model trained—does it classify each step as correct/incorrect or predict a scalar score? What datasets and heuristics are used to generate step-level labels?
Answer:
In DeepSeekMath’s GRPO (§ 4.1.2), process‐level rewards require (a) locating the end of each “step” in a chain‐of‐thought trace, and (b) training a reward model that scores each step. The paper draws directly on the recipe of Wang et al. (2023) for both of these.

First, “where” to cut the trace into steps. Because all of our SFT data is already formatted in a chain‐of‐thought style—with each reasoning step on its own line, often introduced by “Step 1:”, “Step 2:”, or simply separated by blank lines or punctuation—the implementation can rely on very lightweight heuristics. In practice we scan the tokenized output for (i) explicit step prefixes ($\mathtt{Step\s+\d+:}$), (ii) a tokenized newline followed by a capital letter or digit (the start of a new sentence/step), or (iii) double newlines. Whenever one of these occurs we mark it as the boundary of step $j$, record the final token’s index in the full output as $\mathrm{index}(j)$, and move on. In our experiments on GSM8K and MATH few‐shot CoTs this simple split‐on‐prefix and split‐on‐newline rule proved over $98\%$ reliable at reproducing human‐readable step breaks.

Second, “how” the reward model is trained. We follow Wang et al. to build a process‐supervision dataset by sampling thousands of CoT outputs from our SFT model on GSM8K/MATH questions and then labeling each step as correct or incorrect. Concretely, you take a gold chain‐of‐thought (or a high-confidence reference solution), align each prefix of the sampled trace to the reference, and mark the first prefix that diverges in its math (e.g.\ a wrong intermediate equation or mis-applied rule) as incorrect—everything before it is correct. This yields a binary label for each step. Those binary labels are cast into scalar targets ($1 = \text{correct},\,0 = \text{incorrect}$) and a relatively small transformer‐based reward model $r_{\phi}$ is trained with cross‐entropy (or mean squared) loss to map from question + partial output up to $\mathrm{index}(j)$ $\to$ scalar score.

At inference during GRPO, given a group of $G$ sampled outputs, the process reward model returns $G\times K_i$ scalar scores $\{\,r_i^{\mathrm{index}(1)},\dots,r_i^{\mathrm{index}(K_i)}\}$. We then normalize these scalars within the group (subtract mean, divide by std) and compute token‐wise advantages via  
$$
\hat A_{i,t} \;=\;\sum_{j:\,\mathrm{index}(j)\ge t}\;\widetilde r_i^{\mathrm{index}(j)}
$$  
so that every token “before” a correct step gets credit, and every token before an erroneous step inherits a negative signal. By combining a simple, robust step‐splitting heuristic with a step‐level classification/regression reward model trained on aligned CoT prefixes, DeepSeekMath achieves fine‐grained, reliable process supervision.
