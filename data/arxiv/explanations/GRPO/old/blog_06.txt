# Building a Multilingual Math LLM: Lessons on Cross-Lingual Reasoning

Mathematical reasoning has long been a proving ground for large language models (LLMs). Yet most open-source efforts focus almost exclusively on English, leaving non-English learners and researchers underserved. In “DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models,” the authors confront this gap head-on by building a 7-billion-parameter model that tackles math problems in both English and Chinese. In this post, we’ll walk through the key ingredients that made DeepSeekMath multilingual, explore how its cross-lingual training pays dividends, and explain why English-only corpora often stumble on non-English benchmarks.

## Why Multilingual Math Matters

Mathematics is often dubbed a “universal language,” yet the educational resources, worked examples, and problem statements we encounter vary dramatically across different regions and languages. A model trained only on English math pages will struggle when presented with Chinese Gaokao questions or specialized Chinese explanatory texts. In practice, this means students and educators operating in languages other than English see far less benefit from LLMs. Bridging this divide requires:

1. **Diverse data curation** that captures mathematical discourse in multiple tongues.  
2. **Careful decontamination** to avoid leaking benchmark problems into the training set.  
3. **Balanced training and fine-tuning** so that improvements in one language do not erode performance in another.

DeepSeekMath’s journey offers concrete lessons on each of these points.

## Mining Multilingual Math Web Pages

At the heart of DeepSeekMath is its **DeepSeekMath Corpus**—120 billion tokens of high-quality mathematical text drawn from Common Crawl. Unlike prior work that filtered only for English content, the team deliberately kept non-English pages, most notably Chinese. Here’s how they did it:

1. **Seed with High-Quality Examples**  
   They began with OpenWebMath, a curated collection of English math pages, as their positive seed. Negative examples came from random Common Crawl pages.

2. **Train a FastText Classifier**  
   A fastText model was trained to distinguish “math-y” pages based on the seed. With dimensions of 256 and word n-grams up to length 3, it became a lightning-fast filter.

3. **Iterative Domain Expansion**  
   After the first pass, they examined domains where the classifier scored highly (e.g., mathoverflow.net). By manually adding Chinese-language sub-paths—say pages under zhihuishu.com or high school math forums—their seed grew to include bilingual examples. Each iteration improved recall of both English and Chinese math content.

4. **Deduplication and Decontamination**  
   To prevent “leakage” of benchmark problems (like GSM8K or Chinese Gaokao questions), any training page containing a 10-gram overlap with benchmark problems was dropped. This ensures that performance gains reflect genuine reasoning rather than memorization.

By the fourth iteration, the corpus stabilized at 35.5 million pages, roughly 120 billion tokens spanning English, Chinese, and a smattering of other languages. Crucially, nearly 98 percent of this data had been discovered by the third pass, showing that a focused, iterative approach quickly saturates the space of high-quality math pages.

## Transfer Effects Across Languages

What happens when you train a model on English only, then test it on Chinese math benchmarks? In DeepSeekMath’s experiments, the answer was telling: English-only pre-training often **worsened** Chinese math performance compared to no math pre-training at all. By contrast, the multilingual DeepSeekMath Corpus **boosted** accuracy across both English and Chinese tasks.

For example:

- On the Chinese CMATH benchmark, open-source base models saw little to no gain after English-only math pre-training. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, trained on the multilingual corpus, jumped to an accuracy of over 88 percent on CMATH, comfortably outperforming larger English-centric models.

- In Gaokao-MathQA—real‐world Chinese high-school math questions—models trained solely on English data actually regressed. DeepSeekMath-Base’s inclusion of Chinese content reversed that trend, delivering a clear edge.

This multilingual boost stems from two effects:

1. **Direct Exposure**  
   The model sees Chinese problem statements, notation conventions, and common pedagogical phrasing during pre-training. It doesn’t have to “reverse-engineer” Chinese from English examples.

2. **Shared Mathematical Structure**  
   Once the model grasps algebraic reasoning or geometric proofs in one language, many of those patterns carry over. But without language exposure, the model cannot link the symbolic math steps back to Chinese natural language prompts.

In short, **mathematical reasoning and language understanding are tightly coupled**. To excel on non-English benchmarks, LLMs need explicit, high-quality examples in those languages.

## Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning in Two Tongues

Pre-training sets the stage, but fine-tuning sharpens a model’s ability to produce coherent, step-by-step solutions. DeepSeekMath’s approach combined:

- **Supervised Instruction Tuning** with Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning examples in both English and Chinese. Their curated set of 776 thousand examples included K-12 Chinese puzzles and college-level English challenges.

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an efficient variant of PPO that drops the large critic model. By sampling multiple candidate solutions per question and using each group’s score distribution as a baseline, GRPO further hones reasoning quality across the board.

The result? DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B not only beat all other open-source 7B models on English MATH problems but also held its own against Chinese proprietary models on Gaokao and CMATH. With GRPO, the chain-of-thought accuracy on English GSM8K leapt from 82.9 percent to 88.2 percent, while Chinese benchmarks saw similar jumps.

## Why English-Only Corps Fall Short

English-only math corpora can be vast—Proof-Pile-2 clocks in at 52 billion tokens—but they carry two key drawbacks for non-English reasoning:

1. **Vocabulary and Syntax Mismatch**  
   Chinese uses different mathematical conventions (e.g., “求极限” vs. “find the limit”) and different characters for constants or variables. Without seeing these surface forms, a model will flounder on tokenization and context.

2. **Cultural and Curriculum Differences**  
   The style of posing word problems, the types of diagrams used, or the typical difficulty curve can differ drastically. A purely English corpus cannot capture the nuance of Chinese high-school math contests.

DeepSeekMath’s bilingual corpus sidesteps both issues. By purposefully mining and filtering Chinese content alongside English, it lays a foundation for robust cross-lingual reasoning rather than ad-hoc zero-shot guesses.

## Key Takeaways for Building Your Own Multilingual Math LLM

1. **Seed broadly, then specialize**. Start with a trusted set of math pages in multiple languages, train a lightweight classifier, and iteratively mine web data to expand coverage.

2. **Decontaminate with precision**. Use n-gram matching to strip out training pages that overlap with benchmark problems in any language.

3. **Balance your data**. Aim for a healthy mix of English and non-English math examples so that neither language overwhelms the model’s capacity.

4. **Fine-tune bilingually**. Collect instruction-tuning examples in all target languages and formats (natural language, code, tool-assisted) to align the model’s output style.

5. **Use group-based RL**. Techniques like GRPO, which compare multiple candidates per prompt, can efficiently boost reasoning without the heavy memory footprint of a separate value network.

By following these principles, you can bring the power of LLM-driven math tutoring, homework help, or automated proofs to audiences around the globe—no matter the language. With the multilingual momentum set by DeepSeekMath, the next generation of math-savvy LLMs promises to be truly universal.
