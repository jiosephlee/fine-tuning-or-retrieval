Chapter Title: ArXiv in Math Pre-Training: Evidence, Experiments, and Implications

# Background: What Makes Mathematical Pre-Training Work?

Mathematical reasoning challenges language models by demanding precise symbolic manipulation, long-horizon dependencies, and consistently structured arguments. The DeepSeekMath project directly addresses this challenge by building and analyzing a large-scale, domain-specific pre-training corpus and by studying reinforcement learning strategies that further shape reasoning behavior. A pivotal component of the work is the construction of the DeepSeekMath Corpus from Common Crawl, which contains approximately 120B math-related tokens mined via a fastText-based classifier. This corpus underpins the strong performance of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B and subsequent instruction-tuned and reinforcement-learned variants.

There are three properties of the DeepSeekMath Corpus that are central to understanding why it is effective. First, it is high quality: models trained on it outperform models trained on prominent mathematical corpora across diverse benchmarks. Second, it is multilingual, primarily spanning English and Chinese, with measurable benefits on both English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks. Third, it is large-scale: the sheer volume of high-quality mathematical text provides consistent improvements without the rapid performance saturation seen when training on smaller corpora that must be repeated.

Within this context, the role of arXiv papers is an important question. Many prior efforts in mathematical pre-training have incorporated arXiv extensively. However, the DeepSeekMath study separates out and tests arXiv’s actual contribution under controlled conditions. The outcome of these tests drives a clear conclusion for their setup: in their experiments, arXiv papers did not yield notable gains in mathematical reasoning benchmarks, and thus their inclusion or exclusion in the pre-training mixture would most likely have little effect.

# The Data Landscape: Web-Scale Math Versus arXiv

The DeepSeekMath Corpus is collected through an iterative mining process over deduplicated Common Crawl pages. Beginning with a seed set derived from OpenWebMath, a fastText classifier is trained to retrieve additional mathematical content at web scale. After ranking pages by classifier confidence and filtering for benchmark decontamination, the process is repeated with an expanded seed comprising additional domains and human-annotated URL segments to capture more diverse mathematical sources. After four iterations, the authors obtain 35.5M mathematical web pages totaling 120B tokens. Evaluation shows that this corpus provides superior downstream gains compared to other math corpora, including those that rely heavily on arXiv.

By contrast, arXiv papers are a conventional ingredient in math corpora, often introduced with the intuition that formal academic texts should convey high-quality mathematical knowledge and structure. Indeed, several baselines and related corpora include arXiv as a major component. Nonetheless, empirical evidence must determine whether this inclusion is effective for improving reasoning performance in language models.

To this end, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is trained on a mixture that includes arXiv, but the authors also perform ablations designed specifically to test arXiv’s unique contribution. Their results allow a direct comparison between outcomes driven by web-mined mathematical content and outcomes driven by arXiv-only pre-training.

# The Experiments: How arXiv Was Tested

The authors investigate the effect of arXiv by conducting pre-training experiments on models of two sizes—a 1.3B-parameter model (DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B) and a 7B-parameter model (DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B)—using arXiv-focused corpora. They adopt two distinct arXiv sources to ensure that the conclusion is not tied to a single pipeline:

1) MathPile: an 8.9B-token corpus built from multiple sources with heuristic cleaning and filtering, of which more than 85% are scientific arXiv papers.

2) ArXiv-RedPajama: approximately 28.0B tokens extracted from arXiv LaTeX files with preambles, comments, macros, and bibliographies removed.

The training protocol mirrors the general settings used elsewhere in the paper to ensure a fair comparison. For DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B, they train for 150B tokens on each arXiv corpus. For DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, they train for 40B tokens on each arXiv corpus. The evaluations target a broad range of mathematical competencies:

- Quantitative reasoning benchmarks without tool use such as GSM8K and the competition-level MATH dataset.

- STEM multiple-choice reasoning via MMLU-STEM.

- Formal mathematics via informal-to-formal theorem proving on miniF2F with Isabelle.

Under these controlled settings, the question is whether arXiv-only pre-training improves any of these math-focused abilities, either in aggregate or in any specific subdomain.

# The Findings: ArXiv Was Ineffective in This Setting

The results are consistent across model sizes and arXiv data variants. When trained on an arXiv-only corpus, both DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B show no notable improvements on the evaluated mathematical benchmarks. In some cases, there is even deterioration relative to baselines. This pattern appears across quantitative reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH), multiple-choice STEM reasoning (MMLU-STEM), and formal mathematics (miniF2F).

The authors therefore conclude, in the scope of their experiments, that arXiv papers seem ineffective at improving mathematical reasoning. This conclusion is notable because other components of their pipeline do show clear benefits. In particular, the DeepSeekMath Corpus assembled from the web not only scales larger than prior alternatives but also exhibits higher average quality as measured by consistent downstream gains. The contrast highlights that not all mathematical text sources contribute equally to learned reasoning behaviors, at least under the training conditions tested here.

# Interpreting the Conclusion: Why Removing arXiv Likely Changes Little

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is trained on a heterogeneous mixture totaling 500B tokens, with 10% drawn from arXiv. Given the ablation results described above, the most reasonable expectation—grounded in the paper’s evidence—is that removing arXiv from this mixture would most likely result in no notable change to the measured mathematical reasoning performance. This inference rests on two pillars established in the text.

First, the dedicated arXiv-only experiments indicate that pre-training on arXiv does not translate into gains on the targeted math benchmarks and sometimes correlates with declines. If a data source is ineffective in isolation, adding it to a mixture tends not to be a driver of improvements unless it plays a synergistic role with other sources. The study does not observe such synergy.

Second, the web-mined DeepSeekMath Corpus itself yields strong and sustained improvements, outperforming other math corpora in both English and Chinese. The learning curves on this corpus are steeper and less prone to early plateauing. This suggests that the heavy lifting in performance gains is already accomplished by the web-mined mathematical content. Removing a source that did not prove beneficial in controlled tests is, therefore, unlikely to degrade the final outcome in a meaningful way under the same training setup.

Together, these observations justify the conclusion that excluding arXiv would most likely leave performance essentially unchanged for the benchmarks and settings studied.

# Important Boundaries: What This Conclusion Does Not Claim

While the evidence in this work is clear for the tested settings, the authors make explicit that their conclusion has limitations and should be interpreted with care. They highlight several untested areas where arXiv might still matter:

- Task specificity: The experiments do not assess tasks such as informalization of theorems, where mapping formal content to informal explanations might benefit from arXiv-style exposition.

- Data interactions: The effect of arXiv when combined with other data types in more complex mixtures is not fully explored. It remains possible that certain mixtures or sampling strategies could elicit complementary effects.

- Scale dependence: Benefits might emerge at larger model scales. The present experiments use 1.3B and 7B models for the arXiv ablations; different dynamics could arise for substantially larger models.

These caveats mean that the claim is intentionally circumscribed. It applies to the training mixtures, model scales, and evaluation settings used in this paper. Within that scope, arXiv showed no notable benefit; outside it, the question is left open for future work.

# Practical Implications for Pre-Training Design

The practical message for dataset construction, as conveyed by the paper’s results, is twofold. First, carefully mined web-scale mathematical content can be both high quality and effective, particularly when collected via a targeted pipeline that iteratively improves coverage and diversity. This corpus, being multilingual and large, not only advances English benchmarks but also strengthens Chinese mathematical reasoning—an advantage not observed for English-centric corpora.

Second, the conventional wisdom that arXiv should be a staple component of math pre-training warrants re-examination. In the tested setting, arXiv does not deliver the anticipated gains. For practitioners operating at similar scales and with similar evaluation targets, omitting arXiv would most likely not change outcomes in a noticeable way. Given finite training budgets, resources might be better allocated to expanding and refining web-mined mathematical data, ensuring multilingual coverage, and maintaining rigorous decontamination to avoid benchmark leakage.

At the same time, the authors openly acknowledge that further investigation is needed. Exploring different mixtures, larger scales, and task-specific scenarios may uncover contexts in which arXiv becomes more valuable. Until such evidence is shown, the present findings justify a strong emphasis on high-quality web-mined mathematical corpora as the primary driver of improved reasoning performance.

# Summary

The DeepSeekMath study constructs a large, multilingual, high-quality mathematical corpus from Common Crawl and demonstrates that it yields strong improvements across diverse benchmarks. In contrast, the paper’s controlled ablations show that arXiv-only pre-training does not improve, and can even degrade, performance on quantitative reasoning, STEM multiple choice, and formal mathematics. Given these findings, removing arXiv from the overall pre-training mixture used for DeepSeekMath-Base 7B would most likely result in no notable change in performance under the same experimental conditions. The authors emphasize that this conclusion is bounded by their setup and scales, and they point to future research directions where arXiv’s value could be reassessed.
