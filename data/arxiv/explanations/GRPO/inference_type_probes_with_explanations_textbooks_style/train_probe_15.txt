Chapter Title: Multilingual Pre-Training for Mathematical Reasoning: Evidence from DeepSeekMath

# The Role of Corpus Design in Mathematical Reasoning

Mathematical reasoning in large language models depends critically on the nature of the pre-training data. The DeepSeekMath study underscores that corpus composition—not merely model size—plays a decisive role in downstream performance. In particular, the paper builds a case that multilingual data coverage is a key factor for strong results on Chinese mathematical benchmarks. This stands in contrast to prior work that focuses on English-only mathematical web data. The central empirical observation is that the DeepSeekMath Corpus, which is multilingual with substantial English and Chinese content, improves mathematical reasoning not only in English but also in Chinese. Conversely, English-centric corpora can yield limited gains and may even hinder Chinese performance.

This chapter elaborates on how the DeepSeekMath pipeline constructs a multilingual corpus from Common Crawl, why this multilinguality matters for Chinese mathematical reasoning, and how the reported evaluations support the claim that restricting the corpus to English would likely lead to worse performance on Chinese benchmarks. We proceed by mapping the data collection process, summarizing the multilingual characteristics of the corpus, and linking these characteristics to the cross-lingual evaluation outcomes reported in the paper.

# Constructing a Multilingual Mathematical Corpus from Common Crawl

The DeepSeekMath Corpus is built through an iterative selection process applied to Common Crawl. The pipeline begins with a seed corpus of high-quality mathematical text and expands to a large-scale set of mathematical web pages. The process uses a fastText classifier trained to identify web pages with mathematical content. In early iterations, the classifier is trained with OpenWebMath-like positives and randomly sampled negatives from Common Crawl. In later iterations, the positive set is enriched by mining math-heavy domains and annotating math-relevant URLs within those domains. After four iterations, the pipeline yields approximately \(35.5\) million mathematical web pages, totaling \(120\) billion tokens.

A defining attribute of the DeepSeekMath Corpus is that it is multilingual. The paper explicitly states that it encompasses multiple languages, with English and Chinese being the most represented. This multilinguality does not arise by accident. It follows from the choice to mine mathematical content broadly from Common Crawl, rather than restricting selection to English-language pages. The iterative domain-driven expansion also naturally incorporates math content in non-English communities, once their domains are identified and annotated as math-relevant. The resulting corpus thus departs from prior English-only constructions by design.

The paper also notes a rigorous decontamination stage to avoid benchmark leakage. Decontamination filters any pages containing exact \(10\)-gram matches to test benchmarks, with additional exact matching rules for shorter substrings. This prevents artificially inflated performance on evaluation sets in both English and Chinese. The multilingual decontamination policy matters because it ensures that any measured gains on Chinese mathematical tasks are not artifacts of contamination.

# Evidence That Multilingual Pre-Training Improves Chinese Reasoning

The study provides two complementary lines of evidence that multilingual pre-training improves performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks.

First, when validating corpus quality using a controlled \(1.3\)B-parameter model trained for \(150\)B tokens, the paper compares different mathematical corpora: MathPile, OpenWebMath, and Proof-Pile-2. In this comparison, the DeepSeekMath Corpus leads across multiple mathematical benchmarks. Crucially, the authors highlight that DeepSeekMath’s multilingual coverage enhances performance in both English and Chinese. In contrast, English-centric corpora “show limited improvement and may even hinder performance in Chinese mathematical reasoning.” This comparative observation directly supports the claim that restricting the corpus to English would likely degrade Chinese benchmark results.

Second, the paper’s broader evaluations with DeepSeekMath-Base \(7\)B and its instruction-tuned and RL-refined variants show strong Chinese performance. The authors attribute their Chinese gains in part to the multilingual nature of the pre-training data. They state that their model is superior on Chinese benchmarks, “likely because we don’t follow previous works to collect English-only math pre-training data, and also include high-quality non-English ones.” This attribution links improvements on Chinese benchmarks to multilingual data coverage and contrasts the multilingual approach with English-only pre-training regimes.

Together, these points yield one coherent conclusion: multilinguality in the pre-training corpus confers an advantage on Chinese mathematical benchmarks, and restricting the corpus to English would work against that advantage.

# Why Multilingual Coverage Matters in This Setting

Within the constraints of the paper, we can analyze why multilingual coverage matters by carefully examining how the data pipeline and evaluations interact.

The data selection pipeline is designed to recall diverse mathematical content from Common Crawl by iteratively refining the fastText classifier and manually annotating math-oriented domains. Because this process operates on the full multilingual web, it naturally exposes the model to mathematical expressions, problem styles, and explanatory conventions across languages. The paper repeatedly emphasizes that the resulting corpus contains substantial English and Chinese content, and that this design decision correlates with improved Chinese benchmark performance. 

Moreover, the authors explicitly compare their multilingual corpus to prior English-only corpora and report that English-centric training can even hinder Chinese performance. This observation suggests that merely scaling English data does not automatically transfer to Chinese tasks. It also suggests that when the target evaluations include Chinese benchmarks, the pre-training distribution must include Chinese mathematical content to avoid degraded performance. The evaluations in the paper are consistent with this implication: they report gains in both English and Chinese, with a specific note that multilingual training improves Chinese reasoning while English-only corpora may not.

Finally, in training DeepSeekMath-Base \(7\)B, beyond the \(56\%\) drawn from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, the data mix includes both English and Chinese in the remaining components, including natural language from Common Crawl. Thus, multilingual exposure is not limited to the purely mathematical segment. This reinforces the model’s capacity to generate step-by-step reasoning and tool-integrated solutions in both languages. The reported improvements on Chinese benchmarks align with this multilingual exposure at both the mathematical and general-language levels.

# Comparative Perspective: English-Only vs. Multilingual Corpora

The paper positions DeepSeekMath’s corpus strategy relative to prior work. OpenWebMath and Proof-Pile-2 are discussed as strong baselines, but they are characterized as primarily English-centric. In the study’s controlled experiments, when models are trained on these English-only or English-dominant corpora, the gains on Chinese benchmarks are limited and can even be negative. This contrasts with DeepSeekMath’s multilingual approach, which shows positive performance on both English and Chinese benchmarks.

The authors also report that DeepSeekMath-Base \(7\)B surpasses existing open-source base models across a range of benchmarks and is particularly strong on Chinese benchmarks. They further support this claim by noting that they did not restrict the pre-training corpus to English; instead, they intentionally incorporated high-quality non-English data. The comparative findings are consistent across the model’s development stages, from base pre-training to instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, and they reinforce the central insight that multilinguality is beneficial for Chinese mathematical reasoning performance.

# Implications for Corpus Restriction and Expected Performance

The conclusions drawn in the paper lead directly to the following implication: if the pre-training corpus were restricted to English, performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks would likely worsen. The argument proceeds from two specific observations reported by the authors:

1. The DeepSeekMath Corpus encompasses English and Chinese and improves performance on both English and Chinese benchmarks. The paper states that this multilingual coverage yields measurable gains in Chinese reasoning tasks.

2. English-centric corpora show limited improvement and can even hinder Chinese performance. This is explicitly noted in the corpus validation results and the comparative narrative of the study.

Taken together, these findings imply that English-only pre-training would deprive the model of the multilingual mathematical signal that the DeepSeekMath Corpus provides, and would therefore be expected to reduce performance on Chinese benchmarks. The authors’ wording is cautious yet clear: they attribute their superiority on Chinese benchmarks to not following English-only collection strategies and to including high-quality non-English data. Hence, restricting the corpus to English would remove a factor that the paper identifies as beneficial for Chinese performance.

# Connecting Multilingual Pre-Training to Broader Outcomes

Beyond the central claim about Chinese benchmarks, the paper’s results show that the benefits of multilingual math pre-training coexist with strong English performance. The DeepSeekMath-Base \(7\)B model is competitive with or superior to large-scale baselines on English mathematical benchmarks, and it shows gains on general reasoning and understanding benchmarks as well. This suggests that incorporating multilingual content does not dilute English mathematical capability. Instead, it supports a broader strengthening of reasoning skills across languages.

The study also reports improvements in Chinese benchmarks after instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, even when the reinforcement learning phase uses a subset of English instruction-tuning data. The overall trajectory—from pre-training on a multilingual corpus to instruction tuning and GRPO-based reinforcement learning—cumulatively yields robust performance across both English and Chinese tasks. This coherence supports the conclusion that multilingual mathematical pre-training is a sound design choice when aiming for strong performance in Chinese mathematical reasoning.

# Conclusion

The DeepSeekMath study provides explicit evidence that multilingual pre-training—especially the inclusion of both English and Chinese mathematical content—improves performance on Chinese benchmarks. In contrast, English-only corpora can lead to limited gains or even hinder Chinese performance. The data collection pipeline’s multilingual reach, combined with careful decontamination and large-scale coverage, underpins these results. The authors’ evaluations and comparative analyses consistently support the conclusion that restricting the corpus to English would likely yield worse performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks.
