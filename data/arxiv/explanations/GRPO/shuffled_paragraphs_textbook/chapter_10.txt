Why iterative RL and replay matter. As the policy improves, the distribution of candidate outputs shifts; a reward model trained only on old data may become misaligned. Iterating with periodic retraining of the reward model on fresh outputs (and a historical fraction) keeps the reward signal relevant and mitigates drift. The 10% historical-replay strategy trades off freshness and stability. The authors also observed that performing two iterations of this iterative RL yields noticeable gains, particularly in the first iteration.

Trade-offs and practical implications. Larger G increases sampling and inference cost for each prompt because the policy must generate and the reward model must score many outputs. This raises compute and storage demands for exploration. Smaller G reduces cost but yields noisier baselines and possibly inferior training stability. The authors chose G=64 as a practical working point that yielded strong performance; practitioners should balance compute budget and baseline variance when selecting G.

Instead of moving the KL penalty into the scalar reward (as is commonly done in PPO with per-token KL in the reward), GRPO adds a direct KL regularizer to the GRPO objective:

This pipeline trades the memory and complexity of a learned critic for a statistically estimated group baseline. It leverages the comparative nature of reward models (pairwise preferences) and produces per-token advantages either by broadcasting normalized outcome-level scores or by composing normalized step-level signals into cumulative advantages. With appropriate choices of G, clipping \(\varepsilon\), KL coefficient \(\beta\), and conservative learning rates, GRPO delivers significant gains in mathematical reasoning while reducing the memory footprint relative to PPO.

- Group-normalized advantages align well with reward models trained on comparisons and remove the need for an explicit critic, yielding substantial memory savings.

Why this matters. Process supervision produces fine-grained, temporally-aware learning signals that can better guide multi-step chain-of-thought style reasoning. The paper reports that GRPO combined with process supervision (GRPO+PS) outperforms GRPO with outcome supervision (GRPO+OS), showing the value of step-aware rewards for complex mathematical reasoning. From an algorithmic viewpoint, process supervision gives different gradient coefficients for different tokens depending on the part of the reasoning they support, which can more precisely raise the probability of good intermediate steps and lower it for harmful ones.

- Batch size and parallelism: Training batch size for policy updates was 1024 (number of prompts per batch). Combined with G=64, this implies heavy parallel inference and reward scoring, so use distributed or batched inference for both policy and reward models.

Intuition and practical effect. Normalizing by the group mean and standard deviation centers the advantages so that the group average advantage is zero and the scale is comparable across groups. This implements a relative baseline: an output's advantage depends only on how it compares to the other outputs sampled for the same question. That design suits reward models trained on pairwise comparisons and removes the need to fit a separate value function for per-token baselines. In practice the authors used large groups (see the Group size section), making the group statistics a reasonably stable baseline and providing a direct variance reduction mechanism.

\mathbb{D}_{KL}\big[\pi_\theta\|\pi_{ref}\big] \approx

- \log\frac{\pi_{ref}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}
- 1,

- Critic-free variance reduction. Using group statistics substitutes for a learned value function by providing a sample-based baseline across alternatives. When G is large enough the sample baseline performs the variance-reduction role with much lower memory cost.

- Comparative alignment. Reward models are often trained on pairwise preferences; normalizing within a reasonably large sample respects the comparative structure of those reward signals and yields advantages consistent with relative quality.
