According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the clip term in GRPO's PPO-style objective primarily guards against the risk of large policy shifts. This follows directly from the GRPO objective (Equation (4)), which retains PPO’s clipped surrogate: for each token the update is the minimum of the unbounded importance-weighted term, \( \frac{\pi_\theta(o_{t}\mid q,o_{<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{t}\mid q,o_{<t})} \hat{A}_{t} \), and its clipped counterpart, \( \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}},\,1-\varepsilon,\,1+\varepsilon\right)\hat{A}_{t} \). As in PPO, the clipping constrains the effective importance ratio to lie in \([1-\varepsilon,\,1+\varepsilon]\), thereby preventing excessively large gradient steps when the new policy deviates too much from the old policy on a token-level basis. The paper explicitly notes that \(\varepsilon\) is “a clipping-related hyper-parameter introduced in PPO for stabilizing training,” which is precisely about mitigating unstable, large policy shifts that can arise from high-variance advantages or skewed importance weights.

This stabilizing role is especially salient in GRPO because it removes the learned value function and computes advantages from group-relative normalized rewards (either outcome-level \(\widetilde{r}_i\) or process-level \(\sum_{j:\,index(j)\ge t}\widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}\)). Such normalization can produce sharp per-token signals if the relative rewards within a group are high contrast, making the raw importance-weighted term more prone to overshooting. While GRPO also adds a KL regularizer, \(-\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}]\), to anchor the policy to a reference, the clip term acts locally and immediately at each token to bound the effective update magnitude, complementing the global KL penalty. Together, the clipped surrogate and KL regularization ensure that updates do not induce large policy shifts: the clip caps the per-token ratio, and the KL discourages distribution-wide drift, yielding the training stability the paper emphasizes.
