In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the decontamination procedure removes any text segment that contains an exact n-gram match with evaluation benchmarks, and therefore the rule implicitly assumes that a 10-gram exact match suffices. This is directly supported by their stated filtering criteria: for English and Chinese math benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, AGIEval), they remove “any text segment containing a 10-gram string that matches exactly with any sub-string from the evaluation benchmarks,” while using exact matching down to 3-grams only when benchmark texts themselves are shorter than 10 tokens. The choice of an exact 10-gram threshold operationalizes the assumption that a contiguous sequence of 10 tokens is specific enough to reliably indicate benchmark leakage without requiring more complex semantic or fuzzy matching. It balances precision against practicality: a 10-gram is long enough to sharply reduce false positives in noisy web text, yet short enough to be computationally tractable across billions of tokens.

Statistically, the likelihood of a spurious 10-gram exact match in unrelated web text is negligible under reasonable token distributions, which makes a 10-gram a high-precision proxy for contamination. If we denote the (effective) token vocabulary size by \(V\) and assume approximate independence for contiguous tokens at the granularity used in matching, the probability that a random 10-token sequence matches a particular benchmark 10-gram exactly is on the order of \(V^{-10}\). Even accounting for corpus-scale multiple comparisons—say \(N\) candidate 10-grams scanned across Common Crawl—the expected number of accidental matches is roughly \(N \cdot V^{-10}\), which remains vanishingly small for typical values (e.g., \(V \sim 10^4\)–\(10^5\) and \(N \sim 10^{11}\)–\(10^{12}\)). This probabilistic argument explains why a 10-gram exact match “suffices”: it delivers high recall for genuine benchmark overlap while maintaining very low false positive rates. For benchmark snippets shorter than 10 tokens, they relax to exact matches at \(\geq 3\)-grams to avoid missing short but distinctive items, which preserves the same logic—use the longest exact match available to maintain specificity without overfiltering.
