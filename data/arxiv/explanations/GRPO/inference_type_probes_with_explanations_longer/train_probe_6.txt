According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', if the DeepSeekMath Corpus had been English-only, the authors would most likely expect the model's performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks to be worse performance. The paper explicitly emphasizes that the DeepSeekMath Corpus is multilingual—“predominantly featuring English and Chinese”—and reports that training on this corpus “enhances mathematical reasoning performance in both English and Chinese,” while “existing mathematical corpora, which are primarily English-centric, show limited improvement and may even hinder performance in Chinese mathematical reasoning.” They further note that DeepSeekMath-Base is “superior on Chinese benchmarks, likely because we…include high-quality non-English ones,” directly linking the inclusion of Chinese math data to stronger out-of-English performance. Under this evidence, an English-only corpus would remove precisely the data modality credited for the gains in Chinese, so the expected effect would be degraded accuracy on Chinese tasks like MGSM-zh, CMATH, and Gaokao-Math benchmarks.

More generally, the authors’ evaluation narrative supports a data–domain alignment argument: performance improvements arise from coverage of linguistic and notational conventions specific to each language. Mathematical discourse in Chinese differs lexically, syntactically, and in the idioms used to express steps and statements; without exposure to such patterns during pre-training, the conditional distribution \( \pi_\theta(o \mid q) \) over reasoning tokens for Chinese prompts is poorly calibrated. The paper’s comparative results show that multilingual pre-training produces steeper learning curves and “more lasting improvements,” while English-centric corpora plateau and underperform on Chinese. Coupled with their finding that adding non-English tokens is a likely cause of superiority on Chinese benchmarks, it is sensible to infer that removing those tokens—i.e., making the corpus English-only—would lower the expected accuracy \( \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{Chinese}} \), consistent with their observed trend that language coverage is a key driver of cross-lingual mathematical reasoning quality.
