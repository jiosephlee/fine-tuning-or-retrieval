In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', preserving only the top-ranking pages predicted by the fastText model as part of the math corpus rests on the unstated assumption that rank indicates math quality. This is sensible because the fastText classifier is trained to discriminate math-related pages from general web pages using a curated set of positive examples (OpenWebMath and later human-annotated math URLs) and diverse negatives; its score for a page effectively serves as a proxy for the posterior likelihood of being math content, i.e., a score function \(s(x) \approx \mathbb{P}(y=\text{math}\mid x)\). By ranking pages by \(s(x)\) and keeping only the top tail, the pipeline prioritizes content closest (in the learned embedding/feature space) to the distribution of high-quality math text. Since the positive distribution is iteratively refined with human annotation and domain-level URL targeting, the classifier’s decision boundary and score calibration improve across iterations, strengthening the monotonic relationship between \(s(x)\) and useful math signal. In practice, this implements a high-precision filter where the retained set has a higher expected density of mathematically relevant tokens, even if “quality” is multidimensional.

The paper empirically validates that this ranking-as-quality proxy works: models trained on the resulting DeepSeekMath Corpus achieve superior downstream performance relative to baselines, even at comparable token counts, and exhibit steeper learning curves as scale increases. Formally, if we define a latent utility \(q(x)\) for a page \(x\) as its expected contribution to math reasoning capabilities after pre-training, the pipeline assumes a positive correlation \(s(x) \uparrow \Rightarrow q(x) \uparrow\). The observed gains—DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforming larger or arXiv-heavy corpora and the 1.3B ablations showing better performance at 50B tokens than Proof-Pile-2—support that selecting top-\(k\) pages by \(s(x)\) increases \(\mathbb{E}[q(X)\mid X \in \text{Top-}k]\). Additional safeguards like deduplication, decontamination via exact \(n\)-gram filtering, and multilingual coverage reduce noise and leakage, further aligning high \(s(x)\) with practical math quality. Thus, while “quality” is not explicitly measured at crawl time, the iterative, score-ranked selection coupled with downstream validation makes the assumption both operational and effective.
