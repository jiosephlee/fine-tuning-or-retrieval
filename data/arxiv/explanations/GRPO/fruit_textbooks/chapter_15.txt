# Reward model n and uncertainty estimation
The paper identifies the reward model as a critical n and lists three priorities for future research on reward modeling. First, improving the d ability of reward models so they accurately evaluate out-of-distribution questions and d outputs is essential. If the reward model cannot e, RL will mainly produce a policy that fits the reward model’s idiosyncrasies rather than improving true problem-solving ability.

Second, explicitly modeling the uncertainty of reward predictions is proposed as an important direction. A reward model that returns both a score and an uncertainty estimate allows the RL algorithm to temper updates when the model is unsure and to weight high-confidence signals more strongly. This uncertainty-aware approach is consonant with the weak-to-strong alignment idea: it treats reward outputs as noisy measurements, not absolute truth, and uses uncertainty to modulate learning.

Third, the paper emphasizes developing high-quality process reward models that score intermediate reasoning steps, not just final outcomes. Process-level scores provide denser supervision, which can be especially valuable in complex reasoning tasks where a single o at the end gives little guidance. In short, investment in reward-model robustness, calibration, and stepwise scoring is highlighted as a key route to more effective RL.

# Building scalable process reward models
Process supervision—rewarding intermediate reasoning steps—was shown in the paper to yield stronger improvements than outcome-only supervision when used inside >. However, producing reliable process reward models at scale is challenging: it requires annotated t where individual reasoning steps are scored or compared. The paper discusses an iterative strategy used in RL: generate candidate outputs, use comparisons or heuristics to label them, and then continually re-train the reward model with a replay buffer.

Future work should address both the annotation and the algorithmic aspects of scalable process reward models. On the annotation side, one avenue is to bootstrap process labels using rule-based checks or automated theorem provers where applicable; on the algorithmic side, efficient training loops (including replay mechanisms and iterative re-labeling) are essential to keep the reward model synchronized with a changing policy. The research goal is to make process reward models both more accurate and cheaper to create, so that fine-grained supervisory signals become a practical part of RL pipelines for reasoning tasks.
