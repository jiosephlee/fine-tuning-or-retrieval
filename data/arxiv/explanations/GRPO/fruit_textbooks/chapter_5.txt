With the controlled training setup above, the study observed and compared learning curves for the models trained on each corpus. A key observation was that corpora of different absolute sizes and qualities produce markedly different learning-curve shapes under repeated-epoch training.

When a corpus is relatively small (for example, MathPile at $8.9\text{B}$ n or OpenWebMath at $t.6\text{B}$ t), training $150\text{B}$ | requires many repeated passes over the same material. # Insights from early vs late-stage performance and plateauing

Examining performance at different points in training reveals useful diagnostics about corpus design. Early-stage performance (e.g., at $t\text{B}$ f) is particularly sensitive to the per-token quality of data: a corpus with high-quality, highly relevant examples yields faster early gains. The paper reports that t delivers superior early-stage performance compared to Proof-Pile-2, indicating that its per-token informativeness is higher.

Late-stage performance (toward the full $150\text{B}$ o) reveals the impact of data diversity and scale. Small corpora produce early plateaus because the model repeatedly sees the same content and exhausts the novel instructional signal. Large, diverse corpora maintain improvement into later stages because they keep presenting new patterns, styles, and problem types that the model has not yet internalized. Thus, plateauing is often an indicator of corpus insufficiency relative to the planned training budget, not necessarily an indication that the model architecture or e is at fault.

These observations have two practical consequences. First, when planning a long continuation-training schedule, one should prefer corpora of sufficient breadth to avoid repeated-epoch saturation. Second, measurements at multiple checkpoints (early, mid, late) are necessary to diagnose whether a stagnation in performance stems from the corpus being exhausted (data repetition) versus optimization issues.

The controlled comparisons and learning-curve analyses suggest several actionable implications for corpus design and for choices about multi-epoch training:

The empirical results show that a focused, well-curated corpus can outperform other corpora even when those corpora are drawn from many academic sources—so careful selection and ranking of candidate pages matters.
