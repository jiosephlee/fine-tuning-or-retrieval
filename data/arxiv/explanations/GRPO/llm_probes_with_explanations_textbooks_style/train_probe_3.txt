Chapter Title: Why Code-Initialized Pre-Training Improves Mathematical Reasoning

# The Role of Initialization in Domain-Specific Mathematical Models
DeepSeekMath is presented as a domain-specific language model designed to excel at mathematical reasoning. A central design choice in this work is the decision to initialize DeepSeekMath-Base 7B from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B rather than from a general-purpose LLM. The paper explicitly observes that starting from a code-trained checkpoint is a better choice than starting from a general LLM. This choice is not incidental; it underpins the model’s downstream performance on a wide range of mathematical tasks, both with and without tool use, and across English and Chinese benchmarks.

The evidence is empirical and multifaceted. First, the authors demonstrate that DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, initialized from a code model and then continually trained for $500$B tokens on a mixture that includes $56\\%$ of the DeepSeekMath Corpus and substantial code, outperforms strong open-source base models and even matches or surpasses much larger closed models on several math benchmarks. Second, they conduct detailed ablation-style experiments that isolate the effect of code training on subsequent mathematical training. These experiments support the broader finding: code initialization confers tangible benefits to mathematical reasoning performance, whereas starting from a general LLM would most likely miss those advantages and yield weaker math outcomes.

# Evidence that Code Training Improves Mathematical Reasoning
The paper directly addresses the popular hypothesis that code training improves reasoning by running controlled training protocols. The authors compare models that undergo code training before math training against models that do not, accounting for two regimes: a two-stage pipeline and a one-stage pipeline. In both regimes, the benefits of code are documented for program-aided reasoning and, crucially, for mathematical reasoning without tool use.

In the two-stage setting, one variant trains DeepSeek-LLM $1.3$B on $400$B code tokens first, followed by $150$B math tokens. As a comparison point, another variant replaces the $400$B code tokens with $400$B general tokens (sampled from a large-scale general corpus) before the same $150$B math tokens. The results show that code training alone already significantly enhances the ability to solve GSM8K and MATH using Python, and subsequent math training further boosts performance. By contrast, substituting general tokens for code tokens does not deliver these gains. In other words, code training is not merely equivalent to generic pre-training at scale; it is specifically more efficacious for mathematical reasoning.

In the one-stage setting, the authors mix $400$B code tokens with $150$B math tokens and train in a single pass. This mixed training maintains and even synergizes coding skills and program-aided mathematical reasoning. Although mixing code and math tokens in a single stage compromises mathematical reasoning without tool use for the $1.3$B model—likely due to limited capacity—the model still benefits in the program-aided setting. Across these experiments, the pattern is consistent: code training enhances performance on math tasks, indicates better sample efficiency for later math training, and strengthens robustness in tool-based solving.

# Empirical Outcomes with Code-Initialized DeepSeekMath-Base 7B
The practical consequence of initializing from a code-trained checkpoint is clear in the reported numbers. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B achieves strong results on diverse benchmarks. Without relying on external tools, it leads open-source base models across eight English and Chinese datasets, including GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, and MMLU-STEM, often by a large margin. On the competition-level MATH dataset, it surpasses existing open-source base models by more than $10\\%$ absolute and even outperforms Minerva $540$B, a closed-source base model roughly $77$ times larger.

When tool use is allowed via program-of-thought prompting, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B also outperforms a strong math-pretrained baseline (Llemma $34$B) on GSM8K and MATH. The model demonstrates competency in formal mathematics as well, showing strong few-shot autoformalization performance on miniF2F with Isabelle by generating proof sketches that can be completed by an automated prover. These results provide converging evidence: starting from a code-trained foundation makes the math-specialized model more capable across settings—free-form reasoning, tool-integrated problem solving, and formal proof tasks.

# Why Code Helps: Intuition from the Training Dynamics
The paper offers an intuitive reading of why code training helps mathematical reasoning. Code data encourages the model to internalize structured thinking, precise syntax, and stepwise execution patterns. When the model later encounters math data—especially tasks that require multi-step derivations, consistent notation, and algorithmic computation—these code-induced competencies transfer. This transfer appears in two ways documented by the authors.

First, program-aided reasoning benefits substantially. With Python-based problem solving, models must translate problem statements into sequences of executable instructions, use libraries such as \textit{math} and \textit{sympy}, and interpret results. Prior code exposure equips the model to write correct and reliable programs, which directly raises accuracy on datasets like GSM8K and MATH under program-of-thought prompting.

Second, even without tools, code training improves chain-of-thought performance. The effect is evident in the two-stage experiments: an initial code training stage yields moderate improvements in mathematical reasoning without tool use and increases the efficiency of subsequent math training. The model learns to organize reasoning steps coherently, respect constraints, and avoid brittle logical jumps—properties that carry over from programming practice to mathematical discourse.

# Initialization Choice and Its Downstream Implications
Given these findings, the initialization choice critically influences downstream math performance. DeepSeekMath initializes from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B specifically because a code-trained base confers advantages that a general LLM does not. The contributions section states this choice explicitly: starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM, and code training prior to math training improves models’ ability to solve mathematical problems both with and without tool use. The extensive evaluations further corroborate that math training on top of a code foundation produces better results on GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, and related benchmarks.

The paper also shows that math pre-training improves general reasoning capabilities beyond mathematics. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B exhibits significant enhancements on MMLU and BBH relative to its precursor DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, while maintaining code performance on HumanEval and MBPP by continuing to include code tokens. This reinforces the idea that code initialization not only sets a stronger foundation for math but also aligns the model toward more rigorous reasoning broadly.

# Contrasting Unhelpful Data Choices: The Case of arXiv
To further clarify what does and does not help mathematical reasoning, the paper analyzes the role of arXiv papers in pre-training. Although arXiv is commonly included in math-related corpora, the experiments reported here find that arXiv-only training does not improve math performance and can even degrade it, across models of different sizes and multiple benchmarks including GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and miniF2F. This negative result stands as a counterpoint to the positive effect of code initialization. It emphasizes that not all mathematically flavored data is equally beneficial, and that careful data selection matters as much as, if not more than, raw scale.

At the same time, the authors clarify the limitations of this finding: they have not tested all possible math-related tasks, combinations with other data, or scaling regimes where arXiv might show benefits. Nevertheless, within the scope of the experiments conducted, arXiv tokens did not contribute positively, while code tokens did.

# Reinforcement Learning Builds on a Strong Initialization
After supervised instruction tuning, the paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a resource-efficient variant of PPO that removes the critic and estimates baselines from group scores. Applying GRPO to DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B yields substantial gains on GSM8K and MATH under chain-of-thought evaluation, improving from $82.9\\%$ to $88.2\\%$ on GSM8K and from $46.8\\%$ to $51.7\\%$ on MATH. RL also enhances out-of-domain performance, including Chinese benchmarks such as CMATH.

These gains depend on the underlying model’s capacity to represent and refine mathematical reasoning. A stronger initialization provides a better starting point for RL to shape the output distribution. The paper analyzes why RL works in this context: improvements primarily manifest in metrics like Maj@K, suggesting enhanced robustness of the output distribution rather than a fundamental leap in single-sample capability (Pass@K). This interpretation aligns with the idea that RL can stabilize and amplify correct trajectories once the model already possesses effective reasoning patterns—patterns encouraged by the code-initialized pre-training.

# Conclusion: Code-Initialized Foundations Are Better for Math
Collectively, the paper’s results lead to a coherent takeaway: initializing the model from a code-trained checkpoint sets the stage for superior mathematical performance. The authors explicitly state that starting from a code model is preferable to starting from a general LLM. They also provide comprehensive empirical support showing that code training improves both program-aided and free-form mathematical reasoning, increases efficiency of subsequent math training, and contributes to broader reasoning improvements. In contrast, other data sources such as arXiv-only corpora did not help within the tested scope.

Therefore, if one were to initialize from a general LLM rather than a code-initialized checkpoint, the most likely outcome—consistent with the findings—is decreased math performance. The DeepSeekMath pipeline demonstrates that careful initialization, targeted math data, and reinforcement learning can jointly push open models toward high-level mathematical reasoning, but that foundation critically depends on beginning with the right kind of prior knowledge, namely, code.
