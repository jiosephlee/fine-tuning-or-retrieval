According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the iterative domain-expansion step marks a domain as math-related when over $10\\%$ of its pages are collected; the implicit assumption underlying using that heuristic to add other pages from the same domain to the seed corpus is domain homogeneity. This is sensible because the pipeline first uses a fastText classifier trained on OpenWebMath-like positives to recall high-scoring math pages and then aggregates by domain, so if a domain has a fraction $p>0.1$ of pages already recalled as math, the posterior that the domain is predominantly mathematical (e.g., mathoverflow.net) is high under a simple mixture view where domains are either mostly-math or mostly-non-math; web content tends to be topically coherent at the domain or subpath level (e.g., StackExchange sub-sites, dedicated math blogs), and the authors further mitigate intra-domain heterogeneity by manually annotating math-specific URL paths (e.g., “/questions”) and only adding uncollected pages linked under those paths, effectively enforcing homogeneity at the subdomain/URL-pattern level rather than relying on the entire domain. Empirically, this heuristic proved effective: after four iterations they reached 35.5M pages (120B tokens) with the fourth iteration adding only about $2\\%$ new data beyond the third, suggesting that the domain-based expansion converged, and models trained on this corpus outperformed baselines across GSM8K, MATH, and multilingual benchmarks, indicating that the added pages were indeed predominantly mathematical rather than noisy off-topic data.
