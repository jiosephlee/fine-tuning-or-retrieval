In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', a concrete change to the fastText training seed that improved its ability to recall more mathematical pages during later iterations was seed diversification. Initially, the fastText classifier was trained with positives drawn solely from OpenWebMath and random negatives, which the authors observed yielded limited recall because the positive set lacked diversity; to fix this, after the first pass they grouped Common Crawl by domain, flagged domains with more than \(10\%\) of pages already retrieved as math-related, manually annotated math-specific URL patterns within those domains (e.g., question subpaths on math sites), and then added previously uncollected pages from these annotated sources to the positive seed set before retraining the classifier. This deliberate enrichment of positive examples broadened the classifier’s notion of “math-like” web content, enabling it to recall more heterogeneous mathematical pages in subsequent iterations and culminating in 35.5M pages (120B tokens), with the fourth iteration showing diminishing returns (\(\sim 98\%\) overlap with the third), which is direct evidence that diversifying the seed improved recall effectiveness.
