In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors claim that the prior initialization choice that causally improved downstream mathematical reasoning compared to starting from a general LLM was a code-trained model. This is supported by both the model initialization decision—DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B—and controlled ablations showing that code pretraining before math pretraining yields superior performance to starting from a general LLM: in two-stage training, “Code for 400B tokens → Math for 150B tokens” outperforms “General for 400B tokens → Math for 150B tokens” on both tool-free and program-aided reasoning (e.g., GSM8K and MATH), with code training alone already providing substantial gains in PoT-based solving and further amplifying the effect of subsequent math training; moreover, the authors observe that code-first pretraining improves general reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, BBH) while preserving coding performance, and they explicitly note that “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” consistent with their broader evidence that code signals structure and execution affordances beneficial for mathematical reasoning, whereas alternative prior choices like arXiv-only pretraining show no notable gains or even deterioration; combined with the result that mixing code and math in a one-stage setup trades off tool-free math gains due to capacity limits at 1.3B, these comparisons isolate code pretraining as the causal prior that improves downstream math reasoning when followed by math-focused continual training.
