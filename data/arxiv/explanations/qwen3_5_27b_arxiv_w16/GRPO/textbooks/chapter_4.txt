Chapter 4: # Chapter 4: Supervised Fine-Tuning for Instruction Alignment

The transition from a raw, pre-trained language model to a specialized assistant capable of solving complex mathematical problems requires a critical intermediate step known as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). While the DeepSeekMath-Base 7B model, introduced in the previous chapter, possesses strong foundational reasoning capabilities derived from its extensive pre-training on the DeepSeekMath Corpus and code data, it lacks the specific alignment required to follow user instructions and generate structured solutions. This chapter details the construction of the DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B model, focusing on the meticulous curation of instruction datasets, the adoption of diverse reasoning formats, and the evaluation of the resulting model against both proprietary giants and open-source peers. The primary objective of this stage is to teach the model not just to "know" mathematics, but to "communicate" its reasoning process effectively, thereby laying the necessary groundwork for the subsequent reinforcement learning phase.

## Curating instruction tuning datasets: English and Chinese problems

The foundation of an effective instruction-tuned model lies in the quality and diversity of its training data. For DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, the research team constructed a comprehensive mathematical instruction-tuning dataset comprising 776,000 training examples. This dataset was designed to cover a broad spectrum of mathematical fields and varying levels of complexity, ensuring that the model is exposed to problems ranging from elementary arithmetic to advanced college-level concepts. A distinctive feature of this curation process is its explicit multilingual focus, addressing both English and Chinese mathematical problems. This approach contrasts with many prior works that relied heavily on English-only data, allowing DeepSeekMath to achieve superior performance on Chinese benchmarks such as CMATH and Gaokao-MathQA.

For the English component of the dataset, the researchers annotated problems from well-known benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH with solutions that integrate tool use. Additionally, they incorporated a subset of the MathInstruct dataset and utilized the training set from Lila-OOD. These sources were selected because they provide high-quality examples where problems are solved using either Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or Program-of-Thought (PoT) reasoning strategies. The resulting English collection spans diverse mathematical domains including algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry. This breadth ensures that the model learns to handle different types of logical structures and problem-solving heuristics inherent in various branches of mathematics.

The Chinese component was curated with equal rigor to support non-English mathematical reasoning. The team collected K-12 level mathematical problems spanning 76 distinct sub-topics, such as linear equations. Crucially, these Chinese problems were annotated with solutions in two specific formats: Chain-of-Thought and Tool-Integrated Reasoning. By including these diverse formats in both languages, the dataset ensures that DeepSeekMath-Instruct learns to articulate its reasoning steps clearly in natural language while also knowing when and how to leverage computational tools for verification or complex calculation. This dual-language strategy not only broadens the model's applicability but also demonstrates that high-quality mathematical data exists beyond English-centric sources like arXiv or Western educational repositories.

## Data formats: Chain-of-Thought, Program-of-Thought, and Tool-Integrated Reasoning (ToRA)

To maximize the reasoning capabilities of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, the instruction tuning data was structured around three distinct problem-solving paradigms: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning (ToRA). Each format serves a unique purpose in guiding the model toward accurate solutions while maintaining transparency in its logical process. Chain-of-Thought prompting involves generating a sequence of intermediate natural language steps that lead to a final answer. This format encourages the model to break down complex problems into manageable sub-problems, mimicking human deductive reasoning processes where intermediate conclusions are explicitly stated before reaching a final result.

Program-of-Thought represents a shift from purely linguistic reasoning to executable code generation. In this format, instead of writing out every step in natural language, the model is trained to write Python programs that solve the problem at hand. These programs can utilize standard libraries such as `math` for basic operations or `sympy` for symbolic algebra and calculus. The solution is then derived by executing this code rather than relying solely on the model's internal arithmetic capabilities. This approach leverages Python's precision for computation while allowing the model to focus on translating word problems into algorithmic logic. The inclusion of PoT data is particularly significant given that DeepSeekMath was initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5; it capitalizes on the model's inherent coding strengths to solve mathematical tasks more reliably than pure text generation might allow for intricate calculations.

Tool-Integrated Reasoning (ToRA) combines elements of natural language explanation with program execution within a single coherent response flow. In this format, models are prompted to interleave natural language reasoning steps with code snippets that perform specific calculations or verifications before proceeding to the next logical step in their explanation. This hybrid approach allows for a more dynamic interaction where the model can reason about a concept in text, execute code to verify a hypothesis or perform a heavy computation, interpret those results back into text, and then continue its reasoning chain. By training on ToRA data alongside CoT and PoT examples, DeepSeekMath-Instruct learns flexibility; it can choose whether to rely entirely on internal logic (CoT), offload computation entirely (PoT), or use tools selectively within a broader narrative (ToRA). This versatility is crucial for handling real-world mathematical queries where different strategies may be optimal depending on problem complexity.

## Training configuration for DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B

Once the diverse instruction dataset was curated, it was used to fine-tune DeepSeekMath-Base 7B into DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B through supervised learning techniques specific to instruction alignment. The training process involved randomly concatenating training examples until reaching a maximum context length of 4K tokens per batch sequence.
