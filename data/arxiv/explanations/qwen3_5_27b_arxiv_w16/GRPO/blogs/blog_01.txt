\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# Small Models, Big Brains: How 120 Billion Web Pages Beat Giant AI

The prevailing wisdom in artificial intelligence has long suggested that to solve complex problems, you need massive models. For years, the path to state-of-the-art performance seemed strictly tied to scaling up parameter counts into the hundreds of billions. However, a new study introduces DeepSeekMath, a 7-billion parameter model that challenges this notion head-on. By leveraging a meticulously curated dataset of 120 billion math-related tokens mined from the public web, DeepSeekMath achieves an impressive 51.7% accuracy on the competition-level MATH benchmark. This performance places it on par with closed-source giants like Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4, proving that data quality and architectural innovation can sometimes outweigh sheer model size.

## Mining Gold from Common Crawl

The cornerstone of DeepSeekMath's success lies not in its architecture, but in its fuel: the DeepSeekMath Corpus. Instead of relying solely on academic papers or synthetic data, the researchers turned to Common Crawl, a vast repository of scraped web pages often considered noisy and unstructured. The challenge was to extract high-quality mathematical content from this ocean of data without introducing benchmark contamination.

To achieve this, the team engineered an iterative data selection pipeline using a fastText-based classifier. They started with a seed corpus from OpenWebMath to train an initial classifier that could distinguish between mathematical and non-mathematical web pages. This classifier was then deployed to scan deduplicated Common Crawl data, ranking pages by their likelihood of containing useful math content. The process did not stop there; after each iteration, the team analyzed domains where their classifier had missed significant amounts of math content (such as specific forums or educational sites), manually annotated them, and retrained the classifier with these new positive examples.

This bootstrapping approach resulted in a final corpus of 35.5 million mathematical web pages totaling 120 billion tokens. Remarkably, this dataset is nearly seven times larger than the math data used by Minerva and nine times larger than OpenWebMath. The quality of this corpus is evident in its multilingual nature; unlike many predecessors focused exclusively on English, DeepSeekMath includes significant Chinese content, leading to superior performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks like CMATH and Gaokao-MathQA. Experiments confirmed that training on this high-quality web data yielded better results than training on arXiv papers alone, suggesting that publicly available web pages hold untapped potential for mathematical reasoning that academic repositories might miss.

## From Code to Calculus: The Power of Pre-Training Strategy

Another critical insight from the study is the strategic choice of initialization models. Rather than starting with a general-purpose language model, DeepSeekMath was initialized with DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. The authors hypothesized that code training inherently improves logical reasoning capabilities necessary for mathematics. Their experiments validated this intuition: models trained on code before undergoing math-specific pre-training showed significant improvements in solving mathematical problems both with and without external tools like Python interpreters.

The training regimen involved continuing pre-training for 500 billion tokens using a mix of data: 56% from the newly minted DeepSeekMath Corpus, alongside portions dedicated to code (Github), natural language text (Common Crawl), and specialized algebraic stacks. This hybrid approach ensured that while the model became an expert in mathematics, it retained its strong coding abilities and general reasoning skills measured by benchmarks like MMLU and BBH. Even before instruction tuning or reinforcement learning began, the base model outperformed Minerva 540B—a model with 77 times more parameters—on several key metrics simply due to the density and relevance of its training data.

## GRPO: Reinforcement Learning Without Overhead

While high-quality pre-training laid the foundation, pushing past the limits required a novel approach to Reinforcement Learning (RL). Traditional RL methods for Large Language Models often rely on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which requires training a separate "critic" or value function model alongside the main policy model to estimate future rewards accurately. This dual-model setup is computationally expensive and memory-intensive, creating a barrier for smaller teams or resource-constrained environments.

DeepSeekMath introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as a streamlined alternative to PPO. GRPO eliminates the need for a separate critic model entirely. Instead of predicting values for every token generated by the policy model, GRPO estimates baselines by comparing groups of outputs generated for the same prompt within a single batch. For each question $q$, the algorithm samples $G$ different outputs $\{o_1, o_2, \dots, o_G\}$. It then normalizes the rewards received by these outputs based on their group mean and standard deviation to calculate advantages $\hat{A}_{i,t}$.

This group-relative approach aligns naturally with how reward models operate—they are typically trained to compare pairs or groups of responses rather than assign absolute values to individual tokens mathematically precise at every step. By removing the critic network and simplifying advantage estimation through group statistics, GRPO drastically reduces memory usage while maintaining stability during training. When applied to DeepSeekMath-Instruct using only instruction-tuning data subsets from GSM8K and MATH, GRPO boosted performance significantly: GSM8K accuracy jumped from 82.9% to 88.2%, and MATH accuracy rose from 46.8% to 51%. Crucially, these gains were observed even in out-of-domain tasks like CMATH ($84.6\% \to 88.8\%$), demonstrating that RL via GRPO enhances general reasoning robustness rather than just memorizing specific problem patterns.

## Why Smaller Can Be Better

The story of DeepSeekMath serves as a powerful reminder that scaling laws are not absolute mandates but guidelines influenced heavily by data curation and algorithmic efficiency. By proving that a compact 7B parameter model can rival industry titans through smart data engineering and efficient reinforcement learning strategies like GRPO, DeepSeek opens new avenues for open-source development in AI mathematics research area . The findings suggest that future breakthroughs may come less from building bigger models and more from finding smarter ways to teach them what matters most: rigorous logic derived from diverse real-world knowledge sources found right here on our public internet infrastructure today!
