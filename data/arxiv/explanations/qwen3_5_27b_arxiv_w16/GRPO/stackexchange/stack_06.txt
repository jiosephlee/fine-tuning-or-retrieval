\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### Understanding the 'Unified Paradigm': How does DPO fit into Online vs Offline sampling?
Question:
The paper proposes a unified paradigm categorizing methods based on Data Source (Online vs Offline), Reward Function (Rule vs Model), and Algorithm. They classify DPO as using 'Offline sampling' because it uses augmented outputs sampled from the *initial* SFT model.

However, DPO typically involves generating pairs $(y_w, y_l)$ where both are generated by a policy (often iteratively updated or bootstrapped). If I run DPO iteratively where I re-sample from my current policy to create new preference pairs for the next round of training, wouldn't that technically become 'Online'? Where is the strict boundary between DPO's standard implementation and 'Online RFT' in their taxonomy?
Answer:
The distinction between DPO as "offline" and your proposed iterative approach as "online" hinges entirely on the **source of the data** ($\mathcal{D}$) used to compute the gradient, rather than the frequency of updates or whether the policy changes between steps. According to the DeepSeekMath paper's unified paradigm, a method is classified as "offline" if the training data is sampled from a fixed initial reference model (typically the SFT model), whereas it is "online" if data is continuously sampled from the current, evolving policy model itself.

In standard DPO implementations described in the literature and referenced in this paper, preference pairs $(y_w, y_l)$ are generated once from a static reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ (usually the initial SFT model). Even if you were to update your policy weights multiple times using this same static dataset, it remains an offline procedure because the distribution of data $\mathcal{D}$ does not shift with the policy; you are simply reusing historical samples. The paper explicitly states that DPO falls under "offline sampling" because it relies on augmented outputs sampled from the *initial* SFT model, meaning the data distribution is decoupled from the real-time behavior of the training agent.

Your proposal to iteratively re-sample from your current policy to create new preference pairs fundamentally alters this classification. If you generate new $(y_w, y_l)$ pairs by querying your updated policy at every iteration and immediately use them for training, you are engaging in **online sampling**. In this scenario, the data source $\mathcal{D}$ becomes dynamic and dependent on $\pi_{\theta}$ at that specific timestep. The paper identifies this exact mechanism in its definition of "Online RFT," where outputs are sampled from the "real-time policy model." Consequently, your iterative DPO setup would technically cross the boundary into an online paradigm similar to Online RFT or PPO/GRPO, as it closes the loop between action generation and feedback collection within a single optimization trajectory. The critical boundary is thus whether the dataset evolves alongside the policy (online) or remains frozen based on an earlier snapshot (offline), regardless of whether you call it DPO or RFT.
