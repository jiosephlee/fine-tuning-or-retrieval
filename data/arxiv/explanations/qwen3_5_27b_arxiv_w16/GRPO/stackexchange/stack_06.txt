\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### What exactly constitutes 'Code Training' vs 'Math Training' in their two-stage experiment?
Question:
In Section 5.1 ('Code Training Benefits Mathematical Reasoning'), they compare 'Code Training for 400B Tokens -> Math Training for 150B Tokens' against other baselines.

I am unclear on what data constitutes 'Code Tokens' versus 'Math Tokens' in their DeepSeek-Coder base vs DeepSeekMath corpus.
1. Since they start with DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 (which already has code), are they doing *continual* pre-training on more code before adding math?
2. Or does 'Code Training' refer to fine-tuning on code data?
The results show code training improves program-aided reasoning but mixing them degrades non-tool reasoning for small models (1.3B). Does this imply that code tokens act as a strong prior that helps structure logical flow (like Python syntax) but might crowd out natural language reasoning patterns if not balanced correctly?
Answer:
The "Code Training" and "Math Training" described in Section 5.1 refer to **continual pre-training** stages, not fine-tuning, and the data sources are distinct corpora used to extend the model's knowledge base. Specifically, the experiment starts with a general model (DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B) and performs two-stage pre-training: first, it is trained on **400B tokens of code** (sourced from GitHub, as indicated by the DeepSeek-Coder lineage and the general composition of their code corpus), followed by **150B tokens of math** (sourced from the DeepSeekMath Corpus mined from Common Crawl). This is distinct from fine-tuning because it involves updating the model's foundational weights on raw text data to learn patterns and reasoning structures, rather than aligning it to specific instruction-response pairs.

Regarding your hypothesis on why mixing these tokens degrades non-tool reasoning for small models: the paper explicitly supports your intuition that capacity limits play a critical role. The authors conjecture that for a smaller model like DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B, "mixing code tokens and math tokens for one-stage training compromises mathematical reasoning without tool use" because the model "lacks the capacity to fully assimilate both code and mathematical data simultaneously." In this context, code tokens act as a strong structural prior that excels at program-aided reasoning (where syntax and logic are rigid), but when mixed indiscriminately with math data in a small parameter regime, it appears to crowd out or interfere with the acquisition of natural language reasoning patterns required for solving problems without tools. The two-stage approach (Code $\to$ Math) allows the model to first solidify its logical/structural foundation via code before specializing in mathematical domain knowledge, whereas simultaneous training forces a competition for representational capacity that the 1.3B model cannot resolve effectively.
