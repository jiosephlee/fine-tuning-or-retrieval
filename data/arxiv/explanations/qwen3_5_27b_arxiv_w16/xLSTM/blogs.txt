# Is the King Dead? Why LSTMs Are Making a Massive Comeback Against Transformers

For nearly a decade, the narrative in deep learning has been clear: the Transformer reigns supreme. Since 2017, the self-attention mechanism has displaced the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network as the foundation of Large Language Models (LLMs). While LSTMs powered early breakthroughs in speech recognition and machine translation, they eventually hit a ceiling due to sequential processing constraints and limited memory capacity. However, a new research paper titled "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory" challenges this status quo. By integrating modern architectural techniques with the fundamental principles of LSTMs, researchers have created a hybrid model that not only rivals state-of-the-art Transformers but often surpasses them in efficiency and performance.

The core premise of xLSTM is deceptively simple: if we take the robust gating mechanisms of the 1990s LSTM and fix its historical weaknesses using insights from modern scaling laws, can we reclaim its throne? The answer appears to be yes. The authors introduce two critical innovations—exponential gating and novel memory structures—that transform the humble LSTM into a scalable powerhouse capable of competing with billion-parameter models.

## Overcoming Historical Limitations

To understand why xLSTM works, we must first look at why traditional LSTMs struggled at scale. Despite their success in learning abstractions and avoiding vanishing gradients through the "constant error carousel," classical LSTMs suffered from three primary bottlenecks. First, they lacked the ability to revise storage decisions effectively; once information was written to memory, updating it with newer, more relevant data was difficult. Second, their storage capacity was limited because information had to be compressed into scalar cell states, leading to poor performance on rare token prediction tasks. Third, and perhaps most critically for modern hardware, standard LSTMs relied on hidden-hidden connections that enforced sequential processing, making them impossible to parallelize efficiently during training like Transformers can.

The xLSTM architecture addresses these issues directly without abandoning the recurrent nature that makes RNNs so effective for sequence modeling. Instead of discarding the LSTM entirely, the researchers extended it with two distinct variants designed to tackle specific shortcomings: sLSTM and mLSTM.

## Introducing sLSTM: Exponential Gating and Revisability

The first variant, sLSTM (scalar LSTM), focuses on solving the issue of revising storage decisions and improving memory mixing. Traditional LSTMs use sigmoid activation functions for their input ($i_t$) and forget ($f_t$) gates, which constrain values between 0 and 1. While stable, this limits dynamic range. xLSTM replaces these with **exponential gating**, allowing gate values to grow exponentially based on learned parameters. This change enables the model to drastically alter its memory state when necessary, effectively allowing it to "overwrite" or significantly adjust previous memories when new information contradicts or supersedes old data.

However, exponential growth introduces numerical instability risks like overflow. To mitigate this, sLSTM employs a clever normalization technique involving a "normalizer state" ($n_t$) that tracks the cumulative strength of input and forget gates over time. Furthermore, a stabilizer state ($m_t$) is introduced to shift log-values dynamically during computation, ensuring numerical stability without altering the final output or gradients mathematically. This combination allows sLSTM to handle complex state-tracking problems—such as determining parity in formal languages or tracking entities in long narratives—that often stump standard Transformers and State Space Models lacking true memory mixing capabilities.

## Introducing mLSTM: Matrix Memory and Parallelism

While sLSTM improves decision-making logic through scalar updates with mixing capabilities, it remains sequential due to its reliance on hidden-state dependencies across time steps. To break this bottleneck and increase storage capacity simultaneously, researchers developed mLSTM (matrix LSTM). This variant upgrades the cell state from a single scalar value $c \in \mathbb{R}$ to a full matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.

In mLSTM, information is stored as key-value pairs $(\mathbf{k}_t, \mathbf{v}_t)$ derived from the input $\mathbf{x}_t$. The memory update follows a covariance rule:
$$ \mathbf{C}_t = f_t \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + i_t (\mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top) $$
Here, $f_t$ acts as a decay factor (forget gate) and $i_t$ as a learning rate (input gate). Retrieval is performed by multiplying this matrix $\mathbf{C}_t$ by a query vector $\mathbf{q}_t$, yielding a hidden state that aggregates relevant past information weighted by similarity between keys and queries. Crucially, because mLSTM removes hidden-hidden recurrent connections in favor of this matrix operation driven solely by current inputs and accumulated matrices, it becomes **fully parallelizable**. This allows mLSTM to leverage GPU acceleration similarly to FlashAttention or linear attention mechanisms while retaining an associative memory structure superior to standard scalar RNNs for tasks requiring high-capacity recall of rare tokens or distant associations.

## Scaling Up: Performance Against Giants

The theoretical elegance of xLSTM holds up under rigorous empirical testing when scaled to billions of parameters trained on massive datasets like SlimPajama (up to 300 billion tokens). When comparing xLSTM models against established baselines—including GPT-3 style Transformers (like Llama), State Space Models (like Mamba), and other RNN variants (like RWKV)—xLSTM consistently demonstrates superior performance metrics.

On validation sets measuring perplexity (a standard metric for language modeling quality), xLSTM configurations outperform all other tested methods across various model sizes ranging from 125 million to 1.3 billion parameters. Notably, xLSTM excels at **length extrapolation**. While many models degrade significantly when asked to process contexts longer than those seen during training (e.g., moving from 2k to 16k tokens), xLSTM maintains low perplexity even at these extended ranges. This suggests that its compressive memory representation generalizes exceptionally well beyond training distribution boundaries.

Furthermore, xLSTM shines in specialized tasks where memory capacity is paramount. In Multi-Query Associative Recall tests involving hundreds of key-value pairs over long sequences, xLstm [specifically configurations utilizing mLSTM] outperformed non-Transformer alternatives significantly and approached Transformer-level performance despite having linear computational complexity relative to sequence length ($O(N)$) rather than quadratic ($O(N^2)$). In downstream tasks covering diverse domains from news articles to Reddit discussions via the PALOMA benchmark, pure mLSTM configurations achieved lower perplexity than Mamba in over 99% of domains compared against Llama in roughly 85%.

## Efficiency and Practical Implications

Beyond raw accuracy metrics, xLSTM offers compelling advantages regarding inference speed and resource utilization because it decouples computation cost from sequence length during generation unlike Transformers which suffer from growing key-value cache requirements as context grows longer). # From Scalars to Matrices: How xLSTM Solves the 'Rare Word' Problem

For decades, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network has been a cornerstone of sequence modeling, powering everything from early speech recognition to the first generation of Large Language Models. Despite its longevity, traditional LSTMs face a fundamental bottleneck when dealing with complex language tasks: limited storage capacity. Specifically, they struggle significantly with "rare token prediction," where the model fails to recall infrequent words or specific details buried deep within a long conversation. The recently proposed xLSTM architecture addresses this directly by evolving the internal memory mechanism from a simple scalar container to a dynamic matrix structure.

To understand why this shift matters, we must look at how traditional LSTMs handle information. In the classic formulation, the memory cell state $c_t$ is essentially a scalar (or a vector of scalars) updated additively at each time step. The update rule involves multiplying the previous state by a forget gate and adding new input modulated by an input gate. While effective for smoothing errors over time, this approach forces all historical information to be compressed into a single fixed-size vector. As noted in the research, this compression creates a bottleneck where distinct pieces of information compete for space. When faced with rare tokens—words that appear infrequently in training data—the model often lacks sufficient capacity to isolate and preserve their unique representations amidst more common noise. Consequently, performance drops sharply for these rare elements because the scalar memory simply cannot hold enough distinct "slots" for every necessary detail.

The breakthrough in xLSTM comes from the introduction of mLSTM (matrix LSTM), which replaces the scalar cell state with a matrix memory $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. Instead of squeezing history into a single vector, mLSTM treats memory as an associative system capable of storing key-value pairs dynamically. Drawing inspiration from Bidirectional Associative Memories and covariance update rules, mLSTM stores information by updating its matrix state with outer products of learned key ($\mathbf{k}_t$) and value ($\mathbf{v}_t$) vectors derived from the input. Mathematically, this update looks like $\mathbf{C}_t = f_t \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + i_t \mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top$, where $f_t$ acts as a decay factor and $i_t$ as a learning rate controlled by gates. This mechanism allows the model to accumulate associations without immediately discarding old ones in favor of new averages, effectively creating a richer tapestry of stored knowledge.

Retrieval in this new system works similarly to how humans associate concepts: via queries. To access stored information at time $t$, mLSTM generates a query vector $\mathbf{q}_t$ and multiplies it by the accumulated memory matrix $\mathbf{C}_t$. The resulting hidden state is then normalized by a special normalizer state $\mathbf{n}_t$, which tracks the weighted sum of past keys to ensure stable retrieval even when signals are weak or conflicting. This architecture transforms the memory cell from a passive bucket into an active lookup table that can distinguish between thousands of unique interactions simultaneously. Because the storage capacity is now tied to the dimensions of the matrix rather than just the width of a vector, mLSTM can retain specific details about rare events or obscure vocabulary items that would have been lost in a standard LSTM's compressed representation.

The practical impact of this architectural change is profound for language modeling tasks involving long contexts and diverse vocabularies. Experimental results demonstrate that xLSTM models utilizing mLSTM blocks significantly outperform traditional LSTMs and rival modern Transformers on rare token prediction benchmarks. By decoupling storage capacity from strict sequential compression limits, mLSTM ensures that even low-frequency tokens receive adequate representation during training and inference. Furthermore, unlike standard RNNs that suffer from sequential processing constraints, mLSTM is designed to be fully parallelizable during training because it eliminates hidden-hidden recurrent connections in favor of this global matrix update rule. This combination of expanded associative memory and computational efficiency suggests that we may not need to abandon recurrent architectures entirely; instead, by upgrading them with matrix memories, we can achieve performance comparable to attention-based models while retaining their inherent advantages in handling variable-length sequences efficiently.



# Speed Meets Intelligence: Can Recurrent Networks Finally Beat Transformers at Scale?

Transformers have dominated the landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) since their inception, powering everything from chatbots to advanced reasoning engines. However, their reliance on self-attention comes with a significant cost: quadratic memory and computation complexity relative to sequence length. As context windows grow, inference slows down dramatically, making real-time applications and edge deployment increasingly challenging. Enter xLSTM (Extended Long Short-Term Memory), a new architecture that revisits the classic Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) paradigm but equips it with modern innovations to rival—and potentially surpass—Transformers in efficiency and performance. By offering linear computation and constant memory complexity regardless of sequence length, xLSTM promises to bring the speed of recurrent networks back into the spotlight without sacrificing intelligence.

## The Efficiency Bottleneck of Modern LLMs

The primary advantage of Transformers lies in their ability to parallelize training across massive datasets. Yet, this strength becomes a weakness during inference, particularly for long sequences. Standard attention mechanisms require computing interactions between every pair of tokens in a sequence, leading to a computational complexity that grows quadratically ($O(N^2)$) as the sequence length $N$ increases. This necessitates storing large Key-Value caches in memory, which quickly exhausts resources on consumer hardware or edge devices. While techniques like FlashAttention have optimized these operations, the fundamental scaling issue remains: longer contexts inevitably demand exponentially more compute and memory bandwidth.

In contrast, traditional RNNs process sequences step-by-step, maintaining a fixed-size hidden state that summarizes the past. This results in linear time complexity ($O(N)$) and constant memory usage ($O(1)$) with respect to sequence length. Historically, however, RNNs struggled with vanishing gradients and limited capacity to retain information over long distances, causing them to fall behind Transformers in language modeling tasks. The xLSTM architecture addresses these historical weaknesses directly by introducing exponential gating and novel memory structures that allow RNNs to scale effectively while retaining their inherent efficiency benefits.

## How xLSTM Achieves Linear Scaling

The xLSTM framework extends the original LSTM concept through two primary architectural variants: sLSTM (scalar LSTM) and mLSTM (matrix LSTM). Both variants incorporate exponential gating mechanisms designed to improve stability and allow for dynamic revision of storage decisions, solving issues where traditional LSTMs failed to update stored information effectively when encountering new data.

The sLSTM variant retains the scalar memory cell but enhances it with exponential activation functions for input and forget gates. Crucially, it includes a normalizer state that tracks the cumulative influence of gates over time, ensuring numerical stability even with exponential growth potentials. This variant supports "memory mixing," where hidden states from previous steps interact with current inputs through recurrent connections. While this mixing prevents full parallelization during inference—a trait inherited from classic RNNs—it enables sophisticated state tracking capabilities essential for tasks like code evaluation or entity resolution in long narratives.

On the other hand, the mLSTM replaces the scalar memory cell with a matrix memory $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. Instead of simple addition updates, mLSTM utilizes a covariance update rule involving outer products of key-value pairs derived from the input sequence:
$$ \mathbf{C}_t = f_t \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + i_t \mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top $$
Here, $f_t$ acts as a decay rate (forget gate) and $i_t$ as a learning rate (input gate). Because mLSTM abandons hidden-hidden recurrent connections (memory mixing), its recurrence can be reformulated entirely in parallel during training using standard matrix operations similar to FlashAttention or linear attention mechanisms. During inference, however, it maintains the linear time complexity characteristic of RNNs while providing significantly expanded storage capacity compared to scalar memories. This dual approach allows xLSTM architectures to combine blocks of both types (denoted as xLSTM[$a:b$]) to balance expressiveness and parallelizability according to specific application needs.

## Head-to-Head: Performance Against Giants

When pitted against state-of-the-art models like Llama (Transformer), Mamba (State Space Model), and RWKV (RNN), xLSTM demonstrates remarkable competitiveness across various benchmarks trained on 300 billion tokens from SlimPajama datasets. In terms of raw language modeling capability measured by perplexity on validation sets, xLSTM consistently outperforms its counterparts across multiple model sizes ranging from 125 million to 1.3 billion parameters. Notably, pure mLSTM configurations often achieve lower perplexity than hybrid setups on diverse text domains covering news articles to social media discussions, indicating superior generalization abilities despite lacking explicit attention mechanisms.

Beyond static metrics, xLSTM excels in scenarios requiring long-context understanding where Transformers traditionally degrade rapidly without specialized optimization tricks like sliding window attention or sparse patterns. Experiments show that while models like Llama suffer increased perplexity when tested on contexts significantly longer than their training distribution (extrapolation beyond 2048 tokens up to 16384), xLSTM maintains stable low perplexities throughout these extended ranges. This robustness suggests that the compressive nature of its matrix memory effectively manages information density without succumbing to interference errors common in other architectures when dealing with ultra-long sequences. Furthermore, synthetic tasks designed specifically for associative recall reveal that mLSTM's matrix memory allows it to retrieve key-value pairs with accuracy approaching that of Transformers while operating under strict resource constraints typical of recurrent systems.

# The Best of Both Worlds: Inside the Hybrid Architecture of xLSTM

The Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network dominated the landscape of sequence modeling for decades, serving as the foundation for early Large Language Models (LLMs). However, the arrival of the Transformer architecture shifted the paradigm toward parallelizable self-attention, leaving traditional LSTMs behind due to their sequential nature and limited storage capacity. A recent breakthrough challenges this status quo by asking a fundamental question: How far can we push LSTMs if we scale them to billions of parameters while fixing their historical weaknesses? The answer lies in xLSTM, a hybrid architecture that integrates two specialized variants—the scalar LSTM (sLSTM) and the matrix LSTM (mLSTM)—to create a system capable of rivaling state-of-the-art Transformers and State Space Models.

At the heart of xLSTM is a recognition that a single type of memory cell cannot optimally solve every problem. Traditional LSTMs struggle with revising storage decisions once made and lack the capacity to store complex associations efficiently. To address these issues, researchers introduced exponential gating and two distinct memory structures. The resulting architecture does not rely on a monolithic block design but instead stacks different types of blocks residually, allowing the model to leverage the unique strengths of each component depending on the task requirements.

## sLSTM: Precision Logic and State Tracking

The first engine in this hybrid system is sLSTM, designed specifically to overcome the inability of standard LSTMs to revise storage decisions. In classic LSTMs, once information is written to the cell state via sigmoid-gated updates, it is difficult to overwrite or correct that decision if new, more relevant information appears later in the sequence. This limitation is critical for tasks requiring precise state tracking, such as evaluating code or maintaining consistency in long narratives.

sLSTM addresses this by introducing exponential gating alongside normalization and stabilization techniques. Instead of using sigmoid functions for input and forget gates, sLSTM employs exponential activations ($\exp(\cdot)$). This change allows for more dynamic weighting of information flow. Crucially, sLSTM incorporates a "memory mixing" mechanism where hidden states from previous time steps influence the current cell inputs and gates through recurrent connections. This mixing allows the model to maintain complex internal states and effectively track variables over long distances. While this sequential dependency prevents full parallelization during training compared to attention mechanisms, it grants sLSTM superior expressiveness for logical reasoning tasks where context must be actively updated rather than just recalled.

## mLSTM: Massive Parallelizable Memory Storage

If sLSTM handles logic and state tracking, mLSTM serves as the high-capacity storage unit designed for scalability and speed. The primary bottleneck of traditional LSTMs is their scalar memory cell, which forces all information into a compressed vector representation. This compression leads to poor performance on tasks involving rare tokens or large vocabularies where distinct pieces of information need to be stored separately without interference.

mLSTM expands the memory concept from a scalar $c$ to a matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. This shift transforms the memory update rule into a covariance update mechanism inspired by Bidirectional Associative Memories (BAMs). In this setup, key-value pairs are stored directly into the matrix memory via outer products ($\mathbf{v}_t \mathbf{k}_t^\top$). Retrieval is performed by multiplying this matrix with a query vector $\mathbf{q}_t$, allowing for direct access to stored information without compressing it into a fixed-size vector first. Because mLSTM abandons hidden-hidden recurrent connections (memory mixing), it eliminates the sequential bottleneck entirely. This makes mLSTM fully parallelizable during training, similar to modern attention mechanisms or FlashAttention implementations, enabling rapid processing on GPUs despite its computationally intensive matrix operations.

## The Hybrid Strategy: Stacking for Optimal Performance

The true innovation of xLSTM lies not just in creating these two variants individually, but in architecting them together within a single residual backbone. An xLSTM architecture consists of stacked blocks where each block can contain either an sLSTM or an mLSTM unit. Researchers denote this configuration using ratios such as xLSTEM[$a$: $b$], representing $a$ mLSTM blocks versus $b$ sLSTM blocks out of a total count. For instance, an xLSTEM[7:1] configuration utilizes mostly parallelizable matrix memories while reserving specific layers for scalar state tracking logic.

This modular design allows engineers to tailor models for specific needs without sacrificing overall efficiency. By placing sLSTM blocks strategically within the network, the model gains the ability to solve complex formal language tasks and perform state tracking that pure Transformers or State Space Models often fail at simultaneously maintaining high-speed inference through dominant mLSTM layers that handle bulk context retention. Experimental results indicate that adding even a few sLSTM blocks significantly boosts performance on associative recall tasks without diminishing memory capacity, suggesting that these components complement rather than compete with one another.

## Real-World Implications and Scaling Laws

When scaled up using billions of parameters on datasets like SlimPajama (up to 300 billion tokens), xLSTM demonstrates remarkable capabilities across diverse benchmarks. In synthetic tests involving nearest neighbor search and rare token prediction—tasks where traditional LSTMs historically falter—xL STM excels thanks to its dual-engine approach. Furthermore, unlike Transformers whose inference cost grows quadratically with sequence length due to attention matrices, x LSTM maintains linear computation complexity relative to sequence length while offering constant memory footprint per token during inference (excluding pre-computed caches). This characteristic makes it particularly suitable for edge devices and applications requiring extremely long contexts without prohibitive latency costs downscaling curves suggest that as models grow larger beyond current limits, x LSTM will continue to outperform or match leading Transformer-based architectures while retaining advantages in context extrapolation up to 16k tokens and beyond . Whether deployed for writing code where variable scope matters deeply or generating coherent stories spanning thousands of words , this hybrid architecture represents a significant leap forward in making recurrent networks viable again as top-tier contenders in large language modeling .
