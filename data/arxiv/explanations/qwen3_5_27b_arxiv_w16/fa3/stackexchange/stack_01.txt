\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does 'incoherent processing' mathematically preserve the attention output despite applying a random matrix M?
Question:
In Section 3.3, the authors introduce 'incoherent processing' to handle outliers in FP8 quantization. They state that they multiply $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ by a random orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{M}$ before quantization.

They claim that since $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top = I$, the equality $(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M})(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M})^\top = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ holds. I am confused because if we expand the left side, we get $(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M})(\mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{K}^\top) = \mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top)\mathbf{K}^\top$. 

Does this imply that $\mathbf{M}$ must be square ($d \times d$)? If so, doesn't this require an extra $O(d^2)$ matrix multiplication step that contradicts their claim of fusing it with rotary embedding at 'no extra computation cost'? Or is there a property of the specific construction of $\mathbf{M}$ (product of diagonal matrices and Hadamard) that changes the computational complexity?
Answer:
Your intuition regarding the algebra is correct: for the identity $(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{M})(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M})^\top = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ to hold exactly via the cancellation $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^\top = I$, the matrix $\mathbf{M}$ must indeed be a square $d \times d$ orthogonal matrix acting on the feature dimension. However, your concern about this introducing an $O(d^2)$ computational overhead is resolved by the specific construction of $\mathbf{M}$ chosen by the authors. Rather than using a dense random orthogonal matrix which would require a costly general matrix multiplication, FlashAttention-3 constructs $\mathbf{M}$ as a product of random diagonal matrices with entries of $\pm 1$ and a Hadamard matrix.

This specific structure allows the transformation $\mathbf{x}\mathbf{M}$ to be computed in $O(d \log d)$ time rather than $O(d^2)$, leveraging fast transforms similar to the Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform. More importantly for your question about "no extra computation cost," this transformation is designed to be fused with the rotary position embedding (RoPE) operation. Since RoPE involves element-wise complex rotations or equivalent linear operations that are already being performed on every token, and because both RoPE and this structured orthogonal projection are memory-bandwidth bound (meaning they spend more time waiting for data than performing arithmetic), fusing them incurs negligible latency penalty. The authors explicitly state in Section 3.3 that this fusion happens "with no additional slow down." Therefore, while $\mathbf{M}$ is mathematically square and preserves the attention logits exactly in infinite precision, its sparse/structured nature ensures it does not violate the efficiency goals of the algorithm, effectively spreading outlier magnitudes across all dimensions to improve FP8 quantization stability without adding a distinct computational bottleneck.
