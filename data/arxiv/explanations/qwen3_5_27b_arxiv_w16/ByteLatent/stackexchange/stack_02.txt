\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Clarification on the FLOPS equation: Why is the vocabulary size $V$ zero for the Global Transformer?
Question:
Looking at Equation (5) in Section 3.6 regarding FLOPs estimation:
$$\text{FL}_{\text{{\textsc BLT}}} = \dots + \text{Transf. FL}(h_{\mathcal{G}}, l_{\mathcal{G}}, m=n_{ctx}/n_p,V=0) / n_p + \dots$$
The term for the Global Latent Transformer sets $V=0$. I understand that $V$ usually refers to the output projection matrix size (embedding dimension $\times$ vocabulary size).

Since BLT outputs *patches* rather than raw bytes directly from the global model, does this mean there is no final softmax/projection layer in $\mathcal{G}$? Or is the projection handled entirely by the Local Decoder ($\mathcal{D}$)? If there is no projection, how does the global model output discrete decisions if it's autoregressive?
Answer:
The reason the vocabulary size $V$ is set to zero in the FLOPs equation for the Global Latent Transformer ($\mathcal{G}$) is that this component of the architecture does not perform a final projection onto a discrete output vocabulary; instead, it operates entirely in a continuous latent space.

In standard transformer architectures, the final layer projects hidden states onto a vocabulary of size $V$ (e.g., tokens or bytes) to compute probabilities via a softmax function, which incurs a computational cost proportional to $V$. However, in the Byte Latent Transformer (BLT), the Global Transformer $\mathcal{G}$ receives patch representations from the Local Encoder and outputs refined patch representations. Crucially, as stated in Section 3.1, $\mathcal{G}$ maps "a sequence of latent input patch representations... into a sequence of output patch representations." It never directly predicts individual bytes or selects from a fixed dictionary. Therefore, there is no output projection matrix of shape $(\text{hidden\_dim} \times V)$ within $\mathcal{G}$, rendering that specific FLOP contribution zero.

The responsibility for decoding these latent patch representations back into raw bytes lies exclusively with the **Local Decoder** ($\mathcal{D}$). As detailed in Section 3.3 and confirmed by Equation (5), the Local Decoder is the only component where $V=256$ (the byte alphabet size). The Local Decoder takes the continuous output vectors from $\mathcal{G}$ and uses cross-attention mechanisms combined with its own transformer layers to autoregressively predict individual bytes one by one. This separation allows $\mathcal{G}$ to focus purely on modeling complex dependencies between patches without being bottlenecked by a massive vocabulary projection at every step, while $\mathcal{D}$ handles the granular byte-level generation efficiently. Consequently, even though BLT generates discrete bytes autoregressively overall, the "global" decision-making unit itself remains vocabulary-agnostic until the signal reaches the local decoder stage.
