\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### How does BitNet b1.58 handle Rotary Embeddings (RoPE) with ternary weights?
Question:
Section 2 mentions that BitNet b1.58 adopts LLaMA-alike components, including rotary embedding (RoPE). RoPE typically involves multiplying token embeddings by rotation matrices containing sine and cosine values.

If all linear layers ($\text{nn.Linear}$) are replaced with $\text{BitLinear}$ using ternary weights \{-1, 0, 1\}, how are these rotation matrices implemented? Are they computed in full precision and then applied as an operation before/after the ternary matrix multiplication? Or are they also quantized? If they remain full precision, does this reintroduce floating-point multiplication costs that would negate some of the energy savings claimed for the matrix multiplication layers?
Answer:
In BitNet b1.58, Rotary Embeddings (RoPE) are implemented using full-precision floating-point arithmetic and are **not** quantized to ternary weights, nor are they replaced by a ternary matrix multiplication.

The paper explicitly states in Section 2 that BitNet b1.58 adopts "LLaMA-alike components," which includes RoPE, but clarifies that the quantization to ternary values $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ applies specifically to the parameters of the linear layers (replacing `nn.Linear` with `BitLinear`). RoPE is fundamentally a positional encoding mechanism that involves element-wise multiplication of token embeddings by rotation matrices containing sine and cosine values ($\sin(\theta)$ and $\cos(\theta)$). These rotation matrices are not learnable parameters; they are deterministic functions of the token position and dimension index. Consequently, they do not undergo the `absmean` quantization process described in Equation 1, which is reserved for the weight matrices $W$ of the linear projections.

Regarding your concern about energy savings, the full-precision computation required for RoPE does not negate the efficiency gains of BitNet b1.58 because RoPE operations constitute a negligible fraction of the total computational cost compared to the massive matrix multiplications in the feed-forward networks (FFN) and attention projections. As detailed in the "Energy" subsection of Section 3, the paper estimates that matrix multiplication contributes the vast majority of energy consumption in LLMs. While RoPE involves floating-point multiplications, these are applied element-wise to vectors (complexity $O(d)$ per token), whereas the quantized linear layers involve matrix-vector multiplications with dimensions scaling with hidden size and intermediate size (complexity $O(d^2)$ or higher). The paper notes that BitNet b1.58 achieves a 71.4x reduction in arithmetic operation energy for matrix multiplication specifically because it replaces FP16 multiplications with integer additions. Since RoPE remains a small overhead relative to these dominant matrix operations, retaining it in full precision allows the model to maintain compatibility with LLaMA's architecture and performance characteristics without significantly impacting the overall energy or latency benefits derived from quantizing the bulk of the model's parameters.
