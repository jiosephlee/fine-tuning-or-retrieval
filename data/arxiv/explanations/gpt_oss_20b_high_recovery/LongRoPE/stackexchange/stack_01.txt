\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### What intuitively does Rotatory Position Encoding (RoPE) encode according to Equation (1)?
Question:
In Equation (1) RoPE pixels appear as \[cos(n\theta_0),\sin(n\theta_0),...,cos(n\theta_{d/2-1}),\sin(n\theta_{d/2-1})\]. The paper states \theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d} with \theta=10000.
I am confused about why the rotation angle appears divided by β^i instead of multiplying directly.
Could someone explain how the frequency grows (or shrinks) with higher dimensions i and what effect changing θ has on the representation ability across dimensions?

Also could you walk through an example illustrating what happens when going from frame n=8606 to 46090 tokens – how do these angles scale?

Any visual intuition—such as plotting cosine‑sine pairs over time—would help.
Answer:
Rotary positional embeddings were introduced simply so that **every token vector is rotated by a different amount**, depending both on its absolute index $(n)$ and on the embedding‐dimension $(i)$.

---

### What Equation (1) actually encodes

For a stream‑of‑tokens model we write the RoPE vector at position $(n)$ as  

$$
v_n=\Bigl[\cos(n\theta _{0}),\,\sin(n\theta_{0}),\
          \cos(n\theta _{1}),\,\sin(n\theta_{1}),\,\dots,\
          \cos(n{\theta}_{d/2-1}),\,\sin(n{\theta}_{d/2-1})\Bigr].
$$

The pair $(\\cos , \\sin )$ covers one complex plane – think of it as a two–dimensional vector whose direction is rotated by the phase $ n{\color{blue}\\Theta}_{{}} $.  
If you apply this rotation independently in every $d/2$ planes and then take inner products between queries $q_n$ and keys $k_m$, you obtain terms proportional to  
$q_nk_m^\top \\cos({\\Theta}_{ij}(m-n))+\dots $
which is exactly the *relative–position* component used in attention calculations
(Jiao & Kiyoki–Yamamoto, “RoFormer”).  
In short, RoPE does **not add a positional scalar** – it tilts each sub‑vector
by a unique phase so that later token embeddings are no longer oblivious
to their exact ordinal location.



---

### Why a division appears instead of multiplication

The paper writes  

$$
{\Theta}_{i}= {\Theta}^{-\frac{{2} i}{d}},
\: {\bf {\Theta}}=10000 .
$$

This can be rewritten as  

$$
n{\color{blue}\\Theta}_{i}= 
\dfrac{ n } { ({ {\Theta}}^{\:{{2}/d}})^{{}} }
     =\dfrac{ n } {( {{\\beta }} )^{{}} },
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~{\bf {where}}\;
        {{\\beta }}={{\\Theta}}^{{{{2}/d}}}\;(>1).
$$

Thus dividing by ${\\beta}^{{} }$ is algebraically *the same* as multiplying  
by ${\\Theta}_{i}$.  
The notation ‘divide’ has become conventional because it makes clear how
the phase shrinks monotonically when ${}{}{}{}{}{}${} grows – higher planes rotate more slowly than lower planes – exactly the intent behind assigning diminishing frequencies across dimensions.



---

### How frequencies behave over the encoder’s dimensions  

* __Low–${}_{}${}<sup>$ {}${}</sup>)__ → No division  ⇒ phases grow quickly.    
The cosine–sine pair oscillates many times over short stretches,
giving us fine granularity necessary to distinguish neighbours.

* __High–${}_{}${}<sup>$ {}${}</sup>)__ → Division by larger powers ⇒ phases grow slowly.    
Those planes change only gradually across the whole sequence,
which lets the model keep track over very long distances without running into early wrap‑around collisions.  

Because ${}\\beta >1,\;\Beta ^{{{}ij}}}$ explodes geometrically;
hence even though we double ${}{}{}, most higher planes barely rotate
within millions of tokens – precisely what makes RoPE suitable already
for long horizons without extra parameters.



##### Effect of changing the base ${\\boldsymbol \\Theta}$  

Increasing ${\\boldsymbol \\Theta}$ makes every ${}\\Beta$ larger,
thereby pushing all angular increments further apart;
the beam covers more distinct rotations before it repeats,
so representations stay distinct even farther along the sequence.
The inverse – reducing ${}\\boldsymbol \\Theta$ – compresses rotations;
the “period” ($ \\pi\times {\\Beta}^{{}{}} )$
shrinks and two distant tokens start looking alike much sooner,
which hurts long‑context performance unless we compensate via some kind
of rescaling/interpolation (exactly what LongRoPE does).

### A concrete walk‑through  

Assume an encoder dimensionality ${ d_{\rm total}=32 }$
(i.e., ${16}$ cosine–sine planes).  
Let us pick three illustrative planes:
${ I_{\ell}=0,\ I_m=5,\ I_h=15 }.$

With the standard choice ${ {\bf \\Theta}=10000 },$
we have  

```python
b     = theta ** (   2 / d_total )
#         ≃ exp((ln 10000)* (   0     /32 )) → b ≃ √10   ≃ 3.162 
```

Hence  


| Plane | I | b<sup>I</sup>| Phase at n |
|-------|---|--------------|-------------|
| Low   | 0 | b<sup>0</sup>| $ n / b^{0}\approx n$
| Medium|5   | b<sup>5</sup>| $\displaystyle\dfrac{ n}{31}$ |
| High  |15 | b<sup>15</sup>| $\displaystyle\dfrac { n}{9907}\,$ |

Now let $ n_1=8606 $ and $ n_2=46090 $.  

Compute three quotients :

After plugging those arguments into `\mathsf {coss}`/\`\mathsf{sinn}`, you see clearly :

* In __low__ planes the argument jumps almost five times larger,

+ In __high__Planes almost nothing changes—the phases remain tiny—so two far‑apart tokens differ only slightly in those components,
capturing global structure rather than local detail.


This demonstrates exactly why “division” keeps low indices fast while producing modest rotations at high indices.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------



#### Bottom line

Equation $(1)$ tells us that every token vector gets rotated in many independent two–dimensional planes,
each plane turning at a different rate given by `${}\\Beta^{-I}` –
higher rates → quick oscillations → capture local order;
lower rates → slow oscillations → encode distant relationships safely.
The divide notation has nothing mystical behind it—it’s just rewriting multiplication with an inverse power because frequencies logically decrease according to dimensional index.
Adjusting this progression—exactly what LongRoPE optimises—is what makes modern models extend naturally beyond millions of input tokens without catastrophic loss of positional awareness.​
