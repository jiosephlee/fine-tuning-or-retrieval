### How robust is DPO if the Bradley–Terry model is misspecified (e.g., Thurstone or context-dependent preferences)?
Question:
If the true preference process is better modeled by Thurstone–Mosteller or exhibits context effects violating Luce's choice axiom, how does DPO behave? Are there known generalizations of the DPO derivation for other pairwise choice models that retain the partition-function cancellation and closed-form optimal policy connection?
Answer:
In the DPO paper, the key simplification—that you can re-parameterize the reward via  
$$
r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y \mid x)}
$$  
and then cancel out the intractable partition function—hinges entirely on assuming a Bradley–Terry (BT) or more generally a Plackett–Luce (PL) form for the human choice model. Under BT you have  
$$
p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x)
= \frac{\exp\bigl(r^*(x,y_1)\bigr)}{\exp\bigl(r^*(x,y_1)\bigr) + \exp\bigl(r^*(x,y_2)\bigr)}
= \sigma\bigl(r^*(x,y_1) - r^*(x,y_2)\bigr),
$$  
and when you substitute the log-ratio form of $r^*$ the problematic normalizer drops out exactly.

If the true preference generator were Thurstone–Mosteller instead—e.g.  
$$
p_{\rm Thurstone}(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x)
= \Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{r^*(x,y_1) - r^*(x,y_2)}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\Bigr)
$$  
with $\Phi$ the Gaussian CDF—or if choices exhibit context-effects that violate Luce’s Independence-of-Irrelevant-Alternatives, the luxury of that neat partition-function cancellation disappears. Concretely, under a probit link you no longer get a softmax policy of the form $\pi(y)\propto \pi_{\rm ref}(y)\exp(r)$, you get integrals over Gaussians, and there is no closed-form “log-ratio” reparameterization that both (a) spans the same set of reward functions up to the usual additive constant and (b) yields a policy you can write down without approximating a normalizer.

In other words, DPO remains statistically consistent in the sense that it computes the maximum-likelihood estimate of the BT model even if that model is wrong, but it is then solving the “wrong” surrogate problem. You will recover the best BT-approximation to your data, but there are no guarantees it recovers the true Thurstone or context-dependent structure. And there is no known Thurstone-analogue of the DPO closed-form trick, since the cancellation relies on the exponential-family (Gumbel noise) assumption.

That said, DPO does extend immediately from the Bradley–Terry pairwise case to the full Plackett–Luce ranking family (as sketched in the paper’s Appendix)—because PL is also driven by exponentiated utilities and inherits the same IIA structure. But once you step outside of the random-utility models whose noise law is Gumbel (i.e.\ whose link is logistic), you generally lose both the neat partition-function cancellation and the exact mapping to a “soft” KL-constrained policy. In those settings one must fall back on either explicit reward-model estimation plus RL (or approximate inference) or develop a new surrogate that respects the true link function, typically at the cost of tractability.
