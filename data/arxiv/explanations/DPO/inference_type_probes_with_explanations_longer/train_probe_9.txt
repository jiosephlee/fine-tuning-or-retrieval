According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', because DPO avoids sampling from the policy and the reinforcement learning loop used in PPO-based RLHF, one would expect gradient variance during fine-tuning to typically be lower. In PPO-style RLHF, the policy update optimizes an expectation over trajectories sampled from the current policy, e.g. maximizing Eq. (3) via Monte Carlo estimates of gradients like
$\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{D},\, y\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[r_\phi(x,y) - \beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\Vert\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})\bigr],$
which is implemented with stochastic samples $y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ and advantage/baseline estimators. This introduces multiple sources of variance: on-policy sampling noise, credit assignment over long sequences, variance in advantage estimates, and sensitivity to baselines and value-function fitting (see Eq. (12), where the soft value function term must be estimated). By contrast, DPO transforms the preference likelihood into a supervised objective over offline pairs (Eq. (8)), eliminating the partition function via cancellation and yielding a per-example binary cross-entropy that is evaluated on fixed $(x,y_w,y_l)$ without on-policy trajectory sampling.

Concretely, the DPO gradient (Eq. (9)) can be written as
$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\Bigl[\sigma\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr)\,\bigl(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) - \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\bigr)\Bigr],$
where $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$. This gradient is computed directly from the minibatch without any Monte Carlo sampling from $\pi_\theta$ and without estimating a value baseline or advantage; the only stochasticity is standard minibatch subsampling, as in supervised learning. Moreover, the logistic weight $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$ acts as an importance factor that down-weights easy, correctly ordered pairs and up-weights hard, misordered ones, further stabilizing updates compared to naive ratio objectives. Because DPO’s reparameterization removes the need to estimate normalization terms (partition functions) and avoids the actor-critic machinery that PPO relies on, the resulting gradients tend to have lower variance and yield more stable and efficient optimization.
