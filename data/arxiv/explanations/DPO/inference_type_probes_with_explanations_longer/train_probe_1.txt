In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors note that the Bradley-Terry two-item comparison model, which can be written as $p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\frac{\exp\bigl(r(x,y_1)\bigr)}{\exp\bigl(r(x,y_1)\bigr)+\exp\bigl(r(x,y_2)\bigr)}$ and equivalently as $p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\left(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\right)$, is mathematically analogous to the common binary statistical model known as logistic regression. This analogy follows directly from the form of the Bradley-Terry probability: the log-odds of preferring $y_1$ over $y_2$ is
$\log \frac{p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)}{1-p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)} = r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2)$, which is a linear predictor in the single scalar “feature” given by the reward difference. Logistic regression models binary outcomes with a logistic link $\sigma(\cdot)$ applied to a linear predictor; here, the linear predictor is precisely the contrast $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$. Consequently, maximum likelihood training under the Bradley-Terry model reduces to minimizing a binary cross-entropy (negative log-likelihood) loss of the form $-\log \sigma\!\bigl(r_\phi(x,y_w)-r_\phi(x,y_l)\bigr)$, as the paper explicitly writes in Eq. (2).

This equivalence is central to the paper’s development of DPO, because the change-of-variables $r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ preserves the Bradley-Terry structure while expressing the same log-odds in terms of the policy and the reference policy. Substituting this reparameterization into the Bradley-Terry likelihood yields
$p^*(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)=\sigma\!\Bigl(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\Bigr)$, which is again a logistic regression with “features” equal to log probability ratios under the policy versus the reference. As a result, the DPO objective (Eq. (6)) is precisely a binary cross-entropy over pairwise preferences, just like logistic regression, but with the predictor tied to the policy itself rather than a standalone reward network. This is why DPO can optimize the RLHF objective with a simple classification loss, mirroring the statistical mechanics of logistic regression applied to paired comparisons.
