According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', if preference datasets contained full rankings instead of pairwise preferences, the part of the learning pipeline that would need to be changed to accommodate models like Plackett-Luce is the preference model. The paper explicitly frames the human feedback stage through a parametric preference model that maps observed human judgments to a likelihood over data: for pairwise preferences it uses the Bradley–Terry (BT) model, e.g., p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma\!\big(r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2)\big) (Eq. 1), and the corresponding maximum-likelihood loss for the reward model is a binary logistic objective (Eq. 2). When moving from pairwise comparisons to full rankings, the BT likelihood is no longer appropriate; instead, one would use the Plackett–Luce (PL) likelihood over permutations, which factors as p^*(y_{(1)} \succ y_{(2)} \succ \cdots \succ y_{(k)} \mid x) = \prod_{j=1}^k \frac{\exp(r(x,y_{(j)}))}{\sum_{m=j}^k \exp(r(x,y_{(m)}))}. The paper notes that DPO’s change-of-variables derivation can be carried out under PL as well (Appendix on Plackett–Luce), so the modification resides in the preference model and its induced likelihood, not in downstream RL or sampling procedures.

Moreover, DPO turns the preference likelihood into a policy objective by substituting the closed-form mapping r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} (Eq. 6), which cancels partition terms in the pairwise BT case and similarly yields tractable expressions under PL. Concretely, the DPO loss under BT (Eq. 8) is a binary cross-entropy on the log-odds of policy ratios, while under PL it becomes the log-likelihood of the ranked list with each factor expressed in terms of \pi/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}. Because this reparameterization preserves the class of reward models consistent with BT/PL (Theorem 1) and keeps the KL constraint implicit via \beta, the policy optimization, reference model choice, and training dynamics remain the same; only the form of p^*(\cdot \mid x)—i.e., the preference model—must be swapped to Plackett–Luce to reflect full rankings.
