According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', under DPO's preference model, if $\\pi_\\theta = \\pi_\\text{ref}$ for a pair, the predicted preference probability is 50%. This follows directly from the DPO likelihood in Eq. (6), which models the probability of preferring $y_w$ over $y_l$ as
$\\sigma\\bigl(\\beta \\log \\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_w\\mid x)} - \\beta \\log \\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_l\\mid x)}\\bigr)$. If $\\pi_\\theta = \\pi_\\text{ref}$, then each log-ratio $\\log \\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y\\mid x)}$ is zero, so the argument to $\\sigma(\\cdot)$ is $0$, and therefore the predicted preference is $\\sigma(0)=\\tfrac{1}{2}$. In other words, when the current policy matches the reference policy on the pair, the DPO model is exactly indifferent between the two completions.

Intuitively, DPO’s reparameterization defines an implicit reward $\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y) = \\beta \\log \\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y\\mid x)}$. If $\\pi_\\theta = \\pi_\\text{ref}$, then $\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y)=0$ for all $y$, so the Bradley–Terry preference model—which depends only on reward differences $\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y_w) - \\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y_l)$—assigns zero difference and hence a 50–50 preference. This is consistent with the closed-form mapping from rewards to optimal policies in Eq. (4) and its inversion in Eq. (5): when the policy provides no advantage over the reference for either completion, there is no basis to prefer one over the other, yielding a tie.
