According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', when the model already rates \(y_w\) much higher than \(y_l\), the per-example weight in the DPO gradient, \(\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w))\), becomes near zero. This follows directly from the DPO gradient expression: \(\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\text{DPO} \propto \sigma\!\big(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w)\big)\big[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) - \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\big]\), where \(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)}\). If \(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w) \gg \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l)\), then the argument \(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w)\) is a large negative number, and the logistic function \(\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}\) saturates toward \(0\). Consequently, the multiplicative weight on that example’s push to increase \(\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)\) and decrease \(\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\) vanishes, reflecting that the model already ranks the pair correctly with high confidence under its implicit reward.

This importance weighting is exactly the behavior one expects from maximum likelihood under the Bradley–Terry preference model. The DPO loss is a binary cross-entropy over the log-odds difference \(\beta\big(\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)} - \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\). In logistic regression, the per-example gradient scales with the prediction error; when the model’s predicted preference probability \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is close to \(1\) (strongly favoring \(y_w\)), the error term is small and so is the gradient. DPO thus automatically downweights “easy” pairs and focuses learning on pairs where the implicit reward \(\hat{r}_\theta\) misorders the completions (i.e., when \(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) \ge \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w)\), the weight \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is closer to \(1\)). This adaptive weighting helps prevent degeneracy observed with naive unlikelihood objectives by avoiding excessive updates on already-correct preferences while still respecting the KL constraint via \(\hat{r}_\theta\)’s dependence on \(\beta\).
