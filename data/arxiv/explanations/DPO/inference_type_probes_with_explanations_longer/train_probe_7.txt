In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors state that conceptually the quantity $\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ in DPO corresponds to the model's implicit reward. This is justified by the KL-constrained RL objective underlying RLHF: the optimal policy under a reward $r(x,y)$ satisfies $\pi_r(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$. Rearranging yields $r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta \log Z(x)$, where $Z(x)$ is a partition function depending only on $x$. Under the Bradley–Terry (or more generally Plackett–Luce) preference models, preferences depend only on reward differences, e.g., $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$, so the additive term $\beta \log Z(x)$ cancels. Consequently, the preference likelihood can be written entirely in terms of the policy ratio, and the effective reward governing preferences is exactly $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$.

Moreover, the paper shows that rewards are identifiable only up to an $x$-dependent shift, and defines an equivalence relation $r\sim r'$ if $r(x,y)-r'(x,y)=f(x)$. They prove that all reward functions in an equivalence class induce the same optimal policy and the same Bradley–Terry preference distribution. Theorem 1 then constructs, for any reward class, a representative of the form $\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ via a projection that subtracts the log-partition function, ensuring the induced $\pi$ is a valid distribution. Hence, optimizing the Bradley–Terry likelihood directly over $\pi_\theta$ is equivalent to fitting a reward in that class, with the implicit reward being the policy-relative log-density scaled by $\beta$. Intuitively, this quantity measures how much the learned policy improves the log-probability of $y$ relative to the reference, acting like a soft “advantage” and thus serving the role of a reward in the DPO framework.
