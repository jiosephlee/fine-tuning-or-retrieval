According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', if the assumptions required by Theorem 1 do not hold for the preference data distribution, recovery of the optimal reward class by DPO is not guaranteed. Theorem 1 establishes that, under “mild assumptions,” any reward class consistent with Plackett–Luce (and Bradley–Terry in particular) preferences can be represented via the reparameterization \(r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\). This claim relies on the preference model being correctly specified (e.g., the Bradley–Terry logistic link over reward differences), and on identifiability conditions that ensure the maximum-likelihood estimates are consistent with the true preference-induced reward class (cf. the cited consistency results in \cite{bong2022generalized}). It also hinges on the equivalence-class lemmas: if rewards differ only by an \(x\)-dependent offset \(f(x)\), they induce the same preference distribution and the same optimal KL-constrained policy. These statements make the change-of-variables in DPO valid and allow the partition function term to cancel in the Bradley–Terry likelihood, yielding Eq. (10) and the DPO loss in Eq. (11). If preferences are not generated by a Plackett–Luce/Bradley–Terry-like mechanism, or the data are not drawn from the assumed distributional setting, then the logistic preference likelihood is misspecified, the equivalence-class reasoning does not align with the true data-generating process, and the reparameterization may not capture the optimal reward class.

Moreover, several practical assumptions interact with this guarantee. The derivation assumes a well-defined reference policy \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) closely matched to the distribution that generated the preference pairs, so that the KL constraint and the projection operator \(f(r;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\beta)\) select a representative within the correct reward equivalence class. In practice, the paper notes that preference datasets are often collected with \(\pi^{\mathrm{SFT}}\), and recommends setting \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}=\pi^{\mathrm{SFT}}\) or approximating it by maximizing likelihood of preferred completions. If the actual data come from a significantly different policy, or if the Bradley–Terry model’s under-specification is not resolved by suitable identifiability constraints, then the MLE over the DPO-transformed likelihood may converge to a reward-policy pair outside the true optimal class. Consequently, while DPO is provably sound when the model and data assumptions of Theorem 1 hold, violations of those assumptions (model misspecification, distribution shift, or lack of identifiability) break the theoretical conditions under which DPO’s reparameterization exactly recovers the optimal reward class, making recovery no longer guaranteed.
