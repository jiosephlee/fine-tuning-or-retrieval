In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors note that in the RL objective $\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi(y\mid x)}\bigl[r(x,y)\bigr] - \beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl[\pi(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr]$, the $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ term conceptually acts to keep policy near reference. This is explicit in their formulation and analysis: the KL penalty regularizes the optimization to avoid drifting far from the supervised fine-tuned reference policy $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, which is where the learned reward model (or implicit reward) is trustworthy and where generation diversity is maintained. The optimal solution under the KL constraint has the Boltzmann form
$\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$,
showing directly that $\pi_r$ is a multiplicative reweighting of $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ by exponentiated reward. As $\beta$ decreases, the reweighting becomes sharper, allowing greater deviation; as $\beta$ increases, the exponential factor flattens and $\pi_r$ stays closer to $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Thus, the KL term’s coefficient $\beta$ explicitly tunes how tightly the policy is tethered to the reference distribution.

The paper also motivates this constraint practically: without the KL term, optimizing for reward alone can cause mode collapse and reward over-optimization, pushing the policy into regions where the reward model is inaccurate and harming generation quality. In the RLHF pipeline, the KL penalty “keeps the policy near reference” by penalizing $\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ in the objective, which in actor-critic form appears as $-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ and in DPO emerges as the implicit reward $\hat{r}_{\theta}(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$. The authors emphasize this role in multiple places: the KL “prevents the model from deviating too far from the distribution on which the reward model is accurate” and “maintains generation diversity.” Their empirical frontiers further corroborate that methods balancing reward with KL (including DPO) achieve higher reward at comparable KL, illustrating the intended effect of the KL term as a conceptual and operational anchor to the reference policy.
