# Beyond RLHF: Where DPO Could Go Next

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) reframes the usual RLHF pipeline by turning the implicit reward-policy relationship inside-out: instead of first fitting a reward model rφ and then running reinforcement learning to obtain πθ, DPO parameterizes the reward so that the optimal policy is available in closed form and directly fits πθ to human pairwise preferences with a simple binary cross‑entropy objective. The original paper shows that this yields a stable, efficient alternative to PPO-based RLHF for tasks like controlled sentiment, summarization, and single‑turn dialogue. Here I outline concrete directions for taking DPO beyond the text experiments in the paper, and highlight important open questions the authors themselves raise: scaling, self‑labeling, potential reward over‑optimization, safety‑targeted preference data, and extensions to other generative modalities.
