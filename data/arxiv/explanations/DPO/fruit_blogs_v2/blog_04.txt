- PPO (or other RL approaches) remain useful if you want to explicitly inspect a learned reward model for diagnostics or to reuse an explicit reward for other <|endoftext|> uses <|endoftext|> policy search beyond language modeling). ## Practical notes for adoption
- Implementation: DPO is just a cross-entropy-style binary classification loss over preference pairs, using the log-probability ratios to π_ref. No RL library or on-policy sampling loop required.
- Hyperparameters: The main new knob is β. The paper reports good results with minimal tuning of β across tasks; typical values explored were {0.05, 0.1, 1, 5} in their sweeps.
- Reference policy π_ref: When an SFT model is available, use that as π_ref (common in RLHF pipelines). If not, fit π_ref by maximizing likelihood on the preferred completions from the preference dataset (the authors do this for the dialogue experiments).
- Sampling/temperature: DPO <|endoftext|> to be more robust across sampling temperatures than PPO in the experiments shown.

## Bottom line

That yields a simple cross-entropy loss, faster and more stable training, and better empirical reward–KL efficiency and win rates on the tested tasks. If you're fine-tuning LMs from <|endoftext|> preferences and want a practical, low-friction method, DPO should be high on your list to try.
