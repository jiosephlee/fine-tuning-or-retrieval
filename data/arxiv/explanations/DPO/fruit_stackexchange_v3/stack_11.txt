### How should we compute and report sequence-level KL in practice for DPO?
Question:
The evaluation reports the average sequence-level KL \text{KL}(\pi\,\Vert\,\pi_\text{ref}), described as the sum of per-timestep KLs. In practice, for autoregressive LMs, do we estimate this as \sum_t \mathbb{E}_{y_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})}[\log \pi(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}) - \log \pi_\text{ref}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})]? If so, how is the expectation approximated—by teacher forcing on sampled sequences from \pi, or on a held-out set? And does using greedy/temperature sampling affect the KL estimate, potentially confounding the reported frontiers?
Answer:
Short answer: the sequence-level <|endoftext|> you want is the expectation under the policy $\pi$ of the tokenwise log-probability ratio summed over timesteps,
<|endoftext|> for a prompt $x$
\[ \mathrm{KL}(\pi\Vert\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\mid x)
= \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)}\Biggl[\sum_{t=1}^{T(y)} \bigl(\log\pi(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}) - \log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})\bigr)\Biggr]. \]
In practice this expectation is <|endoftext|> by Monte Carlo over continuations <|endoftext|> under $\pi$ (and you must be careful about <|endoftext|> method because the estimate depends on how you sample). Below I explain the practical choices, what they measure, and how to avoid confusing results when reporting frontiers.

How to estimate the expectation
- Direct Monte Carlo (most faithful to the objective). For each prompt $x$, draw $K$ rollouts $y^{(k)} \sim \pi(\cdot\mid x)$ using the <|endoftext|> procedure you care about <|endoftext|> ancestral sampling at temperature $T$, or greedy <|endoftext|> if that’s what you will use). For each <|endoftext|> sequence compute the <|endoftext|> log-probabilities under both models and sum them:
  \[ \widehat{\mathrm{KL}}(x) \approx \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{t=1}^{T(y^{(k)})} \bigl(\log\pi(y^{(k)}_t\mid x,y^{(k)}_{<t}) - \log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{(k)}_t\mid x,y^{(k)}_{<t})\bigr). \]
  Average this over prompts to get the reported sequence-level <|endoftext|>
  This is exactly the sample-based estimator implied by the paper’s description (they reported “sum of per-timestep KLs”, which is the same quantity written above).

Alternatives and their meaning
- Teacher‑forcing / held‑out data prefixes. If you compute
  \[ \sum_t \mathbb{E}_{y_t\sim \text{data}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})}[\log\pi - \log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}], \]
  <|endoftext|> evaluate tokenwise <|endoftext|> on gold continuations or on a held‑out <|endoftext|> using teacher forcing) you get the <|endoftext|> averaged under the data distribution of continuations, not under $\pi$. That is a different metric: it tells you how the models differ on the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> (useful for measuring faithfulness to reference data), but it is not the <|endoftext|> term in the RLHF/DPO objective (which is an expectation under the learned policy $\pi$). So do not mix them if you want to report the same frontier the paper optimizes.
- Exact per‑timestep <|endoftext|> sums by marginalizing the vocabulary. It is possible, for a given prefix $y_{<t}$, to compute the exact tokenwise <|endoftext|>
  \[ \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\pi(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\Vert\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\bigr)
  = \sum_{w\in\mathcal{V}} \pi(w\mid x,y_{<t})\bigl(\log\pi(w\mid x,y_{<t})-\log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(w\mid x,y_{<t})\bigr). \]
  Then sum those over prefixes encountered under rollouts and average. This yields a lower-variance, unbiased estimator of the same expectation, but it is more expensive because it requires full softmaxes and vocabulary sums at every visited prefix. It can be used if you want accurate <|endoftext|> estimates with fewer rollouts.

Effect of <|endoftext|> (greedy, temperature, etc.) and confounding
Because the objective’s <|endoftext|> is an expectation under $\pi$, the decoding/sampling procedure used to produce rollouts matters. If you:
- use greedy <|endoftext|> to produce a single deterministic sequence per prompt, your Monte Carlo estimator <|endoftext|> to that single path and yields the <|endoftext|> under the degenerate distribution <|endoftext|> on the greedy output. - change sampling temperature, nucleus parameters, or otherwise alter the stochastic policy you sample from, you change the distribution of prefixes and next-token <|endoftext|> under $\pi$, which changes the estimated expectation. In short: sampling strategy directly affects the <|endoftext|> estimate and therefore can shift the reported frontier.

Recommendations to avoid confounding (and to match the paper)
To report frontiers like in the paper, be explicit and consistent about how you estimate the expectation:
1. Match the sampling procedure to the policy you are evaluating. If the intended policy is $\pi$ as trained (usually ancestral sampling at some temperature), sample from $\pi$ with that procedure when computing <|endoftext|> If you evaluate greedy/argmax outputs, make that clear and compute <|endoftext|> under the greedy-induced distribution (recognize that is a different metric).
2. Use Monte Carlo rollouts with $K$ large enough to get stable averages, or compute exact tokenwise KLs for visited prefixes (vocabulary sum) to reduce variance if compute/time budget permits.
3. Terminate sums at end-of-sequence; include the EOS token probability in the sums <|endoftext|> sum over $t$ up to $T(y)$ where $T(y)$ includes the EOS).

Summary
