In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors note that the Bradley-Terry two-item comparison model, which can be written as $p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\frac{\exp\bigl(r(x,y_1)\bigr)}{\exp\bigl(r(x,y_1)\bigr)+\exp\bigl(r(x,y_2)\bigr)}$ and equivalently as $p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\left(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\right)$, is mathematically analogous to the common binary statistical model known as logistic regression. This is sensible because the paper explicitly casts preference modeling as a binary classification problem with the negative log-likelihood loss $\mathcal{L}_R(r_{\phi},\mathcal{D})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\bigl[\log\sigma\!\bigl(r_{\phi}(x,y_w)-r_{\phi}(x,y_l)\bigr)\bigr]$, which is exactly the cross-entropy objective used by logistic regression, and because the Bradley-Terry parameterization yields a log-odds that is linear in the “score difference”: $\log\frac{p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)}{1-p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)}=r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$, mirroring logistic regression’s $\log\frac{p}{1-p}=w^\top x$ but with the feature map being the difference in item scores; furthermore, the paper’s DPO objective maintains this logistic structure by replacing $r(x,y)$ with the implicit reward $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ and optimizing $-\log\sigma\!\bigl(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)\bigr)$, reinforcing that both BT and logistic regression model pairwise win probabilities via a sigmoid of a linear (here, difference) score, with identifiability up to an additive $f(x)$ absorbed as an intercept that cancels in the difference.
