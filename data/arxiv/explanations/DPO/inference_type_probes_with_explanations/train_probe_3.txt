According to the paper “Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model,” if the assumptions required by Theorem 1 do not hold for the preference data distribution, recovery of the optimal reward class by DPO is not guaranteed. This is because Theorem 1 provides the core representational guarantee for DPO—namely, that under “mild assumptions” all reward classes consistent with the Plackett–Luce/Bradley–Terry (PL/BT) preference model can be represented as r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} for some policy \pi, which in turn ensures that maximizing the DPO likelihood recovers a member of the correct equivalence class \{r : r - r' = f(x)\} that induces the optimal policy \pi^{*} via Eq. (3)–(6); however, the paper explicitly notes both the under-specification of PL/BT (Lemma 1) and the need for identifiability/consistency “under suitable assumptions of the preference data distribution,” so if the data are not generated by a PL/BT-consistent latent reward, or if identifiability/coverage conditions fail, then the cancellation that yields Eq. (6) is model-misspecified and the MLE in Eq. (7) can converge to a pseudo-true solution not in the optimal reward class, meaning DPO’s change-of-variables no longer guarantees recovery of the class that induces \pi^{*}.
