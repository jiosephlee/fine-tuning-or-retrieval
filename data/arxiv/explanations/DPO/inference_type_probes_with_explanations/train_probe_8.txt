In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors note that in the RL objective $\max_{\pi}\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi(y\mid x)}\bigl[r(x,y)\bigr]-\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl[\pi(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr]$, the $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ term conceptually acts to keep the policy near the reference because it arises as the penalty (Lagrange multiplier form) for a KL-constrained reward maximization that explicitly limits deviation from $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and the resulting optimal policy has the closed form $\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\bigr)$, which is a multiplicative “tilt” of the reference distribution whose departure is controlled by $\beta$; this penalty prevents the policy from straying into regions where the reward model is unreliable, preserves generation diversity, avoids mode collapse, and ensures learning remains in-distribution relative to $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, as emphasized in their discussion of Eq. (3) and the practical role of the KL term in RLHF.
