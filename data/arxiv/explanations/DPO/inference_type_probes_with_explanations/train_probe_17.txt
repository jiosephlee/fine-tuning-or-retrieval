In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the normalization term $\beta \log\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r_{\phi}(x, y)\right)$ in the actor-critic objective conceptually represents the model's soft value function. This follows from the control-as-inference derivation of the KL-constrained RLHF objective, where the optimal policy takes the Boltzmann form $\pi^{*}(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r_{\phi}(x,y)\right)$, making the normalizer $Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r_{\phi}(x,y)\right)$ a partition function over actions at state/prompt $x$; taking $\beta\log Z(x)$ yields the log-sum-exp “soft” backup $V_{\text{soft}}(x)$ that aggregates action-values $Q_{\text{soft}}(x,y)=r_{\phi}(x,y)$ under the reference prior $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, exactly as in maximum-entropy RL. In the actor-critic objective (Eq. (AC)), this term appears as $f(r_{\phi},\pi_{\text{ref}},\beta)=r_{\phi}(x,y)-\beta\log\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r_{\phi}(x, y)\right)$, where the subtracted $\beta\log Z(x)$ depends only on $x$ and thus acts like a value baseline; it does not change the optimum but stabilizes policy gradients by centering them around the soft state value, which explains both its conceptual interpretation as $V_{\text{soft}}(x)$ and the paper’s observation that omitting or poorly estimating it leads to high-variance, unstable learning.
