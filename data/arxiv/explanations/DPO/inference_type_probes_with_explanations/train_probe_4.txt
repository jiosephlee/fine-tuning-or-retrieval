According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', from the optimal policy formula $\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$, as $\beta \to 0$, the optimal policy becomes concentrated on the highest-reward outputs. This follows from the Boltzmann form of the KL-constrained optimum (Eq. (4) in the paper): writing $\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$, the log-probabilities scale as $\log \pi_r(y\mid x)=\log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)+\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)-\log Z(x)$, so for fixed $r(x,y)$ the term $\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)$ dominates as $\beta\to 0$, making probabilities exponentially favor any $y$ that maximize $r(x,y)$; formally, letting $Y^*(x)=\arg\max_y r(x,y)$, we have $\lim_{\beta\to 0}\pi_r(y\mid x)=0$ for $y\notin Y^*(x)$ and the mass concentrates on $Y^*(x)$, with tie-breaking proportional to $\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)$ within $Y^*(x)$, which is the standard zero-temperature limit of a Gibbs distribution.
