### How does the equivalence class of rewards (differing by f(x)) interact with the KL-regularized optimal policy?
Question:
Section 5 defines an equivalence relation where r and r' are equivalent if r(x,y) - r'(x,y) = f(x). Lemma 2 states that equivalent rewards induce the same optimal policy under the constrained RL problem. Could someone derive this explicitly starting from \pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)? It seems adding f(x) just rescales the partition function Z(x), but I want to be sure there are no edge cases (e.g., support mismatches or unnormalized densities) that break the argument.
Answer:
Yes — adding a term that depends only on the prefix, i.e. replacing a reward $r(x,y)$ with $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)+f(x)$, does not change the KL-regularized optimal policy given in the paper, provided the usual regularity conditions (positive finite partition function and consistent support) hold. Here is a concise derivation and a statement of the edge cases to watch for.

Start from the closed-form optimal policy (Eq. (4) / Eq. (ref in your quote)):
$$
\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z_r(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big),
\qquad
Z_r(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big).
$$

Now form the alternative reward $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)+f(x)$ where $f$ depends only on $x$. Plug $r'$ into the optimal-policy formula:
$$
\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)
= \frac{1}{Z_{r'}(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}(r(x,y)+f(x))\Big).
$$
Because $f(x)$ does not depend on $y$ you can factor it out of both numerator and partition function:
$$
\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)
= \frac{1}{Z_{r'}(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big),
$$
and
$$
Z_{r'}(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big)
=\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}f(x)\Big) Z_r(x).
$$
Thus the common factor $\exp(\tfrac{1}{\beta}f(x))$ cancels between numerator and denominator, giving
$$
\pi_{r'}(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z_r(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Big)=\pi_r(y\mid x).
$$
So $r$ and $r'$ produce exactly the same optimal policy.

Edge cases / regularity conditions to be aware of:
1. Support of $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)$. If $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)=0$ for some $y$ but $\exp(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y))$ is infinite or extremely large, the formula still gives $\pi_r(y\mid x)=0$ because the factor $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)$ zeros the numerator. Concretely, the family of representable optimal policies is constrained to actions $y$ with $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)>0$; any $y$ with zero reference probability will remain impossible under $\pi_r$. Thus the equivalence statement holds only for rewards considered on the support where $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)>0$. If you want the optimal policy to put mass on actions not supported by $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, that cannot occur via this formula unless $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is changed.

2. Finiteness and positivity of partition function. We require $0<Z_r(x)<\infty$. Adding $f(x)$ multiplies $Z$ by $\exp(\tfrac{1}{\beta}f(x))$, so if $Z_r(x)$ is finite and positive then so is $Z_{r'}(x)$. If $Z_r(x)$ were infinite (e.g., improperly specified $r$ growing too fast over $y$), the closed-form is not well-defined and the cancellation argument is moot.

3. Unnormalized models / numerical issues. In practice with continuous or very large discrete action spaces one estimates or approximates $Z$; the algebraic cancellation above is exact in the analytic formula, but numerical approximations must be handled carefully. The paper explicitly uses this cancellation to reparameterize rewards in terms of the policy (Eq. (6) and following): the additive $x$-only term is exactly the degree of freedom that the Bradley--Terry / Plackett--Luce preference model does not identify, and canceling it is what allows the reparameterization $r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta\log Z(x)$ and then choosing the representative with $\log Z(x)=0$ (or equivalently enforcing the normalization (11) in the paper).

In short: the algebra is straightforward and adding $f(x)$ only rescales numerator and partition function by the same factor, so the optimal policy is invariant. The only caveats are the usual ones: you must work on the support of $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and assume finite, positive partition functions (i.e., the model is well-posed).
