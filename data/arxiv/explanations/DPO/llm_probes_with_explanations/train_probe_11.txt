In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors state that the DPO update increases the relative $\log$-probability of preferred responses compared to dispreferred ones, and that it prevents model degeneration by adding a specific stabilizing component: per-example importance weight. This follows directly from the DPO objective $\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}[\log \sigma(\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)})]$, whose gradient takes the form $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}=-\beta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sigma\!\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\big(\nabla_\theta\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\big)\right]$, where the implicit reward is $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$. The direction of the update explicitly increases $\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$ and decreases $\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$—i.e., it increases the relative log-probability of the preferred completion—while the multiplicative term $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$ serves as a per-example importance weight that is large precisely when the model’s implicit reward orders the pair incorrectly (or only weakly correctly) and small when it already prefers $y_w$, thereby adaptively down-weighting “easy” or already-correct pairs and focusing updates where the model errs. This per-example weighting, together with the KL-based reparameterization $\hat r_\theta=\beta \log \pi_\theta-\beta \log \pi_{\text{ref}}$ that ties updates to a reference model, curbs overly aggressive shifts in probabilities that a naïve unweighted “probability ratio” or unlikelihood objective would apply uniformly (which the authors observe can cause degeneration), thus stabilizing training and preventing collapse while still moving the policy toward human-preferred behavior.
