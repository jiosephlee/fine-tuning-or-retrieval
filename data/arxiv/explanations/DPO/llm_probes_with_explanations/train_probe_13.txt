In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the gradient of the DPO loss assigns the largest update weight to comparison pairs with a large value of $\sigma\bigl(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr)$, i.e., cases where the implicit reward model ranks the dispreferred completion above the preferred completion. This follows directly from the gradient of the DPO objective (Eq. (8)), which is $-\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\!\left[\sigma\!\left(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\right)\left(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\right)\right]$, where $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$; because the loss is a binary cross-entropy on the Bradley–Terry margin, its derivative introduces the logistic factor $\sigma(-m)$ with $m=\beta\left(\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)$, which simplifies to $\sigma\!\left(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\right)$ as shown; since $\sigma(z)$ is monotone increasing, this weight is largest when $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)>\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)$ by a large margin (i.e., the model’s implicit reward misorders the pair), thereby focusing updates on correcting the most egregiously wrong comparisons, and is scaled in magnitude by $\beta$, which reflects the strength of the KL constraint.
