In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the central technical trick that allows DPO to avoid fitting an explicit reward model is a change of variables. Specifically, starting from the KL-constrained RLHF objective, the optimal policy for any reward function satisfies the Boltzmann form $\pi_r(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\big)$, which can be rearranged to express the reward in terms of the policy as $r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)$; substituting this reparameterization into the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood $p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\big(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\big)$ cancels the partition term $\log Z(x)$ and yields a likelihood that depends only on the policy ratios $\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$, leading directly to the DPO objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\big[\log\sigma\big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\big]$, a simple binary cross-entropy over policies instead of rewards; furthermore, Theorem 1 shows this reparameterization spans all preference-equivalent reward classes (i.e., rewards up to $f(x)$), so optimizing the policy under this change of variables is as general as fitting an explicit reward model while making the optimal policy analytically tractable.
