In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the specific mechanism in the DPO update that prevents the model degeneration that occurs with a naive probability ratio objective is the per-example importance weight. Concretely, DPO minimizes a Bradley–Terry-derived logistic loss over preference pairs that, after the change of variables \( \hat{r}_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} \), yields the gradient \( \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{DPO} = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\!\left[\sigma\!\big(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\,\big(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\big)\right] \), where the logistic factor \( \sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)) \) acts as a per-example importance weight that is large when the model’s implicit reward ranks the dispreferred response higher (i.e., when it is “wrong”) and small when it already ranks the preferred response higher; this adaptivity prevents uniformly large updates that can drive probability ratios to extremes and cause mode collapse. Because the implicit reward uses the KL-regularized form \( \hat{r}_\theta = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} \), the weight ties updates to the strength of the KL constraint, further damping over-aggressive shifts away from the reference policy; in contrast, a naive probability ratio objective that lacks this per-example weighting applies similar pressure across examples regardless of confidence, which the authors observe leads to degeneration in practice, whereas the DPO weight concentrates learning where corrections are needed and backs off where the model is already aligned.
