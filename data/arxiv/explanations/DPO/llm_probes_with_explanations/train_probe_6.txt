In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', training $\\pi_\\theta$ with the DPO loss $\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{DPO}}$ (Eq. 8), which applies the logistic function $\\sigma$ to paired comparisons, is most analogous to training a binary classifier. This is because DPO is derived by reparameterizing the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood so that the probability of a preference label $y_w \\succ y_l$ is $\\sigma\\big(\\beta\\,\\log\\frac{\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\\mid x)} - \\beta\\,\\log\\frac{\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\\mid x)}\\big)$ (Eq. 8), and the training objective is the negative log-likelihood over these labels, exactly matching a binary cross-entropy loss on pairwise inputs under the Bradley–Terry model (cf. Eq. 3). In other words, DPO optimizes the likelihood of the “positive” class (the preferred completion) over paired examples, just as a logistic classifier would, with the implicit reward $\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y)=\\beta\\log\\frac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y\\mid x)}$ playing the role of the logit; moreover, the gradient (Eq. 9) takes the familiar form of increasing $\\log\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)$ and decreasing $\\log\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)$, weighted by $\\sigma\\big(\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y_l)-\\hat{r}_\\theta(x,y_w)\\big)$, which mirrors the error-weighted updates in binary logistic regression when the current model misorders the pair.
