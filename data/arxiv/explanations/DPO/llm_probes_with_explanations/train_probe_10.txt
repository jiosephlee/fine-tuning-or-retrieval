In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', DPO replaces the two-stage reward modeling plus RL pipeline with a single-stage optimization using a common supervised loss, and the supervised loss DPO uses to optimize the policy directly is binary cross-entropy. This follows because DPO reparameterizes the KL-constrained RLHF objective by expressing the reward in terms of the optimal policy and the reference policy via \(r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\), and when this is substituted into the Bradley–Terry preference model the partition function cancels, yielding a likelihood over preferences that depends only on the policy’s log-probability ratios; maximizing this likelihood with respect to a parametric policy \(\pi_\theta\) gives the DPO objective \(\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim \mathcal{D}}\big[\log \sigma\big(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\big]\), which is exactly a binary cross-entropy (logistic) loss over pairwise preference labels, analogous to the reward model’s BCE in Eq. (2) but with the policy directly parameterizing the implicit reward; this change-of-variables removes the need to fit an explicit reward model and then run RL, enabling a single-stage supervised optimization without on-policy sampling.
