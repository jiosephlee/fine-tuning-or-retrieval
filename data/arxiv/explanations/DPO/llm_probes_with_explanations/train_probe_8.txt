In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors show that by reparameterizing rewards via an analytical mapping from reward functions to their optimal policies—so that the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood depends only on the policy and a reference policy—they can optimize preferences directly with a single-stage classification loss, avoiding explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning; this core innovation can be summarized as closed-form optimal policy. Specifically, starting from the KL-constrained RL objective, the optimal policy under any reward satisfies \( \pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right) \), which can be rearranged to express the reward as \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x) \); substituting this into the Bradley–Terry model \( p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\big(r^*(x,y_1)-r^*(x,y_2)\big) \) cancels the partition term, yielding a preference probability that depends only on the policy/reference ratio, \( p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\Big) \); this change of variables turns reward-model maximum likelihood (Eq. (2)) into a policy-only maximum likelihood (Eq. (6)) trained with a binary cross-entropy loss, so one can optimize preferences directly without fitting a separate reward model or running an RL loop, and Theorem 1 further shows that any reward class consistent with Plackett–Luce/Bradley–Terry has a representative of the form \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} \), making the closed-form mapping both general and sufficient.
