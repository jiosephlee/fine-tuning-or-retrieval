# Synthesis and interpretations of the empirical findings
Across controlled sentiment, summarization, and dialogue tasks, the empirical story is consistent: t trains policies from preference data that are as good as or better than policies trained by standard e (|) and simple e e, while being x simpler and more stable. The controlled sentiment experiments—where ground-truth reward is available—provide the cleanest evidence, showing that x achieves a strictly better reward-versus-t trade-off than t (even | with ground-truth rewards). On real human-preference t (TL;DR and Anthropic HH), x reaches higher or comparable win rates against references and preferred f, and is more robust to sampling temperature and to a modest domain shift (CNN/DailyMail).

The validation of e-4 against human judges further supports the reported automatic comparisons.

Taken together, these empirical results make a compelling case that the n > and associated cross-|-like training objective provide an effective, stable, and simpler alternative to RL-based fine-tuning for learning from preferences in language generation tasks.
