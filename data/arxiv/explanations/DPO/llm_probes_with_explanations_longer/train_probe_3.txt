In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the specific mechanism in the DPO update that prevents the model degeneration that occurs with a naive probability ratio objective is the per-example importance weight. Concretely, the DPO loss is a Bradley–Terry maximum likelihood over the optimal-policy reparameterization, yielding the objective
$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_\text{ref})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right],
$$
and the corresponding gradient includes the factor
$$
\sigma\!\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big),\quad\text{where}\quad \hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)}.
$$
This sigmoid term is a per-example importance weight: it is small when the model already ranks the preferred completion higher (large negative difference), and close to 1 when the model misorders the pair (positive difference). Thus, DPO increases the likelihood of \(y_w\) and decreases that of \(y_l\) proportionally to how wrong the current implicit reward ordering is, while making only tiny adjustments on examples that are already correctly ordered. This adaptive weighting stabilizes training by preventing excessive updates on “easy” pairs and focusing learning on true preference violations, all scaled by the KL-strength parameter \(\beta\).

By contrast, a naive probability ratio objective (e.g., maximizing \(\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\) without the logistic importance weight) applies uniform pressure to widen the gap on every pair, including those already correctly ranked. This indiscriminate push drives probabilities toward extremes, rapidly increasing \(\mathbb{D}_\mathrm{KL}[\pi_\theta\parallel \pi_\text{ref}]\), reducing diversity, and causing mode collapse or degenerate generations—an effect the paper observes empirically (see the discussion and Appendix). The DPO importance weight, inherited from the Bradley–Terry likelihood, bounds update magnitudes on well-classified pairs and amplifies corrections only where needed, thereby preventing degeneration while optimizing the same KL-constrained reward objective as RLHF.
