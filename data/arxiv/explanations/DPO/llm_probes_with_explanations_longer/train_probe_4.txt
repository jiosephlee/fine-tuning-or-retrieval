In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the central technical trick that allows DPO to avoid fitting an explicit reward model is a change of variables. Starting from the KL-constrained RLHF objective, the optimal policy for any reward function \(r\) has the closed form \(\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\big)\) (Eq. (4)). Rearranging gives an expression for the reward in terms of the policy and the reference model, \(r(x,y)=\beta\log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\) (Eq. (5)). Because Bradley–Terry preferences depend only on reward differences, \(p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\big(r^*(x,y_1)-r^*(x,y_2)\big)\), the unknown partition term \(\beta\log Z(x)\) cancels, yielding a preference likelihood that depends solely on policy-to-reference log-probability ratios (Eq. (6)). This lets the authors replace the usual reward-model MLE (Eq. (2)) with a maximum-likelihood objective over the policy itself, \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}\big[\log \sigma\big(\beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\big]\) (Eq. (7)), i.e., a simple binary cross-entropy loss without sampling in the RL loop.

This change of variables is not merely a heuristic; it preserves the relevant reward class and its optimal policy under the Bradley–Terry/Plackett–Luce framework. Rewards that differ by a function of the context, \(r-r'=f(x)\), are equivalent: they induce the same preference distribution and the same KL-regularized optimal policy. Theorem 1 shows that, for any reward class consistent with Bradley–Terry, there exists a representative of the form \(r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\); this is obtained by projecting an arbitrary \(r\) via subtracting \(\beta\log Z(x)\), i.e., normalizing with the partition function so that \(\sum_y \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\big)=1\). Consequently, optimizing \(\pi_\theta\) under \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}\) is equivalent to fitting a reward in the appropriate equivalence class while directly recovering its optimal policy. The gradient further reveals the implicit reward \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\) and a corrective weighting \(\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\) that stabilizes training—obviating the need for an explicit reward model and actor-critic machinery.
