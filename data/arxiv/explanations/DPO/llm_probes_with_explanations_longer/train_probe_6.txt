In the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", training \( \pi_\theta \) with the DPO loss \( \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} \) (Eq. 8), which applies the logistic function \( \sigma \) to paired comparisons, is most analogous to training a binary classifier. The DPO objective is
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim \mathcal{D}}\left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right],
\]
which is exactly the negative log-likelihood of a Bernoulli label indicating that the preferred completion \(y_w\) “wins” over the dispreferred completion \(y_l\), under a logistic link function applied to a score difference. This mirrors the Bradley–Terry model where \(p(y_w \succ y_l\mid x)=\sigma(r(x,y_w)-r(x,y_l))\), and the classical reward-model training loss (Eq. 4) is a binary cross-entropy over pairwise preferences. DPO simply reparameterizes the “score” as the implicit reward \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\), so the argument to \( \sigma \) becomes a logit constructed from policy log-prob ratios rather than an explicit reward network output, but the statistical form remains a logistic regression on a binary preference label.

Consequently, the learning dynamics and interpretation align with binary classification: the model predicts the probability that \(y_w\) is preferred over \(y_l\) given \(x\), with the logit equal to the difference of policy-induced scores, and optimizes the cross-entropy between these predictions and the observed labels. The gradient in the paper makes this explicit:
\[
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\sigma\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\big(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\big)\Big],
\]
which is the usual logistic regression weighting: examples where the model’s current “logit” is wrong (the dispreferred score exceeds the preferred score) receive higher weight, driving the parameters to increase the probability assigned to the positive class (the preferred response) and decrease it for the negative class (the dispreferred response). Thus, DPO optimizes a pairwise binary cross-entropy objective under a logistic model, making it most analogous to training a binary classifier on preference labels.
