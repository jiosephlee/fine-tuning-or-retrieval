In the paper “Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model,” the authors state that the DPO update increases the relative $\log$-probability of preferred responses compared to dispreferred ones, and that it prevents model degeneration by adding a specific stabilizing component: per-example importance weight. This follows directly from the DPO loss, which maximizes a Bradley–Terry likelihood over the log-probability ratios with respect to a reference policy, namely
$\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_\text{ref}) = -\mathbb{E}\big[\log \sigma\big(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\big]$.
Taking gradients yields
$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\text{DPO} = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\big(\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) - \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\big)\big]$,
where $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)}$. The bracketed term updates the policy to increase $\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$ and decrease $\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$—i.e., it directly increases the relative log-probability of preferred over dispreferred responses—but crucially it is scaled by the logistic factor $\sigma\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big)$, which acts as a per-example importance weight.

This per-example importance weight stabilizes training and prevents degeneration because it adaptively modulates the step size based on how “wrong” the current implicit reward ordering is. When the model already prefers $y_w$ over $y_l$ with a large margin (i.e., $\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\gg \hat r_\theta(x,y_l)$), the weight $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$ is small, yielding gentle updates that avoid overconfident shifts and mode collapse. Conversely, when the model incorrectly assigns higher implicit reward to $y_l$, the weight becomes large, focusing learning on hard examples and correcting misorderings efficiently. This weighting arises from the curvature of the logistic (binary cross-entropy) objective on the margin of the reward differences and, together with the $\beta$ scaling that enforces the KL constraint via $\hat r_\theta$, prevents the aggressive, unbounded probability pushes that the naive probability-ratio or unlikelihood objectives can cause—degeneration that the paper reports empirically in Appendix Table (unlikelihood_generations).
