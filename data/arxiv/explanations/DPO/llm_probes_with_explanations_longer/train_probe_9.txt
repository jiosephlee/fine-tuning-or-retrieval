In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the single technical trick that enables DPO to avoid an RL training loop is a change of variables. Starting from the KL-constrained RLHF objective, the optimal policy under any reward function satisfies
\[
\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right),
\]
which can be rearranged to express the reward in terms of the optimal policy and a reference model:
\[
r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x).
\]
Crucially, under the Bradley–Terry (or Plackett–Luce) preference model, only reward differences matter, so when we plug this reparameterization into the preference likelihood, the partition function term \( \beta \log Z(x) \) cancels. This yields a preference probability that depends solely on log policy ratios relative to the reference,
\[
p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right),
\]
and therefore a maximum-likelihood objective over policies,
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim \mathcal{D}}\!\left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right].
\]
Thus, by changing variables from rewards to log policy ratios, DPO turns the original RLHF problem into a simple binary cross-entropy fit of a policy, eliminating the need for policy sampling and value estimation in an RL loop.

This change of variables is not merely heuristic; it preserves the class of reward models relevant to preferences and recovers the same optimal policies. Because Bradley–Terry/Plackett–Luce models are defined up to an additive function of \(x\) in the reward (i.e., rewards differing by \(f(x)\) are equivalent), DPO’s reparameterization selects a canonical representative in each equivalence class:
\[
\hat{r}_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}.
\]
Theorem 1 shows any reward class consistent with the preference model admits such a representative, obtained by subtracting the log-partition term so that the induced optimal policy is normalized. As a result, optimizing the preference likelihood in terms of \(\pi_\theta\) is equivalent to fitting a reward model in the usual BT framework but with an analytically tractable mapping to the optimal policy; this is the single technical maneuver that collapses the two-stage “fit reward + RL” pipeline into a single supervised objective.
