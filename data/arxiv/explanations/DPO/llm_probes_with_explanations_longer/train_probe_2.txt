According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors' ablation-style reasoning indicates that removing the dynamic, per-example importance weighting from DPO is likely to lead to model degeneration. This follows directly from the DPO gradient analysis: the update for a preference pair is multiplied by the factor \( \sigma\!\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l) - \hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\big) \), where \( \hat r_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} \). When the model already ranks the preferred response higher (i.e., \( \hat r_\theta(x,y_w) \gg \hat r_\theta(x,y_l) \)), the weighting shrinks toward 0, dampening further pushes and preserving diversity; when the ranking is wrong, it grows toward 1, prioritizing large corrective updates. Removing this per-example weight amounts to applying the same push to increase \( \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) \) and decrease \( \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x) \) for every pair, regardless of confidence. That uniform pressure over-optimizes already-correct pairs, drives probabilities toward the extremes, and—because the implicit reward is the log-ratio relative to \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\)—accelerates divergence from the reference distribution beyond what the KL constraint governed by \(\beta\) can counteract, producing low-entropy, collapsed behaviors.

The paper’s empirical observations support this mechanism: the authors note that a naïve probability ratio objective “without the weighting coefficient can cause the language model to degenerate” (cf. the gradient interpretation and Appendix results). Intuitively, the weighting implements a self-normalizing step-size that ties the update magnitude to the current margin in the Bradley–Terry likelihood \( \sigma\big(r^*(x,y_w) - r^*(x,y_l)\big) \) after reparameterizing rewards as \( r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} \). Because the Bradley–Terry model only depends on reward differences, large margins should induce small updates; without that property, training continually amplifies already-high \( \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) \) and suppresses \( \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x) \) even when the pair is correctly ordered, shrinking support under \( \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x) \), increasing variance, and encouraging mode collapse and repetitive outputs—classic signs of degeneration in language model fine-tuning.
