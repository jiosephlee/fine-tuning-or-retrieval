Title: Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model

Core idea
The paper introduces Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a simple, reinforcement-learning-free method to align language models (LMs) with human preferences. DPO replaces the standard RLHF pipeline—which learns a separate reward model and then uses RL (e.g., PPO) to optimize the policy under a KL constraint—with a single, stable, maximum-likelihood objective applied directly to the policy. It achieves this by reparameterizing the reward in terms of the policy and the reference model, enabling direct optimization on pairwise preference data under the Bradley–Terry (BT) or Plackett–Luce models. Empirically, DPO matches or exceeds PPO-based RLHF on tasks like sentiment control, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, while being simpler, more stable, and more compute-efficient.

Motivation and background
- Large LMs acquire broad capabilities through unsupervised pretraining but require alignment to user intent for safe and helpful behavior.
- Supervised instruction tuning helps, but collecting high-quality demonstrations is costly; pairwise preferences are easier to obtain and often more informative.
- RLHF (prevalent approach): (1) train a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model; (2) fit a reward model via pairwise preferences using a BT loss; (3) optimize the LM policy via RL (typically PPO) to maximize the learned reward while constraining KL from a reference policy (usually the SFT model).
- Practical issues with RLHF: complexity, instability, need for in-loop sampling, value baselines, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and computational cost.

Key derivation and DPO objective
- Start from the standard KL-regularized objective used in RLHF: maximize expected reward minus $\beta$ times the KL divergence from a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$.
- The optimal policy for any reward $r$ under this objective has the form:
  $\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{ref}(y\mid x)\cdot \exp\big(r(x,y)/\beta\big)$
- Taking logs and rearranging expresses the reward in terms of the optimal policy and reference policy (up to a per-input normalization term). When plugged into the Bradley–Terry preference model—whose likelihood depends only on reward differences—this normalization cancels.
- Therefore, the preference likelihood can be written purely in terms of the policy and the reference:
  The model learns to assign higher relative log-probability to the preferred completion than to the dispreferred one, measured as differences of log probability ratios $\log\pi(y\mid x)-\log\pi_{ref}(y\mid x)$, scaled by $\beta$.
- DPO replaces reward-model training + RL with a single binary cross-entropy loss over preference pairs:
  Minimize $-\mathbb{E}\log\sigma\!\big(\beta[\log\pi(y_w\mid x)-\log\pi_{ref}(y_w\mid x)]-\beta[\log\pi(y_l\mid x)-\log\pi_{ref}(y_l\mid x)]\big)$
- This is equivalent to maximum likelihood under BT, but parameterized directly by the policy. No partition function estimation, no in-loop sampling, and no value baseline are needed.

Mechanics and intuition
- The implicit reward of the current policy is $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta[\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)-\log\pi_{ref}(y\mid x)]$.
- The gradient increases log-probability of $y_w$ and decreases that of $y_l$, with an importance weight $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$ that is larger when the model ranks them in the wrong order. This weighting prevents degeneration observed in naive unlikelihood objectives that push down dispreferred responses uniformly.

Algorithm outline and practical notes
- Build a preference dataset by sampling pairs of completions from $\pi_{ref}$ (often the SFT model) and collecting human labels $y_w \succ y_l$ for each prompt $x$.
- Optimize $\pi_\theta$ with the DPO loss using $\beta$ to control the strength of the KL regularization implicitly.
- If a suitable $\pi_{ref}$ used to collect the data is unavailable, approximate $\pi_{ref}$ by fitting an LM to maximize likelihood of preferred completions; this mitigates distribution shift between the true reference and the available model.

Theoretical foundation: “Your LM is secretly a reward model”
- Rewards are only identifiable up to an additive function of $x$ under BT/Plackett–Luce ($r$ and $r'$ are equivalent if $r(x,y)-r'(x,y)=f(x)$). Within each equivalence class:
  - The induced preference distribution is the same.
  - The optimal policy for the KL-regularized objective is the same.
- Theorem: Under mild assumptions, any reward (up to this equivalence) can be represented as $r(x,y)=\beta\log\!\left[\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{ref}(y\mid x)}\right]$ for some policy $\pi$. Thus, DPO’s parameterization does not restrict the class of learnable rewards, yet makes the optimal policy analytically tractable.
- Interpretation: DPO selects a canonical representative in each reward-equivalence class—one whose induced policy is a valid normalized distribution relative to $\pi_{ref}$—thereby resolving under-identification in a way that removes the need to estimate partition functions or fit value baselines.

Connection to actor–critic instability
- Standard PPO-based RLHF optimizes the same KL-regularized objective but requires handling the normalization/baseline term (soft value) to reduce variance; poor baselines can cause instability.
- DPO’s reparameterization bakes this normalization into the implicit reward $\hat r$, avoiding value-function learning and significantly simplifying training.

Related work
- Instruction tuning and SFT improve alignment but rely on costly demonstrations.
- Learning from preferences: BT/Plackett–Luce modeling for reward learning; RL-based optimization (REINFORCE/PPO) is common.
- Contextual dueling bandits and preference-based RL study learning from pairwise preferences but typically estimate a scoring function and then optimize it.
- DPO directly optimizes a policy from preferences under BT/Plackett–Luce, eliminating explicit reward modeling and RL.

Experiments and findings
Settings and baselines
- Tasks:
  1) Controlled sentiment generation on IMDb prefixes, with a ground-truth sentiment classifier to label preferences and compute exact reward and KL to $\pi_{ref}$.
  2) Reddit TL;DR summarization using GPT-J SFT and human preference data from prior work.
  3) Single-turn dialogue on Anthropic Helpful–Harmless (HH), using Pythia-2.8B as a base; no off-the-shelf SFT exists, so a Preferred-FT model (trained on preferred completions) serves as the reference.
- Baselines:
  - SFT; Preferred-FT; Unlikelihood training; PPO with learned rewards; PPO with ground-truth reward (sentiment); Best-of-N with a learned reward; zero/2-shot prompting where appropriate.

Results
- Sentiment control (reward–KL frontier):
  - DPO achieves a strictly better reward–KL frontier than PPO, even outperforming PPO with access to ground-truth rewards.
  - Demonstrates that DPO optimizes the intended KL-regularized objective more efficiently and stably.
- Summarization (Reddit TL;DR; GPT-4 as judge):
  - DPO achieves about 61% win rate at temperature 0.0, exceeding PPO’s ~57% at its best temperature.
  - DPO attains higher maximum win rates than Best-of-N (which is computationally heavy at inference).
  - DPO is more robust to sampling temperature; PPO’s performance drops at higher temperatures. Preferred-FT gives little improvement over SFT.
  - Human study validates GPT-4 as a proxy judge: GPT-4’s judgments correlate well with human preferences; a “concise” evaluation prompt aligns best with human raters.
- Single-turn dialogue (Anthropic HH; GPT-4 as judge):
  - DPO improves over Preferred-FT and 2-shot prompting, and rivals or exceeds a Best-of-128 baseline, while being far more efficient.
  - A public PPO RLHF model underperforms the base model; Best-of-128 is used as a proxy for strong PPO. DPO is the only efficient method that consistently improves over preferred responses.
  - DPO converges quickly.
- Out-of-distribution generalization (CNN/DailyMail):
  - Policies trained on Reddit TL;DR are evaluated against gold summaries on CNN/DM. DPO generalizes better than PPO at comparable temperatures, suggesting DPO retains generalization while being simpler and not using extra unlabeled prompts.

Practical benefits
- No RL loop, no in-loop sampling, no value function, no partition function estimation.
- A single cross-entropy loss on preference pairs; few hyperparameters (primarily $\beta$).
- Stable and compute-efficient; easy to implement and tune.
- Optimizes the same objective as PPO-based RLHF but with better reward–KL efficiency in experiments.

Limitations and future work
- Further study is needed on out-of-distribution generalization and whether DPO can leverage unlabeled prompts via self-labeling as effectively as PPO-based pipelines.
- Investigate reward over-optimization dynamics under DPO.
- Explore scaling to much larger models and broader tasks.
- Improve automated evaluation protocols; GPT-4 judgments are prompt-sensitive.
- Apply DPO beyond text to other generative modalities.

Takeaway
DPO reframes preference learning by making the policy itself the parameterization of the (implicit) reward, turning RLHF into a straightforward maximum-likelihood problem over preference pairs. This preserves the theoretical underpinnings of BT/Plackett–Luce models, retains the KL-regularized objective, and delivers state-of-the-art alignment results with a simpler, more stable, and more efficient training procedure.
