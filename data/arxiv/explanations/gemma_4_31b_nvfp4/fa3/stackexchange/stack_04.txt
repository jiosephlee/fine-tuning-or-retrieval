\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Confusion regarding FP8 layout constraints: k-major vs mn-major
Question:
In section 2.2, the paper discusses layout requirements for FP8 WGMMA. It says that for FP16, both mn-major and k-major are accepted, but FP8 only supports k-major. I'm confused about what 'k-major' actually means in the context of the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix. Does this mean the data must be contiguous along the head dimension $d$ or the sequence length $N$?
Answer:
In the context of the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix in FlashAttention-3, **$k$-major means that the data must be contiguous along the sequence length dimension $N$**, rather than the head dimension $d$.

To understand why, we have to look at how the paper maps the attention operation to the hardware's general matrix multiplication (GEMM) terminology. The paper defines a GEMM as the product $A \times B^\top$, where $A$ is an $M \times K$ matrix and $B$ is an $N \times K$ matrix. In this notation, the "$k$-dimension" is the inner dimension that is summed over during the dot product.

In the second GEMM of the attention mechanism, $\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$, we can map the matrices as follows:
1. The matrix $\mathbf{P}$ (the attention weights) acts as matrix $A$. Its dimensions are $B_r \times B_c$ (block of query sequence length $\times$ block of key/value sequence length), meaning $M = B_r$ and $K = B_c$.
2. The matrix $\mathbf{V}$ acts as $B^\top$. Since $\mathbf{V}$ has dimensions $B_c \times d$, the corresponding matrix $B$ would be $\mathbf{V}^\top$ with dimensions $d \times B_c$. Here, $N = d$ and $K = B_c$.

The paper defines a matrix as "$k$-major" if it is contiguous in the inner $K$-dimension. For the $\mathbf{V}$ matrix, the $K$-dimension corresponds to the sequence length (specifically the block size $B_c$). Consequently, for $\mathbf{V}$ to be $k$-major, the elements along the sequence length dimension must be stored contiguously in memory.

Intuitively, most LLM tensors are stored as "$mn$-major" (row-major in the context of $\mathbf{V}$), meaning they are contiguous along the head dimension $d$. This is efficient for loading a single token's full embedding, but the FP8 WGMMA (Warpgroup Matrix Multiply-Accumulate) instructions on Hopper GPUs have a strict hardware requirement: they only support operands that are contiguous along the reduction dimension $K$ to maximize throughput.

Because $\mathbf{V}$ is typically stored contiguous along $d$, it is "$mn$-major" relative to the GEMM operation. To satisfy the FP8 hardware constraint, FlashAttention-3 performs an in-kernel transpose using $\texttt{ldmatrix}$ and $\texttt{stmatrix}$ instructions to convert the $\mathbf{V}$ tiles from being contiguous along $d$ to being contiguous along $N$ before they are passed to the Tensor Cores.
