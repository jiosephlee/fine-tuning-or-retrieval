Chapter 3: # Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Math

The transition from a base language model to an instruction-tuned model is a critical phase in developing an artificial intelligence capable of solving complex mathematical problems. While a base model, such as DeepSeekMath-Base, is trained on vast corpora to predict the next token, it may not inherently know how to format its knowledge into a helpful, step-by-step solution when prompted by a user. Supervised Fine-Tuning, or SFT, is the process used to bridge this gap. By training the model on a curated set of high-quality problem-solution pairs, the model learns the specific behavior of a mathematical assistant: interpreting a question, reasoning through the solution, and providing a final answer in a structured format.

# Mathematical Instruction Tuning: Goals and Data Curation

The primary goal of mathematical instruction tuning is to refine the base model so that it can consistently follow instructions and produce accurate, well-reasoned mathematical solutions. For DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, this process begins with the DeepSeekMath-Base model and involves training on a specialized dataset designed to teach the model how to think through problems. This stage of training is less about learning new mathematical facts and more about learning the "mode" of reasoning required to arrive at a correct answer.

The curation of the SFT process involves specific hyperparameters and data handling techniques to ensure stability and efficiency. The model is trained for 500 steps with a batch size of 256, utilizing a constant learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$. To maximize the use of the available context window, training examples are randomly concatenated until they reach a maximum context length of 4K tokens. This structured approach ensures that the model learns to maintain coherence across longer reasoning chains, which is essential for tackling college-level or competition-level mathematics.

# Reasoning Formats: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning

To achieve high performance in mathematics, it is not enough for a model to simply provide the correct answer; it must demonstrate the process of arriving at that answer. DeepSeekMath-Instruct utilizes three distinct reasoning formats to enhance its capabilities: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning. Each of these formats serves a different intuitive purpose in the problem-solving process.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is the most natural form of reasoning for a language model, where the solution is presented as a sequence of natural language steps. By breaking a complex problem into smaller, manageable intermediate steps, the model can "think aloud," which reduces the likelihood of logical leaps and allows for better error correction during the generation process. This format is essential for problems that require conceptual explanation or a narrative proof.

Program-of-Thought (PoT) shifts the reasoning from natural language to executable code. In this format, the model is prompted to solve the problem by writing a Python program. This is particularly useful for quantitative reasoning where precision is paramount. By leveraging a programming language, the model can avoid the common arithmetic errors that often plague purely text-based reasoning.

Tool-integrated reasoning represents a hybrid approach that combines the strengths of both CoT and PoT. In this paradigm, the model integrates natural language reasoning with the use of external tools, such as Python libraries like $math$ and $sympy$. This allows the model to use natural language to strategize and structure the solution while delegating intricate computations or symbolic manipulations to a tool. This synergy enables the model to handle higher levels of complexity than it could with either format alone.

# Building the SFT dataset: Integrating English and Chinese K-12 and competition problems

The effectiveness of SFT is heavily dependent on the quality and diversity of the training data. For DeepSeekMath-Instruct, a comprehensive dataset of 776K training examples was constructed, covering a wide range of mathematical fields and complexity levels in both English and Chinese. This diversity ensures that the model can generalize across different academic settings, from grade-school basics to advanced competitive mathematics.

The English portion of the dataset is composed of several high-quality sources. The researchers annotated GSM8K and MATH problems with tool-integrated solutions to teach the model how to use programming tools for complex queries. Additionally, the dataset incorporates a subset of MathInstruct and the training set of Lila-OOD, where problems are solved using CoT or PoT. These sources ensure that the model is exposed to a broad spectrum of mathematical disciplines, including algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry.

The Chinese mathematical dataset focuses specifically on K-12 education. It includes problems spanning 76 different sub-topics, such as linear equations, which are annotated in both CoT and tool-integrated reasoning formats. By integrating both English and Chinese data, the model develops a multilingual capacity for mathematical reasoning, allowing it to perform well on benchmarks regardless of the language of the prompt.

# Evaluating DeepSeekMath-Instruct: Performance metrics without vs. with tool use

To understand the impact of SFT, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is evaluated under two distinct settings: one where tool use is disallowed and one where tool use is integrated. This distinction allows researchers to measure the model's "pure" reasoning capability versus its "augmented" capability.

In the setting where tool use is disallowed, the model must rely entirely on step-by-step text reasoning. Under these conditions, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates exceptional performance. On the competition-level MATH dataset, it surpasses all open-source models and the majority of proprietary models, such as Inflection-2 and Gemini Pro, by at least 9% in absolute terms. While it rivals some Chinese proprietary models like GLM-4 and Baichuan-3, it still trails behind the most capable closed-source models, such as GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra.

When the model is permitted to integrate natural language reasoning with program-based tool use, its performance increases significantly. In this augmented setting, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approaches an accuracy of 60% on the MATH benchmark, outperforming all existing open-source models. Furthermore, its performance becomes competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, a model that is ten times larger. This comparison highlights the efficiency of the SFT process and the power of tool integration in overcoming the limitations of a smaller parameter count.
