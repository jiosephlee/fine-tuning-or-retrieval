\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# Can a 7B Model Really Beat the Giants? Meet DeepSeekMath

For a long time, there has been a noticeable gap in mathematical reasoning between closed-source industry giants like GPT-4 and Gemini-Ultra and the available open-source community. While larger models often dominate benchmarks through sheer scale, the team behind DeepSeekMath has demonstrated that strategic data selection and innovative training can allow a much smaller model to punch way above its weight class. DeepSeekMath 7B achieves a score of $51.7\%$ on the competition-level MATH benchmark, bringing it remarkably close to the performance of the world's most powerful proprietary models.

## Scaling Math Pre-training

The foundation of DeepSeekMath is the DeepSeekMath Corpus, a massive dataset of 120 billion math-related tokens sourced from Common Crawl. Instead of relying on a static dataset, the researchers developed an iterative data selection pipeline. They started with a seed corpus (OpenWebMath) and trained a fastText-based classifier to identify similar mathematical content across the web. By repeatedly refining this classifier through human annotation and domain-based filtering, they were able to extract high-quality data at a scale nearly seven times larger than that used by Minerva.

Interestingly, the researchers discovered that while arXiv papers are a staple in many math-centric models, they provided no notable improvements across the benchmarks used in this study. Instead, the real breakthrough came from the model's initialization. DeepSeekMath-Base was initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. The team found that starting with a model already trained on code significantly improves mathematical reasoning capabilities, both when the model is solving problems through natural language and when it is using tools like Python.

## From Instruction Tuning to RL

After pre-training, the model underwent Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using a variety of formats, including chain-of-thought (CoT), program-of-thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning. This produced DeepSeekMath-Instruct, which already outperformed most 7B counterparts and rivaled some 70B models. However, the most significant gains came from the reinforcement learning (RL) phase.

The researchers introduced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a streamlined variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). In standard PPO, a "critic" model is required to estimate a baseline for the advantage function, which consumes a massive amount of memory and computational power because the critic is typically as large as the policy model itself. GRPO eliminates the critic model entirely. Instead, it samples a group of outputs for each question and uses the average reward of that group as the baseline.

To keep the model stable, GRPO regularizes the training by adding the KL divergence between the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$ and a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ directly to the loss function. This efficiency allows the model to be reinforced using only a subset of instruction-tuning data while still achieving substantial gains in both in-domain and out-of-domain mathematical tasks.

## Why It Works and What It Means

The results are striking: DeepSeekMath-RL 7B outperforms Minerva 540B—a model 77 times its size—on several benchmarks. The team analyzed why RL provides such a boost and found that it doesn't necessarily increase the model's fundamental ability to find a correct answer (the Pass@K metric). Instead, RL makes the model's output distribution more robust, meaning the model is more likely to generate the correct answer as its primary response (improving the Maj@K metric).

DeepSeekMath serves as a powerful reminder that model size isn't the only path to intelligence. By prioritizing high-quality, domain-specific data and optimizing the RL process to be more computationally efficient, the gap between open-source and proprietary models continues to shrink.
