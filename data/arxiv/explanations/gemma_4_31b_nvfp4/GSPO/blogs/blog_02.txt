# Taming the MoE Beast: How GSPO Stabilizes Mixture-of-Experts Training

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models have become a cornerstone for scaling large language models, offering a way to increase parameter count without a linear increase in computational cost. However, bringing these models through the rigors of reinforcement learning (RL) is notoriously difficult. Many state-of-the-art RL algorithms, including Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), suffer from severe stability issues when training gigantic MoE models, often leading to catastrophic and irreversible model collapse.

The team behind the latest Qwen3 models identified a fundamental culprit for this instability: **expert-activation volatility**. In this post, we'll explore what this phenomenon is and how a new algorithm called Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) eliminates the need for complex workarounds, allowing MoE models to reach their full potential.

## The Problem: Expert-Activation Volatility

To understand why MoE models struggle with RL, we have to look at how algorithms like GRPO handle "off-policy" data. During training, a large batch of responses is generated by an old policy $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$ and then used to update the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$. To correct for the fact that the data was sampled from a different version of the model, GRPO uses a token-level importance ratio:

$$w_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})}$$

In a dense model, this ratio is relatively stable. But in an MoE model, the "experts" activated for a specific token can change significantly after even a single gradient update. For instance, in the Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base model, researchers found that roughly 10% of the activated experts change for the same sample after one update.

This "volatility" causes the token-level importance ratios to fluctuate drastically. Because these ratios are multiplied by the advantage and accumulated over long sequences, they introduce high-variance noise into the gradients. This noise progressively builds up, eventually triggering a model collapse that cannot be fixed by simply reverting to a checkpoint or tuning hyperparameters.

## The "Band-Aid": Routing Replay

Before GSPO, the primary way to handle this was a strategy called **Routing Replay**. The idea was to cache the experts activated by the old policy $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$ and force the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$ to use those same routing modes when computing the importance ratios. 

While Routing Replay allows MoE models to converge, it comes with a price. It introduces additional memory and communication overhead and, more importantly, it places an artificial constraint on the model by limiting its ability to explore new, potentially better routing paths.

## The Solution: Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO)

GSPO solves this problem by shifting the unit of optimization. The researchers observed a core principle: the unit of the optimization objective should match the unit of the reward. Since rewards are typically granted to an entire sequence, it makes sense to perform off-policy correction at the sequence level rather than the token level.

GSPO replaces the token-level weight with a length-normalized sequence-level importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$:

$$s_{i}(\theta) = \left( \frac{\pi_{\theta} (y_i | x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \log \frac{\pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})} \right)$$

By using the sequence likelihood, GSPO becomes indifferent to which specific expert fired for which token. As long as the model maintains its overall language modeling capability, the sequence likelihood remains stable, even if the internal routing changes.

## Why GSPO is a Game Changer for MoE

The transition to sequence-level optimization provides several critical advantages:

*   **Inherent Stability:** Because it is not sensitive to individual token likelihoods, GSPO fundamentally resolves expert-activation volatility. This means it converges stably without needing the Routing Replay workaround.
*   **Full Capacity Utilization:** Without the constraints of Routing Replay, the MoE model can leverage its full capacity and explore routing modes more freely.
*   **Infrastructure Simplification:** GSPO is more tolerant of precision discrepancies between inference engines (like vLLM) and training engines (like Megatron). This allows the system to use likelihoods directly from the inference engine, avoiding the need for costly recomputations in the training engine.

By stabilizing the training dynamics, GSPO has enabled the continued scaling of RL for the Qwen3 models, delivering superior training efficiency and performance over GRPO across complex reasoning benchmarks. For those training large-scale sparse models, moving the importance ratio from the token to the sequence level may be the key to avoiding the dreaded model collapse.
