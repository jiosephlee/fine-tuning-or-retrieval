\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Does LongRoPE perform extrapolation or interpolation for the first $\hat{n}$ tokens?
Question:
I'm a bit lost on the terminology. The paper says they 'retain the starting tokens without position interpolation'. In the context of RoPE, does 'no interpolation' ($\lambda=1$) imply that these tokens are being treated as extrapolated, or does it simply mean they are using the original indices as seen during the initial 4k pre-training? I'm trying to figure out if these initial tokens are still within the original distribution of the pre-trained model.
Answer:
For the first $\hat{n}$ tokens, LongRoPE is neither extrapolating into unseen territory nor performing interpolation; it is **preserving the original RoPE embeddings** as they were during the initial pre-training.

To understand this, look at the rescale factor $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})$ defined in Equation $\ref{eq:problem}$. The paper specifies that for $n < \hat{n}$, the factor is exactly $1$. This means the rotary angle remains $\frac{n}{\beta^i}$, which is the original formula used during the 4k pre-training. In contrast, for tokens where $n \ge \hat{n}$, a rescale factor $\frac{1}{\lambda_i}$ is applied to "downscale" the indices, which is the essence of positional interpolation.

The term "extrapolated" appearing in "Finding 2" (which suggests initial tokens should be "extrapolated with less interpolation") is used here to contrast the behavior with interpolation. In the context of RoPE, "interpolation" refers to squeezing a larger range of positions into the original training range. "Extrapolation" typically refers to using the original frequencies for positions beyond the training range. However, since $\hat{n}$ is searched within a small range (up to $256$) and the original pre-training window $L$ was $4096$, these first $\hat{n}$ tokens are always well within the original distribution.

Intuitively, the authors are treating the "anchor" tokens at the start of a sequence as special. Because these tokens often receive high attention scores (as noted in the paper's reference to StreamingLLM), any interpolation—which would "crowd" the positions—could lead to significant information loss. By setting $\lambda=1$ for the first $\hat{n}$ tokens, LongRoPE ensures that the model perceives the start of the sequence exactly as it did during its original training, maintaining the high-resolution positional signal for those critical initial tokens.
