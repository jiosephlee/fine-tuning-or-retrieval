\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Why does extending the context window to 2048k degrade performance on short sequences?
Question:
The paper mentions in the 'Shorter context window recovery' section that extending the window to 2048k causes a performance drop for shorter contexts (like 4k) because the positions become 'particularly crowded'. I don't quite understand the geometry of this. If we are only processing 4k tokens, why does the fact that the model *can* handle 2048k tokens make those 4k tokens feel 'crowded' in the embedding space? Does the value of $\lambda_i$ change depending on the actual sequence length during inference?
Answer:
The performance degradation on short sequences when extending the context window to 2048k is a direct consequence of how **Positional Interpolation (PI)** modifies the geometry of the Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) space.

To understand "crowding," recall that RoPE represents token positions as rotations in a multi-dimensional space. Each dimension $i$ has a specific rotation frequency $\theta_i$. For a token at position $n$, the angle is $n\theta_i$. The model distinguishes between two tokens based on the difference between their angles. When we extend the context window by a ratio $s = L'/L$ using linear interpolation, we effectively divide the rotation frequencies by $\lambda$ (where $\lambda \approx s$). 

In your case, extending the window from 4k to 2048k implies an extension ratio $s$ of 512. This means the rotation angles are scaled down by a factor of 512. Geometrically, this "squeezes" the entire angular range that the model originally used for 4k tokens into a tiny fraction (roughly $1/512\text{th}$) of its original size. If two tokens were originally separated by a distinct angle, they are now separated by an angle 512 times smaller. This is what the authors mean by "crowded"; the angular resolution becomes so low that the model can no longer effectively distinguish between closely positioned tokens, leading to a loss of precision in positional encoding.

Even if you are only processing 4k tokens, if you use the rescale factors $\lambda_i$ optimized for 2048k, those 4k tokens are still being projected into this highly compressed, "crowded" angular space. Because the model was pre-trained on the original, high-resolution RoPE, it struggles to interpret these squeezed embeddings.

Regarding your question about $\lambda_i$: yes, the value of $\lambda_i$ does change depending on the sequence length during inference. To solve this exact problem, the authors implement a "Shorter context window recovery" mechanism. They performed a separate evolutionary search to find optimal rescale factors specifically for short lengths (e.g., 4k and 8k) that involve less interpolation. The paper explicitly states that during inference, if the sequence length is less than 8k, the model dynamically updates the RoPE rescale factors to these "short-context" values, thereby expanding the angular distance between tokens and recovering the original performance.
