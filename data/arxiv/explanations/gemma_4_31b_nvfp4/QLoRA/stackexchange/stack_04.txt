\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### How does backpropagation work through frozen 4-bit weights?
Question:
The paper states that QLoRA backpropagates gradients through a frozen, 4-bit quantized pretrained model into the LoRA adapters. Since the weights $\mathbf{W}^{NF4}$ are frozen and quantized (which is a non-differentiable operation), I'm confused about how the gradient $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ is calculated. Does the gradient simply flow through the dequantized $\mathbf{W}^{BF16}$ as if it were a standard constant weight, or is there something special about the backward pass in the CUDA kernels?
Answer:
To understand how backpropagation works in QLoRA, it is first necessary to distinguish between the **storage data type** and the **computation data type**. 

As described in the paper, QLoRA stores the pretrained weights in a 4-bit NormalFloat ($\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}$) format to save memory. However, it does not perform matrix multiplications directly in 4-bit. Instead, whenever a weight tensor is needed for a calculation, the model performs a "double dequantization" step to convert those weights back into BFloat16 ($\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$). 

The forward pass for a linear layer is expressed as:
$$\mathbf{Y}^{\text{BF16}} = \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}} \text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, c_2^{\text{k-bit}}, \mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}) + \mathbf{X}^{\text{BF16}}\mathbf{L}^{\text{BF16}}_1\mathbf{L}^{\text{BF16}}_2$$

In this equation, the term $\text{doubleDequant}(\dots)$ effectively produces $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$. Because the base model weights are **frozen**, they act as constants during the training process. 

Your confusion regarding the non-differentiable nature of quantization is a common one, but it applies to the *quantization* process (mapping a high-precision number to a low-precision bin). In QLoRA, we are not differentiating through the quantization process itself; rather, we are differentiating through the **dequantized** weights. 

During the backward pass, the goal is to calculate the gradient for the LoRA adapters, $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$. According to the chain rule, this requires the gradients to flow through the linear operation. Since the weights $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$ are treated as constants, the gradient simply flows through them as if they were any other fixed 16-bit weight matrix in a standard neural network. The paper explicitly states that the calculation of the derivatives is performed in BFloat16 precision, using the weights converted from storage $\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}$ to computation $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$.

In short: there is nothing "special" happening in the CUDA kernels to bypass the non-differentiability of quantization. Instead, the model avoids the problem entirely by dequantizing the weights to a differentiable format (BFloat16) and treating them as constants. The gradients flow through the $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$ "constant" to reach and update the trainable $\mathbf{L}_i$ adapters.
