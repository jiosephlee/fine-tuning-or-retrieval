\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# Faster, Leaner, Stronger: Comparing OFT to LoRA

Finetuning large text-to-image diffusion models for downstream tasks—such as subject-driven generation or controllable generation—presents a difficult balancing act. On one hand, you need the model to learn new constraints or identities; on the other, you must preserve the high-fidelity generative capabilities of the original pretrained model. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) has become the industry standard for this, but a new approach called Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) suggests that we can achieve better stability and efficiency by focusing on the geometry of the weights.

## The Core Philosophy: Preserving Hyperspherical Energy

The central premise of OFT is that the semantic knowledge of a neural network is encoded in the angles (directions) between neurons rather than their magnitudes. The researchers use a measure called hyperspherical energy, which captures the pairwise relational structure among neurons on a unit hypersphere.

While standard finetuning and LoRA focus on minimizing the Euclidean distance between the pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$ and the finetuned weights $\bm{W}$, OFT argues that this is an insufficient measure of semantic preservation. Instead, OFT employs an orthogonal transformation $\bm{R}$ to update the weights. Because orthogonal transformations preserve the inner products between vectors, the hyperspherical energy of the model remains provably unchanged. 

Mathematically, the forward pass is modified from $\bm{z}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$ to:
$$\bm{z}=\bm{W}^\top\bm{x}=(\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x},~~~\text{s.t.}~\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{R}\bm{R}^\top=\bm{I}$$

By learning a layer-shared orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$, OFT adjusts the coordinate system of the neurons without destroying the intrinsic pretrained structure.

## Parameter Efficiency and Complexity

LoRA reduces the number of trainable parameters by assuming a low-rank structure for the weight update, resulting in a parameter complexity of $\mathcal{O}(d^2+dn)$ for a weight matrix of size $d \times n$ with rank $r'$. OFT takes a different route by exploiting sparsity via a block-diagonal structure for $\bm{R}$.

To keep the number of parameters lean, OFT represents $\bm{R}$ as a block-diagonal matrix with $r$ blocks:
$$\bm{R}=\text{diag}(\bm{R}_1,\bm{R}_2,\cdots,\bm{R}_r)$$
This reduces the parameter complexity to $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r)$. If the blocks are shared across the diagonal, this can be further reduced to $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r^2)$. In practical terms, the paper demonstrates that for a $128 \times 128$ weight matrix, OFT can utilize fewer trainable parameters than LoRA while achieving superior results.

## Faster Convergence and Greater Stability

OFT demonstrates a significant advantage in convergence speed and stability across two primary tasks:

*   **Subject-Driven Generation:** In experiments similar to DreamBooth, OFT and its constrained variant, COFT, proved far more stable than LoRA. While LoRA often struggled to preserve subject identity or suffered from "color bleeding" (e.g., generating yellow fur instead of a yellow shirt), OFT maintained the subject's identity consistently even across a high number of iterations.
*   **Controllable Generation:** When tasked with following control signals like Canny edges or face landmarks, OFT converged significantly faster. For instance, in face landmark tasks, OFT reached a performance level in 8 epochs that took LoRA 20 epochs to achieve.

The authors attribute this speed to the fact that modifying neuron directions is the most effective way to alter visual semantics. Because OFT operates directly on these directions within a smooth hypersphere manifold, it provides a more efficient optimization landscape.

## The Inference Advantage: Zero Overhead

One of the most compelling advantages of OFT for AI engineers is the total absence of inference overhead. While adapter-based methods (like ControlNet) introduce additional computational layers that must be processed during every forward pass, OFT simply transforms the existing weights.

During the inference stage, the learned orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ can be multiplied into the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$ to produce a final weight matrix $\bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$. Consequently, the resulting model has the exact same architecture and inference speed as the original pretrained model.

## Summary: OFT vs. LoRA

| Feature | LoRA | OFT |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanism** | Low-rank additive update | Multiplicative orthogonal transform |
| **Constraint** | Rank constraint $r'$ | Orthogonality $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R} = \bm{I}$ |
| **Preservation** | Implicit Euclidean distance | Provable hyperspherical energy |
| **Convergence** | Standard | Faster (targets neuron directions) |
| **Inference** | Possible overhead (if not merged) | Zero additional overhead |
