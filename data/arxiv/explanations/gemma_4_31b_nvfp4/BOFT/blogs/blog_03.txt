# One Framework, Many Worlds: BOFT's Versatility Across LLMs, Vision, and Diffusion

Adapting foundation models to specific downstream tasks is a central challenge in modern AI. Whether it is a Large Language Model (LLM) like GPT-3 or a text-to-image model like Stable Diffusion, the sheer size of these models makes training from scratch prohibitively expensive. While Parameter-Efficient Finetuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA have become the industry standard, there is always room for more principled approaches that preserve the rich knowledge embedded in pretrained weights.

Enter **Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)**, a novel finetuning framework that combines the stability of orthogonal transformations with the extreme parameter efficiency of butterfly structures.

## From Orthogonal Finetuning to the Butterfly Structure

To understand BOFT, we first have to look at Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). The core idea of OFT is to update a pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$ by multiplying it by a learnable orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$. Because orthogonal matrices preserve the pairwise angles between neurons, OFT is designed to keep the semantic knowledge of the pretrained model intact. However, a dense orthogonal matrix for a layer of size $d$ requires $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters, which is often too many. To solve this, OFT previously used block-diagonal structures, but this limited the model's expressiveness and introduced rigid inductive biases.

BOFT solves this trade-off by treating the generation of a dense orthogonal matrix as an information transmission problem. Drawing inspiration from the Cooley-Tukey fast Fourier transform (FFT) algorithm, BOFT uses a **butterfly factorization**. Instead of one massive dense matrix, BOFT represents the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ as a product of $m$ sparse orthogonal matrices:

$$\bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$$

By using this structure, BOFT can generate a dense orthogonal matrix using only $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ parameters. This allows the model to maintain the expressiveness of a dense transformation while remaining computationally lean.

## Versatility Across Modalities

The true strength of BOFT lies in its versatility. While previous orthogonal methods were primarily tested on specific tasks like controllable text-to-image generation, the authors of the BOFT paper demonstrate that this framework is a generic tool for adaptation across entirely different domains:

*   **Large Language Models (LLMs):** BOFT can efficiently adapt the linguistic capabilities of LLMs to specific downstream natural language processing tasks.
*   **Vision Transformers (ViTs):** The framework proves effective in adapting large-scale vision models to various computer vision tasks.
*   **Diffusion Models:** In text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion, BOFT enables precise control and adaptation without the instability often seen in additive methods like LoRA.

Across these diverse "worlds," BOFT consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, validating its superior generalization ability and parameter efficiency.

## Why BOFT Works: Stability and Bias

Beyond the numbers, BOFT provides several theoretical advantages that explain its success:

**Spectral Stability.** Unlike additive updates, BOFT preserves the spectral norm of the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$. Because the product of an orthogonal matrix and the original weights does not change the largest singular value, the training process remains significantly more stable, which is critical when finetuning massive models.

**Beneficial Inductive Bias.** The butterfly structure is not arbitrary; it is the foundation of many classic linear transforms, such as the Discrete Fourier Transform and the Hadamard Transform. By constraining the finetuning process to this structured subset of the orthogonal group, BOFT introduces an implicit inductive bias that helps prevent overfitting and improves generalizability.

**Zero Inference Latency.** One of the biggest practical advantages of BOFT is that it introduces no overhead during inference. Once the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ is learned, it can be multiplied directly into the pretrained weights. The resulting model has the exact same architecture as the original, meaning there is no need for additional "adapter" layers during deployment.

## Conclusion

BOFT demonstrates that we don't have to choose between the expressiveness of dense matrices and the efficiency of sparse ones. By leveraging butterfly factorization, BOFT provides a unified framework capable of adapting LLMs, vision models, and diffusion models with minimal parameters and maximum stability. As foundation models continue to grow, structured approaches like BOFT will be essential for making these giants flexible and accessible.
