\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

Chapter 7: # Empirical Applications and Results

The theoretical framework of Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) provides a sophisticated mechanism for adapting large foundation models through the use of sparse matrix factorization. However, the true utility of any finetuning paradigm lies in its empirical performance across diverse tasks. The transition from a pretrained foundation model to a specialized downstream model requires a balance between expressivity—the ability of the model to learn new patterns—and regularity—the ability to preserve the vast amount of knowledge already captured during pretraining. BOFT is designed to navigate this trade-off by utilizing a butterfly structure to construct dense orthogonal matrices with significantly fewer parameters than traditional methods.

# Adapting Large Language Models (LLMs)

Large Language Models, such as GPT-3, possess an enormous number of parameters, often reaching into the hundreds of billions. This scale makes full finetuning computationally prohibitive for most researchers. BOFT addresses this challenge by applying its parameter-efficient orthogonal transformations to the linear layers of these models. In the context of natural language processing, the goal is to adapt the model to specific downstream tasks without erasing the general linguistic capabilities acquired during the pretraining phase.

By employing the butterfly factorization, BOFT allows the model to learn a dense orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ that transforms the neurons of the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$. Because BOFT can represent a dense matrix using only $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ parameters, it can maintain a high level of expressivity while keeping the number of trainable parameters low. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that BOFT outperforms current state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning methods by a considerable margin in various natural language tasks, validating its effectiveness as a generic tool for LLM adaptation.

# Finetuning Large Vision Transformers (ViTs)

The versatility of BOFT extends beyond the domain of text to the realm of computer vision, specifically when applied to Large Vision Transformers (ViTs). Adaptation in vision tasks often requires the model to refine its understanding of spatial features and object relationships to suit a specific dataset or task. Applying BOFT to these architectures involves the same principle of multiplying the frozen pretrained weights by a learnable orthogonal matrix composed of butterfly components.

The application of BOFT to ViTs showcases its capacity as a general-purpose finetuning method. By preserving the pairwise angles between neurons through orthogonal transformations, BOFT ensures that the semantic knowledge of the pretrained vision model is largely preserved. The results in computer vision applications mirror those found in language processing, where BOFT exhibits superior parameter-efficiency and generalization ability compared to existing methods. This suggests that the inductive bias introduced by the butterfly structure is beneficial across different modalities of data.

# Controllable Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

The roots of orthogonal finetuning were initially established through Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) in the context of controllable text-to-image diffusion models. The primary objective in these models is to adapt the generative process to produce images that adhere to specific constraints or styles. OFT demonstrated that preserving the relational information of weight matrices—specifically the angles between neurons—leads to more stable training and better generalizability than additive methods.

BOFT subsumes OFT as a special case, meaning it encompasses the original orthogonal finetuning framework while providing a more efficient parameterization. In text-to-image diffusion tasks, BOFT replaces the restrictive block-diagonal structure of OFT with a butterfly structure. This allows the model to achieve a dense orthogonal transformation without the parameter explosion associated with full orthogonal matrices. By doing so, BOFT improves the efficiency of adapting these generative models, proving that the butterfly-based approach is not only effective for discriminative tasks in vision and language but also for complex generative tasks.

# Comparative Analysis: BOFT vs. LoRA and Standard OFT

To understand the advantages of BOFT, it is helpful to compare it with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and the original Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). LoRA achieves parameter efficiency by adding a product of two low-rank matrices to the pretrained weights. In contrast, BOFT uses a multiplicative orthogonal update. One of the most significant advantages of the orthogonal approach is the preservation of the spectral norm. Because $\bm{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix, the product $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ does not change the largest singular value of the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$. This property is believed to greatly benefit training stability and generalization, a feature that is not inherently guaranteed by the additive nature of LoRA.

When compared to standard OFT, the primary difference lies in the structure of the orthogonal matrix. Standard OFT often relies on a block-diagonal structure to remain parameter-efficient. While this reduces the number of parameters, it limits expressivity because dimensions in different blocks are transformed independently, and it cannot approximate classic linear transforms. A full dense orthogonal matrix in OFT would require $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters, which is often too costly. BOFT breaks this dichotomy by using butterfly factorization to produce a dense orthogonal matrix with only $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ parameters. Consequently, BOFT is provably more expressive than block-diagonal OFT with the same block size, allowing it to find a better trade-off between expressivity and regularity.

# Discussion on Inference Latency and Training Overhead

A critical consideration for any finetuning method is the cost associated with its deployment and training. One of the most attractive features of BOFT is that it introduces zero additional inference latency. After the finetuning process is complete, the learned orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ is simply multiplied into the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$. This results in a new weight matrix that can be used in the standard forward pass of the model, meaning the final architecture is identical to the original pretrained model.

However, there is a trade-off during the training phase. Because the final orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ in BOFT is constructed as the product of $m$ separate orthogonal butterfly components $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$, the training runtime overhead is slightly larger than that of standard OFT. In standard OFT, a single block-diagonal matrix is applied; in BOFT, multiple sparse matrices must be multiplied in each iteration. Despite this slight increase in training time, the significant reduction in the number of trainable parameters and the resulting improvements in generalization and stability make BOFT a compelling choice for adapting large-scale foundation models.
