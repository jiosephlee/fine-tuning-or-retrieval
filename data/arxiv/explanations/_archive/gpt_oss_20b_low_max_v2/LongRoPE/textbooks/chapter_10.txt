\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 10: # 10. Ablation Studies & Sensitivity Analyses

Large‑scale language models (LLMs) often rely on subtle architectural tweaks that can dramatically influence performance, especially when those tweaks are designed to extrapolate something as fragile as positional encoding across orders of magnitude in sequence length. In LongRoPE this delicate balance is achieved through a two‑fold non‑uniform interpolation scheme: (i) **dimension‑wise** rescaling of RoPE angles, and (ii) a **token‑position threshold** that protects the earliest tokens from excessive interpolation.  Because both components are intertwined in the evolutionary search, it is crucial to quantify how each contributes to the overall success of the method.  
This chapter dissects LongRoPE into its elementary parts, reports systematic ablations, and explores how the model’s sensitivity varies with respect to key hyper‑parameters.

## 10.1 Dimension‑Only Non‑Uniform Interpolation

The first knob we isolate is the **dimensional non‑uniformity**.  
During evolution we allow every pair of sine–cosine channels \((\theta_i,\theta_{i+1})\) to receive a distinct factor \(\lambda_i\), subject only to the monotonicity constraint \(\lambda_i\leq\lambda_{i+1}\).  In the “dimension‑only” variant, we set the token‑position threshold \(\hat n=0\), i.e., **every** token in the input sequence is subjected to the learned rescaling.

### Experimental Setup
- **Model**: LLaMA2‑7B
- **Evaluation**: Perplexity on PG19 and Proof‑pile at 8k, 16k and 32k context lengths
- **Training**: No fine‑tuning; weights frozen at pre‑training checkpoints
- **Search Horizon**: 40 evolutionary iterations, population size 64

### Results
| Context length | PI ·  | NTK ·  | YaRN ·  | Dimension‑Only |
|----------------|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| 8k             | 16.24| 16.01  | 16.07 | **15.88** |
| 16k            | 17.45| 17.06  | 17.12 | **16.73** |
| 32k            | —    | —      | —      | **18.65** |

The dimension‑only ablation outperforms all baselines up to an eightfold increase in context window (32 k) without any token‐position protection, demonstrating that even a purely frequency-aware interpolation preserves more positional fidelity than conventional PI or NTK.

### Interpretation
Lower dimensions encode high–frequency positional patterns; therefore reducing their rescaling dampens interference caused by crowded positions in long sequences. The monotone increase in \(\lambda_i\) across dimensions mirrors what NTK theory predicts for Fourier series expansions of neural networks: higher frequencies are more sensitive to interpolation and should be preserved.

## 10.2 Token‑Position–Only Non‑Uniform Interpolation

Next we examine how guarding only the initial tokens influences performance while maintaining linear PI over all dimensions (i.e., constant \(\lambda_i=s\)).

### Experimental Setup
Same as above but with a discretised search over \(\hat n \in \{0,2,4,\dots,256\}\), with \(s=4\).

On longer windows (16k/32k), gains persist but plateau after about \(\hat n=8\).  

### Interpretation
Early tokens receive disproportionately large self‑attention scores; preserving their original RoPE prevents them from drifting into crowded regions on subsequent layers, thereby stabilising the entire transformer stack across long horizons.

## 10.3 Combined Dimensional and Token–Position Non‑Uniformity

When both forms of non-uniformity are jointly optimized, the evolutionary algorithm discovers an almost globally optimal configuration for every target context window.

Whereas PI exhibits a perplexity rise above \(30\,000\), underscoring how inadequate linear scaling becomes when approaching Megabyte-scale contexts.

### Relative Contributions  
By ablating one component at a time from the combined configuration we see:

1. **Remove dimensional non-uniformity** → perplexity climbs by ~\(+1\,PPL\).
2. **Remove token-position protection** → perplexity rises by ~\(+0.\!5\,PPL\).
3. **Remove both** → perplexity exceeds \(+2\,PPL\).

Thus dimensional non-uniformity accounts for roughly \(50–60\%\) of overall accuracy gains, while token protection contributes an additional \(20–30\%\), with synergy due to reduced interference between early tokens and high-frequency positional harmonics.

## 10.4 Sensitivity Analysis of Hyper‐Parameters

LongRoPE’s performance hinges on two hyper‐parameters that control the search space:

1. **Upper bound on λ:** \(s\times1{+}0{?}25\)
2. **Granularity for λ steps:** Δλ=0⋅01

We performed a grid sweep on LLaMA2 for an extension ratio \(s=64\times\):

- With Δλ=0⋅005 we noted negligible improvement (<0⋅02 WHR) over Δλ=0⋅01 but doubled computational cost.
- Raising upper bound from \(s\times1{+}0·25\) to \(s\times1{+}0·50\) produced a slight (<5% relative perplexity) gain at extreme lengths (>180k), but caused training failures under finer tunnel constraints due to unseen interpolation ratios.
- For token thresholds α ranging between [0,128], performance was robust beyond α=4; values larger than α=64 brought marginal gains (<3%) but increased variance.

These observations suggest that LongRoPE’s design operates comfortably within modest hyper­parameter ranges; users may safely adopt default settings without extensive tuning.

## 10.5 Interaction with Fine-Tuning

In practice LongRoPE is coupled with a lightweight fine-tuning phase—typically \(400+600=1\,000\) steps—at an intermediate target length (e.g., \(256\,k\)) before extending again to \(2\,048\,k\).  
Ablation experiments reveal that omitting fine-tuning leads to:

- A perplexity surge of about +3 units at mid-lengths (e.g., ≤512 k)
- Significant degradation in recall on long-passkey retrieval tasks (>20% absolute drop)

In contrast, retaining dimension-only or token-only non-uniform interpolations during fine-tuning yields only marginal benefits compared with preserving both components; this again underscores their complementary nature.

## 10 Summary

Through systematic ablations we have demonstrated:

1. The primary source of performance uplift comes from carefully sculpting rotor angles per dimension—aligning closely with NTK intuition—while preserving high-frequency details that would otherwise collapse under aggressive interpolation.
2. Protecting early tokens from rescaling mitigates attention overload at critical positions and further sweetens gains.
3. When these two mechanisms are combined within an evolutionary search that auto-tunes both factors for each desired extension ratio, LongRoPE lifts LLMs’ contextual understanding from handcrafted baselines onto unprecedented scale—over two million tokens—without impairing short-context functionality.
4. Hyperparameters governing λ range and granularity remain largely predictable; practitioners can adopt default settings while retaining optimal speed–accuracy trade-offs.

These insights confirm that each component in LongRoPE is not merely auxiliary but foundational; together they constitute a robust framework that can be transferred or trimmed depending on application constraints or hardware budgets.
