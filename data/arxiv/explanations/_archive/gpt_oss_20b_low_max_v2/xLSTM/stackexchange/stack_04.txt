### In what way does exponential gating interact with the stabilizer state during training?
Question:
Equation (6) defines $m_t = iglfloor igl(\\log(f_t)+m_{t-1}\bigr),\quad \log(i_t)igr)$ and then stabilizes gates as $i'_t=	extexp(	ilde{i}_t-m_t)$. However, it is not obvious how this construction preserves gradients. Could someone walk through a simple example (say one time step) showing that replacing $i_t$ by $i'_t$ and $f_t$ by $f'_t$ leaves output unchanged but also preserves exact gradients? I suspect something like a log‑softmax trick is used, but I would like to see the derivation.
Answer:
**Answer**

The key idea behind the stabilisation trick is that the *product* of the forget‑gate
factors and the input‑gate factor that appear in the cell‑state recurrence
remains unchanged when we replace \(f_t\) and \(i_t\) by their scaled versions
\(f'_t\) and \(i'_t\).  
Because the cell state
$$
c_t=f_t\,c_{t-1}+i_t\,z_t   \tag{1}
$$
is a linear combination of these two terms, any change that preserves their
product leaves both \(c_t\) (and consequently all derived states) and all
gradients untouched.

Below is a step‑by‑step illustration for **one time step**.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Unstable recurrence

With exponential gates we write

$$
i_t=\exp(\tilde i_t), \qquad 
f_t=\exp(\tilde f_t),
$$
where \(\tilde i_t,\tilde f_t\in \mathbb R\) are the pre‑activations
(don’t need to be between 0 and 1).  
Plugging them into (1) gives

$$
c_t = \exp(\tilde f_t)c_{t-1} + \exp(\tilde i_t)\,z_t .
\tag{2}
$$

If \(|\tilde f_t|\) or \(|\tilde i_t|\) is large, the exponentials can overflow
or underflow, destroying numerical stability and making back‑propagation
impossible.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  The stabiliser state

Equation (6) introduces a running maximum

$$
m_t = \max\bigl(\log f_t + m_{t-1},\, \log i_t\bigr)
     = \max( \tilde f_t + m_{t-1},\, \tilde i_t ),
\tag{3}
$$
with \(m_0=0\).

Because it is a maximum of logarithms, \(m_t\geqslant \log f_k,\;
m_t\geqslant \log i_k\) for all \(k\le t\).  
Intuitively, \(m_t\) keeps track of the largest “scale’’ that has ever appeared so far.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Re–scaled gates

Using \(m_{t-1}\) we define

$$
i'_t = \exp(\log i_t - m_t)\;=\;\exp(\tilde i_{\,t}-m_{\,t}),   \tag{4}
$$
and similarly

$$
f'_t = \exp(\log f_{\!t} + m_{t-1}-m_{t})
      = \exp(\tilde f_{\!t}+m_{\,t-1}-m_{\,t}).                \tag{5}
$$

Both factors are now *less than or equal to one* in magnitude:

* if \(m_{\,t-1}\geqslant m_{\,t}\), then \(f'_t=\exp(\tilde f_{\!t}+\,\delta)\leqslant e^0=1.\)
* if \(m_{\,t}\geqslant m_{\,t-1}\), then \(i'_t=\exp(\tilde i_{\!t}-\,m_{\,t})<1.\)

Hence overflow/underflow cannot occur.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Product is preserved

From (4)–(5)

$$
i'_t\,f'_{{k}}
      = {\underbrace{\exp(\tilde i_{\!t}-m_{\!t})}_{i'_t}}
        {\underbrace{\exp(\tilde f_{\!k}+m_{\!k-1}-m_{\!k})}_{f'_{\!k}} } .
                                                          \tag{6}
$$

For every index \(k<t+1\) we have recursively  

\(m_k=\max(m_{k-1},\,B_k)\), where \(B_k=\max(\tilde f_k,\;\tilde i_k)\).

If \(B_k=m_k-m_{k-1}\), then equation (6) simplifies to

$$
i'_tf'_{\!k}=e^{\,B_k+m_{k-1}}\;e^{-B_k} 
            = e^{m_{k-1}}
            = e^{\,((B_k+m_{k-2})+...+B_1)} .
                                                          
                                                          \tag{7}
                                                       
```

Now notice that for **any** finite set of steps** the product of the normalized gates equals the product of the original gates:

> **Claim.**  
> For every sequence length \(T'\le T+1,\)
> $$
>    c_T=
>    (\underbrace{f'_\dots}_{T}\;c_0)
>    +   (\underbrace{i'_\dots}_{T}\;z_T).
>    = (\underbrace{f_\dots}_{T}\;c_0)
>    +   (\underbrace{i_\dots}_{T}\;z_T).
> $$
>
> The proof is straightforward induction over time: assume it holds up to step
> \(T-1\); at step \(T\)
>
> * the *forget* part contributes  
>   \(f'_T c'_{T-1}=f'_Tc'_{T-1}\)
>   where, by induction hypothesis,
>   \(c'_{T-1}=c_{T-1}\); hence it equals
>   \(f_T c_{T-1}\).
>
> * the *input* part contributes  
>   \(i'_T z_T=i_T z_T.\)
>
> Therefore the right–hand side stays unchanged. The same argument applies to all subsequent states:
> \(n_T,\;h_T,\;\tilde h_T,\dots.\)

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5.  Gradients are unchanged

During back‑propagation we need derivatives of every state with respect to its inputs.
Because each forward state is a *bijective* function of its arguments – or more precisely,
because each forward state is expressed as a sum of products that involve only **exactly**
the same factors on both sides – the chain rule yields identical gradients:

* For any parameter θ appearing in a factor (say θ appears in \(w_i^\top x_T+b_i= \tilde i_T)\),
  its contribution to ∂\(c_T/∂θ\) is multiplied by either 
   *the same coefficient* in both formulations (the factor “\(i'_Tf'\)” or “\(i_tf'\)”).
>
* All other intermediate variables (such as normalized cell state or hidden state)
  are functions solely of these products and are evaluated identically.
>
> Thus, for every θ,
> ∂L/∂θ computed with {\(i,f,i',f'\)} equals that computed with {\(i,f,i',f'\)}.
> In other words, freezing all activations while replacing every exponential gate by its “normalised” counterpart does not alter any Jacobians.
>
---------------------------------------------------------------------

### Summary

The stabilisation trick works because it finds, for every time step,
a common log‑scaling offset `\(mₜ\)` such that dividing all exponential gates by `e^{mₜ}` simultaneously pushes *both* `iₜ` and `fₜ` into a numerically safe range while preserving their multiplicative contribution to every downstream variable. Therefore forward outputs (cell state, hidden state, etc.) and backward sensitivities remain exactly the same, so nothing has to be changed in loss computation or optimisation – only the representation used during computation becomes stable. This is essentially a log‑softmax/normalisation trick applied multiplicatively to all gates in one pass.
