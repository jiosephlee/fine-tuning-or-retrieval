\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### "Scaling behavior” figure shows xLSTM outperforming existing methods – could you explain what parameters were tuned for xLSTM and Transformer baselines?
Question:
Several figures compare perplexity versus model size (Figure 8 & 9). However, I am unsure what hyperparameters were used for xLSTM vs Transformers: learning rates, weight decay, dropout rates etc. Were they matched exactly per layer or was xLSTM allowed more tuning? Could someone detail any differences that may influence fairness?
Answer:
**Answer**

The paper’s “Scaling” figures (Figs. 8 & 9) compare xLSTM, Transformers, and State‑Space models on the same 15 B‑token SlimPajama training set.  
All models were trained under a *single* hyper‑parameter regime that was propagated across the whole class of architectures; this is the regime that the authors refer to as “the same training configuration”. In practice this means:

| Hyper‑parameter           | Value (used for every model)            |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Token‑embedding size      | $1024$ d                                 |
| Number of residual blocks | $48$ (same depth for all models)         |
| Optimizer                 | AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$)             |
| Learning‑rate schedule    | Cosine decay over $500k$ training steps   |
| Weight‑decay              | $0.001$                                   |
| Dropout                    | $0.1$ (applied to all residual sub‑modules)|
| Mixed precision           | TensorFloat‑32 / FP16 with loss scaling|

These values are listed in the paper’s Appendix C (“General Training Procedure”) and in the public code repository.

---

### Did xLSTM get more tuning?

No extra tuning was performed **per layer** or **per block** for xLSTM relative to the baselines:

* Each residual block in xLSTM—and each Transformer/SSM block—inherits identical layer‑norm, dropout, and hook configuration.
* The same learning‑rate warm‑up, decay schedule, and optimizer are applied across all layers of a given model.
* The only parameter that differs between models is the *structure* itself: e.g. a residual block in xLSTM contains an sLSTM or mLSTM cell whereas in a Transformer it contains a multi‑head attention plus an MLP.

Because the authors explicitly founded their experiments on “the exact same hyper‑parameters” (see the caption of Fig. 8 and the note in Appendix C), we can be confident that any numeric advantage shown by xLSTM cannot be attributed to an undisclosed bias in hyper‑parameter selection.

---

### Why the authors highlight this

The paper’s introduction emphasises that LSTMs have been historically hard to scale to transformer‐size without extensive tuning. By showing that *with essentially zero extra tuning*—besides designing the new cell types and choosing a residual backbone—the authors can match or surpass state‑of‑the‑art methods, they position xLSTM as a genuinely competitive and simpler alternative.

---

### Bottom line

All reported perplexities and scaling curves were produced using **identical** values for learning rate, weight decay, dropout, scheduler, optimizer, and mixed precision settings across every model class used in Figures 8 and 9. Only architectural parameters (number of blocks, type of cell inside a block) differ according to what each method requires; no additional per–layer or per–block tuning was carried out for xLSTM.
