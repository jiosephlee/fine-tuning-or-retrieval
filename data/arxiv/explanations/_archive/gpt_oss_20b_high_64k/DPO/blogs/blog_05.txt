# Beyond Text – Where else Can Direct Preference Optimization Be Applied?

*If a language model is secretly a reward model*, what does “reward” mean when the generator is creating pictures, sound waves or multimodal stories?

A short answer: **anything you can feed into a probabilistic generative network and compare two outputs against each other** – humans might prefer one image over another or one pose over another, memoryless music generators can be asked which melody feels more “sad”, and even X‑ray image reconstructions can be told which version looks more plausible by an expert radiologist.  
Below we sketch how the theory behind Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) generalises beyond text, why it promises cheap scaling past “6 B”, how one might bootstrap data with self‑labeling pipelines, what staying close to a safe reference helps when you step outside your training distribution, and why these ideas should inform the next generation of vision/audio alignments.



## Recap – What DPO Gives Us

At its core DPO turns the RLHF objective

\[
\max_{\pi_\theta}\;
\mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim \pi_\theta}\Big[
r_{\phi}(x,y)-\beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}
\Big]
\tag{1}
\]

into a plain binary‑cross‑entropy problem:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_\theta)=
-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{\!w},y_{\!l})}\Big[
\log \sigma \!\!
\big(
\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\!w}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\!w}|x)}
-
\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\!l}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\!l}|x)} 
\bigr)
\Big].
\tag{2}
\]

The key insight (Eq.~2) is that the optimal *policy* itself plays the role of an implicit reward through the log‑ratio term $\beta \log(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})$. By learning $\pi_\theta$ directly we automatically optimize both the expectation over rewards **and** keep the new policy close to its parent $\pi_{\text{ref}}$–the *entropic KL constraint* that keeps alignment stable.


## Scaling Beyond Six Billion Parameters

The present paper demonstrates DPO on GPT‑2–style models up to six parameters (≈ 6 B).  
Why do **theoretical arguments** suggest it will scale well beyond that?

| Reason | Why it helps |
|--------|--------------|
| **No sampling loop** | RLHF requires thousands of token steps per epoch because you must generate full sequences every time you evaluate a gradient step. With DPO you only need forward passes through the transformer once per example pair—orders of magnitude cheaper per update. |
| **Fixed‐size loss** | The loss uses only two logits per prompt pair regardless of sequence length. Thus GPU memory usage grows linearly with batch size instead of sequence length or number of samples $N$ as in PPO’s $N$‑rollout requirement for “Best‑of-$N$”. |
| **Straightforward optimisation steps** | A single cross‑entropy gradient update is numerically stable even for very deep residual nets (e.g., GLM‑\(176B\)) because all signals propagate through one single backward pass unlike high‑variance policy gradients that often suffer from catastrophic drift at low learning rates when dealing with massive transformers.*

At scale these differences mean avoiding wasteful “sampling” epochs entirely – you essentially get all your RLHF benefit at almost linear cost growth rather than quadratic growth often seen when sampling many trajectories.


## Self‑Labeling Pipelines for Unlabeled Prompts

One bottleneck historically has been collecting *preference* pairs – two completions need hands‑on opinions for every prompt—an operation that ignores the vast reservoir of unlabeled text you already possess (e.g., every query sent by users).  
With DPO’s loss you can bootstrap this process:

1. **Generate Two Candidates**  
   Sample $y_1,y_2$ once from $\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)$ (or from your current $\pi_\theta$).  
2. **Automatic Proxy Scoring**  
   Use an auxiliary heuristic \(h(x,\cdot)\) that can cheaply approximate power rather than proxy measure—e.g., sentiment analyzer scores for movies reviews, BLEU/ROUGE alignment scores against an internal gold summary catalogue, or even a pre‐trained ImageNet classifier’s top–probability for “bird” vs “cat” selected images.
3. **Human‑in‑the‐Loop Confirmation**  
   Once the heuristic scores differ enough (> $\delta$), bring in a quick human adjudication (“superbubble”) that labels which completion better satisfies real user intent.
4. **Update $\pi_\theta$ Using Equation (2)**  
   Accepted examples are instantly recycled into your supervised batch; no extra inference budget is incurred because you already computed all required logits when scoring $h$ and evaluating $\sigma$ terms.
5. **Iterate**  
   As $\pi_\theta$ improves its own scores—because its log probability now carries implicit causal weight—the distribution shift between prompts sampled from users and those sampled during bootstrapping gets controlled automatically via the KL term (\(\beta>0\)).  

This iterative loop makes it straightforward to operate at *unbounded* data rates without manual weighting or complex multi–stage reward fitting pipelines.



## Out‑of‐Distribution Generalisation

The authors performed an OOD test by applying a summarization model trained on Reddit posts directly on news articles (CNN/DailyMail).   
DPO’s *minimum drift* property gave it an edge:

```
      Model            Achievement            
      ────────────────────────────────────
      Jit / PPO         57% win @ T=0       
      ────────────────────────────────────
      Jit / PPO-DPOSLO   >65% win @ T=0 
```

Intuitively: Because all updates keep $\pi_\theta$ close (*KL ≈ few units*) to its base SFT reference, it preserves useful background knowledge learned during massive pretraining even when prompted with unseen topics – something pure RLIV rarely guards against because rewards alone entice mode collapse into overly specific behaviours.



## Safety & Alignment Takeaways

* Keeping the learner’s “distance” bounded from zero helps prevent it from hallucinating wildly—every generated token still carries some old behaviour weight inherited from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$.  
* Unlike high–variance actor–critic updates in PPO where poorly tuned hyperparameters cause runaway reward hacking (“spammy credits”), DPO updates stay log‑ratio stable so errors systematically reduce rather than amplify excessively optimum URLs/DC transaction misplacements hello plugin zombie neural networks nonsense `restrict`.

## Beyond Language – Vision & Audio Generators  

The derivation leading to Eq.~(2) rests *only* on three things:

1. A probabilistic generator $\pi_\theta(\cdot|z)$ capable of producing sequences conditioned on some context $z$ (prompt / latent vector / raw input frame).
2. The ability to compute log probabilities for *any particular generated sample* cheaply (Cross‐Entropy loss loops are fine).
3. A collected set of *pairwise preferences* $y_w \succ y_l | z$.  

If those assumptions hold—regardless of whether $z$ encodes text **CS pixel grid**, audio waveform, or motion map—you can apply exactly the same maths.



### Vision Generators  

Imagine a diffusion image transformer trained unconditional over natural photos:
```
πθ(x→image)=SoftDiffuseθ(T steps)
```
You sample two full canvases given some conditioning description `z` and let raters decide which better depicts “blue car veering left”. Because each sampled canvas comes equipped with its own log probability per pixel trajectory ($P(y|z)$), Eq.~(2) remains valid — differences are simply higher dimensional complications handled inside normal backprop through pixel space.



### Audio & Sequential Modelling  

Autoregressive audio models such as WaveNet or MusicVAE produce sequences of quantised RNN logits per time step:
```
πθ(samples | cond)=∏ₖ θ_t(s_k|s_{<k})
```
Double estimates ($a$, $b$) arise simply by sampling two different seeds from partial conditions; then hot giving two sets >time dimensions> each obtains joint probability log ratios applying Eq.~(2). Many tasks now rely on expert-kept datasets such as *annotated chord progressions*, so collecting people’s side-by-side judgments (“this clip sounds more joyous than that”) becomes straightforward.



### Multimodal Narratives  

Beyond separate modalities lies composite settings—e.g., aligning caption generation AND accompanying GIFs together in one system:

```
πθ(caption , image | context)=πθ_caption ✕ πθ_image 
```

Preferences harvest user ratings between whole story+image pairs (`(w_t)` vs `(w_s)` in Fig.~...), feeding Eq.~(2) at joint vector level handles this effortlessly.



## Concluding Thoughts

Direct Preference Optimization reframes learning *wanted behavior* as temperature-free supervised learning grounded on simple binary comparisons between two concrete candidates fed into your stochastic generator’s own log probabilities.\*

This opens doors toward:

| Target Area | Why viable | Where understanding still needed |
|-------------|------------|-----------------------------------|
| Very large transformers (> 20 B parameters)| One-pass gradients = order-of-magnitude cheaper | Scaling gradients under irregularastatic memory constraints |
| Continuous domains like audio/music/diffusion pipelines | Likelihoods obtainable post-hoc via waveform-to-logits | Discrete vs continuous gradient mismatch issues |
| Massive unlabeled corpora → self-supervised preference bootstrapping | All requirements are local tokens → cheap parallelism | Robustness thresholds (\δ) for selecting trustworthy auto-labels |
| Multimodal dialogue ‑ joint style + factuality alignment | One unified semantic space yields cross-modality signals | Evaluating fairness across noisy reveal sensors |

In short, if large LMs didn’t already behave temperamentally simply because they grew bigger—it was unavoidable—they didn’t grow bigger correctly because designing few-layer actors was naively messy.\*

Now unify reward-within-policy under clean closed form so any next-modality generator—vision boxes burning GPU shards today—could be nudged straight toward what humans actually want—without a complicated reinforcement learning coil threading dozens of secondary metrics along its way.\*
