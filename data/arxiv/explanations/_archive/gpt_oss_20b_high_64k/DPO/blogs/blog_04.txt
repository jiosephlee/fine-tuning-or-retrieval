# From Movie Reviews to News Articles – Real‑World Experiments That Show DPO Works

In recent work researchers have shown how **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** lets us train large language models directly from human preference data—without ever fitting an explicit reward function or running an RL algorithm.  
Here we walk through three realistic experiments reported in the paper that put this idea through its paces:

| Experiment | Dataset | Ground‐truth / proxy | Main finding |
|------------|---------|----------------------|--------------|
| Sentiment control | IMDb movie review snippets | Sentiment classifier scores | Learns stronger positive language while staying closer to its reference policy |
| Summarisation | Reddit TL;DR | GPT‑4 “win‐rate” against official TL;DR summaries | Outperforms PPO‐based RLHF even without extensive hyperparameter tuning |
| Single–turn dialogue | Anthropic Helpful & Harmless | GPT‑4 “win‐rate” versus heldout human choices | Comparable or stronger than costly “best of N” baselines |

Let’s dive into each one and see how DPO stacks up against conventional pipelines.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1&nbsp;&nbsp;Controlled Sentiment Generation  
### What’s being tuned?
The goal is very modest yet illustrative: **generate sentences following a movie review prefix that feel positively biased** (“I loved …”). Offstream users would mainly care whether tenants can steer tone reliably while keeping overall style sensible—a perfect hedging task for alignment research.



### Dataset & Preference construction  
1️⃣ **Prompt corpus:** Prefixes extracted from IMDb reviews (`train` split).  
2️⃣ **Ground truth:** An off–the–shelf XGBoost sentiment classifier scores candidates (`p(positive | x,y)`).
3️⃣ **Preference pairs:** For every prefix three responses are produced by the reference LM (`π_ref`, which starts as an SFC‑fine tuned GPT‑2-large).  
   A pair `(y_w ≻ y_l)` is labeled whenever `classifier(x,y_w) > classifier(x,y_l)`


### Who’s being compared?
| Method            | Description                                                                 |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **SFT only**     | Pure supervised fine–tuning on the review corpus                           |
| **Preferred FT** | Supervised training exclusively on the `y_w` responses                    |
| **Unlikelihood** | Binary cross entropy loss applied directly (+ optional weight α          |
| **PPO**, **PPO-GT** | Classical RLHF – fit a reward model then perform Proximal Policy Optimisation |
| **DPO**          | Direct pref­er­ence optimisation – doesn’t fit an explicit reward          |

All algorithms receive exactly the same preference set.



### How do we judge worthiness?
Two complementary signals are profiled during training:
1️⃣ **Reward achieved:** The true sentiment score calculated via our classifier  
2️⃣ **KL divergence:** The average log dissimilarity between π_current(· ‖ π_ref)



### Result snapshot  
For several target KL levels `{3,6,9,12}` (`β∈{0…}` mapped appropriately), every policy was run ten times until convergence. The plotted reward–KL frontiers tell an unambiguous story:



> **DPO consistently reaches far superior frontiers:** With its tight coupling between policy update and implicit reward signal it simultaneously boosts positive sentiment scores while keeping divergence low—an outcome unattainable even when “golden’’ rewards are supplied to PPO.

## 2&nbsp;&nbsp;Summarisation on Reddit TL;DR  
### Setting up reality   
The TL;DR competition requires turning forum posts into short summaries—a classic NLP benchmark where standard metrics notoriously undercorrelate with human judgment.



#### Data preparation  

* **Source corpus** – Reddit posts (`train`) paired with short “TL;DR” annotations (`dev`).   
* **Preference set** comes straight from prior work (Stiennon et al.) where crowd workers chose favourite answers among several samples produced by another strong LM.



#### Models run overfeed  

| Algorithm | How it works                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------|
| Zero‑shot GptJ      | Prompted once per input                   |
| Two‑shot Pythia     | Prompted twice per input                  |
| **SFT baseline**       | Fine–tuned GPT-J on judged summaries     |
| **Preferred FT**       | Supervised learner trained only on chosen completions |
| Unlikelihood (α=any)   | Direct likelihood differential            |
| PPO                   | Reward learning + policy optimisation     |
| **Best of N (128)**  – Sample N=128 outputs from reference policy and pick highest-scoring one under learned reward |

All models share one common ground truth score surface estimated by our learned preference model.



#### Calibration & gpt‑4 evaluation  

A single prompt plugs every generated completion into GPT‑4 (“Which answer better summarizes?”). Each completion pair is then scored (>50 % win → correct side). Temperatures varied‐over `{0,…1}`.



#### Key observations  

```
Temperature spectrum :
   • High temperature → diverse outputs → weaker matches at ∅≥0⅔ .
   • Low temperature → deterministic outputs → consistent wins up until ∅≈50 °C .
```

Actual figures median across seeds were：

> *DPO:* ≈61 % argument-winning probability at temperature = 0 versus everything else  
> *PPO:* ≈57 % winning probability at its optimum temp = 0  
> *Best of 128:* slightly lower peaks (<60 %) even when greedily scoring resides  


Stepwise conclusions:

1️⃣ Even without hand–tuned β-values our surprises come straight out of principle—a light hyperityzy requirement clarifies practical promise.\n2️⃣ Temperature invariance shines dramatically—for popular inference ranges practitioners differ rarely regret rapid decisions.\n3️⃣ When matched against “perfect’’ offline selection (`best-of-N`) every seed’s mean wins never eclipse what end users sequentially experience—demonstrating recoverability without explosive computation.

## 3&nbsp;&nbsp;Single–Turn Dialogue – Anthropic Helpful & Harmless  

Unlike generation tasks governed largely by inference behaviour alone here alignment sophistication matters most.



### Data bits  

* Mid-size conversation logs (`train`) paired only with two assistant completions per user turn (`happy/vs light`; size exposed ≈170k pairs).  
* No ready-made SFT routine exists —so building one became necessary first.



### System buildup  

```text
template foundation: Generic Pythia → Preferred−FT → Storage →
Detailed substrate = Reference Policy π_ref
```

The ``Preferred−FTC`` objective is exactly your classical supervised loss applied solely to labelled winners—an inexpensive way to bring model distribution squarely into range before launching preference optimisation.


### Competing approaches considered  

Same lineup nad interleaved as above + one potent RL variant trained underneath <19× cost> —PPO turned around same prediction surfaces yet dominated under hardware expense embedding limits.


### Evaluation voice  

Again invoked GPU‐backed GPT ⁴ deaths assessing each candidate against heldout preferred completions recorded during training split—with identical prompt composition between runs (
“Which assistant response best addresses this query?”).


### Resultian summary  

```
Top performers #model−temp;
   • Preferred−FTC alone ≤70 % wins vs base reference (~60 %).
   • RR ← BOP128 Prev_ours unmatched soon albeit limited compute weight (~70 %).
   • Our freshly optimised DP O scores exceed BOP128
     massively translating roughly similar level outcomes while shedding sampling cost (§30×).
```

Notably this thunder blew away a publicly released RLHF agent attempting exactly this pipeline—the agent failed spectacularly under fine-tuned hyperconditions akin input tuning which served proof-of-concept elegantly insufficient overhead cost wise.

## Takeaway Messages


1️⃣ **Doppelganger transfer capacity:** Modeling preferences through an implicit policy directly admits straightforward gradient updates—no intermediate score net required.\n2️⃣ Under modest hyperparameters it consistently beats or ties fully-fledged actor–critic systems.\n3️⃣ When paired with reliable automated predictors (Lang fundamentals + neural raters), experiments evaluate unbiased radiance efficiently —the principle continuous bootstrap outweighs black-box uncertainty.\n\nThese three practical trials together sketch how Direct Preference Optimization allows you—data scientist or engineer—to plant purposeful alignment trees across many generative frontends without spending memory allowances underlying reinforcement loops.
