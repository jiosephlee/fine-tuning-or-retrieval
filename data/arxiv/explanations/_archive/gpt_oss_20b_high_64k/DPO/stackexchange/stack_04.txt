#### Why ML philosophy makes classic actor–critic fragile


1\. **High‑variance return estimates**  

Reinforcement learning samples discrete completions from $\displaystyle (\boldsymbol y|\mathbf x)$;
estimating $r_\phi(x,\mathbf y)$ after every sample gives huge Monte Carlo noise.
Actor–critic methods latch onto this noise,
constantly mis­profiling advantages—especially when coupled with large language models whose output space grows exponentially with prompt length.
When the stance flips suddenly,
PPO’s clipping step might either do nothing (gradient vanished) or take an overly greedy step toward a single high‑reward regime (*mode collapse*).
