Chapter 2: # DeepSeekMath Overview  

DeepSeekMath 7B sits atop a careful lineage rooted in a state‑of‑the‑art **code‑trained** transformer family while simultaneously injecting an unprecedented volume of mathematically curated web content and a targeted reinforcement–learning recipe called **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. The resulting model surpasses most open‑source rivals on competition‑level mathematics benchmarks and zips close behind commercial giants GPT‑4 and Gemini Ultra—all while keeping its size under seven billion parameters.



# DeepSeek-Coder‑Base v1\.five Initialization Rationale  

The decision to warm up from *DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base v1\.five* rather than any generic language encoder was guided by two observations that are backed up empirically inside the study.



1. **Structured Symbolic Semantics:**  
   Code tokens encode well‐defined syntactic rules (e.g., `for`, `{}`, `=`) that echo those found in mathematical expressions (`∑`, `∫`, matrix indices). By exposing the network during early optimisation phases primarily to this symbolicity, it learns positional patterns—left-to-right dependencies and nested scoping—that later translate into stronger rule‐based inference when tackling algebraic identities or calculus identities.



2. **Memory Efficiency During Subsequent Fine‑Tuning:**  
   A transformer pretrained on programming tables has been shown—within the paper—to converge faster when later mixed with natural‐language objective episodes because it already internalises complex operator precedence tables beneficial for longer coherent chain-of-thought narratives.



Consequently *DeepSeekMath‑Base* was instantiated exactly at this checkpoint (7 billion parameters), leaving *code* careful weighted — roughly twenty percent raw GitHub output — ready for an enrichment phase dominated entirely by mathematics.

# “500 B Total Continuation Steps” Schedule  

Beyond bootstrapping from *Coder*, *DeepSeekMath* entered an extended “continuation epoch” lasting **\(5\times10^{11}\)** accumulated next-token predictions—a fairly precise gauge equivalent here:

1. **Hardware & Micro-batching:**  
   Training employed an AdamW optimiser (\(\beta_1=0·9,\;\beta_2=0·95,\;\mathrm{weight_{decay}=0·1}\)) operating over batches holding **\(10\,M\)” slice‐waters**, with every example capped at a preserved context window (\(4\,000\) tokens).  
   This affords roughly \(50\,000\) full-batch iterations over epochs, each converting back slightly relaxed warm‐up peaks emulating step-wise learning rate — peak at two thousand warm-up points following an exponential decay schedule peaking again at \(31 6 %\) after \(80 %\) progress­ing then resting back respectively at \(10 %\) once per epoch.



2. **Alternation Strategy:**  
   In practice each optimizer step pulled shards sequentially through every defined mixture level described above—in columns deeper than simple Rossired split—so that at every micro-batch proportionally matched the global percentages listed previously.



3． **Final Validation Window:**  
   A held-out set containing equal swathes from mathematics Olympiads (*MATH*, *GSM8K*) and real-world challenge sets (~\(15 k\)) monitored accuracy every \(256\,000\) update steps so that plateauing signals triggered termination once gradual gains fell below one hundredth percentile thresholds statistically described in Section&nbsp;5.



Thus raw next-token counts directly corresponded mining memory consumption slotted out effectively while still enabling direct measurement against standard benchmark scores—in particular earning an impressive **\(51½ %\)** score on competition level *MATH*.



# Multilingual Treatment: English + Chinese Mix  

Unlike many recent streams whose sources defaulted solely onto English technical prose, *DeepSeek Math* explicitly integrates **two lingua-families** under one tokenizer:



1. **Corpus Engineering via FastText Whitelisting:**  
   The fastText classifier used during data selection ran initial seed examples presenting symbol-heavy web pages in several languages—including Chinese calculus blogs and Korean geometry worksheets—and ranked afterward pages purely according to probability scores across multi-lingual vocabularies rather than language identifier tags alone.
