# Mining Mathematics From The Web  

In large‑language‑model development there is one special class of knowledge – mathematics – where depth of reasoning rarely equals breadth of facts. GPT‑4 or Gemini Ultra can answer many arithmetic questions because they **see** plenty of them during pre‑training, but open‑source models lag far behind even while sized several hundred times larger in memory and compute consumption than their closed‑source counterparts.  

A team at DeepSeek addressed this imbalance by turning one of the biggest public datasets ever built – **Common Crawl** – into a clean, multimodal “math corpus” containing **120 billion tokens** across dozens of languages (English & Chinese dominate). Their approach combined a simple supervised learner (fastText) with an iterative data‑engineering spiral that keeps pulling new pages while shedding noise and benchmark contamination until every page left would only add value.


---

## Why pulling maths out of Common Crawl matters

* **Scale:** Existing open‑source maths corpora rarely exceed two dozen billions of tokens – far smaller than many general‑purpose corpora vetting an entire public web index each day.
* **Quality:** Simply taking every ``math'' page harms reasoning ability – you end up memorising huge PEG sub‐sets rather than learning how *to* reason through proofs or genuine problem solving traits needed for synthetic evaluation sets such as *GSM8K*, *MATH*, *CMATH*.
* **Renewability:** Keyword‑based extraction (e.g., “math”) often misses higher level knowledge such as geometry insights buried under vernacular scripting sites or forum threads hidden behind “calculus” titles.


DeepSeek built an extractable pipeline that continually iterates over three core concerns:
1) find mathematically relevant fragments;
2) prune harmful duplications;
3) guarantee no overlap with the evaluation benches themselves.


---

## The pipeline – step by step

Below is an overview you can implement yourself if you wish – the process stayed around five days' worth of compute on commodity GPU hardware (~108 TB raw ingested).

| Step | Action | Tool / Model | Notes |
|------|--------|--------------|-------|
| **1** | Create an initial seed set | `OpenWebMath` (≈ 13 billion page tokens) | A hand–curated set covering many textbook‐style explanations |
| **2** | Train a fastText keyword detector | `fasttext.train_supervised()` | *Parameters:* size = 256 dimensional vectors, LR = 0.1, n‑gram up to 3 words, minimum word freq = 3 over 3 epochs |
| **3** | Deduplicate raw crawl | URL–based+near duplicate hash hashing | Reduces raw CC down from >40 billion URLs → ≈ 40 billion HTML files |
| **4** | Retrieve docs via classifier | scoreᵢ ← fasttext.predict(prob).top() | Choose top scoring set until max token budget reached (`∼40 billion` first round). |
| **5** | Evaluate retained pages scaling choices | Pre‑train small debug models on `(40  → 80 → 120 → 160)` token subsets | Keeps tuning very quickly – drop earlier if growth plateaus |
| **6** | Spot domains missed because seeds weren’t diverse | Identify domains where `>10%` footprints were recovered during Step‑4 | Typical winners show reddit/js sidebars linked to long discussion threads |
| **7** | Manually annotate URLs rich in math content (e.g., `/questions` subfolders) | Add each annotated page back into new seed set | Expands variety without sacrificing curation quality |
| **8** | Retrain fastText with expanded seeds → Step‑4 again *for a different chunk* of CC data | Repeat until plateau (~four iterations) | Stop when next round would increase coverage by <2%, i.e., already seen ​≥ 98% |

### Decontaminating against eval benchmarks

Common practice now requires scrubbing any text fragment that could leak solutions into your training set—especially since large training corpora unwittingly contain entire exam question transcripts!

* Find every substring whose length ≥10 characters appears verbatim in any problem set used later (`GSM8K`, `MATH`, `CMATH`, `AGIEval`);
* Remove every whole web snippet containing such match;
* For shorter fragments (<10 characters), search only against exact `≥ 3` trigrams—this avoids accidentally excising legitimate context around names or symbols that happen to appear close together only by chance.

This strict decontamination guarantees no hidden memorized answers float through transformer weights during downstream inference.



---

## What does the finished corpus look like?

* **Size:** Around ​35 million unique webpages → ≈​120 billion scientific/mathematical tokens  
* **Languages:** English and Chinese dominate; additional slivers exist from Spanish and French hosts thanks to CC’s global footprint  
* **Multilingual richness:** Because CC is served globally it naturally contains statements written in dozens of scripts – all fed through fastText’s character n‑grams indiscriminately  
* **Production sanity:** Removing duplicate near‐identical pages saves GPU memory during pre‑training—pre‑trained models experience fewer catastrophic interference effects when later finetuned


---


## Why does it work?

[Benchmarks](#evaluation-results — see below) show deep advantage versus competing corpora (`MathematicsPile`, `OpenWebMath`, `Proof-Pile–2`). In short:

1. **Coverage matters more than size alone** – DeepSeek’s train range was exhausted latehandly while others spent taking repeated passes through fewer items capped at <30 billion text units;
2. **Fine voice tuning empyricists far better reflect human problem sets** – our seed Vectors were conceptually close enough so that hierarchical grouping during later SFT stages produced many rules backed up posteriorly from sparsely observed datapoints;
3.Aligns directly with inference tasks where solutions must walk ∼5–12 logical steps—how best done is efficient vocabulary spotting + cluster searching across discoverable yet distinct actinage binding cues.
