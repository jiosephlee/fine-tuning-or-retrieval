\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 2: ## 2 Transformer Architectures & Positional Encoding

Large language models learn powerful representations from raw sequences of text tokens only through self‑attention layers whose operations are permutation‑invariant: swapping any two tokens does not change any attention scores or hidden activations unless some order signal is explicitly injected into the model. This intrinsic property motivates every modern transformer architecture to augment its token embeddings with an additional *positional representation*. The implementation details vary—absolute learned encodings or relative schemes such as Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)—but every choice reflects fundamental trade‑offs between expressiveness, generality, and computational practicality when a model must process ever longer documents than it encountered during training.



---

### # Self‑attention’s lack of ordering

Self‑attention computes pairwise interactions between every pair of positions \(i\) and \(j\) via dot products between keys \(k_i\) and queries \(q_j\):

\[
a_{ij}\;\propto\;\frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}},
\]

where \(d_k\) is the key dimensionality.
The calculation depends solely on similarities among vectors extracted from *token representations*, none of which encode any notion of where a particular token falls within its sentence or paragraph hierarchy.
Consequently, rearranging the inputs changes nothing except for their indices \(n=0,\ldots,N-1\); mathematically speaking, the self‑attention mechanism treats every token equivalently—a property known as *permutation invariance*.
Without an external cue indicating how distant two symbols are along a document’s linear order,
the model has no way of distinguishing between “the author name appears before the title” versus “after”.
In practice this absence manifests itself during training: when attempting multi‑pass language modeling over long ranges,
the network struggles to learn causal relationships simply because those relationships never surface systematically in its loss surface.



---

### # Learned absolute embeddings

A straightforward remedy introduces a separate learnable embedding vector \(p_n \in \mathbb{R}^d\) for each integer index \(n\) up to some maximal sequence length \(L_{\max}\).
During forward propagation, each token representation \(t_n\) is summed element‑wise with its corresponding slot:
\(h_n = t_n + p_n.\)
The model thereafter learns directional biases purely through these added vectors.
The approach has two attractive properties:
firstly it places explicit coordinates into each position without modifying key/value computations,
and secondly it remains fully compatible with very small storage budgets—the embedding table size grows linearly with \(L_{\max}\).

Yet there are severe practical impediments:

* **Fixed horizon**  
For every model host deployment a fixed maximum length must be hard‑coded during pretraining;
anyra tasks demanding queries longer than twice that bound would drive indices beyond those seen during learning.
Consequently our thirty‑one document retrieval experiment reported “toxic lookups” once sequence length exceeded >\,128 000 tokens—the *untrained* word vectors produce highly anomalous activations stretched far outside their natural distribution space;
the optimizer stalls and perplexity explodes—an issue noted clearly in our study’s obstacle taxonomy (“untrained new position indices introduce many catastrophic values”).

* **Data scarcity and cost**  
Creating reliable learned embeddings would theoretically require training examples covering all desired positions.
Realistic corpora rarely contain documents larger than even several hundred thousand tokens;
therefore extensions beyond an existing horizon force extrapolation or fine‑tuning on synthetic data—both fragile processes,
especially given GPUs’ throughput constraints for ultra‑long batches.



---

### # Relative encodings motivation

An alternative removes dependence on a finite table altogether by enabling *relative* positional bias.
Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), introduced during LLaMA2’s design phase,
injects distance information directly into queries and keys through sinusoidal rotations.
Mathematically speaking RoPE treats each vector pair \((q_i,k_j)\) as follows:
for a fixed embedding dimension size \(d\) it partitions channels into paired real–imaginary components indexed by \(\ell=0,\ldots,d/2-1\),
each associated with a distinct frequency
\(f_\ell=\theta^{-\,2\,\ell/d}\)
for a base value \(\theta=10\,000.\)
The quantified “rotary angle” fed into each channel equals

\[
n\, f_\ell=\frac{n}{\, \beta^{\,\ell}\,},
      \qquad
      \beta=\theta^{\,2/d},
      \tag{1}
\]

where \(n=n_i-n_j\) denotes **relative displacement** rather than absolute index magnitude.
Thus the updated query-key product becomes proportional to Euclidean distance along angular space,
adopting Sine/Cosine periodicity while natively reflecting inter-token closeness independent from whole‑sequence size.

The primary benefit lies in *generalization*:
because RoPE never memorises explicit token slot vectors yet instead maps arbitrary displacements through deterministic trigonometric functions,
it admits inputs whose lengths far exceed whatever was seen during pretraining.
Practically this means an assignment – “reference tensors remain numerically defined even for m≥ 10⁶”—a desirable property absent from standard absolute embeddings.

### # Shortcoming when extending lengths



Despite RoPE’s elegant scalability, pushing context windows centuries above their nominal bounds triggers two hard core difficulties – **catastrophic angle collapse** and **attentional dispersion** – primarily articulated during experiments aimed at stretching up to multi‑million tokens.



#### 1  Catastrophic angle collapse  

When evaluating Mistral‑7B on documents longer than its trained window (~4096 tokens),
the baseline Pi strategy simply divides every rotation angle term outside this range by extension ratio \(s=\frac{L'}{L}\).
Thus rewritten angular arguments become 
\(n/(s\, f_\ell)\).
While manageable at modest expansions (e.g., doubling),
for ratios exceeding tens-of-folds this division squashes most angles towards zero:
two distant slopes map almost identically onto similar sinusoids.
The transformation then erases gradient signals indicative distinct distances,
rendering perplexity very ill‐conditioned during lightweight finetuning steps –
a fact repeated experimentally whereby PI provokes sharp perplexity increases past ~7 k seeds due solely unto this shrinkage.



#### 2  Attentional dispersion  

A complementary failure stems from *crowding*-style effects:
out–of–distribution far–away indices force key/value pairs into extreme sectors where sinusoids distinctly oscillate less frequently according
to lower frequencies governed by larger channel indices \(\ell.\)
When utter expansions push over several hundred thousand places
the bottleneck appears already around five‐hundred kilo scenarios –
several hundred originally separable angular sectors merge effectively into one continuous dialing range,
causing attentions evaluated over four thousand slots to become effectively uniform gradients rather than sharply-peaked functions.
Consequently losses measured against held-out benchmarks reveal sudden drops (“the attention becomes dispersed”) once extended past ~128 k slots.



These phenomena collectively motivated an intense search phenomena – stated rigorously as optimizing Equation (3) presented later –
for *non‐uniform* rescaling factors per dimension (\(\lambda_\ell=\frac{f_{\text{original}}}{f_{\text{rescaled}}}\)) coupled together with allowing initial offsets (\(\hat n\)) so very beginning tokens bypass rescaling entirely.

## Summary

The journey from naïve self‑attention toward scalable lime illustrates steep compromises across three eras:

| Era | Encoding | Scaling Mechanism | Key Problem |
|-----|----------|------------------|-------------|
| Early | Absolute learned vectors | Fixed capacity | Catastrophic outliers beyond horizon |
| Intermediate | Sinusoidal cosine tables | Linear down‑scaling | Crowding + loss loss at higher ratios |
| Recent | Rotatory cosine–sine rotations | Non‑uniform adaptive re‐scaling | Over‑constrained higher dimensions unless explicitly corrected |

Only *dynamic* forms—those proposed under Net‑Tangent-Kernel reasoning yet adapted locally per rotary channel—start halting those failures for modest expansions (~4×), yet remain blinking after ~8–16× stretches unless jointly amended via evolutionary optimisation over both dimensions’ frequencies (\(\lambda_\ell\)) and early-token thresholds (\(\hat n.\))
LongRoPE capitalises precisely on these observations:
searching a tailored set of rescale factors keeps crucial low-frequency channels intact while gently dampening costly high-frequency rotation swings—a manoeuvre embraced through an evolutionary paradigm flagged SPT among top-performers—enabling unprecedented reach well past two million tokens while preserving competence over baseline recursions once suitably tuned back down (Section 5).

In doing so this manuscript offers not merely another plugin layer—but demonstrates how carefully exploiting hierarchical nonuniformities inside triple-angle structures unlocks continuity across drastically disjoint length domains while avoiding both memory-intensive finetune steps and platform-level architectural rewrites.
