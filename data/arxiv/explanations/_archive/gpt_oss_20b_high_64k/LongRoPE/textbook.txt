# Chapter Title  
**1 Introduction – Long Context Needs**

---

# Typical transformer context limits  
Modern large‑language models (LLMs) adopt the Transformer architecture, which relies critically on positional information because its self‑attention mechanism is permutation‑invariant. In most state‑of‑the‑art systems today—such as LLaMA2—this positional information is encoded using rotary position embeddings (RoPE). A practical consequence is an inherent cap on how many input tokens can be processed reliably during one forward pass. For example, the released version of LLaMA2 is trained only on sequences up to \(4\,096\) tokens [llama2]. When a model encounters‐for example a sentence list exceeding this bound—the model attempts to place all later tokens into fresh position indices it has never seen before during pre‑training. Those new indices correspond to rotary angles that lie far outside the distribution seen during training, producing what the authors refer to as *catastrophic values* [pi]. This discrepancy exposes an out‑of‑distribution problem so severe that subsequent layers cannot rely on accurate positional cues any more, resulting in degradations in both prediction accuracy and stability.

---

# Impact on downstream applications  
The shortness of existing windows artificially truncates laws that humans can unfold over extremely long texts or conversations. **In‑context learning** becomes problematic when exemplars are stacked together—each demonstration may easily consume several hundred hundred symbols—and models retrain outside their nominal length they simply cannot attend jointly over all examples at once [Huang2023FewerIM]. Similarly **LLM agents** must keep track of hundreds or thousands of dialogue turns before deciding what action next [park2023generative | madaan2023selfrefine]. In such scenarios a truncated input forces artificial ablation strategies such as hierarchy summarisation or chunk safeguarding which dilute faithfulness or introduce hallucinations during generation. Another vivid illustration occurs in *passkey retrieval* benchmarks where a five‑digit secret is randomly injected somewhere inside a multi‑million‑token document—if the model’s external window does not span the location containing the key trading alone no meaningful sequence correlation surfaces [passkey]. Finally tasks such as summarising entire books or enterprise logs demand retaining every word across often‑to‐megabyte text streams. When forced under \(4\,096\) tokens these tasks either give incomplete summaries or produce fragmented reasoning chains which break user trust in safety‑critical settings.

These practical For­-applications show a clear imperative: **LLMs ought continue leveraging their fundamental ability to consume much longer inputs** without losing sharpness inside the familiar \(4\,096\) realm.

---

# Current extensions up to about 128 k  
Recent literature has indeed taken steps toward larger windows by combining *positional interpolation* with modest amounts of *fine tuning*. Three families dominate this effort:

1. **Linear Positional Interpolation (PI)** simply rescales all rotational angles by a uniform factor equalling the target-to‑original ratio \(s=L'/L\) [pi].
2. **NTK‑aware interpolation/extrapolation** distributes scaling unevenly over dimensions according to their frequencies following Neural Tangent Kernel theory [ntk] (dynamically adjusting \(s\) per layer).
3. **YaRN** partitions rotational dimensions into three frequency groups—high receives extrapolation (\(\lambda=1\)), low linear PI (\(\lambda=s\)), middle uses NTK–style weighting (\(\lambda=s^i\)) [yarn].

All these strategies report successful breadth increases up to roughly \(128\,000\) tokens ([LongLoRA], [Liu2023Scaling], [Yuan2024Soaring]) but they share crucial shortcomings:

* **New position indices pose catastrophic value problems**, adding more than ninety percent untrained indices when scaling past a few hundred times relative increases—a hard convergence bottleneck.
* **Scarcity of corpora containing features larger than one million** degrades effective sample support; moreover training directly at those enormous lengths demands unreachable compute hours.
* **Positional crowding** disperses attention signals extremely thinly across tens‑of‑thousands even within what was originally an “adequate” frame, leading models’ accuracy over shorter windows (\(<L'\)) noticeably deteriorate once fine tuned at huge scales—that effect manifests already after doubling a size base[ pi].

Thus valuable albeit incremental progress half‐delivered earlier motivates our deeper investigation into how precisely they handle positional information—and therefore how they might be further stretched outward without incurring unacceptable costs or accuracy loss.

---

# Why closer inspection matters  
The underlying assumption behind most Naïve–interpolation designs is *uniformity*: every rotary dimension behaves identically regardless its intrinsic frequency band, and every subsequent token behaves identically regardless whether it sits among head‑tokens or deep tail ones.\

In Section “Non‐uniformity in Positional Interpolation”, empirical study reveals two noteworthy characteristics hidden beneath this adoptive symmetry:

1. **Dimension‐wise non‐uniformity** – Low dimensional pairs encode high frequencies (\(\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}\)) whereas high dimensional pairs carry low frequencies.\ A direct dilation \(\lambda=s\) equally applied corrupts critical high-frequency behaviors early while underutilising spacious lower-frequency channels where larger dilations could be safely passed through.
   
   The authors’ evolutionary search uncovers *diskrete* rescaling factors \(\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{d/2-1}\), markedly diverging from both \(s\) and even previously proposed groupings in YaRN—demonstrated by artificially better perplexities across \(8\,000–16\,000\)-token evaluations whilst remaining unfa­t matched by PI/NTK/YaRN.
   
2 . **Token–position nonuniformity** – In any sequence early symbols receive much higher attention scores compared with farthest symbols—their gradients drive most learning updates.\  
   By allowing an initial subset \(\hat n\in[0,\dots]\) of positions untouched (\(\mathbb I=1)\), researchers empirically observed immediate gains—e.g., embodiments fixing merely two thousand initial index rotaries dramatically lowered perplexity versus purely interpolated versions.\   Moreover experiments showed varying optimal \(\hat n\)-lengths depending upon desired target size—from none needed at small-scale extensions \(<10^{- k}\), yet beneficially leaving them untouched even when extending towards millions facilitates smoother continuity.
   
The consequence? Instead of one bulk factor dilating whole journey happily into unnatural regimes naïve PI produces unnecessarily aggressive crowding for early spatial registers harming gradient flow.\ By strategically keeping shots open exactly where they matter—for both frequency spacing among bytes and front/back density –the trimmed representations old enough inspire stable forward passes without heavy padding corrections; i.e., “closer inspection” opens up an injection point they call \(\hat{\lambda}_i\).

Therefore investing precious computational budget into systematically searching *both* axes yields immediate payoff—a tangible path toward deploying far larger windows without massive iterative tuning cycles—a key motivation grounding setup behind LongRoPE’s design choices below.

---

# What LongRoPE seeks to achieve  
At its core *LongRoPE* defines three engineering goals that neatly tie back those insights:

1. **Decoupled two–layer dimensional/momentum search** → By enumerating candidate sets \((\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{d/2-1},\,\hat n)\), then feeding them through an evolutionary algorithm which regards ℕ constraints rooted in NTK theory (`λ_i ≤ λ_{i+1}`), authors locate subtler yet globally consistent embeddings far faster than exhaustive enumeration would allow.\ This step alone brings almost an eightfold leap—new settings recover performance even when naive linear PI would utterly fail beyond roughly doubling its original limit.\ The breakthrough demonstrates not just unsolved engineering trickiness but strengthens argument that memory width expansion essentially requires rethinking between specific dimension scopes rather than boosting universal stretch rates militarily along each step.
   
2.) **Progressive hybrid extension strategy** → Recognizing extreme–length training keeps cost prohibitive (hundreds-of-thousands GPU hours), the authors orchestrate two cheap intermediaries:\  

   • First extend Naïvely pre-trained model down-to​-256 000 via long-aware search optimized for `s≈32` (pairs ≤ \(256\times 4 ≈1024\); i.e., four times original).  

   • Fine-tune briefly using either ~400+600 steps dictionary-in-segment~(inductive content truncated into `256×` substrings).  

   • Then run another deepersized search exactly again over dimensions—with now learned weights functioning nearer final shapes—and ultimately increase leftover scale factor eightfold again (`32` → `512`) without penalising train time because controlled initialization reduces divergence drastically.\

   Consequently obtaining a genuine \(2048\,000~(≈512×L_0)\)-token support takes merely one thousand training iterations while standard baseline LoRA approaches would already struggle past $\approx200$ ones lines pure until penalty derivatives diverge.
    
3.) **Symmetry restoration back-shortcard boundaries** → Completely expanding width alone does not preserve functional quality in formerly comfortable `$4\,096$` range—attention dispersion overwhelms lower layers severely when division span grows wide enough `[π]`.    
    To counteract this post-facto adjustment mechanism reverts position-rescaling coefficients specifically tuned back down at `8 k` length during inference phase—they are selected via small secondary evolutionary runs leaving primary coefficients intact otherwise.\ This step assures near-identical benchmark results such as MMLU/TruthfulQA whilst still comfortably retaining >90% retrieval success during passkey tests even inside millions-of-token hardware allowed evaluations via synthetic scenarios.\

By achieving exactly those three junctures — novel expansion harnessing exhaustive but quickly-converged optimization over two independent nonuniform spaces while steering only little extra hardware budget ─ *LongRoPE* unlocks unprecedented “beyond millions” contextual capability using pre-trained Rope-organised thousands-sized architecture unchanged aside from embedding scalars.(figure overview & figure fig-non-ft confirm visualizations).

## 2 Transformer Architectures & Positional Encoding

Large language models learn powerful representations from raw sequences of text tokens only through self‑attention layers whose operations are permutation‑invariant: swapping any two tokens does not change any attention scores or hidden activations unless some order signal is explicitly injected into the model. This intrinsic property motivates every modern transformer architecture to augment its token embeddings with an additional *positional representation*. The implementation details vary—absolute learned encodings or relative schemes such as Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)—but every choice reflects fundamental trade‑offs between expressiveness, generality, and computational practicality when a model must process ever longer documents than it encountered during training.



---

### # Self‑attention’s lack of ordering

Self‑attention computes pairwise interactions between every pair of positions \(i\) and \(j\) via dot products between keys \(k_i\) and queries \(q_j\):

\[
a_{ij}\;\propto\;\frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}},
\]

where \(d_k\) is the key dimensionality.
The calculation depends solely on similarities among vectors extracted from *token representations*, none of which encode any notion of where a particular token falls within its sentence or paragraph hierarchy.
Consequently, rearranging the inputs changes nothing except for their indices \(n=0,\ldots,N-1\); mathematically speaking, the self‑attention mechanism treats every token equivalently—a property known as *permutation invariance*.
Without an external cue indicating how distant two symbols are along a document’s linear order,
the model has no way of distinguishing between “the author name appears before the title” versus “after”.
In practice this absence manifests itself during training: when attempting multi‑pass language modeling over long ranges,
the network struggles to learn causal relationships simply because those relationships never surface systematically in its loss surface.



---

### # Learned absolute embeddings

A straightforward remedy introduces a separate learnable embedding vector \(p_n \in \mathbb{R}^d\) for each integer index \(n\) up to some maximal sequence length \(L_{\max}\).
During forward propagation, each token representation \(t_n\) is summed element‑wise with its corresponding slot:
\(h_n = t_n + p_n.\)
The model thereafter learns directional biases purely through these added vectors.
The approach has two attractive properties:
firstly it places explicit coordinates into each position without modifying key/value computations,
and secondly it remains fully compatible with very small storage budgets—the embedding table size grows linearly with \(L_{\max}\).

Yet there are severe practical impediments:

* **Fixed horizon**  
For every model host deployment a fixed maximum length must be hard‑coded during pretraining;
anyra tasks demanding queries longer than twice that bound would drive indices beyond those seen during learning.
Consequently our thirty‑one document retrieval experiment reported “toxic lookups” once sequence length exceeded >\,128 000 tokens—the *untrained* word vectors produce highly anomalous activations stretched far outside their natural distribution space;
the optimizer stalls and perplexity explodes—an issue noted clearly in our study’s obstacle taxonomy (“untrained new position indices introduce many catastrophic values”).

* **Data scarcity and cost**  
Creating reliable learned embeddings would theoretically require training examples covering all desired positions.
Realistic corpora rarely contain documents larger than even several hundred thousand tokens;
therefore extensions beyond an existing horizon force extrapolation or fine‑tuning on synthetic data—both fragile processes,
especially given GPUs’ throughput constraints for ultra‑long batches.



---

### # Relative encodings motivation

An alternative removes dependence on a finite table altogether by enabling *relative* positional bias.
Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), introduced during LLaMA2’s design phase,
injects distance information directly into queries and keys through sinusoidal rotations.
Mathematically speaking RoPE treats each vector pair \((q_i,k_j)\) as follows:
for a fixed embedding dimension size \(d\) it partitions channels into paired real–imaginary components indexed by \(\ell=0,\ldots,d/2-1\),
each associated with a distinct frequency
\(f_\ell=\theta^{-\,2\,\ell/d}\)
for a base value \(\theta=10\,000.\)
The quantified “rotary angle” fed into each channel equals

\[
n\, f_\ell=\frac{n}{\, \beta^{\,\ell}\,},
      \qquad
      \beta=\theta^{\,2/d},
      \tag{1}
\]

where \(n=n_i-n_j\) denotes **relative displacement** rather than absolute index magnitude.
Thus the updated query-key product becomes proportional to Euclidean distance along angular space,
adopting Sine/Cosine periodicity while natively reflecting inter-token closeness independent from whole‑sequence size.

The primary benefit lies in *generalization*:
because RoPE never memorises explicit token slot vectors yet instead maps arbitrary displacements through deterministic trigonometric functions,
it admits inputs whose lengths far exceed whatever was seen during pretraining.
Practically this means an assignment – “reference tensors remain numerically defined even for m≥ 10⁶”—a desirable property absent from standard absolute embeddings.



---

### # Shortcoming when extending lengths



Despite RoPE’s elegant scalability, pushing context windows centuries above their nominal bounds triggers two hard core difficulties – **catastrophic angle collapse** and **attentional dispersion** – primarily articulated during experiments aimed at stretching up to multi‑million tokens.



#### 1  Catastrophic angle collapse  

When evaluating Mistral‑7B on documents longer than its trained window (~4096 tokens),
the baseline Pi strategy simply divides every rotation angle term outside this range by extension ratio \(s=\frac{L'}{L}\).
Thus rewritten angular arguments become 
\(n/(s\, f_\ell)\).
While manageable at modest expansions (e.g., doubling),
for ratios exceeding tens-of-folds this division squashes most angles towards zero:
two distant slopes map almost identically onto similar sinusoids.
The transformation then erases gradient signals indicative distinct distances,
rendering perplexity very ill‐conditioned during lightweight finetuning steps –
a fact repeated experimentally whereby PI provokes sharp perplexity increases past ~7 k seeds due solely unto this shrinkage.



#### 2  Attentional dispersion  

A complementary failure stems from *crowding*-style effects:
out–of–distribution far–away indices force key/value pairs into extreme sectors where sinusoids distinctly oscillate less frequently according
to lower frequencies governed by larger channel indices \(\ell.\)
When utter expansions push over several hundred thousand places
the bottleneck appears already around five‐hundred kilo scenarios –
several hundred originally separable angular sectors merge effectively into one continuous dialing range,
causing attentions evaluated over four thousand slots to become effectively uniform gradients rather than sharply-peaked functions.
Consequently losses measured against held-out benchmarks reveal sudden drops (“the attention becomes dispersed”) once extended past ~128 k slots.



These phenomena collectively motivated an intense search phenomena – stated rigorously as optimizing Equation (3) presented later –
for *non‐uniform* rescaling factors per dimension (\(\lambda_\ell=\frac{f_{\text{original}}}{f_{\text{rescaled}}}\)) coupled together with allowing initial offsets (\(\hat n\)) so very beginning tokens bypass rescaling entirely.

## Summary

The journey from naïve self‑attention toward scalable lime illustrates steep compromises across three eras:

| Era | Encoding | Scaling Mechanism | Key Problem |
|-----|----------|------------------|-------------|
| Early | Absolute learned vectors | Fixed capacity | Catastrophic outliers beyond horizon |
| Intermediate | Sinusoidal cosine tables | Linear down‑scaling | Crowding + loss loss at higher ratios |
| Recent | Rotatory cosine–sine rotations | Non‑uniform adaptive re‐scaling | Over‑constrained higher dimensions unless explicitly corrected |

Only *dynamic* forms—those proposed under Net‑Tangent-Kernel reasoning yet adapted locally per rotary channel—start halting those failures for modest expansions (~4×), yet remain blinking after ~8–16× stretches unless jointly amended via evolutionary optimisation over both dimensions’ frequencies (\(\lambda_\ell\)) and early-token thresholds (\(\hat n.\))
LongRoPE capitalises precisely on these observations:
searching a tailored set of rescale factors keeps crucial low-frequency channels intact while gently dampening costly high-frequency rotation swings—a manoeuvre embraced through an evolutionary paradigm flagged SPT among top-performers—enabling unprecedented reach well past two million tokens while preserving competence over baseline recursions once suitably tuned back down (Section 5).

In doing so this manuscript offers not merely another plugin layer—but demonstrates how carefully exploiting hierarchical nonuniformities inside triple-angle structures unlocks continuity across drastically disjoint length domains while avoiding both memory-intensive finetune steps and platform-level architectural rewrites.

3 Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)

Large language models rely on transformers whose attention mechanism treats every token independently unless an explicit notion of order is supplied through positional information. The most common strategy before rotary positional embeddings was a static lookup table of positional vectors appended element‑wise to each token embedding (“absolute” encoding). While simple, this construction requires discretising every possible position up to the maximum supported sequence length, leading both to memory bloat and numerical instability when a transformer never observes fresh positions during training—as soon as a new positional index appears that has never been seen during training an activation pattern far outside train‑time distribution (‘catastrophic’ values) arises.\[\]  Rotational embeddings solve this issue by letting each token’s position enter directly into trigonometric signals whose arguments grow linearly with the position index \(n\). Because adding extra tokens simply means evaluating trigonometric functions at larger arguments \(n\theta_i\), these signals preserve their statistical distribution regardless of how far apart two positions are in token space.\(\)

### # Formulation equation

Let \(d\) denote the embedding dimensionality allocated for queries or values inside every transformer layer (the total hidden size being \(d_{h}\)). Rotational embeddings pair half‑the‑dimensional axes into complex plane vectors,
each axis representing either a cosine or sine component:
\[
\boxed{
\left[
\cos( n \theta_0 ),\, \sin( n \theta_0 ),
\, \cos( n \theta_1 ),\, \sin( n \theta_1 ),
\, \dots ,
\, \cos( n \theta_{d/2-1} ),\, \sin( n \theta_{d/2-1} )
\right]
}
\tag{1}
\]
where \(n\in [0,\dots]\) enumerates token positions along a sequence.
The rotation angles themselves are determined through a progressive frequency schedule
\[
\theta_i=
\theta^{-\,{2i}/{d}}, 
\quad i=0,\dots,\frac{d}{2}-1 ,
\]
with a default base value \(\theta=10{000}\).  
For each low‑index dimension \(i=0\), the angle grows slowly (\(\theta_0=\theta^{0}=1\)), while higher dimensions receive progressively larger negative exponents meaning substantially higher angular speed along those axes.\(\)
By inserting these rotated vectors into query or key matrices,
the inner product between two keys becomes proportional to
\(e^{i\,(\Delta n)\,\cdot\,(\text{frequency vector})}\),
thereby encoding pairwise distances between compressed phase angles instead
of discrete indices.\(\)

### # Rotation frequency choice

Frameworks such as LLaMA adopt a *base* reciprocal exponential scheme for frequency selection.
Choosing
\(f_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^i}}\)
with
\(\beta=\theta^{\,{2}/{d}}\)
ensures exponentially decreasing frequencies.
This structure means nearby tokens differ wildly along the first few (\(i < d/4\)) dimensions yet change very little along higher‑dimensional ones.
Consequently two types of algebraic interactions arise:
high‑frequency axes emphasise short‑range contextual cues,
while low‑frequency axes retain signal even when two tokens are thousands apart.\(\)
This contrasts sharply with what would happen were all frequencies set identically,
where distant token pairs would collapse onto nearly identical angle vectors,
rendering their rank‑wise relationships indistinguishable.\(\)

The final implication is that model weights trained on relatively short documents learn useful geometry over *all* dimensions once they encounter any measure \(\Delta n=n_j-n_k\).
Because no weights depend explicitly upon an arbitrary upper bound on sequence length,
the rope embedding formulation remains well‑behaved even when extrapolated beyond its pretraining window;
refining retinal layer growth amounts merely pulling down essential rotational speeds via an additional multiplicative *rescale* factor \(λ≥1.\)(See § # Non‑uniformity below.)

### # Relation between \(θ_i\) and dimensionality

Because each index’s corresponding pair has one cosine and one sine term,
the effective number of distinct angular components doubles per global embedding dimension.
Equation (1) gives us exactly one ‘pair’ per half‑dimension.
When moving through layers incrementally increase \(i\),
the exponentiation division feature scale halts back toward zero:
Each succeeding dimension receives an argument smaller by
\(β^{-1}\).
Thus lower dimension signals alternate frequently while higher ones progress slowly.
A crucial result discovered empirically is that *high‑frequency* components tend towards more regular spacing regardless how long the document becomes—
they help capture strict syntax close-ups,
whereas *low‑frequency* components manage broader topic drift laterality:
precisely those directions preserve inter‐token synergy once distances grow enormously.\(\)
Hence preserving key dimensions during interpolation—as LongRoPE does—keeps far‑apart token interactions inside numerical safety limits
while still delivering descriptive capacity where needed.\(\)

### # Benefits over sinusoidal absolute encodings

Absolute positional encodings normally construct an \(L×d/2×2\) table once at initialization:
each row holds either
\(0,\dots,L-1\)
directly fed into a cosine/sine formula:
if this table were collapsed into `sin(i·n)` style functions then global frequency would still be uniform;
one would still encounter numerically explosive behavior beyond pretraining bounds because positional identifiers conjugate differently per column.
Rotaries circumvent two principal problems:

1. **No precomputed lookup table** — Because each new index can instantly compute its own angle vector internally,
deployment does not require storing billions of constants—an impossible load for memory‑constrained GPUs—
and new lengths emerge without further model modifications;
this “statelessness” perfectly aligns with Latent Variable Model theory where self‐attention layers remain stable under unseen contexts.[^†]

2. **Smooth ordering structure** — Rotations generate continuous changes in angles linearly proportional unto distance.
For example adjacent tokens change negligibly along low‑frequency axes under mild rotations;
separated tokens maintain measurable separation regardless of overall chain length.[^♠]
If positional encodings were purely additive random noise or fixed one–hot complements—for instance adding fixed integer scalars—the model had learned arbitrary biases tightly intertwined with particular early indices.
Those biases collapse catastrophically once one steps outside distribution—a situation especially urgent when extending beyond four thousand tokens,’’

In practice:
Comparisons in LongRoPE’s ablation studies show naive “linear” interpolation obtained through shared rescaling across *all* dimensions (akin almost identically to simple uniform sinusoid stretching),
produces severe perplexity spikes already beyond twice its baseline support.
In contrast retraining only selective dimensions thanks partly allied angular magnitude preservation delivered consistent perplexities up through eight times longer sequences—even without re‑training.\(^††\)
These results validate that underlining potence above static sine/cos added tables lies fundamentally within rotational geometry itself rather than just remembering deterministic scalars.\(^‡§§§^‡^\)

---

Together, these insights lay cheap root reason why oscillatory rotational embeddings become core bedrock behind recent works—including LongRoPE—that push context lengths beyond several million tokens while keeping efficient adaptation cost low.\
