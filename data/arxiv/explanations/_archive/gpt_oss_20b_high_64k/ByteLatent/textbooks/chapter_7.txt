Local Decoder Design**

Large‑language models inevitably translate high‑level latent representations back into observable tokens—in our case “bytes” rather than “sub‑word units”.  
The *local decoder* part of BLT is precisely that middleman: it converts the latent output patches \((o_j)\) produced by BLT’s global autoregressive network into concrete byte predictions \((y_i)\).  
Because it deals directly with byte‑level sequences created during inference, its behaviour differs fundamentally from usual token‑based decoders—most notably in how attention flow is oriented and how residual connections tie together recurrent states.  The remainder of this chapter sketches these aspects carefully so that even first‑year students who are just learning about transformers can trace how BLT performs its reconstruction step by step.



### Cross‑Attention Decoding Orientation

In standard causal language models every query attends “from left‑to‑right” toward its past keys/values (“self‑attention”).  
BLT introduces two distinct attention families:

* **Encoder Cross‑Attention** (`Encoder → Decoder`) – gathers *byte* events into a *latent* set.
* **Decoder Cross‑Attention** (`Decoder → Encoder`) – performs the reverse operation needed during generation/transduction (bytes → latent).

In contrast with its encoding partner where *queries* look toward *latent values*, here **queries are byte representations** while *keys* and *values* come from latents:

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_0 &= h^{\text{enc}}_{l_\mathcal E},\\
B_l &= D_{l-1} \;+\;
      W_o
        \!\left( 
          \operatorname{softmax}\!
            \left(
              \frac{QK^{\!\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}
            \right)
          V
        \right),\\
Q_i &=
    W_q\!\big( d^{\,}_{l-1,i}\big),
    &K_i &= 
      W_k\!\big( \mathcal D_C(o_j)\big),
      &
V_i &=
    W_v\!\big( \mathcal D_C(o_j)\big),\\
D_l &= 
   \text{Decoder‑Transformer–Layer}_{l}\big(B_l\big).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here  

* \(h^{\text{enc}}_{l_\mathcal E}\) consists of per‑byte hidden vectors produced by the *local encoder*.  
* \(D_0\) seeds every byte location `i` in hindsight before attending any latents—making decoding purely autoregressive on already generated bytes.\footnote{
  Each timestep `i` receives its own byte query vector even when subsequent generated bytes change due to lookback capability afforded later}
* `\(W_q,W_k,W_v,W_o\)` are learned projection matrices creating \(U_D\) independent attention heads;\;
\(d_k=\frac{\text{size}}{\!U_D}\) scales dot‑products properly.\footnote{
  Multi–head statistics are kept uniform throughout BEF IDL although dimensions differ slightly because latent dimension \(h_\mathcal G\) exceeds byte dimension \(h_\mathcal E\); nonetheless \(U_D=U_E=1/2\,k/n_p\) holds concretely once hyperparameters settle}
* Finally Bar RHB merges this attended signal `\(B_l\)` through a conventional feed‑forward pathway before exiting as `\(D_l\)` into another stage—exactly mirroring an ordinary Transformer deck except roles inverted.


This crossed orientation delivers two crucial things:

1. **Localized Contextualization** – Bytes belonging jointly to one output patch receive a distilled summary pulled from latents using selective query scopes determined by segmentation boundaries computed during patching (§ 4). Consequently every decoded byte feels intimate awareness of all past latents except itself , matching autoregressive logic while staying computationally lean.
2. **Controlled Signal Flow** – Because key/value vectors live outside true shipping sequence boundaries—a vital property when generating sequentially—we can safely employ “no‐positional” inner designs without violating exclusivity rules fundamental to right‑to‑left generation (\S 5).

Switching other defaults such as masking strategies yields precisely correct causal relationships (bytes ignore future patch bits automatically because those keys belong to untouched latents).
