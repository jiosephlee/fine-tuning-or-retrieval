Answer:
The stabiliser does **not change** what the network actually computes – it only moves every gate value by a *constant additive factor*.  
Let  

* \(Q_T=\prod_{k=2}^{T}e^{g_k}
        =e^{\,{\sum_{k=2}^{T}g_k}}\), where \(g_k=\log f_k(t)\).

All cell‑state terms have one product over forget gates followed
by one input gate:
\(c_T=\sum_{\tau}(\prod_{k=\tau+1}^{T}f_k)i_\tau z_\tau\).
If every gate were replaced simply by its exponential,
every summand would acquire an extra multiplier \(Q_T\).
Because **the same \(Q_T\)** multiplies *every* term,
the numerators and denominators appearing in  
\(h=\psi(c/n)\) both contain this common factor and it cancels when

|~~~~~~~~~~~~~~   h = ψ(c/n)\,*  |
|------------------------------------------------------------|

Hence the hidden state produced after normalisation is *identical*.
Since differentiation respects products linearly (chain rule),
multiplying every intermediate variable by a constant that never
depends on learnable parameters cancels out everywhere:
\(∂h/∂θ =∂h/∂Q_T ·∂Q_T/∂θ =0.\)

Therefore the stabiliser keeps exactly the same forward map,
so all Jacobians—and consequently gradients—remain unchanged,
even though individual exponentials stay safely below overflow limits.
