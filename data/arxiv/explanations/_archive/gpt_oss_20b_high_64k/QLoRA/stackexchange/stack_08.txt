<p><strong>Short answer:</strong></p>

<p>Yes – once the base‑model tensors are <em>properly frozen</em> (i.e., their <code class="language-text">requires_grad</code> flag is set False and they are registered as buffers rather than trainable parameters) internal PyTorch bookkeeping cannot write any “cached” gradients back onto those tensors during normal training loops.</p>

<p>The gradients – even those produced while computing derivatives through the frozen tensors – are either discarded or routed exclusively into the LoRA adapters that appear on the optimizer’s parameter list.</p>

<h2 id="what-freeze-means-in-pytorch">## What “freeze” means in PyTorch</h2>

<p>When a tensor has <code class="language-text">requires_grad=False</code>, PyTorch treats it </p>
<ul>
<li><strong>as a leaf ‑node without a <code class="language-text">​.grad</code> attribute</strong> – an operation involving it does *not* create any backwards‑tracking node.</li>
<li><strong>not part of any optimiser’s state</strong> – even if you later call <code class="language-text">optimizer.step()</code>, items whose <code class="language-text">​.grad</code> is missing won’t receive an update.</li>
<li><strong>immutable unless you modify it explicitly</strong> – any operation that would alter such a tensor must be performed <em>in place</em> on a clone or after detaching it (<code class="language-text">tensor.clone().detach()</code>).  </li>
</ul>

<p>This differs from merely locking​ ‑<em>You locked its learning rate so gradient survives </em>- recursively discards possible updates downstream.</p>
