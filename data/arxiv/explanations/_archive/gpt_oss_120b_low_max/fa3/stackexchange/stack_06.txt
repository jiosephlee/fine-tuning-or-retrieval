\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How is FLOP counting performed for forward and backward passes in the benchmark section?
Question:
The authors compute forward FLOPs as
$$4 \cdot \text{seqlen}^2 \cdot \text{head
dim} \cdot \text{num	head
ds}$$
and then halve it for causal masking and multiply by 2.5 for backward pass. Why is there a factor of 4, and why exactly 2.5 for backward? Does this account for recomputation of intermediate softmax values or any extra matrix multiplications?
Answer:
In the benchmark the authors report the number of floating‑point operations (FLOPs) that would be performed by a **naïve** implementation of exact attention – i.e. the three matrix‑multiply steps that dominate the runtime:

1. **\(QK^{\top}\)** – a GEMM with shapes \((N\times d)\times(d\times N)\);
2. **\(\operatorname{softmax}(QK^{\top})\)** – an element‑wise exponential, reduction and division (the cost of which is tiny compared with the GEMMs);
3. **\(PV\)** – a second GEMM with shapes \((N\times N)\times(N\times d)\).

For a *single* head the two GEMMs each require

\[
\underbrace{N^{2}d}_{\text{multiplications}}+\underbrace{N^{2}d}_{\text{additions}}=2\,N^{2}d
\]

floating‑point operations. Because there are two such GEMMs, the total for the forward pass of one head is

\[
4\,N^{2}d .
\]

Multiplying by the number of heads gives the formula that appears in the paper

\[
\boxed{4 \;\text{seqlen}^{2}\;\text{head‑dim}\;\text{num‑heads}}
\]

The **factor 4** therefore does **not** come from any hidden constant; it is simply “two GEMMs × (multiply + add)”.  

---

### Causal masking

When causal (or lower‑triangular) masking is applied only the entries on or below the diagonal are actually computed. The upper‑triangular half is skipped, so roughly half of the \(N^{2}\) elements are omitted. Consequently the FLOP count is divided by two:

\[
\frac{4\,N^{2}d}{2}=2\,N^{2}d .
\]

---

### Backward pass – why “\( \times 2.5 \)”?

During back‑propagation through attention we need gradients for \(Q,K,V\). As shown in § 2.1 of the paper, this requires **five** matrix multiplications:

* \(dV = P^{\top} dO\)  (1)  
* \(dP = dO\,V^{\top}\)  (2)  
* \(dS = \mathrm{dsoftmax}(dP)\)  (element‑wise, negligible)  
* \(dQ = \alpha\, dS\,K\)  (3)  
* \(dK = \alpha\, dS^{\top} Q\)  (4)

In addition, the forward pass already performed two GEMMs (the same \(QK^{\top}\) and \(PV\)). If one counts every matrix multiplication that *needs to be executed* in a standard autograd implementation, the backward pass therefore incurs

\[
5 \text{ (backward)} + 2 \text{ (forward)} = 7
\]

GEMMs, while the forward pass alone uses only 2. The ratio of backward‑to‑forward FLOPs is therefore

\[
\frac{7}{2}=3.5 .
\]

However, in practice three of those seven multiplications are *reused*: the \(QK^{\top}\) and \(PV\) results are already in registers or shared memory when we start back‑propagation, so we do **not** recompute them. Only three new GEMMs (the ones listed as (1)–(4) above that involve a fresh operand—\(dO V^{\top}, dS K, dS^{\top} Q\)) have to be executed anew.

Hence the effective number of **additional** matrix multiplies in the backward sweep is three, giving a total of

\[
2 \text{ (forward)} + 3 \text{ (extra backward)} = 5
\]

GEMMs versus 2 in the forward pass, i.e.

\[
\frac{5}{2}=2.5 .
\]

That is exactly the factor quoted by the authors: they count all FLOPs required to produce both outputs and gradients, assuming that intermediate softmax values (\(P=\operatorname{softmax}(QK^{\top})\)) are kept in registers/SMEM and not recomputed, and that only the *new* matrix products needed for gradients are performed.

---

### What about “extra” work such as softmax recomputation?

The softmax itself involves an exponential, a reduction, and a division per element of the score matrix; these operations are \(O(N^{2})\) but their arithmetic intensity is far lower than that of a GEMM (the GPU can execute roughly two orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs per second for special functions than for Tensor‑Core matrix multiplication). Because their cost is negligible compared with matrix multiplies on H100, they are omitted from the FLOP tally used for reporting TFLOP/s.

In short:

* **Factor 4** = two GEMMs × two FLOPs per multiply‑add.  
* **Division by 2** for causal masking reflects halving of the dense score matrix.  
* **Factor 2.5** for backward comes from: forward needs 2 GEMMs; backward adds three *new* GEMMs; total = 5 → \(5/2 = 2.5\).  
* No extra FLOPs are counted for recomputing softmax or intermediate values; those are assumed to be cached within the kernel as described in § 2.1 and § 3.

Thus the reported FLOP counts faithfully represent the arithmetic work performed by a conventional exact‑attention implementation on which their speed measurements are based.
