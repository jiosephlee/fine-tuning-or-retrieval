\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### What exact loss function is used during the evolutionary search when optimizing for perplexity?
Question:
The optimization problem (Eq. 4) minimizes a loss \(\mathcal{L}\) over samples longer than the target length, and later they say they use perplexity as a proxy. Is \(\mathcal{L}\) simply the average negative log‑likelihood (i.e., cross‑entropy) over next‑token predictions on a small validation set? Are any regularization terms added to discourage extreme \(\lambda_i\) values?
Answer:
In the paper the quantity that drives the evolutionary search is **the language‑model perplexity on a small validation set**, and this is used directly as the loss $\mathcal{L}$ in Eq. (4).  

Perplexity is defined as  

\[
\text{PPL}= \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}-\log p_\theta (x_t\mid x_{<t})\Bigr),
\]

where the sum runs over all tokens in the validation samples and $p_\theta$ is the next‑token distribution produced by the LLM with the candidate RoPE rescaling.  Because $\log p_\theta (x_t\mid x_{<t})$ is just the negative log‑likelihood (i.e., cross‑entropy) for a single token, minimizing perplexity is equivalent to minimizing the **average negative log‑likelihood** over the chosen validation documents.  

The authors therefore compute $\mathcal{L}$ by feeding each candidate set of rescale factors $\{\lambda_i\}$ (and the position‑threshold $\hat n$) into the model, running inference on five randomly sampled PG‑19 validation passages whose length is at least the target window, and measuring the resulting perplexity.  The candidate with the lowest perplexity becomes the parent for the next generation of the evolutionary algorithm.

No additional regularisation terms are added to $\mathcal{L}$.  The only “soft” constraint imposed on the search is a **monotonicity constraint** on the rescaling factors ($\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}$), which follows from NTK theory and greatly reduces the number of useless candidates that would have to be evaluated.  This constraint is enforced during mutation and crossover; it does not appear as an extra term in the loss function.

Thus, during search $\mathcal{L}$ is simply the average cross‑entropy (negative log‑likelihood) over a tiny validation corpus, reported as perplexity, and there are no explicit penalties on extreme values of $\lambda_i$ beyond the monotonicity rule.
