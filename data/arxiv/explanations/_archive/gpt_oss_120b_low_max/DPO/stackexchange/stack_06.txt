\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### How does DPO handle multi‑token sequences when computing $
abla_{	heta}\log \pi(y\mid x)$?
Question:
The loss involves log‑probabilities of whole completions $y$. Practically, language models factorise $\pi(y\mid x)=\prod_t \pi(y_t\mid x, y_{<t})$. When back‑propagating through Eq. (12), are all token probabilities weighted equally by the importance term, or is there any per‑token weighting? Does this affect training stability for long generations?
Answer:
In the DPO objective the model is trained directly on whole‑sequence log‑probabilities, but the way the gradient is computed makes it clear that every token of a completion receives the **same scalar importance weight** for a given preference pair.  

Recall the DPO loss (Eq. 12 of the paper):

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)=-
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})\sim\mathcal{D}}\!
\Bigl[\log \sigma\!\Bigl(\beta\,
\underbrace{\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{w}\mid x)}}_{A_{w}}
-
\beta\,
\underbrace{\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{l}\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{l}\mid x)}}_{A_{l}}
\Bigr)\Bigr].
\]

For a single pair \((y_w ,y_l)\) define the *importance term*

\[
w(x,y_w ,y_l)=\sigma\!\bigl(A_l-A_w\bigr)
       =\sigma\!\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr),
\]
where \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}\) is the implicit reward.  
The gradient with respect to the policy parameters (Eq. 13) is

\[
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}=
-\beta\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\,w(x,y_w,y_l)
   (\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w|x)-\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l|x))\,\bigr].
\]

Because \(\log \pi_\theta(y|x)\) factorises as a sum of token‑level log‑probs,

\[
\log \pi_\theta(y|x)=\sum_{t=1}^{T_y}\log \pi_\theta(y_t|x,y_{<t}),
\]

the gradient decomposes into a sum over tokens:

\[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x)=
   \sum_{t=1}^{T_y}\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t|x,y_{<t}).
\]

Thus the **scalar weight \(w(x,y_w ,y_l)\)** multiplies the entire token‑wise gradient for the preferred sequence and, with a minus sign, for the dispreferred sequence. There is no additional per‑token factor that depends on the position or length of the token; every token inside a given completion is updated proportionally to the same importance weight.

### Why this does not cause instability for long generations

1. **Length‑independent weighting** – Since the importance term does not decay with sequence length, a long generation does not receive a smaller effective learning signal than a short one. The total gradient magnitude grows roughly linearly with the number of tokens, which is exactly what standard language‑model training does (the loss is summed over tokens). Consequently, DPO inherits the same numerical stability properties as ordinary next‑token cross‑entropy training.

2. **Implicit KL regularisation** – The term \(\log \frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)}\) already encodes a KL penalty between the current policy and the reference model for *the whole sequence*. This penalty prevents the model from arbitrarily inflating probabilities for very long preferred sequences; any increase in total log‑probability must be balanced against an increase in KL, which grows with length in exactly the same way.

3. **Weighting by mis‑ranking** – The factor \(w=\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\) becomes large only when the current policy ranks the dispreferred response higher than the preferred one (i.e., when it makes a mistake). If a long generation is already correctly ranked, its weight will be close to zero, so noisy updates do not accumulate.

4. **No per‑token variance amplification** – Because each token’s contribution appears inside a sum, variance does not explode with length; it is comparable to variance in ordinary teacher‑forcing training where gradients are also summed across timesteps.

Empirically (see § 4.1 of the paper), DPO achieves a *strictly better reward–KL frontier* than PPO even on tasks where sequences can be dozens of tokens long, confirming that training remains stable without any special per‑token reweighting.

### Summary

- The importance term in DPO is **global to each completion pair**; it multiplies the whole log‑probability difference between preferred and dispreferred outputs.
- When we back‑propagate, this scalar weight applies uniformly to **all token‑level gradients** belonging to that completion.
- No extra per‑token weighting is introduced; stability follows from exactly the same properties that make ordinary next‑token cross‑entropy training stable, together with the KL regularisation that grows proportionally with sequence length.
- Consequently, DPO can handle long generations without suffering from the instability issues sometimes observed in RLHF pipelines that rely on separate reward estimators or unweighted unlikelihood losses.
