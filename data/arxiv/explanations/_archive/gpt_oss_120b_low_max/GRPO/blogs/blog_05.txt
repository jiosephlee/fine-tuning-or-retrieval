\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# Why Bigger Isn’t Always Better: DeepSeekMath 7B Beats a 540B Closed Model  

Mathematics has become one of the toughest frontiers for large language models (LLMs). For years the narrative has been “more parameters = better performance,” especially on demanding benchmarks like **MATH** (the competition‑level suite of Olympiad‑style problems). Yet the latest open‑source release, **DeepSeekMath 7B**, flips that script: it outperforms **Minerva‑540B**, a closed‑source model that is almost 80 times larger in parameter count. How does a tiny 7‑billion‑parameter model pull ahead of a giant? In this post we break down the key ingredients—data quality, multilingual coverage, and clever fine‑tuning—showing why scale alone can be a misleading metric for mathematical reasoning.

---

## 1. The Numbers That Matter  

| Model | Parameters | Training Tokens (math) | MATH (competition) Accuracy |
|-------|------------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| DeepSeekMath 7B (base) | 7 B | 120 B math tokens (Common Crawl) | **51.7 %** (after RL) |
| Minerva‑540B (closed) | 540 B | – (proprietary mix) | **≈51 %** (reported) |
| GPT‑4 (closed) |  – | – | > 70 % (proprietary) |

*DeepSeekMath 7B reaches 51.7 % on MATH without any external toolkits or voting ensembles, placing it on par with Minerva and inching toward GPT‑4. The gap is especially striking because the open model uses **self‑consistency over 64 samples** to boost its score to **60.9 %**, a technique also employed by many closed models.*

---

## 2. Data Wins the Day  

### 2.1 A Massive, High‑Quality Math Corpus  

- **120 B math tokens** mined from Common Crawl — roughly 7× more than Minerva’s web math portion and 9× more than the OpenWebMath collection.
- The pipeline starts with a small “seed” set (OpenWebMath), trains a fastText classifier, retrieves more pages, then iteratively refines the classifier using manually annotated URLs.  
- After four rounds, **35.5 M** web pages pass quality checks, covering both English and Chinese content.

### 2.2 Quality Over Quantity  

Training experiments on a modest 1.3 B LLM showed that models exposed to the DeepSeekMath corpus consistently beat those trained on popular alternatives:

- **Proof‑Pile‑2**, MathPile, and OpenWebMath all lag behind when evaluated on GSM8K, MATH, and Chinese benchmarks.
- Learning curves indicate that after about **50 B tokens** the DeepSeekMath data still yields gains, whereas smaller corpora plateau early.

> *Takeaway*: A well‑engineered data selection pipeline can extract far richer reasoning signals than raw token counts alone.

### 2.3 Multilingual Edge  

Unlike many academic corpora that are heavily English‑centric, DeepSeekMath’s web mining retained high‑quality Chinese pages. Consequently:

- The model improves on Chinese benchmarks such as **CMATH**, **MGSM‑zh**, and **Gaokao‐MathQA**.
- This multilingual exposure also seems to help English reasoning—likely because diverse mathematical notation and exposition styles reinforce the same underlying concepts.

---

## 3. Code First, Math Later – A Training Trick That Pays Off  

DeepSeekMath starts from **DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B**, a model already proficient in code generation. Why does that matter?

1. **Program‑of‑Thought Reasoning** – The model can naturally switch to Python or SymPy for tool‐integrated solutions, outperforming prior instruction‑tuned models on GSM8K’s program‑aided variant.
2. **Transferable Reasoning Skills** – Experiments show that pre‑training on code tokens before math improves both tool‑free and tool‑assisted reasoning; the “code → math” pipeline yields higher final scores than “general → math”.

In short, code training seeds the model with structured execution logic that later translates into tighter symbolic manipulation during math solving.

---

## 4. Reinforcement Learning Without the Heavy Critic: GRPO  

After supervised instruction tuning (CoT, PoT, tool‐integrated formats), DeepSeekMath receives another boost from a novel RL algorithm called **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**.

- **No separate value network**: GRPO estimates baselines from the average reward of a group of sampled outputs for the same question, cutting memory usage dramatically compared with standard PPO.
- **Outcome vs. Process Supervision**: Both end‑of‑answer rewards and stepwise rewards are supported; process supervision gives finer gradients for multi‑step reasoning.
- Using only the English instruction data (~144 K questions) from GSM8K/MATH, GRPO lifts GSM8K accuracy from **82.9 % → 88.2 %** and MATH from **46.8 % → 51.7 %**.

The result is a small model that rivals much larger ones while staying computationally affordable.

---

## 5. What This Means for Open‑Source AI  

### 5.1 Scale Is Not Destiny  

DeepSeekMath demonstrates that:

- **High‑quality data pipelines** can compensate for orders of magnitude fewer parameters.
- Targeted domain pre‑training (math + code) can yield *specialized* competence surpassing generalist giants.

### 5.2 Community Can Replicate & Extend  

All components described—fastText‐based web mining, multilingual filtering, GRPO training scripts—are publicly reproducible:

- Researchers can adapt the pipeline to other domains (e.g., physics or law).
- Open models can now approach closed models on niche benchmarks without needing massive compute budgets.

### 5.3 Future Directions  

- **Richer multilingual corpora**: Extending beyond English/Chinese could further improve cross‑lingual reasoning.
- **Better reward models**: The paper notes that current reward signals mainly improve *Maj@K* (top‐k robustness) rather than fundamental ability; more nuanced process rewards might push accuracy higher.
- **Geometry & Formal Proofs**: DeepSeekMath still lags behind closed models on geometry and theorem proving; curating geometry‐rich web sources could close that gap.

---

## 6. TL;DR – Bigger Isn’t Always Better  

DeepSeekMath 7B’s triumph over Minerva’s 540B model boils down to three pillars:

1. **A meticulously curated 120 B token math corpus**, harvested from publicly available web pages.
2. **Starting from a code‐savvy base**, which injects programmatic reasoning skills.
3. **Efficient reinforcement learning with GRPO**, boosting performance without exploding memory costs.

For the open-source community, this is an encouraging proof point: with smart data engineering and algorithmic tweaks, even modestly sized models can compete at the frontiers of mathematical AI.
