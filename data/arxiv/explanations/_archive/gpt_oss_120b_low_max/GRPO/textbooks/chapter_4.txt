\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 4: # Pre‑Training DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B

In this chapter we describe how the DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B model is built from a code‑oriented founder, how a massive mixture of mathematical, programming, and natural‑language data is combined, and which training recipes are employed to obtain a strong reasoning engine.  After the training details we examine the model’s performance on a broad spectrum of tasks: step‑by‑step mathematical problem solving, tool‑augmented reasoning, formal theorem proving, and generic language understanding, reasoning, and coding benchmarks.  Throughout the discussion we remain faithful to the experimental observations reported in the DeepSeekMath paper.

---

## Model Initialization from DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5

DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B does not start from a generic language model.  Instead it inherits the parameters of **DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B**, a model that was originally trained on a large corpus of source code together with natural language.  This choice is motivated by two empirical findings reported in the paper:

1. **Code pre‑training endows the model with strong execution‐oriented capabilities** (e.g., handling Python libraries such as `math` and `sympy`).  
2. **A code foundation provides a better starting point for subsequent mathematical pre‑training** than a purely general LLM.  

Consequently, all subsequent learning stages begin with the weights of DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5, after which only the newly introduced mathematics‑focused data are presented to the model.

---

## Token Distribution (Math, Code, Natural Language)

During continual pre‑training the authors mix several data streams in fixed proportions:

| Data source                              | Approximate share of total tokens |
|------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **DeepSeekMath Corpus** (120 B math tokens collected from Common Crawl) | **56 %** |
| **AlgebraicStack** (mathematical code)   | 4 % |
| **arXiv** papers                         | 10 % |
| **GitHub** source code                    | 20 % |
| **General Natural Language** (English & Chinese web text) | 10 % |

The dominant portion (more than half) consists of web pages that have been filtered for high mathematical content using a fastText classifier trained on OpenWebMath.  The remaining streams serve complementary purposes: AlgebraicStack supplies well‑structured symbolic code; arXiv offers scientific discourse (although later experiments show its limited impact on reasoning); GitHub reinforces the model’s ability to read and generate executable code; and natural language data maintain fluency across English and Chinese.

Because the training corpus is multilingual—English dominates but Chinese appears in substantial quantity—the resulting model exhibits improved performance on Chinese math benchmarks compared with prior English‑only pipelines.

---

## Training Hyperparameters

All stages of the pre‑training follow the same optimization framework that was already successful for DeepSeek LLMs:

* **Optimizer:** AdamW with β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.95, weight decay = 0.1.  
* **Learning rate schedule:** A multi‑step schedule that linearly ramps up to a peak after 2 000 warm‑up steps. The learning rate then decays to 31.6 % of its peak at 80 % of total steps and finally to 10 % of the peak at 90 % of steps.  
* **Peak learning rate:** For DeepSeekMath‑Base the peak is set to **4.2 × 10⁻⁴** (slightly lower than in earlier quality‑validation experiments).  
* **Batch size:** Tokens are accumulated into batches of **10 M tokens** per update, substantially larger than the 4 M token batches used for smaller experiments, which improves throughput on modern clusters.  
* **Context length:** Sequences are truncated or padded to a maximum length of **4 K tokens**, ensuring that long mathematical passages can be processed while keeping memory consumption tractable.  
* **Total training budget:** The model is exposed to **500 B tokens** drawn from the mixture described above.

The same hyperparameters that proved effective for quality validation experiments (see Section “Validating the Quality of the DeepSeekMath Corpus”) are retained here, underscoring their robustness across scales.

## Mathematical Problem Solving with Chain‑of‑Thought Prompting

To assess pure reasoning ability—i.e., solving problems without invoking external tools—the authors evaluate DeepSeekMath‑Base using *few‑shot chain‑of‑thought* (CoT) prompting on eight benchmarks spanning English and Chinese:

* **Quantitative reasoning:** GSM8K, MATH (competition level), CMATH (Chinese).  
* **Multiple‑choice style:** MMLU–STEM, Gaokao–MathQA (Chinese).  

The CoT protocol presents a small number of exemplars that explicitly show intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer.  Results (Table `base_math_cot` in the paper) demonstrate that DeepSeekMath‑Base:

* Outperforms all open‑source base models—including Mistral 7B and Llemma 34B—by large margins across all eight datasets.
* Beats Minerva 540B—a closed‐source base model that is more than seventy times larger—on the competition‐level MATH benchmark by more than ten absolute percentage points.

These findings confirm that *high‐quality math pre‐training can compensate for much larger parameter counts*, provided the training data are suitably curated.

---

## Program‑of‑Thought (Tool‐Use) Evaluation

Beyond textual reasoning, modern benchmarks often allow models to generate executable Python programs whose outputs constitute the answer—a paradigm called *program‐of‐thought* (PoT) or *tool‐integrated reasoning*.  

The authors test DeepSeekMath‑Base on GSM8K and MATH using few‑shot PoT prompts where libraries such as `math` and `sympy` may be called for exact symbolic manipulation or numerical evaluation. The pipeline is:

1. The model receives a problem statement and is asked to write a Python script that solves it.
2. The generated script is executed in a sandbox; its printed result is compared with the ground truth answer.

DeepSeekMath‑Base attains higher accuracy than Llama‐based baselines such as Llemma 34B, confirming that its *code foundation plus extensive math token exposure* enables robust program synthesis for mathematical tasks.

---

## Formal Mathematics: MiniF2F Autoformalization

Formal theorem proving provides an even stricter test: given an informal statement together with an informal proof sketch, the model must output a formally correct proof in a proof assistant (here Isabelle).  

The authors evaluate DeepSeekMath‑Base on *miniF2F*, a benchmark derived from Olympiad‐level problems curated for autoformalization research. The evaluation protocol follows prior work:

1. Provide few examples of informal statements, their formal counterparts, and informal proof outlines.
2. Ask the model to generate a “proof sketch” in Isabelle syntax.
3. Feed this sketch to *Sledgehammer*, an automated prover that attempts to fill missing details and produce a complete formal proof.

Despite being only 7 B parameters, DeepSeekMath‑Base shows “strong few‐shot autoformalization performance,” indicating that large‐scale math pre‐training yields representations useful for bridging informal mathematics and rigorous formal systems.

---

## General Language Understanding (MMLU), Reasoning (BBH), and Coding (HumanEval/MBPP)

Mathematical pre‐training should not degrade other abilities; instead it can enhance them because many reasoning patterns are shared across domains.

* **MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding):** Evaluates 57 multiple‐choice subjects including STEM topics; DeepSeekMath‑Base scores substantially higher than its predecessor DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base for STEM categories, confirming transfer from math to broader knowledge tasks.
* **BBH (BIG Bench Hard):** Consists of challenging multi-step reasoning problems; DeepSeekMath‑Base again outperforms both its code precursor and general LLM baselines.
* **Coding Benchmarks (HumanEval & MBPP):** These require generating correct Python functions given docstrings or problem descriptions. Since DeepSeekMath retains its original code training data (~20 % of tokens), it preserves coding competence comparable to DeepSeek‑Coder 7B while gaining additional mathematical insight.

Overall, Table `understanding_reasoning_code` in the paper shows unified gains across all three dimensions: language understanding, reasoning, and coding.

## Ablation Insights: Code Pre‑Training vs. Pure Math Pre‑Training

A central research question addressed by the authors is whether *code pre-training contributes to mathematical reasoning*. To answer this they conduct controlled ablations:

1. **Two–stage pipeline:**  
   *Stage A:* Train on 400 B code tokens → *Stage B:* Fine tune on 150 B math tokens.  
   *Baseline:* Train on 400 B generic text → fine tune on math tokens.

   Results reveal that models receiving explicit code exposure before math training achieve significantly higher performance on both GSM8K/MATH *with* tool use *and* on pure CoT tasks compared with those trained only on generic text first.

2. **One–stage mixture:**  
   Combine code tokens and math tokens into a single training corpus (400 B code + 150 B math). This mitigates catastrophic forgetting observed when code pre-training is followed by pure math training; it also synergistically improves both coding ability and program-assisted math solving.

3. **Effect on non-tool mathematics:**  
   Even without tool use, code pre-training yields modest gains early in training; however when mixed with math tokens in one stage, very small models (1.3 B) may still struggle to fully absorb both modalities simultaneously.

These ablations support the claim made in Section “Contributions → Math Pre-Training at Scale” that *code training indeed improves mathematical reasoning*, especially when followed by or interleaved with extensive math token exposure.

### Summary

DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B demonstrates that a modestly sized model can achieve near–state‐of‐the‐art quantitative reasoning performance when:

* It starts from a strong code foundation (DeepSeek‑Coder).
* It is continually exposed to an unprecedented volume of high-quality webderived mathematics (≈120 B tokens), carefully filtered by fastText classifiers.
* It receives balanced exposure to programming material, scholarly articles, and bilingual natural language text.
* It follows robust optimizer settings—AdamW with aggressive warmup/decay schedules—and processes large token batches over half a trillion tokens total.

Evaluations confirm improvements across pure chain-of-thought solving, program-aided reasoning, formal proof generation, as well as broader linguistic and coding competencies. Ablation studies underscore that *code pre-training plays an essential role* in catalyzing these gains, validating longstanding hypotheses about cross-domain transfer between programming and mathematical reasoning.
