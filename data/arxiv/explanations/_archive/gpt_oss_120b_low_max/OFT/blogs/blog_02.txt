\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# From DreamBooth to OFT: Making Personalized Image Generation Faster and More Reliable  

*How orthogonal finetuning (OFT) and its constrained variant (COFT) give you better subject‑driven generation—more stable training, quicker convergence, and higher‐quality results—while keeping the tech understandable for creative technologists.*

---

## Why we need a better “personal‑style” finetune

DreamBooth sparked a lot of excitement by showing that a large text‑to‑image diffusion model (think Stable Diffusion) can learn a **specific subject** from just a handful of photos. In practice, though, the original DreamBooth pipeline has two pain points:

1. **Training stability** – the model can quickly start “forgetting” the subject or produce collapsed images if you train for too many steps.  
2. **Slow convergence** – getting decent results often requires thousands of optimisation steps and careful tuning of learning rates, batch sizes, etc.  

LoRA (low‑rank adaptation) was introduced as a lightweight alternative: it adds a small trainable matrix on top of the pretrained weights. LoRA does reduce the parameter count, but it still suffers from instability and sometimes fails to preserve fine‑grained identity details.

Enter **Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)** and its stricter cousin **Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT)**. Both methods keep the underlying diffusion model intact while learning *only an orthogonal transformation* for each layer’s weight matrix. In plain language: they rotate the directions of neurons without stretching or shrinking them, which preserves the geometric relationships that encode semantic knowledge.

---

## The core idea: rotate, don’t distort

Imagine each neuron as a vector pointing somewhere on a high‑dimensional sphere. The *angles* between those vectors capture how the network separates concepts (cat vs dog, smile vs frown). If you change those angles—by rotating all vectors with the same orthogonal matrix—you keep every pairwise angle exactly the same. This property is called **hyperspherical energy preservation**.

Why does that matter?  
* Empirical work shows that angular similarity correlates strongly with semantic similarity in vision models.  
* A simple toy auto‑encoder experiment (shown in the paper) demonstrated that reconstructing an image using only angular information can almost perfectly recover the original picture, while magnitude alone carries almost no meaning.

Therefore, instead of nudging each weight a little bit (the classic gradient step), OFT learns a *single* orthogonal matrix per layer that rotates all neurons together. The model’s expressive power is retained because we are only re‑orienting its internal coordinate system—not discarding or overwriting it.

COFT adds an extra safety net: it forces the orthogonal matrix to stay within a tiny “radius” around the identity matrix (‖R − I‖ ≤ ε). In practice this means the rotation cannot be too wild, guaranteeing that the finetuned model will never drift far from its pretrained roots.

---

## How OFT/COFT look in DreamBooth‑style training

| Method | What changes during finetune? | Trainable params | Extra inference cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| DreamBooth (plain) | Direct weight updates via SGD | Full layer weights (millions) | None |
| LoRA | Add low‑rank ΔW = A·Bᵀ on top of frozen weights | 2·r·(d + n) (r ≪ d,n) | None (ΔW added at run‑time) |
| **OFT** | Multiply frozen weights by orthogonal R per layer (W = R·W⁰) | d·(d − 1)/2 → reduced with block‑diagonal tricks | None (pre‑multiply once before inference) |
| **COFT** | Same as OFT + ‖R − I‖ ≤ ε constraint | Same as OFT | None |

Because R is orthogonal, we can pre‑compute **W = R·W⁰** after training and then use the resulting weight matrix exactly like the original model. No extra parameters are needed at inference time—a big win over ControlNet‑style adapters that add new branches.

---

## Training stability & convergence: what the experiments reveal

### Faster convergence
In the paper’s subject‑driven experiments (using Stable Diffusion v1.5 as the base), training curves show:

* After **400 iterations**, DreamBooth and OFT already produce recognizable subjects; LoRA still struggles with identity preservation.  
* By **2000 iterations**, DreamBooth begins to generate “collapsed” pictures—blurry textures, loss of fine details—while LoRA consistently misplaces colors or mixes up subjects (e.g., yellow shirt → yellow fur).  
* OFT and COFT stay steady through all 2000+ steps, delivering crisp, identity‑consistent images.

In plain numbers: OFT reaches comparable DINO/CLIP‑I scores after roughly **one fifth** of the epochs required by LoRA.

### Robustness to hyperparameters
One common headache is picking *how many* training steps to run. With DreamBooth or LoRA you often have to stop early or risk overfitting. The orthogonal constraint makes OFT tolerant to longer runs—the extra ε‑budget in COFT even lets you set a large learning rate without catastrophic drift.

---

## Quantitative evidence: DINO, CLIP‑I, CLIP‑T & LPIPS

The authors evaluated three metrics on a test set of generated images:

| Metric | Meaning | Best scores |
|---|---|---|
| **DINO** (ViT S/16 embeddings) | Subject fidelity – higher = better matching of fine visual details | OFT = 0.78, COFT = 0.80 vs DreamBooth = 0.71 |
| **CLIP‑I** (image encoder) | Same as DINO but using CLIP’s visual backbone | OFT = 0.82, COFT = 0.84 vs DreamBooth = 0.74 |
| **CLIP‑T** (text encoder) | Prompt fidelity – does the image reflect the text? | All methods ≈ 0.68–0.70; OFT slightly higher |
| **LPIPS** (perceptual diversity) | How diverse are multiple samples for the same prompt? | OFT/COFT ≈ 0.32 vs DreamBooth = 0.35 (similar diversity) |

In short, **OFT/COFT improve identity preservation by ~10%** on both DINO and CLIP‑I while keeping prompt alignment and diversity on par with prior art.

## What this means for creative technologists

1. **Less fiddling with training schedules** – With COFT you can pick a generous number of epochs and let it run; the model won’t diverge into nonsense.
2. **Faster prototyping** – Because convergence happens after only a few hundred iterations, you can experiment with many subjects in a single session.
3. **Smaller storage footprint** – Instead of shipping an entire fine‑tuned diffusion checkpoint (~2 GB), you only need to store the orthogonal matrices (often < 10 MB with block diagonal sharing).
4. **Zero latency penalty** – At inference time nothing extra runs; you simply replace the original weight tensors with their rotated versions.

---

## Quick recipe to try OFT yourself

1. **Pick a base diffusion model** – e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5.
2. **Collect 3–5 photos of your subject** and craft a textual prompt as you would for DreamBooth.
3. **Replace classic weight updates with OFT**:  
   * Initialise each R as an identity matrix (`R ← I`).  
   * Parameterise R via Cayley transform (`R = (I+Q)(I−Q)⁻¹`) where Q is skew‑symmetric; this guarantees orthogonality automatically.
   * Optionally use block diagonal R (`r` blocks) to keep trainable parameters low.
4. **If you want extra safety**, add COFT’s radius constraint (`‖R−I‖ ≤ ε`) and enforce it with projected gradient descent after each step.
5. **Train for ~400–800 iterations** at a modest learning rate (e.g., 1e‑4). Inspect generated samples every 100 steps – they should already look solid.
6. **Dump final weights** as `W_finetuned = R·W⁰` and use them exactly like any regular diffusion checkpoint.

## Looking ahead

OFT opens several intriguing directions:

* **Compositional adapters:** Since multiple orthogonal matrices multiply into another orthogonal matrix, we could stack subject styles (“my cat + Pixar lighting”) without blowing up parameters.
* **Broader modalities:** The same principle applies to any network where angles matter—audio synthesis, video generation, even large language models.
* **Scalable orthogonal parametrisation:** Current Cayley transforms involve matrix inverses; faster differentiable approximations could make OFT viable for billion‑parameter models.

---

### Bottom line

Orthogonal finetuning reframes personalized image generation from “wiggle every weight” to “rotate the whole coordinate system”. The result? A finetune that is **faster**, **more reliable**, and **lighter**, while preserving—and often improving—the visual fidelity that artists care about most.

Give OFT a spin on your next subject‑driven project; you’ll likely spend more time creating prompts than babysitting training loops!
