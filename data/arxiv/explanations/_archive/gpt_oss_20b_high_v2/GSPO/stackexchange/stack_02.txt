A language model produces token probabilities typically ranging around ${𝑝}\\in[0\\.01,.001].\
Because these quantities multiply over many tokens, even small biases amplify—and products become astronomically large or tiny:

* For a 10↦token reply with per‑token ratios ≈ 𝑏𝑎𝑐𝑘𝑏𝑧 ≈ ${0\\.98}$,

Unconstrained ratios therefore send very long responses far outside usable numerical ranges—forcing either very tight clipping bounds (leading most gradients discarded) or exploding/vanishing gradients themselves.
