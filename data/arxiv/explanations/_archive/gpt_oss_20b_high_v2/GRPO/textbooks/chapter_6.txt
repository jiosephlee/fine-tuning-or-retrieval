# Curated Instruction Dataset Composition  

The transition from a plain pre‑trained language model (the “Base” stage) to an instruction‑aware system (“Instruct” stage) relies primarily on the quality and diversity of the supervision data supplied during fine‑tuning. For DeepSeekMath – Instruct 7 B we assembled a deliberately heterogeneous collection totaling approximately \(776\,000\) carefully hand‑crafted example pairs that span two languages (English and Chinese) and three different solution stylistic templates –chain–of–thought (CoT), program–of–thought (PoT), and tool–integrated reasoning (TIR).  

**English Contributions**  
The bulk of our English demonstrations arises from three established tertiary sources:

1. *GSM8K* ([GeSample Math Solutions](https://github.com/openai/gsm8k)).  
   Each maths problem was repurposed into a dual–style pair sending both a natural language CoT response *and* a succinct pythonic TIR solution employing standard libraries (`math`, `sympy`).  
2. *MATH* ([competition level math benchmarks](https://github.com/chengqa/qant)).  
   Similar hand‑annotated solutions were generated directly using Express language templates that allow direct recursion through intermediate algebraic derivations before producing an answer token sequence suitable for scoring methods employed later during reinforcement learning experimentation.  
3. *Miscellaneous Instructional Corpora* drawn from subsets of *MathInstruct* and *Lila‑OOD*. Both corpus sets typically contain semi–automatically produced self‑contained explanations that were curated further so each example could be interpreted either as an elaborative CoT narrative or transcribed into concise PoT code – thereby enriching our stack toward solving multi‑step arithmetic quickly through programmatic insight while preserving human interpretable justification patterns held within CoTs.

Each English problem thus exists in two variants – one featuring natural‐language deliberation culminating in a final numeric answer, another embedding procedural code snippets designed explicitly for programmatic execution according to the schema described in Section III.B § process supervisions back‐end reward modeling approaches developing in later reinforcement iterations.
