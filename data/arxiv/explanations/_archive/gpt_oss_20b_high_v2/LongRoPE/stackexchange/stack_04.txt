Answer:
### How Eq.(3) works – a concrete walk‑through

Recall that RoPE turns a scalar position $(n)$ into a pair of cosine‑sine vectors whose phase grows linearly with $(n)$.
For the $(k^\text{th})$ pair $(k=i)$

$$
(\cos(\phi_k(n)),\,\sin(\phi_k(n))), 
   \qquad   
   \phi_k(n)=\frac{n}{\beta^{\,k}},
$$

where  

$\displaystyle 
    \beta=\theta^{\,2/d}
$
is a constant that depends only on the transformer’s embedding size $d$, and  

$\;\theta\simeq10\,000$
is the default “base frequency’’ used in RoPE embeddings in almost every LLM today.

#### Numerical illustration


Let us plug everything into concrete numbers :

* **Embedding size** – take an arbitrary large $d$;
it only affects how fast the denominator grows as $k$
increases, so it’s irrelevant for looking at one single  
dimension $(i=k=0)$.
* **Extended‑length ratio** – let $s=4`.
* **Base value** – stay with paper’s standard choice  
 ${\:[\nu]}=\;\theta^{-2 k/d}$,
so ++with any plausible choice of $d$, $\nu=\theta^{-\,{\delta}}\sim10^{-x}$;
again its numerical magnitude does **not**
influence our illustration because it cancels when $i=k=0$. 

Now imagine LongRoPE chose **for this very first pair**
a rescaling headed optimistic number  
$\;\lambda_{f}=tfrac32$, i.e.\  

$n_{\max}=256$, 
    
$\gamma:=\<-\!\!\cong\fbox{$<\/$\div>} \$?$::~$

> This means that until position 256 every token receives exactly


those cosine‑and‑sinusoids it would have gotten during pre‑training.
From $n ≥256$ every phase squeezes towards zero because it gets multiplied by 
 $\gamma=\dfrac１}{λ_f}<１.$ Concretely this happens element‑wise
inside every hidden‑state vector –
later coefficients start “moving‐slower’’ along the unit circle compared
with early coefficients,
stretching out how many distinct rotations fit inside an enormously long sequence.

#### Why two constants matter

* **$(λ_K≠ s)$**    
 The classical linear interpolation method applies one single scale–down fraction  
 to all dimensions uniformly $(〈λ〉={s})$.     
LongRoPE instead maps each dimension $i↦λ_i$
and feeds it back through  

$$φ_i←φ_i×√〈γ〉,$$

with monotonicity enforced (higher $i→larger λ_i').  
Everything ensures low-frequency dimensions still cover their full “phase interval’’ even when millions of positions are squeezed into it –
whereas high-frequency dimensions do not become crowded early on.



* **The special breakpoint $(ˆn)$**    
 Keeping a chunk untouched preserves precisely those positional cues most heavily attended during training –
early parts influence attention differently from very far future positions.
Setting moderate nonzero breakpoint holds back their interference until truly needed.
