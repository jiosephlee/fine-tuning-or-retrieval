# Evolution Meets Embeddings – Searching For the Best Angle Rescales  

Large‑language models (LLMs) usually win stories because they can see many tokens at once—yet their *context window* is still usually capped around **4 000** tokens for LLaMA‑2 or **8 000** for newer variants.  
Extending this window requires more than just tossing in longer prompt text; every new position forces the model’s *rotary* positional embeddings—called Rotated Position‑Encoding (RoPe)—into regions it never saw during pre‑training and causes catastrophic loss spikes (“out‑of‑distribution” tokens).  

In *LongRoPE*, the authors discovered that a smarter way of “down‑scaling” rotation angles can leapt LLMs out of this hole without any fancy architectural changes—just an evolutionary filter that hunts per‑dimension rescaling knobs called **angle rescales** (\(\lambda_i\)).  
Below is a walk‑through of that search engine—how it trims an impossible combinatorial garden into a handful of practical solutions that preserve critical information while keeping computation light.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Core Problem – Why Uniform Interpolation Fails  

Rotary Positional Embedding writes the positional signal into a pair of sinusoids for every hidden dimension \(d\):

\[
\text{RoPe}_{n}^{(i)} = \bigl[\cos(n\theta_{i}),\,\sin(n\theta_{i})\bigr], \qquad
\theta_{i}= \beta^{-\,2i/d}\;\text{with}\;\beta =10000 .
\]

When we need a longer context (\(L' > L\)) we “interpolate” the angle:
\[
n \;\rightarrow\;\frac{\,n\,}{\lambda^{\,i}} ,
\]
where \(s=L'/L\) guides how big \(\lambda\) is expected to be – typically \(\lambda=s\) (**linear PI**) or something more complex as proposed by **NTK**/**YaRN** groupings.



**Why does one size not fit all?**

| Observation | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|
| Low‑frequency dimensions hold coarse structural cues | Smoothing them too much blows up higher frequency signals |
| Very first tokens capture most attention | Interpolating their angles hurts too much |
| Very long extensions (*> 100×*) amplify any imbalance | Standard interpolants hit catastrophic spikes early |

Thus we need two independent knobs:
* per‑dimension scaling \(\lambda_i\),
* a token‑position threshold \(\hat n\) after which scaling ceases.


--------------------------------------------------------------------

## From Exponential Brute Force To Evolutionary Search  

**The brute‑force paradise:**  
For an \(s=4\times\) expansion on a model with \(d/2=64\) rotary pairs you’d have  
\(400^{64}\times14~>~10^{167}\) possibilities!  
Clearly exhaustive trials are impossible—even if you had unlimited GPUs.



**The clever compromise:**  
Treat each candidate set \(\{\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{63},\,\hat n\}\) as an individual in a *population*, evaluate its quality via short **perplexity** runs on five sample books from PG19, keep only the best ones as parents, mutating them under constraints that keep candidates valid forever.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🔍 What Each Individual Looks Like  

* **Per‑dimension scale \(\lambda_i \in [1,\;s\times1.25]\)**  
     * Lower bound means *no* interpolation (“direct extrapolation”)  
     * Upper bound slightly relaxes PI by letting some angles stretch beyond naïve factor \(s\)

* **Token cutoff \(\hat n \in \{0,\,1,\dots,\!256\}\)**  
     * Tokens before this point retain their original angles
     * Tokens after continue with scaled angles


With a step size of \(0.01\), each dimension alone presents over \(125\times s\times12500=\!…!\!\) grids—therefore we keep only ~64 individuals per generation.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🧬 Anatomy Of The Search Algorithm  

<p>This recipe follows roughly Algorithm 1 in the paper but glossed down into concrete commands.</p>

| Stage | What Happens | Why It Helps |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| **Initialization** | • Create \(P=64\) individuals.<br>• Insert three hand‑crafted starters – linear PI (\(\lambda=s,\;\hat n=0\)), NTK (\(\lambda=s^{i/d}\)), YaRN (grouping based).<br>• Mutate each starter randomly with probability \(p=0.3.\)<br>• All mutated vectors clipped eastward if outside bounds.<br>• All individuals interfere well‐formed constraint from next row.| Kickstarts near promising corners of space so evolution doesn’t wander randomly past useful regimes. |
| **Evaluation** | Run LLaMA/Mistral forward passes on five PG19 validation slices whose lengths equal target \(L'\).<br>Record perplexity per individual.| Gives us a metric directly linked to learning quality over long prompts without any gradient updates – cheap compared to full finetune cycles.|

| **Pruning & Parent Selection** | Keep top‑\(k=\phi=32\) individuals—those with lowest perplexities.| Uses confidence measure itself; best candidates definitely worth expanding upon.|

| **Mutation & Crossover** | • For each parent select two random siblings.<br>• Mutate each dimension independently by adding Gaussian noise scaled against interval size.<br>• Apply simple one‑point crossover between pairs.<br>• After changes enforce <u>monotone slope constraint</u>: \( \lambda_0 \leqslant \lambda_1 \leqslant ... \leqslant \lambda_{63}.\)| Monotonicity mirrors neural–tangent kernel intuition: low‑frequency (> high \(i)\) dimensions can tolerate more stretching than high-frequency ones (< low \(i)\).<br>This massive pruning eliminates huge swaths where any missing trend would push positions far apart and blow up loss—saving millions byte computations.|

| **Iteration Loop** | Repeat evaluation → pruning → mutation/crossover → new generation until budget ⅄ T≃40 iterations.| Fast because evaluation runs are tiny slices; overall cost is usually <10 k GPU–seconds – far less than an hours’ worth you would spend finetuning a whole LLaMA on millions tokens!|

After termination pick the single best individual—the one leading to globally lowest sparse series perplexity.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

### ⚡️Why It Boils Down To Upgrades We Care About   

* **No retraining needed:**  
   Once searched rescales are baked into RoPe tensors you already have an *already extended* model—a simple read‑only parameter update no gradients required.*

* **Large extensions without extra data:**  
   Because the evolver searches online using only very short PG19 scraps it sidesteps any scarcity in genuine ‘million‑token’ corpora.*

* **Fine‑tune friendliness:**  
   Same rescales give a solid starting point when you decide later – say after reaching ∼256 k length – you’ll only need ~1 000 gradient steps versus many megabyte epochs.*

* **Adaptive recovery at short windows:**  
   At inference time you simply switch–off coarse interpolation again if your prompt shrinks below ~8 k tokens – no extra computation involved.*

In practice these innovators made it possible for LLaMA‐2‐7B(x86?) and ‑Mistral‐7B reach performance stable through **2048 000 tokens**, while retaining comparable zero/few-shot behavior in ≤4 096 contexts.

## Take‑away Insights ✨  

1. **Non-uniform rescaling beats “one‑size‐fits‐all.”**   
   Even modest deviations from linear PI let some low-frequency rotary pairs retain grittier detail while high-frequency ones get looser spacing—a tune your base model was trained thirsty for!

2. **Monotone ordering isn’t cosmetic—it culls bad candidates en masse**, turning an astronomically huge combinatorics problem into an inexpensive genomic hill‑climb whereby every successful child obeys PTNN logic automatically.

3. **Evolving just logits yields global gains:** Eleven seconds on tiny validation batches outcompete days’ worth of unsupervised backprop over tens of GB data!

Feel free to drop by https://github.com/microsoft/LongRoPE once its public release drops—the community will find it trivial enough And then start feeding lifelong “books” into your GenAI pipeline without running off topic—and see how your model keeps hitting people’s heads quite literally even when those heads were built word by word far away.
