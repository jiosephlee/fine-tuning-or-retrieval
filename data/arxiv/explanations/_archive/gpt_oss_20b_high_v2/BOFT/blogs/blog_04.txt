# Why Preserving Spectral Norms Matters – The Hidden Advantage Of Orthogonality In Fine‑Tuning

Fine‑tuning a frozen foundation model usually feels like fishing in a vast sea of parameters – you want your cast precisely where it hits target tasks without pulling you off course vis‑à‑vis the enormous pre‑training portfolio underneath you.  
Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), and its recent refinement called Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (**BOFT**, § 3), address this issue by multiplying every layer’s weights \(W^{0}\) by a *single* learnable orthogonal matrix \(R\).  
Because \(R\) remains strictly unitary throughout colourful optimisation dares us to keep most structural qualities of \(W^{0}\).  
The most decisive consequence is that every *singular value* of \(W^{0}\) – nothing more than its spectrum’s peak – stays exactly unchanged along the entire training path.  
Below we unpack why this seems trivial yet unlocks substantial practical advantages for model precision and stability.



## Spectral Norm Survives Every Update

Let us write a linear layer’s frozen weights as  

\[
W^{0}=U\,\Sigma\,V^{\top},
\]

where \(U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) are orthogonal bases and \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{d})\) contains all non–negative **singular values** \(\sigma_{i}\).  
A *spectral norm* refers precisely to \(\|W^{0}\|_{2}=\max_i\sigma_{i}\).

In OFT/BOFT each forward pass privately multiplies these weights by  

\[
R\,W^{0}=R\,U\,\Sigma\,V^{\top}.
\]

Because \(R\in O(d)\), i.e., \(R^{\top}R=RR^{\top}=I_d\), left–multiplication does **not alter** \(\Sigma\); effectively it only rotates the column basis \(U \mapsto RU.\) Consequently,

\[
(R\,U)\,\Sigma\,V^{\top}
=\tilde U\,\Sigma\,V^{\top}
= \text{(new basis)}\,(\text{unchanged spectrum}) .
\]

Hence all sparse bits propagate faithfully through each layer:
– every layer preserves its maximum activation magnitude,
– gradients flowing back through those layers inherit the same bounded scaling as if nothing had changed.



## Stable Gradients and Training Trajectories

When a model’s weights borrow arbitrary directions (as in an *additive* low‑rank update such as **LoRA**) their spectra can shift wildly – some eigenvalues spiking while others become tiny.  
This induces two well‑known problems:

1. **Exploding / vanishing gradients:** multiplicative factors amplify or dampen error signals unpredictably,
2. **Shifted activation spread:** hidden units suddenly operate in disjoint regimes causing optimisation stalls.

By guaranteeing \(\|R\,W^{0}\|_{2}=\|W^{0}\|_{2}\) at every step, BOFT keeps these forces balanced until data‑driven forces explicitly require deviation under controlled angular deformation only — essentially forcing *rotations*, never scales or shearings — and thereby preventing dramatic outbreaks or collapses.



## Simpler Regularisation Landscape

Because we never touch the eigenvalue spectrum, typical penalisations meant for unbounded growth—like explicit weight decay or spectral normalisation—become redundant.  
Instead one needs only regularise rotation *angles*.  
Easier constraints translate into smoother optimisation surfaces:
starting right “on” next‑to‐identity \(R=I_d+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\), gradients adapt through tiny skew‑symmetric updates (\(Q=-Q^\top\)), thus leading straight toward optimal orientations without accidentally scaling activations.



## Learning Bilinear Similarities Under Hard Rotations

If one looks more closely at each neuron’s kernel \(w_k^0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\), any activation becomes  

\(w_k^\top x=(w_k^0)^{\top}(Rx)\).

So mathematically <span style="font-style:italic">“boosting”</span> presumes computing cosine similarities between transformed input vectors—and because measured angles are preserved (`both rotation`s preserve inner products)—we’re essentially solving a *bilinear similarity* learning problem subject to a rigid rotation constraint on the `feature map`.  
This matches motivations studied broadly under metric learning frameworks ([Studying distance‐based embeddings](https://arxiv.org/) often pursue similar hard constraints), suggesting why BOFT enjoys robust performance across Vision Transformers (ViTs), large language bifurcations (e.g., GPT variants), & even diffusion renderers.



## Butterfly Structure = Structured However Dense Rotations

Entangling these rotations does not come for free—orthogonal matrices aren’t inherently sparse enough for wide adoption once sizes grow beyond hundreds of thousands of dimensions.  
BOFT resolves this by decomposing any dense rotation into a product of \(\mathcal{O}(\log d)\) sparse “butterfly” factors:

```
          ──> ──> ──>
         A1    A2    …
          │     │    
          │     │    
          ▼     ▼    
          B1    B2    
```

Each factor contains roughly `b` trainable parameters per row (when `b`=2 they’re mere rotations among four neurons).  
Thus total effective trainable knobs amount only  
`½(b−1)d m`, where `m≈log₂d`.  

Comparatively:
 – Full OFT requires `O(d²)` trainable bits;
 – Block‑diagonal OFT demands `O(bd)` knobs;
 – BOFT lands comfortably at `O(d log d)` — a dramatic win.




### What do those “butterfly” factors actually do?

They echo the Cooley‑Tukey Fast Fourier Transform network topology – nodes succeedively exchange information in logarithmic layers while keeping sparsity intact (`O(d log d)` edges instead of `O(d²)` FULL connectivity).  
Because sparsity patterns exactly mirror those efficient transforms, BOFT inheres an *implicitly* desirable inductive bias reminiscent of classical signal processing primitives such as FFT or Hadamard transforms—something block‑diagonal schemes cannot emulate directly.



## Practical Take‑Aways for Deployments

| Task | How BOFT helps |
|------|----------------|
| **Vision Transformers** | Preserve layer norms → fewer exploding hidden tiles when fine‑tune on classification datasets |
| **Large Language Models** | Angles kept → stable perplexity drops while adjusting token embeddings without overfitting |
| **Diffusion Models** | Dictates sharp yet smoothly varying pixel denoising when transferring styles |

Other peripheral perks chime nicely:
• **Zero inference latency:** after finishing fine‑tune you fuse learned rotations back into original matrices – inference simply runs through reparameterised weights,
• **Multiplicative Dropout:** set selected butterfly components back temporarily into identity during certain epochs → reduces overfitting while staying inside rotational manifold,
• **Expressiveness outpaces vanilla block diagonal cases:** ablation studies show smaller `b` together with larger `m=log₂d` achieve better approximations vs larger blocks without incurring extra knobs … what truly matters is *structured richness* rather than sheer parameter count.



### Remaining Challenges & Future Directions

Though extremely lightweight relative to full fine‑tune,
each layer still performs multiple sparse matrix products per forward/backward pass,
so BOFT’s wall time costs sit slightly above vanilla OFT one‐step updates—
an aspect ripe for optimisation (specialised GPU kernels200?).  

Also question still stands whether butterflies are optimal topology for information exchange –
perhaps drawing deeper insights from computer-network routing could inspire even leaner yet more expressive factorizations.



## Concluding Thought

Keeping spectra intact turns out one sliver transformation into an elegant blueprint that systematically protects gradients, simplify regularization logic, and gracefully convolves pretrained knowledge into downstream intents—all with far less computational plumbing than conventional PEFT tricks.  
When designing adapters or fine‑tunes today,
remembering that “every learned weight remains \(R W^{0}\)” tightens your model’s geometry just enough so it stays true—angles kept fixed—yet moves smartly enough so your downstream task sees its promised gains.
