\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

Chapter 3: Orthogonality‑Based Finetuning Principles

# Definition \(W'=RW_0\) with \(R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) Orthogonal  
In Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), a pretrained linear layer represented by its weight matrix \(W_0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\) is reparameterised during downstream adaptation as  
\[
W' \;=\; R\,W_0,
\]
where \(R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) is an orthogonal matrix satisfying \(R^{\top}R=RR^{\top}=I_d.\)  The forward computation of a single layer thus changes from  
\(z=(W_0)^\top x\) to  
\(z=(RW_0)^\top x,\)  
with input vector \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\) and output vector \(z\in \mathbb{R}^n.\)  Since every column of \(W'\) is simply a rotated version of the corresponding column of \(W_0,\) the learning process now consists solely of determining how best to rotate all neurons within a given layer so that their collective behaviour satisfies new task objectives while preserving correlations present in pretraining data.  Crucially, every neuron shares the same transformation matrix \(R,\) guaranteeing a uniform, global relabelling of dimension space rather than independent parameter updates per neuron.

# Zero Initialization via Identity  
A central goal in parameter‑efficient finetuning is to keep early training dynamics stable and close to the pretrained baseline so that task‑specific signals are added rather than overwhelming learned representations.  In OFT this optically intuitive requirement is satisfied by initializing \(R\) exactly as an identity matrix:
\[
R \;\coloneqq\; I_d.
\]
With this choice, at epoch zero the adapted weights equal their pretrained counterparts exactly (\(W'=W_0\)), meaning no change whatsoever has occurred yet and all pairwise neuronal relationships remain identical.  Subsequent gradient steps gradually deform an initially flat manifold—with each deformation restricted to remain within the set of valid orthogonal matrices—thus preserving pre‑existing knowledge while allowing modest expressive adjustments suited for downstream tasks.



# Cayley Parametrisation  
Maintaining exact orthogonality during optimization requires a parameterisation that automatically enforces it throughout training iterations without imposing expensive explicit constraints at every step.  OFT adopts the Cayley transform,
which writes any special orthogonal matrix as
\[
R \;=\; (I+Q)\,(I-Q)^{-1},
\]
where \(Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) is skew‑symmetric (\(Q=-Q^\top\)).  This construction has two elegant properties:

1. **Every resulting \(R\)** satisfies \(R^\top R=RR^\top=I_d,\)** since Cayley’s map sends skew‑symmetric matrices bijectively onto all rotation matrices except reflections by det = –1 (which remain excluded if required).

2. **The parameters to be optimised** reduce to the independent components of \(Q,\)** whose dimensionality is \(\tfrac12\,d(d-1)\).  Updating these free parameters using standard gradient‑descent automatically guarantees that every intermediate choice stays on an exact orthogonal manifold.

Zero initialization translates into setting all entries of \(Q\) initially to zero – precisely correspond­ing to an identity transformation – after which optimisers can perturb this point while always respecting orthogonality.



# Angle Preservation Guarantees  
Orthogonal transformations possess an exceptionally clean geometric interpretation: they are composed solely of rotations and reflections which preserve Euclidean distances and triangle angles within any vector space they act upon.  Consequently for any two columns \(\mathbf{w}_i^0,\mathbf{w}_j^0\) of the pretrained weight matrix,
their inner product remains untouched under application of any orthogonal matrix:
\[
(\mathbf{w}_i^{'})^\top \mathbf{w}_j^{'}  
= (\!\,RW_0)_i^\top(\!\,RW_0)_j
= (\!\mathbf{w}_i^{0})^\top R^\top R\,(\mathbf{w}_j^{0})
= (\!\mathbf{w}_i^{0})^\top(\mathbf{w}_j^{0}). 
\]
The last equality follows directly from \(R^\top R = I_d.\)

Because inner products uniquely determine angles between vectors—a fact given by
\( \cos \theta_{ij}=\frac{\langle \mathbf{w}_i,\mathbf{w}_j\rangle}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\rVert\,\|\mathbf{w}_j\rVert }, \)
it follows immediately that every pair of neurons keeps exactly its original angle throughout OFT training:
\( \theta_{ij}'=\theta_{ij}. \)

Preserving these inter‑neuron geometries affords two practical benefits exploited by OFT:

1. **Retention of Semantic Knowledge:** Features extracted by neurons in earlier layers are often highly correlated or complementary tasks derived from pretraining data distribution patterns (such as language syntax or visual texture). Maintaining their mutual relations prevents catastrophic forgetting and keeps high‑level abstract knowledge intact while still letting overall representation shift globally under rotation.
   
2. **Spectral Stability:** Because rotations leave singular values invariant, spectral norms—critical for guarding against exploding or vanishing gradients—are preserved exactly when left‑multiplying by \(R.\)  This contributes markedly towards smoother optimisation trajectories compared with additive finite‑rank updates employed by other parameter‑efficient approaches such as LoRA.


In summary, Orthogonal Finetuning harnesses a planted symmetry—rigid rotations—to subtly reorient entire weight layers so downstream objectives drive learning logically far enough so new patterns emerge but never disturb relationships learned in pretraining phases—an approach grounded firmly in simple Euclidean geometry yet powerful enough to elevate finite‑rank methods into genuinely expressive finetuners consistent with architecture sizes defined in large foundation models.
