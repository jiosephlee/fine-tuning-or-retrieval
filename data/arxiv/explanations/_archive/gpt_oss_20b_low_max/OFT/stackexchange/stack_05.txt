\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why is re‑scaled OFT more flexible yet still preserves hyperspherical energy?
Question:
Section 3.5 adds a learnable diagonal matrix D to scale neuron magnitudes: z = (R·W⁰·D)^⊤x. Since scaling doesn't affect HE, why does this addition help performance? Does it effectively allow per‑neuron temperature adjustments that enable learning of residual magnitudes while keeping angles invariant? Is there an intuitive explanation for why including D improves image quality without breaking the hyperspherical preservation guarantee?
Answer:
**Short answer**

Adding a diagonal scaling matrix \(D\) does not change the hyperspherical energy because the energy is computed from *normalised* neurons.  
The scaling gives each neuron a learnable length so that the model can adjust the *magnitude* of feature contributions while keeping all pair‑wise angles (hence the energy) fixed.  
This extra flexibility lets the network tune how strongly each direction is used, which improves image quality without violating the orthogonal‑preservation guarantee.

---

### 1. Why hyperspherical energy is unchanged by a diagonal scale

Hyperspherical energy (HE) of a weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}\) is

\[
\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}) \;=\;\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j\|^{-1},
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i \;=\;\frac{\mathbf{w}_i}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\|}\!,
\]

so it depends only on the *directions* of the neurons, not on their lengths.  
If we multiply every neuron by a positive scalar \(s_i\),

\[
\mathbf{w}_i' = s_i\, \mathbf{w}_i,
\]

then \(\hat{\mathbf{w}}'_i = \hat{\mathbf{w}}_i\) and therefore
\(\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}')=\text{HE}(\mathbf{W})\).  
Hence a diagonal matrix \(D=\mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n)\) leaves HE unchanged.

---

### 2. What does the extra scale do for finetuning?

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) learns a single orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf R\) per layer:

\[
z = (\mathbf R\,\mathbf W^0)^{\!\top}\!x ,
\quad
\mathbf R^{\!\top}\!\mathbf R = \mathbf I .
\]

This preserves all pair‑wise angles, so the “semantic geometry” encoded in the pretrained weights is untouched.  
However, after an orthogonal rotation some neurons may be more useful for the downstream task than others; their *magnitude* in the forward pass determines how much influence they have on the next layer.

By allowing a per‑neuron scale \(s_i>0\), OFT learns

\[
z = (\mathbf R\, \mathbf W^0\, D)^{\!\top}x ,
\quad
D = \mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n).
\]

Each \(s_i\) can grow or shrink independently while all angles remain exactly what they were after rotation.  
In effect, we add a **temperature‑like** factor to each neuron: neurons whose directions become favourable for the new task can be amplified; those that are less useful can be dampened.  This gives OFT a modest but powerful extra degree of freedom without destroying the guarantee that pair‑wise similarities stay put.

---

### 3. Intuitive picture: phase vs magnitude

Section 3.5 of the paper draws an analogy to Fourier analysis: the *phase* (direction) carries most of the semantic structure, while modifying *magnitude* changes stylisation or intensity.  
OFT’s orthogonal step changes phase; re‑scaled OFT also adjusts magnitude locally, much like tuning per‑filter gains in a convolutional network.  The combination lets us perform strong style or control modifications while staying on the same hypersphere surface.

---

### 4. Why it improves image quality

Empirically (Appendix E), re‑scaled OFT consistently yields higher CLIP‑I / DINO scores and better control consistency than plain OFT or LoRA:

- **Higher fidelity** – because scaling lets neurons emphasise subtle semantic cues that were previously muted.
- **Better regularisation** – HE stays fixed, so we avoid catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge.
- **Stable optimisation** – optimisation landscape is smoother: we’re still moving on a manifold but with an extra radial direction that can be adjusted more cheaply than changing angles.

In short, scaling grants a cheap way to modulate how much each learned direction participates in synthesis, enabling finer control over style and content while keeping semantic structure intact.

---

### 5. Summary

Re‑scaled OFT adds a learnable diagonal matrix \(D\).  Scaling does not affect hyperspherical energy because HE depends only on normalised neurons.  The additional degrees of freedom let each neuron adjust its contribution strength, akin to per‑neuron temperatures or gains, which improves controllability and visual fidelity.  Meanwhile, pairwise angular relationships—and thus the preserved semantic geometry—remain untouched, so OFT’s core theoretical guarantee is maintained.
