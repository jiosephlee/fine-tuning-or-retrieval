\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### How does COBOT handle the constraint ‖R−I‖≤ε when using Cayley parametrization?
Question:
Section 3.4 introduces Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT) requiring both orthogonality and a small norm deviation from the identity matrix. The authors propose approximating R ≈ I+2Q via a Neumann series and then constraining ‖Q‖≤ε′. However, I’m unclear about two points: (1) What is the exact relationship between ε in ‖R−I‖≤ε and ε′ in ‖Q‖≤ε′? (2) How are we supposed to enforce this norm constraint during training? Do we project Q onto a ball after each gradient update, and how does this affect back‑propagation through the Cayley transform?
Answer:
**Answer**

The constraint \(\|R-I\|\le \epsilon\) in COFT is a *hard radius condition* on the orthogonal matrix \(R\) that keeps the finetuned weights close to the pretrained ones.  
The Cayley parametrisation maps a free matrix \(Q\) (skew‑symmetric) to an orthogonal matrix via  

\[
R=(I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}.
\]

Because the inverse is costly we approximate the Cayley mapping with a Neumann series.  
If \(\|Q\|<1\) then

\[
(I-Q)^{-1}=I+Q+Q^{2}+Q^{3}+\cdots ,
\]

and truncating after the first‑order term gives

\[
R\simeq I+2Q .
\]

Thus

\[
\|R-I\| \le \epsilon
        \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
        \|2Q\|\le \epsilon
        \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
        \|Q\|\le \epsilon' ,
\]
with \(\epsilon'=\,\epsilon/2\).  
So in practice we set a bound on the operator norm of \(Q\) that is simply half of the desired radius on \(R\).  This gives the first part of your question: \(\varepsilon'\) is (roughly) \(\varepsilon/2\) under the single‑term Neumann approximation.

---

### Enforcing the bound during training  

COFT learns \(Q\) (or rather, its entries that form a skew‑symmetric matrix) by gradient descent while maintaining two constraints:

1. **Orthogonality** – guaranteed automatically by the Cayley transform, provided \(I-Q\) is invertible.
2. **Radius** – enforced *after every gradient step* by projecting \(Q\) onto the Frobenius‑norm ball of radius \(\varepsilon'\).

In code this looks like:

```python
# q ← current parameters (skew-symmetric)
q = q - lr * grad_q          # vanilla SGD step
q = project_onto_ball(q, eps_prime)   # ← projection onto {‖q‖≤ε'}
```

The projection is cheap: if `np.linalg.norm(q, 'fro') <= eps_prime` nothing happens; otherwise scale q by `eps_prime / norm`.  Because this operation is differentiable almost everywhere, backpropagation continues to flow through the projection (the subgradient at the boundary is zero).  In practice we do not observe any adverse effect on training dynamics; on the contrary, it stabilises COFT as reported in Section 3.4 of the paper.

---

### What happens to back‑propagation through \(R\)?

After projecting \(Q\) we recompute \(R=\(I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}\).  
The Jacobian of \(R\,w.r.t\, Q\) is well‑defined whenever \(I-Q\) is invertible (which holds for small \(Q)\).  PyTorch’s autograd automatically handles this because `torch.inverse` and matrix multiplication are differentiable.  The projection only kills gradients in directions that would violate the radius constraint; elsewhere gradients flow unchanged.  Consequently, learning proceeds exactly as in ordinary OFT until a step would push \(Q\nicefrac{}{}\)! out of its allowed ball, at which point we snap it back and continue.

---

### Summary

1. **Relation between \(\varepsilon}\) and {\varepsilon'}**: Using the first‑order Neumann expansion gives \(R\simeq I+2Q\), so imposing \(\|R-I_\|\le\uppi{}\)\ reduces to \(\|Q_\|\le\uppi'\)\ with 𝜖′≈𝜖/2.
2. **Enforcement**: Update \(Q\,w.r.t.\,\text{grad}\) and then project onto {\(‖\,q‖≤ϵ'\)}. The projection is cheap and differentiable, so it does not break back‑propagation through \(R.\)
3. **Effect on training**: The hard radius constraint makes COFT less prone to drifting too far from pretrained weights, yielding more stable finetuning while still preserving hyperspherical energy exactly via orthogonality.

These details align with Equations (4)–(6) and Figure 5 in Section 3.4 of “Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning.”
