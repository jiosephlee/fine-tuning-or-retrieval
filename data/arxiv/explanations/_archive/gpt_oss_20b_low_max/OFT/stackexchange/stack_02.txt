### How exactly does an orthogonal matrix preserve pairwise cosine similarity?
Question:
Equation (2) in the paper states that if W = R · W^0 with R orthogonal, then HE(W) = HE(W^0). This relies on the fact that multiplying all neurons by a common orthogonal matrix keeps pairwise angles unchanged. Could someone provide a detailed mathematical derivation showing how 
\(\hat{w}_i^T \hat{w}_j = (R\hat{w}_i^0)^T(R\hat{w}_j^0)=\hat{w}_i^{0T}\hat{w}_j^0\) holds, and also explain what happens if R has determinant -1 (a reflection)? Does the sign change affect any result?
Answer:
**Answer**

In the paper the authors say that if we take a weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}^{0}\) of a fully‑connected (or linear) layer and multiply it on the left by an orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf{R}\), i.e.

\[
\mathbf{W}= \mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^{0}\; ,\qquad
\mathbf{R}^{\top}\mathbf{R}= \mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^{\top}= \mathbf{I},
\]

then the *hyperspherical energy* of the layer is unchanged.  
That is, for every pair of neurons \(i\) and \(j\)

\[
\hat{\mathbf w}_i^{\top}\hat{\mathbf w}_j \;=\;
(\mathbf R\,\hat{\mathbf w}_i^{0})^{\top}
(\mathbf R\,\hat{\mathbf w}_j^{0}) \;=\;
\hat{\mathbf w}_i^{0\,\top}\,\hat{\mathbf w}_j^{0},
\]

where \(\hat{\mathbf w}_k = \dfrac{\mathbf w_k}{\|\mathbf w_k\|}\) is the unit‑norm vector corresponding to neuron \(k\).
