\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does the partition function $Z(x)$ cancel out when converting from a reward‑based Bradley‑Terry model to a policy‑based preference model?
Question:
Equation (7) in the paper shows that after substituting $r(x,y)$ with its reparameterization, the $	ext{log}igl(Z(x)igr)$ term drops out of the preference probability.  Can someone walk through a step‑by‑step algebraic proof that demonstrates this cancellation explicitly?
Answer:
**Answer**

Let  
\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)
      =\frac{\exp \bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)}
             {\exp \bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)+\exp \bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr)}
\tag{1}
\]
be the Bradley–Terry (BT) preference model.  
The authors re‑parameterise the reward as

\[
r(x,y)=\beta\,\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}
      +\beta\,\log Z(x),          \tag{2}
\]
where  

*  \(\pi_r(\cdot\mid x)\) is the *optimal* policy under reward \(r\),
*  \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) is a fixed reference policy (e.g., the SFT model),
*  \(Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\,\exp \bigl(r(x,y)/\beta\bigr)\) is the
   partition function, and
*  \(\beta>0\) is the KL‑constraint parameter.

We want to see that, when we plug (2) into (1), the term
\( \log Z(x)\) disappears.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Write the two exponentials in (1)

Let  
\(q_i = r^{*}(x, y_i)\) for \(i=1,2\).  
Using (2) for each \(q_i\):

\[
q_i
   = \beta\,\log
        \frac{\pi^*(y_i\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i\mid x)}
     + \beta\,\log Z(x),
     \qquad i=1,2.
     \tag{3}
\]

Hence

\[
e^{q_i}
   = e^{\,\beta\,\log
          \frac{\pi^*(y_i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i)}}
      \;e^{\,\beta\,\log Z}
   = Z^\beta
      \left(\frac{\pi^*(y_i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i)}\right)^{\,\beta}.
     \tag{4}
\]

For brevity we write
\(a_i=\dfrac{\pi^*(y_i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i)}>0.\)

Thus

\[
e^{q_1}=Z^\beta a_1^\beta,
  \qquad
e^{q_2}=Z^\beta a_2^\beta .
     \tag{5}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Substitute into the BT formula

The BT probability becomes

\[
p^{*}(y_1\succeq y_2)
  = 
  \frac{Z^\beta a_1^\beta}{Z^\beta a_1^\beta+Z^\beta a_2^\beta}
  = 
  \frac{a_1^\beta}{a_1^\beta+a_2^\beta } .
     \tag{6}
\]

The common factor \(Z^\beta>0\) has cancelled in numerator and
denominator: it does **not** appear in the final expression.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Replace $a_i^\beta=\bigl(\tfrac{\pi^*(y_i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i)}\\bigr)^{\,β}$

Using the definition of $a_i^{β}$,
equation (6) can be rewritten directly in terms of policies:

{
$$
p^{*}(y_1\succeq y_2)
   =
   \frac{
         \!\!\left(\dfrac{\pi^*(y_1)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1)}
                  \right)^{β}}
        {
         \!\!\left(\dfrac{\pi^*(y_1)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1)}
                  \right)^{β}
        + 
         \!\!\left(\dfrac{\pi^*(y_2)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2)}
                  \right)^{β}}
   =
   \sigma
   \!\left(
       β\, 
       \log
       \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{2})}{π(y_{2})}-
       β\, 
       \log
       \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1})}{π(y_{1})}
     \right)
$$
}

which is exactly equation (9) in the paper after a small rearrangement of signs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Summary of the cancellation

The exponential terms coming from the reward reparameterisation each carry a factor
\(Z(x)^{\,β}\). In both numerator and denominator of the BT ratio this factor appears with **the same power**; hence it divides out completely. The only remaining dependence on \(Z(x)\) would be if it had appeared with different powers or in only one of the two terms, but because BT involves *two* rewards symmetrically, whatever fan‑out factor we insert multiplies each exponential by the *same* amount and therefore disappears.

Thus, when we replace \(r^*(x,y)\) by its reparameterised form (Eq. (2)), the partition function does not influence the probability that a human prefers one answer over another—exactly why it “cancels out” in equation (7)/(9). The resulting preference model is expressed solely in terms of the optimal policy and reference policy, enabling a direct policy‑based loss without having to estimate \(Z(x)\).
