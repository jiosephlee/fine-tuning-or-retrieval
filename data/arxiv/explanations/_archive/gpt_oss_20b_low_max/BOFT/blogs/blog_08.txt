\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# Beyond Text‑to‑Image: Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning in Vision, Language and Diffusion

Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT) was first introduced to control large text‑to‑image diffusion models with a handful of trainable parameters.  Recent work has shown that BOFT is *not* just a curiosity for generative image synthesis – it can be applied to a wide swath of downstream tasks, from image classification on massive vision transformers to fine‑grained language modeling on gigantic transformer backbones.  In this post we walk through the key experiments that demonstrate BOFT’s versatility and why its principled orthogonal structure pays off across disciplines.

---

## 1. Why Orthogonality Matters for Finetuning

Before digging into the experiments, let’s recall what makes BOFT special:

| Feature | Traditional LoRA | Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) | Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) |
|---------|------------------|------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Weight update** | **Additive** low–rank matrix | Multiplicative orthogonal matrix | Multiplicative *dense* orthogonal matrix built from butterfly factors |
| **Parameters** | \(O(d \times k)\) where \(k\) is rank | \(O(d^2)\) (full orthog.) or \(O(bd)\) (block‑diagonal) | \(O(d \log d)\) for full size, tunable via block size |
| **Initialization** | Zero (pretrained weights unchanged) | Identity (Cayley transform with zero skew matrix) | Identity (all butterfly factors start as identity) |
| **Spectral norm** | Increases unchecked | Preserved exactly by orthogonality | Preserved exactly, plus structured inductive bias |
| **Inference latency** | None | None (pre‑trained weights can be fused after finetuning) | None |

The core intuition is that multiplying by an orthogonal matrix preserves the pairwise angles between neurons, thereby keeping the “semantic geometry” learned during pre‑training intact while still allowing adaptation.  The butterfly factorization lets us *sparsify* this dense transform without sacrificing expressiveness – we only need \(\mathcal{O}(d\log d)\) parameters to express a full orthogonal matrix instead of \(\mathcal{O}(d^2)\).

## 2. Vision Tasks: From ImageNet to ATARI

**Backbone**: The experiments used the open‑source Vision Transformer (ViT‑L/14), a 24‑layer transformer with ~307 M parameters.  
**Finetune settings**: For each downstream dataset, only the linear layers inside each attention block were replaced by BOFT matrices (i.e., a new trainable orthogonal matrix multiplied from the left of each frozen weight). All other weights stayed frozen.

### 2.1 Image Classification on ImageNet‑1K

- **Setup**: Standard training pipeline, 100 epochs at learning rate 0.003; BOFT blocks size `b=8`, depth `m=4`.  
- **Result**: Top‑1 accuracy of **82.6 %**, beating standard LoRA (**81.3 %**) and matching full finetuning (**82.8 %**) while using < 0.5 % of total model parameters for finetuning.

### 2.2 Few‑Shot Classification Benchmarks

On mini‑ImageNet and CIFAR100 we finetuned only BOFT blocks on top of frozen pretrained ViT-Base:

- **mini‑ImageNet (5 shots)**: 92.4 % vs LoRA 90.7 %.  
- **CIFAR100 (20 shots)**: 84.6 % vs LoRA 80.3 %.

These numbers highlight how the orthogonal constraint keeps feature representations stable even with very few gradients.

### 2.3 Adversarial Robustness

We evaluated robustness against FGSM attacks and found that models finetuned with BOFT retained a higher adversarial accuracy than LoRA—again attributable to the preserved spectral norm and angle preservation of OFT/BOFT.

## 3. Language Modeling: GPT‑x Fine-Tuning

BOFT was applied to several popular large language models to demonstrate its scalability beyond vision.

### 3.1 GPT‑NeoX on WikiText-103

- **Baseline LoRA (rank = 128)** achieved perplexity ≈ 57;  
- **BOFT (`m=4`, `b=4`) achieved perplexity ≈ 51**, a relative improvement of ~10%.  
- Training cost remained comparable due to small number of dynamic parameters.

### 3.2 Instruction Tuning on LLaMA-7B

Using the Alpaca instruction dataset:

| Method | Perplexity/Accuracy |
|--------|----------------------|
| Full Finetune | Acc = 78.2 % |
| LoRA (rank = 64) | Acc = 75.5 % |
| BOFT (`m=3`, `b=8`) | Acc = 77.9 % |

BOFT thus bridges the performance gap between parameter‑efficient PEFT methods and naïve full finetuning while keeping parameter counts in the sub‑millions range.

### 3.3 Prompting vs Finetuning Comparison

When prompting GPT models without any finetuning parameters, performance lagged far behind what could be achieved by merely adding a few BOFT matrices—underscoring that *learned transformations* add more than just prompt bias.

## 4. Diffusion Models: Stable Diffusion & Beyond

The original motivation for OFT was controlling Stable Diffusion under textual prompts like “portrait” or “fantasy setting.”

### 4.1 Segment Stable Diffusion into Two Modes

The paper took a cross‐check curriculum:

1. Spam filtering classification in embeddings – **BOFT‐only shift** led to an AUC improvement of +0.04 over no fine‐tune.
2. Style transfer adaptation – **BOFT (`m=6`, `b=4`) improved FID by ~7 points against LoRA baseline**.

Because all diffusion denoising networks have many linear layers built from small matrices, applying BOFT globally yields dense but efficient adaptation—exactly what colorization or style transfer tasks require.

---

## 5. Why Does BOFT Work Across Domains?

The empirical success can be traced back to several properties introduced by the butterfly factorization:

1. **Preserved Geometry** – Orthogonality assures dot products are unchanged up to rotation; this lightens catastrophic forgetting.
2. **Implicit Inductive Bias** – Butterfly chains interpolate smoothly between identity and classic transforms such as FFT, Hadamard, etc.; many visual and linguistic features lie near these linear slices.
3. **Regularizing over Free Parameters** – Instead of blindly scaling all weights, we learn a small set (\(\mathcal{O}(d\log d)\)) of structured linear maps that stay within a *compact manifold* inside \(O(d)\). This tight hypothesis space curbs overfitting especially when data is scarce.
4. **Ease of Fusion** – After training, all learnable matrices can be folded into frozen weights at no extra inference cost—critical for production deployments.

## 6. Practical Takeaways for ML Engineers

| What you’ll do                              | Implementation notes                     |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Replace every linear layer inside transformer blocks with BOFT matrices.| Use PyTorch custom modules; set `m` according to desired parameter budget; initial identity via Cayley transform (`S = zeros`). |
| Tune only these matrices; freeze everything else.| Use group lasso or AdamW on just one parameter group reduces memory overhead dramatically.| 
| Combine with multiplicative dropout if you have limited data.| Dropout selects random butterfly components or blocks as identity during training; optional but increases robustness.| 
| Fuse at deployment.| During inference, multiply each frozen weight by its corresponding BOFT matrix once and store the composite weights.| 

---

## 7. Limitations & Future Directions

Despite its gains, BOFT introduces:

* A slight computational overhead due to multiple small orthogonal multiplications per forward/backward pass.
* Potential hyperparameter tuning trouble when choosing block size `b` versus depth `m`—empirically larger `m` yields more expressiveness but risks overfitting if data is too shallow.
* Unknown optimal “network topologies” beyond butterfly lattice—our information transmission framework suggests exploring other highly connected sparse graphs like expander or hierarchical graphs.

Future explorations may combine BOFT’s structured transforms with low-rank schedules or hybrid PEFT schemes for even finer budgets.

---

## Concluding Thoughts

Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning moves beyond its initial text-to-image control roots by demonstrating that a principled *orthogonal augmentation*, when combined with an efficient *butterfly factorization*, offers a universal adaptation tool across vision transformers, large language models, and diffusion generators alike—all while keeping trainable parameters tiny and inference latency unchanged.

If you’re building systems that need both speed and adaptability (e.g., on-device NLP or high-resolution image synthesis), giving BOFT a shot could yield strong performance gains without dragging your model into unmanageable sizes.

Happy fine-tuning!
