Chapter 9: # 9. Empirical Evaluation Across Modalities  

In this chapter we bring the theoretical contributions of Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT) into the empirical realm.  We first outline the experimental toolkit: the models, datasets, and competing parameter‑efficient finetuning baselines (LoRA, QLoRA, BitFit, and Adapter tuning).  Then we report systematic evaluations across three canonical modalities—vision, language, and generative image modelling—focusing on accuracy, FID, loss curves, and generalisation as a function of the learnable parameter budget.  Finally, we dissect the experiments with ablations on BOFT hyper‑parameters (\(m\) and \(b\)), identity versus multiplicative dropout and training dynamics.

## Vision Fine‑Tuning on ImageNet/COCO

We finetune a large Vision Transformer (ViT‑Large/3B) pretrained on ImageNet‑21K and evaluate it on downstream ImageNet‑1K classification and COCO object detection.  BOFT replaces the linear projection heads in all `MLP` blocks with an orthogonal butterfly matrix \(\bm{R}(m,b)\).  For comparison we also finetune with:

* **LoRA** – additive rank‑\(r\) factorisation of each linear weight.  
* **QLoRA** – quantised LoRA that further reduces memory overhead.  
* **BitFit** – bias–only fine‑tuning (only learning scale & shift)  
* **Adapter Tuning** – small bottleneck adapters inserted after each residual layer.

The hyper‑parameters are tuned to utilise a *fixed budget of learnable parameters* for each method (e.g., \(0.5\%\), \(1\%\), \(5\%\) of the full weight set).  After optimisation we record top‑1 accuracy and FLOPs over a held‑out validation set.

### Accuracy versus Parameter Budget

| Method | \(\%\) of Learnable Parameters | ImageNet Accuracy | COCO mAP |
|--------|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------|
| Baseline (no finetune) | 0 | 83.4 % | 42.1 % |
| BitFit | 0.2 | 81.2 % | – |
| LoRA | 1.0 | 86.6 % | 47.3 % |
| QLoRA | 1.0 (quantised) | **86.9 %** | **47.8 %** |
| Adapter tuning | 2.0 | 85.8 % | 44.6 % |
| BOFT (m=5,b=8) | 1.0 | **88.1 %** | **48.7 %** |
| BOFT (m=7,b=2) | 0.5 | **87.4 %** | **48.0 %** |

Key observations:

1. For any fixed parameter budget < 2 %, BOFT achieves higher accuracy than LoRA/QLoRA, particularly at *low budgets* where orthogonality preserves internal representations better.
2. The gains are modest at *high budgets* because the free‑form LoRA/QLoRA can approximate arbitrary perturbations more aggressively; however BOFT still maintains competitive performance without sacrificing interpretability or inducing overfitting.

### Interpolation Analysis

BOFT’s forward pass is formally:
\[
\bm{z}=((\bm{R}(m,b)\bm{W}^{0})^\top \bm{x}).
\]
During optimisation we can optionally interpolate between the identity matrix \(\bm{I}\) and \(\bm{R}\):
\[
\bm{z}_{\lambda}=(([(1-\lambda)\bm{I}+\lambda \bm{R}]\,\bm{W}^{0})^\top \bm{x}), \quad \lambda\in[0,1].
\]
Figure 9‑1 demonstrates that setting \(\lambda<1\) often stabilises training: losses decrease linearly while matching final accuracy at \(\lambda=1\). This is especially useful when the downstream task differs drastically from pretraining (e.g., COCO detection).
