\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why does Orthogonal Finetuning preserve pairwise angles between neurons?
Question:
In Section 3 the paper states that OFT applies an orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf R\) to a pretrained weight matrix so that "pairwise angles between neurons are provably preserved". I understand that an orthogonal transformation preserves inner products, but I am confused about how this translates into preserving angles between *columns* of \(\mathbf W^0\) (i.e., neurons). Does this hold for any orthogonal \(\mathbf R\) applied on the left side only? And how does this angle preservation relate to retaining semantic knowledge from pretraining?
Answer:
In Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) the pretrained linear layer is written as  

$$
z=(W^{0})^{\,\top}x ,\
      W^{0}\in\rule[-1mm]{1pt}{3mm}\mathbb{R}^{d\times n},
$$  

so each **neuron** (i.e. each output coordinate) is associated with a  
column vector  

$$
w_{i}^{0}=W^{0}_{:,i}\in\rule[-1mm]{1pt}{3mm}\mathbb{R}^{d}.
$$  

OFT replaces the weight matrix by  

$$
W=RW^{0},\
      R\rule[-1mm]{1pt}{3mm}\in\rule[-1mm]{1pt}{3mm}\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\
      RR^{\,\top}=I,
$$  

and therefore  

$$
z=(RW^{0})^{\,\top}x .
$$  

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### Why do the column‑wise angles stay unchanged?

For any two vectors $u,v\nin\rule[-1mm]{1pt}{3mm}\mathbb{R}^{d}$,

$$
(Ru)^{\,\top}(Rv)=u\nitenerrightarrowzero?? 
$$

because $R^{-1}=R^\transpose$. Thus $(RU)(RV)$ preserves inner products.
Since an orthogonal matrix also preserves Euclidean norm,
$\lVert Ru\rVert=\lVert u\rVert$, the cosine of the angle between $u$
and $v$

$$
\vphantom{\Big|}
    \cos \theta_{u,v}
      = {\displaystyle 
         {\frac{ u^\transpose v }
                {\lVert u\rVert\,\<space>\lVert v\rVert}} ,
$$

is unchanged,

%
$$
\vphantom{\Big|}
    \cos \theta_{Ru,Rv}
      =
     {\displaystyle 
       {\frac{
            (Ru)^\transpose(Rv)
          }{
            \lVert Ru\rVert\,\<space>\lVert Rv\rVert}}
       }
     =
     {\displaystyle 
        {\frac{
             u^\transpose v }
          {
             \lVert u\rVert\,\<space>\lVert v\rVert}}
     }
     = 
     \cos \theta_{u,v}.
$$

Applying this fact to the columns of $W^ {  00 }$ gives

$$
w_i'=Rw_i^ { 00 },\
    w_j'=Rw_j^ { 00 }\;
\xRightarrow{\text{orthogonality}}
    \
    \cos(\theta_{w_i',w_j'})
    =
    \
    \cos(\theta_{w_i^ {00},w_j^ {00}}).
$$

Thus **any** orthogonal matrix multiplied on the *left* of a weight 
matrix leaves all pair‑wise angles between its columns exactly 
the same. If one were to multiply on the right ($WQ,\ Q∈ O(n)$)
this property would generally fail because each column would become 
a linear combination of multiple original columns, changing their mutual 
inner products.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### Connection with preserving semantic knowledge

During pretraining each column $w_i^ {00 }$ typically aligns with a 
semantic direction in the input space—for example one column may act 
as a “cat detector”, another as a “dog detector”. Many downstream 
behaviours of a deep network depend **only on the relative geometry**
of these directions:

* Cosine similarities $w_i ^ {00\,{\!\transpose}} x/
      (\lVert w_i ^ {00 }\rVert\: \<space>\lVert x\rVert)$ are used 
      directly by attention or classification heads.
* Subsequent layers treat their inputs through further linear maps;
  those maps care about *how* different directions relate rather than 
  their absolute coordinates.
* The singular‑value spectrum of $W^ {00 }$ encodes its Lipschitz constant;
  an orthogonal left‑multiplication leaves every singular value untouched,
  keeping the spectral norm—and hence training stability—identical   
  to the pretrained model.

Because OFT guarantees that every cosine similarity among the **neurons**
is exactly preserved, the relational structure learned during pretraining 
remains intact after finetuning. In other words, the “semantic map” stored 
in $W^ {00 }$ is merely rotated as a whole inside $\mathbb R ^ d$; rotating a 
constellation does not alter which points are close or far from one another.
Consequently:

* The model retains its ability to recognise previously learned concepts,
  reducing catastrophic forgetting.
* Down‑stream adapters or classification heads can specialise without having 
  to remodel an already‑distorted feature space.
* Training remains well‑conditioned because neither norms nor singular values 
  are perturbed, which matches observations that OFT often converges more 
  stably than additive low‑rank updates such as LoRA.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### Summary

The statement in Section 3 follows directly from two elementary facts about 
orthogonal matrices:

1. **Inner‑product preservation:** $(Ru)^\transpose(Rv)=u^\transpose v.$  
2. **Norm preservation:** $\lVert Ru\rVert=\lVert u\rVert.$  

Applied uniformly on the left side of a weight matrix,
these facts imply that every column vector—the neuron’s weight—
undergoes exactly the same rotation, so all pair‑wise cosines (hence all   
angles) stay unchanged regardless of which orthogonal matrix $R∈ O(d)$   
is chosen. By keeping this geometric relationship intact, OFT safeguards   
the relational information encoded during pretraining, allowing downstream   
finetuning to adapt efficiently while retaining the original semantic   
knowledge.
