\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 5: Empirical Discovery of Non‑Uniformities in Positional Interpolation  

# Finding 1: Variation across RoPE dimensions improves perplexity  

Large language models rely on rotary position embeddings (RoPE) to encode the absolute order of tokens. For a token appearing at position index $n$, RoPE produces a sequence of cosine–sine pairs whose arguments are linear functions of $n$ scaled by dimension‑specific frequencies $\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}$:

\[
\bigl[\cos (n\theta_0),\;\sin (n\theta_0),\;\cos (n\theta_1),\;\sin (n\theta_1),\;\dots ,\;\cos (n\theta_{d/2-1}),\;\sin (n\theta_{d/2-1})\bigr].
\tag{1}
\]

When the context window is extended from its pre‑training length $L$ to a longer length $L'$, most prior works simply rescale every $\theta_i$ by a single factor $\lambda$ that depends only on the extension ratio $s=L'/L$. This “uniform” treatment assumes that all dimensions contribute equally to positional information.  

The LongRoPE investigation challenges this assumption by allowing each dimension $i$ to have its own rescale factor $\lambda_i$. In Eq.~(2) we unify the notation for any positional interpolation method:

\[
\bigl[\cos \bigl(\tfrac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}\bigr),\, \sin \bigl(\tfrac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}\bigr),\, 
      \cos \bigl(\tfrac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^1}\bigr),\, \dots ,\, 
      \sin \bigl(\tfrac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^{d/2-1}}\bigr)\bigr],
\tag{2}
\]
where $\beta=\theta^{2/d}$ and $\lambda(\beta)$ denotes the effective scaling applied to each frequency band.

To discover a set of $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=0}^{d/2-1}$ that minimizes language modeling loss, the authors deployed an evolutionary search guided by perplexity computed on five random validation excerpts from PG19. Each candidate solution encodes a distinct vector of rescaling factors; candidates are evaluated by applying the resulting RoPE to LLaMA2‑7B and measuring next‑token loss.  

The empirical results summarized in Table ⟨exp1⟩ reveal a striking reduction in perplexity for both PG19 and Proof‑Pile when using the searched non‑uniform factors compared with three baselines:

| Method | PG19 perplexity | Proof‑Pile perplexity |
|---|---|---|
| Linear PI | high | high |
| NTK | moderate | moderate |
| YaRN | moderate → spikes after ≈7 k tokens | moderate |
| **LongRoPE (searched $\lambda_i$)** | **lowest** | **lowest** |

These numbers demonstrate that treating each RoPE dimension uniformly discards valuable information intrinsic to the transformer’s multi‑head attention architecture. In particular, lower‐frequency dimensions (large $i$) tolerate greater scaling because they capture coarse‑grained positional patterns, while higher‐frequency dimensions (small $i$) must preserve finer resolution. By allowing $\lambda_i$ to increase monotonically with $i$—a constraint motivated by Neural Tangent Kernel theory—the evolutionary algorithm discovers precisely this spectrum of scalings, thereby preserving key high‑frequency components while still accommodating an extended range of positions.

Intuitively, one may view each RoPE dimension as a “lens” focusing on a specific range of spatial frequencies along the token line. Uniformly shrinking all lenses squeezes the entire image and blurs details; assigning each lens its own shrink factor restores clarity where it matters most—most notably at early layers where attention heads frequently attend to nearby tokens. The resulting embedding stays faithful enough that LLaMA2‑7B can distinguish closely spaced tokens even when it operates over an 8× longer sequence without any fine‑tuning.

# Finding 2: Initial token positions benefit from reduced interpolation  

Even after accounting for dimension-wise heterogeneity, another source of distortion persists: every token after position $\hat n$ is subjected to scaling, while earlier tokens retain their original angles. The authors hypothesize that this uniform treatment harms performance because the very first tokens typically receive disproportionately large attention weights during inference—a phenomenon documented in streaming LLMs and LM‑Infinite systems. To test this hypothesis they introduce an indicator function $\mathbb I(\hat{\lambda}_i,\hat n)$ that leaves the rotation angles untouched for all positions $n<\hat n$:

\[
\mathbb I(\hat{\lambda}_i,\hat n)=
   \begin{cases}
     1 & n<\hat n\\[4pt]
     \frac{1}{\lambda_i} & n\ge \hat n.
   \end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

In practice they explore several values for $\hat n$, ranging from zero (no exemption) up through $256$, thereby progressively preserving more initial positions. They then reapply standard linear PI or NTK scaling beyond $\hat n$. 

Table ⟨tokenposition⟩ reports perplexities when extending LLaMA2‑7B’s context window from its native 4 k tokens up to 8 k and 16 k while varying $\hat n$. The two salient observations are:

1. **Retaining early positions consistently lowers perplexity** – any nonzero $\hat n$ improves over the baseline where every position is interpolated.
2. **The optimal size of $\hat n$ grows with target length** – longer extensions benefit from keeping a larger prefix unaltered because more attention mass remains concentrated near these unchanged anchors.

These findings align with an intuitive picture of attention mechanics: early tokens serve as “anchors” around which later context folds back during self‑attention calculation. If these anchors are distorted by aggressive interpolation, later attention becomes noisy, leading to higher loss. By shielding them from scaling, LongRoPE supplies stable reference points which guide later layers’ queries, keys, and values toward a coherent positional geometry even when many new indices have been introduced.

# Finding 3: Non‑uniform interpolation works both with and without fine‑tuning  

Having demonstrated that tailored $\{\lambda_i,\hat n\}$ pairs mitigate information loss in pure inference settings, the authors examine whether these same interpolations also provide a superior starting point for further fine‑tuning on ultra‑long sequences. They conduct two complementary experiments:

* **Non-fine-tuned regime** – extens​ion ratios up to eightfold (e.g., 4 k → 32 k) are achieved solely by applying the searched non‑uniform factors at inference time. Figure ⟨non-ft⟩ illustrates that LongRoPE maintains low perplexity across this entire range, whereas PI or NTK rapidly degrade after roughly a 2× increase.
* **Fine-tuned regime** – for more ambitious extensions such as 64 k tokens, they first apply LongRoPE’s searched factors as an initialization step before performing modest fine-tuning (e.g., 1000 training steps). Table ⟨finetune⟩ documents perplexities before (“pre”) and after (“post”) fine-tuning for three methods—PI, YaRN, and LongRoPE—on LLaMA2‑7B:

| Method | Pre‑fine‐tune PF \(64\,k\) | Post‑fine‐tune PF \(64\,k\) |
|---|---|---|
| PI   | high   | modest improvement |
| YaRN | moderate → spikes | modest improvement |
| **LongRoPE** | **low** | **lowest** |

“PF” denotes perplexity measured on held–out long documents at exactly \(64\,000\) tokens length. The superiority persists after training because the search supplies an embedding configuration that already preserves most high-frequency information; consequently gradient descent needs only minor adjustments during fine-tuning rather than rebuilding positional relationships from scratch.

A particularly noteworthy consequence is computational efficiency: once an adequate non‑uniform configuration has been discovered for an intermediate length such as \(256\,k,\) only a thousand optimizer steps suffice to reach convergence—they report exactly \(400 + 600 = 1000\) steps split between two progressive stages in Section 3 (“Progressive extension”). This stands in stark contrast with naïve approaches that would require orders of magnitude more steps—or even infeasible amounts of GPU memory—to train directly at \(2048\,k.\)

These experiments substantiate two claims put forward by LongRoPE:

1. **Non‑uniform interpolation alone can dramatically extend usable context without any parameter updates**, enabling rapid prototyping for tasks requiring up to tens of thousands of tokens.
2. **When further extension is desired**, initializing training from a well–designed non­uniform RoPE dramatically accelerates convergence and yields lower final perplexities than any uniform or hand-crafted scheme.

# Analysis of Tables and Figures Demonstrating Perplexity Gains  

Across the empirical study several visualizations reinforce the quantitative claims:

* **Figure ⟨overview⟩** visualizes how standard linear PI compresses rotary angles linearly across all dimensions versus how LongRoPE’s evolved factors stretch certain frequency bands while leaving others nearly untouched.
* **Figure ⟨non-ft⟩** plots perplexity versus context length ranging from \(4\,k\) up through \(32\,k.\) The curve for LongRoPE stays flat or even gently declines—reflecting better utilization of added context—while curves for PI/NTK rise sharply after a modest extension.
* **Table ⟨exp1⟩** supplies concrete numerical evidence on two benchmark corpora (PG19, Proof–Pile). Relative reductions ranging from \(10–30\,%\) over PI indicate that preserving dimension-wise entropy has practical impact on downstream language modeling tasks.
* **Table ⟨tokenposition⟩** isolates the effect of preserving early positions (\(\hat n>0.\) Across several values—\(0,\;8,\;16,\;32,\;256)\)—the measured perplexities form a convex shape where intermediate \(\hat n.\) achieve minima; this pattern underscores that excessive preservation diminishes returns beyond a certain prefix length.
* **Table ⟨finetune⟩** demonstrates that after only one thousand gradient steps initialized from LongRoPE’s embedding configuration,
    * baseline PI reduces its loss only marginally,
    * YaRN improves but still lags behind,
    * LongRoPE achieves both lower pre‐fine‐tune loss and further improvement after training—highlighting its dual role as initialization + regularizer.

Overall these empirics reinforce a coherent narrative: positional embeddings are not homogeneous carriers of information; their dimensional frequencies encode qualitatively different signals about token order, while early token locations act as privileged anchors during self‑attention integration. By letting these two axes vary independently via an evolutionary search constrained by monotonic scaling (\(\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}\)), LongRoPE uncovers embeddings that preserve crucial structure even when billions broader indices are introduced. This discovered non‐uniform structure underlies every subsequent success reported later in the paper—including bringing LLaMA2‐7B and Mistral‐7B up to a staggering \(2048\,k\) context window while still matching or surpassing baselines on standard short-context benchmarks.
