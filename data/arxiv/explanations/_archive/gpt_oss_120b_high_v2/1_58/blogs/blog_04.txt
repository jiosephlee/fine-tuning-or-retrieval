# Energy‑Smart AI: From Matrix Multiplications to Integer Additions  

Large language models have become the workhorses of modern AI, but their appetite for compute and electricity is growing faster than Moore’s law can keep up with. A recent paper—*The Era of 1‑bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits*—shows that we can turn this trend around by swapping floating‑point matrix multiplications for plain integer additions. In this post we unpack the underlying energy model, explain why a **71×** reduction in arithmetic‑operation energy is realistic on today’s 7 nm silicon, and discuss why power—not raw FLOPs—is now the primary bottleneck for AI chips.

---

## Why Multiplications Burn So Much Power  

A transformer layer is essentially a giant collection of dense linear maps (`nn.Linear`). Each call to a linear layer performs a matrix–matrix multiplication:

\[
Y = XW^\top,
\]

where \(X\) holds activations (usually stored as FP16 or BF16) and \(W\) holds weights (also FP16/BF16). Every element of the result requires one floating‑point multiply and one floating‑point add. On modern GPUs a single FP16 multiply–add (FMA) consumes roughly **10 pJ**, while an INT8 addition costs **≈0.14 pJ** according to the silicon‑level measurements compiled in~\cite{energycost,pokebnn}.  

Even though an INT8 add looks cheap by itself, its impact multiplies dramatically in an LLM because matrix multiplication dominates the total operation count—often **>90 %** of all arithmetic during inference. Replace every multiply–add pair with a simple add and you instantly slash the arithmetic portion of the energy budget by roughly

\[
\frac{\text{FP16 FMA}}{\text{INT8 Add}} \;\approx\; \frac{10}{0.14}\;\approx\;71.
\]

That factor matches the **71.4×** reduction reported by the authors for BitNet b \( \text{b} {=}\! \mathbf{b_{\,\!{{1}.58}}} \) on a **7 nm** process node (the same node used by contemporary A100 GPUs).

---

## From Floating Points to Ternary Weights  

BitNet b \( \text{b}_{\,\!{{1}.58}} \) takes this idea one step further by quantizing *every* weight tensor into ternary values \(\{-1,\;0,\;+1\}\). In practice this means:

* **Storage:** Each weight needs at most **2 bits**, but thanks to entropy coding they achieve an *effective* bitrate of **\( \mathbf{1.58} \) bits** per parameter.
* **Computation:** Multiplication by \(\pm 1\) reduces to copying or sign flipping; multiplication by \(0\) drops out entirely.
* **Matrix–matrix product:** Reduces from a dense FMA loop to an *integer‑addition‑only* accumulation over three possible contributions:
  * add \(+X_{ij}\),
  * subtract \(X_{ij}\),
  * skip \(X_{ij}\).

The quantization pipeline that enforces ternary weights uses an “abs‑mean” scaling followed by a rounding‑and‑clipping step:

\[
\widetilde{W}
= \operatorname{RoundClip}\!\Bigl(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon},\, -1 ,\, +1\Bigr),
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{i,j}\!|W_{ij}|,
\]

where `RoundClip` confines each scaled entry to the nearest integer within \([-1,+1]\). This operation is performed *every step* during training so the network learns directly under ternary constraints rather than being forced into them after training—a key reason why performance does not collapse despite extreme compression.

## Energy Savings Translate Into Real‑World Speedups  

Energy reductions matter because power budgets dictate how fast a chip can run without overheating or draining its power supply. When arithmetic consumes far less power:

* **Higher clock frequencies** become feasible without exceeding thermal design limits.
* **More cores** can be packed into the same die area while staying under a fixed TDP (thermal design power).
* **Memory bandwidth becomes dominant**, so shrinking weight footprints from 16 bits down to ~2 bits cuts DRAM traffic dramatically—less data movement means even lower overall power draw.

The paper quantifies these secondary benefits:

| Model | Precision | Speedup (token latency) | Memory Reduction |
|-------|-----------|------------------------|------------------|
| LLaMA 3B | FP16 | – | – |
| BitNet b₁․₅₈ 3B | Ternary | **2.71×** faster | **3.55×** less GPU RAM |
| BitNet b₁․₅₈ 70B | Ternary | **4.10×** faster | ~4× less RAM |

Throughput experiments on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs show that a 70‑billion‑parameter ternary model can process **≈9×** more tokens per second than its FP16 counterpart simply because twice as many kernels fit into memory and each kernel runs at higher frequency thanks to lower power consumption.

---

## Power Is Now The Bottleneck  

Historically, AI hardware races were driven by raw FLOP counts—more MAC units meant higher performance if you could feed them quickly enough. In recent silicon generations several trends have shifted that balance:

| Trend | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Frequency scaling stalled** around ~2 GHz due to voltage limits → Performance must come from parallelism rather than higher clocks |
| **Power density caps** limit how many MAC units you can place per mm² without exceeding thermal budgets |
| **DRAM access dominates** energetic cost once weights shrink → Moving bits faster becomes critical |

Consequently any technique that reduces *both* compute‐intensive operations and *data movement* yields disproportionate gains under a fixed TDP budget—exactly what BitNet b₁․₅₈ delivers.

## What This Means For Deployments  

### Server‑Side Inference  
Large data centers run thousands of GPUs around the clock; cutting per‑token energy by >70× translates into megawatt savings at scale—a tangible climate benefit alongside lower operating costs.

### Edge & Mobile  
Embedded devices cannot afford large DRAM buffers or high‐power GPU cores. The ternary weight format fits comfortably into on‑chip SRAM or even emerging non‑volatile memories, enabling models like a **3B** BitNet b₁․₅₈ to run locally on smartphones or microcontrollers while keeping latency sub‑second.

### Future Hardware  
Products such as Groq’s LPUs already focus on integer‑centric pipelines.^[Groq] The authors argue that new “LPU‑style” accelerators could be built expressly around native ternary matmul units—no floating‐point units required—pushing throughput further while staying within tight power envelopes.

---

## TL;DR  

*Matrix multiplications in traditional LLMs are expensive because each element requires an FP16 multiply–add (~10 pJ).  
BitNet b₁․₅₈ replaces those operations with plain INT8 additions (~0.14 pJ), giving about a **71×** drop in arithmetic‐operation energy on modern 7 nm silicon.*  

Because arithmetic now consumes only a fraction of total power, chips can run faster and pack more cores without blowing their thermal budget—turning *power* into the new limiting factor rather than raw FLOPs. The result is an LLM family that matches full‑precision perplexity and downstream task performance while delivering multiple‑fold speedups, slashing memory usage, and dramatically lowering electricity use across cloud farms and edge devices alike.

As we push toward ever larger models, embracing “energy‑smart” architectures like ternary transformers may be the only way to keep AI affordable—and sustainable—in an era where every joule counts.
