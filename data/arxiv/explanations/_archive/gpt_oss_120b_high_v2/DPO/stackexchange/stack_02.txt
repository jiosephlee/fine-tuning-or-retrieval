### How does the partition function $Z(x)$ cancel out in the derivation from Eq. (3) to Eq. (5)?
Question:
Equation (3) defines the optimal policy as $\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$. The authors then rewrite the Bradley–Terry preference probability in terms of $\pi^*$ and $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, obtaining Eq. (5) where $Z(x)$ no longer appears. I am confused about which algebraic steps cause $Z(x)$ to disappear. Specifically, how does substituting Eq. (4) for $r^*(x,y)$ make the partition function cancel when taking the difference $r^*(x,y_1)-r^*(x,y_2)$?
Answer:
In the paper the “optimal‑policy” form  

$$
\pi_{r}(y|x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\,
      \exp!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr)
$$  

is obtained by solving the KL‑constrained maximisation problem (2).  
Here  

$$
Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\,
      \exp!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\,r(x,y)\Bigr)
$$  

is a normalising constant that depends **only on the prompt** $x$; it does not vary with the particular completion $y$.  

If we take natural logarithms of (3),

$$
\log \pi_{r}(y|x)= -\,\log Z(x)+\log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)
                  + \frac{1}{\beta}\, r(x,y),
$$  

and multiply by $\beta$, we obtain  

$$
r(x,y)= \beta\,\log
        \frac{\pi_{r}(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}
        + \beta\,\log Z(x).
$$  

Equation (4) expresses any reward that generates the optimal policy
$\pi_{r}$; it simply isolates the part of $r$ that varies with $y$
—the first term — from an additive term that is constant for a given
$x$.  

The Bradley–Terry preference model used in the paper is  

$$
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}|x)
   = \sigma!\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr)
   = \frac{
        1}
        {1+e^{-\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})-r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr)}} .
$$  

Now substitute the re‑parameterisation (4) for the *ground‑truth*
reward $r^{*}$:

$$
r^{*}(x,y_i)
   = \beta\,\log
     \frac{\pi^{*}(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i|x)}
     + \beta\,\log Z(x),
     \qquad i=1,2 .
$$

\begin{aligned}
