\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# DeepSeekMath 7B: An Open‑Source LLM That Rivals GPT‑4 on Math Benchmarks  

*An accessible overview of the new DeepSeekMath 7B model, why it matters for the open‑source community, and how its $51.7\%$ score on the competition‑level **MATH** benchmark brings it within striking distance of closed models such as GPT‑4 and Gemini‑Ultra.*

---

## Why mathematical reasoning is still a hard problem for LLMs  

Large language models have proved remarkably good at *language* tasks, yet solving mathematically rich problems remains tricky because mathematics demands precise symbolic manipulation, multi‑step logical chains, and often external tools (e.g., Python libraries).  
Closed models like **GPT‑4** or **Gemini‑Ultra** already outperform most public models by a wide margin on benchmarks such as **MATH** (a competition‑level set of Olympiad problems) and **GSM8K** (grade‑school word problems). The gap has kept many researchers—and potential users—locked behind commercial APIs.

*DeepSeekMath 7B* narrows that gap dramatically while staying completely open source, offering a “research‑ready” math specialist that anyone can download, fine‑tune further, or plug into applications without worrying about licensing restrictions.


## What DeepSeekMath 7B actually is  

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Base architecture** | Starts from **DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1\.5** (a 7 B decoder‑only model pretrained for code generation). |
| **Continual pre‑training** | An additional **500 billion** tokens are ingested after the code phase.<br>– **56 %** come from a newly built *DeepSeekMath Corpus* (~120 billion mathematics tokens)<br>– **20 %** are natural language web text<br>– **20 %** are GitHub code<br>– The rest are algebraic stack data & arXiv papers |
| **Instruction tuning** | ~776 k chain‑of‑thought / program‑of‑thought examples covering English *and* Chinese K–12 problems.<br>Solutions are annotated in three formats:<br>1️⃣ plain CoT<br>2️⃣ PoT (Python snippets)<br>3️⃣ Tool‐integrated reasoning |
| **Reinforcement learning** | A lightweight RL stage called **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** replaces the heavyweight critic used in traditional PPO.<br>Only the policy network is updated; baselines come from group‐wise reward averages, cutting memory demand dramatically.|

Consequently the final **DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B** plus its GRPO fine‐tuning become *DeepSeekMath‑RL 7B*, which we evaluate throughout this post.


## Mining a massive “math web” – the DeepSeekMath Corpus  

Mathematical content exists abundantly across the public web—think forum threads, lecture notes, or calculation tutorials—but raw crawls are noisy. The authors designed an *iterative classifier pipeline*:

1️⃣ **Seed set:** Use OpenWebMath (~500 k high‐quality pages) as positive examples for a fastText classifier.<br>
2️⃣ **Initial recall:** Apply this classifier to ~40 billion deduplicated Common Crawl pages and keep only top‐scoring pages.<br>
3️⃣ **Domain analysis:** Identify domains where >10 % of pages were selected (e.g., `mathoverflow.net`). Manually annotate additional URLs inside those domains → richer positive set.<br>
4️⃣ **Re‐train & repeat:** Feed new positives back into fastText and repeat steps 2–3 four times total.<br>
5️⃣ **Decontamination:** Strip any n‑gram that appears verbatim in evaluation benchmarks (GSM8K/MATH/CMATH/AGIEval) to avoid leakage.<br>

The result: **35.5 million web pages = ~120 billion tokens**, roughly **seven times** larger than the math pages used by Minerva’s original web crawl and nine times larger than OpenWebMath alone.


### Quality checks

The authors compared three corpora by training a small 1.3 B model for 150 billion tokens each:

| Corpus | Tokens | Main source mix | Performance boost (few-shot CoT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MathPile | 8.9 B | Mostly arXiv papers | Modest gain |
| OpenWebMath | 13.6 B | CC filtered | Slightly better |
| **DeepSeekMath Corpus** | **120 B** | Multilingual CC + refined filtering | **Largest gain across eight benchmarks**, notably better than Proof–Pile‐2 even when Proof–Pile‐2 sees one full epoch (~50 B tokens). |

Thus the corpus is *high quality*, *multilingual* (English + Chinese dominate), and *large enough* to keep learning curves steep throughout training.


## Code pretraining ≠ “just more data” – it actually helps math  

A key insight reported in the paper: initializing from a *code*-focused model yields stronger math reasoning than starting from a generic LLM of identical size.

*Why?*  
Coding data forces the network to learn precise token–level execution semantics (e.g., loops, function calls). Those same patterns surface when solving algebraic manipulations or generating Python programs during *program‐of‐thought* inference (`sympy`, `math` libraries). Two-stage experiments confirmed this:

| Training order | Result on GSM8K / MATH (CoT) |
|---|---|
| Code → Math | Strongest gains both with/without tool use |
| General → Math | Smaller gains |
| Math only | Baseline |

Mixing code & math tokens in one stage also mitigates catastrophic forgetting—i.e., retaining coding ability while still improving arithmetic solving.


## From “base” to “instruct” – stepwise performance lifts  

### DeepSeekMath‑Base (7 B)

After continu­ed pretraining:
* **GSM8K:** $64.2\%$
* **MATH:** $36.2\%$

These scores already *match* closed Minerva 540 B despite being >70× smaller—a clear signal that *data quality outweighs pure scale* for mathematical reasoning.


### DeepSeekMath‑Instruct (7 B)

Supervised fine‐tuning adds CoT / PoT examples:
* Outperforms every other open‐source model under ***10 billion parameters***.<br>
* Beats most ***70 billion*** proprietary models except GPT‑4/Gemini Ultra.<br>
* On tools‐allowed evaluation:
    * Approaches **60 %** accuracy on competition‐level MATH – still below GPT‐4 but well above any prior open release.


### DeepSeekMath‑RL (7 B) – the GRPO boost  

The authors applied GRPO only on the instruction‐tuned checkpoint using ~144k GSM8K/MATH questions:

| Metric | Before GRPO | After GRPO |
|---|---|---|
| GSM8K (CoT) | $82.9\%$ → $88.2\%$ |
| MATH (CoT)    | $46.8\%$ → $\mathbf{51.7\%}$ |
| CMATH (Chinese) | $84.6\%$ → $88\.8\%$ |

Self‐consistency—sampling ***64*** answers per question then voting—pushes MATH further up to **$60.9\%$**, eclipsing many closed ranges reported for earlier LLMs.


#### How GRPO works (in plain words)

Traditional PPO needs two networks:
* A *policy* network that generates responses.
* A *critic* network estimating value (“how good will future tokens be?”).

GRPO discards the critic entirely:
1️⃣ Sample **G** answers per question with the current policy (*old policy*).  
2️⃣ Score them with a learned reward model.
3️⃣ Compute each answer’s advantage relative to its peers (**group average**) instead of subtracting a separate value estimate.
4️⃣ Update only the policy while regularizing against KL divergence to a reference copy of itself.

Because advantages come straight from comparison within each group, there’s no extra memory cost for a separate value head—crucial when fine‐tuning a multi-billion token LLM.


## Putting numbers into perspective  

The competition version of MATH has historically seen private scores around:
* GPT‑4 ≈ $80+ \! \%
* Gemini Ultra ≈ similar range
* Open-source max before this work ≈ $40 \! \%
  
DeepSeekMath RL reaching ***$51.7\%$*** therefore represents:
* The first open-source model crossing the *50 %* barrier on a truly challenging Olympiad benchmark.
* A gap shrinkage by roughly ***30 percentage points*** compared with prior public baselines.
* Evidence that even modestly sized models ($<$10 B parameters) can compete when fed curated data plus focused RL.


## Why this matters for the open-source AI ecosystem  

1️⃣ **Democratizing research:**  
   Researchers no longer need expensive API access to test new prompting tricks or RL pipelines on high-quality mathematical reasoning; everything fits onto consumer GPUs once quantized or distilled.

2️⃣ **A strong baseline for downstream applications:**  
   Education platforms, automated grading tools, or symbolic assistants can now embed an LLM that solves algebraic equations *and* writes correct Python snippets—all without needing external toolchains at inference time.

3️⃣ **Proof that scale isn’t everything:**  
   By beating Minerva’s closed174 × higher parameter count through smarter data selection (+ multilingual coverage) and efficient reinforcement learning, DeepSeekMath underscores an alternative path—focus resources on curated datasets rather than blindly scaling parameters.

4️⃣ **Open foundation for community extensions:**  
   The released training scripts (`HAI-LLM` framework), fastText classifiers, and pipeline documentation make it straightforward for anyone to add new domains (*e.g.*, physics derivations or geometry visualizations).


## Limitations & next steps  

While impressive, DeepSeekMath still lags behind GPT‑4 in several areas:
* Geometry-heavy problems (triangles/ellipses) remain weak—likely reflecting underrepresentation in web mining.
* Few-shot prompting doesn’t give sizable gains over zero-shot yet.
* Reward models rely heavily on simplistic “correct answer” signals; richer process supervision could further raise core reasoning capability.

Future research directions outlined by the authors include:
1️⃣ Expanding the mining pipeline toward richer visual/math domains.<br>
2️⃣ Exploring more sophisticated sampling strategies during RL such as tree search.<br>
3️⃣ Building noise‐robust reward functions that express uncertainty (“weak-to-strong” alignment).<br>
4️⃣ Integrating larger-scale code assistance without catastrophic forgetting.


## TL;DR – Bottom line  

- **DeepSeekMath 7 B** continues pretraining a code specialist model with a *120 billion token*, multilingual mathematics corpus harvested from Common Crawl.
- It reaches ***$51.7\%$*** accuracy on competitive MATH benchmark after just one round of lightweight reinforcement learning (**GRPO**)—the first time an open model breaches the fifty-percentage mark.
- The result narrows the gap between public LLMs and hidden giants like GPT‑4/Gemini Ultra while proving that careful data engineering plus efficient RL can outweigh raw parameter counts.
- For anyone building or studying AI systems with mathematical abilities today, DeepSeekMath offers an openly available research platform ready to plug into downstream tools or serve as a testbed for novel prompting/RL ideas.
