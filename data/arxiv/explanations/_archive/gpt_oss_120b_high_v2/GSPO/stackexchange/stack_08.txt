\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Can you clarify how ‘Routing Replay’ works and why it is unnecessary under GSPO?
Question:
The paper mentions caching expert routing decisions from the old policy and replaying them when computing token‑level ratios for GRPO. How is this implemented in practice (e.g., storing routing masks per token)? Moreover, why does switching from token‑level ratios to sequence‑level ratios eliminate the need for such caching?
Answer:
**Answer**

In GRPO the off‑policy correction is applied to every token through the ratio  

$$
w _ { i , t }( \vartheta ) =
     \frac { \pi _ { \vartheta }(
            y _ { i , t } |
            x , y _ { i ,< t } )
         }
          { 
            \pi _ { {\vartheta } _ {\text { old }}
                 }(
            y _ { i , t } |
            x , y _ { i ,< t } )
          } .
$$  

For a Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) model each token‑wise probability is obtained from a *different* sub‑network that is chosen by the router (the gating function).  
When the policy changes from $\,\pi _{\vartheta _{\text { old }}}\,$ to $\,\pi _{\vartheta }\,$ the router may select a different set of experts for the same token; in our experiments roughly ten percent of the experts switch after a single RL update. Because $\,w _ { i , t }\,$ compares two probabilities that were produced by *different* experts, its value can jump arbitrarily and the whole GRPO objective becomes extremely noisy.  

### What “Routing Replay” actually does
