\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Why does length normalisation in \(s_i(θ)\) reduce variance compared to using raw sequence likelihood ratios?
Question:
If we omitted the exponent \(1/|y_i|\), we would have raw ratios that can vary dramatically with response length. How exactly does taking the geometric mean (or log‑average) lower variance? Is there an analytic argument based on properties of log‑normals or central limit theorem?
Answer:
In GSPO the importance weight for a sampled response is defined as  

$$
s_{i}(\theta)=\Bigl(\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i}\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i}\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{|y_{i}|}}
   =\exp\Bigl(\frac{1}{|y_{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{i}|}
               \log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}
                        {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y}_{id{i},t}\mid x,y}_{id{i},<t})}\Bigr).
\tag{1}
$$

If the exponent $1/|y_i|$ were omitted we would use  

$$
\tilde s_{i}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i}\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y}_{id{i}}\mid x)}
          =\prod_{t=1}^{|y{i}|}w_{{id{i},t}}(\theta),
\tag{2}
$$

where $w_{{id{i},t}}(\theta)$ are the token‑level importance ratios introduced in GRPO.
Equation (2) is a product of many random factors; its magnitude can therefore vary
exponentially with the sequence length.  The authors observe that “the likelihood
changes of a few tokens can result in dramatic fluctuations of the
sequence‑level importance ratio” and that clipping would then need
different ranges for different lengths (Section 3).

---
