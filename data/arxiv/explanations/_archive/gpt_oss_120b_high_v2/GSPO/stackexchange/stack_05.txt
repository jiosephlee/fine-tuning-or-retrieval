\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Why does clipping a larger fraction of tokens in GSPO still lead to higher training efficiency compared to GRPO?
Question:
Figure 3 shows that GSPO clips two orders of magnitude more tokens than GRPO, yet achieves better sample efficiency. Intuitively I would think discarding more data hurts learning speed. Could someone explain why sequence‑level clipping removes noisy gradients more effectively than token‑level clipping, perhaps with reference to variance reduction arguments?
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
GSPO throws away a much larger *fraction* of tokens because it discards whole **responses** whose *sequence‑level* importance ratio $\,s_i(\theta)=\Bigl(\dfrac{\pi_\theta(y_i\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\!1/|y_i|}\,$ lies outside the clipping interval $[\,1-\varepsilon,\;1+\varepsilon\,]$.  
Those responses are precisely the ones whose IS weight would be extreme – they would contribute a disproportionately large, noisy term to the gradient if they were kept. By removing an entire response we also discard all its constituent tokens, which by construction inflates the *token‑level* clipping proportion even though the *number* of useful samples has not decreased markedly. Because the remaining on‑policy responses are weighted uniformly (every token gets the same factor $s_i(\theta)$), their gradients have far lower variance than in GRPO, where each token carries its own possibly wildly different weight $w_{i,t}(\theta)$. Hence GSPO enjoys a better signal‑to‑noise ratio per training step and achieves higher sample efficiency despite “clipping” more tokens.

Below is a more detailed intuition together with a compact variance‑reduction argument that follows directly from the equations in the paper.

---

### 1. What is being clipped?

* **GRPO** – clips *per‑token* importance ratios  

$$
w_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}
                       {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}.
$$

  Every token survives or is clipped independently of its siblings.

* **GSPO** – clips *per‑response* importance ratios  

$$
s_i(\theta)=\Bigl(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i\mid x)}
                        {\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\!1/|y_i|}
            =\exp\Bigl(\frac{1}{|y_i|}
                     \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}\log w_{i,t}(\theta)\Bigr).
$$

  If $s_i(\theta)$ falls outside $[\,1-\varepsilon,\;1+\varepsilon\,]$, **the whole response $y_i$ is excluded** from the update.

Because a single off‑policy response typically contains dozens or hundreds of tokens, discarding it inflates the “fraction of clipped tokens’’ measured on the raw token count even though only a few *responses* are removed.
