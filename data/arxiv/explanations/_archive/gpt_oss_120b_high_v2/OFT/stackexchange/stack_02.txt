### Why does an orthogonal transformation preserve hyperspherical energy?
Question:
The authors state that applying a common orthogonal matrix \(R\) to all neurons in a layer (i.e., \(W = R W^0\)) leaves \(\text{HE}(W)\) unchanged because pairwise angles are preserved. I would like a step‑by‑step derivation:

* Show that for any two columns \(w_i,w_j\), \(\langle R w_i, R w_j \rangle = \langle w_i,w_j \rangle\).
* Explain why this leads to \(\|\widehat{R w_i} - \widehat{R w_j}\| = \|\hat{w}_i - \hat{w}_j\|\
Answer:
In the paper the **hyperspherical energy** of a weight matrix  

$$
W=\bigl[w_{1},\,dots ,\, w_{n}\bigr]\in{\mathbb R}^{d\times n}
$$  

is defined as  

$$
{\rm HE}(W)=
      \sum_{\substack{i,j \\ i\,{\neq}\,j}}
      \Bigl(\,\bigl\|\,
            \widehat w _ i-\widehat w _ j
           \bigr\|
       ^{-1}\Bigr), 
      \qquad    
      \widehat w _ i:=%
      {\displaystyle%
       {\frac{\,w _ i}{%
            {\Vert }\,w _ i{\Vert }}}}.
      \tag{1}
$$  

If we replace every column by a **common orthogonal transformation**

$$
W^{*}=RW,\qquad   
R^{T}R=I,
$$  

the authors claim that ${\rm HE}(W^{*})={\rm HE}(W)$.
Below is a step‑by‑step derivation that explains why this holds.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1 Orthogonal matrices preserve inner products  

Take any two columns $\,w_{i},\,w_{j}\in{\mathbb R}^{d}$.  
Because $R^{T}R=I$,  

$$
   \langle R\,w_{i},\,R\,w_{j}\rangle
   =(R\,w_{i})^{T}(R\,w_{j})
   =w_{i}^{T}R^{T}Rw_{j}
   =w_{i}^{T}I\,w_{j}
   =\langle w_{i},\,w_{j}\rangle .
   \tag{2}
$$  

Equation (2) says that **the dot‑product (hence the cosine of the angle)
between any pair of vectors is unchanged** after applying $R$.  
A direct corollary is that norms are preserved:

$$
   \Vert R\,w_{i}\Vert ^2
   =(R\,w _ i)^T(R\, w _ i)
   = w _ i ^T R^TR\, w _ i
   = \Vert w _ i\rVert ^2,
   \
   \Longrightarrow 
   \
   \Vert R\, w _ i\rVert = 
          \Vert  w _ i\rVert .
   \tag{3}
$$  

Thus an orthogonal matrix acts as a rigid rotation or reflection—it does not stretch or shrink vectors.
