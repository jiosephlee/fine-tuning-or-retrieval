\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# OFT vs. LoRA vs. ControlNet: A Head‑to‑Head Comparison for Practitioners  

*How three major finetuning tricks stack up when you want to steer a text‑to‑image diffusion model toward a new subject or a new control signal.*

---

## Why Finetuning Matters  

Large text‑to‑image diffusion models — think Stable Diffusion, Imagen or DALL·E 2 — already know how to turn a prompt into a photorealistic picture.  
In practice you often need **more precise control**:

| Use case | Goal |
|----------|------|
| **Subject‑driven generation** | Given a handful of photos of a particular dog, generate new scenes that still feature *that* dog. |
| **Controllable generation** | Condition generation on an additional signal (edges, segmentation maps, facial landmarks) while still obeying the textual prompt. |

Both scenarios boil down to **finetuning** a massive pretrained diffusion model without erasing what it already knows. Three families of methods have become de‑facto standards:

1. **Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT / COFT)** – learns a layer‑shared orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ ($\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{R}=\bm{I}$) that rotates the pretrained weight $\bm{W}^0$ into $\bm{W}= \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$. The rotation leaves every pairwise neuron angle unchanged, i.e., it preserves *hyperspherical energy* — a proxy for the semantic structure of the model.
2. **LoRA** – injects a low‑rank additive update $\Delta\bm{W}= \bm{A}\bm{B}^{\top}$ on top of $\bm{W}^0$, where $r'$ is the rank hyperparameter.
3. **Adapter‑based methods (ControlNet & T2I‑Adapter)** – attach an extra “control branch” that receives the additional signal and is merged with the original UNet at inference time.

Below we walk through an apples‑to‑apples comparison that matters when you actually train and ship these models.

---

## Quick Reference Table  

| Aspect | **OFT / COFT** | **LoRA** | **ControlNet / T2I‑Adapter** |
|--------|----------------|----------|-------------------------------|
| **Trainable parameters** | $O(d^2/r)$ per layer via block‑diagonal $\bm{R}$ <br>*(e.g., $960$ vs $2048$ for a $128\times128$ weight matrix)* | $r'(d+n)$ per layer <br>*(low‑rank, typically $r'\approx 4$–$8$)* | Full additional control network (often > 10 × the base params). |
| **Inference overhead** | None – $\bm{R}$ can be folded into $\bm{W}^0$, yielding exactly the original forward graph. | Negligible when folded; otherwise adds two small mat‑muls per layer. | Extra forward pass through the control branch → ~ 30 % more FLOPs on typical UNet sizes. |
| **Convergence speed** | Reaches target performance in ≈ 8 epochs on landmark‑to‑face finetuning; stable even after 400 iterations in DreamBooth settings. | Needs ≥ 20 epochs on the same task; slower on few‑shot subject learning. | Often requires many epochs (> 20) and suffers sudden “phase transition” when control starts working. |
| **Final image fidelity** | Higher *subject fidelity* (DINO & CLIP‑I scores) and *control consistency* across Canny edge, segmentation‐map & landmark tasks; comparable or better CLIP‐T & LPIPS scores than DreamBooth/LoRA. | Good prompt adherence but weaker identity preservation; lower DINO/CLIP‑I scores in subject‐driven experiments; modest control consistency on adapters. | Strong control *once converged* but sometimes compromises overall realism and diversity; lower hyperspherical energy preservation leads to occasional artefacts. |
| **Training stability** | Robust to over‑training – retains semantics even after thousands of steps; COFT adds an explicit radius constraint ($\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$) for extra safety. | Sensitive to learning rate & iteration count; can collapse or lose subject identity after ≈ 2000 steps in DreamBooth experiments. | Stable once converged but early training is noisy; may need careful scheduling of control loss weight. |
| **Implementation effort** | Requires orthogonal parameterization (Cayley transform) + optional block diagonalization – a few extra lines of code around each weight matrix.| Simple insertion of two low‑rank matrices per target layer – widely supported by libraries.| Requires adding a whole new conditional UNet branch plus data pipelines for each control modality.|

## Diving Deeper  

### 1️⃣ Parameter Efficiency  

Both OFT and LoRA aim at *few-shot* adaptation where every extra byte counts:

- **OFT** leverages the fact that an orthogonal transformation lives on a manifold of dimension $d(d-1)/2$. By constraining $\bm{R}$ to be block‑diagonal with $r$ blocks we shrink it further to $d(d/r-1)/2$, and sharing blocks reduces it again to $d(d/r^{2}-1)/2$. The authors report **≈ 50 % fewer trainable weights** than an equivalently sized LoRA update in their $128\times128$ example.
- **LoRA** is already cheap because it only stores two skinny matrices ($\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r'}$, $\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r'}$). The trade‑off is that those matrices add *new* directions rather than rotating existing ones.
- **ControlNet/T2I‑Adapter** essentially train an *entire auxiliary UNet*. Even though the adapters themselves are lightweight compared with a full model, they still bring an order of magnitude more parameters than either orthogonal or low‑rank updates.

If your deployment budget caps you at “< 1 M added parameters”, OFT or LoRA are the only viable choices.

---

### 2️⃣ Inference Overhead  

A practitioner’s nightmare is a finetuned model that slows down serving latency:

- With OFT you can precompute $\bm{W}= \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ once after training and discard $\bm{R}$. The resulting checkpoint looks exactly like the original diffusion UNet — *no extra matrix multiplication during inference*.
- LoRA can also be merged at export time (`merge_weights=True` in many toolkits). If you keep them separate for experimental flexibility you pay a tiny per‑layer mat‑mul ($\mathcal O(d\,r')$), which is usually negligible compared with the UNet’s convolutional workload.
- ControlNet/T2I‑Adapter keep their side branch alive during generation because they need to inject the control signal at each denoising step; this adds roughly one extra UNet pass worth of FLOPs → noticeable slowdown on GPU or CPU inference pipelines.

Bottom line: **OFT gives you exact inference parity with the base model**, which is rare among controllable diffusion approaches.

---

### 3️⃣ Convergence Speed & Training Stability  

Finetuning diffusion models is expensive: each epoch touches billions of denoising timesteps. Faster convergence saves GPU hours:

| Task | Epochs needed for near‑optimal performance |
|------|--------------------------------------------|
| Landmark→Face (L2F) | **~8** epochs for OFT/COFT vs ≥ 20 epochs for LoRA |
| DreamBooth subject learning | Stable after **400** iterations (OFT/COFT); DreamBooth collapses after ≈ 2000 iterations |
| Canny→Image (C2I) & Segmentation→Image (S2I) | OFT reaches peak consistency within half the epochs required by ControlNet |

The authors attribute this speedup to two factors:

1. **Angle‐preserving updates:** By rotating neurons rather than arbitrarily reshaping them, OFT moves along smoother directions on the hypersphere manifold, leading to better optimization landscapes.
2. **Explicit constraints:** COFT’s radius bound ($\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$) keeps updates close enough to the pretrained weights that training does not overshoot — consequently fewer epochs suffice and hyperparameter tuning becomes less brittle.

In contrast, standard finetuning or DreamBooth rely purely on small learning rates with no structural guarantee; they often wander off into regions where semantic knowledge degrades.

---

### 4️⃣ Final Image Fidelity  

#### Subject–driven Generation  
The paper evaluates three metrics:

| Metric | Meaning | Result trend |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| **DINO similarity** – ViT‐based representation similarity between generated and reference subject images | Subject identity preservation | *OFT/COFT > DreamBooth ≈ LoRA* |
| **CLIP‐I similarity** – CLIP image embedding similarity between generated and reference images | Same as DINO but using CLIP’s joint space | *OFT/COFT lead* |
| **CLIP‐T similarity** – Alignment with textual prompt | Prompt fidelity | Comparable across all methods |
| **LPIPS diversity** – Perceptual difference among multiple generations under same prompt | Diversity / mode coverage | Similar across methods |

Qualitatively the authors show that OFT keeps fine details such as fur texture or facial expression far better than LoRA, while also obeying prompts more faithfully than vanilla DreamBooth.

#### Controllable Generation  
A new “control consistency” metric quantifies how well the output reflects its conditioning signal:

- **Canny→Image:** IoU / F1 between edge map extracted from generated image and input edges — *OFT/COFT achieve highest scores*.
- **Segmentation→Image:** Mean IoU + pixel accuracy vs input segmentation — *again best for OFT/COFT*.
- **Landmark→Face:** Mean $\ell_2$ distance between predicted landmarks from generated faces and input landmarks — *OFT/COFT reduce error by >30 % relative to ControlNet at convergence*.

Across all three tasks these metrics line up with visual examples: sharper edges respecting structures, faithful color propagation from segmentation masks, accurate facial pose from landmarks—all while preserving photorealism that sometimes slips in ControlNet outputs due to its heavier plug‑in network impacting texture synthesis.

---

### 5️⃣ Practical Takeaways  

| Situation | Recommended method(s) |
|-----------|-----------------------|
| You only have a few labelled images (< 10) and need quick subject adaptation → high fidelity ⇒ ***OFT or COFT*** |
| You already work in an ecosystem built around low‑rank adapters & need minimal code changes → modest speedup needed ⇒ ***LoRA*** |
| You require powerful pixel‐level conditioning (depth maps, sketches …) and are willing to pay extra latency ⇒ ***ControlNet/T2I‑Adapter*** |
| You want both fast convergence AND zero inference penalty ⇒ ***COFT*** (adds just a radius bound for stability) |

In practice many teams combine strategies: start with COFT for rapid prototyping, then freeze its orthogonal transforms into the base checkpoint and optionally stack a tiny LoRA head if you later need efficient continual updates.

---

## Closing Thoughts  

Orthogonal Finetuning reframes diffusion adaptation as *rotating* rather than *distorting* learned representations. By preserving hyperspherical energy it keeps the semantic skeleton intact while giving us just enough freedom to imprint new subjects or new control signals. The experimental evidence shows:

- **Parameter frugality:** fewer tunable weights than low-rank adapters.
- **Zero runtime cost:** nothing extra at inference time.
- **Speedy learning:** stable convergence in half as many epochs as competing baselines.
- **Higher fidelity:** both identity preservation in DreamBooth settings and tighter adherence to external controls across edge / segmentation / landmark tasks.

For anyone building production pipelines around Stable Diffusion or any latent diffusion backbone, OFT — especially its constrained variant COFT — offers an attractive middle ground between “tweak everything” (DreamBooth) and “attach another network” (ControlNet). As research continues toward even larger foundation models, methods that respect geometric invariants like orthogonality may become essential building blocks for safe and efficient model stewardship.
