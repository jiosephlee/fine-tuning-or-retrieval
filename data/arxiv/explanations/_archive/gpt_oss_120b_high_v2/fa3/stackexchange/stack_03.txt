\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What do SS‑GEMM and RS‑GEMM stand for in Algorithm 1?
Question:
In Algorithm 1 lines 9–15 the authors label two GEMM calls as SS‑GEMM and RS‑GEMM respectively. Are these abbreviations meant to indicate "shared–shared" versus "register–shared" operand locations, or something else? How does this affect register pressure and memory traffic?
Answer:
**Short answer:**  
In *FlashAttention‑3* the two tags simply describe **where the left‑hand matrix of the WGMMA instruction lives** when a block $\times$ block GEMM is issued:

* **SS‑GEMM** – “*Shared $\to$ Shared*”. Both tiles that participate in the multiplication are read directly from **shared memory** (SMEM). In Algorithm 1 this is used for the query–key product  
  $\displaystyle \mathbf S^{(j)}_i=\;\mathbf Q_i\,\mathbf K_j^{\top}$.

* **RS‑GEMM** – “*Register $\to$ Shared*”. The left operand has already been materialised in **registers** (after soft‑max), while the right operand still sits in **shared memory**. This form is used for the probability–value product  
  $\displaystyle \mathbf O_i=
      \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(e^{\,m_i^{\text {old}}-m_i}\bigr)^{-1}
      \!\,\mathbf O_i
      +\,\widetilde{\!\mathbf P}_i^{(j)}\,\mathbf V_j.$

The letters do **not** refer to “shared–shared vs. register–shared” as a generic description of *all* operands; they only indicate where **the first input** of the WGMMA comes from (the second input is always taken from SMEM).

---

### Why this distinction matters

#### 1. Register pressure  
*SS‑GEMM* needs only enough registers for accumulation (the usual FP32 accumulator used by Tensor Cores). Since both inputs are streamed straight out of SMEM by WGMMA, no extra per‑thread storage is required.

*RS‑GEMM* forces each consumer warpgroup to keep an entire tile of the softmax probabilities $\widetilde{\!\mathbf P}_{{i}}^{(j)}$ live in its private register file until it has been multiplied by $\displaystyle V_j$. For a block size $B_r\times B_c$ this adds roughly  

$$
B_r\,B_c\times\operatorname{sizeof}(\texttt{float})
$$  

bytes per threadblock. On Hopper this extra usage would reduce occupancy if all warps kept their full set of registers, so *FlashAttention‑3* deliberately **reallocates registers** between producer and consumer warpgroups with ``setmaxnreg``: producer warps give up many regs because they only issue TMA loads; consumer warps take them back to hold $\widetilde{\!\mathbf P}$.

#### 2. Memory traffic  
With *SS‑GEMM* no extra movement beyond what was already done when loading $\displaystyle Q_i$ and $\displaystyle K_j$ into SMEM via TMA occurs – WGMMA pulls tiles straight out of SMEM onto Tensor Cores.  

With *RS‑GEMM* we avoid an additional write–read round‑trip that a naïve implementation would incur if it stored $\displaystyle \widetilde{\!\mathbf P}$ back to shared (or global) memory before multiplying by $V$. Because $\displaystyle \widetilde{\!\mathbf P}$ stays resident in regs, each key/value block incurs **one load of $V_j$** but zero extra store / load of intermediate probabilities. Consequently global‐memory traffic stays at the theoretical minimum needed for attention (the three reads of $Q,K,V$ plus one write of $O$).

#### 3. Interaction with asynchrony & warp specialization  
Hopper’s Tensor Cores execute *asynchronously*: a WGMMA launched by a consumer warp can run while another warpgroup issues TMA loads for the next key/value pair. The *SS/RS* distinction fits naturally into this pipeline:

*Producer* warps load $K_j,V_j$\;\;$\rightarrow$\;\;signal ready $\rightarrow$\;\;consumer warps start an **SS‑GEMM** ($QK^{\top}$). As soon as all rows of softmax are available, they hold $\displaystyle \widetilde{\!\mathbf P}$ in regs and issue an **RS‑GEMM** ($\displaystyle \widetilde{\!\mathbf P}V$) *without waiting* for any further store/load operations. Meanwhile producer warps begin loading $K_{j+1},V_{j+1}$. Thus computation on Tensor Cores overlaps with data movement and with lightweight non‑matmul work (exponential, reduction), which is exactly what “producer–consumer asynchrony” aims at.

---

### Broader perspective

The naming convention mirrors earlier FlashAttention papers where “S’’ denotes “operand residing in *shared memory’’’’ – a location that can be consumed by asynchronous WGMMAs without copying – whereas “R’’ denotes “operand already materialised in *registers’’.’’ The choice between these two paths is a classic GPU performance trade‑off:

- **More data movement → lower register usage → higher occupancy**, but potentially higher bandwidth consumption.
- **More data kept close (registers) → higher occupancy cost**, yet reduction of intermediate traffic and enabling deeper software pipelining.

On modern GPUs such as Hopper these tradeoffs become visible because Tensor Core throughput far outpaces special‐function units (exponential), making every saved load/store worthwhile when overlapping becomes possible.

---

### TL;DR

- **SS‑GEMM** = both tiles supplied directly from *shared memory*. Used for $QK^{\top}$; low register usage.
- **RS‑GEMM** = left tile supplied from *registers*, right tile still from *shared memory*. Used for $\displaystyle \widetilde{\!\mathbf P}V$; incurs extra register pressure but eliminates an extra shared‐memory roundtrip.
- The distinction drives how much register space consumer warps reserve (`setmaxnreg`), how many loads/stores occur, and ultimately enables the asynchronous pipeline that gives FlashAttention‑3 its \(1\dfrac12{-}2\times\) speedup on H100.
