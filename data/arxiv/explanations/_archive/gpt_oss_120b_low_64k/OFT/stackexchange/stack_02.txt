\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does applying a layer‑shared orthogonal matrix preserve hyperspherical energy?
Question:
In Section 3, the authors claim that if the finetuned weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}\) satisfies \(\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\) with \(\mathbf{R}\) orthogonal, then the hyperspherical energy remains unchanged. Could someone walk through the derivation showing that \(\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j\| = \|\widehat{(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}^0_i)}-\widehat{(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}^0_j)}\|\) holds, and clarify why this equality holds for any orthogonal \(\mathbf{R}\)?
Answer:
Let  

\[
\mathbf W^{0}= \bigl[\mathbf w^{0}_{1},\dots ,\mathbf w^{0}_{n}\bigr]\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},
\qquad 
\hat{\mathbf w}^{0}_{i}= \frac{\mathbf w^{0}_{i}}{\|\mathbf w^{0}_{i}\|}
\]

be the pretrained neurons and their unit‑norm versions.  
During Orthogonal Finetuning we replace the weight matrix by  

\[
\mathbf W = \mathbf R\,\mathbf W^{0},
\qquad 
\mathbf R^\top \mathbf R = \mathbf I ,
\]

so that each finetuned neuron is  

\[
\mathbf w_{i}= \mathbf R\,\mathbf w^{0}_{i}.
\]

The hyperspherical energy depends only on the pairwise Euclidean distances of the **normalized** neurons:

\[
\operatorname{HE}(\mathbf W)= \sum_{i\neq j}\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf w}_{j}\bigr\|^{-1},
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}= \frac{\mathbf w_{i}}{\|\mathbf w_{i}\|}.
\]

Hence we must show that for any orthogonal \(\mathbf R\)

\[
\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf w}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf w}_{j}\bigr\|
   = 
   \bigl\|\widehat{(\mathbf R \mathbf w^{0}_{i})}
          - \widehat{(\mathbf R \mathbf w^{0}_{j})}\bigr\|.
\tag{1}
\]

   \[

        = \mathbf v^\top (\mathbf R^\top \mathbf R) \mathbf v
        = \|\mathbf v\|^2 .
   \]

   Consequently,  

   \[
   \|\mathbf R \mathbf w^{0}_{i}\| = \|\mathbf w^{0}_{i}\|,
   \qquad
   \|\mathbf R \mathbf w^{0}_{j}\| = \|\mathbf w^{0}_{j}\|.
   \tag{2}
   \]

### Why it holds for *any* orthogonal \(R$

The proof above relied only on two algebraic properties of orthogonal matrices:

* **Norm preservation** – \(‖R v‖=‖v‖.\)  
* **Isometry** – distances are unchanged under a common left‑multiplication: \(‖R a - R b‖ = ‖a-b‖.\)

Both properties are satisfied by every matrix whose transpose equals its inverse, i.e., by every element of the orthogonal group \(O(d)\). No further structure (determinant = 1, block‑diagonal form, etc.) is needed for the invariance; those extra constraints are introduced only for parameter‑efficiency, not for correctness of the distance preservation.

Because hyperspherical energy is a sum of a (monotonically decreasing) function of these distances—typically \(‖·‖^{-1}\)—the whole energy remains exactly the same:

```latex
HE(R W^0) 
  = 
  {\sum_{i≠j} 
    ‖\,R w_i^0/‖R w_i^0‖-R w_j^0/‖R w_j^0‖‖^{-1}}
  =
  {\sum_{i≠j} 
    ‖\,w_i^0/‖w_i^0‖-w_j^0/‖w_j^0‖‖^{-1}}
  =
  HE(W^0).
```

Therefore, as stated in Section 3 of the paper, any layer‑shared orthogonal transformation leaves hyperspherical energy untouched, which is why Orthogonal Finetuning can modify a model while guaranteeing that this structural measure of the pretrained network is preserved.
