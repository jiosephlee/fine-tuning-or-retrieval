# Benchmarks & Beyond: How xLSTM Stacks Up Against Transformers, SSMs, and Modern RNNs

In the past two decades, Long Short‑Term Memory (LSTM) networks have been the workhorse of sequence modeling. Yet, since 2017 Transformers have dominated language modeling, and more recently State‑Space Models (SSMs) and linear‑attention RNNs have entered the arena. The new **xLSTM** family—built on exponential gating, scalar‑mixing sLSTM, and matrix‑memory mLSTM—asks a simple question: *What happens if we bring LSTMs up to the scale of today’s large language models (LLMs) while fixing their classic shortcomings?*  

Below we walk through the extensive empirical suite presented in the xLSTM paper, translating perplexities, accuracy numbers, and scaling‑law plots into practical advice for practitioners who are deciding which architecture to try next.

---

## 1. From Toy Problems to Real‑World Text

The authors evaluate xLSTM on four increasingly realistic benchmarks:

| Benchmark | What it tests | Why it matters |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Synthetic formal‑language tasks** | State tracking / memory revision (e.g., parity, nested parentheses) | Highlights the ability to *revise* stored information—something vanilla LSTMs struggle with. |
| **Multi‑Query Associative Recall** | Storing and retrieving many key–value pairs (up to 256 pairs, sequence length 2048) | Directly probes *storage capacity* of the model’s memory. |
| **Long‑Range Arena (LRA)** | Diverse long‑sequence tasks (list ops, image classification from pixels, etc.) | Measures how well a model scales with context length. |
| **Large‑scale language modeling** | Autoregressive training on 15 B and 300 B tokens from SlimPajama; evaluation on validation perplexity, downstream reasoning tasks, and the PALOMA benchmark (571 domains). | The ultimate test: does the model compete with state‑of‑the‑art LLMs? |

---

## 2. Synthetic Formal Languages – Do Exponential Gates Help?

### The problem
Classic LSTMs have a hard time *revising* a decision once a token is stored (think of a nearest‑neighbor search where a later vector is more similar). The paper uses a suite of formal languages that require such revisions.

### Results at a glance
- **xLSTM[0:1]** (pure sLSTM) and **xLSTM[1:0]** (pure mLSTM) both achieve near‑perfect accuracy (> 0.95) on parity and copy tasks.
- Competing Transformers, SSMs (e.g., Mamba), and even vanilla RNNs hover around 0.6–0.7 accuracy—far from solving the task.
- Adding *memory mixing* (the hidden‑to‑hidden connections in sLSTM) is crucial; models without it cannot track state.

### Takeaway
If your application needs to constantly overwrite or refine internal state—think online recommendation systems or incremental code analysis—xLSTM’s exponential gating gives you a solid advantage over both Transformer‑style attention and traditional RNNs.

---

## 3. Associative Recall – How Much Can You Remember?

### The setup
Each sequence contains many random key–value pairs; later a query asks for the value belonging to a specific key. The task is scaled up to 256 pairs with sequences up to 2048 tokens.

### Head‑to‑head numbers  
(Accuracy reported for a 2‑block architecture; higher is better)

| Model                | 64 pairs | 128 pairs | 256 pairs |
|----------------------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Transformer (Llama)** | 0.98     | 0.95      | 0.88 |
| **xLSTM[1:1]** (mixed)   | **0.99** | **0.97**   | **0.93** |
| **xLSTM[1:0]** (mLSTM only) | 0.98     | 0.96      | 0.91 |
| RWKV‑5 / RWKV‑6      | 0.94     | 0.89      | 0.80 |
| Mamba                | 0.93     | 0.86      | 0.74 |

### What’s happening?
- The matrix memory of mLSTM implements a *covariance update rule* (essentially an outer‑product fast weight), which dramatically expands storage capacity without blowing up parameters.
- Surprisingly, adding an sLSTM block on top (**xLSTM[1:1]**) improves performance even on the hardest setting, indicating that scalar mixing helps organize the matrix memory.

### Takeaway
When you need high‐capacity associative memory—e.g., retrieval‑augmented generation or large knowledge bases—xLSTM’s matrix memory beats most modern RNNs and even rivals Transformers while keeping linear time complexity in sequence length.

---

## 4. Long‑Range Arena – Scaling to Long Contexts

The authors evaluate xLSTM on all six LRA tasks (ListOps, byte-level text classification, etc.). Across the board:

- **xLSTM** matches or slightly exceeds the best SSM baseline.
- Performance degrades gracefully as context length grows to 4096 tokens.
- Unlike vanilla Transformers whose quadratic cost forces severe truncation, xLSTM maintains constant memory usage.

**Practical implication:** For workloads where you cannot afford to truncate inputs—long documents, DNA sequences, or video streams—xLSTM provides linear‐time inference while staying competitive in accuracy.

---

## 5. Large‑Scale Language Modeling – Real LLM Competition

### Training regimes
- **15 B token run:** Models roughly comparable in size to GPT‑3 “small” (≈ 350 M parameters).  
- **300 B token run:** Four scales (125 M → 1.3 B parameters), matching recent LLM training budgets.

All models share the same pre‑LayerNorm residual backbone; only the recurrent core varies.

### Validation perplexity (15 B tokens)

| Model                     | Validation ppl |
|---------------------------|----------------|
| GPT‑3 (Transformer)       | 13.4 |
| LLaMA                     | 12.9 |
| Mamba (SSM)               | 12.7 |
| RWKV‑4 (RNN)              | 13.2 |
| **xLSTM[7:1]**            | **12.3** |
| **xLSTM[1:0]**            | **12.2** |

*Lower perplexity = better predictive power.*

### Scaling law curves
When plotting loss vs. compute (`tokens × parameters`), all families follow a clear power law,
\[
\text{Loss} = A \cdot (\text{Compute})^{-\alpha} + B,
\]
with:
- Similar exponent \(\alpha \approx 0.07\) across models,
- **xLSTM’s offset \(A\)** being ~10–15 % lower than Transformers and ~5–8 % lower than SSMs.

Thus for larger models (10+ B parameters), xLSTM is expected to stay ahead by roughly the same margin.

### Sequence length extrapolation (1.3 B models)
All models trained at context = 2048 tokens:
- Transformers’ perplexity climbs sharply beyond ≤ 4096 tokens.
- Mamba shows moderate increase.
- **xLSTM retains near‑training perplexity up to ≈ 16k tokens**, confirming linear scaling of compute and memory.

### Downstream reasoning tasks
Across five common-sense benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, HellaSwag, etc.):

- xLSTM wins on *four* of five tasks for every model size.
- Only ARC sometimes favors Mamba at the largest scale.

### PALOMA benchmark (571 domains)
For each domain we compute per-token perplexity:

- xLSTM[1:0] beats Mamba in **568/571** domains (~99.5 %).
- Beats LLaMA in **486/571** domains (~85 %).
- Beats RWKV‑4 in **570/571** domains (~99.8 %).

The improvement is especially pronounced on low‑resource or highly specialized domains where long context matters.

### Inference speed & throughput
Because xLSTM’s recurrence is linear in sequence length:

- Generation time grows linearly like Mamba/RWKV but *much slower* than quadratic Transformers.
- At batch size 1 and prefill = 16 tokens, xLSTM achieves higher throughput than LLaMA even when LLaMA uses an optimized KV cache.
- Larger batch sizes are feasible without exhausting GPU memory—a key advantage for serving many concurrent requests.

## 6. What Should Practitioners Take Away?

1️⃣ **If you need long context + constant memory**, choose xLSTM over vanilla Transformers or SSMs—especially for tasks such as legal document analysis or scientific paper summarization.

2️⃣ **When high storage capacity is critical**, rely on the mLSTM variant with its matrix memory; it outperforms fast-weight baselines like RWKV while remaining linear in time.

3️⃣ **For state tracking / revision problems**, incorporate sLSTM blocks with exponential gating; they solve formal languages that other modern architectures cannot.

4️⃣ **Scaling confidence:** The scaling-law analysis shows that xLSTM’s advantage persists as you increase data and parameters; you can expect similar relative gains at multi‑billion parameter scales.

5️⃣ **Implementation notes:**  
   - sLSTM cannot be fully parallelized due to hidden–hidden mixing; however, a custom CUDA kernel makes it only ~2× slower than fully parallel mLSTM.  
   - mLSTM’s matrix ops are still heavier than FlashAttention but can be accelerated with optimized kernels; current overhead is modest compared to overall training time.

---

## 7. Bottom Line

The experimental suite paints a consistent picture: ***xLSTM matches or exceeds Transformer and State‑Space baselines across synthetic reasoning tasks, associative recall, long-range benchmarks, and full-scale language modeling***—all while preserving linear time/memory complexity.

For anyone building next-generation LLMs—or even domain-specific models where context length and memory revision are decisive—xLSTM offers a compelling alternative that blends the classic strengths of recurrent networks with modern scaling tricks borrowed from Transformers.
