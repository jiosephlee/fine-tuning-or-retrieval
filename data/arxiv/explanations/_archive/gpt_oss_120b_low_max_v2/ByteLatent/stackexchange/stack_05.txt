\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Why does the FLOPs per byte equation include a term \(V=256\) only for the decoder?
Question:
In Section 3.5, FLOPs per byte are summed over encoder, global transformer, decoder, and cross‑attention components. The decoder term multiplies \(\text{Transf.FL}(h_{\mathcal{D}}, l_{\mathcal{D}}, m_{\mathcal{D}}, V=256)\). I assume \(V\) stands for vocabulary size for the output projection, but why is this term omitted for the encoder and global transformer? Are those modules truly vocabulary‑free, or is there an implicit assumption that their output dimension does not depend on \(V\)?
Answer:
In the FLOP‑per‑byte budget the authors separate the cost of each block of the model and then sum them.  
The term  

$$
\text{Transf.FL}(h_{\mathcal D},l_{\mathcal D},m=w_{\mathcal D},V=256)
$$

appears only in the **decoder** because the decoder is the only component that has to **produce a probability distribution over the byte vocabulary** at every generation step.

Recall how the three modules are wired:

1. **Local encoder \( \mathcal E\)** receives a sequence of raw bytes, embeds them (the embedding lookup is treated as zero‑flop) and passes the resulting hidden states through a few transformer layers. Its output is a set of *patch embeddings* that are fed to the global latent transformer. The encoder never has to map its hidden vectors onto concrete byte symbols, so there is no final linear layer whose size depends on the vocabulary.

2. **Global latent transformer \( \mathcal G\)** operates entirely on those patch embeddings. It is an autoregressive transformer that predicts the *next patch representation*; again the output is a latent vector of dimension \(h_{\mathcal G}\). No soft‑max over bytes is performed here, therefore its FLOP count does **not** involve the vocabulary size.

3. **Local decoder \( \mathcal D\)** receives the patch representations produced by \( \mathcal G\), expands them back to a byte‑level sequence via cross‑attention and a few transformer layers, and finally projects each byte‑level hidden state onto the 256‑element byte vocabulary before applying a soft‑max (or computing cross‑entropy during training). This projection can be written as a matrix multiplication  
   $$
   \mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}}\,\mathbf{d}_i,\qquad 
   \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times h_{\mathcal D}},
   $$  
   which costs \(2\,h_{\mathcal D}\times 256\) FLOPs per token (multiply‑add counted as two FLOPs). Because this term dominates the decoder’s computational load for every generated byte, the FLOP formula for the decoder includes an explicit factor \(V=256\).

Hence **\(V\) is omitted for the encoder and global transformer simply because those modules never perform a vocab‑size linear projection**; their work is confined to transforming latent vectors whose dimensionality does not grow with the size of the byte alphabet. The only place where the size of the output layer matters is in the decoder, where we must map from hidden space to a distribution over 256 possible bytes, and that is why the authors write \(V=256\) only for that term.
