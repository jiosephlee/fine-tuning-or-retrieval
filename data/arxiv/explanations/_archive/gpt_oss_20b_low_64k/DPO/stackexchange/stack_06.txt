### Can you walk through an example of deriving Equation (8) from Equation (7) by canceling the partition function?
Question:
Equation (7) expresses human preferences via a Bradley‑Terry model using rewards derived from Eq (6). The paper then simplifies to Eq (8), where $Z(x)$ disappears. Could you provide a step‑by‑step derivation showing exactly how $Z(x)$ cancels and why it only depends on $x$? This would help me understand why preferences only depend on ratios of policies.
Answer:
**Derivation of Eq. (8) from Eq. (7)**  
Recall the two equations in the paper:

* **Bradley–Terry preference model** (Eq. (7) in the paper)

\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)=
\frac{\exp\!\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)}%
     {\exp\!\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{1})\bigr)+\exp\!\bigl(r^{*}(x,y_{2})\bigr)} .
\tag{7}
\]

* **Re‑parameterization of a reward in terms of an optimal policy** (Eq. (6) in the paper)

\[
r(x,y)=
\beta\,\log \frac{\pi_{r}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}
+\beta\,\log Z(x),      \qquad
Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)
      \exp \!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}\, r(x,y)\Bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

Here \(Z(x)\) is a *partition function* that depends only on the conditioning prompt \(x\); it does not involve the particular completion \(y\).

We want to replace the ground‐truth reward \(r^{*}\) in (7) by its re‑parameterized form.  
Below are the algebraic steps showing that \(Z(x)\) disappears altogether.

---

### 1. Substitute the re‑parameterized reward

Put 
\(r^{*}(x,y)=
   \beta\, \log \tfrac{\pi^{*}(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}
   + \beta\, \log Z(x)\)
into (7).  
For brevity write \(a_{i}=r^{*}(x,y_{i})\). Then

\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)
  = \frac{e^{a_{1}}}{e^{a_{1}}+e^{a_{2}}}
  = \frac{e^{a_{1}}}{e^{a_{1}}+e^{a_{2}}}.
\]

Now substitute for each \(a_{i}\):

\[
e^{a_{i}}
= e^{\,\beta\, \log \tfrac{\pi^{*}(y_{i}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{i}|x)}}
  \; e^{\,\beta\, \log Z(x)}
= Z(x)^\beta
  \, \Bigl(\tfrac{\pi^{*}(y_{i}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{i}|x)}\Bigr)^\beta .
\]

Because the same factor \(Z(x)^\beta\) multiplies **both** numerator and denominator, it cancels:

\[
p^{*}(y_{1}\succ y_{2}\mid x)
 = 
 \frac{\bigl(\tfrac{\pi^{*}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1}|x)}\bigr)^\beta}
      {\bigl(\tfrac{\pi^{*}(y_{1}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{1}|x)}\bigr)^\beta
       + 
       \bigl(\tfrac{\pi^{*} (y_{2}|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{2}|x)}\bigr)^\beta }.
     \tag{A}
\]

Notice how every occurrence of \(Z(x)\) has vanished; the quantity that remains depends **only** on the two policies evaluated at their respective completions.

**Answer**

To go from Eq.(7)—the Bradley–Terry preference model—to Eq.(8)—the same model expressed only through policies—the paper replaces the reward function by its *re‑parameterised* form that contains a partition function \(Z(x)\). Because this partition function depends only on the conditioning prompt \(x$, it vanishes when we take ratios of two rewards, leaving preferences to depend solely on ratios of optimal and reference policies.
