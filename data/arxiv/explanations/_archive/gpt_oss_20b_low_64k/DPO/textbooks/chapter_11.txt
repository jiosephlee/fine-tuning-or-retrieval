### Transfer from Reddit TL;DR to CNN/DailyMail News Summarization

A key concern when deploying language models in practice is their ability to generalize beyond the data distribution used during training. In the context of preference‑driven fine‑tuning, it is particularly important to understand whether the preferences captured on one domain remain valid on another and whether the fine‑tuned policy can extrapolate effectively without catastrophic overfitting. To address this question we assembled an experiment that transfers a policy trained on Reddit forum posts to a completely different news article domain – the CNN/DailyMail summarization dataset.

#### Experimental Setup

The source domain consisted of 200 k forum posts from Reddit’s *r/TL;DR* sub‑reddit together with human‐labelled preference pairs indicating which of two candidate summaries better captures the main story. Following prior work, we used a standard GPT‑2–based encoder–decoder model pre‑trained on large corpora, fine‑tuned with supervised learning (SFT) on human‑written forum summaries and then further refined via one of three preference–learning algorithms:

1. **PPO + reward model** (our baseline RLHF pipeline);
2. **DPO** (direct preference optimisation);
3. **Preferred–FT** (supervised fine‑tuning only on the better of each pair).

For each algorithm we selected the training run that achieved the highest win rate on a held‑out validation set at its optimal sampling temperature \(T^*\). Importantly, all three models were *identical* in architecture and initialisation and differed only in the objective used during fine‑tuning.

The target domain comprised 10 k news articles from the CNN/DailyMail dataset, chosen because they share the same summarization task but differ significantly in style (formal news prose versus informal forum text), vocabulary frequency distribution, and content scaffolding (e.g., anchor headlines). There was no overlap in documents or entities between source and target corpora to preclude trivial memorisation.

#### Evaluation Metrics

Since ground‑truth summarisation quality in CNN/DailyMail is notoriously hard to evaluate automatically, we employed two complementary metrics:

- **Human‐style Win Rate via GPT‑4**: For each prompt we sampled \(N=5\) candidate summaries from each model using its recommendation for likelihood temperature. A carefully curated GPT‑4 prompt (the “concise” instruction set) classified which of two summaries is more concise while maintaining coverage of key facts. The win rate is defined as
  \[
  \text{win\_rate} = \frac{\#\{\text{GPT–4 selects policy A}\}}{\#\{\text{comparisons}\}}.
  \]
- **ROUGE‐L Score**: Although not perfect, we report ROUGE‐L between the model summary and reference headline as a secondary sanity check.

We also computed per‑token KL divergence between each policy’s output distribution and that of the reference SFT model on source data to quantify drift during fine‑tuning.

#### Results

Table 1 displays all three models’ performance on both domains:

| Model | Source Domain Win % | Target Domain Win % | Delta | ROUGE-L Source | ROUGE-L Target |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| PPO | 57 ± 3 | 42 ± 4 | –15 | 29 ± 1 | 19 ± 1 |
| DPO | 61 ± 2 | **53 ± 3** | –8 | 30 ± 1 | **23 ± 1** |
| Prefet ‑ FT | 55 ± 4 | 40 ± 5 | –15 | 28 ± 2 | 18 ± 2 |

Numbers in bold denote statistically significant improvements over PPO under paired bootstrap testing (\(p<0.05\)). The DPO policy demonstrates a notably smaller performance drop when transferred to media articles (Δ = –8 %) compared with PPO (Δ = –15 %), suggesting it retains more generalizable structure from human preferences.

The KL divergence analysis revealed that DPO drifted less away from its source reference distribution (\(\Delta_{\text{KL}}=0.12\) bits per token) than PPO (\(\Delta_{\text{KL}}=0.31\)), indicating that DPO’s implicit reward reparameterisation preserves policy entropy and hence exploratory capacity across domains.

In qualitative inspection, DPO outputs balanced coverage: they tended to include more factual grounding sentences even when structurally divergent from source forum posts, whereas PPO outputs displayed a higher variance with some summaries overly generic or missing key details—consistent with larger KL drift leading towards mode collapse on unfamiliar inputs.

#### Discussion

These findings highlight that directly optimising logits against human preferences via DPO confers an advantage in out‑of‑distribution generalisation relative to reinforcement learning–based approaches. By sidestepping an explicit reward model, DPO eliminates an intermediate source of misalignment that can magnify when shifting domains: rewards learned from Reddit may be poorly calibrated for news articles, but PPO must learn a policy that first maximises those potentially misspecified rewards, thereby amplifying errors before sampling is performed at test time. In contrast, DPO’s loss already incorporates the KL constraint implicitly; thus it can preserve more faithful probability mass across diverse vocabularies and constructions present in unseen inputs.

### GPT-4 Win Rate Comparisons Under Domain Shift

The experimental design above relies crucially on GPT‑4 as a stand­alone proxy for human evaluation under domain shift scenarios where rapid human annotation would be impractical or prohibitively expensive. To validate that GPT‑4 judgments remain trustworthy after changing text type from informal forum posts to formal newspaper articles, we performed a controlled study comparing GPT‑4 win rates against small sets of human annotators in both domains.

#### Methodology

We randomly selected 60 prompts from each domain (Reddit TL;DR and CNN/DailyMail), generating two summaries per prompt using both PPO and DPO at their optimal temperatures (T=0 for PPO, T=0.25 for DPO). Each pair was given joint evaluation requests to:

1. A GPT‑4 instance with the “concise” prompt,
2. Five independent crowd workers (via Mechanical Turk) who rated “Which summary better captures essential content while remaining concise?”

All annotative tasks were presented without revealing which summary corresponded to which model to prevent bias.

#### Result Summary

Table 2 reports match rates between GPT‑4 decisions and human majority votes:

| Domain | Model | GPT–4 % Match vs Humans | Inter-Human Agreement |
|--------|-------|-------------------------|-----------------------|
| Reddit TL;DR | PPO | 81 % | N/A |
| Reddit TL;DR | DPO | **88 %** | N/A |
| cnn/dailymail newssummary | PPO | **75 %** | N/A |
| cnn/dailymail newssummary | DPO | 82 % | N/A |

The interhuman agreement was calculated at ~83 % using Fleiss’ κ across majority votes for all pairs and aligns closely with GPT‑4 agreement against humans—particularly when evaluating the DPO outputs under domain shift.

#### Interpretation

These results confirm that GPT‑4 retains discriminative power across domains even after shifting content style dramatically; its judgments correlate well with those of real humans measured through majority voting—specifically exceeding interhuman agreement slightly but within expected statistical variance given sample sizes.

Moreover, since both training algorithms exhibited smaller drop‐offs under domain shift for DPO versus PPO as seen earlier, tools such as GPT‑4 can effectively aid researchers by substituting costly human evaluation pipelines during out-of-distribution tests without sacrificing reliability.

### Final Remarks

The chapter demonstrates systematically that policies trained with Direct Preference Optimization not only compete with but can surpass RLHF baselines when deployed outside their training distribution. By harnessing an analytic mapping between policies and reward functions without requiring intermediate reward modelling or stochastic policy optimisation loops, DPO inherently restricts excessive drift whilst still rewarding preferred behaviours—a trait visibly manifesting as reduced performance degradation upon domain transfer.

Future work should investigate whether similar benefits extend to other modalities (e.g., dialogue agents encountering novel user demographics) and whether augmenting DPO training with self-supervised signals or synthetic preferences can further stabilise out-of-distribution behaviour at scale.»
