# xLSTM in Action: Benchmarking on Real‑Language Tasks (PALOMA, Downstream Reasoning)

## TL;DR
xLSTM—an extended LSTM architecture that plugs exponential gating and a matrix memory into modern residual backbones—outperforms contemporary Transformers (e.g. Llama) and State‑Space Models (e.g. Mamba) both on perplexity and on downstream reasoning tasks. In the PALOMA benchmark, which spans 571 heterogeneous text domains, xLSTM[1:0] achieves lower perplexities on 99 % of the domains, while still being faster at generation time thanks to its linear‑in‑sequence‑length computation.

---

## 1. Why benchmark on *real* data?

In language‑model research we often test on synthetic tasks (copying, associative recall) or small corpora because they expose individual architectural traits. However, LLM performance ultimately hinges on **generalization to billions of tokens** and **extrapolation across styles and formats**. The PALOMA benchmark was designed precisely for this: 571 distinct domains ranging from news sites to Reddit discussion threads. Evaluating across this spread gives a robust picture of a model’s *domain resilience*—something that is critical when deploying an LLM in the wild.

---

## 2. Setting up the experiments

### 2.1 Data & training

All models were trained on **300 B tokens from SlimPajama** (a curated mix of public text). The tokenization was consistent across models: a BPE vocabulary of 50 k tokens with the same sub‑token statistics as used for Llama-2.

Four model families were compared:

| Family | Representative | Size | Key innovations |
|--------|----------------|------|-----------------|
| Transformers | Llama | 350 M–1.3 B | Multi‑head self‑attention, off‑policy sampling |
| State‑Space Models | Mamba | 350 M–1.3 B | Gated SSM with depthwise convolution |
| RNN / Linear Transformers | RWKV-4 | 350 M–1.3 B | Linear attention + embedding weight tying |
| **Extended LSTMs** | **xLSTM[1:0]** <br> xLSTM[7:1] | 350 M–1.3 B | Exponential gating + matrix memory + residual blocks |

All models used the same training recipe: AdamW with a learning‑rate warmup for 20 k steps followed by cosine decay over 140 k steps, mixed precision (`float16`/`bfloat16`), and the same dropout schedule.

### 2.2 Evaluation metrics

* **Perplexity** – standard next‐token prediction measure.
* **Downstream reasoning scores** – via GPT‐4‐based evaluation on ARC, OpenBookQA, Winograder, etc.
* **Generation speed & throughput** – sampled from inference benchmarks at batch = 1 with pre‑fill length = 16.

## 3. PALOMA: Perplexity over 571 domains

Figure 1 (illustrated here in chart form) displays the per‑domain perplexities for three representative sizes: 350 M, 760 M, and **1.3 B** parameters.

```
Domain     Llama   Mamba   xLSTM[1:0]
nytimes.com    6.8     5.9     5.4
reddit.com/r/askscience   13.2    12.0    10.8
...
```

### Key observations

| Observation | What it means |
|-------------|---------------|
| **xLSTM[1:0] beats Llama in ~99% of domains** at all sizes | The matrix memory improves recall of long‑range dependencies that Transformer caches miss on shaky contexts (e.g., informal Reddit threads). |
| **xLSTM[7:1] performs worse than [1:0] but better than Mamba** | Mixing a small proportion of sLSTM layers helps with state tracking but introduces serial bottlenecks that degrade overall throughput; plenty of matrix layers dominate capacity on large datasets. |
| **Perplexity gap widens with size** ≈ 0.8 points at 1.3 B | Larger models benefit more from the exponential gate’s ability to *revise* earlier storage decisions — a property unique to RNNs.*

The cumulative distribution plot shows that while Llama’s best performance peaks around perplexity ≈5, xLSTM consistently stays below that threshold across domains.

## 4. Downstream reasoning prowess

Table 2 reports the zero‑shot accuracy on a handful of common sense question sets:

| Dataset      | Llama-2 Large (13B) | Mamba (350M) | xLSTM[1:0] (350M) |
|--------------|--------------------|--------------|-------------------|
| ARC          | 42%                | 34%          | **46%**           |
| OpenBookQA   | 57%                | 50%          | **61%**           |
| Winograder   | 73%                | 69%          | **77%**           |

Even at comparable parameter budgets (~350 M), xLSTM overtakes its Transformer counterparts by roughly ~5 points per task—a sizeable margin given the hardness of these benchmarks.

#### Why does it help?

- Exponential gating gives a sharper “forget” signal enabling dynamic pruning of irrelevant state bits.
- The matrix memory permits *joint storage* of key–value pairs across all hidden dimensions without requiring explicit cross–token attention.

## 6. Takeaways for practitioners

### Engineering trade–offs

- If you need a single–node inference pipeline that scales to longer contexts (>16k tokens), go for xLSTM[1:0]. Its linear runtime keeps latency predictable.
- If your deployment budget is *very tight* and batch sizes are small (<8), the marginal speedup over LWKV or even Transformer may not justify integrating cuDNN kernels for xLSTM; however you gain significantly better perplexity on noisy or informal text sources.
- For *extremely large contexts* (>32k), architecture changes (hierarchical s/m mixing) might be required; current mLSTM numpys quasilinear scaling is already robust up to *16k*, beyond which memory bandwidth becomes dominant.

### Research directions

- **Hybrid memory mixing:** Combine sLSTM layers early in depth to capture state-tracking nuance while leveraging mLSTM later to saturate capacity.
- **Cache optimizations:** Wrap mLSTM updates into fused kernels akin to FlashAttention’s scan primitives; expected speedups > ×2.
- **Scaling laws transfer:** Since xLSTM follows similar power–law scaling as Transformers but with an offset (~0.6–0.8 bits lower), extrapolating to >10 B params should yield competitive even against Gopher or GPT‑4 sized models with equivalent compute budgets.

---

## Conclusion

The benchmark evidence substantiates the claim made in the *xLSTM* paper that scaling an RNN variant can match or surpass model families built around self-attention or SSMs when carefully engineered:

- Lower perplexities across almost all real‑world domains.
- Higher zero-shot reasoning accuracy.
- Faster inference thanks to linear-time decoding.

For teams looking to push language modelling beyond current transformer ecosystems—especially where latency precision or energy efficiency matters—xLSTM offers a compelling alternative that unifies the strengths of memory gating with large-scale parallelism.@"
