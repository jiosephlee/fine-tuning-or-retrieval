# From Chain‑of‑Thought to Tool‑Integrated Reasoning:
## Instruction‑Tuning Secrets Behind DeepSeekMath’s Success

DeepSeekMath has taken the open‑source math arena by storm.  Its “instruct” version—**DeepSeekMath‑Instruct 7B**—achieves record scores on both English and Chinese benchmarks (over 50 % on the competition–level MATH dataset) without relying on gigantic model sizes or costly external pipelines.  
What makes this happen?  The answer lies in a **carefully curated instruction‑tuning dataset of 776 k examples** that blends three complementary styles:

1. **Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT)** – step‑by‑step text reasoning  
2. **Program‑of‑Thought (PoT)** – solutions that generate Python code  
3. **Tool‑Integrated Reasoning** – human‑written solutions that explicitly invoke libraries (e.g., `math`, `sympy`) or call out to external services  

Below we unpack how each component is constructed, why multilingual coverage matters, and how the training pipeline turns these examples into a model that genuinely understands math.

---

## 1. Building the Instruction Corpus

### 1.1 Sourcing Problems

- **English collections**: GSM8K, MATH (both quantitative reasoning, with the CNN version, etc.), subsets of MathInstruct, and the Lila-OOD dataset.  
- **Chinese collections**: ~76 K K–12 problems covering core topics such as linear equations, probability, geometry basics, and higher‐order algebraic identities.

The datasets span grades from elementary to college level, ensuring that the model sees a spectrum of difficulty and problem styles.

### 1.2 Annotating Solvers

For every problem we generate *three* solution formats:

| Format | What it looks like | Why it matters |
|--------|--------------------|----------------|
| **CoT** | A narrative “let \(x =\ …\)” disk wrapped in natural language. | Trains the model to reason *by writing* in plain text – the classic approach for hard arithmetic and algebraic steps. |
| **PoT** | A fenced block of Python code that computes the answer (often leveraging `sympy` for symbolic manipulation). | Turns hard symbolic steps into executable code; reinforces the link between language and numerical computation. |
| **Tool‑Integrated** | A hybrid mix—first a text explanation, then an inline or separate call to an API/library, finishing with a final answer line. | Closest to real conversational agents that may invoke calculators or theorem provers; this format keeps the chain of reasoning visible while still allowing tool use. |

Human annotators rewrote solutions for a subset (the pilot set) and an *automatic* pipeline generated CoT/PoT from existing annotated datasets where possible.

### 1.3 Multi‑Language Mixing

Unlike many prior math models that focus exclusively on English data, DeepSeekMath tackles both English and Chinese at once:

- **Why dual languages?** The same mathematical concepts are presented with different vocabularies and syntactic patterns in Mandarin vs English.  
- **How does it improve?** Training on two natural languages forces the model’s internal representations of symbols (e.g., variables, operators) to be language‑agnostic while still capturing linguistic nuances, leading to better transfer between languages.

---

## 2. How Three Reasoning Styles Complement Each Other

### 2.1 Strengthening Universal Reasoning

- **CoT** teaches “thinking aloud” – each token is conditioned on previously generated tokens; useful for long, interdependent chains common in proof sketches.
- **PoT** gives a concrete grounding: if you hit an algebraic bottleneck, you can simply *compute* it with code instead of remembering a rule.
- **Tool‑Integrated** unifies both: you reason in text but can fall back on a tool when necessary – a close match to how humans solve problems: they keep an outline but use calculators for heavy lifting.

### 2.2 Reducing Overfitting to Specific Formats

If training only saw pure CoT stories, the model might overproduce verbose fictions even when a succinct formula would suffice—and vice versa for PoT-only training. Mixing formats:

- Forces the policy network to *choose* appropriate style per question,
- Encourages robustness: if one style fails under certain prompts (e.g., token limits), another may succeed.

### 2.3 Enabling Reinforcement Learning Off the Shelf

The RL stage (GRPO) feeds back *on skill*, not just style:

- Reward models are trained on pairwise comparisons between styled solutions,
- Policy updates can shift preference toward exact matching or proper tool usage,
- Because every question has multiple annotated formats, RL can learn *which style earns higher rewards* without hand–engineering loss terms.

---

## 3. Training Pipeline Highlights

1. **Data Mixer** – Randomly concatenates problem + solution pairs up to 4 k tokens during SFT so each batch contains diverse contexts.
2. **Batch Size & Optimisation** – Uses `AdamW(β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95)` with learning rate `5e‑5`, batch size `256`, and stops after `500` steps of SFT.
3. **KL Regularisation** – Keeps policy close to SFT initialization (`β=0` initially), gradually ramped during RL so earlier generations aren’t too wild.
4. **Single‐Step Updates per RL Iteration** – Keeps compute light while still converging fast thanks to GRPO’s critic-free advantage estimation.

---

## 4. Why It Works Across Languages & Domains

| Metric | MATH (English) | CMATH (Chinese) |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| Accuracy after SFT | ~48 % | ~45 % |
| Accuracy after GRPO RL | ~52 % | ~50 % |

Key observations:

- Rounding towards +4 % absolute gains using RL shows that *reward shaping* via well-annotated multi-style data is critical.
- Performance lift is comparable across English & Chinese tests—even though the corpora differ in size—demonstrating strong cross-lingual generalisation granted by shared symbolic structures.
- In out-of-domain tasks like Gaokao-MathQA or MMLU-STEM, DeepSeekMath-Instruct remains competitive with larger proprietary models because it inherits robust reasoning pathways from its diverse instruction set.

---

## 5. Lessons for Future Instruction Tuning Projects

1. **Curate Diverse Explanation Paths**  
   A single style limits adaptability; include at least two complementary scripts per problem.

2. **Balance Language Coverage Early On**  
   Training on multiple languages simultaneously helps learn language-neutral abstract symbols instead of language-bound heuristics.

3. **Chunk Your Data Smartly**  
   Concatenating up to 4 k tokens encourages holistic context handling while keeping GPU memory manageable.

4. **Iterate Reward Models With Human Feedback Loops**  
   Start with rule-based baseline rewards (correctness), refine with LLM-generated pairwise preferences over time.

5. **Don’t Overlook Post‐Training Routines (RL & GRPO)**  
   Even modest policy updates using grace-overhead algorithms can unlock further gains atop an already strong SFT foundation.

---

## Final Thought

DeepSeekMath's success story shows that *the content* of instruction tuning is as crucial as model size or architecture tweaks when chasing high mathematical reasoning scores in open-source territory. By blending CoT, PoT, and tool‐integrated templates across two major languages—and feeding them into a lightweight PPO variant called GRPO—the team turned a modest 7 B parameter base into a near GPT‑4 level math performer without expensive external toolchains or voting ensembles.

If you’re building your own math LLMs or just curious about what really drives reasoning performance up the ladder, dive into those multi-format annotations next—you’ll find they’re exactly what mattered for DeepSeekMath’s meteoric rise.*
