# Why Orthogonal Finetuning Beats LoRA: A Gentle Intro to Preserving Knowledge in Foundation Models

When a gigantic language or vision model—think GPT‑3, ViT‑Large, or Stable Diffusion—has already learned a vast amount of world knowledge, the real challenge is: **how do we adapt it to a new task without losing what it already knows?**  
Two popular ways to do this are LoRA (Low‑Rank Adaptation) and Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT).  In this post we’ll show why OFT has an edge over LoRA: it keeps the geometry of the neural network intact and guarantees more stable training. No heavy math, just intuitive ideas and concrete examples.

---

## 1. The Core Problem – “Don’t Shake the Built‑Up Structure”

A foundation model comes from a long pre‑training run that paints every layer with a very specific structure—angles between neurons, distances between embedded representations, all tuned to capture language or visual semantics.  
When we finetune, unless we control how much we disturb that structure, we risk:

* **Catastrophic forgetting** – the model suddenly forgets patterns it just learned.
* **Training instability** – gradients can explode or vanish when the shape of the weight matrices drifts wildly.
* **Overfitting** – extra capacity may memorize the new task instead of building on old knowledge.

LoRA tackles this by *adding* a small low‑rank update to each weight matrix. It is cute and efficient: you only learn a few extra parameters per layer. But adding weight means you can change both *directions* and *magnitudes* of neuron vectors—think of rotating and scaling vectors at will.

OFT approaches the problem differently—it **rotates** neurons but never scales them. By keeping magnitudes fixed, OFT deliberately preserves the *angles* between neurons throughout training.

---

## 2. Why Keeping Angles Matters

### 2.1 Angles = Semantic Relationships

Imagine each neuron’s weight vector as a point on a sphere (because all have unit length).  
The angle between two such points measures how “related” the two neurons are. If angle stays stable:

- The relative importance of features remains consistent.
- The distance metrics inside larger networks (e.g., attention scores) stay calibrated.

### 2.2 Spectral Norm & Stability

The spectral norm of a matrix is essentially its largest “stretching” factor—think of how much a map can squash or blow up space when applied to data.  
Because OFT applies an orthogonal rotation \(\mathbf{R}\) to a pretrained weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}^0\):

\[
\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^0.
\]

Any orthogonal matrix satisfies \(\|\mathbf{R}\|_2 = 1\), so

\[
\|\mathbf{W}\|_2 = \|\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 \le \|\mathbf{R}\|_2\,\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 = \|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2.
\]

The spectral norm never grows—a safety valve against exploding activations. LoRA’s additive updates lack this guarantee; adding even small matrices can bump up the spectral norm unpredictably.

---

## 3. Intuitive Analogy: Rotating vs Adding

* **LoRA:** Imagine you have a fleet of ships on tranquil waters (the pretrained weights). LoRA gives each ship an extra engine that can push them forward in any direction—sometimes tilting them towards (or away from) each other’s path. They can go faster, but they might also veer off course or collide.

* **OFT:** Instead of adding engines, you give every ship an electric motor that spins them around their own axis but keeps speed constant—just re‑orients them without pushing them further forward or backward. The relative spacing between ships stays almost unchanged; they may turn, but not speed up or slow down relative to each other.

This simple reorientation preserves global geometry while allowing local adjustments.

---

## 4. The Finetuning Process in Practice

1. **Start with Zero Change** – OFT initializes its orthogonal matrix as the identity: no rotation yet.
2. **Apply a Tiny Rotation** – During back‑propagation, gradients decide *how* to rotate neurons just enough to accommodate the new task.
3. **Keep the Scale Fixed** – Because rotations preserve norms, every neuron’s magnitude stays exactly like in pretraining.

By contrast, LoRA would train two tiny matrices added to each weight channel — they can *scale up or down* neuron strengths basically at will.

---

## 5. Empirical Findings – Booming Results with Minimal Parameters

Researchers measured several tasks spanning vision, language, and image generation:

| Task | Method | Parameter Count | Accuracy / FID |
|------|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Image classification | LoRA | ~700 k | 86% |
| Text‑to‑image generation | OFT | ~350 k | FID 12 vs FID 15 |
| Language generation | BOFT (BETTER OFT) | ~200 k | Perplexity 22 vs 24 |

The pattern is clear: when you preserve angles (OFT/BOFT), you get comparable or better performance while pumping out fewer trainable parameters than LoRA’s additive tweaks—in many settings even half the cost!

---

## 6. When DFH – Densely Factorized Hybridization?

Orthogonal Finetuning sometimes carries too many parameters for very large models: \(d^2\) scaling for a \(d\times d\) linear layer is prohibitive.  
Enter *Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning* (BOFT), which builds large orthogonal rotations from several smaller sparse orthogonal pieces—a trick inspired by fast Fourier transforms’ butterfly graph structure.

The payoff? You get dense rotations with only \(\mathcal{O}(d\log d)\) trainable knobs—still tiny compared to full fine‑tuning or even LoRA’s rank‑\(r\) additions when \(r\approx d/2\).

This shows that preserving angles need not mean preserving every detail; smart sparse factorizations let us stay in the same expressive but frugal regime.

---

## 7. Takeaways for Practitioners

| ✅ Things you should know |
|-----------------------------|
| • If your downstream task demands stability and retention of prior knowledge, orthogonal finetuning usually outperforms LoRA's additive updates.| 
| • Rotations keep spectral norms bounded; no need for extra tricks like gradient clipping for stability.| 
| • For huge models where full orthogonal matrices are too expensive, use BOFT—or any butterfly‑based factorization—to keep budgets small.| 
| • Even simpler: start with an identity matrix and let your optimizer only tweak angles; your pretrained weights stay close.| 

In short, think of **orthogonality as dancing without changing size**, while **LoRA is more like stretching ropes** that may snap if you’re not careful.

---

## 8. Final Thought

The most powerful models are not just big—they’re *well–structured*. Finetuning should respect that structure rather than trade it for blind parameter injection. Orthogonal methods, rooted in geometry preservation and spectral guarantees, give us just that—stable learning plus efficient adaptation.
