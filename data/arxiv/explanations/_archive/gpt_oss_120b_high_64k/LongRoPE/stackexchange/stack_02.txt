\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How does Equation (2) unify different positional interpolation methods?
Question:
Equation (2) rewrites positional interpolation as
$[\cos(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}), \sin(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^0}), \dots , \sin(\frac{n}{\lambda(\beta)^{d/2-1}})]$.
I am unclear about three points:
1️⃣ What exactly is $\beta = \theta^{2/d}$ and why is it raised to powers inside $\lambda(\beta)^i$?
2️⃣ How does varying $\lambda$ across dimensions correspond to the three baselines PI, NTK, and YaRN?
3️⃣ Is $\lambda$ a global scaling factor (same for all $i$) or does it become dimension‑specific ($\lambda_i$) in practice?
A concrete example—say extending LLaMA‑2 from 4k to 32k tokens—showing how each method sets $\lambda_i$ would be very helpful.
Answer:
**Answer**

The paper rewrites the rotary‑position embedding in a way that makes every existing interpolation scheme look like a single formula – Eq.~(2).  
Understanding that formula clears up what the symbols mean, how the three baselines differ, and why in practice we end up with a different *per‑dimension* scale $\lambda_{i}$.

---

### 1 Why the paper introduces  

$$
\beta = \theta^{2/d}
\qquad%
\text{and writes }%
\qquad%
\cos.\Bigl(\frac{n}{[\lambda(\beta)]^{\,i}}\Bigr),%
\;\sin.\Bigl(\frac{n}{[\lambda(\beta)]^{\,i}}\Bigr)
\fbox{(2)}
$$

Recall the original RoPE definition (Eq.~(1))

$$
[%
\cos(n\theta_{0}),%
\sin(n\theta_{0}),%
\dots,% 
\cos(n\theta_{d/2-1}),%
\sin(n\theta_{d/2-1})%
],%
\quad%
\theta_{i}= \theta^{-\, 2 i/d},
$$

so that the $i^\text{th}$ rotary angle can be written as  

$$
n\,\theta_{i}= n\,\theta^{-\, 2 i/d}= 
            \frac{n}{(\theta^{  2/d})^{ i}}%
          = \frac{n}{\,    \beta^{ i}},%
      \qquad%
      {\rm with}\;\;\beta:=\theta^{  2/d}.
$$

Thus every dimension already contains a **frequency decay** term $\beta^{ i}$; higher–index dimensions correspond to lower frequencies because $\beta>1.$

When we want to **stretch** or **compress** the positional grid we multiply every angle by a factor $c_{i}$:
$$
    n/\beta^{ i}\;\mapsto c_{i}\,\frac{n}{\beta^{ i}}
    =        \frac{n}{(c_{i}^{-1}\,\beta)^{ i}} .
$$
If we denote  

$$
    \lambda_{i}:=\frac{1}{c_{i}},%
    \qquad%
    {\rm so}\;\;
    c_{i}=       \frac{1}{    \lambda_{i}},
$$

the new angle becomes  

$$
    \frac{
        n}{
        (\lambda_{i}\,\beta)^{ i}}
     ~=~ 
    \frac{
        n}{
        [%
        {\underbrace{\lambda}_{=:~{\rm global~part}}%
        (\underbrace{\beta}_{=:~{\rm frequency~base}})}
        ] ^{
           i}} .
$$

Equation (2) therefore simply collects **the global scale** ($\lambda$) together with the intrinsic base frequency ($\beta=\theta^{  2/d}$).  
The power ${}^{ i}$ comes from two places:

* the natural $\beta^{ i}$ that already exists in RoPE,
* an optional extra factor $\lambda^{ i}$ if we let the same multiplier act on every dimension.

If an algorithm chooses different multipliers for different dimensions it merely replaces $\lambda ^{i}$ with **dimension‑specific** values ${\lambda _{i}}.$

### 2 How the three baselines appear inside Eq.~(2)

All three methods decide what $c_{i}$ (or equivalently $\lambda _{i}$) should be,
given an **extension ratio**  

$$s:=L'/L.$$

| Method | How $c_{i}$ / $\displaystyle \lambda _{i}$ are set | What Eq.~(2) looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear Positional Interpolation (PI)** | One‐size‐fits‐all: every angle is compressed by exactly the same amount, $c_{i}=s^{-1}$. Hence $\displaystyle \lambda _{i}=s.$ | All denominators become $\displaystyle n/(s\,\beta ^{i}),~~ i=0,\dots,d/2-1.$ |
| **NTK‑based interpolation** | Theory tells us that high‑frequency components should be changed little while low‑frequency components should be stretched more. A simple analytical recipe used in prior work is  
   $$c_{i}= s^{-p(i)},~~p(i)=   
      \begin{cases}
         0                     & {\rm high~freq}\\[4pt]
         p_{\rm mid}(i)       & {\rm mid~freq}\\[4pt]
         p_{\rm low}(i)=1      & {\rm low~freq}
      \end{cases}.$$  
   It is convenient to write $c_{i}=s^{-a(i)}$, which yields  
   $$   
      \displaystyle   
      \lambda _{i}=s^{a(i)} .
   $$  
   In many implementations $a(i)\approx\dfrac{i}{d/2-1}$, so  
   $$   
      c_{{0}}=s^{-0}=1,\quad    
      c_{{d/2-1}}=s^{-1},    
      c_{{i}}=s^{-\, { i}/{(d/2-1)}} .
   $$ | Denominators become  
   $$   
      n/\bigl(s^{-a(i)}\:        \beta\bigr ) ^{-a(i)}
      ~=~
      n/\bigl(s^{{a(i)}}\:      \beta\bigr ) .
   $$  
   Hence low‑frequency dimensions receive a larger factor ($\simeq s$), high‑frequency ones stay almost unchanged ($\simeq 1$). |
| **YaRN** | First split every RoPE dimension into three contiguous groups (high, mid, low frequency). The groups get three fixed multipliers: high–freq $c=1$, mid–freq $c=s^{-a_{\rm mid}}$, low–freq $c=s^{-1}$. This is exactly a *piecewise constant* version of NTK, so Eq.~(2) contains three blocks of identical $\displaystyle \lambda _{i}$:**  
   $$   
      \displaystyle   
      \begin{aligned}
          &{}\!&    \lambda _{{\rm high}}&=1,                       \\[4pt]
          &{}\!&    \lambda _{{\rm mid }}&=s^{{a_{\rm mid}}},     \\[4pt]
          &{}\!&    \lambda _{{\rm low }}&=s .
      \end{aligned}

   $$   
      n/\bigl[\underbrace{
           s^{{a_{\rm block}}}\,
           }\_{\displaystyle =[\;\cdots\:]\;}
         ] .
   $$   
   for each block exactly as described above.|

### 3 Is $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ global or per‑dimension?

*In PI* it is **global** ($\forall i:\;\lambda _{i}=s$).

*In NTK* it is **dimension‑dependent**, usually following an analytically prescribed monotone curve (e.g.\ a power law).

*In YaRN* it is **piecewise constant**, still dependent on dimension because different blocks receive different constants.
