\title{Blogs about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

# DeepSeekMath vs. GPT‑4: Closing the Gap in Open‑Source Math Reasoning  

*How a 7 B parameter model is now running neck‑and‑neck with proprietary giants on the toughest math benchmarks.*

---

## Why Math Reasoning Still Matters  

Large language models (LLMs) have turned “solve this equation” into a fresh test of their reasoning chops. Publicly available models have historically lagged far behind commercial behemoths such as **GPT‑4** or **Gemini Ultra**, especially on *competition‑level* tasks that demand multi‑step deduction rather than simple pattern matching.  

The newly released **DeepSeekMath 7B** challenges that narrative head‑on. By combining a massive web‐scraped mathematics corpus, code pretraining, instruction fine‑tuning, and a lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm called **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, DeepSeekMath attains scores that were previously only seen in closed‑source systems—and it does so with a fraction of the parameters.

---

## Benchmarks at a Glance  

| Benchmark | What it tests | Typical scale | DeepSeekMath 7B (best variant) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| **MATH** (competition level) | College‑level contest problems requiring long chain of reasoning | 21 k questions | $51.7\%$ accuracy (no tool) – self‑consistency over 64 samples → $60.9\%$ |
| **GSM8K** | Grade‑school word problems solvable with CoT | 8 k questions | $88.2\%$ after RL |
| **CMATH** / **MGSM‑zh** / **Gaokao–MathCloze/QA** | Chinese K–12 curriculum covering algebra, geometry, probability | ~10 k each | $84.6\!\to\!88.8\%$ (CMATH) after RL |
| **MMLU–STEM**, **BBH**, **HumanEval** | General language understanding / multi‑step reasoning / code generation | Various | Consistent improvements over the underlying code model |

*All numbers are drawn directly from the DeepSeekMath paper; for GPT‑4 and Gemini Ultra only relative statements are available in the literature.*

### What “competition‑level” really means  

The **MATH** benchmark is built from high school olympiad problems (AMC/AIME/IMO style). Solving one item typically requires *several* logical hops—identifying relevant lemmas, setting up equations, performing algebraic manipulation, and finally checking edge cases. Because success hinges on both *knowledge* and *process*, raw token prediction accuracy is a poor proxy; instead we report exact answer match percentages which truly reflect problem‐solving ability.

---

## DeepSeekMath’s Three Evolution Stages  

1. **DeepSeekMath‑Base** – a continuation of the code‐oriented **DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5** (7 B) trained on an extra $500\,$B tokens (56 % math tokens).  
   - GSM8K: $82.9\!\to\!88.2\%$ after RL  
   - MATH: $46.8\!\to\!51.7\%$ after RL  

2. **DeepSeekMath‑Instruct** – supervised fine‑tuning on $776$ k instruction examples containing chain‐of‐thought (CoT), program‐of‐thought (PoT), and tool‐integrated solutions.  
   - Beats every open‑source model ≤ 70 B on plain CoT reasoning  
   - With Python tool use reaches ≈ 60 % on MATH—still ahead of all open models  

3. **DeepSeekMath‑RL** – reinforcement learning via GRPO using only the instruction data (≈ 144 k GSM8K/MATH questions).  
   - Gains +5–6 pp on GSM8K & +5 pp on MATH compared to Instruct  
   - Demonstrates that *the same data* can be leveraged more effectively through RL  

---

## Standing Side by Side With Closed Sources  

While exact scores for GPT‑4 or Gemini Ultra on these specific splits aren’t disclosed in the DeepSeekMath paper, the authors repeatedly note that their model “approaches” those performance levels—meaning the gap has narrowed dramatically:

* In prior open‐source work, hitting **$>30\%$** on MATH was considered impressive; DeepSeekMath pushes past **$50\%$**, a threshold previously attainable only by proprietary systems.
* On GSM8K several closed models report high $80$+$$ accuracies; DeepSeekMath’s $88.2\%$ puts it squarely within that range despite having ~1/10th the parameters.
* For Chinese curricula—where most open models barely cross $70\%$—DeepSeekMath’s $88\!+\!$\% scores outrank even many larger multilingual LLMs released by local tech firms.

In short: a modestly sized open model is now delivering results that would have required *hundreds* of billions of parameters just a year ago.

## How a Tiny Model Beats Much Bigger Ones  

### 1️⃣ Massive high‑quality math pretraining  
*120 B math tokens* were harvested from Common Crawl using an iterative FastText classifier trained initially on OpenWebMath and refined over four rounds of human annotation & domain pruning.  
Key takeaways:

- The corpus is roughly **7× larger** than Minerva’s math web slice and **9× larger** than OpenWebMath.
- It’s *multilingual*: English + Chinese dominate > 80 %, which explains gains on Chinese benchmarks.
- Careful decontamination removes any exact overlap with evaluation suites (10‐gram matching).

### 2️⃣ Starting from a code foundation  
Pretraining began with a code model (“DeepSeek‑Coder”). Experiments show this seeding boosts both pure CoT reasoning *and* program‐aided solving because:

- Code syntax familiarizes the model with precise symbolic manipulation.
- The same embeddings that understand function calls transfer to handling mathematical operators and symbols.

### 3️⃣ Instruction tuning with CoT & PoT formats  
Instead of plain question–answer pairs, training data includes explicit reasoning traces:

- **Chain of Thought (CoT)** – stepwise natural language derivations.
- **Program of Thought (PoT)** – Python snippets that execute exact arithmetic via `math` or `sympy`.
- Integrated tool pipelines mirror how modern LLMs like ChatGPT use external calculators.

This exposure forces the model to internalize *process*, not just final answers.

### 4️⃣ Efficient reinforcement learning – GRPO  
Traditional PPO needs an extra value network (“critic”), inflating memory consumption for LLMs. GRPO sidesteps this by:

1. Sampling *G* responses per question from the current policy.
2​.​ Computing rewards with an existing reward model.
3​.​ Using the *group’s average reward* as a baseline—no separate critic needed.
4​.​ Adding an explicit KL term between policy & reference model rather than embedding it in per-token rewards.

The net effect? A ***40–50 % reduction*** in GPU memory while still gaining +5–6 percentage points on both GSM8K and MATH compared to pure SFT—in line with what PPO achieves at far higher cost.

### 5️⃣ General reasoning spillover  
Even though training focused on mathematics, downstream tests such as MMLU STEM and BIG-Bench Hard see noticeable uplift—underscoring that structured numeric reasoning also sharpens broader logical abilities.

---

## Multilingual Edge  

Because the corpus contains large swaths of Chinese web pages alongside English ones, DeepSeekMath shows consistent superiority on:

- **CMATH** – +4 pp over the best English-centric baselines.
- **MGSM‑zh**, Gaokao variants – reaching mid‑80s percentages where other open models stall around low 70s.

This bilingual advantage is rare among math-specialized LLMs that usually train almost exclusively on English data.

---

## Where It Still Falls Short  

No model is perfect yet:

| Weakness | Likely cause |
|----------|--------------|
| Geometry & theorem proving (triangles, ellipses) | Data selection bias – web sources contain fewer geometric diagrams/explanations |
| Few-shot adaptability | Smaller scale limits rapid prompt conditioning compared with massive models like GPT‑4 |
| Formal proof automation beyond basic autoformalization | Requires richer formal mathematics corpora beyond informal web text |

Future work suggested by the authors includes expanding the data pipeline to capture more geometry content and refining RL algorithms to handle out-of-distribution prompts more robustly.

---

## Bottom Line  

DeepSeekMath proves that *data quality* plus *smart fine-tuning* can compensate for raw parameter count when it comes to mathematical problem solving:

- A 7 B LLM reaches **$51.7\%$** accuracy on competition-level MATH without any tool assistance—once unheard of outside closed research labs.
- Reinforcement learning via GRPO pushes this further while keeping training affordable.
- Multilingual coverage lets it dominate not only English but also Chinese national exams.

If you’re looking for an openly available model that truly “understands” mathematics—not just memorizes patterns—DeepSeekMath is arguably the most compelling option right now and signals a major shift toward democratizing high-performance scientific AI.
