\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# Benchmarking BOFT: Real‑World Gains on Vision Transformers, Large Language Models, and Diffusion Systems  

*How a butterfly‑structured orthogonal finetuning method manages to beat the current PEFT leaders while slashing the number of trainable weights.*

---

## Introduction  

Foundation models such as GPT‑3, ViT‑Giant and Stable Diffusion have shown that scaling up parameters brings dramatic performance jumps—but training them from scratch is no longer feasible for most teams.  The community therefore relies heavily on **parameter‑efficient finetuning (PEFT)** techniques that adapt a frozen backbone with only a tiny set of new weights.  

Two families dominate today’s PEFT landscape:

* **Low‑rank reparameterizations** – e.g. LoRA adds a rank‑$r$ update $\Delta \mathbf{W}= \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^\top$.
* **Adapter / prompt tricks** – inject small MLP blocks or learn prefix tokens.

Both approaches are powerful but they either modify the weight matrix additively (low rank) or enlarge the network architecture (adapters). A more recent line of work argues that *preserving* the relational geometry of pretrained neurons yields better stability and generalisation. **Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)** embodies this idea by left‑multiplying each linear layer with an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ so that pairwise angles between neurons stay exactly the same.  

The original OFT however requires $\mathcal{O}(d^{2})$ trainable parameters for a dense orthogonal matrix—still too expensive for very large models.  The authors of *Parameter‑Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization* introduced **BOFT** (Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning), which replaces a single dense $\mathbf{R}$ with a product of **butterfly‑structured sparse orthogonal factors**:

\[
\mathbf{R}\bigl(m,b\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\,b}_{(d,i)},
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\,b}_{(d,i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},
\]

where each $\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\,b}_{(d,i)}$ has only $\mathcal{O}(d)$ non‑zero entries.  
When $b=2$ and $m=\log_{2}d$, BOFT needs only $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ trainable numbers—an **order of magnitude fewer** than vanilla OFT—while still being able to generate a dense orthogonal transformation thanks to its butterfly topology.

The original paper backs this claim with an extensive empirical suite spanning three major model families:

| Model family | Downstream tasks | Baselines compared | Parameter budget |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Vision Transformers | Image classification / VTAB-1k | LoRA, Adapter, Block‑diagonal OFT | $\sim0.5\%$–$2\%$ of total weights |
| Large Language Models | GLUE & few‑shot QA | LoRA, Prefix‐tuning, Full finetune | $\sim0.3\%$ of total weights |
| Text‑to‑Image Diffusion | Prompt steering & style control | Original OFT (block diagonal) | $\sim1\%$ of total weights |

Below we walk through each benchmark, highlight the gains BOFT delivers, and explain why those gains make sense from a theoretical standpoint.

## Experimental Design & Benchmarks Overview  

### Common Setup  

* **Pretrained backbones** – ViT‑B/16 (ImageNet‑21k), LLaMA‑7B for language, Stable Diffusion v1.5 for diffusion.
* **Finetuning protocol** – All methods start from identical weight checkpoints; learning rates are matched after grid search; training budgets (epochs/steps) are identical across baselines.
* **Parameter counting** – Only the *new* trainable parameters are reported.  
  * For vanilla OFT: $\Theta(d^{2})$ per linear layer.
  * For block‐diagonal OFT ($b$): $\Theta(bd)$.
  * For BOFT($m,b$): $\frac12(b-1)dm$, which collapses to $\Theta(d\log d)$ when $b=2,m=\log_{2} d$.
* **Evaluation metrics** – Top‑1 accuracy for vision; average GLUE score for language; CLIP‐based alignment score + human preference for diffusion control.

The experiments also include **ablation studies** varying $m$ and $b$, investigating multiplicative dropout, and measuring training stability via loss variance.

---

## Vision Transformers – From ImageNet to VTAB-1k  

### Task Suite  

* **ImageNet‐1k fine‑tuning** – standard 1000‑class classification.
* **VTAB-1k** – ten diverse visual tasks covering natural images, medical imaging and structured data; each task provides only 1000 labeled examples.

### What was observed?  

* **Higher accuracy with far fewer parameters**  
  * On ImageNet finetuning, BOFT reaches within $0.4\%$ of full model performance while using roughly *10× less* trainable weights than full OFT and *3–4× less* than LoRA.
  * On VTAB-1k average accuracy, BOFT outperforms LoRA by **~1–2 points**, especially on “structured” tasks where preserving neuron geometry appears critical (e.g., CIFAR-100, CLEVR).
* **Training stability** – loss curves show markedly lower variance across random seeds compared with LoRA or block diagonal OFT. The orthogonal constraint keeps singular values fixed (`‖𝑅‖₂ = 1`), preventing exploding or vanishing gradients.
* **Parameter scaling** – For a ViT layer with hidden size $d=1024$, a dense orthogonal matrix would need about $1\,\text{M}$ scalar parameters ($1024^{2}$). BOFT($m=10,b=2$) needs only around $10\,000$ effective parameters ($1024·10·(b−1)/2≈10k$) yet still produces a fully dense transformation thanks to its butterfly connectivity.

### Takeaway  

In the vision domain where each linear projection already processes high‐dimensional token embeddings, having an expressive yet “geometry preserving” update is crucial. The butterfly factorization offers exactly that balance: enough flexibility to adapt to new visual concepts without overwhelming the parameter budget.

## Large Language Models – Scaling Orthogonal Finetuning  

### Benchmarks  

* **GLUE** (MNLI, QQP, SST‑2 etc.) – classic supervised language understanding suite.
* **Few‐shot QA** – evaluating transfer ability when only a handful of labeled examples are available.
* **Instruction tuning subset** – checking how well BOFT can inject new task instructions into LLaMA without breaking its existing knowledge.

### Results Synopsis  

| Method | Trainable % | GLUE avg.| Few‑shot QA Δ |
|--------|-------------|----------|---------------|
| Full finetune | 100 % | +6.8 pts over frozen baseline | +5 pts |
| LoRA (r = 8)   | ~0.5 % | +5.3 pts | +3 pts |
| Block‐diagonal OFT (b = 64) | ~0.7 % | +5.6 pts | +3 pts |
| **BOFT(m=9,b=2)** | **~0.3 %** | **+5.9 pts** | **+3.4 pts** |

Key observations:

* With under one percent additional parameters BOFT consistently eclipses LoRA’s score by roughly **0.5–0.6 GLUE points**, which is statistically significant across runs.
* In few-shot settings where overfitting is easy, BOFT’s implicit inductive bias—stemming from structured butterfly layers—produces smoother loss trajectories and higher downstream accuracy.
* Unlike adapters that add extra feedforward blocks at each layer (“capacity increase”), BOFT leaves inference unchanged because after training the learned orthogonal matrices can be multiplied into the frozen backbone once and for all.

### Why it matters  

Language models rely heavily on subtle directional relationships among token embeddings; an orthogonal left-multiplication respects those relationships globally rather than perturbing them locally as low-rank updates do. The butterfly construction preserves this global geometry while keeping the overhead tiny enough for even billions‑parameter LLMs.

## Text‑to‑Image Diffusion – Controlling Generation with Orthogonal Butterflies  

### Scenario  

The original OFT was introduced for controllable diffusion generation (e.g., steering image style via textual prompt). The authors extended this setting to test:

* **Style transfer control** – enforcing an artist’s palette while preserving prompt semantics.
* **Spatial layout conditioning** – aligning generated objects with coarse bounding boxes.
* **Domain adaptation** – finetuning Stable Diffusion to generate medical images from textual descriptions alone.

### Findings  

* Using BOFT($m=6,b=4$) yields **higher CLIP similarity scores (~3 % relative gain)** over original block-diagonal OFT while using ten times fewer trainable parameters.
* Human raters preferred BOFT-generated samples in >70 % of paired comparisons against both LoRA‐style low-rank updates and plain classifier-free guidance finetunes.
* Training time per epoch is modestly higher due to multiple sparse matrix multiplications ($\approx15\%$ overhead), but this cost disappears at inference time once the product $\mathbf R(m,b)$ is folded into the frozen UNet weights.

These results underscore that orthogonal transformations are not limited to discriminative settings—they also excel at subtle generative steering where preserving spectral norms directly contributes to stable diffusion steps.

## Ablation Highlights & Practical Tips  

| Variable | Effect on Performance | Effect on Parameters |
|----------|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Number of factors $m$ | More factors → richer connectivity → higher accuracy up to saturation (~$m=\log_2 d$). Diminishing returns after that point.| Linear increase ($\propto dm$).|
| Block size $b$   | Larger blocks give each factor more local capacity → small boost when $b>2$, but at cost of extra params ($\propto b d m$). Smaller $b$ ($=2$) already hits strong performance due to deep factorization.| Quadratic in $b$.|
| Multiplicative Dropout rates $(p_1,p_2)$ | Prevents overfitting on tiny data regimes; modest dropout ($p_1=10\%,p_2=20\%$) improves few-shot scores without hurting convergence.| No impact on total trainable count.|

**Implementation notes**

* Initialize every butterfly component as identity (`Cayley` transform applied to zero skew-symmetric matrix) – guarantees zero change at start.
* Use standard AdamW optimizers; schedule learning rate identically across baselines so improvements can be attributed solely to representation power.
* After training compress $\mathbf R(m,b)=\prod_i \tilde{\mathbf B}_i^{\,b}$ into a single dense orthogonal matrix once; this eliminates any runtime overhead during inference—a key advantage over adapters/prefixes which require extra layers at test time.

## What Makes BOFT Tick?  

1️⃣ **Dense connectivity through sparsity:**  
   Butterfly graphs achieve full source–sink coverage with only $\mathcal O(d \log d)$ edges—exactly what our information transmission view predicts as “minimum free edges”.

2️⃣ **Orthogonality guarantees spectral norm preservation:**  
   Multiplying by $\mathbf R(m,b)$ leaves all singular values unchanged (`‖R‖₂ = 1`). This stabilizes gradient flow especially in deep Transformer stacks or UNets used by diffusion models.

3️⃣ **Structured inductive bias:**  
   Butterfly factorization mirrors classic fast linear transforms (FFT, Hadamard). Consequently it encodes priors about global mixing patterns while still allowing learned deviations—an elegant middle ground between fully expressive orthogonal matrices and overly restrictive block diagonal forms.

4️⃣ **Better expressivity per parameter:**  
   Theorem 1 in the paper shows that even with modest depth `$m`', a composition of butterflies can approximate any orthogonal matrix when combined appropriately (`ℝ^{d×d}` hierarchy). Practically this means you get near‐full expressivity at logarithmic cost!

## Closing Thoughts  

The experimental campaign presented in *Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization* demonstrates that you don’t have to choose between “tiny fine–tune” and “full expressive power”. By borrowing an age-old signal-processing trick—the butterfly graph—and marrying it with modern orthogonal training tricks (Cayley transform), **BOST manages to keep both camps happy**:

* It beats low-rank PEFTs such as LoRA across vision classification, NLP benchmarks and generative diffusion control—all while consuming just a fraction of their parameter budget.
* It retains zero inference overhead because once trained the learned orthogonal map can be baked into existing weights.
* It offers robustness against overfitting—a persistent pain point when adapting massive models on limited downstream data—thanks to its built-in geometric regularisation.

If you’re looking for a “plug‑and‑play” finetune method that scales from ViTs through LLMs up to diffusion backbones without adding latency or bloating storage footprints, give Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning a try. Its blend of elegant mathematics and practical efficiency might just become the next staple in your model adaptation toolbox.
