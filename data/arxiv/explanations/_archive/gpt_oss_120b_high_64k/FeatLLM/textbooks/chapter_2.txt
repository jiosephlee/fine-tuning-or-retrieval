# Conventional Baselines – Logistic Regression, Gradient Boosting Machines and Random Forests  

The most frequently employed family of models for tabular prediction consists of linear classifiers such as logistic regression together with tree‑based ensembles including gradient boosting machines (often instantiated as XGBoost) and random forests.  Logistic regression represents each input sample $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb R^{d}$ by a linear combination $\sigma(\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf{x})$, where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes the sigmoid function for binary problems or the softmax function when $c>2$ classes are present.  Because its hypothesis class is low‑dimensional, logistic regression typically requires only a modest amount of labeled data before its parameters become well constrained.  Gradient boosting machines construct an additive model 
$$
f(\mathbf x)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_t h_t(\mathbf x),
$$ 
where each weak learner $h_t$ is a shallow decision tree trained sequentially on the residuals left by its predecessors.  XGBoost augments this procedure with regularisation terms that penalise tree complexity and improve generalisation under limited supervision.  Random forests follow a distinct ensemble philosophy: $M$ independent decision trees are grown on bootstrapped subsets of the training set and their predictions are averaged,
$$
\hat y(\mathbf x)=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M} h_m(\mathbf x).
$$  
Both tree ensembles excel at handling heterogeneous feature types (continuous, ordinal and categorical) without extensive preprocessing—a crucial advantage when dealing with real‑world tables whose columns often possess diverse domains.  

In low‑shot regimes ($k$ labelled examples with $k \ll N$, where $N$ denotes the total number of samples), conventional baselines suffer from two intertwined difficulties. First, fitting high‑capacity trees on only a handful of labelled instances risks memorising idiosyncratic patterns rather than true signal—a phenomenon often called overfitting to spurious correlations. Second, although linear models have low variance, they lack expressive power when relationships between features and target are highly non‑linear or involve intricate logical conditions (“if age > 65 and cholesterol < 200 then …”). Consequently, while logistic regression, XGBoost and random forests serve as essential reference points throughout empirical evaluations, researchers have sought complementary strategies that inject external inductive bias into “data‑starved” settings.



# Transfer Learning Across Many Tables  

A prominent line of research leverages **cross‑table transfer**: instead of learning solely from the scarce labelled rows belonging to one table, an algorithm simultaneously observes many heterogeneous tables drawn from related domains.  By sharing parameters across tasks it can extract reusable statistical regularities—e.g., how column names such as *age* or *blood pressure* typically influence medical outcomes—thereby providing an implicit prior that alleviates label scarcity on any single table.

One concrete instantiation is **TransTab**~\cite{wang2022transtab}.  TransTab adopts a Transformer architecture whose input consists both of raw cell values and their associated column descriptors $f_j$.  During pre‑training it ingests thousands of publicly available tabular corpora; attention mechanisms learn embeddings that capture semantic relations among column names (“temperature” often co‑occurs with “humidity”) independently of raw numerical values.  When faced with a new downstream task containing only $k$ labelled rows ($k$–shot setting), TransTab merely forwards the fresh table through its pretrained encoder–decoder stack—a process that requires no gradient updates—and produces predictions directly via a lightweight classifier head attached to the shared representation layer.

The benefits are twofold. First, since semantic knowledge has been distilled from many tables beforehand, TransTab can make sensible initial predictions even when $k=1$ or $k=2$.  Second, because it does not demand fine‑tuning on the target table itself, deployment remains feasible when only inference APIs are available (`e.g.\ white‑box model access`).   Nonetheless several practical constraints temper its applicability. The Transformer backbone typically expects all columns to fit within its fixed context window; consequently very wide tables (`d` exceeding several dozens) cause truncation whose effect on downstream accuracy remains unpredictable. Moreover, transferring knowledge across domains presumes sufficient overlap between column vocabularies—a premise violated when dealing with highly specialised industrial datasets whose attribute names bear little resemblance to any entry encountered during pre‑training.



# Synthetic Data Pretraining – Methods Exemplified by TabPFN  

When real labelled examples are scarce it can be advantageous to **pretrain** a learner on artificial samples whose statistical properties deliberately span a broad variety of possible distributions!   This idea underlies **TabPFN** (Posterior Function Networks)~\cite{hollmann2022tabpfn}.  

TabPFN builds an ensemble‐averaged Bayesian neural network whose prior is obtained by sampling numerous synthetic tabular datasets drawn from mixtures of parametric families (e.g., Gaussian mixtures for continuous attributes combined with categorical Dirichlet draws). Each synthetic dataset defines both feature values $\{\mathbf{x}^{(s)}_i\}_{i=1}^{n_s}$ and binary targets $\{\mathbf y^{(s)}_i\}_{i=1}^{n_s}$ generated according to randomly sampled conditional distributions $p(\mathbf y|\mathbf x)$. By exposing the network repeatedly to such diverse scenarios it learns how **posterior predictive functions** react under extreme variations—in other words it learns meta–knowledge about mapping *any* plausible table plus its associated few labelled rows into useful predictions.

During downstream inference one concatenates **all available labelled rows** together with every unlabelled test row into a single joint input matrix $(X_{\text{train}}, X_{\text{test}})$,\footnote{The original TabPFN formulation treats training samples simply as additional conditioning datapoints fed alongside each query example;} subsequently performs a single forward pass through the pretrained network producing probability estimates $\hat p(\mathbf y_{\text{test}}|\cdot)$ without further optimisation.  

The primary virtue lies in **zero additional training cost** after synthesis—the heavy lifting occurs entirely during offline generation of synthetic priors—which readily aligns with scenarios where computational resources are limited at deployment time yet timely predictions matter critically (e.g., embedded healthcare devices).  
Conversely ‑ the method’s reliability hinges upon how faithfully synthetic mixtures emulate real underlying generative processes; if an applied domain exhibits distributional traits never observed among synthesized families—for instance heavy‐tailed financial returns—the learned posterior may systematically miscalibrate predictions on genuine data.



# Semi‑Supervised Strategies That Leverage Unlabeled Records  

An alternative route exploits abundant unlabelled rows accompanying virtually every industrial database while preserving economical usage of scarce labels ($k$\-shot). By devising an auxiliary objective defined solely over inputs $\mathbf{x}$ one can learn feature embeddings $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ that capture salient structure before confronting any supervised loss.\footnote{$k$ denotes number-of‐shots throughout this chapter following standard few‑shot terminology introduced earlier ($|\mathcal D_{\text{train}}|=k$).}  

One influential proposal is **SCARF**~\cite{bahri2022scarf}, which adopts *contrastive self‑supervision*. Here each sample participates simultaneously as an anchor $(x_i)$ and as part(s) of positive counterparts generated through stochastic augmentations such as Gaussian noise injection after normalisation (`feature perturbations`). Negative pairs consist simply of distinct samples within a mini­batch. By maximising agreement between embeddings $\phi(x_i)$ and $\phi(x_i^+)$ while minimising similarity against $\phi(x_j^-)$ under an InfoNCE loss,
$$
\mathcal L_{\textit{iNCE}} = - \log \frac{\exp(\phi(x_i)\!\cdot\!\phi(x_i^+)/\tau)}{\exp(\phi(x_i)\!\cdot\!\phi(x_i^+)/\tau)+ \sum_{j}\exp(\phi(x_i)\!\cdot \!\phi(x_j^-)/\tau)},
$$
SCARF encourages representations which group nearby points irrespective of label information.\footnote{$\tau$ denotes temperature controlling distribution sharpness—a hyperparameter whose default set equates akin to commonly employed values around $0.\!5$.}  
After this unsupervised phase one attaches a cheap linear classifier trained on the tiny labelled subset ($k$ samples). Empirically SCARF exhibits improved robustness against overfitting compared with purely supervised learners trained directly on raw features whenever enough unlabelled rows exist.\footnote{These benefits echo observations made throughout recent literature designated “semi‐supervised” tabular learning Note however that SCARF’s gains tend diminishes dramatically when available labels become vanishingly few ($k<4$), reflecting difficulty in propagating supervision through soft cluster structures alone."

Another dimly documented method incorporated into our experimental suite is **STUNT**~\cite{nam2023stunt}. While specific architectural details lie outside this chapter’s scope—owing largely to their exposition solely through cited work—STUNT likewise falls under the umbrella “use unlabeled records together with very few annotated rows”. It typically harnesses pseudo‑labelling strategies wherein an early weak predictor assigns provisional labels to unlabelled instances; those provisional labels then augment standard supervised optimisation loops iteratively refining both predictor strength and label confidence.\footnote{Interpretations stem directly from how STUNT was introduced among “semi‐supervised” baselines alongside SCARF—a placement suggesting analogous design principles rather than exact replication.)  

Overall semi‑supervised constructions offer evident appeal when large corpora devoid objects exist (`|U|\gg|L|`). Yet their practical efficacy suffers two recurring impediments specialized by dented observations reported throughout our surveys:

* First, non‑parametric contrastive losses assume features share semantic continuity across samples—a condition often violated in highly categorical domains such as medical billing codes where discrete categories bear little distance information.
* Second—and most salient—few‑shot regimes amplify noise introduced by pseudo−labels sought by methods like STUNT because any misstep propagates rapidly through subsequent re‑training cycles.



# Language Interfaced Tabular Learning – Direct Prompting (In‑Context), Parameter‑Efficient Tuning (LoRA/IA³), Hybrid Systems Like TABLET  

The arrival of large language models (LLMs) has sparked an entirely new paradigm whereby tabular reasoning can be delegated partially—or entirely—to a foundation model trained predominantly on natural language sequences.~Recent investigations view LLMs essentially as *knowledge reservoirs* capable enough either **to answer directly** once presented with serialized tables (`in–context’’ style) or **to act as trainable encoders** once endowed with modest structural adaptations (`parameter‐efficient tuning’).  

### Direct Prompting / In–Context Learning  

The most elementary approach supplies an LLM prompt consisting jointly   i)   *task definition*: “Predict whether this patient suffers chronic heart failure?” plus ii)   *feature descriptions*: “age – integer years”, “cholesterol – mg/dL”, … iii)   *few labelled exemplars*, each expressed using free‐form sentences such as “Age = 68 and Cholesterol = 210 → Answer = Yes”.  
Given this composite input catalogueing up-to-$k$ demonstrations (\emph{$k$‐shot}), the model produces an answer token string (“Yes”) completing one forward pass per query sample.\footnote{
The foregoing mirrors state‐of‐the‐art prompt pipelines detailed e.g.~by Wei et al.~[35] wherein chain–of–thought reasoning chains optionally guide LLM attention toward salient attributes.
}   
Empirically this mechanism enables powerful zero–parameter adaptation even when underlying domain expertise resides implicitly inside model weights (\emph{"prior knowledge"}). Nevertheless it suffers heavily from **per‐sample API calls** during deployment—inference latency scales linearly with test set size—and becomes infeasible whenever prompts surpass fixed token limits because large tables contain hundreds⁎of columns.


### Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA / IA³)  

When API calling constraints prohibit repeated prompting yet weight updates remain permissible through open-source checkpoints—the community has pioneered lightweight adapters inserted into existing Transformer layers.\  
*LoRA* *(Low-Rank Adaptation)* augments each weight matrix $\mathbf W_\ell$ by an additive term $\Delta \mathbf W_\ell = \mathbf A_\ell \mathbf B_\ell^{\top}$ where matrices $\mathbf A_\ell,\mathbf B_\ell$ are trained whilst keeping base weights frozen (\emph{idempotent fine-tuning})~\cite{hu2021lora}. Moreover *IA³* manipulates intermediate activations through elementwise scaling factors instead Of full matrix gradients (\emph{i.e.~Inference–time Adaptive Activations}) ~\cite{liu2019ia3}. Both techniques dramatically reduce memory consumption compared with full backpropagation yet still afford sufficient expressivity allowing LLMs implicitly learn mappings between textualised tabular patterns and class labels across dozens–of­shots.\footnote{
Relevant works—including TableLLM—that employed IA³ reported gains when constrained laboratory budgets permitted fine-tuning modest parameter subsets but did not expose those advantages once only black-box API endpoints were provided.
}

### Hybrid Systems Such As TABLET  

Enrichments atop bare prompting mitigate some aforementioned restrictions via auxiliary components determined outside the LLM’s core forward pass.~The **TABLET** framework introduces external classifiers generating rule sets or prototype vectors derived via feature clustering over unlabelled rows.\footnote{
Prototype vectors signify typical patterns discovered through unsupervised attunement—the system subsequently inserts these descriptors into prompted queries encouraging idealized reasoning pathways.
}  
These supplemental hints become part of step (i)’s textual description so that during generation the LLM receives both human‐written task wording *and* structured guidance concerning which attribute clusters tend toward particular outcomes.\newline  
Experiments reported alongside TABLET demonstrate modest boosts over pristine chain-of-thought prompting while still retaining pure inference through LLM,—yet central shortcomings persist unchanged:\newline  
(i) Every test item demands an individual call `$API\,({prompt}_i)$`, producing latencies incompatible with high-throughput pipelines;\newline  
(ii) Adaptation relies implicitly upon preserving all column name tokens inside prompts—forcing designers either discarding less informative columns (“feature bagging”) or encountering forced truncation once token capacity overshoots.~Recent studies therefore report noticeable degradation (>10% AUC loss) after crossing roughly six dozen variables owing precisely ``prompt size limitation’’ effects ==[$26]​.   

Collectively these explorations confirm that leveraging linguistic priors yields impressive gains under low supervision while simultaneously exposing nontrivial engineering obstacles absent from classic tree ensembles.



# Key Limitations Encountered by Existing Methods  

Across all surveyed families—conventional classifiers, cross-table transfer mechanisms such as TransTab,\linebreak synthetic pretraining exemplified by TabPFN,
unsupervised/self‑supervised schemes like SCARF / STUNT,
and language-interfaced pipelines spanning raw prompting up through LoRA/IA³ adapters—the following recurring failures constrain large-scale adoption in practical environments:

* **Per-sample API invocations at inference time**, characteristic of direct prompting approaches including vanilla In‐Context Learning,
TABLET,
and many contemporary LLM variants employed solely as black-box predictors.
These calls inflate computational cost proportional both to test set cardinality `$|\mathcal D_{\text {test}}|`' 
and underlying request latency inherent behind commercial endpoints ($0.{xxx}$ seconds per token).

* **Dependence upon weight-update capabilities**, i.e., access ot full model checkpoints necessary for LoRA / IA³ fine-tuning.
Commercially dominant foundation models such as GPT‑4 currently expose merely inference APIs lacking any tunable layer;
restricting practitioners either backtrack onto unmodified prompting variants (reincurring per-sample costs) or resort entirely toward non‑LLM baselines despite missing valuable domain knowledge stored inside millions of parameters

* **Prompt length bottlenecks**, arising whenever `$d$,\,the number of columns,’ exceeds feasible token budgets.
Serializing every column name plus value quickly dominates allotted windows (~4K tokens for GPT‑3 series); beyond this point important columns become silently dropped yielding abrupt performance declines~=often observed starkly pronounced (>10% AUC degradation).

* **Susceptibility towards spurious correlations**, particularly salient when only `$k` labelled instances form supervisory signal.
Tree ensembles often latch onto coincidental co-occurrences observed within tiny bags;
conversely naive LLM prompts occasionally extrapolate irrelevant contextual cues present among injected demonstrations;
even sophisticated semi-supervised learners like SCARF may propagate noisy cluster assignments if underlying augmentations fail capture true causal drivers.C

Together these contingencies illuminate why curiosity grew around rethinking how LLM capabilities could be harnessed without imposing them directly onto every prediction request nor obligating full fine-tuning procedures—a motivation precisely driving newer frameworks such as ‘FeatLLM’. By relegating LLMs instead into *feature engineers*, extracting reusable logical rules once per dataset rather than per example,
the new direction mitigates API overheads,
sidesteps stringent prompt limits via bagged feature subsets,
and preserves interpretability through explicit rule representations—all while retaining considerable gains derived from rich linguistic priors incumbent within massive language models.

Low-shot tabular learning deals with predictive tasks in which only a very small number \(k(< N)\) of labeled examples are available while potentially many unlabeled records remain unused. Classical machine-learning algorithms rely heavily on abundant supervision; consequently researchers have explored auxiliary sources—including rich priors learned across many unrelated tables, synthetic corpora generated offline, semi-supervised objectives built upon unlabelled data, and linguistic knowledge stored inside large language models—to compensate for severe label scarcity. The following sections describe major families of approaches that have been proposed before introducing newer directions.

Survey of Prior Approaches to Low-Shot Tabular Learning  

Low-shot tabular learning studies how well predictive models perform when only a handful \(k(< N)\) of labeled rows are available while potentially many more unlabeled instances exist. Because most classical supervised learners rely heavily on abundant supervision, researchers have investigated several complementary sources of inductive bias––including knowledge transferred across many unrelated tables, synthetic data generated offline, semi-supervised objectives built upon unlabelled records, and linguistic priors embedded within large language models (LLMs). The following sections review these families chronologically and highlight why each still faces practical obstacles.



# Conventional Baselines – Logistic Regression, Gradient Boosting Machines and Random Forests  

Logistic regression constitutes the simplest linear classifier for tabular problems. For \(C\)-class classification it computes logits  
\(z_c(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol w_c^{\,\top }\boldsymbol x,\;\ c=1,\dots ,C,\)
and converts them into probabilities via softmax  
\(p(y=c\,|\,\boldsymbol x)=e^{z_c}/ {\sum_{c'}e^{z_{c'}}}.\)
Because only \(d\times C\\)(plus bias terms optional) parameters need estimation,
the model tolerates very small training sets––making it attractive when \(k≤4.\)

Gradient boosting machines enlarge expressive power through additive composition  
\(f(\boldsymbol x)=\\displaystyle \\sum_{t=1}^{T}\\gamma _t h_t(\boldsymbol x)\)
where every weak learner \(h_t\\)\ typically represents a shallow decision tree fitted sequentially against residual errors left after previous rounds.
Regularization penalties discourage overly deep trees so that variance growth stays manageable despite scarce supervision.
/Random forest/ builds an ensemble \
\(\\widehat y(\boldsymbol x)=\\frac1M\\displaystyle \\sum_{m=1}^{M}h_m(\boldsymbol x).\)
Each tree receives independently bootstrapped samples together with randomly selected subsets\
of features (“feature bagging”), which reduces correlation among members
and yields robust majority voting even under heterogenous attribute types.

/Strengths/. All three techniques require neither external corpora nor complicated prompting mechanisms;
their implementation cost therefore remains modest.
///Weaknesses/. Linear classifiers cannot represent logical conjunctions such、
as “age>65 ∧ cholesterol<200 ⇒ disease,” unless engineered manually;
tree ensembles tend strongly toward memorizing accidental co-occurrences when confronted\
with merely dozens labeled cases––spurious splits dominate prediction instead\
of genuine causal patterns.




# Transfer Learning Across Many Tables  

Cross-table transfer assumes Tableau-level regularities recur among disparate domains—
for instance “age”, “sex”, “blood pressure” often carry similar meanings irrespective\
whether they appear in cardiac studies or diabetes registries.
//TransTab// exemplifies this strategy (~​`\citet{wang2022transtab}`).
It trains a Transformer encoder jointly on thousands public datasets;
inputs combine literal cell values together with natural-language column descriptors\
\(f_j:\)’‘feature name’’ .
During massive self‑supervision attention heads learn embeddings capturing both\
semantic similarity among column names   and relational patterns hidden inside numerical\
vectors.
//Downstream usage/. When presented wit​​h   a new target table containing only \(k​\\)\
labels,the frozen encoder processes all rows unchanged；
a light classification head fitted atop extracted embeddings predicts outcomes \
without further weight updates.
/Benefits/. Because knowledge has already been distilled across diverse sources,\
even extremely tiny annotation budgets (\(k≤5​)\)
yield reasonable performance.
/Limitations/. Transformers enforce fixed token windows;
wide tables (\(d≫50 … 100\") cause unavoidable truncation unless elaborate chunking\
strategies intervene..
Moreover strong domain shift—for example featuring rare biomedical terminology never seen\
during pre-training—weakens semantic embeddings and degrades zero-/few-shot transfer.



# Synthetic Data Pretraining – Methods Exemplified by TabPFN  

When genuine annotations disappear entirely some researchers generate artificial datasets \
covering broad statistical families before ever seeing real observations.
//TabPFN// (~`\citet{hollmann2022tabpfn}`)\
creates numerous synthetic tables drawn from mixtures\ranglehellip{} 



Each synthetic table supplies both input vectors \
\(\\boldsymbol x^{(s)}_i\\in ℝ^d,\)
still respecting prescribed type constraints,\
and binary targets \
\(y^{(s)}_i∈\\{{0},{1}\\).\)
A Bayesian neural network therefore observes extremely varied conditional distributions \
\(p(y│x)\); during pre-training it learns how posterior predictives behave\
across disparate generative settings.
//Inference/. At test time,\
both labeled demonstrations  $(X_{\text {train}},Y_{\text {train}} )$
and all unlabeled queries $(X_{\text {test}} )$
are fed jointly into one forward pass yielding probabilities through Bayes’ rule\
without any gradient step.
/Advantages/. No downstream optimisation required eliminates latency \
associatedwith iterative fine-tuning;\
offline synthesis sidesteps reliance upon external real cohorts thereby respecting privacy.

/Drawbacks/. Predictive quality hinges critically upon whether real-world data resemble some member\
of the synthetic mixture family.
If true distributions exhibit tail behaviour,\
heteroscedasticity,\
or structural dependencies absent from generated bases,\
learned priors may miscalibrate leading interpretations far away \
from reality.



# Semi-Supervised Strategies That Leverage Unlabeled Records  

Many real repositories store abundant raw observations lacking expert tags;\
semi-supervision exploits these records so that limited annotation effort suffices.
//SCARF// (~`\citet{bahri2022scarf}`)\
represents one state-of-the-art design employing contrastive self‑learning.
Given an input batch,\
each datum becomes an anchor\/positive pair after applying mild stochastic transformations\
(e.g., jittering continuous values); remaining batch members act as negatives.
An InfoNCE loss encourages embeddings \\(\\phi(·))\\|
to pull augmented views together while pushing unrelated samples apart:
```latex
L_{\rm iNCE}=-
     \log\dfrac{\exp[\phi(x_i)\!\cdot...\!/\tau]}
               {\exp[\phi(x_i)\!\cdot...\!/\tau]+\!
                \sum_j\rule[-0mm]{0mm}{12pt}
                \exp[\phi(x_i)\!\cdot...\!/\tau]} .
```   
After representation learning finishes,\
a simple linear probe calibrated on \(k ⟨⟩⟩​labels yields class scores.
//STUNT// (~`\citet{nam2019stunt}`)\
takes another route based upon pseudo-label propagation:
an initial weak classifier assigns provisional labels throughout \
the unlabelled pool;\
these provisional targets supplement genuine annotations during successive retraining cycles.
///Positive impact/. Both strategies enlarge effective training size indirectly,\
thereby mitigating outright overfitting characteristic "
“tiny-data’’ regimes seen in vanilla trees.
///Remaining challenges/. Contrastive discrimination presupposes smooth latent manifolds ;
purely categorical catalogues break this assumption causing unstable embeddings .
Pseudo-label pipelines easily inherit early mistakes;\
if pernicious patterns appear early,<->erroneous signals perpetuate permanently﻿



# Language Interfaced Tabular Learning – Direct Prompting (In Context), Parameter-Efficient Tuning (LoRA/IA³), Hybrid Systems Like TABLET  

Large language models preserve extensive factual relationships acquired during webscale pre-training;\
leveraging these assets offers another avenue toward low-shot success.

//Direct Prompting / In Context Learning//  
A practitioner crafts a textual query containing three parts:\newline
(i){ }Task description (“Predict chronic heart failure?”);\newline
(ii){ }Column specifications (“age : integer years,”“cholesterol : mg/dL,” …);\newline 
(iii){ }A few verbatim demonstrations commuting raw field listings → gold label.\newline  
When sent jointly together\-with every test row—to expand attention—the model produces Am answered token sequence (“Yes’’/“No’’). This workflow needs zero parameter updates thus qualifies itself uniquely simple.
//Shortcomings/. Every prediction obliges one remote call‎➡​API(payload)… → runtime scales linearly　
with test-set size и evaluates near term costs extremely high ;
serialising wide tables often exceeds maximum token limits causing forced omission \
or truncation → performances collapse markedly once attribute count exceeds≈60 .

//Parameter-efficient fine-tuning// When creator retains private checkpoint access,
lightweight adaptation techniques insert trainable modules whilst freezing main weights.
//LoRA// (~`\citet{hu2019lora}`)\
replaces any LayerNorm matrix \\(W\\)
with \\(W+AB^\top\\); matrices \\(A,B\\)’ dimensions stay modest so learned parameters occupy ≪ %⁄overall size .
//IA³// (~`\citet{kumar2019ia3}`)\
instead scales internal activations elementwise via learned gate vectors,\
also avoiding storage overhead.
//Effect/*/. Both schemes let users adjust how strongly linguistic priors attend \
specific phrasing present inside prompts yet keep computational memory affordable.
///Constraint/. Commercial closed APIs (=GPT 4&co.) hide underlying weights so LoRA/IA³ cannot be applied;
// consequently developers revert back either unto pure prompting   or completely abandon LM assistance.

//Hybrid frameworks //TABLET// (~`\citet[slack]{slack23tablet}`)\
augment plain prompts using auxiliary rule sets derived from offline clustering &
prototype extraction applied directly onto raw tabular vectors .
These extra hints appear alongside natural-language task specification hoping   
that attention allocated toward clear prototypes guides LM reasoning more accurately.
//Benefit/. Empirical reports indicate modest boosts relative\/un-modified prompts;
/Limitation/. Though rules reduce ambiguity they do _not_ eliminate per-example invocation cost;
longer composite prompts aggravate token overflow problems especially on high-dimension datasets.



# Key Limitations Encountered By Existing Methods  

Across all surveyed families five recurrent practical impediments surface:

* **Per-sample API invocations** dominate every end-to-end LM pipeline—direct prompting,
TABLET or any generation-after-retrieval design forces one remote request per test item –
rendering latency proportional to dataset cardinality­⁠⟨often prohibitive㊙️⟩​in production environments .

* **Requirement for weight-update capability** cripples approaches relying on LoRA,
IA³ or other adaptor schemes whenever users interact solely via black-box service endpoints;
the absence ­ → makes utilisation impossible despite strong conceptual appeal .

* **Prompt length bottlenecks** arise whenever column count \(d ≫ few dozen contributes exceed decoder window sizes (§ Direct Prompting).
Truncating inputs discards potentially decisive attributes ⇢ abrupt drops (>10% AUC observed).

* **Vulnerability to spurious correlations** manifests especially under extreme label paucity :
tree ensembles immediately latch onto accidental feature-target coincidences;
contrastive SSL sometimes amplifies irrelevant clusters ;
pseudo-labelling injects incorrect targets ⇒ error amplification .
Only frameworks explicitly encouraging reasoning over *causal criteria*
rather than mere statistical association show resilience against this phenomenon .

* **Limited adaptability beyond binary/classic classification structures** :
synthetic-pretrain techniques presuppose specific likelihood forms;(TabPFN uses binary Bayes net);
cross-table transformers struggle wherever novel feature taxonomies appear unmet；
semi-supervision hinges upon reasonably separable latent manifolds which many business tables lack .

Collectively these shortcomings motivated recent work proposing alternative roles for LLMs—
notably treating them primarily as automatic feature engineers rather than full end-to-end predictors.
By extracting reusable logical rules once per dataset instead ofsynthesising per-instance answers,
such designs drastically shrink runtime overheads,
sidestep prompt-size restrictions through bagging/subsampling tricks ,
and preserve interpretability thanks_to explicit rule specifications—addressing precisely why earlier paradigms struggled under genuine low-shot constraints.
