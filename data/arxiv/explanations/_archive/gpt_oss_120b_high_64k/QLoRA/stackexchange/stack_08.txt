\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### Why does applying LoRA only to query/value projection matrices fail to match full finetuning performance on LLaMA‑7B?
Question:
The paper says that LoRA adapters must be added at every linear transformer block layer to recover full‑finetuning accuracy.

I'm trying to understand:
* Which parts of the transformer contribute most to performance loss when kept frozen?
* Is there empirical evidence that attention query/value layers alone cannot capture necessary task‐specific adaptations?
* Would increasing the rank \(r\) compensate for fewer adapter locations, or is layer coverage more critical?
Answer:
In “QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs” the authors show that an LoRA setup that touches **every** linear mapping inside a transformer block is required if we want the adapter‑only fine‑tuning to reach the same quality as full model fine‑tuning. When LoRA is confined only to the query $(W_{Q})$ and value $(Wi V)$ projections – the practice most papers follow – the resulting model falls noticeably short of the full‑finetune baseline on LLaMA‑7B (Alpaca instruction data).

### Why query/value alone are insufficient  

A transformer block contains four families of learnable linear maps:

1. the three attention‑projection matrices $(W_{Q}, W_{K}, W_{V})$,  
2. the output‑projection matrix $(W_{O})$,  
3. the two feed‑forward (FFN) matrices $(W_{\text up}, W_{\text down})$.  

The two FFN matrices dominate the parameter budget: in LLaMA‑7B roughly half of all weights live in the feed‑forward network, while the attention part accounts for only about one third (the three $(W_{Q/K/V})$ plus $(W_O)$). Because LoRA works by adding a low‑rank correction

## What this tells us about where adaptation lives  

Because LoRA’s memory footprint ≪ activation gradients _(dominant cost)_, QLoRA can afford add adapters after *every* linear map without hurting overall GPU usage．Key takeaways：

* **The feed‐forward network is essential**‐it holds most weights& performs non‐linear feature expansion reshaped during instruction tuning．  
* **Attention output ($$(...) *(something)* )** matters­–even if queries/values adapt well ⁠⁠without updating how attended information mixes back into token representations you lose crucial freedom．  
* **Increasing rank does ‑not rescue thin coverage**——an expanded subspace across few matrices cannot replace missing transformations elsewhere．  
* **Full‐layer coverage yields parity with full fine‐tuning**—when every linear layer carries its own low‐rank pair $(L₁,L₂)$ QLoRA matches 16­bit full fine­tuning across benchmark tasks ‑GLUE、T5 instruction sets、MMLU．  

Thus applying LoRA solely to query⊕value projections leaves large portions … 

By distributing tiny adapters throughout *all* linear components ⟨queries⟩⟨keys⟩⟨values⟩⟨output projection⟩⟨both FFN projections⟩ ——i.e., assigning low­rank updates collectively regain virtually all expressive power lost by freezing base weights§so explains why “layer wise” strategy succeeds while “ Q / V ‑only ” does not．
