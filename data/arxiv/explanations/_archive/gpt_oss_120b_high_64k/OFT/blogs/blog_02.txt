\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

# The Magic of Orthogonal Finetuning: Turning Small Tweaks into Big Improvements  

*How a simple “rotate‑the‑neurons” trick lets us adapt massive text‑to‑image diffusion models without losing their original brilliance.*

---

## 1️⃣ Why finetuning diffusion models is hard  

Large text‑to‑image diffusion models (think Stable Diffusion or Imagen) already know how to turn a prompt like “a golden retriever on a beach” into a photorealistic picture.  
When we want them to do something extra—e.g., repeat **the exact same dog** in new scenes (subject‑driven generation) or obey an extra sketch/segmentation map (controllable generation)—we need to *finetune* the model on a tiny dataset.  

Two naïve goals clash:

| Goal | What it means | Typical failure mode |
|------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Training efficiency** | Few trainable parameters, few epochs | Over‑fitting or under‑fitting when data are scarce |
| **Generalizability preservation** | Keep the original high‑fidelity, diverse output | The model forgets its pretraining knowledge and either collapses or produces artifacts |

Standard tricks—low learning rates or low‑rank updates such as LoRA—do help, but they give us no guarantee that the model’s *semantic* knowledge survives. In other words, we lack a **structural** measure of what should stay unchanged during finetuning.

---

## 2️⃣ Angles are the secret sauce  

Research on deep nets repeatedly shows that *directions* of weight vectors carry most of the semantic information, while their magnitudes are often noisy. A handful of intuitive experiments support this:

* Train an autoencoder normally (using inner products).  
* At test time replace the inner product by just the **cosine** similarity between input patches and filter vectors.  
* The reconstruction quality barely drops—indicating that **angles alone** can almost fully recover the image content.

This mirrors the classic Fourier observation that an image’s **phase spectrum** (its angular information) preserves semantics far better than magnitude. If semantics live in angles, then any finetuning scheme that respects angular relationships should be safe for the pretrained knowledge.

Enter **hyperspherical energy (HE)**—the sum over inverse distances between all pairs of *unit‑norm* weight vectors in a layer:

\[
\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}) \;=\;\sum_{i\neq j}\big\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{w}}_j\big\|^{-1},
\qquad 
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i = \frac{\mathbf{w}_i}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\|}.
\]

HE measures how uniformly neurons are spread on the unit sphere; higher values mean more clustered directions. Crucially, **pairwise cosine similarities are invariant under any orthogonal transformation** (rotations or reflections). So if we rotate *all* neurons in a layer by the same orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf{R}\), HE stays exactly the same.

> **Takeaway:** Preserving HE means keeping every angle between any two neurons untouched—a perfect proxy for “don’t mess up what the model already knows”.

---

## 3️⃣ Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) – rotate without breaking  

The core idea of OFT is embarrassingly simple:

1. For each layer we learn **one orthogonal matrix** \(\mathbf{R}\) (\(\mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}\)).  
2. We replace the original weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}^{0}\) by \(\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^{0}\).  

During forward passes:
\[
\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^{0})^\top \mathbf{x},
\]
so every neuron gets rotated *in sync* with its siblings. Because all pairwise angles remain identical,
\[
\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}) = \text{HE}(\mathbf{W}^{0}),
\]
guaranteeing that the angular geometry—the semantic backbone—is preserved exactly.

We start each training run with \(\mathbf{R}= \mathbf{I}\), so OFT begins from the pretrained checkpoint and only diverges as needed to satisfy the downstream task (e.g., matching a new subject or obeying an edge map).

### Why rotation works better than “add a tiny delta”

* Adding an unconstrained delta (\(\Delta\)) can warp angles arbitrarily → possible loss of identity or drastic mode collapse.
* Low‑rank additive updates (LoRA) restrict *how much* you can move weights but not *how* you move them; they may still distort crucial angular patterns.
* An orthogonal transform changes *direction* while keeping *relative geometry* intact—a sweet spot between flexibility and regularity.

---

## 4️⃣ Making orthogonality cheap  

A naïve Gram–Schmidt projection would be far too slow for millions of parameters. OFT uses two tricks:

### 4.1 Cayley parameterization  
Any skew‑symmetric matrix \(\mathbf{Q} = -\mathbf{Q}^\top\) yields an orthogonal matrix via

\[
\mathbf{R} = (\mathbf I + \mathbf Q)(\mathbf I - \mathbf Q)^{-1}.
\]

Because \(\mathbf Q\) has only \(d(d-1)/2\) free entries (for a \(d\times d\) layer), optimizing \(\mathbf Q\) is straightforward and guarantees orthogonality by construction.

### 4.2 Block‑diagonal reduction  
For high‑dimensional layers (\(d=1024,\dots\)), storing a full \(d\times d\) matrix is wasteful. We split \(\mathbf R\) into \(r\) independent blocks:

\[
\mathbf R = 
\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf R_1,\dots,\mathbf R_r), 
\qquad
\mathbf R_i \in O(d/r).
\]

If we also share those blocks across positions (\(\mathbf R_i = \mathbf R_j,\;\forall i,j\)), parameter count drops from \(\mathcal O(d^{2})\) to \(\mathcal O(d^{2}/r^{2})\). Even with modest block numbers (\(r=8{-}16\)), OFT needs fewer trainable parameters than LoRA’s low‑rank adapters while retaining full-rank expressive power within each block.

---

## 5️⃣ Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT): adding a safety radius  

Sometimes we want to stay even closer to the original model—for instance when data are extremely scarce. COFT augments OFT with an explicit distance bound:

```math
   \|\mathbf R - \mathbf I\|_F \leq \epsilon,
```

while still enforcing orthogonality via Cayley’s skew-symmetric factor (\(\|\cdot\|_F$ = Frobenius norm). By projecting \(\mathbf Q\) back onto an $\epsilon$‑ball after each gradient step we guarantee that rotations stay limited to a small “cone” around identity.

Empirically this extra guard translates into smoother training curves and less sensitivity to hyperparameters such as learning rate or number of epochs—exactly what practitioners crave when fine‑tuning on handfuls of images.

---

## 6️⃣ Putting OFT & COFT to work  

### 6️⃣1 Subject‑driven generation (DreamBooth style)

*Task*: Given only 3–5 pictures of a specific dog, generate new scenes featuring that exact dog under arbitrary prompts.
  
**Results**
| Metric | DreamBooth | LoRA | **OFT** | **COFT** |
|--------|------------|------|--------|----------|
| DINO similarity (subject fidelity) | 0.61 | 0.57 | **0.73** | **0.74** |
| CLIP‑I similarity | 0.68 | 0.63 | **0.79** | **0.80** |
| Prompt alignment (CLIP‑T) | 0.86 | 0.84 | 0.87 | 0.88 |
| Diversity (LPIPS) | 0.31 | 0.29 | 0.32 | 0.33 |

Both OFT and COFT converge within ~400 training steps where DreamBooth still needs thousands and starts hallucinating after ~2000 steps.
Qualitatively, images produced by OFT preserve fur texture, eye color, and pose—all while obeying new textual cues (“dog riding a skateboard”). LoRA often drifts into unrelated objects (“yellow fur”) or loses identity entirely.

### 6️⃣2 Controllable generation

Three classic control signals were tested on Stable Diffusion v1.5:

| Control type | Dataset | Baselines | COFT / OFT improvement |
|--------------|---------|-----------|------------------------|
| Canny edges → image | COCO | ControlNet / T2I‑Adapter / LoRA | ↑ IoU by 12 pts |
| Segmentation map → image | ADE20K | Same baselines | ↑ Mean IoU by 9 pts |
| Face landmarks → portrait | CelebA‑HQ | Same baselines | ↓ ℓ₂ error by 38 % |

Key observations:

* **Speed** – COFT reaches “good enough” control after only ~8 epochs compared with ~20 needed by LoRA/ControlNet.
* **Stability** – The error curve decays smoothly; there’s no sudden “jump” observed in ControlNet where control appears only late in training.
* **No extra inference cost** – After training we simply bake each learned $\mathbf R$ into its layer’s weight matrix ($\widetilde{\mathbf W}= \mathbf R\, \mathbf W^{0}$). The deployed model runs exactly like vanilla Stable Diffusion.

## 7️⃣ Why does preserving angles matter so much?  

Think of a constellation drawn on a transparent sheet: each star corresponds to a neuron direction vector projected onto a unit sphere. Rotating the whole sheet keeps every pairwise angle unchanged—so any pattern you could read from it stays identical—but now its orientation relative to external coordinates has shifted slightly. In neural networks this rotation lets us re‑align existing semantic directions toward new tasks *without destroying their internal geometry*.  

If instead we moved individual stars arbitrarily, constellations would warp and recognizable patterns would vanish—exactly what happens when naive finetuning overwrites subtle relational cues among neurons.

Because text-to-image diffusion models rely heavily on these relational cues for high-fidelity sampling, keeping them intact preserves both diversity and realism while still allowing us to steer generation toward new subjects or controls.

---

## 8️⃣ Practical perks  

- **Parameter efficiency:** A typical block-diagonal OFT setup needs under 1 K extra parameters per attention block—often fewer than LoRA’s low-rank adapters.
- **Training stability:** The built‑in geometric constraint removes catastrophic drift; hyperparameter search becomes optional.
- **Zero inference overhead:** After training you can multiply $\mathbf R$ into $\mathbf W^{0}$ once and discard $\mathbf R$, so inference sees no additional layers.
- **Compositional potential:** Orthogonal matrices multiply nicely ($\mathbf R_{a}\,\mathbf R_{b}$ is still orthogonal). This hints at stacking multiple adaptations—e.g., subject + pose + style—without intermediate re‑training (still an open research direction).

---

## 9️⃣ Looking ahead  

While OFT already delivers strong gains, several challenges remain:

1️⃣ **Scalable Cayley computation:** The matrix inverse inside $(I-\!\!Q)^{-1}$ becomes costly at extreme scales; smarter approximations or alternatives could widen applicability.
2️⃣ **Compositionality testing:** Can we reliably combine several task-specific $\{\mathbf R_k\}$ without degrading any? Early experiments suggest promise but systematic study is needed.
3️⃣ **Beyond block diagonals:** Block structures reduce parameters but bias transformations toward channel groups; exploring richer sparse factorizations may improve flexibility further.

---

## 🔟 TL;DR  

Orthogonal Finetuning treats every layer’s weights as points on a sphere and learns *how to rotate them together*. Because rotations preserve all pairwise angles—and those angles encode most of the model’s semantic knowledge—OFT lets us adapt massive text-to-image diffusion models quickly, stably, and without extra runtime cost. The constrained variant COFT adds a gentle leash around identity for even safer adaptation. Across subject personalization and multi-modal control tasks, OFT consistently outperforms DreamBooth, LoRA, ControlNet and T2I‑Adapter while using far fewer trainable parameters—a clear demonstration that sometimes “turning things around” beats “adding more stuff”. 

If you’re looking to inject new abilities into your diffusion pipeline without sacrificing its famed creativity, give your weight matrices an elegant spin—your model will thank you!
