### How should one choose the block size \(r\) in the block‑diagonal orthogonal matrix, and what are the trade‑offs?
Question:
The paper proposes representing \(\mathbf R\) as a block‑diagonal matrix with \(r\) blocks to reduce parameter count from \(O(d^2)\) to \(O(d^2/r)\). I am unclear about:

1. What practical guidelines exist for selecting \(r\)?
2. How does increasing \(r\) (more blocks → smaller blocks) affect model flexibility versus training stability?
3. When sharing blocks across positions (so parameter cost becomes \(O(d^2/r^2)\)), what bias is introduced into the learned transformation?
Answer:
The block–diagonal construction is introduced only as a **parameter–efficiency trick** – it does not change the fact that the finetuned weight matrix is obtained by an orthogonal transform  
$$
\mathbf W = \mathbf R\,\mathbf W^{0},\qquad 
\mathbf R^{!\top}\!\,\mathbf R=\mathbf I .
$$

$$
{\bfR}= {\rm diag}({{\bfR}}_1,\dots ,{{{\bfR}}}_{{\,} r}),		&&
