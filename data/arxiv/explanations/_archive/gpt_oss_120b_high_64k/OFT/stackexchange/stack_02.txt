### How does an orthogonal transformation guarantee that hyperspherical energy remains unchanged?
Question:
The paper claims that applying the same orthogonal matrix \(\mathbf{R}\) to all neurons in a layer preserves pair‑wise angles and thus keeps \(\text{HE}(\mathbf{W})\) identical to \(\text{HE}(\mathbf{W}^0)\). Could someone provide a concise proof or intuitive argument showing that

$$\text{HE}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0) = \text{HE}(\mathbf{W}^0)$$

for any orthogonal \(\mathbf{R}\)? Specifically, I am unsure how normalising the rows after multiplication interacts with the distance term inside HE.

### Orthogonal maps preserve norms and inner products

Let $\bR\in{\mathsf O}(d)$ be any orthogonal matrix,
$\bR^{T}\bR=\bI.$  

For every vector $\bv∈\bbr^{d}$

* **Norm is preserved**

  $$
  \norm{\bR\bv}^{2}
     = (\bR\bv)^{T}(\bR\bv)
     = 	\bv^{T}\!
           (\bR^{T}\!\bR)\,
          	\bv
     = 	\norm{\bv}^{2}.
  $$

* **Inner product is preserved**

  $$
  \<\,\bR\a,\;\bR\b>
     = (\bR\a)^{T}(\bR\b)
     = \<\,a,b>.
  $$

Consequently an orthogonal matrix acts like a *rigid rotation or reflection* of the whole space: it does not stretch, shrink or shear any direction.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### What happens to the rows after the transform?

Apply the same orthogonal matrix to every row of $\bW:$  

$$
\tW=	\bR\,	\bW ,
		qquad	
\tw_i=	\bR\times	w_i .
$$

Because norms are preserved,

$$
\norm{\tw_i}
   =	\norm{w_i},
    
    	
\uwh\tw_i    
:=      
\tw_i/\norm{\tw_i}
=
	\rho\times	
	\uwh	w_i .
$$

Thus normalising *after* multiplying by $\bR$ is exactly the same as first normalising and then rotating the unit vectors.

### Intuition

Geometrically an orthogonal matrix rotates (or mirrors) **the whole point cloud**
formed by the weight rows on the unit hypersphere.
Just as rotating a rigid object does not change distances between any two of its points,
rotating all neurons together does not alter their angular relationships.
Since hyperspherical energy is exactly a monotone function of those angles,
it remains invariant.



---------------------------------------------------------------------

### Remarks specific to the paper

* The invariance holds for any exponent used in HE (the paper uses $s=1,\;\norm·^{-1}$;
with other powersthe same argument applies because only the distance value changes).
* The proof requires *the same* orthogonal map for **all** rows of a layer –
different maps per neuron would break pair‑wise angle preservation.
* Normalising after multiplication does **not** interfere with the distance term,
because orthogonal matrices keep each vector’s length unchanged;
consequently normalisation simply commutes with $\bR.$



Therefore Equation (3) in the paper,
$
 	H_E(\bRW⁰)=H_E(\bW⁰),
  ∀ \bR∈{\mathsf O}(d),
$
follows directly from norm‑ and inner‑product preservation under orthogonal transformations.
This guarantees that OFT never changes hyperspherical energy,
which explains why it can fine‑tune while keeping pretrained semantic structure intact.
