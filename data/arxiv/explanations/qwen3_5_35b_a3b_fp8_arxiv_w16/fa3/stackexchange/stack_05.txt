\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Why does causal masking penalize FP8 FlashAttention-3 performance more than FP16?
Question:
In Table 3 and Figure 5 of the experimental section, it is noted that for large sequence lengths with causal masking, FP8 FlashAttention-3 falls behind cuDNN's FP8 implementation, whereas FP16 remains competitive or superior. The footnote mentions head dimensions 128 and 256 behave differently than 64. What is the structural reason why causal masking interacts negatively with the specific pipelining strategies (like blocking or register reuse) used for FP8? Is it because causality forces irregular memory access patterns that clash with TMA/WGMMA assumptions?
Answer:
The structural reason why causal masking penalizes FP8 FlashAttention-3 performance more than FP16 lies in the rigid layout constraints of FP8 hardware combined with the irregular memory access patterns required by causal masking, which exacerbates the overhead of necessary data transpositions and register permutations. Unlike FP16, where the WGMMA instruction accepts both `mn-major` and `k-major` layouts, FP8 WGMMA strictly enforces a `k-major` layout for its input operands. In the context of FlashAttention-3, this creates a critical dependency: while the Key matrix ($\mathbf{K}$) is naturally accessed in a `k-major` fashion during the dot-product phase, the Value matrix ($\mathbf{V}$) must be transposed from its native `mn-major` (contiguous in head dimension) format to `k-major` (contiguous in sequence length) to satisfy the second GEMM requirement.

Under normal non-causal conditions, this transposition is handled efficiently using Warp-Level Transpose Instructions (`LDSM`/`STSM`) and can be perfectly overlapped with computation via the kernel's aggressive pipelining strategies. However, causal masking introduces a fundamental asymmetry in how blocks are processed. When applying a causal mask, the algorithm cannot simply process entire rectangular blocks of $\mathbf{V}$ uniformly; instead, it must handle jagged triangular regions where valid computations stop earlier depending on the query index. This irregularity disrupts the smooth, predictable stride assumptions that TMA (Tensor Memory Accelerator) and warp-specialized pipelines rely upon for optimal latency hiding.

Specifically, for larger head dimensions like $128$ and $256$ (but less so for $64$), the overhead of managing these jagged boundaries becomes significant enough to interfere with the tight coupling between TMA loads and WGMMA execution. The paper notes that FP8 performance lags behind cuDNN specifically with causal masking because cuDNN likely employs a persistent kernel design or different blocking strategies that better accommodate these irregularities without stalling the pipeline. In contrast, FlashAttention-3's reliance on precise register reuse and block quantization assumes a regular flow that is broken by the conditional skipping inherent in causal masks. Consequently, the pipeline stalls waiting for data alignment or suffers from inefficient register allocation due to the inability to fully amortize the cost of transposing $\mathbf{V}$ tiles across variable-sized chunks.

Furthermore, this issue is compounded by the fact that FP8 operations require specific byte-permute instructions to align accumulator layouts with operand layouts after every GEMM step. While effective for regular blocks, these permutations add latency that becomes visible when the natural overlap between softmax computation and GEMM is disrupted by the branching logic of causal masking. In essence, while FP16 retains enough flexibility to absorb these scheduling disruptions through its looser memory layout requirements, FP8's strict adherence to `k-major` formatting turns what would be minor control-flow penalties into major structural bottlenecks when dealing with non-uniform block sizes imposed by causality.
