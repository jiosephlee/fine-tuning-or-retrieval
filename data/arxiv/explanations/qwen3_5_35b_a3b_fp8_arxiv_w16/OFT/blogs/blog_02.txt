# DreamBooth vs. OFT: Solving the Stability Crisis in Custom AI Models

Have you ever tried to teach an AI to draw your dog, only for it to turn your pet into a fuzzy blob after too many training steps? This frustrating phenomenon, often referred to as model collapse or loss of semantic integrity, is a common hurdle in subject-driven generation. While tools like DreamBooth revolutionized the field by allowing users to personalize text-to-image models with just a few photos, they often struggle with stability. If you train for too long, the model forgets how to generate anything else; if you stop too early, it hasn't learned your subject well enough. In this post, we explore a new solution proposed in recent research: Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). By leveraging a principled mathematical approach rather than just tweaking learning rates, OFT offers lightning-fast convergence and rock-solid stability, ensuring your custom AI models remain sharp and reliable.

### The Hidden Cost of Small Learning Rates

Traditional methods for personalizing diffusion models, such as DreamBooth and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), rely on a somewhat heuristic strategy: they update the model's weights using a very small learning rate. The underlying assumption here is that keeping the Euclidean distance between the finetuned weights and the original pretrained weights small will preserve the model's general abilities. However, this approach lacks a rigorous theoretical foundation. It treats the vast parameter space of a diffusion model as a flat surface where tiny steps are safe, ignoring the complex geometric relationships between neurons that actually encode meaning.

When researchers analyze these models through the lens of hyperspherical geometry, they find that semantic information is deeply tied to the angles between neurons rather than just their magnitudes or absolute positions. Existing methods fail to explicitly protect these angular relationships. Consequently, as training progresses, the delicate structure required for high-quality generation can degrade unpredictably. This leads to instability where increasing training iterations eventually destroys the very capabilities we are trying to enhance. We need a method that doesn't just hope for stability but mathematically guarantees it.

### Preserving Semantics with Orthogonal Transformations

The core innovation of Orthogonal Finetuning lies in its treatment of neuron directions. Inspired by observations from deep learning theory that angular features carry significant semantic information—similar to how phase spectra preserve image content while magnitude spectra vary—the authors propose that we should modify *how* neurons point rather than just moving them arbitrarily. OFT achieves this by applying an orthogonal transformation to the weight matrices of specific layers during finetuning.

An orthogonal transformation is essentially a rotation or reflection in high-dimensional space. Crucially, rotating vectors does not change the angles between them. By enforcing that all neurons in a layer undergo the same orthogonal transformation relative to their pretrained state, OFT ensures that "hyperspherical energy" remains invariant. Hyperspherical energy measures how uniformly distributed neurons are on a unit hypersphere; preserving this value means preserving the pairwise relational structure among neurons. Because this structure is fundamental to how the network represents knowledge, maintaining it allows the model to adapt to new tasks without erasing its pre-trained generative prowess. This stands in stark contrast to standard gradient descent updates, which can inadvertently scramble these critical angular relationships.

### From Theory to Practice: OFT and COFT

While standard OFT guarantees structural preservation by design, practical applications sometimes require tighter control over how far the model deviates from its original state during adaptation. To address this nuance, researchers introduced Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT). COFT adds an explicit radius constraint on top of the orthogonality requirement, limiting how much the transformed weights can drift from their initial configuration within a hypersphere of fixed radius centered on the pretrained weights.

This dual-constraint approach creates a robust framework where flexibility meets strict regularity:
*   **Orthogonality** ensures that internal semantic structures remain intact regardless of how much we try to adapt the model externally.
*   **Radius Constraints** prevent overfitting by keeping adjustments within a manageable neighborhood of the original weights.

Furthermore, implementing these constraints efficiently was critical for adoption on massive diffusion models like Stable Diffusion v1.5 directly addressing computational costs through techniques like Cayley parameterization and block-diagonal matrices reduces trainable parameters significantly compared to LoRA while maintaining performance parity or exceeding it in stability tests.

### Empirical Evidence: Speed and Quality Wins

The impact of OFT becomes immediately apparent when comparing it against established baselines like DreamBooth and LoRA across two primary tasks: subject-driven generation and controllable generation involving external signals like edges or segmentation maps. In subject-driven scenarios where users provide only a handful of images (often fewer than ten), OFT demonstrates remarkable resilience against convergence issues that plague other methods after thousands of iterations.
