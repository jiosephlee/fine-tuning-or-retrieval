# Benchmarks that Matter: FeatLLM vs. State‑of‑the‑Art on 13 Diverse Tabular Tasks  

---

## Why tabular learning still needs a new hero

Tabular data keep re‑appearing in industry: credit scoring, medical diagnosis, real‑time fraud detection, and even synthetic experiments used to stress‑test algorithms.  When only a handful of labeled examples are available—a reality for many domains where data are expensive or privacy‑bound—the classic “train a large model” mantra simply fails.  

Large Language Models (LLMs) have begun to show promise here because they come pre‑trained on billions of words of text and can reason about associations without explicit supervision.  But most LLM‑based tabular approaches still hit two hard walls:

1. **Inference cost** – each test sample must be fed to the LLM, which is slow and expensive.  
2. **Prompt size limits** – many datasets have dozens or hundreds of columns; when the serialized text grows beyond the LLM’s context window, performance drops or the method even becomes infeasible.

A new framework called **FeatLLM** sidesteps these problems by letting an LLM *act as a feature engineer* rather than a prediction engine.  The rules it writes become simple binary features that a lightweight downstream model can use for fast, accurate predictions.

Below we walk through how FeatLLM stacks up against state‑of‑the‑art baselines across 13 very different datasets spanning medical, financial, synthetic and newly released problems—all in the low‑shot regime.
