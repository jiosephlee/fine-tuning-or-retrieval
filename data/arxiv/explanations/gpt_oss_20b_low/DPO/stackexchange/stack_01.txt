\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does the partition function cancel out when reparameterizing the reward in DPO?
Question:
In the DPO derivation (Equation 12), we express the reward as \(r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y|x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\). Later, in Equation 13 the partition function \(Z(x)\) disappears. Could someone explain algebraically how exactly the \(\log Z(x)\) terms cancel out when we substitute \(r\) into the Bradley‑Terry preference probability? I want to see the intermediate steps.
Answer:
In DPO we start from the Bradley‑Terry model for a human preference over two completions \(y_{1},y_{2}\) given a prompt \(x\)

\[
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=
\frac{\exp\!\bigl(r^*(x,y_1)\bigr)}
     {\exp\!\bigl(r^*(x,y_1)\bigr)+\exp\!\bigl(r^*(x,y_2)\bigr)} .
\tag{BT}
\]

Using the optimal‑policy/reward relationship derived in Eq. (9) of the paper,

\[
r^*(x,y)=\beta \log\frac{\pi^*(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)}
          +\beta \log Z(x),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\pi^*\) is the optimal policy corresponding to the ground‑truth reward
\(r^*\) and \(Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)
               \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac1\beta r^*(x,y)\bigr)\)
is a *partition function* that depends only on the prompt \(x\).

The only thing that matters for the Bradley‑Terry likelihood is the *difference*
\(r^*(x,y_1)-r^*(x,y_2)\); constants that are the same for both completions cancel out.
Hence we can substitute the expression (1) directly into the BT model:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)
&=\frac{
      \exp\!\bigl(
      \beta \log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}
      +\beta \log Z(x)
      \bigr)}
     {\exp\!\bigl(
      \beta \log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}
      +\beta \log Z(x)
      \bigr)
     +\exp\!\bigl(
      \beta \log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_2\mid x)}
      +\beta \log Z(x)
      \bigr)}\\[4pt]
&=\frac{
      Z(x)^{\beta}\,
      \Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}}
     {Z(x)^{\beta}\,
      \Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}
     +Z(x)^{\beta}\,
      \Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_2\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}} .
\end{aligned}
\]

Because the factor \(Z(x)^{\beta}\) is common to numerator and denominator,
it cancels completely:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)
&=\frac{
      \Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}}
     {\Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_1\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}
      +\Bigl(\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_2\mid x)}\Bigr)^{\beta}}\\[4pt]
&=\frac{1}{1+
\exp\!\Bigl(
  \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_2\mid x)}
  -\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm \ref}(y_1\mid x)}
  \Bigr)} .
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality follows from the identity
\(\displaystyle
\frac{a^{\beta}}{a^{\beta}+b^{\beta}}
=\frac{1}{1+\exp(\beta\log(b/a))}\).

Thus, after inserting the re‑parameterised reward into the Bradley‑Terry likelihood,
the partition function \(Z(x)\) disappears entirely, leaving a probability that depends
only on the optimal policy \(\pi^*\) and the reference policy \(\pi_{\rm \ref}\).
This algebraic cancellation is exactly what the paper refers to when it says
“the partition function cancels”, and it is the key step that allows the
policy‑directed objective (Eq. 13) to be written without any explicit
normalisation term.
