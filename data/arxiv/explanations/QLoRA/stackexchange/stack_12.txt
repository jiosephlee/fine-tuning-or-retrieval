### Information-theoretic optimality of NF4: under what objective is equal-bin-mass optimal?
Question:
The paper calls NF4 "information theoretically optimal for zero-centered normally distributed data" by ensuring equal expected number of values per bin (quantile quantization). Under which precise optimality criterion is this true?

For example, is the claim about minimizing mean squared quantization error (MSE) for a scalar non-uniform quantizer with fixed codebook size and unconstrained decision thresholds (i.e., the Lloyd–Max optimum for a known source)? Or is it about maximizing mutual information between input and quantized output under an entropy constraint? Since the Lloyd–Max solution for a Gaussian is not, in general, exactly the equal-probability quantizer, could you clarify the exact sense in which NF4 is optimal and whether the centroids are set to conditional means or mid-quantile points?
Answer:
Short answer: $NF_4$ is "optimal" in the information-theoretic sense used in the paper: it implements a $k$-bit quantizer whose bins have equal probability mass under a zero-mean normal prior, i.e., a quantile (equal-probability) quantizer. That is information-theoretically optimal if your objective is to make the discrete quantized symbol distribution uniform (maximize the entropy of the quantized output / maximize coding efficiency) for a Gaussian source after a fixed rescaling. It is not claiming Lloyd–Max (MSE) optimality, and the stored levels in $NF_4$ are mid-quantile points (not the conditional means that Lloyd–Max would use).

Explanation and connection to broader concepts (concise):

The paper constructs $NF_k$ by taking the quantiles of a standard normal and using the midpoints between adjacent quantiles as the representer values $q_i$ (Equation in the paper):
$q_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(Q_X\!\left(\frac{i}{2^k+1}\right) + Q_X\!\left(\frac{i+1}{2^k+1}\right)\right),$
where $Q_X$ is the Gaussian quantile function. This yields bins that, by construction, each contain equal expected probability under $N(0,1)$ once the tensor is rescaled to the data-type range. Two consequences follow immediately from how the paper motivates $NF_4$:

1) Information-theoretic interpretation in the paper: equal-mass bins make the discrete output symbols equiprobable for the assumed Gaussian source. Equiprobable discrete symbols maximize entropy among all discrete distributions on the same alphabet size, so $NF_4$ is optimal if your goal is to maximize the entropy (or equivalently, to make the quantized symbol stream as information-dense as possible) under the Gaussian prior. This is the sense of “information-theoretically optimal” used in the paper: $NF_k$ matches the quantiles of the Gaussian so the quantized outputs carry maximal symbol entropy for that prior and fixed alphabet size. This is also the idea behind quantile quantization referenced in the paper.

2) It is NOT the same as minimizing MSE (Lloyd–Max / rate-distortion for squared error). The MSE-optimal scalar quantizer for a given source places decision boundaries and reconstruction levels to minimize expected squared error; optimal reconstruction levels are the conditional means of the source within each cell, and for a Gaussian the resulting cell widths are not equal in probability in general. So the Lloyd–Max solution for MSE generally differs from an equal-probability quantizer. $NF_4$ uses mid-quantile points (midpoints between quantiles) as the stored values, not the conditional means. Thus $NF_4$ is not claiming to be MSE-optimal.

Why choose the $NF_4$ criterion in practice? The paper argues that pretrained network weights are well approximated as zero-mean Gaussians, and that (a) quantile/EQ bins avoid saturating some codes (good code utilization), (b) equal-mass bins give high symbol entropy (good for compact representation and for minimizing information loss under a coding viewpoint), and (c) for their workflow (blockwise rescaling into a fixed $[-1,1]$ range) it is cheap and robust. Empirically $NF_4$ outperforms $FP_4/Int_4$ for model quality after dequantization and QLoRA finetuning, even though it is not MSE-optimal in the Lloyd sense.

So to summarize: the paper’s “information theoretically optimal” refers to the equal-probability (quantile) design being optimal for the information-theoretic objective of producing equiprobable symbols (maximizing entropy / coding efficiency) under a Gaussian prior after rescaling. It is not a claim of MSE (Lloyd–Max) optimality, and the centroids used are mid-quantile midpoints rather than conditional means.
