According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', if you increase the blocksize used for storing weight scales to reduce per-parameter scale overhead without using Double Quantization, you are most likely to sacrifice quantization precision. The paper’s block-wise k-bit quantization scheme normalizes each block by its absolute maximum to compute a per-block scale \(c\), i.e., \(\mathbf{X}^{\text{k-bit}}=\mathrm{round}\!\left(c\,\mathbf{X}^{\text{FP32}}\right)\) with \(c=\frac{2^{k-1}-1}{\mathrm{absmax}(\mathbf{X})}\). As the blocksize grows, the probability of an outlier within a block increases, so \(\mathrm{absmax}\) becomes larger and the effective quantization step \(\Delta\) (the distance between adjacent representable values after dequantization) grows proportionally, yielding higher mean-squared quantization error. This is why the paper explicitly notes that “a small blocksize is required for precise 4-bit quantization,” and uses a blocksize of 64 for weights: small blocks limit outlier-induced scaling and better match local statistics, which is especially critical at 4 bits where the number of bins \(2^k\) is small and bin utilization must be high.

Moreover, NormalFloat (NF4) is designed to be information-theoretically optimal when the weights are normalized to a fixed range and their local distribution is close to \(N(0,1)\). With larger blocks, a single scale \(c\) must service a more heterogeneous set of weights whose local standard deviations \(\sigma\) differ, so rescaling by a global \(\mathrm{absmax}\) misaligns many elements with the NF4 quantile bins \(q_i\). Formally, if weights in different subregions of a large block have variances \(\sigma_j\), then using one \(c\) implies a shared dequantization \(\hat{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}}/c\), which cannot simultaneously minimize quantization error across the mixture \(\sum_j \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_j^2)\). The paper introduces Double Quantization specifically to avoid this precision–memory trade-off: keep the small blocksize for weights (preserving precision) while reducing scale memory from \(\frac{32}{B}\) bits/parameter to \(\frac{8}{B}+\frac{32}{B\cdot 256}\), i.e., from \(0.5\) to \(\approx 0.127\) bits/parameter at \(B=64\), without increasing blocksize and thus without sacrificing quantization precision.
