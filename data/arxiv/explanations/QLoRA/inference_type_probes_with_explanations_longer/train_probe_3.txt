In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', conceptually, the role of the quantization constant $c$ in mapping between low-bit and high-bit representations is to act as a scale factor. This is explicit in the quantization/dequantization formulas: to quantize a high-precision tensor $\mathbf{X}^{\text{FP32}}$ into an Int8 tensor, the paper rescales by the constant $c=\frac{127}{\text{absmax}(\mathbf{X}^{\text{FP32}})}$ so that the dynamic range of the target low-bit type is fully used, i.e., $\mathbf{X}^{\text{Int8}}=\text{round}(c\cdot \mathbf{X}^{\text{FP32}})$. The inverse mapping is simply division by the same $c$, $\text{dequant}(c,\mathbf{X}^{\text{Int8}})=\frac{\mathbf{X}^{\text{Int8}}}{c}=\mathbf{X}^{\text{FP32}}$, which shows $c$ functions as a linear scale linking the discrete code values to their continuous floating-point counterparts. In block-wise quantization, each block gets its own $c_i$ to adaptively scale local distributions and avoid outlier-driven underutilization of bins, reinforcing the interpretation of $c$ as a per-block scale factor that aligns the data’s range with the quantizer’s range.

The same scaling role appears in QLoRA’s extensions. For NF4, weights are normalized into a fixed range $[-1,1]$ and quantized using a codebook derived from $N(0,1)$ quantiles; the normalization step effectively rescales the tensor’s spread to match the datatype’s range, which is again a scaling operation. In Double Quantization, the first-level quantization constants $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ are themselves quantized to $c_2^{\text{k-bit}}$ with a second set of scales $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$, and the forward computation dequantizes via a nested application of scales: $\text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}},c_2^{\text{k-bit}},\mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}})=\text{dequant}(\text{dequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}},c_2^{\text{k-bit}}),\mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}})=\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$. Here each $c$ term linearly maps from a lower-precision representation back to a higher-precision one. Across these formulations, $c$ consistently serves as the proportionality factor that preserves relative magnitudes when moving between quantized codes and their dequantized floating tensors, enabling high-fidelity computation and gradient flow in 16-bit while storing parameters in 4-bit.
