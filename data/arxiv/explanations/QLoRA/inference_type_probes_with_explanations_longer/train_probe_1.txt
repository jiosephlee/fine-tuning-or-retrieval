In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the authors halved the learning rate and doubled the batch size for larger models to stabilize training. This choice is sensible because larger base models (33B and 65B) typically exhibit higher curvature in their loss landscape, which tightens the stability bound for first-order optimizers, often approximated by the condition \(\eta < 2/\lambda_{\max}\), where \(\eta\) is the learning rate and \(\lambda_{\max}\) is the largest Hessian eigenvalue. As model scale increases, \(\lambda_{\max}\) tends to grow, so reducing \(\eta\) decreases the per-step update \(\Delta \theta_t = -\eta\,\hat{g}_t\) and mitigates the risk of divergence or oscillation. In QLoRA, gradients are backpropagated through dequantized 4-bit weights, introducing additional sources of stochasticity and approximation error in \(\hat{g}_t\). A smaller \(\eta\) reduces how strongly these errors are amplified in each update, helping preserve the empirical parity with 16-bit finetuning observed in the paper.

Doubling the batch size, \(B \to 2B\), complements the reduced learning rate by lowering gradient noise via variance scaling \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat{g}) \propto \sigma^2/B\). With quantization (NF4 plus double quantization), dequantization and block-wise scales can introduce small, structured errors; increasing \(B\) averages these errors across more examples, yielding a more stable \(\hat{g}_t\). Together, halving \(\eta\) and doubling \(B\) reduce the effective “noise-induced” update magnitude: the expected update scales like \(\eta\,\mathbb{E}[\hat{g}]\) while its stochastic fluctuation scales like \(\eta\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\hat{g})} \sim \eta \sqrt{\sigma^2/B}\). Moving from \((\eta, B)\) to \((\eta/2, 2B)\) decreases this fluctuation by a factor of \(\sqrt{2}/2 < 1\), improving stability precisely where larger models and low-precision storage make training more sensitive. The paper’s training-setup results reflect this: most 7B hyperparameters generalize to 33B/65B except learning rate and batch size, which are adjusted to balance curvature-induced instability and quantization noise while retaining throughput with paged optimizers.
