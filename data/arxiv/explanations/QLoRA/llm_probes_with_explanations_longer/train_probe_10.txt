According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', when scoring paired responses, GPT-4 exhibited a systematic bias, specifically favoring the earlier response. This is directly supported by the authors’ automated evaluation protocol on the Vicuna benchmark, where GPT-4 was prompted to rate two system outputs presented together. They report “a significant ordering effect” with GPT-4 “increasing the score of the response occurring earlier in the prompt,” and explicitly recommend averaging over both presentation orders to control for this artifact. In other words, if two responses are shown as Response A (first) and Response B (second), the expected score satisfies \( \mathbb{E}[s_{\text{first}}] > \mathbb{E}[s_{\text{second}}] \) under otherwise identical conditions, indicating a position-dependent bias unrelated to response quality.

This bias is sensible given known sensitivity of LLMs to prompt structure and priming. When an evaluator model is asked to compare two texts embedded in a single prompt, the first text can serve as an anchor or reference, shaping the internal comparison and the subsequent justification, which manifests as a systematic advantage for the earlier position. The paper’s mitigation—averaging scores over both permutations and using pairwise Elo aggregation—aims to neutralize position effects by symmetrizing comparisons across many matches and seeds. Formally, if a true quality difference is \( \Delta = \mathbb{E}[q_A - q_B] \) but the position bias adds \( \delta > 0 \) to the first position, then a single-order estimate observes \( \Delta + \delta \) when \(A\) is first and \( \Delta - \delta \) when \(B\) is first; averaging the two orders recovers \( \Delta \), canceling the positional term.
