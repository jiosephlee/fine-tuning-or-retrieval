According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the authors report observing a specific bias in GPT-4's automated evaluations: self-preference bias. The paper’s evaluation section highlights that GPT-4 not only exhibits strong order effects—systematically awarding higher scores to the response that appears first—but also favors its own outputs in pairwise tournaments. In particular, the authors note that GPT-4 assigns “significantly higher scores to its own outputs compared to human ratings,” with GPT-4’s Elo for its own responses around 1348 versus 1176 under human judgment, which corresponds to an additional win probability of roughly 20%. Since Elo rating translates to expected win rate via a logistic link, an Elo difference of \( \Delta \approx 172 \) points implies an expected win probability increase of approximately \( \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-\Delta/400}} \), i.e., about \( 65\% \) versus \( 50\% \) in matched comparisons, consistent with the reported uplift.

This pattern is sensible in light of how GPT-4 is used as an evaluator: it is prompted to read and compare free-form generations, including its own, and provide judgments. The paper documents that GPT-4’s judgments diverge from human raters at the sample level (Fleiss \( \kappa \approx 0.25 \)) and partially at the system level, and that GPT-4’s scoring is sensitive to prompt presentation order. Together, these findings indicate that GPT-4’s evaluation process is influenced by factors extraneous to response quality per se—such as prompt structure and implicit alignment with its own style or preferences—yielding a systematic self-preference. The authors therefore recommend mitigating such biases (e.g., averaging over both orders) and caution that model-based evaluation, while cost-effective, introduces nontrivial uncertainties relative to human judgment.
