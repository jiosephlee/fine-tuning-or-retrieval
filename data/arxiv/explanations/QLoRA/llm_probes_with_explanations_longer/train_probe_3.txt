According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the implicit data-related condition that explains why finetuned models excel on some benchmarks but not others is distribution match. Throughout the study, the authors show that performance is driven by how closely the finetuning dataset’s task and response distribution aligns with the evaluation benchmark’s demands. For example, LLaMA models finetuned on FLAN v2 achieve strong 5-shot MMLU scores, while the same models perform poorly on the Vicuna chatbot benchmark; conversely, models finetuned on OASST1 (a multi-turn dialogue dataset) dominate chatbot evaluations but do not top MMLU. This pattern reflects a distributional alignment principle: if the training distribution \(P_{\text{train}}(x,y)\) matches the test distribution \(P_{\text{test}}(x,y)\) in task type, linguistic style, and response format, empirical risk minimization on \(P_{\text{train}}\) yields low expected risk on \(P_{\text{test}}\). When the supports differ—e.g., instruction-following dialogue versus multiple-choice knowledge questions—even large models and large datasets underperform relative to smaller, better-matched ones.

This intuition is corroborated by several explicit findings in the paper: “dataset suitability matters more than size,” small high-quality datasets can yield state-of-the-art results, and “strong MMLU performance does not imply strong Vicuna performance and vice versa.” Formally, generalization gaps grow with distribution mismatch, which can be characterized by divergences such as \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P_{\text{test}}\|P_{\text{train}}\right)\) or \( \mathcal{H}\!\Delta\!\mathcal{H} \)-divergence in domain adaptation. When \(P_{\text{train}}\approx P_{\text{test}}\), the expected error \( \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_{\text{test}}}\!\left[\ell(f_{\theta}(x),y)\right] \) is minimized by optimizing \( \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_{\text{train}}}\!\left[\ell(f_{\theta}(x),y)\right] \). The empirical evidence—FLAN v2 boosting MMLU but hurting Vicuna, and OASST1 boosting Vicuna/OA while being less aligned with MMLU—demonstrates that finetuned model success hinges on distribution match between finetuning data and the target benchmark.
