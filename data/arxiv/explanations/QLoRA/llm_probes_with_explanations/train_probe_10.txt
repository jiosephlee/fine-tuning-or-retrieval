According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', when scoring paired responses, GPT-4 exhibited a systematic bias, specifically favoring the earlier response. This arises from their automated evaluation setup in which GPT-4 is prompted with a query and two candidate answers to assign scores, where the authors observed a “significant ordering effect” such that the response appearing first received higher ratings; to mitigate this, they recommend averaging over both permutations of ordering, i.e., reporting the mean score across (response A first, response B second) and (response B first, response A second), effectively using an estimator like $\frac{1}{2}(s_{A\prec B} + s_{A\succ B})$, and they further corroborate positional bias by noting broader evaluation biases (e.g., GPT-4 also inflates scores for its own outputs) and only moderate agreement with humans, all consistent with LLM sensitivity to prompt structure and anchor effects in comparative judgments.
