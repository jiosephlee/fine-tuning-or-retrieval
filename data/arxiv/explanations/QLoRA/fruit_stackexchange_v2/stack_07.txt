### What exactly are the layers that receive LoRA adapters when you say "adapters at every network layer"?
Question:

- MLP: gate, up, and down <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> \(W_{gate}, W_{up}, W_{down}\))?
- <|endoftext|> and output (LM head) layers?

Also, do you use shared LoRA across heads or per-matrix, and are there any modules you explicitly exclude <|endoftext|> layer norms)?
Answer:
Short answer: In the paper <|endoftext|> “adapters at every network layer” means “add LoRA to every linear projection inside each Transformer block” — i.e., all attention and MLP weight matrices — not to layernorms, and not to layer-wise biases. Concretely for a LLaMA-style block this means you put LoRA on the attention query/key/value/output matrices and on the MLP <|endoftext|> (the gate / up / down dense layers). LoRA is applied per projection matrix (per linear weight), not per individual attention head, and the paper does not apply LoRA to LayerNorms, embedding/table lookups, or to the LM head as a special separate target.

Explanation and grounding in the paper
The paper reports that the standard “apply LoRA only to $W_q$ and $W_v$” recipe underperforms and that recovery of full 16-bit finetuning requires adding adapters more broadly. They state (Section “Memory Requirement of <|endoftext|> Finetuning” and the later ablations) that the critical hyperparameter is “how many LoRA adapters are used in total” and that “LoRA on all linear transformer block layers are required to match full finetuning performance.” In other words, every linear layer inside the transformer block that participates in the forward pass should get a LoRA adapter.

For a canonical <|endoftext|> transformer block that means:
- Self-attention: apply LoRA to $W_q$, $W_k$, $W_v$, and $W_o$ (the attention output projection).
- MLP: apply LoRA to the MLP dense/projection matrices (often termed the down/projection into the hidden MLP, the up/projection back to model dimension, and any gated linear unit projection(s) such as $W_{\text{gate}}$ / $W_{\text{up}}$ / $W_{\text{down}}$ used by the particular MLP form).
- Do not add LoRA to layer <|endoftext|> parameters (paper explicitly excludes non-linearities / layernorms) and the paper’s emphasis is on linear layers inside transformer blocks.
- <|endoftext|> and LM head: the paper’s statement focuses on “all linear transformer block layers.” They do not claim special treatment or mandatory LoRA on <|endoftext|> or the final LM head; the experiments and wording emphasize the block-local linear layers. In practice the <|endoftext|> codebase/adapters released applies LoRA to the linear layers in the blocks (attention + MLP). If you want to finetune <|endoftext|> or the head, you can add adapters there, but the paper’s emphasis is that covering the block linear layers was the key change versus the common $W_q$/$W_v$-only recipe.

Per-matrix vs per-head
The paper uses LoRA in the standard way: adapters are attached per weight matrix (i.e., per $W_q$, $W_k$, $W_v$, $W_o$, $W_{\text{mlp}}$, …) rather than creating separate adapter matrices per attention head. That is the typical and practical LoRA implementation used in the experiments: one <|endoftext|> factorization per projection matrix (shared across heads because attention <|endoftext|> are implemented as a single large matrix whose columns <|endoftext|> to all heads). The paper does not describe using distinct per-head LoRAs or tying LoRA modules across different projection matrices; they increase coverage by instantiating LoRA at each projection matrix independently.

Modules explicitly excluded
LayerNorms are not given LoRA adapters. The paper also focuses on adapters rather than updating the 4-bit base weights, so the base weights and biases are frozen; only the adapter parameters are learned (and <|endoftext|> state for adapters is what they manage with <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> In short: all linear layers in each transformer block get LoRA; layernorms, non-linearities and the frozen <|endoftext|> base weights do not.
