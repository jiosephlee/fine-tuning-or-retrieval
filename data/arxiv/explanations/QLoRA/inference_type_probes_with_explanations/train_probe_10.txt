According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', if you increase the blocksize used for storing weight scales to reduce per-parameter scale overhead without using Double Quantization, you are most likely to sacrifice quantization precision. The paper explains that block-wise quantization normalizes each block by its absolute maximum, i.e., for a block of size \(B\) with scale \(c=\frac{\alpha}{\operatorname{absmax}(\mathbf{X}_\text{block})}\), and that a small blocksize is “required for precise 4-bit quantization” because outliers otherwise force \(\operatorname{absmax}\) to be large, compressing most values into a narrow range and leaving many bins underutilized, which increases quantization error; while increasing \(B\) does cut the scale-memory overhead from \(\frac{32}{B}\) bits/parameter, it aggregates more values (and potential outliers) per scale, amplifying this loss of precision, whereas their Double Quantization reduces the overhead to \(\frac{8}{64}+\frac{32}{64\cdot 256}\) bits/parameter without enlarging \(B\) and thus preserves the higher quantization fidelity achieved with small blocks and NF4’s optimal binning.
