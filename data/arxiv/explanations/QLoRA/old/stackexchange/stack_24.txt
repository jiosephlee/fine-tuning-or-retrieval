### How does QLoRA handle outliers within a block despite using quantiles designed for normals?
Question:
Block-wise quantization mitigates outlier impact vs global scaling, but within a 64-sized block there can still be outliers. Since NF4 codebook is fixed based on a normal distribution, how do extreme block values get represented without saturating? Does the use of per-block scaling via $c_2$ largely resolve this, or are there failure cases where saturation is common?
Answer:
In NF4 we never use a *global* Min/Max but instead normalize every 64-element block by its own scale,  
$$
c_2 = \max_{i\in \text{block}} \lvert w_i\rvert
$$  
so that after dividing by $c_2$ every value lies in $[-1,1]$. Because our 4-bit codebook $q_0,\dots,q_{15}$ was precomputed as the equal-mass quantiles of a standard normal $N(0,1)$ (and then linearly squeezed into $[-1,1]$), this per-block rescaling guarantees that *no* weight ever lies outside the representable range: the single largest absolute weight in the block becomes exactly $\pm 1$ and is assigned to the two extreme bins $q_{15}$ or $q_{0}$. All other values—outliers relative to the rest of their block or not—are simply slotted into one of the remaining 14 bins according to their normalized magnitude.  

In effect, block-wise $c_2$ “shrinks” any extreme spikes so they don’t force a global saturation, and the NF4 quantiles then distribute codepoints *optimally* for a Gaussian-like spread within $[-1,1]$. The only failure mode is a pathological block where one weight is so much larger than the other 63 that after scaling they all collapse into a tiny sub-interval—then you do lose resolution among the “small” weights, since nearly all of them will quantize to the same few middle bins. In practice, however, pretrained LLM weight blocks of size 64 very rarely exhibit such extreme tail behavior, and the QLoRA paper’s empirical results (Section 3) show negligible saturation or information loss from NF4’s fixed codebook plus per-block scaling.
