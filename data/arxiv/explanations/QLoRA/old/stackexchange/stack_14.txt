### What is the memory breakdown that leads to >780 GB for 16-bit finetuning of LLaMA-65B?
Question:
The introduction states that regular 16-bit finetuning of LLaMA-65B requires over 780 GB of GPU memory. Can you provide a breakdown across parameters (2 bytes each), optimizer states (e.g., Adam’s $m$ and $v$, potentially 4 or 8 bytes each), gradients, activation checkpoints, and any model-parallel buffers? I’d like to reproduce the 780+ GB figure with clear assumptions about batch size and sequence length.
Answer:
In the QLoRA paper the authors quote a “>$780\ \mathrm{GB}$” figure for ordinary 16-bit fine‐tuning of LLaMA-65B, but don’t spell out the line‐by‐line accounting. Below is a back‐of‐the‐envelope that will let you reproduce that number (or see why it really lives just above 780 GB). The key assumptions are:

  • We shard the 65 B parameters evenly across 8 GPUs (i.e.\ an 8-way tensor‐parallel split).  
  • Everything—model weights, gradients, and optimizer states—is kept in (at most) 16- or 32-bit precision.  
  • We do gradient‐checkpointing for activations (so we don’t store all layer activations at once).  
  • Peak‐size model‐parallel communication buffers are a few GB per GPU.

— Per-GPU parameter split  
Total params: $65\times10^9$. With an 8-way split, each GPU holds  
  $$\frac{65\times10^9}{8}\approx8.125\times10^9\text{ parameters.}$$

— Weight storage (FP16)  
$$2\text{ bytes}\times8.125\times10^9\approx16.25\ \mathrm{GB}$$

— Gradients (same precision as weights)  
$$2\text{ bytes}\times8.125\times10^9\approx16.25\ \mathrm{GB}$$

— Adam optimizer states  
Adam keeps two moment buffers, $m$ and $v$. If we store them in FP32 (4 bytes each):  
  $$
    m:\ 4\text{ bytes}\times8.125\times10^9\approx32.5\ \mathrm{GB}\\
    v:\ 4\text{ bytes}\times8.125\times10^9\approx32.5\ \mathrm{GB}
  $$  
→ total optimizer states per GPU $\approx65\ \mathrm{GB}$

Summing those four major terms gives  
$$16.25+16.25+65=97.5\ \mathrm{GB\ per\ GPU}$$  
Across 8 GPUs that is  
$$97.5\ \mathrm{GB}\times8=780\ \mathrm{GB}$$

— Activations with checkpointing  
A rough upper bound: one full pass of checkpointed activations per layer costs on the order of  
$$\text{batch\_size\_per\_GPU}\times\text{sequence\_length}\times\text{hidden\_size}\times2\text{ bytes}.$$  
For LLaMA-65B: $\text{hidden\_size}\approx8192,\ \text{seq\_len}=2048,\ \text{batch}=1\ \to\approx0.25\ \mathrm{GB/GPU}.$  
Even if you carve that up into a handful of checkpoints you stay well under $1\ \mathrm{GB/GPU}$, say $\sim0.3\ \mathrm{GB}$.

— Model-parallel buffers  
You need send/receive buffers during tensor‐parallel matmuls—let’s budget $\sim2\ \mathrm{GB/GPU}$.

Putting it all together, per GPU:  
$$\text{weights }16.3 + \text{grads }16.3 + \text{Adam (m+v) }65 + \text{activations }0.3 + \text{comm bufs }2 \approx100\ \mathrm{GB/GPU}.$$

Across 8 GPUs that is  
$$\approx800\ \mathrm{GB\ total,} \text{ i.e. }“>780\ \mathrm{GB}.”$$

If you collapse the smaller activation and buffer terms into a single “miscellaneous” line, you get:

  • Parameters (2 B each): $65\times10^9\times2\text{ B}=130\ \mathrm{GB}$  
  • Gradients (2 B each): $65\times10^9\times2\text{ B}=130\ \mathrm{GB}$  
  • Adam $m$ (4 B each):    $65\times10^9\times4\text{ B}=260\ \mathrm{GB}$  
  • Adam $v$ (4 B each):    $65\times10^9\times4\text{ B}=260\ \mathrm{GB}$  
  • Activations + comm:      $\approx8\ \mathrm{GB}$  
  → total $\approx130+130+260+260+8=788\ \mathrm{GB}$  

which rounds to the “>$780\ \mathrm{GB}$” claim.

Intuitively, you see why 16-bit (or mixed‐precision) fine‐tuning of a 65 B-param model is so hungry: you’re keeping two big moment-buffers ($m$ and $v$) at full 32-bit precision, plus a second copy of every weight’s gradient, and you shard only across a handful of GPUs. QLoRA’s core innovation is to collapse almost all of that by (a) quantizing the frozen base model to 4 bits (so those 65 B params now take only 32 GB!), (b) never storing frozen‐model gradients, and (c) relegating optimizer states to pageable CPU memory—dropping the peak from $\sim800\ \mathrm{GB}$ to under $48\ \mathrm{GB}$.
