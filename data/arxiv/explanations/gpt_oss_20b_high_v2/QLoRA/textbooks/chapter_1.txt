# Scale‑Driven GPU Wall  
Large language models today span tens of billions of parameters and their naively‑finited networks entail enormous amounts of intermediate activation data during training. A straightforward example is stated early in our source manuscript: *“regular \(16\)-bit finetuning of a LLaMA 65 B parameter model requires more than \(780\,{\rm GB}\) of GPU memory”* [Abstract]. This wall does not merely reflect the sheer count of weighted connections—instead it arises from the computation graph where every weight update requires storing gradients over every layer’s activations while performing backward checks for gradient accumulation and normalization routines such as AdamW update statistics.  

Fortunately, many well‑known large‑model architectures already employ **block‑wise \(k\)-bit quantisation** when deploying pretrained checkpoints at inference time [Background]. This technique rescales individual blocks \(i=1,\dots,n\) of weight matrices \(W_i^{({\rm FP32})}\in{\mathbb R}^{r_{\rm o}\times r_{\rm i}}\) into an integer range:
\[
W^{({\rm Int8})}_i \;\!=\;\!\operatorname{round}\!\bigl(c_i^{({\rm FP32})} · W_i^{({\rm FP32})}\bigr), 
\bbox{\(\displaystyle c_i^{({\rm FP32})}
=\frac{{127}}{{{\rm absmax}(W_i^{({\rm FP32}))}}\;}
.\]
The reciprocal operation,
converting back into floating point establishes a round–trip
and preserves higher resolution numerical values during inference.
When these units are frozen and retained at integer precision during thousands–of‑token sequences,
the number of floating point operations shrinks dramatically while staying amortised over very few parameter reads per forward pass.  

This translation bridges two opposing worlds:
intuitively small primary data store followed by dequantising dynamically when fed into dense layers,
allowing researchers who possess only consumer GPUs (\(48\,{\rm GB}\)) 
to explore far larger models without purchasing expensive multi‑node clusters.
The ensuing term “scale‑driven GPU wall” encapsulates how splitting out two parts—weight storage versus active activation computations—removes an otherwise impossible barrier while preserving sufficient representational breadth downscaled into \(k=4,\!8,\dotsb\nobreak^\textit{k bit}\).

# Shortcomings Of Float-\(16\) Full‑Finetune  
Naïve adaptation routines update all pretrained parameters regardlessly.
Even low‑rank adapters (LoRAs), which expand existing linear projections via rank‐restricted bi‐linear corrections,
invest detectable additional overhead.
The stark inequality exposed in the manuscript reveals that,
although core LoRa parameter sizes remain modest (~\(26 {\rm MB}\)),
the *gradient* tensors captured during forward–backward passes explode:
without compression techniques such as **gradient checkpointing**,
the same hidden states would compel roughly \(567 {\rm MB}\) per batch slice—exceedingly larger than generator statistics alone.\citep[][empirical WB11]{lf}

Therefore practitioners typically adopt **per‑block floating point scalars**, multiplying de−quantized weight matrices on-the-fly.
Nevertheless this schema introduces its own nuance:
although weights retain their bulk statistical support,
the exact value retained is nonetheless truncated;
derivatives flowing through \(W^{({\rm BF16})}=c^{-1}∘W^{({k {\rm (-bit)}})}(∝⟨∘⟩)\)
rarely propagate partially approximated values correctly,
and *full fine-tunning* fails catastrophically beyond roughly eleven
billions without physical farm support.\citep {fic04}

Adding further context,
fine-tuned models considered five tiers wide—RoBERTa-large (~\(3550 {\rm M}\)),
T5 variants (~\(770 {\rm M}\)),
and fluent GPT‑like transformer families (~\(65 {\rm B}\))
exhibit exponential growth both in bit‑precision count (`\(1800 × 70=126k` million words)
and interface specification (`\(384\,{\rm tokens/batch}`).
Even modest loops over \(18 batch‐size×256 token`\)
are heavyweight without sequence stretches exceeding a thousand tokens—
precisely where posed bottlenecks sprout.
Hence standard floor-level training scripts become unfeasible
within budget-subcritical studios or even sole-officers-friendly labs:\emph{
Normal high-quality teacher networks running full adaptation simply exceed capacity}.  

Finally note how critical tuning hyperparameters become:
figure duplication evidence argues strongly
that naive defaults ‘apply LoRAs only on query/value projection-only’ do **not** recapture three-timeably matching baseline scores even at moderate scales,
as balancing effort variable becomes enormous around ``$\sim 10^−6$–$5×10^{-5}$’ indices.\citep {det05}
In short—full-fine tuning pushes calculation onto hardware incompatible hardware without unique reconstruction tricks.
