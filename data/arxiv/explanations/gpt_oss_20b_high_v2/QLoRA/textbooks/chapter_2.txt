Chapter 2: # Foundational Concepts

Large language models (LLMs) possess billions of floating‑point parameters trained primarily on massive corpora.
To leverage these models on downstream tasks while keeping computational resources tractable,
researchers have devised two intertwined ideas that underpin QLoRA:
(1) **blockwise k‑bit quantisation**, a compact way of storing parameter tensors;
and (2) **low‑rank adapters (LoRa)**, a parameter‑efficient strategy that replaces costly dense updates.
This chapter expounds these concepts—how they work individually and why together enable
high‑fidelity finetuning even when all trainable weights are held at merely four bits.



## Blockwise k‑Bit Scaling Constants

Quantisation transforms a high‑precision tensor—typically stored in 32‑bit floating point (FP32)—into a lower‑bit representation.
For an integer scheme one usually rescales the tensor before rounding;
the scaling factor \(c^\text{FP32}\) is chosen such that every element maps cleanly into
the target integer range.
For a simple whole‑tensor rescaling into an 8‑bit signed integer one would write  

\[
\mathbf X^{\text{Int8}}
   =\operatorname{round}
     \!\left(
          \frac{\tiny127}
               {\operatorname{absmax}
                    (\mathbf X^{\text{FP32}})} 
          \,\mathbf X^{\text{FP32}}
     \right)
   ,
\tag{1}
\]
where  
\( c^\text{FP32}=127/\!\operatorname{absmax}(\mathbf X^{\text{FP32}})\).
Dequantisation simply inverts this operation,
\(x=\mathbf x^{\text{Int8}}/c^\text{FP32}\).

Instrumental memories tempt this straightforward approach:
if an isolated random entry has a large magnitude,
all integer bins become densely populated except those near zero,
resulting in severely uneven utilisation of representational capacity.
To mitigate this problem one **splits** each tensor horizontally into many contiguous **blocks**,
each block being approximately independent in norm.
Given a flattened tensor \( \mathbf X \in \mathbb R^{b\times h}\),
we form  
\( n=\frac {b\times h}{B}\) blocks each comprising \(B\) elements;
each block receives its own scale constant \(c_i^\text{FP32}\).
The routine above is applied per block,
and dequantisation recovers every chunk via its dedicated
scale factor before feeding it into the model.
This procedure is called **blockwise k‑bit quantisation**:
the “\(k-\)” comes from collapsing values into \(k=4,5,\dots,\) bits per element;
the “blocksize” controls how finely local statistics are preserved,
often chosen between 16–256 elements for LLMs.



### Double Quantisation

An important subtlety arises because every block requires its own scale value,
typically stored once per block.
When blocks are small—necessary for good precision—the aggregate overhead
from these scalars can eclipse the savings gained by reducing weight bit depth.
QLoRA addresses this via **double quantisation**:
scales themselves become tensors subjected to a secondary roundtrip
to low precision.
In practice one first obtains linear per‑block scales \(c_2^\text{FP32}\)
(which are positive).
These scales are mean–centred so they become symmetric around zero,
allowing efficient reduction;
they are then discretised into 8‑bit floating point,
producing \(c_2^\text{k-bits}\), typically with blocksize 256.
A second average scalar \(c_1^\text{FP32}\) records the remnant drift.
During inference/reference computations one performs two successive 
dequantisations,

\( c^\textBF16=\!{\rm dequant}( {\rm dequant}( c_1^{\!{\rm FP32}}, 
    c_2^{\!{\rm k{-bit}}}),   W^{\,{\rm k{-bit}}}) , 
      W^{\,{\rm BF16}}\),

exactly following Eq.~(5) below.
The net effect is a reduction from roughly half a bit per parameter down
to approximately one eighth bit—a vast saving without affecting task quality.



## Gaussian Weight Statistics

All modern neural‐network training pipelines initialise weights from a zero‑centred normal distribution
and preserve this property throughout epochs—
a phenomenon supported empirically throughout large language models 
(LLaMA, OPT, BLOOM, Pythia,...).

Mathematically setting  
\( W_{\alpha}\sim {\cal N}(0,\sigma^2)\allowbreak ,  
σ>0,\)  
the density falls off symmetrically around zero.
Quantisation strategies quantile‑based methods aim at giving every bin
an equal probability mass (“information theoretically optimal”),
accumulating exemplars into almost identical count per bin.
The standard “slice” exploitation begins by estimating all  
\(q_i=\frac12(Q(i/(2^k+1))+Q((i+1)/(2^k+1)))f=0,\quad i=0,\dots ,2^k-1,\)
where \(Q(\cdot)\) denotes the standard normal cumulative distribution function,
to arrive at **k‑bit NormalFloat (NF_k)** centroids spread over (-∞,+∞).
As usual generic int‑based representation does not feature an 
exact discrete zero point;
however NLP inference frequently collides padding zeros,
compression sparsity exploits and security hardening all require exact zeros.
TF–style solutions therefore enforce asymmetry:
two collections—\(2^{\,k-1}\) negative centroids 
and \(2^{\,k-1}+1\) positive centroids—are merged together while discarding one duplicate zero,
yielding an **asymmetric NF_k** datatype featuring exactly  
one zero bin while retaining statistical optimality.

## Parameter-Efficient Adapters

Fine‐tuning LLMs usually demands stochastically updating millions—or billions—of parameters,
which host large incremental gradients during back‑propagation.
The introduction of **Low Rank Adapters** changes this paradigm dramatically:
rather than adjusting each transformation matrix \(W∈ℝ^{h×o}\),
one appends two lightweight projection matrices  
\(L_1∈ℝ^{h×r},\,L_2∈ℝ^{r×o}\), keeping \(W₀=W∈ℝ^{h×o}\)
frozen during training.
Any input vector \(x∈ℝ^h\) thus sees both branches simultaneously:

‖⟨⟨      
\( y = xW₀ + s\, xL_1L_2 ,        s∈ℝ.\)

Herethe adaptative term adds control freedom without touching original knowledge;
gradients flow through both terms:
a candidate (-attached pathways ) returns parameter updates solely applied at Li’s
but never changing components of W₀ itself.
Consequently total learnable parameters can shrink from billions down
to merely dozens-of-million items (\(~0.2 %\)),
while activation gradients dominate memory consumption—
making it plausible even when GPU budgets are modest.



### Why LoRa Remains Powerful After Quantising If We Freeze W₀？

A naive expectation would say “if W₀ becomes powerless after compression”
perhaps all downstream advancement disappears.
QLoRA’s insight shows otherwise:
by storing W₀ in four consecutive quantum blocks (**NF₄**),
one computes forward activations through exact dequantised versions  
(we retrieve **W_BF16**) whilst writing lightweight LoRa components live during inference/fine-tune occasions simultaneously–
the overall forward pass remains mathematically unchanged compared with an uncompressed net—
and acceptance error originates entirely from tolerable quantum noise within reduce magnitude ranges –
that noise propagates backward exactly along LoRa branches;
because grad ∂E/∂L depends solely on *inputs* x already computed during forward pass,
any minor deviation caused by coarse representation rarely corrupts adaptation signal.
