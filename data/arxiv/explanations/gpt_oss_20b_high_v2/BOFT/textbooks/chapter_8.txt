\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

Chapter 8: # Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning Methodology (BOFT)

Large‑scale foundation models possess enormous expressive power that is difficult—or infeasible—to exploit directly when data resources voor downstream tasks are scarce. Parameter‑efficient finetuning provides a principled way to harness pre‑training knowledge while adjusting only a modest number of weights. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) preserves pairwise neuron angles through an orthogonal transformation applied uniformly across an entire layer,
yet its naïve instantiation demands \(O(d^2)\) trainable parameters when operating on \(d\)-dimensional representations.
BOFT replaces the dense mono‑block orthogonal matrix
with a carefully crafted product of sparse orthogonal blocks—an *orthogonal butterfly factorisation*—that reproduces a dense linear operator while keeping parameter count down essentially logarithmic in \(d\).
In what follows we unpack how BOFT solves this challenge through three interconnected ideas:
the mixed‑matrix forward‑pass formalism;
the skew‑symmetric parametrisation via Cayley transforms;
and an adaptive multiplicative dropout routine.



## mixed‑matrix forward–pass formulation

Rather than modify every individual weight entry,
BOFT injects a global linear map \(R(m,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) into every slice
of a pretrained module \(W^0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\).
Formally,
each transformed activation reads
\[
z=\big(R(m,b)\,\!W^{0}\big)^{\!\top}\!x,\qquad
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~~z,\!y_{i}\!\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
\]
which expands to an explicit series
\(R(m,b)=B_{m}\dotsm B_{1}\)
where each factor \(B_{i}\) contains very few non‑zero entries,
yet whose product remains invertible—and most importantly,
orthonormal.
By inserting a *mixed* chain instead of one bulk matrix,
the forward operation preserves computational speed:
each interaction between \(x\), the pretrained weight \(W^0\),
and one butterfly component operates in linear time \(O(d)\).
Warm restarts remain trivial:
at initialization every block equals identity,
so that immediately before training starts \(R(m,b)=I_d\)
and consequently \(z=W^{0\,\top}x\).

A key intuition behind Equation (1) comes from band‑limited signal processing.
Just as rhythms propagate globally through successive stages in Fast Fourier Transforms,
a cascade of small mixing units suffices to let every input coordinate influence
every output coordinate ultimately.
Graphically this transaction looks like boats traveling along prescribed routes
in an interstitial grid;
the sequence of routes captures all admissible paths,
hence every source node eventually reaches every sink node
while private communications remain restrained thanks to sparsity.



## skew‑symmetric parameterisation via Cayley transforms

Guaranteeing each component \(B_i\) remains strictly orthonormal places strong constraints on its trainable entries.
An elegant way circumvents directly optimising over Stiefel manifold
is through the *Cayley transform*
which maps any skew‑symmetric matrix \(Q=-Q^{T}\)
into an orthonormal map:
\[
B_i=(I + Q_i)(I-Q_i)^{-1}.
\]
Hence optimisation runs over entries constituting \(Q_i\),
whose dimensionality equals half­–of–the Pairs’ count
and automatically obeys unitary norms upon convergence.
For elementary “butterfly factors’’ defined later
each \(Q_i\) squeezes down
to merely cosine–sine angular parameters—
basically tiny rotations—making training both cheaper and more numerically stable compared
with naive gradient descent over orthonormal axioms.



### Why skew‑symmetric works well inside butterflies

The bottleneck lies in constructing sparse yet expressive Or­th­ogonal maps.
Every butterfly component enjoys zero entries arranged in rigid blocks mirroring power‑of‑two scales.
This structure lends itself naturally to represent rotations between disjoint subsets:
each diagonal block encodes pairwise mixing among pairings,
mediated mathematically through Hankel–type relations derived from trigonometric identities.
Consequently imposing Cayley‐parametrised rotations gives us faithful
orthonormal sub‐operators without resorting  
to hard projections back onto Stiefel space;
compactness results automatically convey rotational degrees mercy.



## multiplicative dropout reciprocity (trick adaptive)

Traditional additive regularisers—for instance dropping individual weight scalars—
do not mesh seamlessly with BOFT’s multiplicative update scheme:
one perturbation outside identity inevitably cascades through subsequent products,
potentially cropping useful pathways unduly early.
Multiplicative Dropout therefore intervenes directly on *factor level*:  
with probability \(p_1\%\) one chooses whole butterfly components at random  
and replaces them temporarily by identities;
within those selected components a secondary probability \(p_2\bigr>\!\!(%) \)
causes individual diagonal blocks themselves swap out yielding local stasis.
This two‐tier mechanic does two things simultaneously:
it sporadically decouples long chains enough so gradients average out desirable perturbations,
while preserving momentum along remaining routes,
retaining tractable mixing properties structurally imposed by butterflies.



### Practical adaptivity rules

* **Cold start:** initialise every blocker please zero entries inside their corresponding skew matrices  
so indices begin identically neutral (\(B_i=I_d)\).
* **Warm up regime:** set prod optimistic dropout rates say \(p_1=p_2=5\,%\).
* **Schedule adjustment:** gradually decrease both masks during training
so early epochs reap broad exploration  
until later epochs lock onto sharp learned linear combination structures,
ensuring stable convergence while curbing overfitting.

# Summary

Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning departs from classic OFT primarily through three intertwined layers:

1. The **mixed‐matrix cascade** breaks down a single dense transformation into several sparse multiplications mirroring signal propagation trees.
2. Every piece draws energy from **skew‐symmetric parametrisation**, ensuring pristine orthonormality without costly manifold projections.
3. **Multiplicative Dropout** furnishes disciplined stochastic regularisation tailored specifically for serial products rather than infinitesimal weight adjustments.

Collectively these ingredients squash trainable budgets graphically modeled within LOG(movement/communication capacity)
from quadratic (\(O(d^2)\)) magnitude captured directly within OFT’s dense blocks toward logarithmic (\(O(d\,log d)\)) footprints maintained conducive inference latency—a balance crucial when wrestting cutting‑edge foundation models towards specialised downstream applications.
