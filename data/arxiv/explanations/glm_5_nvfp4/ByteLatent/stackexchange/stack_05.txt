\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Why are query/key roles reversed between encoder and decoder cross-attention?
Question:
In the BLT architecture, the local encoder uses cross-attention where patch representations are queries and byte representations are key/values. But in the local decoder, the roles are reversed: byte representations become queries and patch representations become key/values.

Why is this reversal necessary? Intuitively, what is each cross-attention trying to accomplish, and why does the direction of attention matter?
Answer:
The reversal of query/key roles between encoder and decoder cross-attention reflects the fundamental information flow direction in each component of the BLT architecture.

**In the Local Encoder**, the goal is to **compress** byte-level information into patch representations. Patches are queries because they actively "pull" information from the byte representations to construct themselves. Each patch $p_j$ needs to aggregate information from its constituent bytes, so it makes sense for the patch to query the bytes. This is essentially a learnable pooling operation—going from many bytes to fewer patches—where each patch representation is built by attending to the bytes within it.

**In the Local Decoder**, the goal reverses: to **expand** patch representations back into byte predictions. Here, each byte position needs information from the patch representation to make its prediction. Bytes are queries because each byte is being generated and needs to "pull" relevant information from the patch representation. This is an expansion operation—going from fewer patches to more bytes.

The key insight is that **queries represent what you're computing**, while **keys/values represent what you're attending to**:
- Encoder: computing patches → patches are queries, bytes are keys/values
- Decoder: computing bytes → bytes are queries, patches are keys/values

This mirrors the Perceiver architecture mentioned in the paper, where latent representations attend to inputs during encoding. The decoder naturally inverts this relationship since it's generating in the opposite direction. The masking is also different: encoder cross-attention restricts each patch query to attend only to bytes within that patch, while decoder cross-attention allows byte queries to attend to relevant patch representations (typically the current patch and previous ones due to the autoregressive structure).

This design ensures information flows appropriately: encoder aggregates (many-to-few), decoder distributes (few-to-many).
