\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

Chapter 8: # Performance Results: Matching Full-Precision Models

# Perplexity Comparison Across Model Sizes

Perplexity serves as one of the most fundamental metrics for evaluating language model performance, providing a direct measure of how well a model predicts the next token in a sequence. In the experimental evaluation of BitNet b1.58, the researchers conducted a comprehensive perplexity comparison against a reproduced FP16 LLaMA LLM across various model sizes. To ensure a fair and rigorous comparison, both architectures were pre-trained on the RedPajama dataset for 100 billion tokens, controlling for training data and computational budget. The validation perplexity was reported on two standard benchmarks: WikiText2 and C4 datasets, both of which are widely recognized in the language modeling community.

The experimental results reveal a fascinating pattern in how the 1.58-bit quantization affects model performance at different scales. At smaller model sizes, the ternary weight constraint introduces some performance degradation compared to the full-precision baseline. However, as the model size increases, this gap systematically diminishes. The key finding is that BitNet b1.58 begins to match the perplexity of full-precision LLaMA LLM starting at the 3 billion parameter scale. This threshold represents a critical inflection point where the benefits of model scale begin to compensate for the information loss inherent in quantization.

The relationship between model size and perplexity demonstrates that the ternary quantization approach does not merely approximate full-precision performance but can achieve genuine parity when sufficient model capacity is available. This observation has profound implications for the field: it suggests that the conventional wisdom about quantization—that it inevitably degrades performance—may need revision when considering extreme quantization schemes applied to sufficiently large models. The results challenge the assumption that high-precision weights are essential for competitive language modeling performance.

# The 3B Threshold: Matching FP16 Performance

The emergence of the 3 billion parameter threshold as the point where BitNet b1.58 matches full-precision performance represents one of the most significant findings in this research. At this scale, the ternary weight model achieves perplexity scores that are statistically indistinguishable from its FP16 counterpart, despite using weights that can only take on values in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. This equivalence holds when both models are trained with identical configurations, including model size and training tokens.

The existence of such a threshold carries important theoretical implications. It suggests that large neural networks possess significant redundancy in their weight representations, and that much of the information encoded in 16-bit floating-point values can be effectively captured by ternary weights when the model has sufficient parameters. The 3B threshold indicates that there is a crossover point where the representational capacity lost through quantization can be recovered through increased model width and depth.

Beyond the perplexity matching at 3B, the efficiency gains at this threshold are substantial. BitNet b1.58 at the 3B scale demonstrates inference speeds that are 2.71 times faster than the equivalent FP16 LLaMA model while consuming 3.55 times less GPU memory. These improvements stem directly from the fundamental change in computational primitives: rather than performing energy-intensive floating-point multiplications, the ternary weight matrix multiplication reduces to integer additions, which are dramatically more efficient on modern hardware. The 3B threshold thus marks not just a performance equivalence point but also the beginning of a new efficiency regime.

# BitNet b1.58 3.9B vs LLaMA 3B: Better Performance with Lower Cost

A particularly illuminating comparison emerges when examining the 3.9B parameter variant of BitNet b1.58 against the 3B parameter LLaMA LLM. This comparison demonstrates that BitNet b1.58 achieves a Pareto improvement over existing approaches, offering simultaneously better performance and lower cost. The 3.9B BitNet b1.58 model outperforms the 3B LLaMA LLM in terms of perplexity while maintaining significant advantages in computational efficiency.

The efficiency metrics are striking: the 3.9B BitNet b1.58 operates 2.4 times faster than the 3B LLaMA baseline while consuming 3.32 times less memory. These improvements occur despite the larger parameter count of the BitNet model. This counterintuitive result—that a larger model can be faster and more memory-efficient than a smaller one—stems from the fundamental differences in how information is encoded and processed. The ternary weights of BitNet b1.58 require far less storage bandwidth and computational resources than the 16-bit floating-point weights of traditional models.

The implications of this Pareto improvement extend beyond simple efficiency comparisons. It demonstrates that the 1.58-bit approach does not require trading performance for efficiency but can simultaneously advance both objectives. For practitioners, this means that deploying BitNet b1.58 can yield better results at lower cost, fundamentally changing the economics of language model deployment. The finding that a 3.9B ternary model can outperform a 3B full-precision model suggests that the efficient scaling frontier for language models may be substantially different from what has been assumed under the dominant 16-bit paradigm.

# Zero-Shot Task Accuracy Analysis

Beyond perplexity, the evaluation of BitNet b1.58 encompasses a comprehensive suite of zero-shot language understanding tasks. The researchers evaluated performance on seven distinct benchmarks: ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge for scientific reasoning, Hellaswag for commonsense reasoning, Winogrande for coreference resolution, PIQA for physical commonsense reasoning, OpenbookQA for open-book question answering, and BoolQ for boolean question answering. These tasks span a diverse range of linguistic capabilities, providing a robust assessment of model quality.

The evaluation methodology followed the established lm-evaluation-harness pipeline, ensuring consistency with prior work and reproducibility of results. Zero-shot evaluation is particularly important for assessing how well a model has acquired generalizable knowledge during pre-training, as it requires the model to perform tasks without any task-specific fine-tuning or few-shot examples. The breadth of the benchmark suite ensures that any conclusions drawn are not artifacts of a particular task format or domain.

The zero-shot accuracy results complement the perplexity findings, confirming that the performance matching at the 3B scale extends beyond language modeling metrics to actual downstream task performance. This alignment between perplexity and task accuracy is crucial: it demonstrates that the perplexity improvements are not merely statistical artifacts but translate into meaningful gains in the model's ability to understand and reason about language. The comprehensive task suite provides strong evidence that BitNet b1.58 achieves genuine performance parity with full-precision models rather than superficial metric matching.

# Performance Gap Analysis: How the Gap Narrows with Scale

The relationship between model scale and the performance gap between BitNet b1.58 and FP16 baselines reveals a systematic pattern with important theoretical implications. The experimental results demonstrate that the performance gap between ternary and full-precision models narrows consistently as model size increases. At smaller scales, the information bottleneck introduced by ternary quantization creates a measurable performance deficit. However, this deficit shrinks progressively and eventually vanishes at the 3B threshold.

This narrowing gap phenomenon aligns with emerging understanding of how neural networks utilize their parameters. As models scale, they develop increasing redundancy and overparameterization. The ternary quantization effectively constrains this redundancy, forcing the network to encode information more efficiently. At sufficient scale, the model has enough capacity to maintain competitive performance even under severe weight precision constraints. The pattern suggests that larger models are inherently more robust to quantization, a property that has profound implications for the future of model compression and efficient deployment.

The scaling behavior also points toward a new scaling law for 1-bit LLMs. The results indicate that continuing to scale BitNet b1.58 beyond 3B parameters could potentially yield models that not only match but exceed full-precision performance at equivalent computational cost. The efficiency advantages of ternary weights compound at larger scales, as the proportion of parameters devoted to linear layers increases while the relative cost of full-precision embeddings decreases. The scaling law defined by BitNet b1.58 opens new possibilities for training next-generation LLMs that maintain high performance while dramatically reducing deployment costs.

# 2T Token Training Results: Comparison with StableLM-3B

To assess the scalability of BitNet b1.58 with respect to training data, the researchers conducted experiments training on 2 trillion tokens, following the data recipe of StableLM-3B, which represents the state-of-the-art open-source 3B parameter model. This experimental design tests whether the benefits of ternary quantization persist when models are trained on substantially more data, a crucial question for understanding the long-term potential of 1-bit LLM architectures.

The evaluation benchmark for the 2T token experiments consisted of five tasks: Winogrande for coreference resolution, PIQA for physical commonsense reasoning, SciQ for scientific question answering, LAMBADA for long-range language modeling, and ARC-Easy for scientific reasoning. For tasks measured with both accuracy and normalized accuracy, the researchers reported the average of the two metrics. The StableLM-3B results at 2T tokens were taken directly from its published technical report, ensuring a fair comparison against established baselines.

The results demonstrate that BitNet b1.58 achieves superior performance on all evaluated end tasks compared to StableLM-3B. This finding is particularly significant because it shows that the advantages of the 1.58-bit approach persist even under extensive training regimes. The strong generalization capabilities exhibited by BitNet b1.58 indicate that ternary quantization does not compromise the model's ability to continue learning and improving with additional training data. The 2T token results provide compelling evidence that 1.58-bit LLMs represent not a compromise between efficiency and performance, but rather a genuine advancement that can deliver better results at lower cost across diverse training and evaluation conditions.
