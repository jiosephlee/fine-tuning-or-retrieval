\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### In re-scaled OFT, why is the diagonal matrix multiplied on the right?
Question:
In Section 3.5, the paper introduces re-scaled OFT with the forward pass $\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top\bm{x}$ where $\bm{D}$ is a learnable diagonal matrix for scaling each neuron.

I notice there's an errata note saying the original paper had a typo with $\bm{D}$ on the left. But I'm confused about why the correct placement is $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D}$ rather than $\bm{D}\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$. Since $\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$ where each column is a neuron, shouldn't scaling each neuron involve multiplying from the left? What does multiplying $\bm{D}$ from the right actually do to the weight matrix?
Answer:
The placement of $\bm{D}$ on the right is indeed correct, and the confusion stems from how matrix multiplication interacts with column-wise operations.

Let's think through this carefully. In the paper's notation, $\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$ is organized such that **each column represents a neuron**. That is, if we write $\bm{W}^0 = [\bm{w}_1^0, \bm{w}_2^0, \ldots, \bm{w}_n^0]$, then $\bm{w}_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the $i$-th neuron.

Now, here's the key insight about diagonal matrix multiplication: when you multiply a matrix by a diagonal matrix **on the right**, each **column** gets scaled by the corresponding diagonal element. Specifically, with $\bm{D} = \text{diag}(s_1, \ldots, s_n)$:

$$\bm{W}^0\bm{D} = [s_1\bm{w}_1^0, s_2\bm{w}_2^0, \ldots, s_n\bm{w}_n^0]$$

Each neuron (column) $\bm{w}_i^0$ is independently scaled by its own coefficient $s_i$.

Conversely, if you multiplied by a diagonal matrix **on the left**, you would scale each **row** instead of each column. This would not correspond to scaling individual neurons—it would mix together components across all neurons.

The errata note corrects a typo where $\bm{D}\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ was written. That formula would be problematic for two reasons: (1) the dimensional inconsistency if $\bm{D}$ were meant to be $n \times n$, and (2) even if $\bm{D}$ were $d \times d$ to make dimensions work, it would scale rows rather than neurons, losing the intended semantics.

To summarize the complete re-scaled OFT forward pass $\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top\bm{x}$: first each neuron $\bm{w}_i^0$ is scaled by $s_i$ to give $s_i\bm{w}_i^0$, then all neurons are jointly rotated by the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$, and finally the dot products with input $\bm{x}$ are computed. The diagonal scaling preserves the hyperspherical energy since normalization removes magnitude anyway—this is precisely why the paper notes that "re-scaling neurons does not change the hyperspherical energy."
