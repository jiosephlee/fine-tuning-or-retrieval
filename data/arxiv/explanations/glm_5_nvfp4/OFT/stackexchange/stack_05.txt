\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### What is the relationship between OFT and LoRA?
Question:
The OFT paper compares itself to LoRA throughout, and mentions that 'OFT and LoRA represent two distinct ways to be parameter-efficient.' I understand that LoRA uses a low-rank additive update $\bm{W} = \bm{W}^0 + \bm{B}\bm{A}$ while OFT uses a multiplicative orthogonal transformation $\bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$.

However, the paper also says 'Since $\bm{W}^0$ is typically full-rank, OFT also performs low-rank weight update when $\bm{R}-\bm{I}$ is low-rank.' I find this confusing. If OFT applies a full-rank orthogonal transformation, how can the update be low-rank? What exactly is the connection between these two approaches?
Answer:
The relationship between OFT and LoRA centers on how both methods achieve parameter-efficient updates, but through fundamentally different structural constraints. Let me clarify the apparent contradiction you've identified.

## The Key Distinction: $\bm{R}$ vs. $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$

The confusion arises from mixing up two different matrices. In OFT, the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ itself is **always full-rank**—this is guaranteed by orthogonality, since $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R} = \bm{I}$ implies $\bm{R}$ is invertible.

However, the paper's statement refers to $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$, which is entirely different. To see the connection to LoRA, we can rewrite OFT's forward pass:

$$\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x} = (\bm{W}^0 + (\bm{R} - \bm{I})\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$$

This reveals that OFT's effective weight update is $(\bm{R} - \bm{I})\bm{W}^0$. When $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$ is low-rank and $\bm{W}^0$ is full-rank, the entire update becomes low-rank because $\text{rank}(\bm{A}\bm{B}) \leq \min(\text{rank}(\bm{A}), \text{rank}(\bm{B}))$.

## Why Would $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$ Be Low-Rank?

This occurs due to OFT's block-diagonal parameterization. When $\bm{R}$ is structured as:

$$\bm{R} = \text{diag}(\bm{R}_1, \bm{R}_2, \ldots, \bm{R}_r)$$

the deviation $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$ inherits this block structure. With many blocks (large $r$), each block $\bm{R}_i - \bm{I}_{d/r}$ is constrained to a smaller subspace. Since OFT initializes $\bm{R} = \bm{I}$ and typically learns small deviations (especially with COFT's $\epsilon$-constraint), $\bm{R} - \bm{I}$ often has small entries concentrated along the block-diagonal, leading to a low "effective rank."

## Two Paths to Parameter Efficiency

The paper highlights that OFT and LoRA represent philosophically different approaches:

- **LoRA** uses additive updates with explicit low-rank factorization: $\bm{W} = \bm{W}^0 + \bm{B}\bm{A}$, reducing parameters through rank constraints
- **OFT** uses multiplicative updates with block-diagonal orthogonal matrices, reducing parameters through sparsity structure

Both can achieve low-rank effective updates, but OFT additionally guarantees preservation of hyperspherical energy (pairwise neuron angles), which LoRA cannot. This structural preservation is what gives OFT its stability advantages during finetuning of diffusion models.
