\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### What does the epsilon constraint in COFT actually constrain?
Question:
The paper introduces Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT) with an additional constraint $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\| \leq \epsilon$. They then approximate this using Neumann series to get $\bm{R} \approx \bm{I} + 2\bm{Q}$, and constrain $\|\bm{Q}\| \leq \epsilon'$.

I'm confused about what norm is being used here. Is it the Frobenius norm? Spectral norm? Also, how does constraining $\bm{Q}$ relate back to the original constraint on $\bm{R}$? The Neumann series approximation seems like it would only be valid for small $\bm{Q}$, so what happens when the constraint is tight?
Answer:
The paper specifies that the Neumann series approximation is valid "in the operator norm," which means **spectral norm** (denoted $\|\cdot\|_2$ or $\|\cdot\|_{op}$) is being used, not Frobenius norm.

The relationship between the two constraints works as follows. The Neumann series expansion gives:
$$(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \bm{Q}^k$$

which converges when $\|\bm{Q}\|_{op} < 1$. Using this, we can derive the exact relationship:

$$\bm{R} - \bm{I} = (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} - \bm{I} = 2\bm{Q}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$$

By sub-multiplicativity of the spectral norm:
$$\|\bm{R} - \bm{I}\|_{op} \leq 2\|\bm{Q}\|_{op} \cdot \|(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\|_{op} \leq \frac{2\epsilon'}{1-\epsilon'}$$

where we used $\|(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\|_{op} \leq \frac{1}{1-\|\bm{Q}\|_{op}} \leq \frac{1}{1-\epsilon'}$.

So constraining $\|\bm{Q}\|_{op} \leq \epsilon'$ implicitly bounds $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|_{op}$ by approximately $2\epsilon'$ (when $\epsilon'$ is small), since $\frac{2\epsilon'}{1-\epsilon'} \approx 2\epsilon'$.

Regarding your concern about tight constraints: when $\epsilon'$ approaches 1, the Neumann series approximation indeed becomes invalid. This is why in practice, $\epsilon'$ must be kept small (typically $\epsilon' \ll 1$) to ensure both the series converges and the approximation $\bm{R} \approx \bm{I} + 2\bm{Q}$ remains accurate. The paper's approach enforces this through projected gradient descent on $\bm{Q}$, keeping it within a small neighborhood of zero—precisely where the approximation is valid. When $\epsilon'$ is small, COFT essentially constrains $\bm{R}$ to stay near the identity matrix in the spectral norm sense, which ensures the finetuned weights remain close to the pretrained weights.
