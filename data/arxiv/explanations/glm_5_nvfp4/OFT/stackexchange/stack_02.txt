\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does an orthogonal transformation preserve hyperspherical energy?
Question:
In the OFT paper, the authors claim that applying the same orthogonal transformation to all neurons preserves hyperspherical energy. Specifically, they say that if $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ where $\bm{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix, then the hyperspherical energy difference is minimized to zero.

I understand that orthogonal matrices preserve lengths and angles, but I'm confused about why this guarantees that $\text{HE}(\bm{W}) = \text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$. Can someone explain this mathematically? Is there a proof or intuition for why the sum of inverse pairwise distances remains unchanged?
Answer:
The key insight is that orthogonal transformations are **isometries**—they preserve all distances and angles in the vector space. Let me walk through the mathematics step by step.

Given the hyperspherical energy definition from the paper:

$$\text{HE}(\bm{W}) = \sum_{i \neq j} \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$$

where $\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\|$ is the normalized neuron on the unit hypersphere.

**Proof of preservation:**

If $\bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ with $\bm{R}$ orthogonal, then for each neuron:
$$\bm{w}_i = \bm{R}\bm{w}^0_i$$

For the normalized version:
$$\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \frac{\bm{R}\bm{w}^0_i}{\|\bm{R}\bm{w}^0_i\|}$$

Since orthogonal matrices preserve norms ($\|\bm{R}\bm{v}\| = \|\bm{v}\|$ for any vector $\bm{v}$), we have:
$$\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \frac{\bm{R}\bm{w}^0_i}{\|\bm{w}^0_i\|} = \bm{R}\frac{\bm{w}^0_i}{\|\bm{w}^0_i\|} = \bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}^0_i$$

Now examine the pairwise distances in the energy computation:
$$\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\| = \|\bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}^0_i - \bm{R}\hat{\bm{w}}^0_j\| = \|\bm{R}(\hat{\bm{w}}^0_i - \hat{\bm{w}}^0_j)\| = \|\hat{\bm{w}}^0_i - \hat{\bm{w}}^0_j\|$$

The last equality holds because orthogonal transformations preserve distances. Therefore:
$$\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1} = \|\hat{\bm{w}}^0_i - \hat{\bm{w}}^0_j\|^{-1}$$

Summing over all pairs gives $\text{HE}(\bm{W}) = \text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$.

**Intuition:** Think of the neurons as points on a unit sphere. Hyperspherical energy depends only on the angular relationships (pairwise distances on the sphere surface). An orthogonal transformation rotates or reflects the entire sphere as a rigid body—all points move together, so their relative positions and angles remain unchanged. It's like rotating a globe: the distances between cities on its surface stay the same regardless of how you spin it.
