\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### How does the 'commit but do not wait' pattern in Algorithm 2 enable overlapping?
Question:
In Algorithm 2 of FlashAttention-3, I see a pattern where some WGMMA operations say 'Commit and wait' while others say 'Commit but do not wait.' For example:

- Line 4: `Compute $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}} = \mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_0^T$ using WGMMA. Commit and wait.`
- Line 11: `Compute $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}} = \mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_{j}^T$ using WGMMA. Commit but do not wait.`

I understand that WGMMA is asynchronous on Hopper, but I'm confused about what exactly 'commit' means and why waiting vs. not waiting enables overlapping. If I don't wait for a WGMMA to complete, can I use its output in subsequent operations?

Also, line 13 says 'Wait for the WGMMA $\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_{j}^T$' which happens later. Does this mean the softmax on line 14 is computing based on $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}}$ from the previous iteration while $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ is still being computed?
Answer:
The "commit but do not wait" pattern exploits a key feature of Hopper GPUs: WGMMA (Warp Group Matrix Multiply Accumulate) operations execute asynchronously on the Tensor Cores, separate from the CUDA cores that handle other operations like softmax.

**What "commit" means:** In this context, "commit" means launching or submitting the WGMMA operation to the hardware. The operation begins executing on the Tensor Cores, but control immediately returns to the issuing warp without blocking.

**Why waiting vs. not waiting enables overlapping:** When you "commit and wait," you stall the issuing warp until the WGMMA completes, preventing any other work. When you "commit but do not wait," the issuing warp can proceed to execute other instructions while the Tensor Core continues processing the GEMM in the background. Since softmax operations (which involve exponentials and row-wise reductions) run on different hardware units—the multi-function units and CUDA cores—they can execute concurrently with the asynchronous WGMMA.

**Can you use the output without waiting?** No—you must wait before reading the result. This is why line 14 explicitly says "Wait for the WGMMA $\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_{j}^T$" before line 15 uses $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ in the softmax computation. The pattern is: launch operation, do independent work, then wait when you need the result.

**What overlaps with what:** Looking at the algorithm flow, in iteration $j$:
- Line 11 starts $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}} = \mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^T$ without waiting
- Line 13 starts the second GEMM $\mathbf{O}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{O}_i + \tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{cur}} \mathbf{V}_{j-1}$ without waiting
- Line 14 waits for the first WGMMA to complete
- Line 15 computes softmax on $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ — this can overlap with the second WGMMA from line 13, which may still be running

So yes, your interpretation is correct: the softmax computes based on $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ (not $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}}$) after line 14's wait, but the second GEMM from line 13 can continue executing in the background during this softmax computation. This is the essence of the "2-stage pipelining" mentioned in Section 3.2, where the second WGMMA of iteration $j$ overlaps with the softmax that produces $\tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{next}}$ (which will be used in the subsequent iteration).
