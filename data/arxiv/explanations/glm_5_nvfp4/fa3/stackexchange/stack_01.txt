\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Why does softmax become a bottleneck when Tensor Cores have such high throughput?
Question:
I'm reading the FlashAttention-3 paper and I'm confused about why softmax is described as such a significant bottleneck. The paper states that on the H100, there are 989 TFLOPS of FP16 matmul throughput but only 3.9 TFLOPS for special functions like exponential. 

The paper mentions that for attention with head dimension 128, there are 512x more matmul FLOPS than exponential operations, but exponential has 256x lower throughput, so "exponential can take 50% of the cycle compared to matmul." 

I don't follow this calculation. If matmul has so many more FLOPS and much higher throughput, shouldn't matmul dominate the runtime? How does the softmax end up taking such a large fraction of the execution time?
Answer:
The confusion stems from mixing up *operation count* with *execution time*. The key insight is that while matmul has many more FLOPS, the Tensor Cores execute them so efficiently that the relative inefficiency of exponential operations becomes significant.

Let me work through the math from the paper step by step.

For attention with head dimension 128:
- Matmul FLOPS outnumber exponential operations by a factor of **$512\times$**
- But matmul throughput (989 TFLOPS) exceeds exponential throughput (3.9 TFLOPS) by a factor of **$256\times$**

The execution time ratio is:

$$\frac{\text{time for exponentials}}{\text{time for matmul}} = \frac{\text{exp operations} / \text{exp throughput}}{\text{matmul FLOPS} / \text{matmul throughput}}$$

$$= \frac{\text{exp operations}}{\text{matmul FLOPS}} \times \frac{\text{matmul throughput}}{\text{exp throughput}}$$

$$= \frac{1}{512} \times \frac{989}{3.9} \approx \frac{1}{512} \times 254 \approx 0.5$$

So exponentials take approximately **50% of the matmul execution time**.

Intuitively, the $512\times$ more matmul operations are handled by hardware that's $256\times$ faster, leaving only a $2\times$ net advantage in total work capacity. This nearly cancels out, making softmax competitive with matmul in terms of wall-clock time.

This is a classic manifestation of **Amdahl's Law**: even a small fraction of low-throughput operations can dominate runtime when the rest is highly optimized. The paper addresses this by overlapping softmax computation with asynchronous Tensor Core operations, effectively "hiding" the softmax latency during matmul execution.
