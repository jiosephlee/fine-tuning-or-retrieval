\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### In BOFT, why does multiplying butterfly components preserve orthogonality?
Question:
The paper defines $\bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ and states that each butterfly component is orthogonal. I know that the product of orthogonal matrices is orthogonal, so this makes sense mathematically. But I'm confused about how each $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ is constructed to be orthogonal when the block sizes change (e.g., $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b(d,2)$ vs $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b(d,4)$). The paper mentions 'the non-zero pattern of every butterfly component can be viewed as a permutation of the non-zero pattern of $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2)$' -- how does this permutation preserve orthogonality?
Answer:
The key insight is that the butterfly factor structure ensures that non-zero entries in any butterfly component $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,k)$ naturally form independent $2 \times 2$ groups that can each be parameterized as orthogonal—exactly like the blocks in $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2)$.

Let me walk through this concretely. A butterfly factor $\bm{B}^F(k)$ is defined as:
$$\bm{B}^F(k)=\begin{bmatrix}
\text{diag}(\bm{d}_1) & \text{diag}(\bm{d}_2) \\
\text{diag}(\bm{d}_3) & \text{diag}(\bm{d}_4)
\end{bmatrix}$$

For $k=4$, this gives a $4 \times 4$ matrix with non-zero pattern:
$$\begin{bmatrix}
\times & 0 & \times & 0 \\
0 & \times & 0 & \times \\
\times & 0 & \times & 0 \\
0 & \times & 0 & \times
\end{bmatrix}$$

Now here's the crucial observation: if you permute the rows and columns (swapping positions 2 and 3), this becomes:
$$\begin{bmatrix}
\times & \times & 0 & 0 \\
\times & \times & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \times & \times \\
0 & 0 & \times & \times
\end{bmatrix}$$

This is a block-diagonal matrix with $2 \times 2$ blocks! The same structure as $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2)$.

Since permutation matrices are orthogonal, we can write $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,4) = \bm{P}^\top \tilde{\bm{B}}' \bm{P}$ where $\bm{P}$ is a permutation matrix and $\tilde{\bm{B}}'$ is block-diagonal with $2 \times 2$ blocks. If we parameterize each $2 \times 2$ block in $\tilde{\bm{B}}'$ as orthogonal (using Cayley transform or 2D rotation), then $\tilde{\bm{B}}'$ is orthogonal. And since permutation matrices are orthogonal, $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,4) = \bm{P}^\top \tilde{\bm{B}}' \bm{P}$ is also orthogonal—the product of orthogonal matrices remains orthogonal.

This pattern extends to any block size $k = 2^i$. The "block-wise permutation" for the generalized $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b(d,k)$ works analogously: each $2b \times 2b$ submatrix within the larger blocks can be permuted into a form where the trainable parameters form independent orthogonal blocks.

Intuitively, the butterfly structure ensures that information only flows between carefully chosen pairs of coordinates at each step, and the permutation view reveals that these pairs are exactly the coordinate pairs that would interact in the simpler $k=2$ case—the indices are just shuffled by the permutation. This is why the paper can claim that "all butterfly components can be easily parameterized as orthogonal matrices" once we know how to handle the $k=2$ case.
