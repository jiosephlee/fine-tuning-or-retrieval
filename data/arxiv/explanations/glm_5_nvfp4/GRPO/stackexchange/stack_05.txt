\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### Difference between outcome supervision and process supervision in GRPO
Question:
In the DeepSeekMath paper, they describe two types of supervision for GRPO:

1. **Outcome supervision**: Sets $\hat{A}_{i, t} = \widetilde{r}_i$ for all tokens
2. **Process supervision**: Sets $\hat{A}_{i, t} = \sum_{index(j) \ge t} \widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}$

I understand that process supervision provides rewards at each reasoning step. But I'm confused about the advantage calculation. In process supervision, why is the advantage at token $t$ the sum of all future step rewards from that point onward? Shouldn't it just be the reward at the current step?

Also, what exactly does $index(j)$ represent - is it the token position or something else? The paper says it's 'the end token index of the $j$-th step' but I'm not clear on how steps are defined.
Answer:
The key to understanding this distinction lies in how advantages propagate credit backward through the reasoning chain.

**What $index(j)$ means:**

$index(j)$ is the token position of the *last token* in the $j$-th reasoning step. In mathematical reasoning, a "step" typically corresponds to a distinct part of the chain-of-thought solution. For instance, if solving a problem produces tokens 1-10, with reasoning steps ending at positions 3, 6, and 10, then $index(1)=3$, $index(2)=6$, and $index(3)=10$.

**Why sum future rewards for process supervision:**

The advantage $\hat{A}_{i,t} = \sum_{index(j) \ge t} \widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}$ assigns each token credit for *all future step rewards it contributes to*. This is the correct formulation because early tokens in a reasoning chain lay the groundwork for everything that follows.

Consider an example: if your output has 10 tokens with step rewards at positions 3, 6, and 10, then:
- Tokens 1-3 receive credit for all three step rewards
- Tokens 4-6 receive credit for steps 2 and 3 only
- Tokens 7-10 receive credit for step 3 only

This makes intuitive sense—the first few tokens establish the reasoning direction and affect the entire solution's success. A token at position $t$ cannot influence rewards from steps that already completed before it, but it does influence all subsequent steps.

**Contrast with outcome supervision:**

In outcome supervision, $\hat{A}_{i,t} = \widetilde{r}_i$ for all tokens. This assigns the *same* final reward to every token, regardless of position. The problem is that early reasoning errors and late tokens receive identical credit, even though early mistakes often cascade and doom the entire solution.

Process supervision provides more fine-grained credit assignment: tokens that contribute to more successful reasoning steps receive higher advantages, encouraging the model to focus on improving the quality of each reasoning stage rather than just reaching a correct final answer.
