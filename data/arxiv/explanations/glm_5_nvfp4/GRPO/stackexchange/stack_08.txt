\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### Why does code training before math training help?
Question:
The DeepSeekMath paper shows that code training prior to math training improves models' ability to solve mathematical problems, both with and without tool use. They offer this as a 'partial answer' to whether code training improves reasoning abilities.

Looking at their experiments:
- Code training alone improves GSM8K and MATH performance
- Code training followed by math training yields the best results
- But mixing code and math tokens in one-stage training actually compromises mathematical reasoning without tool use

What mechanism could explain this? The paper mentions 'boosting the efficiency of subsequent math training,' but I'm not clear on what that means.

Why would a model trained on code be better positioned to learn mathematics? And why does the two-stage approach work better than mixing the data together?
Answer:
The DeepSeekMath paper provides valuable empirical findings on this question, though the precise mechanism remains partially an open question. Here's what the paper establishes:

**Code training directly enhances mathematical reasoning.** The experiments in Section 5.1.1 show that code training alone (400B tokens) already improves performance on GSM8K and MATH benchmarks, even before any math-specific training. This suggests code training develops transferable reasoning capabilities.

**Code training "boosts the efficiency of subsequent math training."** The paper uses this exact phrase to describe why the two-stage approach yields the best results. When starting from a code-trained model, the subsequent math training phase achieves better final performance than starting from a general pre-trained model. The code pre-training appears to establish useful representational structures that math training can then refine more effectively.

**Why two-stage outperforms mixing.** The paper offers a specific conjecture: when code and math tokens are mixed in one-stage training, the model "lacks the capacity to fully assimilate both code and mathematical data simultaneously." At the 1.3B parameter scale tested, the model struggles to jointly learn both domains. The two-stage approach allows the model to first develop strong procedural reasoning patterns from code, then specialize those patterns for mathematics.

Intuitively, code and mathematics share structural similarities: both require precise step-by-step execution, logical flow, and syntactic correctness. Code training may teach the model how to maintain coherent multi-step reasoning chains, handle nested operations, and develop a notion of "execution" that transfers to mathematical problem-solving. The sequential approach lets these foundational skills solidify before applying them to the more specialized domain of mathematics.

The paper explicitly positions this as only a "partial answer" to whether code training improves reasoning—it demonstrates the effect clearly in the mathematical domain, but the deeper mechanistic explanation remains an area for future research.
