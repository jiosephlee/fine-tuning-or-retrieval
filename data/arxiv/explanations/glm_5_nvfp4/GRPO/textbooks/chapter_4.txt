# Instruction-Tuning Data Formats: Chain-of-Thought, Program-of-Thought, and Tool-Integrated Reasoning

The transformation of a base language model into an effective mathematical assistant requires careful consideration of how solutions are presented to the model during training. The format of instruction-tuning data plays a fundamental role in shaping how the model learns to approach and solve mathematical problems. DeepSeekMath employs three distinct solution formats that capture different modes of mathematical reasoning, each offering unique advantages for developing robust problem-solving capabilities.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning represents the most intuitive format for mathematical instruction. In this approach, the model is trained to generate solutions that mirror how humans naturally solve mathematical problems: by breaking down complex problems into a sequence of logical reasoning steps. Each step builds upon the previous one, creating a clear trail of logical deduction that leads from the problem statement to the final answer. This format encourages the model to develop a structured approach to problem-solving, where intermediate computations and logical inferences are explicitly stated. The strength of CoT lies in its transparency—by articulating each reasoning step, the model produces solutions that are interpretable and verifiable, allowing users to follow the logical progression and identify potential errors. When trained on CoT data, the model learns that mathematical reasoning is not merely about arriving at an answer, but about constructing a coherent argument that justifies each step along the way.

Program-of-Thought (PoT) reasoning takes a complementary approach by leveraging the computational precision of programming languages. In this format, mathematical solutions are expressed as executable programs, typically in Python, where the logical reasoning is encoded through program structure and the heavy lifting of computation is delegated to the programming language's execution environment. This approach is particularly valuable for problems involving complex calculations, symbolic manipulation, or numerical precision. Rather than relying solely on the model's internal computation capabilities—which can be prone to errors in complex arithmetic—PoT allows the model to offload computational tasks to a reliable execution environment. Libraries such as `math` and `sympy` can be utilized for intricate computations, providing accurate results that might otherwise be difficult to achieve through pure language-based reasoning. The model trained on PoT data learns to translate mathematical problems into computational frameworks, developing the ability to recognize when programmatic solutions are appropriate and how to structure code to solve mathematical challenges.

Tool-Integrated Reasoning represents a synthesis of natural language reasoning and computational tools. This format combines the interpretability of chain-of-thought reasoning with the computational power of program execution. In tool-integrated reasoning, the model alternates between natural language reasoning steps and program execution, using each mode strategically based on the requirements of the problem at hand. The model might begin with natural language analysis, identify where computation is needed, write and execute the necessary code, and then interpret the results within the broader context of the solution. This interleaved approach allows the model to maintain explanatory clarity while ensuring computational accuracy. The training data for tool-integrated reasoning teaches the model to make intelligent decisions about when to reason linguistically and when to invoke computational tools, creating a flexible problem-solving strategy that adapts to the demands of each problem.

These three formats are not mutually exclusive in practice. A well-designed instruction-tuning dataset includes examples across all three formats, allowing the model to develop versatile problem-solving capabilities. The diversity of solution formats exposes the model to different valid approaches to mathematical reasoning, preventing over-reliance on any single strategy and encouraging the development of meta-cognitive skills—knowing which approach is most suitable for a given problem type. During evaluation, models can be tested under different settings: generating self-contained text solutions without tool use (emphasizing CoT capabilities), or solving problems with access to computational tools (leveraging PoT and tool-integrated reasoning skills).

# Curating English and Chinese Mathematical Datasets

The quality and diversity of the instruction-tuning dataset fundamentally determines the capabilities of the resulting model. DeepSeekMath's approach to data curation emphasizes comprehensiveness across languages, mathematical fields, and complexity levels, resulting in a dataset of 776,000 training examples that spans the breadth of mathematical reasoning challenges.

For English mathematical datasets, the curation process draws from multiple established sources to ensure broad coverage. The GSM8K and MATH benchmark problems serve as foundational elements, but these are not used in their original form. Instead, the problems are annotated with tool-integrated solutions, providing the model with examples of how to combine reasoning with computational tools. Beyond these benchmark-derived problems, the dataset incorporates a subset of MathInstruct, a comprehensive collection that spans diverse mathematical fields including algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry. This diversity is crucial for developing a model that can handle the wide range of mathematical challenges encountered in academic and practical contexts. Additionally, the training set of Lila-OOD is included, where problems are solved using either chain-of-thought or program-of-thought formats. The inclusion of multiple solution formats for similar problem types helps the model understand that mathematical problems can often be approached through different valid strategies.

The Chinese mathematical datasets address an important gap in previous work, which often focused exclusively on English-language mathematical content. DeepSeekMath recognizes that mathematical reasoning transcends language barriers, and a truly capable mathematical assistant should be able to reason in multiple languages. The Chinese dataset collection focuses on K-12 mathematical problems spanning 76 sub-topics, including fundamental areas such as linear equations. Each problem is annotated with solutions in both chain-of-thought and tool-integrated reasoning formats, maintaining consistency with the English dataset while respecting the linguistic and pedagogical conventions of Chinese mathematical education. This bilingual approach yields practical benefits: the resulting model demonstrates strong performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks including MGSM-zh, CMATH, Gaokao-MathCloze, and Gaokao-MathQA.

The curation strategy reflects several key principles that contribute to dataset quality. First, the dataset spans problems from grade-school level to college-level complexity, ensuring that the model develops capabilities across the full spectrum of mathematical difficulty. Second, problems are drawn from diverse mathematical fields, preventing the model from developing narrow expertise in only a few areas while neglecting others. Third, the inclusion of both CoT and PoT solutions for similar problems encourages the model to develop flexible problem-solving strategies rather than defaulting to a single approach. Fourth, the multilingual design ensures that mathematical reasoning capabilities transfer across linguistic contexts, making the model useful to a broader user base.

The total of 776,000 training examples represents a careful balance between dataset scale and quality. Each example is designed to provide meaningful learning signal, with clear problem statements paired with well-structured solutions that demonstrate proper reasoning patterns. This comprehensive dataset serves as the foundation upon which the instruction-tuning process builds, transforming the mathematical knowledge encoded in the pre-trained model into practical problem-solving capabilities.

# Training Configuration for Supervised Fine-Tuning

The supervised fine-tuning process transforms the pre-trained DeepSeekMath-Base model into DeepSeekMath-Instruct, an instruction-following model capable of engaging with mathematical problems in a conversational manner. The training configuration for this transformation involves several key hyperparameters and design decisions that optimize the learning process while maintaining the model's foundational capabilities.

The training data organization follows a concatenation strategy where individual training examples are combined until reaching a maximum context length of 4,000 tokens. This approach allows the model to process multiple examples within a single training batch, improving training efficiency while exposing the model to varied problem-solving contexts. The concatenation is performed randomly, meaning that adjacent examples in the training data are combined without any particular ordering scheme, preventing the model from learning spurious patterns based on example sequencing.

The training duration consists of 500 steps, a relatively brief training schedule that reflects the efficiency of fine-tuning on high-quality, well-formatted instruction data. Each training step processes a batch of 256 examples, providing sufficient gradient signal for stable learning while enabling efficient utilization of computational resources. The combination of 500 steps with a batch size of 256 means that the model sees approximately 128,000 example presentations during training—significantly fewer passes than the total dataset size of 776,000 examples, suggesting that each example influences the model only once or a small number of times during training.

The learning rate schedule employs a constant learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ throughout training. This contrasts with the more complex schedules often used during pre-training, which typically include warmup phases and decay schedules. The constant learning rate in fine-tuning reflects a different optimization regime: rather than exploring a vast parameter space as in pre-training, fine-tuning makes more targeted adjustments to a model that already possesses substantial knowledge and capabilities. The constant learning rate ensures stable, consistent updates throughout the fine-tuning process.

The choice of a relatively short training duration with a constant learning rate reflects an important principle in instruction tuning: the goal is not to train from scratch, but to adapt an already-capable model to follow instructions and format responses appropriately. Over-training on instruction data risks degrading the model's foundational knowledge, so the training configuration is designed to achieve the desired adaptation efficiently without excessive modification of the pre-trained weights.

# Evaluation Settings: With and Without Tool Use

Evaluating mathematical reasoning capabilities requires consideration of different modes of problem-solving. DeepSeekMath-Instruct is evaluated under two distinct settings that assess different aspects of mathematical reasoning: generating self-contained text solutions without tool use, and solving problems with access to computational tools.

In the setting where tool use is disallowed, the model must generate complete solutions using only natural language reasoning. This evaluation setting emphasizes the model's ability to perform chain-of-thought reasoning without external assistance. The model receives a mathematical problem and must produce a step-by-step solution that arrives at the correct answer through explicit reasoning. All intermediate computations, logical deductions, and mathematical manipulations must be performed internally by the model. This setting tests the model's intrinsic reasoning capabilities—its ability to understand problems, devise solution strategies, and execute mathematical operations without relying on external computational resources. The evaluation covers quantitative reasoning benchmarks including GSM8K and MATH in English, as well as CMATH and Gaokao benchmarks in Chinese, providing a comprehensive assessment of reasoning capabilities across languages and complexity levels.

The setting where models are allowed to integrate natural language reasoning and program-based tool use presents a different challenge. In this mode, the model can write Python programs to solve problems, utilizing libraries such as `math` and `sympy` for intricate computations. The execution result of the program is evaluated as the answer, allowing the model to leverage computational precision that may exceed its internal arithmetic capabilities. This setting tests the model's ability to recognize when computational tools are beneficial, to write correct and efficient programs, and to integrate programmatic solutions into a coherent problem-solving approach. The evaluation on GSM8K and MATH using few-shot program-of-thought prompting reveals how well the model has learned to translate mathematical problems into computational frameworks.

These two evaluation settings are not merely alternative ways to measure the same capability—they assess fundamentally different aspects of mathematical reasoning. The tool-free setting emphasizes reasoning transparency and interpretability: can the model articulate a clear, logical path from problem to solution? The tool-enabled setting emphasizes computational correctness and efficiency: can the model leverage external tools to overcome limitations of internal computation? A well-designed mathematical model should excel in both settings, demonstrating strong reasoning capabilities while also knowing when and how to employ computational tools effectively.

The distinction between these evaluation settings also has practical implications for deployment. In some contexts, such as educational applications where the goal is to teach reasoning, the tool-free approach may be preferred because it makes the reasoning process explicit. In other contexts, such as solving computationally intensive problems, the tool-enabled approach may be more practical. By evaluating in both settings, the capabilities and limitations of the model are fully characterized, providing users with the information needed to select the appropriate mode for their specific use case.

# Performance Comparison with Open-Source and Closed-Source Models

A comprehensive evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B against both open-source and closed-source models reveals its competitive positioning within the landscape of mathematical language models. The comparison spans a diverse set of models, from general-purpose commercial systems to specialized mathematical models, providing context for understanding DeepSeekMath-Instruct's capabilities and limitations.

Among closed-source models, the comparison includes the GPT family, with GPT-4 and GPT-4 Code Interpreter representing the most capable systems. The Gemini family, including Gemini Ultra and Gemini Pro, provides additional benchmarks from Google's state-of-the-art models. Other commercial models include Inflection-2, Grok-1, and Chinese models such as Baichuan-3 and GLM-4. These closed-source models have typically undergone extensive alignment procedures and benefit from proprietary training data and techniques not available to the open-source community. Their performance represents upper bounds on what current technology can achieve, though their closed nature limits scientific understanding of the specific factors contributing to their capabilities.

The open-source comparison set includes both general models and mathematically-specialized models. General models include DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, Qwen 72B, SeaLLM-v2 7B, and ChatGLM3 6B. Mathematical models include InternLM2-Math 20B, which underwent math training followed by instruction tuning; Math-Shepherd-Mistral 7B, which applied PPO training with process supervision; the WizardMath series, which used evolved instructions and PPO training; MetaMath 70B, fine-tuned on augmented mathematical datasets; ToRA 34B, trained for tool-integrated reasoning; and MAmmoTH 70B, instruction-tuned on MathInstruct. This diverse set of open-source models provides multiple reference points for understanding DeepSeekMath-Instruct's performance relative to different training approaches and model scales.

Under the evaluation setting where tool use is disallowed, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates strong performance on step-by-step reasoning tasks. On the competition-level MATH dataset, the model surpasses all open-source models and the majority of proprietary models by at least 9% absolute. This achievement holds even against models that are substantially larger—such as Qwen 72B—or have been specifically enhanced through math-focused reinforcement learning—such as WizardMath-v1.1 7B. While DeepSeekMath-Instruct rivals Chinese proprietary models GLM-4 and Baichuan-3 on MATH, it still underperforms GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra, indicating that some gap remains relative to the most capable closed-source systems.

Under the evaluation setting where models can integrate natural language reasoning and program-based tool use, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approaches an accuracy of 60% on MATH, surpassing all existing open-source models. On other benchmarks, the model proves competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, which was previously the state-of-the-art open-source model despite being 10 times larger. These results demonstrate that careful data curation and training methodology can enable smaller models to achieve performance competitive with much larger counterparts.

The comparison also highlights the value of multilingual training. DeepSeekMath-Instruct demonstrates strong performance on Chinese benchmarks, a capability that English-centric mathematical models often lack. By including high-quality non-English mathematical content during pre-training and instruction tuning, the model develops reasoning capabilities that transfer across linguistic contexts, making it useful to a broader user base than models trained exclusively on English data.

# Strengths and Limitations of the Instruction-Tuned Model

DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B demonstrates remarkable strengths that establish it as a leading open-source mathematical model, while also exhibiting limitations that define areas for future improvement.

The model's primary strength lies in its exceptional performance on quantitative reasoning benchmarks. On the competition-level MATH benchmark, the model achieves performance levels that approach those of closed-source systems like Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4, a significant achievement for an open-source 7B parameter model. The model surpasses all open-source models in the 7B to 70B range on chain-of-thought reasoning tasks, demonstrating that mathematical capability is not solely determined by model scale. The competitive performance against models ten times larger suggests that the combination of high-quality mathematical pre-training data and carefully curated instruction-tuning data can compensate for having fewer parameters.

Another significant strength is the model's multilingual capability. Unlike previous mathematical models that focused primarily or exclusively on English, DeepSeekMath-Instruct demonstrates strong performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks. This bilingual capability makes the model accessible to a broader user base and demonstrates that mathematical reasoning is not language-bound—the underlying cognitive skills transfer across linguistic contexts when the training data includes appropriate multilingual content.

The model's versatility across different reasoning formats represents another strength. By training on chain-of-thought, program-of-thought, and tool-integrated reasoning formats, the model develops flexible problem-solving strategies. It can generate transparent step-by-step solutions when tool-free reasoning is appropriate, and can effectively leverage computational tools when program execution provides advantages. This versatility makes the model adaptable to different use cases and evaluation contexts.

However, the model also exhibits notable limitations. Despite approaching GPT-4 performance on quantitative reasoning benchmarks, the model underperforms on geometry and theorem-proof tasks. In preliminary testing, the model struggles with problems related to triangles and ellipses, suggesting potential data selection bias in pre-training and fine-tuning. Mathematical reasoning encompasses more than algebraic manipulation and arithmetic—it includes spatial reasoning, geometric intuition, and formal proof construction. The relative weakness in these areas indicates that the training data may not adequately cover all mathematical domains.

Another limitation relates to few-shot capability. Restricted by model scale, DeepSeekMath-Instruct performs worse than GPT-4 in few-shot settings. GPT-4 can improve its performance through few-shot prompting, where providing examples of similar problems helps guide the model's reasoning. In contrast, DeepSeekMath-Instruct shows similar performance in zero-shot and few-shot evaluation, suggesting limited capacity to adapt its reasoning based on provided examples. This limitation constrains the model's flexibility in deployment scenarios where few-shot prompting could enhance performance on specific task types.

The model also underperforms GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra on the most challenging benchmarks, indicating that despite impressive achievements, the most capable closed-source systems remain ahead. This gap may reflect differences in training data scale, model architecture, or alignment techniques that are not publicly documented. Understanding and closing this gap represents an ongoing challenge for the open-source community.

These limitations point toward future directions for improvement. Expanding the training data to cover geometry and formal proof more comprehensively could address domain-specific weaknesses. Exploring techniques to enhance few-shot capability could improve the model's flexibility. Continued research into more effective training methods, including the reinforcement learning techniques explored in the broader DeepSeekMath project, offers paths toward further performance improvements.
