\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 9: # Conclusions, Limitations, and Practical Takeaways

# Summary of DeepSeekMath's Achievements and Contributions

The DeepSeekMath project represents a significant milestone in the development of open-source language models for mathematical reasoning. At its core, the project demonstrates that publicly available web data, when processed through a carefully designed pipeline, can yield high-quality mathematical training corpora that enable smaller models to compete with much larger proprietary systems. The flagship model, DeepSeekMath 7B, achieves an impressive 51.7% accuracy on the competition-level MATH benchmark without relying on external toolkits or voting techniques. This performance approaches that of frontier closed-source models such as Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4, marking a substantial narrowing of the gap between open and closed models in mathematical reasoning capabilities.

When self-consistency techniques are applied over 64 samples, DeepSeekMath 7B achieves an even more remarkable 60.9% on MATH. The DeepSeekMath-Base model, which serves as the foundation, achieves 64.2% on GSM8K and 36.2% on the MATH benchmark, outperforming Minerva 540B despite being approximately 77 times smaller in parameter count. This finding challenges the assumption that model scale is the primary determinant of mathematical reasoning capability, instead highlighting the crucial role of data quality and training methodology.

The project's contributions span both pre-training and reinforcement learning domains. On the pre-training side, the DeepSeekMath Corpus, comprising 120B tokens of mathematical content extracted from Common Crawl, represents a resource nearly 7 times larger than that used by Minerva and 9 times larger than OpenWebMath. This corpus is multilingual, predominantly featuring English and Chinese content, which enables improved performance on both English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks. On the reinforcement learning side, the introduction of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) provides an efficient alternative to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that eliminates the need for a critic model while maintaining competitive performance.

# Key Lessons: Web Data Value, Code Training Benefits, arXiv Ineffectiveness

One of the most significant findings from the DeepSeekMath project is the untapped potential of publicly available web data for mathematical training. Through an iterative data selection pipeline, the researchers demonstrate that Common Crawl contains substantial high-quality mathematical content that can be effectively harvested using fastText classifiers trained on seed corpora. The iterative refinement process, which involves manually annotating URLs within math-related domains and updating the classifier, proves essential for maximizing recall while maintaining precision. After four iterations, the pipeline collects 35.5M mathematical web pages totaling 120B tokens, with convergence indicated by the fact that 98% of the data in the fourth iteration had already been collected in the third iteration.

The quality of the DeepSeekMath Corpus is validated through pre-training experiments showing that models trained on this corpus outperform those trained on existing mathematical corpora such as MathPile, OpenWebMath, and Proof-Pile-2. The corpus demonstrates three key characteristics: high quality as evidenced by downstream performance, multilingual coverage enabling cross-lingual transfer, and large scale enabling continued learning improvements rather than early plateauing. The finding that web data can serve as a foundation for strong mathematical reasoning models has important implications for the research community, suggesting that specialized corpora can be constructed from public sources with appropriate engineering effort.

The relationship between code training and mathematical reasoning receives careful examination in the project. The researchers find that code training prior to math training improves models' ability to solve mathematical problems both with and without tool use. Under two-stage training settings, code training alone significantly enhances the ability to solve GSM8K and MATH problems using Python, and subsequent math training yields further improvements. The code training stage also improves mathematical reasoning without tool use, though the magnitude of improvement is more moderate. Under one-stage training settings, mixing code tokens and math tokens effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while synergizing coding and program-aided mathematical reasoning.

Perhaps counterintuitively, the project finds that arXiv papers seem ineffective in improving mathematical reasoning. Despite being commonly included in math pre-training corpora, experiments with MathPile (over 85% arXiv content) and ArXiv-RedPajama show no notable improvements or even deterioration across mathematical benchmarks including GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and miniF2F. The researchers acknowledge limitations to this conclusion, noting that the impact on specific math-related tasks not included in the study, the effect when combined with other data types, and potential benefits at larger model scales remain unexplored. Nevertheless, this finding prompts reconsideration of assumptions about the value of academic paper corpora for mathematical reasoning tasks.

# Model Limitations: Geometry, Theorem Proving, and Few-Shot Learning

Despite achieving impressive scores on quantitative reasoning benchmarks, DeepSeekMath exhibits notable limitations in certain mathematical domains. The model's capability on geometry problems and theorem-proof tasks is relatively weaker compared to closed-source models. In dry run evaluations, the model demonstrates difficulty handling problems related to triangles and ellipses specifically. This weakness may indicate data selection bias in both the pre-training and fine-tuning stages, suggesting that the corpus construction pipeline may have inadvertently underrepresented certain mathematical domains.

The model's performance on formal mathematics, while strong for its scale, reveals room for improvement. The informal-to-formal theorem proving task on miniF2F with Isabelle shows that DeepSeekMath-Base demonstrates strong few-shot autoformalization performance, but the researchers note that geometry and theorem-proof capabilities remain relatively weaker than closed-source models. This disparity highlights the challenges of achieving comprehensive mathematical reasoning across all domains and suggests that specialized attention to geometric reasoning may be necessary for future improvements.

Another significant limitation concerns few-shot learning capability. Due to model scale constraints, DeepSeekMath performs worse than GPT-4 on few-shot capability evaluations. While GPT-4 can improve its performance with few-shot inputs, DeepSeekMath shows similar performance in both zero-shot and few-shot evaluation settings. This finding suggests that the benefits of few-shot prompting, which are substantial in larger models, may not fully manifest in smaller model architectures. The implications are that users of DeepSeekMath cannot expect to significantly enhance performance through careful prompt engineering with examples, a limitation that practitioners should consider when deploying the model.

The constraint of training data scope also presents limitations. DeepSeekMath-RL is trained only on chain-of-thought-format instruction tuning data from GSM8K and MATH, comprising approximately 144K questions. While this constrained training still yields improvements across all evaluation metrics, the scope limitation means that the model may not have encountered the full diversity of mathematical problem types and solution formats that would enable broader generalization.

# Practical Recommendations for Building Mathematical LLMs

Based on the findings of the DeepSeekMath project, several practical recommendations emerge for researchers and practitioners seeking to build mathematical language models. First, initializing from a code-trained model proves advantageous compared to starting from a general-purpose language model. The DeepSeekMath-Base model is initialized with DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, and the researchers observe that code training provides a better foundation than general LLM pre-training. This recommendation extends to the finding that code training benefits both tool-aided and non-tool-aided mathematical reasoning, making it a versatile starting point for mathematical model development.

Data quality emerges as a paramount concern, with the project demonstrating that smaller models trained on high-quality data can match or exceed larger models trained on lower-quality data. The iterative data collection pipeline, starting from a seed corpus and progressively refining through classifier updates and human annotation, provides a template for constructing high-quality domain-specific corpora. Practitioners should invest in data selection and cleaning processes rather than assuming that scale alone will yield performance improvements.

The recommendation to include multilingual data carries practical significance for models intended to serve diverse linguistic communities. The DeepSeekMath Corpus includes both English and Chinese mathematical content, and this multilingual composition enables improved performance on Chinese mathematical benchmarks such as MGSM-zh, CMATH, and Gaokao tasks. The finding that existing English-centric corpora show limited improvement or even hinder performance on Chinese benchmarks underscores the importance of linguistic diversity in mathematical training data.

For the reinforcement learning stage, the GRPO algorithm provides a practical alternative to PPO with reduced memory requirements. By eliminating the critic model and using group-relative advantage estimation, GRPO reduces training resource requirements while maintaining effectiveness. The algorithm proves particularly suitable for mathematical reasoning tasks where reward models typically provide scores only for complete outputs, simplifying the advantage estimation process.

# Open Problems and Future Research Directions

The DeepSeekMath project identifies several promising directions for future research in reinforcement learning for mathematical reasoning. In the data source dimension, the current approach uses only questions from the instruction tuning stage with naive nucleus sampling for output generation. The researchers suggest that exploring out-of-distribution question prompts in conjunction with advanced sampling strategies, particularly tree-search methods, could enhance exploration efficiency. Such approaches might enable the RL pipeline to improve fundamental capabilities rather than merely stabilizing the output distribution.

Efficient inference techniques represent another important research direction. The exploration efficiency of policy models depends critically on inference speed, suggesting that speculative decoding and other acceleration methods could have substantial impact on RL effectiveness. The relationship between inference efficiency and RL performance warrants systematic investigation, as faster sampling could enable more extensive exploration within computational budgets.

On the algorithm front, current methods fully trust the reward signal when updating model parameters, but reward signals are not always reliable. The PRM800K dataset, despite careful annotation by trained annotators, contains approximately 20% incorrect annotations. This observation motivates research into reinforcement learning algorithms that are robust against noisy reward signals. The researchers propose exploring weak-to-strong alignment methods that could fundamentally change learning algorithms by accounting for reward model uncertainty.

The reward function presents three important research directions. First, enhancing the generalization ability of reward models is critical, as these models must handle out-of-distribution questions and advanced decoding outputs. Without such generalization, reinforcement learning may merely stabilize output distributions rather than improve fundamental capabilities. Second, reflecting the uncertainty of reward models could provide a bridge between weak reward models and weak-to-strong learning algorithms. Third, building high-quality process reward models that provide fine-grained training signals for reasoning steps represents an important technical challenge.

# Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI Development

The DeepSeekMath project engages with issues of openness and accessibility in AI development. By releasing models that approach the performance of closed proprietary systems, the project contributes to democratizing access to advanced mathematical reasoning capabilities. The finding that publicly available web data can yield high-quality training corpora further supports the feasibility of developing capable models without exclusive access to proprietary data sources.

The construction of the DeepSeekMath Corpus involves careful attention to benchmark contamination, with systematic filtering to remove web pages containing questions or answers from evaluation benchmarks. This practice ensures that reported performance reflects genuine model capability rather than memorization of test data. The filtering criteria, which remove any text segment with a 10-gram exact match or shorter benchmarks with at least 3 grams, provide a template for responsible data curation.

The multilingual nature of the corpus and the resulting improvements on Chinese mathematical benchmarks raise considerations about equitable access to AI capabilities across linguistic communities. By including non-English mathematical content, the project demonstrates that mathematical reasoning capabilities need not be concentrated in English-centric models, supporting broader accessibility of these technologies.
