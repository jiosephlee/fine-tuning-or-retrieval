In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors employ the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ where $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric. For the Cayley parameterization to be valid, the implicit mathematical condition on $\bm{Q}$ that must hold is that $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is invertible. This condition guarantees that the inverse in $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ exists and, together with $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$, ensures $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal: using $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T}=(\bm{I}-\bm{Q}^\top)^{-1}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}$, we obtain
$\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T}(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$.
Thus, invertibility of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is exactly the algebraic requirement that makes the Cayley transform well-defined and orthogonality-preserving.

Moreover, for the real skew-symmetric matrices used throughout the paper (including the $2\times 2$ blocks in butterfly components), $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is generically invertible, making the condition both sensible and easy to satisfy in practice. The eigenvalues of a real skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$ lie on the imaginary axis, i.e., $\sigma(\bm{Q})\subset\{i\lambda:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}$, so the eigenvalues of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ are $\{1-i\lambda\}$, all of which have strictly positive modulus $\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}>0$; hence $\det(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})\neq 0$. In the $2\times 2$ case with $\bm{Q}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & a \\ -a & 0\end{bmatrix}$, we have $\det(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})=\det\!\begin{bmatrix}1 & -a \\ a & 1\end{bmatrix}=1+a^2>0$. Therefore, the paper’s use of the Cayley parameterization on skew-symmetric blocks reliably yields orthogonal factors, and the composite product of such factors in BOFT remains orthogonal while staying computationally well-defined.
