In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', replacing BOFT's structured product of sparse orthogonal factors with an unstructured dense orthogonal matrix while keeping the same number of effective parameters is likely to result in worse generalization. The core reason is that BOFT deliberately constrains the hypothesis class to a structured subset of the orthogonal group via butterfly factorization: it composes a dense orthogonal transform as a product of \(m\) sparse, orthogonal components \(\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}\), resulting in \(\bm{R}(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}\) with only \(\mathcal{O}(d \log d)\) parameters when \(m=\log d,b=2\). This structure encodes an inductive bias shared by many classic fast linear transforms (e.g., FFT, Hadamard), which the paper argues “benefits generalizability and prevents overfitting.” In contrast, an “unstructured” dense orthogonal parameterization with the same parameter budget must fix most entries or tie them in ad hoc ways, yielding a hypothesis class whose sparsity/parameter sharing is not aligned with meaningful information-exchange topologies. The information transmission view clarifies this distinction: butterfly graphs achieve dense end-to-end connectivity with the minimal number of trainable edges subject to orthogonality, whereas an unstructured scheme with the same budget cannot guarantee efficient, well-distributed paths and thus tends to either underfit globally or overfit locally.

Moreover, BOFT provides a smoother interpolation between expressivity and regularity than both full orthogonal finetuning and block-diagonal OFT: varying \(m\) and \(b\) controls the size of the hypothesis class while preserving the spectral norm of \(\bm{W}^0\) (since \(\bm{R}\) is orthogonal, \(\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2\)). The paper’s empirical and theoretical discussion shows that increasing \(m\) (more factors) with small \(b\) improves parameter-efficiency and approximation quality of dense orthogonal transforms without exploding degrees of freedom, and Theorem 1 indicates that butterfly compositions can approach the full orthogonal group when needed. An unstructured dense parametrization constrained to the same number of “effective parameters” lacks this principled control: it either becomes too rigid (because most entries are frozen) or too unconstrained in the wrong places, missing the beneficial hierarchy of local \(2\times 2\) rotations that transmit information globally. Hence, under equal parameter budgets, BOFT’s structured factorization induces a favorable inductive bias and better balances expressivity vs. regularity, which the paper identifies as the key to generalization in finetuning.
