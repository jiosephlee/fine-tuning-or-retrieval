# LoRA vs OFT vs BOFT: Which PEFT Method Should You Pick?

In the era of foundation models with billions or even trillions of parameters, training from scratch is no longer an option for most practitioners. Instead, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has emerged as the go-to strategy for adapting massive pre-trained models to downstream tasks without blowing up memory or compute budgets. Among the many PEFT methods, three stand out for their simplicity and effectiveness: LoRA, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), and the newly proposed Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT). In this post, we’ll walk through the strengths and trade-offs of each approach, so you can decide which one best fits your project’s accuracy, stability, hardware, and deployment needs.

## A Quick Tour of the Three PEFT Champions

Before diving into comparisons, let’s briefly recap what each method does under the hood.

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) updates a pre-trained weight matrix \(W^0\) by adding a product of two low-rank matrices. In practice you keep \(W^0\) frozen and learn small matrices \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}\), so that during fine-tuning the model computes
\[
W = W^0 + BA.
\]
By choosing a modest rank \(r\), LoRA drastically reduces the number of extra parameters, keeps memory overhead low, and integrates seamlessly with existing training pipelines.

OFT (Orthogonal Finetuning) takes a different tack. Instead of an additive update, OFT multiplies the pre-trained weights by an orthogonal matrix \(R\):
\[
W = R\,W^0,
\]
where \(R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) satisfies \(R^\top R = I\). This multiplicative approach preserves pairwise angles between neurons—essentially respecting the geometric structure learned during pre-training. To keep the parameter count manageable, OFT typically enforces a block-diagonal form on \(R\). Each diagonal block is a small orthogonal matrix, which reduces trainable parameters but limits expressiveness across blocks.

BOFT (Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning) builds on the OFT insight but shatters the block-diagonal constraint. It factorizes the dense orthogonal transform \(R\) into a product of sparse, orthogonal “butterfly” components:
\[
R = B_m \, B_{m-1} \cdots B_1,
\]
where each \(B_i\) has \(\mathcal O(d)\) nonzero entries arranged in a butterfly pattern inspired by the Cooley–Tukey FFT algorithm. By chaining \(\mathcal O(\log d)\) such sparse matrices, BOFT achieves a full-rank, dense transformation using just \(\mathcal O(d\log d)\) parameters.

## What to Consider When Choosing a PEFT Method

Every project has its own priorities—whether it’s squeezing into tight GPU memory, maximizing final accuracy, ensuring stable training, or minimizing inference latency. Here’s how these three methods stack up:

Accuracy and Expressivity  
LoRA’s low-rank update is very flexible for many tasks, but it may struggle when the adaptation needed is more than a small subspace shift.  
OFT’s orthogonal multiplier often yields improved stability and generalization because it preserves the spectral norm of \(W^0\). However, its block-diagonal shortcut prevents cross-block interactions, which can limit performance on tasks requiring global coordination.  
BOFT recovers much of OFT’s stability benefits while restoring dense connectivity across all dimensions. The butterfly factorization creates a smooth path from the identity matrix to fully expressive orthogonal transforms, so BOFT can match or exceed OFT accuracy with far fewer parameters than a full-matrix OFT.

Training Stability  
LoRA typically converges reliably, but it does not control the spectral norm of the adapted weights, which can occasionally lead to instability in very deep networks.  
OFT guarantees that the spectral norm remains unchanged throughout fine-tuning, promoting smoother gradients and more predictable training.  
BOFT inherits OFT’s spectral-norm preservation and often shows just as stable behavior, with the added benefit of a structured parameter space that discourages overfitting.

Memory Footprint and VRAM  
Because LoRA adds only \(2dr\) parameters (with \(r\ll d\)), it has the smallest memory overhead. For ultra-large models or when GPU memory is the limiting factor, LoRA is often the first choice.  
OFT with full orthogonal matrices needs \(\mathcal O(d^2)\) parameters, which is generally impractical. Its block-diagonal variant reduces this to \(\mathcal O}(bd)\) if each block is size \(b\times b\), but you pay for that by giving up cross-block adaptability.  
BOFT’s parameter count sits between LoRA and full OFT, at \(\mathcal O(d\log d)\) for the classic butterfly instantiation. This is often only a modest multiple of LoRA’s footprint while offering richer adaptation.

Computation and Latency  
In both LoRA and OFT, the fine-tuning update adds minimal inference cost: you either add a small rank-\(r\) product or multiply by a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix. BOFT, in theory, chains several sparse multiplications—yet after fine-tuning you can collapse the butterfly factors into a single dense \(R\) and fold it into the original weights. As a result, BOFT adds no extra inference latency. During training, the repeated sparse multiplications introduce a small overhead, but in practice it remains quite manageable.

## So, Which Method Is Right for You?

• If your primary goal is to squeeze into the smallest possible memory budget and you’re adapting a model to a task where a low-rank shift suffices, LoRA is a solid, battle-tested choice.  
• If you need rock-solid training stability or you care deeply about preserving the pretrained model’s geometry—yet you can afford a bit more memory—OFT with moderate block sizes can deliver.  
• If you want the best of both worlds—nearly the spectral stability and generalizability of OFT combined with a lean parameter footprint—you should give BOFT a try. Its \(\mathcal O(d\log d)\) scaling hits a sweet spot between flexibility and efficiency, and collapsing the learned transform for inference keeps deployment straightforward.

In practice, we often find that BOFT matches or surpasses state-of-the-art PEFT baselines across vision, language, and generative modeling tasks. But every project is unique, so consider your task complexity, hardware constraints, and latency requirements when making your choice. Whichever method you pick, PEFT empowers you to leverage huge pre-trained models without the prohibitive costs of full retraining—and that’s a win for everyone.
