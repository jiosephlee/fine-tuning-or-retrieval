According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', preserving the spectral norm (i.e., the largest singular value $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$) via orthogonal finetuning primarily benefits training by improving stability and generalization. This is because OFT/BOFT update weights multiplicatively as $(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)$ with $\bm{R}$ orthogonal, so by the SVD $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ we have $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0=(\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ where $(\bm{R}\bm{U})$ remains orthogonal and thus the singular values—and in particular the largest one, $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$—are exactly preserved; maintaining the spectral norm keeps the layer’s Lipschitz constant controlled, which prevents exploding/vanishing behavior and has been empirically and theoretically tied to more stable optimization and better generalization (as in spectral normalization), so orthogonal finetuning inherits these benefits while adapting the model to new tasks.
