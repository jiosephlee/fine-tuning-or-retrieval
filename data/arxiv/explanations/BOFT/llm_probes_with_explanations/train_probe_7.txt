According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the orthogonality constraint introduces a common sparsity-pattern effect between matrix entries, e.g., in a $2\times 2$ orthogonal matrix a zero at position $(1,1)$ implies a zero at position $(2,2)$, namely paired zeros. This follows from the orthogonality condition that rows and columns are orthonormal: let $\bm{O}=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{bmatrix}$ with $\bm{O}^\top\bm{O}=\bm{I}$ and suppose $a=0$; then column 1 has norm $1$, so $c\neq 0$ with $|c|=1$, and column 2 has norm $1$, so $b\neq 0$ with $|b|=1$, while column orthogonality gives $a b + c d=0\Rightarrow c d=0$, hence $d=0$ since $c\neq 0$, yielding the paired zero at $(2,2)$; equivalently, the same conclusion follows from row orthogonality $a c + b d=0$ with $a=0$, which forces $d=0$ because $b\neq 0$. In the paper’s information-transmission view, this demonstrates that orthogonality induces dependencies among edges (nonzeros) across adjacent levels: enforcing orthogonality not only reduces degrees of freedom but also couples sparsity patterns so that removing one entry (edge) can force the removal of another to maintain orthonormality and full rank, explaining why paired-zero phenomena arise in small blocks and propagate in factorized sparse constructions.
