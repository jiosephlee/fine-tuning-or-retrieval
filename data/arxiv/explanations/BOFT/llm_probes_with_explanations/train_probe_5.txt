In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors attribute OFT's improved training stability compared to LoRA to orthogonality. Specifically, OFT updates a pretrained layer multiplicatively as \(\bm{W}'=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\) with \(\bm{R}\) constrained to be orthogonal (maintained via Cayley parameterization and identity initialization), which preserves pairwise angles between neurons and, crucially, the spectral norm of the layer: if \(\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top\), then \(\bm{W}'=(\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top\) and hence \(\|\bm{W}'\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2\); the paper cites well-established links between spectral norm preservation and improved training stability/generalization. By contrast, LoRA applies an additive low-rank update \(\bm{W}'=\bm{W}^0+\bm{A}\bm{B}^\top\), which perturbs singular values and can increase \(\|\bm{W}'\|_2\) unless carefully regularized, thereby potentially destabilizing optimization, so the orthogonality constraint in OFT (and BOFT) provides a principled mechanism for stability by keeping the spectrum controlled while enabling adaptation.
