According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the proposed multiplicative Dropout randomly replaces a percentage of the butterfly components and diagonal blocks in each butterfly component with identity matrices $\bm{I}$, relying on the implicit property that replacing these blocks by $\bm{I}$ keeps the model valid, namely that this operation preserves orthogonality. This is sensible because BOFT parameterizes the finetuning transform as a product of orthogonal butterfly components, $\bm{R}(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$, and the identity matrix is itself orthogonal (since $\bm{I}^\top \bm{I}=\bm{I}$), so replacing any factor by $\bm{I}$ keeps the product orthogonal due to closure under multiplication: if $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$ are orthogonal, then $(\bm{A}\bm{B})^\top(\bm{A}\bm{B})=\bm{B}^\top\bm{A}^\top\bm{A}\bm{B}=\bm{B}^\top\bm{B}=\bm{I}$. Moreover, each butterfly component can be permuted into a block-diagonal matrix with independent $2b\times 2b$ orthogonal blocks; replacing some of these diagonal blocks by $\bm{I}_{2b}$ yields another block-diagonal matrix whose blocks remain orthogonal, hence the entire component remains orthogonal. Consequently, stochastic replacement at the component level ($\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}\mapsto \bm{I}$) or at the block level within a component preserves orthogonality of every modified factor and thus of the overall $\bm{R}(m,b)$, keeping the model’s orthogonality constraint intact during Dropout.
