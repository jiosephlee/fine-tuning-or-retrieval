In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors employ the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ where $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric; for the Cayley parameterization to be valid, the implicit mathematical condition on $\bm{Q}$ that must hold is that $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is invertible. This condition is required because the Cayley map is defined via the inverse of $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$, and it guarantees orthogonality by the identity $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R}=\bm{I}$ when $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$ and $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ is invertible: indeed, writing $\bm{R}^\top=((\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1})^\top(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top=(\bm{I}-\bm{Q}^\top)^{-1}(\bm{I}+\bm{Q}^\top)=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$, we get $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$, which relies on $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ existing; moreover, for real skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$ the eigenvalues are purely imaginary, so $\lambda(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})=1-i\alpha\neq 0$, implying $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is automatically invertible and making the parameterization well-defined in their setting (and trivially satisfied at their identity initialization $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$).
