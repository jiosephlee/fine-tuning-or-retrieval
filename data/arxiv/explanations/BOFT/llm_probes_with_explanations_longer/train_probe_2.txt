In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the unstated assumption about pretrained representations that justifies preserving pairwise angles between neurons is that angles encode semantics. The method explicitly aims to “preserve the relational information of the weight matrices, i.e. the pairwise angles between neurons,” and implements finetuning as a left multiplication by an orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \) so that for any two neuron vectors (columns) \( \bm{w}_i, \bm{w}_j \) of \( \bm{W}^0 \), the inner products and norms—and therefore the angles—are unchanged: \( ( \bm{R}\bm{w}_i )^\top ( \bm{R}\bm{w}_j ) = \bm{w}_i^\top \bm{w}_j \) and \( \| \bm{R}\bm{w}_i \|_2 = \| \bm{w}_i \|_2 \). Since the cosine of the angle is \( \cos \theta_{ij} = \frac{\bm{w}_i^\top \bm{w}_j}{\|\bm{w}_i\|_2 \|\bm{w}_j\|_2} \), orthogonal finetuning ensures \( \theta_{ij} \) is invariant under the update. This design choice is motivated by prior work on hyperspherical energy and generalization, where angular relationships (cosine similarity) are treated as the geometry that captures semantic proximity; preserving this geometry during adaptation helps retain the pretrained semantic structure while allowing task-specific rotations. In large foundation models, embeddings are typically compared via cosine similarity, and many objectives (e.g., contrastive, normalized temperature-scaled losses) normalize features so that semantics are reflected principally in angles. Thus, assuming that angles encode semantics explains why protecting pairwise angles is a principled way to conserve pretrained knowledge.

This assumption also aligns with the paper’s stability and generalization arguments. Orthogonal updates preserve spectral properties of \( \bm{W}^0 \) (e.g., the spectral norm via \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) and \( \bm{R}\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) sharing the same largest singular value), which reduces distortion of the learned representation geometry. Writing the finetuned score for input \( \bm{x} \) and neuron \( i \) as a bilinear similarity \( \bm{w}^0_i{}^\top \bm{R}\bm{x} \), the orthogonality constraint on \( \bm{R} \) acts as a strong regularizer that maintains the angular relations among neurons while rotating inputs in a way consistent with the pretrained metric. Because semantic neighborhoods in embedding spaces are determined by angular proximity, maintaining pairwise angles among neurons preserves these neighborhoods, explaining why the paper centers its finetuning paradigm on orthogonal transformations that keep angles intact.
