In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', when BOFT multiplies the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$ on the left by an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$, the property of $\bm{W}^0$ that is preserved is its spectral norm. This follows from the unitary invariance of singular values: write the singular value decomposition $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ with $\bm{U},\bm{V}$ orthogonal and $\bm{\Sigma}$ diagonal with nonnegative entries. Then BOFT produces $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0=(\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$, where $\bm{R}\bm{U}$ remains orthogonal because $\bm{R}$ and $\bm{U}$ are orthogonal. Hence the singular values of $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ equal those of $\bm{W}^0$, so the largest singular value—i.e., the spectral norm $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$—is unchanged: $\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$. Equivalently, since $\|\bm{R}\|_2=1$ for any orthogonal $\bm{R}$, we have $\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2\le\|\bm{R}\|_2\|\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$, and the SVD argument shows equality holds.

This preservation is also geometrically sensible given the goals of orthogonal finetuning: left-multiplying by $\bm{R}$ rotates or reflects the row space of $\bm{W}^0$ without scaling it, so the Lipschitz constant of the linear map (its spectral norm) remains the same. Because orthogonal transformations preserve lengths and angles, $\bm{R}$ changes the left singular vectors but leaves the singular values in $\bm{\Sigma}$ intact. The paper emphasizes this stability benefit—keeping $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$ fixed is known to aid training stability and generalization—while still allowing BOFT to adjust neuron orientations via $\bm{R}$ in a parameter-efficient, structured way.
