According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', given identity initialization of all components, BOFT's initial effect on the pretrained layer's outputs is no change. BOFT reparameterizes the finetuned weights as a left-multiplicative orthogonal transform of the frozen pretrained weights, i.e., for a pretrained layer with weights \( \bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n} \), the forward pass is modified from \( \bm{z} = (\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x} \) to \( \bm{z} = (\bm{R}(m,b)\,\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x} \), where \( \bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \) is a product of orthogonal butterfly components. The paper specifies identity initialization for all butterfly components, which means each \( \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} = \bm{I}_d \), and thus \( \bm{R}(m,b) = \bm{I}_d \). Consequently, the transformed forward pass satisfies \( (\bm{R}(m,b)\,\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x} = (\bm{I}_d \bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x} = (\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x} \), yielding no change to the outputs at initialization. Equivalently, under Cayley parameterization \( \bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1} \) with a skew-symmetric \( \bm{Q} \), setting \( \bm{Q} = \bm{0} \) makes \( \bm{R} = \bm{I} \), again implying zero initial effect.

This identity initialization is both principled and consistent with the finetuning philosophy of starting from the exact pretrained behavior. Because BOFT’s update is multiplicative and orthogonal, initializing at \( \bm{R} = \bm{I} \) preserves the spectral properties of \( \bm{W}^0 \) at the start: if \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), then \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}'\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) for some orthogonal \( \bm{U}' \), keeping the largest singular value unchanged and avoiding destabilizing shifts in the layer’s gain. As the training proceeds, deviations from identity in the butterfly components introduce controlled, structured changes while maintaining orthogonality, but at initialization the product structure and the Cayley-based parameterization ensure that the finetuned layer is behaviorally identical to the pretrained layer.
