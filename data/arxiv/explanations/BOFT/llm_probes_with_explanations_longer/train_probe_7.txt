According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the orthogonality constraint introduces a common sparsity-pattern effect between matrix entries, e.g., in a 2×2 orthogonal matrix a zero at position (1,1) implies a zero at position (2,2), namely paired zeros. This follows directly from the orthonormality of rows and columns. Let a general 2×2 orthogonal matrix be written as
$\bm{R}=\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ with $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R}=\bm{I}$. If $a=0$, then the unit-norm constraint on the first column gives $a^2+c^2=1$, hence $c=\pm 1$. Orthogonality of the two columns requires $a b + c d = 0$, which simplifies to $c d = 0$, and since $c\neq 0$, we must have $d=0$. Thus the zero at $(1,1)$ forces a zero at $(2,2)$, and the nonzeros lie on the anti-diagonal with magnitudes fixed by the unit-norm constraints, yielding matrices like $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & \pm 1 \\ \pm 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ (up to signs). Equivalently, viewing 2×2 orthogonal matrices as rotations/reflections, $a=0$ corresponds to a $\pm \frac{\pi}{2}$ rotation or a reflection that swaps axes, both of which have zeros on the diagonal.

This “paired zeros” phenomenon is representative of the dependency structure orthogonality imposes on sparsity patterns, which the paper frames via its information transmission viewpoint. Orthogonality requires each factor to implement a bijection between nodes at adjacent levels (each column is a unit vector orthogonal to the others), effectively constraining admissible edges in the grid graph. In the 2×2 case, setting $R_{11}=0$ removes one edge; maintaining a full-rank, orthonormal mapping forces the corresponding diagonal edge at $(2,2)$ to vanish as well, pushing the information flow to the anti-diagonal. More generally, such constraints mean that zeros cannot be chosen independently: missing entries in one position propagate to structurally coupled positions to preserve column/row orthogonality, which is why the paper emphasizes that orthogonality can “add or remove” edges beyond naïve sparsity choices and induce shared sparsity patterns across entries.
