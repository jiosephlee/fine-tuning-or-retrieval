In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors attribute OFT's improved training stability compared to LoRA to orthogonality. The key observation is that OFT updates a pretrained layer by left-multiplying an orthogonal matrix, which exactly preserves important geometric and spectral properties of the original weights. Writing the pretrained weights as a singular value decomposition, \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), OFT produces finetuned weights \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) with \(\bm{R}\) orthogonal. Because left-multiplication by an orthogonal matrix simply rotates the left singular vectors, it leaves the singular values unchanged: \( \|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2 = \|\bm{W}^0\|_2 \). This exact preservation of the spectral norm maintains the layer’s Lipschitz constant, which has been shown to promote stable optimization and generalization. Moreover, orthogonality preserves pairwise angles between neuron weight vectors, so the “hyperspherical” structure learned during pretraining is not distorted, reducing the risk of destabilizing distribution shifts during finetuning.

By contrast, LoRA adds a low-rank perturbation, producing \( \bm{W} = \bm{W}^0 + \bm{A}\bm{B}^\top \), which can change the singular values of the layer—potentially increasing \( \|\bm{W}\|_2 \) and amplifying activations and gradients. Even with small learning rates or zero initialization, the additive update can drift the spectrum and angular relationships in ways that are hard to control, leading to training instabilities such as exploding or vanishing gradients. In OFT, orthogonality is enforced throughout training (e.g., via Cayley parameterization \( \bm{R} = (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} \) with \( \bm{Q} = -\bm{Q}^\top \)), guaranteeing that these stabilizing invariants—spectral norm and neuron-angle preservation—hold at every step. This principled constraint explains why OFT (and its butterfly-parameterized BOFT variant) exhibits more robust and stable finetuning dynamics than additive low-rank approaches like LoRA.
