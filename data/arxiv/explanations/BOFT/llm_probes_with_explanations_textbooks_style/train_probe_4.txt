Orthogonality-Preserving Multiplicative Dropout in Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning

# Background: Orthogonal Finetuning and the Need for Parameter Efficiency

Orthogonal finetuning aims to adapt a pretrained model by transforming its neurons with an orthogonal matrix. Given a pretrained linear layer with weights $\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$, orthogonal finetuning reparameterizes the finetuned weights as $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$, where $\bm{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is orthogonal. The forward pass changes from $\bm{z} = (\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x}$ to $\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x}$ for input $\bm{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and output $\bm{z} \in \mathbb{R}^n$. This multiplicative update preserves pairwise angles between neurons and the spectral norm of $\bm{W}^0$, thereby maintaining useful pretrained structure while enabling controlled adaptation.

To preserve orthogonality during training, $\bm{R}$ is parameterized via a Cayley transform as $\bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}$ with a skew-symmetric matrix $\bm{Q} = -\bm{Q}^\top$. Identity initialization is achieved by initializing $\bm{Q}$ to zero, which yields $\bm{R} = \bm{I}$ at the start of finetuning.

A central challenge is parameter efficiency. A dense $d$-dimensional orthogonal matrix entails $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters, which can be prohibitive. Earlier work reduced parameters with a block-diagonal structure, at the cost of expressivity and an inflexible sparsity pattern. The method of Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) resolves this by composing a dense orthogonal transform as a product of multiple sparse orthogonal factors, each designed to promote efficient “information transmission” across dimensions while maintaining orthogonality and significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters.

# Orthogonal Butterfly Factorization

BOFT builds an orthogonal $\bm{R}$ as a product of $m$ sparse orthogonal butterfly components, each of which can be made orthogonal while being highly structured and parameter-efficient. The construction starts from a butterfly factor. For a power-of-two $k \geq 2$, define the butterfly factor
\[
\bm{B}^F(k) =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathrm{diag}(\bm{d}_1) & \mathrm{diag}(\bm{d}_2) \\
\mathrm{diag}(\bm{d}_3) & \mathrm{diag}(\bm{d}_4)
\end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k},
\]
where $\bm{d}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{k/2}$ for $i \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. A $d$-dimensional butterfly matrix $\bm{B}(d) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ for $d=2^N$ is then formed recursively by interleaving butterfly factors with block-diagonal recursion,
\[
\bm{B}(d) = \tilde{\bm{B}}(d,d)\begin{bmatrix}
\bm{B}_1(\frac{d}{2}) & \bm{0} \\
\bm{0} & \bm{B}_2(\frac{d}{2})
\end{bmatrix}
= \tilde{\bm{B}}(d,d)\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,\tfrac{d}{2})\cdots \tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2),
\]
where each $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,k)$ is block-diagonal with blocks of size $k$, namely
\[
\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,k) = \mathrm{diag}\big(\bm{B}^F_1(k), \ldots, \bm{B}^F_{d/k}(k)\big).
\]
To ensure orthogonality, each block in $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2)$ is parameterized as a $2 \times 2$ orthogonal matrix; since other butterfly components are blockwise permutations of $\tilde{\bm{B}}(d,2)$, they are likewise made orthogonal by parameterizing each block orthogonally. This yields an orthogonal parameterization constructed from many $2 \times 2$ orthogonal blocks.

BOFT further generalizes to block butterfly components with block size $b \geq 2$. In this case, each scalar in the butterfly factor is replaced with a $b \times b$ block, and each $2b \times 2b$ block is parameterized as an orthogonal matrix. The permutation structure ensures that all higher-level butterfly components inherit an orthogonal block structure from $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b(d,2)$.

The overall finetuning transform is a product
\[
\bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)},
\]
where we use the shorthand $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ for $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b(d,2^{m-i+1})$, and each factor is orthogonal, that is
\[
\big(\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,j)}\big)^\top \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,j)} = \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,j)} \big(\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,j)}\big)^\top = \bm{I}_d,\quad \forall j \in \{1,\ldots,m\}.
\]
The forward pass is then
\[
\bm{z} = \big(\bm{R}(m,b)\bm{W}^0\big)^\top \bm{x}.
\]
When $m=1$, BOFT reduces to block-diagonal OFT with block size $b$. When $m=\log \tfrac{2d}{b}$ and $b$ is fixed, the product yields a dense $\bm{R}$ while using only $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ parameters for $b=2$, in sharp contrast to the $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters required by an unconstrained dense orthogonal matrix.

# Identity Initialization and Orthogonality Preservation

BOFT initializes each butterfly component to the identity transform so that $\bm{R}(m,b)$ starts as $\bm{I}$. This is achieved by constructing each orthogonal block with a Cayley parameterization and initializing the corresponding skew-symmetric matrices to zero. Because each factor is initialized to $\bm{I}_d$ and remains orthogonal during optimization, the product remains orthogonal throughout training.

A key property repeatedly used in BOFT is closure of the orthogonal group under multiplication. If $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$ are orthogonal, then so is $\bm{A}\bm{B}$ because
\[
(\bm{A}\bm{B})^\top (\bm{A}\bm{B}) = \bm{B}^\top \bm{A}^\top \bm{A}\bm{B} = \bm{B}^\top \bm{B} = \bm{I},
\]
and similarly $(\bm{A}\bm{B})(\bm{A}\bm{B})^\top = \bm{I}$. The identity matrix $\bm{I}$ is orthogonal as well. Consequently, any product that replaces some orthogonal factors by identities remains orthogonal. This observation underpins the design of multiplicative Dropout in BOFT.

# Multiplicative Dropout: Definition and Construction

Conventional Dropout is expressed as an additive masking of activations or parameters. In BOFT, however, the finetuning transform is multiplicative: $\bm{R}(m,b)$ multiplies the pretrained weights on the left. An additive masking does not naturally align with this structure. Instead, BOFT introduces a multiplicative Dropout that acts directly on the multiplicative factors and their blocks.

Each butterfly component $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ can be permuted into a block-diagonal matrix with $2b \times 2b$ orthogonal blocks. Multiplicative Dropout proceeds in two stages during training:

1. It randomly selects a fraction $p_1$ of the butterfly components among the $m$ factors.
2. Within each selected butterfly component, it randomly selects a fraction $p_2$ of its $2b \times 2b$ diagonal blocks.

The selected components and blocks are then replaced by identity matrices. Concretely, if a factor is selected, we set $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \leftarrow \bm{I}_d$; if only some blocks are selected, we set those $2b \times 2b$ blocks to $\bm{I}_{2b}$, leaving the other blocks unchanged. Since identity matrices are orthogonal, this operation preserves the orthogonality of each affected factor and hence preserves the orthogonality of the entire product $\bm{R}(m,b)$.

The overall finetuning transform with Dropout is still a product of orthogonal matrices and identities. Writing the post-Dropout factors as $\widehat{\bm{B}}_i$ with either $\widehat{\bm{B}}_i = \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ (unchanged), $\widehat{\bm{B}}_i = \bm{I}_d$ (entire factor dropped), or a block-diagonal orthogonal where some $2b \times 2b$ blocks have been replaced by $\bm{I}_{2b}$, we have
\[
\bm{R}_{\mathrm{drop}}(m,b) = \widehat{\bm{B}}_m \cdots \widehat{\bm{B}}_1,
\]
and $\bm{R}_{\mathrm{drop}}(m,b)$ remains orthogonal because it is a product of orthogonal matrices. The forward pass is thus
\[
\bm{z} = \big(\bm{R}_{\mathrm{drop}}(m,b)\bm{W}^0\big)^\top \bm{x}.
\]

This multiplicative Dropout is tailored to the structure of BOFT: it directly toggles multiplicative transformations on or off by replacing them with identities. The operation is simple to implement given the block-orthogonal structure of each butterfly component and preserves the central orthogonality constraint by construction.

# Why Identity Replacement Preserves Validity

The validity of BOFT requires that the learned transform be orthogonal at all times. Multiplicative Dropout preserves this property because it replaces orthogonal factors or orthogonal sub-blocks with identity matrices, which are also orthogonal. There are two granularity levels to consider.

First, replacing an entire butterfly component $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ with $\bm{I}_d$ trivially preserves orthogonality of that factor. Since the product of orthogonal matrices is orthogonal, and $\bm{I}_d$ is orthogonal, the overall transform remains orthogonal.

Second, replacing some $2b \times 2b$ diagonal blocks inside a factor with $\bm{I}_{2b}$ maintains orthogonality of the factor itself. Each butterfly component is block-diagonal after permutation into $2b \times 2b$ orthogonal blocks. A block-diagonal matrix is orthogonal if and only if each diagonal block is orthogonal. Replacing some diagonal blocks by $\bm{I}_{2b}$ therefore preserves the orthogonality of that factor. Consequently, the product over factors remains orthogonal as well.

This argument relies only on two facts encoded in BOFT’s design: the blockwise orthogonality of butterfly components and the closure of orthogonal matrices under block-diagonal composition and multiplication. Because multiplicative Dropout performs identity replacements at precisely these structurally orthogonal sites, it preserves all orthogonality constraints required by the method.

# Intuition Through the Information Transmission View

BOFT can be interpreted as transmitting information across a $d \times (m+1)$ grid induced by factorization $\bm{R} = \bm{B}_m \cdots \bm{B}_1$. Each factor $\bm{B}_i$ defines how information flows between adjacent levels. The butterfly topology achieves dense end-to-end connectivity using only $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ nonzero entries across the factors. In this view, multiplicative Dropout temporarily disables some transmission channels by replacing their transforms with identities. Because identity maps still pass information straight through without distortion, global connectivity remains intact across the levels, and the orthogonality of the transform is preserved.

This perspective highlights that multiplicative Dropout regularizes the finetuning transform by stochastically simplifying local exchanges while never breaking the global orthogonal structure. By selectively setting some exchanges to identities, the method constrains the instantaneous hypothesis space in a way that coheres with the butterfly factorization, yet it maintains the end-to-end integrity of the information routing across the grid.

# Practical Implications for Finetuning

Multiplicative Dropout complements the identity initialization and orthogonal parameterization of BOFT. At the start of finetuning, all butterfly components are initialized to identities, so $\bm{R}(m,b) = \bm{I}_d$. As training progresses, the Cayley-parameterized blocks within each butterfly component become nontrivial orthogonal transforms. Multiplicative Dropout periodically resets selected components or selected $2b \times 2b$ blocks within components back to identity during training. Because this substitution preserves orthogonality and because each factor is designed to be independently orthogonal, the operation is guaranteed to keep the model within the valid family of orthogonal transforms at all times.

Moreover, the stochastic identity replacements create a smoother interpolation between expressive transforms and the regularizing identity. BOFT already provides a tunable balance between expressivity and regularity through the choice of depth $m$ and block size $b$. Multiplicative Dropout further moderates the effective complexity by temporarily simplifying parts of the transform. Crucially, none of these simplifications violate the central orthogonality constraint that underpins the stability and generalization benefits of orthogonal finetuning.

In summary, BOFT’s multiplicative Dropout is enabled by the orthogonal block structure of butterfly components and by the algebraic closure properties of orthogonal matrices. Replacing selected butterfly components and selected diagonal blocks with identity matrices $\bm{I}$ preserves orthogonality by construction, ensuring that the finetuning transform remains valid throughout training while providing a principled form of regularization aligned with the multiplicative nature of orthogonal finetuning.
