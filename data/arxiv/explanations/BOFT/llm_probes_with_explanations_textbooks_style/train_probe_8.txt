Chapter Title: Orthogonal Finetuning, Butterfly Factorization, and the Cayley Parameterization

# Foundations of Orthogonal Finetuning

Large pretrained models are costly to train from scratch, so finetuning is crucial for adapting them to new tasks while preserving the knowledge encoded during pretraining. A central idea behind orthogonal finetuning is to preserve the relational structure of neurons inside each layer. Concretely, orthogonal finetuning transforms all neurons in the same layer by a shared orthogonal matrix, which preserves pairwise angles between neuron vectors. This preservation is important because the semantic structure learned in pretraining is carried by those geometric relationships, and keeping them intact discourages destructive updates.

In orthogonal finetuning, a pretrained linear layer with weight matrix $\bm{W}^0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$ is updated multiplicatively. The finetuned forward pass replaces $\bm{z}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$ with $\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$, where $\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is an orthogonal matrix and $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and $\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ are input and output vectors, respectively. Orthogonality ensures that the spectral norm of $\bm{W}^0$ is preserved and the geometry of neurons remains stable. To maintain this property throughout training, the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ is constructed with a specific parameterization that guarantees orthogonality for all parameter values during optimization.

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) uses the Cayley parameterization of $\bm{R}$, which enables direct optimization in the space of orthogonal matrices. OFT further reduces the number of trainable parameters with a block-diagonal orthogonal structure, at the cost of expressivity and a fixed sparsity pattern. The Orthogonal Butterfly method (BOFT) generalizes OFT by composing $\bm{R}$ from a product of sparse orthogonal factors with butterfly structure, achieving dense orthogonal matrices with significantly fewer parameters.

# The Cayley Parameterization and Skew-Symmetric Structure

The Cayley parameterization is employed in OFT and BOFT to ensure orthogonality. It defines an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ using a skew-symmetric auxiliary matrix $\bm{Q}$ as
\[
\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1},
\]
where $\bm{Q}$ satisfies $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$ and $\bm{I}$ is the identity matrix. This parameterization is attractive because it converts the constrained optimization of orthogonal matrices into an unconstrained optimization over skew-symmetric matrices $\bm{Q}$. During finetuning, one optimizes the entries of $\bm{Q}$, and the above formula maps those parameters to an orthogonal $\bm{R}$ at every step.

In practice, two properties are essential for this parameterization in the context of finetuning. First, $\bm{R}$ should begin as the identity matrix to avoid any initial deviation from the pretrained model. This is achieved by zero-initializing $\bm{Q}$, since substituting $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$ yields $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$. Second, $\bm{R}$ must remain orthogonal during training. The skew-symmetric constraint $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$ is critical here, and it is enforced for all learnable components, whether they are full matrices, block-diagonal matrices, or structured butterfly components.

In BOFT, the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ is constructed as a product of multiple orthogonal components, each of which is parameterized via the Cayley transform (or, when the component is $2\times 2$, via a $2$-dimensional rotation, which is consistent with the Cayley approach). Even when these components are block-diagonal, the same principle applies: each block is parameterized by a skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$ and mapped to an orthogonal block via the Cayley formula. This compositional view is essential to achieving parameter efficiency while maintaining orthogonality.

# Why Invertibility of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ Is Required

The Cayley parameterization uses $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$. For this expression to be well-defined, the matrix $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ must be invertible. This is an implicit but necessary mathematical condition for the parameterization to be valid. If $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ were not invertible, the inverse would not exist, and the mapping from $\bm{Q}$ to $\bm{R}$ would be undefined. Therefore, the validity of the Cayley parameterization hinges on ensuring $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is invertible at every step of training.

This requirement is naturally satisfied under the standard finetuning practice in OFT and BOFT. Both methods initialize the orthogonal matrices as identity matrices by zero-initializing their skew-symmetric parameters. At initialization, $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$ implies $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}=\bm{I}$, which is invertible. During optimization, the finetuning procedure maintains the skew-symmetric structure of $\bm{Q}$ and uses suitably structured updates (for example, manipulating $2\times 2$ orthogonal blocks), which produce well-defined orthogonal matrices. In this setup, the multiplicative structure of the BOFT matrix $\bm{R}$, composed of individual orthogonal factors, also relies on the invertibility of $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ in each factor’s Cayley parameterization.

In summary, the Cayley parameterization guarantees orthogonality through a skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$, and the use of the inverse explicitly requires $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ to be invertible. This condition ensures the mapping is defined at initialization and throughout finetuning.

# Identity Initialization and Training Stability

Finetuning methods aim to start exactly from the pretrained model to avoid unnecessary drift at the beginning of training. In OFT and BOFT, this is accomplished by identity initialization of the orthogonal parameters. Under the Cayley parameterization, identity initialization corresponds to setting $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$. Substituting $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$ into
\[
\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}
\]
gives $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$. This initialization has two benefits. First, it preserves the exact pretrained behavior at the start of finetuning. Second, it trivially satisfies the invertibility requirement because $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}=\bm{I}$ at initialization.

Training stability follows from maintaining orthogonality throughout finetuning. Orthogonal updates preserve the largest singular value of $\bm{W}^0$ and therefore its spectral norm. Writing a singular value decomposition $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ with orthogonal $\bm{U}$ and $\bm{V}$ and diagonal $\bm{\Sigma}$, the finetuned weights become $\bm{R}\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$. Left-multiplication by an orthogonal $\bm{R}$ does not change the largest singular value, thereby preserving the spectral norm. This preservation has been associated with improved training stability and generalization. Identity initialization and orthogonal updates thus work together to provide a stable and principled finetuning path.

# Sparse Orthogonal Factorization and Butterfly Components

While a full $d\times d$ orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ provides maximum expressivity, it entails $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters. To improve parameter efficiency without sacrificing the ability to construct dense orthogonal matrices, BOFT factorizes $\bm{R}$ into a product of sparse orthogonal components with butterfly structure. The central insight is to trade space (parameters) for time (computation): instead of learning a monolithic dense orthogonal matrix, learn multiple sparse orthogonal matrices whose product is dense.

The butterfly structure originates from fast linear transforms and induces a connectivity pattern that efficiently transmits information across dimensions. BOFT constructs $\bm{R}(m,b)$ as a product of $m$ orthogonal butterfly components, each of which can be permuted into block-diagonal form with $2b\times 2b$ orthogonal blocks. Each block is parameterized with the Cayley transform. This factorization enables dense connectivity using only $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ parameters in the special case of butterfly matrices with $m=\log d$ and $b=2$, and more generally $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ effective parameters for a layer of size $d\times n$.

The information transmission framework clarifies why butterfly factorization is effective. Representing $\bm{R}=\bm{B}_m\bm{B}_{m-1}\cdots\bm{B}_1$ corresponds to transmitting information across a $d\times (m+1)$ grid of nodes, where each sparse factor $\bm{B}_i$ contributes edges between adjacent levels. To achieve dense connectivity from the first to the last level, the sparsity patterns of the $\bm{B}_i$ must collectively allow each source node to reach every destination node. Butterfly graphs are designed to provide exactly this dense connectivity with far fewer edges than a single dense matrix. When each $\bm{B}_i$ is orthogonal, the resulting product $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal while being parameter-efficient.

# Practical Implications for Finetuning and the Role of Invertibility

The practical use of the Cayley parameterization in OFT and BOFT has two immediate implications. First, invertibility of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is necessary for the parameterization to be defined. Since training relies on repeatedly forming $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ for each orthogonal factor, invertibility cannot be violated. Identity initialization guarantees invertibility at the start and sets a stable baseline. Second, because each factor is orthogonal, their product remains orthogonal, and the spectral norm of $\bm{W}^0$ is preserved throughout finetuning.

BOFT further introduces multiplicative Dropout tailored to its multiplicative update form. By randomly replacing selected orthogonal blocks within butterfly components with identity matrices, BOFT controls overfitting without disrupting orthogonality. This approach is consistent with the identity initialization philosophy and the structured nature of butterfly factorization.

Finally, while BOFT can be extended to increase expressivity by composing more factors (for example, using alternating products of butterfly matrices), the strength of BOFT lies in balancing expressivity and regularity. A more expressive parameterization is not necessarily better for finetuning, as it may reduce the inductive bias that helps generalization. BOFT’s structured subset of orthogonal matrices, along with its sparse factorization, provides a principled and efficient finetuning paradigm where the Cayley parameterization plays a central role. In this paradigm, remembering that $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ requires $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ to be invertible is essential: it is the mathematical condition that underwrites the entire construction.
