In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', if BOFT's sparse factors were not constrained to be orthogonal, the stability benefit tied to SVD that would likely disappear is spectral norm preservation. The paper emphasizes that OFT/BOFT update a pretrained layer by left-multiplying an orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \), so if \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), then the finetuned weights are \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), which leaves the singular values—and in particular the spectral norm—unchanged because \( \bm{R} \) is orthogonal (\( \bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I} \)), a property the paper links to improved training stability and generalization; BOFT ensures this by parameterizing each butterfly component as orthogonal (via Cayley transforms and block-wise permutations). If the sparse factors composing \( \bm{R} \) were not orthogonal, the product would no longer be orthogonal, and left-multiplication could rescale directions in \( \bm{U} \), altering the largest singular value of \( \bm{W}^0 \) (i.e., the spectral norm), thereby forfeiting the cited stability benefit.
