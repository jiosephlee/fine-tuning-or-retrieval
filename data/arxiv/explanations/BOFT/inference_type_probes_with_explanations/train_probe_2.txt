In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', replacing BOFT's structured product of sparse orthogonal factors with an unstructured dense orthogonal matrix while keeping the same number of effective parameters is likely to result in worse generalization. This follows from the paper’s central argument that BOFT constrains the finetuning map to a structured subset of the orthogonal group by parameterizing the update as a product of orthogonal butterfly components, i.e., \(\bm{R}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,i)}\), which uses only \(\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm=O(d\log d)\) effective parameters yet preserves dense connectivity and yields a smooth interpolation between \(\bm{I}_d\) and the full orthogonal group; this structure recovers many classic fast linear transforms and thus induces a beneficial inductive bias that constrains the hypothesis class and mitigates overfitting, whereas allocating the same \(O(d\log d)\) degrees of freedom to an “unstructured” dense \(\bm{R}\in O(d)\) necessarily selects an irregular subset of the \(O(d^2)\) orthogonal degrees of freedom without these priors, enlarging effective search complexity and reducing alignment with known transforms. Under the paper’s information transmission view, the butterfly topology achieves dense end-to-end connectivity with the minimum number of “free” edges, while an unstructured construction with the same parameter budget must arbitrarily zero many entries to meet the parameter cap, producing sparsity patterns that neither approximate classic transforms nor provide the BOFT “smoother interpolation”; moreover, both BOFT and any orthogonal parameterization preserve the spectral norm of \(\bm{W}^0\), \(\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2\), so the generalization advantage arises from BOFT’s structural regularization (identity initialization, factorwise multiplicative dropout, and constrained hypothesis class) rather than norm control. Consequently, for a fixed parameter budget, the paper argues that BOFT’s structured factorization offers a better expressivity–regularity trade-off than an unstructured dense parameterization, leading to improved generalization.
