In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors explain that conceptually converting parameter complexity from $O(d^2)$ to $O(d\log d)$ changes the parameter growth class from quadratic to near-linear. This is sensible because BOFT replaces a single dense $d\times d$ orthogonal matrix (which naively requires $\Theta(d^2)$ degrees of freedom) with a product of $\mathcal{O}(\log d)$ sparse, orthogonal “butterfly components,” each with $\mathcal{O}(d)$ trainable entries, yielding an overall count of $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ effective parameters; concretely, BOFT($m,b$) uses $\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ parameters and the canonical butterfly setting $b=2,m=\log d$ gives $\Theta(d\log d)$, while full OFT uses $\Theta(d^2)$, and the information-transmission view clarifies why the butterfly topology achieves dense connectivity with only $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ edges (trainable nonzeros), mirroring FFT’s trade of computation for space to transmit information globally via sparse factorization.
