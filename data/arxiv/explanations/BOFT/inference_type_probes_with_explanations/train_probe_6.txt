In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors employ the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ where $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric; for the Cayley parameterization to be valid, the implicit mathematical condition on $\bm{Q}$ that must hold is that $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is invertible. This condition is required because the mapping explicitly uses $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$, and it also underpins the orthogonality guarantee: when $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$ and $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ is invertible, one can verify $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I}$ via $(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-\top}(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$ because $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$. Moreover, with $\bm{Q}$ real skew-symmetric, its eigenvalues are purely imaginary (or zero), so $1$ is never an eigenvalue of $\bm{Q}$ and hence $\det(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})\neq 0$, which makes the condition generically satisfied; the paper’s identity initialization ($\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$) trivially meets it since $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}=\bm{I}$.
