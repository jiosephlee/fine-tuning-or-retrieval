\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### Does the success at 2T tokens mean ternary models follow the same data-scaling behavior as FP16 models?
Question:
The paper reports that a 3B BitNet b1.58 model trained on 2T tokens outperforms StableLM-3B on several zero-shot tasks, and concludes that 1.58-bit LLMs have strong generalization capability. I am unsure how far that conclusion should be taken.

Does this result suggest that ternary models benefit from additional data in roughly the same way as full-precision models, or could there still be differences in the data-scaling curve that are not visible from a single 2T-token comparison? In other words, what can we really infer about token scaling from this experiment alone?
Answer:
Not really. The $2T$-token result shows that a ternary model can continue to improve with substantially more data and, at least in this setup, can outperform a strong $\mathrm{FP16}$ baseline of similar size. But it does **not** by itself establish that ternary models follow the *same* token-scaling law as $\mathrm{FP16}$ models.

The key point is that a single comparison at one data budget tells you only one point on the curve, not the curve itself. From the paper’s experiment, you can infer that BitNet b1.58 does not “run out of steam” at $2T$ tokens and that it can make effective use of large-scale training data. That is consistent with the authors’ claim that $1.58$-bit LLMs have strong generalization capability. But it does **not** tell you whether the data efficiency is the same, better, or worse than $\mathrm{FP16}$ as you move from, say, $100B$ to $500B$ to $2T$ to even larger token counts.

In other words, the experiment supports a statement like: “At $3B$ scale and $2T$ tokens, ternary weights do not prevent strong downstream performance.” It does **not** support a stronger statement like: “Ternary models have the same scaling exponent or the same asymptotic data-scaling behavior as $\mathrm{FP16}$ models.” To claim that, you would want several matched runs across multiple token budgets and ideally multiple model sizes, so you could compare the entire scaling trend rather than a single endpoint.

There is also a practical caveat in how to read the result. The paper compares BitNet b1.58 trained on $2T$ tokens with StableLM-$3B$ results taken from its technical report. That is a useful benchmark, but it is still one comparison between two training recipes and two model families. So the result is evidence that ternary training can be highly competitive under a large-token regime, not proof that the underlying scaling law is identical.

So the safest inference is: the paper provides evidence that $1.58$-bit LLMs can benefit from additional data in a meaningful way and can generalize well at large scale. It does **not** let us conclude, from this experiment alone, that their data-scaling curve matches $\mathrm{FP16}$ models point-for-point.
