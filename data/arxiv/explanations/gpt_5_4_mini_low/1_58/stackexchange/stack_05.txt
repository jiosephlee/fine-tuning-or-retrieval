\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits"}

### How can matrix multiplication 'require almost no multiplication operations' when activations are still INT8?
Question:
The paper says that BitNet b1.58 enables a new computation paradigm and that matrix multiplication requires almost no multiplication operations, with the arithmetic being mostly INT8 additions. This is where I got lost.

If a weight is in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and an activation is an 8-bit integer $a$, then multiplying gives either $-a$, $0$, or $a$. I can see that this avoids general-purpose floating-point multiplication, but doesn't the hardware still need some kind of sign handling and conditional logic? In what precise sense is the multiplication removed?

Is the intended implementation closer to selecting, negating, or skipping activations and then accumulating them? I am trying to understand whether this is just a theoretical simplification or a genuine low-level computational advantage on real accelerators.
Answer:
In the paper’s sense, “almost no multiplication operations” does not mean that the model output is produced without any arithmetic at all. It means that the expensive general-purpose multiply-accumulate pattern of standard matrix multiplication is replaced by something much cheaper: conditional add, subtract, or skip.

If a weight is $w \in \{-1,0,1\}$ and the activation is an 8-bit integer $a$, then the product is indeed just $-a$, $0$, or $a$. So the kernel does not need a full integer multiplier for the weight side. For each weight value, the implementation can simply do

$$
w = 1 \Rightarrow \text{accumulate } a,\qquad
w = -1 \Rightarrow \text{accumulate } -a,\qquad
w = 0 \Rightarrow \text{do nothing}.
$$

That is exactly what the paper means by “matrix multiplication only involves integer addition” and by “almost no multiplication operations.” The multiplication has been turned into a sign decision plus an addition or subtraction.

You are right that there is still some low-level logic involved. The hardware has to determine whether each ternary weight is $-1$, $0$, or $1$, and it must negate or skip the activation accordingly. But that logic is much cheaper than a conventional multiplication unit. In particular, there is no general-purpose multiply datapath doing products of two multi-bit numbers. From a systems point of view, that is the real gain.

This is not just a theoretical simplification. The paper’s efficiency claims are explicitly about inference cost on real accelerators. It reports that the matrix multiplication energy is dominated by INT8 additions in BitNet b1.58, whereas standard LLaMA uses FP16 additions and multiplications. They also mention using a specialized 2-bit kernel from Ladder in their runtime experiments, which is evidence that the intended implementation is a low-level optimized kernel, not merely a mathematical abstraction.

So the precise answer is: the multiplication is removed on the weight side, because multiplying by $-1$, $0$, or $1$ can be implemented as sign flip, zeroing, or passthrough. The remaining cost is mostly integer addition and some control logic. That is why the paper describes a new computation paradigm: not “no arithmetic,” but “no general multiplication,” which is the expensive part on conventional hardware.
