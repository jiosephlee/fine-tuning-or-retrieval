\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Does preserving pairwise angles within each layer imply anything about preserving the overall diffusion model function?
Question:
A deeper confusion I have is about what OFT actually guarantees at the network level. The method preserves pairwise angular relationships among the columns of each modified weight matrix, but the diffusion model is a very deep composition of linear maps, nonlinearities, cross-attention, residual paths, and normalization.

So even if every modified layer satisfies some geometric invariance internally, does that imply anything meaningful about the end-to-end denoising function or the generated image distribution? Or is the preservation of hyperspherical energy only a layerwise structural prior with no direct functional guarantee?

I'm asking because the paper sometimes sounds almost causal, as if keeping hyperspherical energy unchanged is the reason semantic generation ability is preserved. I can believe it as an empirical bias, but I'm not sure what should be taken as theorem versus intuition.
Answer:
Short answer: no, not in a strict end-to-end sense. In OFT, the provable guarantee is layerwise: if a weight matrix is updated as $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ with $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R}=\bm{I}$, then the pairwise angles among the columns of that matrix are preserved, so the hyperspherical energy of that layer is unchanged. The paper does not prove that this implies the entire diffusion model computes the same denoising function, nor that the generated image distribution is preserved.

What OFT is really doing is imposing a strong structural prior on each modified layer. The intuition is that the semantic information in a layer is partly encoded in the angular geometry of its neurons, so preserving that geometry should reduce the risk of destroying pretrained knowledge while still allowing the model to move into a new task. But because the full model is a deep composition of linear maps, nonlinearities, residual connections, attention, and normalization, a layerwise invariance does not compose into a global functional invariance. Changing one layer by an orthogonal transform generally changes activations downstream, and once you pass through nonlinearities and attention, there is no theorem in the paper saying the same end-to-end function is preserved.

So the right way to read the paper is: OFT gives a provable geometric constraint on the weights, not a provable guarantee about the final function. The claim that semantic generation ability is preserved is therefore empirical and hypothesis-driven, not a formal consequence of the orthogonality itself. The paper explicitly says that hyperspherical energy is used as a measure of semantic preservation, that preserving it is “crucial” for keeping generative ability, and that OFT “empirically” finds this to work well. That wording is important: it is an observed inductive bias, not a theorem about diffusion models.

COFT makes this interpretation even clearer. Its extra constraint $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq \epsilon$ is there precisely because orthogonality alone may be too flexible; the paper says this improves stability by keeping the finetuned model in a neighborhood of the pretrained one. That is another sign that the method is controlling how much the network is allowed to move, rather than guaranteeing any exact preservation of the global mapping.

So if you want a mathematically precise summary, it is this: OFT guarantees preservation of pairwise angular structure within each finetuned layer, and the authors hypothesize that this preserves the pretrained model’s semantic “geometry” well enough to maintain good generation quality. But the preservation of the overall diffusion model function or output distribution is not guaranteed by the paper, only supported by experiments and intuition.
