\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### Why does re-scaling neurons with a diagonal matrix on the right preserve hyperspherical energy?
Question:
The paper proposes a re-scaled OFT variant with forward pass

$$
\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\mathbf{D})^\top \mathbf{x},
$$

where $\mathbf{D} = \mathrm{diag}(s_1, \dots, s_n)$ and each $s_i > 0$.

I think the idea is that right-multiplying by $\mathbf{D}$ rescales the columns of $\mathbf{W}^0$, so after normalization each column direction stays the same and hyperspherical energy is unchanged. But I want to check that I am interpreting the matrix multiplication correctly.

Since $\mathbf{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$, right-multiplying by $\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ scales each column independently, while left-multiplying would scale rows. Is that the whole reason the erratum says $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\mathbf{D}$ is correct and $\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0$ was wrong?

Also, why do they require $s_i > 0$ instead of allowing negative values?
Answer:
Yes — your interpretation of the matrix multiplication is essentially correct, and that is exactly why the erratum matters.

In the paper, the weight matrix for a fully connected layer is written as $\mathbf{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$, where each column $\mathbf{w}_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is one neuron. Because the neurons are stored as columns, multiplying on the right by a diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D} = \mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ scales each column independently:
$$
\mathbf{W}^0 \mathbf{D} = [\, s_1 \mathbf{w}_1^0,\; s_2 \mathbf{w}_2^0,\; \dots,\; s_n \mathbf{w}_n^0 \,].
$$
So right-multiplication is the correct way to attach a separate learned scale to each neuron. Left-multiplication would instead scale the rows, which is a completely different operation: it changes the coordinates inside every neuron rather than scaling neurons as units.

That is why the corrected forward pass
$$
\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\mathbf{D})^\top \mathbf{x}
$$
is the right one. The erratum says the version $\mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0)^\top \mathbf{x}$ was a typo, and this is not just cosmetic: $\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0$ would generally not mean “rescale each neuron after the orthogonal transform.” The intended construction is “rotate/reflect the neurons with $\mathbf{R}$, then scale each resulting neuron with $\mathbf{D}$.”

As for hyperspherical energy, the key point is that it is computed after normalizing each neuron:
$$
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_i = \frac{\mathbf{w}_i}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\|}.
$$
If you replace $\mathbf{w}_i$ by $s_i \mathbf{w}_i$ with $s_i > 0$, then
$$
\frac{s_i \mathbf{w}_i}{\|s_i \mathbf{w}_i\|} = \frac{\mathbf{w}_i}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\|}.
$$
So positive rescaling does not change any normalized neuron direction at all. Since hyperspherical energy depends only on pairwise relations between these normalized directions, it is unchanged. In the OFT setting, the orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$ preserves angles, and the diagonal scaling $\mathbf{D}$ is “washed out” by normalization, so the hyperspherical energy remains the same.

The restriction $s_i > 0$ is there because negative values would flip a neuron to the opposite point on the sphere:
$$
\frac{-\mathbf{w}_i}{\|-\mathbf{w}_i\|} = -\frac{\mathbf{w}_i}{\|\mathbf{w}_i\|}.
$$
That is not just a harmless rescaling; it changes the direction of the neuron and can alter pairwise spherical relationships. In other words, a negative scale does not preserve the ray spanned by the neuron, only its line through the origin. Since the paper’s invariance argument is about preserving normalized neuron directions up to positive scaling, they explicitly require $s_i > 0$.

So the short answer is: yes, right-multiplication by $\mathbf{D}$ is used because the columns are the neurons, and positive diagonal scaling preserves hyperspherical energy because normalization removes the magnitudes without introducing sign flips. The erratum corrects the matrix order to match exactly that interpretation.
