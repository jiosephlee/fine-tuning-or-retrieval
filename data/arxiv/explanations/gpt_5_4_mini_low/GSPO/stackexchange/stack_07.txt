\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Why does GSPO-token introduce a detach-based factor that is numerically equal to 1?
Question:
In the GSPO-token variant, the paper defines

$ s_{i,t}(\theta) = \mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)] \cdot \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})]}.$

Since

$ \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})]} $

has numerical value 1, this seems very strange at first glance.

Am I right that this is a "gradient trick" whose only purpose is to make the forward value equal to $s_i(\theta)$ while making the backward pass produce token-level score-function gradients weighted by the sequence scalar $s_i(\theta)$?

If so, could someone explain carefully how this detach construction leads to Equation (11)? I want to make sure I understand why the objective is numerically unchanged but the autograd path is different.
Answer:
Yes — this is exactly a gradient-trick, and the point is to make the *forward* value of the token-level ratio equal to the sequence-level ratio $s_i(\theta)$, while making the *backward* pass look like a token-level score-function gradient.

The key identity is that
$$
\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})]}
$$
is numerically $1$, because the denominator is just a detached copy of the same scalar. So in the forward pass,
$$
s_{i,t}(\theta)=\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]\cdot 1 = s_i(\theta)
$$
numerically. That is why the paper says GSPO-token is “numerically identical” to GSPO when all token advantages are the same.

But the gradient is not identical, because the detached terms are treated as constants by autograd. If you differentiate
$$
s_{i,t}(\theta)=\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]\cdot \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})]},
$$
then the only factor that contributes gradient is the numerator $\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})$. The detached denominator blocks the extra derivative you would normally get from a plain ratio. Since $\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]$ is also constant for backprop, the derivative is
$$
\nabla_\theta s_{i,t}(\theta)
=
\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]\cdot \frac{\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})]}
=
s_i(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t}),
$$
because the detached copies evaluate to the same numeric values as the original quantities.

That is the whole trick behind Equation (11). Starting from the objective with $\widehat{A}_{i,t}$,
$$
\mathcal{J}_{\text{GSPO-token}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t s_{i,t}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{i,t}\right],
$$
and differentiating through $s_{i,t}$ gives
$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{J}_{\text{GSPO-token}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i s_i(\theta)\cdot \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t \widehat{A}_{i,t}\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})\right],
$$
which is exactly the paper’s Equation (11). So the detach construction does not change the objective value; it only changes which computational path autograd uses, turning the scalar sequence weight into a constant multiplier on each token’s policy-gradient term.

Intuitively, this is similar to the common “stop-gradient on the baseline” idea in policy gradients: you often want a quantity to affect the loss as a weight, but you do not want gradients to flow through the thing producing the weight itself. Here, the authors use that idea to reuse the sequence-level importance ratio as a frozen multiplier while still applying token-wise advantages.
