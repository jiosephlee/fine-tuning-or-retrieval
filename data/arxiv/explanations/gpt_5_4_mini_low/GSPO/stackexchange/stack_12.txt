\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### How should I interpret the very different clipping ranges used in GSPO and GRPO?
Question:
In the experiments, GSPO uses very small clipping ranges like $3 \times 10^{-4}$ and $4 \times 10^{-4}$, while GRPO uses much larger ranges like $0.2$ and $0.27$. The paper notes that the ranges differ in order of magnitude because the importance ratios are defined differently.

Could someone help me build intuition for these numbers?

Since GSPO uses

$ s_i(\theta) = \exp\left(\frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_t \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})}\right), $

it seems to measure an average per-token multiplicative change rather than a raw token ratio. Does that mean a clipping window of $\pm 10^{-4}$ already corresponds to a meaningful policy shift over long responses?

I'm looking for a concrete interpretation of what these clipping thresholds mean geometrically or statistically.
Answer:
The key is that GSPO is clipping a very different quantity from GRPO.

In GRPO, the ratio is a **token-level** importance weight
$$
w_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})},
$$
so a clipping range like $0.2$ means “for this token, allow the new policy’s probability to be about $20\%$ lower or $27\%$ higher than before.” That is a fairly large local move.

In GSPO, by contrast, the ratio is the **length-normalized sequence likelihood ratio**
$$
s_i(\theta)=\left(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i\mid x)}\right)^{1/|y_i|}
=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}\right).
$$
So GSPO is not asking how much one token moved; it is asking how much the **geometric mean** of the per-token likelihood ratios moved. The clipping window $3\times 10^{-4}$ to $4\times 10^{-4}$ therefore means “keep the average per-token log-likelihood change extremely close to zero.”

For small deviations, $\log(1+\delta)\approx \delta$, so a GSPO clip of $s_i(\theta)\in[1-3\times 10^{-4},\,1+4\times 10^{-4}]$ is roughly
$$
\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_{i,t}\mid\cdot)}
\in [-3\times 10^{-4},\,4\times 10^{-4}].
$$
That is a per-token average shift of only a few $10^{-4}$ nats in log-probability. Geometrically, this is a very narrow trust region around the old policy in the space of **average log-likelihood per token**, not in raw token probabilities.

Your intuition is exactly right that, over a long response, such a tiny per-token change can still correspond to a meaningful shift in the whole sequence. Because GSPO uses the $1/|y_i|$-th power, the sequence ratio itself is
$$
\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i\mid x)} = s_i(\theta)^{|y_i|}.
$$
So if $s_i(\theta)=1.0004$ and $|y_i|=1000$, then the full-sequence ratio is about $(1.0004)^{1000}\approx e^{0.4}\approx 1.49$. That is not tiny at all. The clipping looks microscopic only because GSPO normalizes by length before taking the exponential.

So the clean interpretation is: GRPO clips how much an individual token can change; GSPO clips how much the **average token-level log-likelihood of the whole response** can change. The former is a local, tokenwise trust region; the latter is a sequencewise trust region expressed on a per-token scale. That is why the numerical ranges differ by orders of magnitude, and why the paper explicitly says the ranges are not directly comparable.
