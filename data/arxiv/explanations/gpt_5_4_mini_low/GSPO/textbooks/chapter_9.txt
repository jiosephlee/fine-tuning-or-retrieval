\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

Chapter 9: 9. Empirical Performance: Stability, Efficiency, and Scaling Behavior

# Training reward curves and what they reveal about optimization stability

The paper’s central empirical claim is that GSPO produces much more stable reinforcement learning dynamics than GRPO. This is first seen in the training reward curves. The authors report experiments starting from a cold-start model fine-tuned from Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, and they track reward during RL training while partitioning each rollout batch into four mini-batches for gradient updates. In this setting, the reward curve serves as a direct indicator of whether optimization is progressing in a controlled way or becoming erratic.

The key point is that GSPO’s reward curve remains stable throughout training. This matters because, in the paper’s framing, instability is not just a temporary nuisance but a sign that the underlying update rule is producing noisy and increasingly unreliable gradient estimates. A stable reward trajectory therefore suggests that GSPO is applying updates in a way that better preserves the integrity of learning as training proceeds. In contrast, GRPO is the algorithm that the paper identifies as prone to instability, especially in long-response and large-model settings.

The reward curves are thus more than a visual summary of performance. They are evidence for the paper’s core argument that sequence-level optimization is a better match for sequence-level rewards. If the optimization procedure were fundamentally misaligned with the structure of the task, one would expect unstable and potentially collapsing training behavior. The observed stability of GSPO is presented as confirmation that its sequence-level importance ratio and sequence-level clipping produce a more coherent learning signal.

# Evidence that GSPO avoids collapse during extended training

A major empirical claim of the paper is that GSPO avoids the catastrophic collapse that can occur under GRPO. The introduction and motivation sections explain that GRPO can suffer from irreversible model collapse during large-scale training, particularly when response length grows and when the model is large enough for the instability to accumulate. The experiments are designed to test whether GSPO resolves this issue in practice.

The reported results indicate that GSPO can continue training without falling into collapse even as training is extended. The authors specifically emphasize that GSPO can support continuous performance improvement through increasing the training compute, regularly updating the query set, and extending the generation length. This is important because collapse is not merely a failure to improve; it is a breakdown in training from which recovery is often not possible. The paper notes that once collapse occurs, resuming training is unavailing, even when reverting to earlier checkpoints or carefully retuning hyperparameters.

GSPO’s avoidance of collapse is therefore presented as one of its most important practical advantages. It is not just that GSPO performs better on average, but that it preserves a workable training process over long horizons. That property is essential for any RL method intended for scaling up large language model capabilities, because the value of scaling compute depends on the training algorithm remaining dependable as optimization becomes more aggressive.

# Benchmark performance improvements over GRPO

The paper evaluates GSPO on several benchmarks, specifically AIME'24, LiveCodeBench, and CodeForces. For AIME'24, the metric is average Pass@1 over 32 samplings. For LiveCodeBench, the reported metric is average Pass@1 over 8 samplings, using the 202410–202502 split. For CodeForces, the metric is Elo Rating. These benchmarks are chosen to reflect the kinds of complex reasoning and coding abilities that large-scale RL is meant to improve.

Across these benchmarks, GSPO is reported to outperform GRPO. The paper states that GSPO achieves superior training accuracy and benchmark performance under the same training compute and consumed queries. This means the benefit is not merely that GSPO trains longer or uses more data, but that it extracts more capability from a comparable training budget. The benchmark gains are therefore tied directly to the algorithm’s improved efficiency and stability.

The significance of these benchmark improvements is that they connect the training dynamics to the end task. Stable reward curves are important, but the ultimate goal is better model performance on challenging reasoning and coding tasks. The reported benchmark results show that GSPO’s stability is not achieved at the cost of weak learning; rather, the stable training regime translates into stronger downstream capability.

# Higher training efficiency under equal compute and data usage

The paper makes a strong claim about sample efficiency: GSPO achieves better results than GRPO using the same training compute and the same consumed queries. This is what the authors mean by higher training efficiency. It indicates that GSPO makes better use of each rollout sample and each gradient step.

One way the paper supports this claim is by noting that GSPO clips entire responses rather than individual tokens. Although this causes GSPO to clip significantly more tokens overall, the authors observe that it still outperforms GRPO in efficiency. This is an important and somewhat counterintuitive result. One might expect that clipping more aggressively would discard too much training signal, but the paper argues the opposite: the tokens that are clipped in GRPO are not especially informative because the token-level gradients are inherently noisy and inefficient. In that view, clipping larger fractions of token-level updates in GSPO is not wasteful but beneficial, because it removes unreliable training signal and preserves a cleaner sequence-level learning signal.

The authors interpret this as evidence that GSPO is better at sample exploitation. Even though fewer token-level terms survive clipping, the learning that does occur is more useful. In practical terms, this means that a fixed amount of compute and data leads to stronger learning progress under GSPO than under GRPO.

# Continuous gains from increased RL compute

Another key result is that GSPO continues to improve as RL compute increases. The paper emphasizes that GSPO can deliver continuous performance improvement through increasing the training compute. This is a crucial property for scaling, because many RL methods improve only up to a point before instability or diminishing returns interfere with further training.

Here, the paper’s empirical message is that GSPO does not exhibit the same brittleness as GRPO under more intensive training. Instead, as more compute is invested, GSPO continues to make effective use of the extra optimization steps. That behavior supports the idea that the algorithm is not just locally stable but globally scalable over a longer training trajectory.

This matters in the context of large language model reasoning, where the paper frames RL as a way to unlock deeper and longer reasoning processes. If the algorithm cannot absorb more compute without breaking down, then scaling RL cannot reliably improve capability. GSPO’s continued gains under increased compute are therefore presented as evidence that it is suitable for sustained RL scaling.

# Benefits of regularly refreshing the query set

The experiments also show that GSPO benefits from regularly updating the query set during training. The paper explicitly states that GSPO can provide continuous performance improvement through regularly updating the query set. This suggests that GSPO can continue learning effectively even as the data distribution changes over the course of training.

This is an important empirical point because RL training for language models is often not a fixed-data problem. As the model improves, one may wish to present it with new or refreshed queries so that training remains challenging and aligned with the current capability level. The fact that GSPO continues to improve under query-set refreshes suggests that it is robust to such evolving training conditions.

The paper does not present query-set refreshing as a separate algorithmic trick so much as a sign of scalability. A method that tolerates updated queries without instability is better suited for long-running RL pipelines. In this sense, the benefit of refreshing the query set is not only that it broadens coverage, but that GSPO can sustain learning under a changing stream of tasks.

# Benefits of extending generation length during RL

The paper also reports that GSPO can continue improving when generation length is extended during training. This is especially important because the introduction emphasizes that longer responses and deeper reasoning are central to the capabilities sought from large-scale RL. A method that works only for short outputs would not be adequate for the intended applications.

The authors highlight that GSPO can support extended generation length while maintaining stable training. This is closely connected to the paper’s motivation: GRPO’s instability becomes more severe as response length increases, because token-level importance ratios accumulate noise over long sequences. GSPO’s sequence-level formulation is designed precisely to avoid this problem. Empirically, the fact that it continues to improve when generation length is increased is presented as evidence that the sequence-level update rule is better suited for long-horizon reasoning tasks.

This result supports the broader view that the algorithm’s gains are not restricted to a narrow regime. Instead, GSPO appears able to handle the increasing complexity of longer responses, which is a necessary condition for scaling RL to more demanding tasks.

# Evidence from application to the latest Qwen3 models

The paper states that GSPO has been successfully applied to the RL training of the latest Qwen3 models, and that its merits contributed to the remarkable improvements in those models. This is an especially important piece of evidence because it shows the algorithm working not only in a controlled experiment but also in a practical, high-impact training setting.

The relevance of this result is twofold. First, it shows that GSPO is not merely a theoretical fix or a synthetic benchmark improvement. Second, it suggests that the empirical advantages observed in the paper are large enough to matter in real model development. The authors present the success in Qwen3 as strong proof that GSPO can unlock the power of RL scaling for large language models.

Because the paper positions Qwen3 as an example of large-scale, capability-oriented training, its success serves as a concrete validation of the algorithm’s claimed benefits in stability, efficiency, and performance. In other words, the benchmark and training-curve results are reinforced by the fact that the method has already influenced a state-of-the-art model family.

# Interpreting the results as support for RL scaling with GSPO

Taken together, the empirical findings support a broader conclusion: GSPO provides a stable and scalable foundation for RL in large language models. The paper’s results show that GSPO can train stably, avoid collapse, achieve better benchmark performance than GRPO, and make better use of compute and data. It can also keep improving as training becomes more demanding, including when the query set is refreshed and when generation length is increased.

This pattern of results is important because the paper frames RL scaling as a central route to improved reasoning and problem-solving in language models. If scaling is to work in practice, the algorithm must remain reliable as model size, sparsity, response length, and compute all increase. GSPO is presented as meeting that requirement. Its empirical behavior is not just better than GRPO’s on isolated metrics; it is consistent with the kind of long-horizon, high-compute training regime needed for continued progress.

In that sense, the paper’s experimental section does more than compare two algorithms. It argues that GSPO resolves a bottleneck in RL training that had previously limited the ability to scale language model reasoning. The observed stability, efficiency, and continuous gains are therefore interpreted as evidence that GSPO can serve as a robust algorithmic cornerstone for future RL-driven advances.
