\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### Why is the entropy formula in the paper missing a minus sign if it is supposed to be entropy?
Question:
In the BLT paper, the next-byte entropy is defined as

$$H(x_i) = \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} p_e(x_i = v \mid \mathbf{x}_{<i}) \log p_e(x_i = v \mid \mathbf{x}_{<i}).$$

But standard Shannon entropy is usually written with a minus sign:

$$H(X) = -\sum_x p(x) \log p(x).$$

Without the minus sign, the quantity is non-positive, so larger uncertainty would correspond to values closer to $0$, which seems opposite to how they use thresholding for high-entropy patch boundaries.

Is this just a typo in the paper, or are they intentionally using negative entropy / expected log-probability? If it is intentional, how should I interpret the thresholds $\theta_g$ and $\theta_r$?
Answer:
Yes — as written, that equation is the **negative** of Shannon entropy, so in isolation it is inconsistent with the rest of the paper.

The standard entropy of the next-byte distribution should be

$$
H(x_i) = -\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} p_e(x_i=v \mid \mathbf{x}_{<i}) \log p_e(x_i=v \mid \mathbf{x}_{<i}).
$$

Without the minus sign, the quantity is just the expected log-probability, which is non-positive. That means the equation in the paper is either a typo, or the authors are using “entropy” loosely while actually writing a sign-flipped version of it.

The evidence from the paper strongly suggests this is a typo rather than an intentional redefinition. They describe patch boundaries as being created at “high entropy” positions, and later say that they adjust the entropy threshold from $0.6$ to $0.1$. Those are positive thresholds, which only make sense for the usual nonnegative entropy, not for the signed quantity in the displayed equation. So the intended meaning is almost certainly the usual one: higher entropy means more uncertainty, and those are the places where they start new patches.

If you want to reconcile the formulas literally, you could say they are thresholding a score that is monotonic in uncertainty. But then the threshold should be interpreted on the same sign convention as the score. With the equation as printed, a boundary would be triggered when

$$
\sum_v p \log p > \theta_g,
$$

which means “close to zero” values, i.e. more uncertain predictions. In the standard entropy convention, this would instead be written as

$$
-\sum_v p \log p > \theta_g.
$$

So the clean reading is: **the minus sign is missing in the paper’s equation**. The thresholds $\theta_g$ and $\theta_r$ are meant to be thresholds on entropy as an uncertainty measure, not on negative entropy.
