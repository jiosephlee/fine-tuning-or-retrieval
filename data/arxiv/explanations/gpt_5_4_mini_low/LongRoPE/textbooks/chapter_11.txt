\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 11: 11. Main Results: Language Modeling, Retrieval, and Benchmark Retention

# Perplexity Results Within 256k on PG19 and Proof-pile

The main language-modeling evidence for LongRoPE comes from perplexity measurements on long documents, especially on the PG19 and Proof-pile test splits. These datasets are used because they contain long-form text, making them suitable for evaluating whether an extended context window is actually useful. The paper evaluates perplexity at multiple context lengths, using a sliding window of 256, and reports results for both LLaMA2 and Mistral models extended with LongRoPE.

The key pattern is that LongRoPE models show an overall decreasing perplexity trend as the evaluation length increases from 4k to 256k. This matters because it indicates that the models are not merely surviving longer inputs; they are exploiting the extra context to improve next-token prediction. In other words, the extended window is not just a larger container for text, but something the model actively uses.

The paper emphasizes that this behavior holds on both PG19 and Proof-pile. For PG19, the full test split of 100 documents is used, while for Proof-pile the authors follow the YaRN setting and randomly select 10 samples with lengths of at least 128k. Across these settings, LongRoPE is shown to outperform state-of-the-art baselines even though the evaluated context lengths are far larger than those used in standard language-model benchmarks.

# Evidence That LongRoPE Benefits From Longer Context Rather Than Merely Tolerating It

A central interpretation of the perplexity curves is that LongRoPE genuinely benefits from longer context. This is not simply a matter of not breaking when the sequence becomes long. If a model merely tolerates longer inputs, its perplexity would stay flat or become worse as the sequence grows. LongRoPE instead shows the opposite tendency: the longer context often helps.

The paper presents this as evidence that the model is able to draw useful information from distant tokens. The decreasing perplexity from 4k to 256k suggests that the additional context is being incorporated into prediction rather than ignored. This is especially important because long-context methods can sometimes look impressive on extension length alone while failing to improve actual modeling quality. LongRoPE is framed as avoiding that pitfall.

This interpretation is also reinforced by the fact that LongRoPE performs well at much shorter lengths while scaling to much longer ones. The same model family is used to handle both ends of the range, which helps show that the extension is not bought at the cost of losing basic language-model competence.

# Comparison Against State-of-the-Art Baselines at Shorter and Intermediate Lengths

Within the 256k evaluation range, LongRoPE is compared against state-of-the-art context-extension baselines based on PI, NTK, and YaRN, including open-sourced models such as Together-32k, Code LLaMA, LongLoRA-full-FT-100k, YaRN-LLaMA, and YaRN-Mistral. The paper’s main conclusion is that LongRoPE-2048k models outperform these baselines within 256k context length, even though the LongRoPE models are ultimately designed for much longer windows.

This comparison is important because it shows that LongRoPE is not only better at the extreme high end. It also performs strongly in the intermediate regime where many extended-context applications operate. The paper highlights that this remains true even when the context window is much longer than the evaluation length, which is traditionally a difficult condition for maintaining quality. A 16$\times$ longer context can easily harm short-range modeling, yet LongRoPE still compares favorably against the baselines.

The result supports the idea that the method’s non-uniform positional interpolation and progressive extension strategy do more than simply stretch the model’s reach. They also produce a good initialization and preserve the model’s ability to work effectively at lengths shorter than the full extension target.

# Results Beyond 2000k on Books3

To evaluate truly extreme context lengths, the paper turns to Books3 and tests on documents exceeding 2048k tokens. For efficiency, the authors randomly select 20 books and use a sliding window of 256k. This setting is designed to answer a very direct question: can LongRoPE actually operate at million-token scale?

The answer is yes. The paper reports that LongRoPE successfully extends both LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B to 2048k. More importantly, the models maintain perplexity that is comparable to or better than baselines at shorter lengths such as 8k to 128k. This shows that the method is not narrowly specialized to one long regime. It continues to function across a wide span of lengths.

The Books3 evaluation is especially significant because it stresses the models far beyond conventional extension ranges. The paper uses it as the main evidence that LongRoPE reaches an unprecedented scale while keeping language-model performance stable enough to remain meaningful.

# Successful Extension of LLaMA2 and Mistral to 2048k

One of the most concrete claims in the paper is that LongRoPE extends both LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B to a 2048k context window. This is presented as a genuine extension of the pre-trained models rather than a new architecture. The approach keeps the base model structure intact and modifies only the positional embedding behavior.

The results support this claim across both models. LongRoPE-2048k works for LLaMA2 and Mistral, though the exact performance profiles differ. The paper frames this as evidence that the method is broadly applicable to RoPE-based models, not tied to one particular backbone.

At the same time, the results also reveal that reaching 2048k is not uniform across models. The same extension method can behave differently depending on the pre-trained model and the fine-tuning setup. This makes the comparison between LLaMA2 and Mistral especially informative.

# Differences in Performance Trends Between LLaMA2 and Mistral

The paper notes a clear difference in how LLaMA2 and Mistral behave at extreme lengths. For LLaMA2, perplexity generally decreases with longer contexts, with only marginal increases at 1024k and 2048k. This suggests a relatively smooth transition into the million-token regime.

Mistral behaves differently. It performs better at shorter lengths, but its perplexity exceeds 7 beyond 256k. In other words, while it can still be extended to 2048k, its long-range language modeling degrades more noticeably than LLaMA2’s. The paper presents this contrast as an empirical result, not as a flaw in LongRoPE itself, but as a consequence of how the model and training setup interact with the extension process.

These differences matter because they show that the same extension framework can produce different scaling curves. LongRoPE enables the extension, but the final behavior still depends on the model being extended and the way it was fine-tuned.

# Impact of Fine-Tuning at 128k Versus 256k Before the Second Extension

The progressive extension strategy is a core part of the method, and the results make clear that the choice of fine-tuning length matters. For LLaMA2, the paper reports that LongRoPE-2048k performs better when the intermediate fine-tuning length is 256k rather than 128k. The explanation given is that the secondary extension ratio is smaller in the 256k case, namely $8\times$ instead of $16\times$, making the final jump to 2048k easier.

For Mistral, the opposite trend is observed: performance is better when fine-tuning at 128k. The paper attributes this to the fact that, for Mistral’s 128k and 256k fine-tuning, the setting follows YaRN and uses a 16k training length, which affects the model’s ability to extend further afterward. This highlights that the progressive strategy is effective, but its optimal configuration depends on the base model and training details.

The main lesson is that LongRoPE does not require direct fine-tuning at 2048k. Instead, it can reach that scale by first extending and fine-tuning at a more manageable length and then performing a second positional interpolation search. The results show that this staged process is both feasible and effective.

# Passkey Retrieval as a Direct Test of Usable Long-Context Memory

Perplexity measures language modeling quality, but it does not directly prove that a model can retrieve information from very far away in a long document. For that reason, the paper also uses passkey retrieval, a synthetic task where the model must find a randomly placed five-digit number hidden in a long context. This makes the task a direct test of usable long-context memory.

The task is intentionally simple in terms of what needs to be retrieved, but hard in terms of where the information may appear. The passkey is placed at a random location uniformly distributed across the evaluation context length, and the model is asked to recover it. Because the key is surrounded by irrelevant text, success requires the model to preserve access to the correct position across a very long sequence.

This is a useful complement to perplexity because it tests whether the long context is actually accessible at generation time, not just beneficial in next-token prediction.

# LongRoPE’s Retrieval Accuracy Up to Million-Token Lengths

The passkey results are one of the strongest demonstrations of LongRoPE’s practical long-context ability. The paper reports that LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k, fine-tuned at 256k, maintains retrieval accuracy of at least 90% from 4k all the way to 2048k. That is a remarkable result because it shows the model can consistently locate information even when the target is buried in a million-token-scale context.

The Mistral version also performs strongly, though with a different long-range pattern. LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k, fine-tuned at 128k, keeps 100% accuracy up to 1800k before dropping to 60% at 2048k. The paper connects this decline to the same behavior seen in perplexity: Mistral is strong up to a point, but its performance weakens more near the very end of the extended range.

Taken together, these retrieval results support the claim that LongRoPE extends not just theoretical context length, but usable context length. The model can still recover content from extremely distant positions, which is essential for applications involving long documents and long-horizon reasoning.

# Why Baseline Models Fail Beyond 128k in Retrieval

The paper contrasts LongRoPE with baseline models that use other positional interpolation methods. In the passkey retrieval task, those baselines rapidly lose accuracy beyond 128k, with performance dropping to zero. This is presented as evidence that merely stretching positional embeddings by standard interpolation methods is insufficient for reliable million-token retrieval.

The failure is especially telling because retrieval should, in principle, be easier than open-ended generation if the model can maintain access to the right region of the sequence. Yet the baselines are unable to preserve this access over very long distances. The paper uses this to emphasize that the challenge is not just extending the nominal window size, but preserving meaningful positional structure across the full range.

LongRoPE’s advantage is therefore not just in language modeling metrics but in actual retrieval behavior. The method appears to preserve enough positional information to keep the passkey accessible when baseline methods cannot.

# Performance on Short-Context Benchmarks: ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, and TruthfulQA

A major concern with very long context extension is that it might damage the model’s original capabilities on standard benchmarks. The paper addresses this by evaluating LongRoPE-2048k models on benchmarks designed within the original 4096-token context window: ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, and TruthfulQA. These are tested in zero-shot and few-shot settings, using the specific shot counts described in the paper.

The results show that LongRoPE retains comparable performance on these short-context benchmarks. In some cases it matches the original model closely, and in the case of LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k it even improves slightly on TruthfulQA by $+0.5\%$. This is important because it suggests that the extension process does not catastrophically disrupt the model’s general capabilities.

LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k, fine-tuned at 256k, shows somewhat more degradation than the Mistral variant, but the paper states that the performance remains within reasonable ranges for most tasks. So while there is some tradeoff, the preservation of standard benchmark quality is still strong.

# Interpreting the Claim That LongRoPE Retains Original Short-Context Ability

The short-benchmark results are central to the paper’s claim that LongRoPE preserves the original short-context window behavior. This claim should be understood carefully. The paper is not saying that performance is identical to the unextended model in every case. Rather, it shows that the model remains broadly competitive on benchmarks built for short contexts, even after being extended to 2048k.

This is an important result because long-context methods often suffer from a tension between long-range capability and short-range sharpness. The paper argues that LongRoPE reduces this tradeoff through its non-uniform positional interpolation and its recovery step for short context lengths. The benchmark results provide empirical support for that claim.

Overall, the main results section shows a consistent picture. LongRoPE improves long-document language modeling, supports retrieval over million-token spans, and largely preserves standard benchmark performance. The combination of these findings is what makes the paper’s central claim compelling: the model is not only stretched to 2048k, but remains useful across the short, intermediate, and extreme ranges.
