\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Why does left-multiplying by an orthogonal matrix preserve the spectral norm but not necessarily all singular vectors?
Question:
The paper argues that BOFT has better spectral properties than LoRA because if
$$
\mathbf{W}^0 = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top,
$$
then the finetuned weight becomes
$$
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0 = (\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U})\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top,
$$
and therefore the spectral norm is unchanged.

I understand that orthogonal left multiplication should preserve singular values, but I want to make sure I see the exact reason. Is it because $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U}$ is still orthogonal, so this is another valid SVD with the same diagonal $\mathbf{\Sigma}$?

Also, does this imply that all singular values are preserved, or only the largest one? The paper emphasizes the spectral norm, but it seems like the entire singular value spectrum should remain the same.
Answer:
Yes — for an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$, left-multiplication preserves the entire singular value spectrum, not just the spectral norm.

The cleanest way to see it is exactly through the SVD. If
$$
\mathbf{W}^0=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top,
$$
with $\mathbf{U}$ and $\mathbf{V}$ orthogonal, then
$$
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0=(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U})\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top.
$$
Since $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{U}$ are both orthogonal, their product $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U}$ is also orthogonal. So this is again an SVD of $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0$ with exactly the same diagonal matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$. That means every singular value is unchanged.

An even more invariant way to say this is that singular values are determined by the eigenvalues of
$$
(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0)^\top(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0)
= (\mathbf{W}^0)^\top \mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0
= (\mathbf{W}^0)^\top \mathbf{W}^0.
$$
So $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0$ and $\mathbf{W}^0$ have the same singular values. This is true for all of them, not only the largest one.

What the paper emphasizes is the spectral norm because that is the quantity most often discussed in optimization and generalization arguments, and preserving it is the headline “spectral property” they want to contrast with LoRA. But mathematically, orthogonal left multiplication preserves the full singular value spectrum.

The subtle point is about singular vectors. The singular values stay the same, but the left singular vectors generally change: $\mathbf{u}_i$ becomes $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}_i$. The right singular vectors $\mathbf{v}_i$ stay the same in this factorization. So if by “preserve singular vectors” you mean the vectors themselves are unchanged, then no. What is preserved is the singular-value structure, while the left singular basis is rotated by $\mathbf{R}$.

So your intuition is right: $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U}$ is still orthogonal, and that gives a valid SVD with the same $\mathbf{\Sigma}$. The paper singles out the spectral norm, but the stronger statement is that all singular values are preserved.
