\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Where does the effective parameter count $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ for BOFT come from?
Question:
The paper claims that BOFT($m$, $b$) uses
$$
\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm
$$
effective trainable parameters for a linear layer of input dimension $d$.

I tried to derive this but got confused. My rough intuition was that each butterfly stage is composed of many small orthogonal blocks, and each orthogonal block has fewer degrees of freedom than its number of entries. But I cannot recover the exact formula.

Could someone show the counting step by step? For example, how many $2b \times 2b$ orthogonal blocks are there per stage, and how many independent parameters does each contribute under the paper's parameterization?
Answer:
The key is that BOFT does **not** count the raw entries of the block matrices, but the number of **free orthogonal degrees of freedom** in each block.

In the paper, the orthogonal parameterization is built from butterfly components, and each component is a block-permutation of a base component $\tilde{\mathbf B}^b(d,2)$. After a suitable permutation, this base component decomposes into independent orthogonal blocks of size $b\times b$ along the diagonal. So, per stage, you get about $\frac{d}{b}$ independent $b\times b$ orthogonal blocks.

Now recall a standard fact: an orthogonal matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{b\times b}$ has
$$
\frac{b(b-1)}{2}
$$
degrees of freedom, because it lives on the manifold $O(b)$, whose dimension is $\frac{b(b-1)}{2}$. This is exactly what the Cayley/skew-symmetric parameterization is exploiting: a skew-symmetric $b\times b$ matrix has $\frac{b(b-1)}{2}$ independent entries, and those are the effective trainable parameters for one orthogonal block.

So one butterfly stage contributes
$$
\frac{d}{b}\cdot \frac{b(b-1)}{2}
=
\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d
$$
effective parameters.

Since $\mathrm{BOFT}(m,b)$ uses $m$ such butterfly stages, the total becomes
$$
m \cdot \frac{1}{2}(b-1)d
=
\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm.
$$

If you want to think in terms of the paper’s “$2b\times 2b$ block” phrasing, the important point is that those blocks are still orthogonal blocks with only the orthogonality-compatible degrees of freedom counted; after the blockwise orthogonal parameterization, the number of free parameters per stage reduces to the same $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d$. The raw matrix size is larger, but most entries are not independent once orthogonality is enforced.

So the count is simply:

$$
\text{\# blocks per stage} \times \text{DOF per block} \times \text{\# stages}
=
\frac{d}{b}\cdot \frac{b(b-1)}{2}\cdot m
=
\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm.
$$

That is the paper’s effective parameter count.
