\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### If RL only used GSM8K and MATH chain-of-thought data, why did out-of-domain benchmarks also improve?
Question:
DeepSeekMath-RL is trained only on a subset of instruction-tuning data: chain-of-thought-format questions from GSM8K and MATH, around 144K questions. Yet the paper reports improvements not only on those in-domain tasks but also on out-of-domain mathematical benchmarks such as CMATH.

I'm not sure how to interpret this. Does it suggest the reward model and policy updates are teaching generally useful reasoning habits, such as consistency and better allocation of probability mass over good traces? Or could it simply mean those benchmarks are still close enough in style that they benefit from the same distribution reshaping?

What is the best way to think about this kind of transfer, especially given the paper's later claim that RL may not improve fundamental capability as measured by Pass@$K$?
Answer:
The safest reading of the paper is that the RL stage is not learning “GSM8K-only tricks,” but rather reshaping the model’s answer distribution in a way that transfers to other math benchmarks that share the same underlying reasoning structure.

Two details from the paper matter here. First, the RL data are only chain-of-thought questions from GSM8K and MATH, but the model being updated is already DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, which has been trained on a much broader math instruction set and has strong pre-existing general mathematical competence. So RL is not starting from scratch; it is refining an already general math model. Second, the authors explicitly say in the discussion that RL seems to “enhance the model’s overall performance by rendering the output distribution more robust,” and that the gain is “attributed to boosting the correct response from TopK rather than the enhancement of fundamental capabilities.” That is their own interpretation.

So the improvement on CMATH should not be read as evidence that RL learned a new abstract reasoning algorithm from only GSM8K and MATH. A more plausible interpretation is that RL shifted probability mass toward better reasoning traces, better final answers, and more robust trajectories that are useful across similar math tasks. CMATH is out-of-domain in the narrow sense that it was not in the RL training set, but it is still very much in the same broad domain: mathematical word problems with step-by-step reasoning. If a policy gets better at producing coherent multi-step solutions, avoiding brittle mistakes, and selecting more correct completions among many sampled traces, that often transfers to nearby benchmarks even without direct exposure.

This also fits the paper’s Pass@$K$ versus Maj@$K$ analysis. Their claim is essentially that RL improves the ranking or concentration of good solutions, not necessarily the set of solutions the model is capable of generating at all. In other words, the model may not know fundamentally more, but it becomes better at putting higher probability on the right reasoning path and suppressing weaker ones. That kind of distributional sharpening naturally helps both in-domain and nearby out-of-domain tasks.

So the best way to think about the CMATH gain is: it is evidence of transfer through shared reasoning format and improved calibration/selection over solution traces, not strong evidence that RL discovered a domain-general “reasoning skill” independent of the training distribution. The paper itself leans toward the former, not the latter.
