\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### Why does GRPO subtract the KL term directly in the objective instead of putting it into the reward like PPO?
Question:
The paper contrasts PPO and GRPO in how they use KL regularization. PPO adds a per-token KL penalty into the reward,

$ r_t = r_\varphi(q, o_{\le t}) - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(o_t \mid q, o_{<t})}{\pi_{ref}(o_t \mid q, o_{<t})}, $

while GRPO subtracts a KL divergence term directly in the objective.

I don't fully understand why this change matters. The paper says this avoids complicating the calculation of $\hat{A}_{i,t}$, but I don't see the exact issue. Would including KL inside the reward interfere with the group-relative normalization, or would it make the baseline depend on the current policy in an undesirable way?

Could someone explain the practical and mathematical reason for moving the KL term outside the reward in GRPO?
Answer:
In GRPO, the KL term is moved outside the reward because the role of the KL penalty is different from the role of the task reward, and GRPO’s advantage estimator is built specifically to operate on the task reward only.

The paper’s GRPO advantage $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ is computed from the rewards of the sampled outputs within a group by subtracting the group mean and dividing by the group standard deviation. In the outcome-supervision case, for example, they set every token in an output to the same normalized group reward, $\hat{A}_{i,t} = \widetilde{r}_i$. The point is that $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ measures how much better or worse one sampled completion is than the other completions for the same question. That comparison is supposed to reflect the quality of the answer, not how far the current policy has drifted from the reference model.

If you were to fold the KL penalty into the reward, then the “reward” used to build the group-relative baseline would no longer be a pure score of the sampled solution. It would become

$$
r_i^{\text{total}} = r_i - \beta \, \mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}})
$$

or, in the token-level PPO form, a sum of task reward and per-token KL penalties. Then the group mean and variance used to normalize rewards would mix together two conceptually different signals: answer quality and policy regularization. That makes the relative baseline depend on the current policy’s divergence from the reference model, which is not what the group-relative normalization is meant to measure.

So the practical reason is simply separation of concerns: GRPO wants the group statistics to estimate “which sampled answers are better,” while the KL term is just a regularizer that keeps the policy close to the reference model. Putting the KL term inside the reward would contaminate the group-relative advantage with a policy-shift penalty, making $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ harder to interpret and compute cleanly.

Mathematically, the paper is saying that the KL penalty should be treated as an external regularization term in the objective rather than as part of the reward signal used for advantage estimation. This is especially natural in GRPO because there is no critic/value function; the method relies entirely on group-normalized rewards, so any extra term inserted into the reward would directly affect the baseline estimation. By keeping KL outside, GRPO can compute $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ from the sampled outputs alone, exactly as the paper states, while still regularizing the policy toward $\pi_{\text{ref}}$.

So the short answer is: yes, including KL inside the reward would interfere with the group-relative normalization, and that is precisely why the paper separates it. The KL is meant to constrain the policy update, not to participate in the within-group comparison of answers.
