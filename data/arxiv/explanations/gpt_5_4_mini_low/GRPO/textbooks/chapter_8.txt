\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 8: 8. Evaluating DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B Against Open and Closed Models

# Why instruction-tuned evaluation differs from base-model evaluation

Evaluating DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is not the same as evaluating the base model DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, because instruction tuning changes the model’s behavior from a general next-token predictor into a system explicitly trained to follow problem-solving instructions. In this paper, the instruction-tuned model is built on top of the base model using a curated set of mathematical instruction data, where each example pairs a problem with a solution written in chain-of-thought, program-of-thought, or tool-integrated reasoning format. That means the evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is specifically meant to measure how well the model can perform in a conversational or task-oriented setting after being trained to produce structured solutions.

This distinction matters because the model is evaluated under two different kinds of conditions. In one setting, tool use is disallowed, so the model must generate a self-contained natural language solution. In the other, tool use is allowed, so the model may integrate natural language reasoning with program-based computation. The paper emphasizes both settings because they probe different aspects of mathematical competence. Without tools, the model must express and carry through the reasoning itself. With tools, the model must decide when to use Python-style computation and how to coordinate it with reasoning. A model that excels in one setting may not necessarily excel in the other, so the instruction-tuned evaluation is broader and more application-oriented than base-model evaluation.

# Benchmarking against GPT-4, Gemini, GLM-4, and other proprietary systems

The paper compares DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B against several proprietary systems, including GPT-4, GPT-4 Code Interpreter, Gemini Ultra, Gemini Pro, Inflection-2, Grok-1, Baichuan-3, and GLM-4. These systems are described as closed-source models, many of which are general-purpose and have undergone alignment procedures. The comparison is important because these systems represent the strongest available reference points for mathematical reasoning at the time of the paper.

The main result is that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B becomes highly competitive with many proprietary models, especially in the setting where tool use is allowed. Under the no-tool setting, it performs strongly on the competition-level MATH benchmark and rivals the Chinese proprietary models GLM-4 and Baichuan-3, though it still remains below GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra. The paper therefore presents DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B as a model that closes much of the gap to top proprietary systems, but does not completely eliminate it. This makes the evaluation especially meaningful: the model is not merely strong among open models, but is approaching the performance of major closed systems on a difficult benchmark.

# Benchmarking against open-source general and math-specialized models

The comparison set of open-source models includes both general-purpose LLMs and models specifically enhanced for mathematics. The general models include DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, Qwen 72B, SeaLLM-v2 7B, ChatGLM3 6B, and Mistral 7B. The math-specialized group includes InternLM2-Math 20B, Math-Shepherd-Mistral 7B, WizardMath, MetaMath 70B, ToRA 34B, and MAmmoTH 70B. This is a particularly strong baseline set because it contains models that are not only large, but also designed or tuned specifically for mathematical reasoning.

Against this broad set, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B performs exceptionally well. The paper states that it beats all 7B counterparts and is comparable with 70B open-source instruction-tuned models. This is an important point because it suggests that instruction tuning on carefully chosen math data can make a relatively small model behave like a much larger one in this domain. The comparison also shows that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B surpasses all open-source models on the competition-level MATH benchmark and does so even when compared with systems that are much larger or that have undergone previous math-focused reinforcement learning. In the tool-augmented setting, it surpasses all existing open-source models as well.

# Step-by-step reasoning performance without tool use

When tool use is disallowed, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B must solve problems using only its internal reasoning and natural language output. In this setting, the model is evaluated on quantitative reasoning benchmarks in English and Chinese. The paper emphasizes that this is a demanding test because the model must produce a self-contained solution rather than rely on external computation.

The results show that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B has strong step-by-step reasoning ability. On the competition-level MATH dataset, it surpasses all open-source models and most proprietary models by at least $9\%$ absolute, while still trailing GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra. The paper also notes that, relative to the base model, the instruction-tuned system is especially effective at expressing structured reasoning in a format aligned with benchmark expectations. This suggests that instruction tuning does more than simply polish the output style; it substantially improves the model’s ability to carry out multi-step mathematical reasoning in a way that can be evaluated directly.

The no-tool results are especially significant because they reflect a more stringent form of reasoning. If a model can solve a problem without code execution, it indicates that the model has internalized useful problem-solving patterns rather than depending on external calculation. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B’s performance shows that the curated instruction data successfully teaches the model to perform this kind of reasoning at a high level.

# Tool-integrated reasoning performance on quantitative math tasks

The paper also evaluates DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B when it is allowed to combine natural language reasoning with program-based tool use. In this setup, the model can solve math problems by writing and executing code, and the benchmark assesses the answer produced by that execution. This tool-integrated evaluation is conducted on quantitative reasoning tasks such as GSM8K and MATH.

The results are notably strong. Under this evaluation setting, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B approaches an accuracy of $60\%$ on MATH and surpasses all existing open-source models. This is a major finding because it shows that the model is not only strong at pure reasoning, but also effective at deciding when and how to use computation as part of the solution process. The paper’s training data was designed to support exactly this kind of behavior, since the instruction-tuning set includes examples in program-of-thought and tool-integrated reasoning formats.

What makes this result especially meaningful is that tool use is not treated as a separate specialized capability detached from reasoning. Instead, the model learns to integrate it into the problem-solving process. That is why the paper highlights tool-augmented evaluation alongside standard chain-of-thought evaluation: the two settings together show the model’s flexibility in mathematical reasoning.

# Strong MATH performance among open models

Among the results reported in the paper, the performance on the competition-level MATH benchmark is one of the clearest signs of the model’s strength. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B performs at a level that is unusual for an open-source model of its size. In the no-tool setting, it surpasses all open-source competitors on MATH. In the tool-augmented setting, it reaches nearly $60\%$ accuracy, again exceeding all open-source baselines.

The paper repeatedly emphasizes MATH because it is a difficult benchmark and a strong indicator of advanced mathematical reasoning. Success on MATH is meaningful not just because of the score itself, but because it reflects a model’s ability to handle competition-level problems that require careful multi-step reasoning. The authors view their results as the first time the open-source community crosses the $50\%$ mark on MATH with a model in this family, which underscores how unusually strong the instruction-tuned system is.

This performance is also consistent with the paper’s broader thesis that targeted data selection and instruction tuning matter greatly. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B does not achieve its performance simply by being large. Rather, it benefits from a carefully constructed math pre-training corpus, followed by instruction tuning on a mix of reasoning formats.

# Comparison with much larger 70B-scale models

One of the most striking claims in the paper is that DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B can compete with, and in some cases outperform, much larger 70B-scale models. The benchmark comparisons include models such as Qwen 72B, MetaMath 70B, WizardMath based on Llama-2 70B, and MAmmoTH 70B. These models are important reference points because they represent the conventional assumption that much larger models should dominate smaller ones.

DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B challenges that assumption. The paper reports that it beats all 7B counterparts and is comparable with 70B open-source instruction-tuned models. In the no-tool setting, it outperforms large models that have been specifically tuned for mathematics. In the tool-augmented setting, it surpasses all existing open-source models, despite being far smaller than many of them.

This comparison reinforces a central message of the paper: scale alone is not the decisive factor. High-quality data, especially domain-specific data for mathematics, and instruction tuning in the right formats can produce large gains even in a relatively compact model. The comparison with 70B models shows that the model’s effectiveness comes from how it was trained, not just from parameter count.

# Remaining gap to top proprietary systems

Even though DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is highly competitive, the paper is careful to note that it does not fully match the strongest proprietary models. In the no-tool setting, it still underperforms GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra. This gap is important because it defines the boundary of what the paper achieves. The model gets very close to top proprietary performance, but the state of the art in closed models remains ahead.

The paper also notes that while DeepSeekMath-Instruct rivals GLM-4 and Baichuan-3 on MATH, it still falls short of GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra. In other words, the model closes much of the open-source gap, but not the entire gap to the strongest closed systems. This is consistent with the broader framing of the paper: the contribution is to push open models closer to the frontier, not to claim complete parity with the most advanced proprietary systems.

This remaining gap is also useful because it helps identify what the current approach has and has not solved. The model’s strong performance demonstrates that the training pipeline is effective, but the fact that it still trails the very best closed models suggests that further improvements in data, instruction tuning, and reinforcement learning are still needed.

# What these results reveal about the value of targeted data and SFT

Taken together, the evaluation results show that targeted data and supervised fine-tuning are highly valuable for mathematical reasoning. The model’s performance does not come from generic scaling alone. Instead, the paper shows that a carefully selected math corpus from Common Crawl, combined with math-oriented instruction tuning, yields a model that is unusually strong for its size.

The comparison against both open and closed models helps make this point concrete. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B outperforms large open-source baselines, including models specialized for math, and becomes competitive with several proprietary systems. Its success in both no-tool and tool-augmented settings suggests that SFT with a diverse mix of chain-of-thought, program-of-thought, and tool-integrated examples meaningfully improves mathematical reasoning behavior. The model learns not only to produce answers, but to produce them in the forms that are most useful for solving difficult problems.

The evaluation therefore supports the paper’s larger claim that data quality and task-specific training matter at least as much as parameter count. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B shows that a relatively small model can become highly capable when the pre-training corpus is carefully engineered and the instruction-tuning data is aligned with the kinds of reasoning the benchmarks demand.
