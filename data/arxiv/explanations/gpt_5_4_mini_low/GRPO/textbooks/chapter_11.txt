\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 11: 11. Training and Evaluating DeepSeekMath-RL 7B

# RL Training Data Drawn from GSM8K and MATH Chain-of-Thought Questions

DeepSeekMath-RL 7B is trained by applying reinforcement learning on top of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B. The reinforcement learning data are not broad or unrestricted: they are specifically chain-of-thought-format questions related to GSM8K and MATH, drawn from the supervised fine-tuning data. In total, this RL training set contains around 144K questions. The paper emphasizes that these are reasoning-style problems already formatted for step-by-step solution generation, which makes them suitable for reinforcement learning aimed at improving mathematical reasoning.

This choice of data is important because the model is not being exposed to an entirely new training distribution at this stage. Instead, the RL phase focuses on questions already associated with mathematical reasoning behavior. The goal is to refine and strengthen the model’s reasoning policy, not to relearn mathematics from scratch. By concentrating on GSM8K and MATH, the RL setup also allows the authors to study whether reinforcement learning can improve performance even when the training domain is relatively narrow.

# Why the RL Stage Uses Only a Subset of the SFT Data

The RL stage deliberately uses only a subset of the instruction tuning data rather than the full supervised fine-tuning set. Specifically, the authors exclude other SFT questions and keep only the chain-of-thought-format GSM8K and MATH questions. The reason given is experimental clarity: they want to investigate the effect of RL on benchmarks that do not appear in the RL training phase.

This design helps isolate what reinforcement learning is actually doing. If the RL data included many benchmark-specific question types, then improvements on evaluation tasks might be harder to interpret. By restricting RL to GSM8K and MATH, the authors can better assess whether the gains transfer beyond the exact questions used in training. The resulting improvements on other benchmarks therefore provide evidence that RL is not simply memorizing the training set, but changing the model’s behavior more generally.

# Reward Model Training Based on DeepSeekMath-Base 7B

The reinforcement learning pipeline depends on a reward model, and the paper states that the initial reward model is trained based on DeepSeekMath-Base 7B. The training set for the reward model follows the procedure from the authors’ earlier math reinforcement learning work. In other words, the reward model is not an external black box; it is built within the same modeling family and adapted to the mathematical reasoning setting.

The role of the reward model is to score sampled outputs produced by the policy model during RL. These scores then determine how the policy should be updated. Because the reward model is trained from the base mathematical model, it is intended to provide feedback aligned with the same type of reasoning capability that the main model is trying to improve. The paper frames this as part of the broader GRPO design, in which reward modeling and policy optimization are tightly connected.

# GRPO Hyperparameters: Learning Rate, KL Coefficient, Group Size, and Batch Size

DeepSeekMath-RL 7B is trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization, or GRPO. The paper gives several concrete training hyperparameters. The policy model learning rate is set to $1 \times 10^{-6}$. The KL coefficient is $0.04$. For each question, the model samples $64$ outputs. The maximum output length is $1024$, and the training batch size is $1024$.

These values reflect the RL setup used to make GRPO stable and effective in practice. The group size of $64$ sampled outputs is especially important because GRPO uses comparisons within a group to estimate relative advantage. Rather than depending on a critic model, GRPO relies on group-level reward statistics. The KL coefficient provides regularization toward the reference model, while the batch size and output length define the scale of each RL step.

# Single Update After Each Exploration Stage

The RL procedure is organized so that the policy model receives only a single update after each exploration stage. This means the model first samples a batch of outputs, computes their rewards, and then performs one policy update based on that exploration. The paper presents this as part of the practical GRPO training setup used for DeepSeekMath-RL 7B.

This design matters because it keeps the RL loop tightly coupled to freshly sampled behavior. The policy is not repeatedly optimized on stale samples from an earlier policy state. Instead, the model explores, receives feedback, and updates once before the next round of exploration. In the paper’s framing, this supports the idea of reinforcement learning as a process that continually responds to the current policy’s output distribution.

# Defining In-Domain and Out-of-Domain Evaluation Tasks

The evaluation protocol distinguishes between in-domain and out-of-domain tasks. For DeepSeekMath-RL 7B, GSM8K and MATH under chain-of-thought reasoning are treated as in-domain tasks, because these are the same benchmark families that supply the RL training questions. All other benchmarks are treated as out-of-domain tasks.

This distinction is central to interpreting the results. Improvements on GSM8K and MATH show that RL is effective on the target reasoning tasks it directly trains on. Improvements on the remaining benchmarks, including Chinese benchmarks and other mathematical tasks, indicate that the learned policy changes are not narrowly confined to the training distribution. The paper uses this in-domain versus out-of-domain split to show both direct gains and transfer effects.

# Performance Gains on GSM8K and MATH

The main quantitative result for DeepSeekMath-RL 7B is that it achieves $88.2\%$ on GSM8K and $51.7\%$ on MATH using chain-of-thought reasoning. The paper describes this as surpassing all open-source models in the $7$B to $70$B range, as well as the majority of closed-source models. The $51.7\%$ result on MATH is especially significant because it is on the competition-level benchmark and represents a major step for open-source mathematical reasoning.

These results show that RL can improve a model that is already strong after supervised fine-tuning. DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B is already competitive, but DeepSeekMath-RL 7B improves further. The paper reports that RL increases performance from $82.9\%$ to $88.2\%$ on GSM8K and from $46.8\%$ to $51.7\%$ on MATH. This gain is notable because it comes from training on a narrow RL dataset centered on just GSM8K and MATH chain-of-thought questions.

# Improvements on Chinese and Other Out-of-Domain Benchmarks

The paper also reports improvements beyond the RL training domain. In particular, DeepSeekMath-RL 7B improves on out-of-domain mathematical tasks, including Chinese benchmarks such as CMATH, where performance rises from $84.6\%$ to $88.8\%$ during reinforcement learning. The authors interpret these gains as evidence that the RL stage strengthens mathematical reasoning more broadly, rather than only boosting exact-match behavior on the training benchmarks.

This is consistent with their broader observation that math training and RL can influence general reasoning ability. Because the model is evaluated on both English and Chinese benchmarks, the gains suggest that the policy update learned useful reasoning refinements that transfer across languages and problem styles. The paper presents this as one of the key strengths of the reinforcement learning stage.

# Why the RL Improvements Are Notable Despite Narrow RL Training Data

One of the most important points in the paper is that the RL improvements are achieved using only a restricted subset of the SFT data. The RL phase uses chain-of-thought questions from GSM8K and MATH, yet the resulting model improves not only on those benchmarks but also on several out-of-domain tasks. This makes the gains notable because they are not purchased with broad additional supervision.

The authors highlight that DeepSeekMath-RL 7B outperforms DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B across all evaluation metrics, despite the constrained RL training scope. This indicates that reinforcement learning can extract additional reasoning performance from an already well-trained instruction model. In the paper’s interpretation, RL is effective not because it introduces large amounts of new knowledge, but because it reshapes the model’s output distribution toward better answers.

# Self-Consistency and Multi-Sample Decoding Results

The paper also reports that self-consistency improves performance further. For DeepSeekMath-RL 7B, the authors emphasize the gains achieved through multi-sample decoding, and more generally they note that self-consistency over 64 samples from DeepSeekMath 7B reaches $60.9\%$ on MATH. This shows that sampling multiple candidate solutions and aggregating them can improve final accuracy beyond a single decoded answer.

This result fits the paper’s broader view of reasoning as a distributional problem. A model may already contain a strong correct solution path, but it may not always produce it in the top response. By sampling many outputs, self-consistency can recover correct reasoning more reliably. In the context of DeepSeekMath-RL, these results reinforce the idea that the model’s output distribution has become more favorable after RL, making correct responses more accessible under multi-sample decoding.

The paper’s overall conclusion is that reinforcement learning, especially with GRPO, strengthens the model even after strong supervised fine-tuning. The improvement is seen in direct benchmark performance, in out-of-domain transfer, and in multi-sample decoding behavior, all while using a relatively focused RL training set.
