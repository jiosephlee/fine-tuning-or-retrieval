\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does DPO not need on-policy sampling during training if the RLHF objective is an expectation over $y \sim \pi_\theta$?
Question:
One thing I find surprising is that standard RLHF usually samples from the current policy during optimization, but DPO trains only on a fixed dataset of preference pairs.

Since the original RL objective is

$$
\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \|\| \pi_{\text{ref}}),
$$

I would have expected the optimization to depend on the model's current generations. But DPO turns it into a binary classification objective over offline pairs.

What is the exact reason this offline objective is enough? Is it because the Bradley-Terry likelihood already contains all the information needed about the optimal policy ratios, or is there still some hidden distribution-shift issue that is being ignored?
Answer:
The key point is that DPO is not directly optimizing the original expectation over $y \sim \pi_\theta$ in the way PPO does. Instead, the paper shows that under the Bradley-Terry preference model and the KL-regularized RLHF objective, you can reparameterize the reward in terms of the optimal policy itself, and then the policy optimization problem becomes equivalent to fitting a pairwise preference classifier on an offline dataset.

Concretely, the RLHF objective is

$$
\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \| \pi_{\text{ref}}),
$$

and its optimizer has the closed form

$$
\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right).
$$

That means you can invert the relationship and write the reward as

$$
r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x).
$$

Now the crucial observation in the paper is that Bradley-Terry preferences depend only on reward differences, $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$. So when you substitute this expression into the Bradley-Terry likelihood, the partition term $\beta \log Z(x)$ cancels out. The result is a preference probability written entirely in terms of the policy ratio $\pi/\pi_{\text{ref}}$, and maximizing the likelihood of the observed preference pairs gives the DPO loss

$$
-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim \mathcal D}\left[\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right].
$$

So the offline dataset is enough because DPO is not trying to estimate an expectation by sampling from the current policy. It is instead doing maximum likelihood on a reparameterized preference model whose optimal policy is analytically linked to the reward-maximizing RLHF solution. In that sense, the dataset already contains the supervision signal needed to identify which completions should be relatively more probable than others.

This does not mean distribution shift disappears entirely. The paper is explicit that the reference model matters, because the learned reward is only valid relative to that reference distribution. That is why they sample preference data from $\pi^{\text{SFT}}$ in the standard setup, and when an SFT model is unavailable they initialize $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ by maximizing likelihood on the preferred completions to reduce mismatch. The paper also notes that the reward class is only identifiable up to an additive function of $x$, which is fine because such terms do not affect the optimal policy or the preference probabilities.

So the short answer is: yes, the Bradley-Terry form is doing the heavy lifting, but more precisely the paper exploits the fact that the KL-regularized RLHF optimum can be written as a policy ratio against $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, and that the pairwise preference likelihood depends only on differences. That is why an offline pairwise dataset is sufficient for DPO. What is being “ignored” is not the preference structure, but the need for online rollouts during training; the paper argues that this is unnecessary once you make the right reparameterization.
