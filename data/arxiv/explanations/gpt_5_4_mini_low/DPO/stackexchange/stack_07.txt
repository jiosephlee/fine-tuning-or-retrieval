\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why are reward functions that differ by $f(x)$ considered equivalent?
Question:
The paper defines two reward functions $r(x,y)$ and $r'(x,y)$ as equivalent if

$$
r(x,y) - r'(x,y) = f(x)
$$

for some function of the prompt only.

I can see that such a shift would cancel in pairwise differences like $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$, so the preference model would be unchanged. But the paper also says these reward functions induce the same optimal policy under the KL-constrained RL objective.

I do not immediately see why adding a prompt-dependent constant to all completions leaves the optimal policy unchanged. Is this because it only shifts the expected reward by a constant for each $x$? If so, can someone show that carefully using the objective in the paper?
Answer:
Yes — the invariance comes from the fact that, for a fixed prompt $x$, adding $f(x)$ to every completion shifts the objective by a constant that does not depend on the policy.

The RLHF objective in the paper is, for each prompt $x$,
$$
\max_{\pi(\cdot \mid x)} \; \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta \, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\right).
$$
Now suppose we replace $r$ by
$$
r'(x,y)=r(x,y)-f(x),
$$
where $f(x)$ depends only on the prompt. Then the objective becomes
$$
\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}[r'(x,y)] - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})
= \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}[r(x,y)-f(x)] - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}).
$$
Since $f(x)$ does not depend on $y$, it comes out of the expectation:
$$
= \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}[r(x,y)] - f(x) - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}).
$$
For a given prompt $x$, the term $-f(x)$ is just a constant with respect to the policy $\pi(\cdot \mid x)$. So it cannot change which policy maximizes the expression. In other words, the argmax over $\pi(\cdot \mid x)$ is identical for $r$ and $r'$.

Intuitively, the policy only cares about *relative* utility among completions for the same prompt. A prompt-dependent baseline shifts all completions equally, so it never changes which completion is better than another, nor the tradeoff between reward and KL regularization. This is exactly analogous to the standard RL fact that adding a state-dependent baseline to a reward function does not change the optimal policy.

You can also see this directly from the closed-form optimizer given in the paper:
$$
\pi_r(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x,y)\right).
$$
If you replace $r$ by $r'(x,y)=r(x,y)-f(x)$, then
$$
\pi_{r'}(y \mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}(r(x,y)-f(x))\right)
= e^{-f(x)/\beta}\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right).
$$
The factor $e^{-f(x)/\beta}$ is the same for all $y$ at that $x$, so it gets absorbed into the normalization constant $Z(x)$ and disappears. Therefore $\pi_{r'}(\cdot \mid x)=\pi_r(\cdot \mid x)$.

So the paper’s equivalence relation is saying: reward functions that differ only by a prompt-dependent offset represent the same “preference geometry” and induce the same optimal KL-constrained policy. The offset matters neither to pairwise comparisons nor to the optimal policy, only to the absolute scale/level of the reward.
