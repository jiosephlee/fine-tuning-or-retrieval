\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### Why does FP8 WGMMA only supporting k-major operands create a special problem for attention?
Question:
The FP8 section says that for FP8 WGMMA, only k-major input operands in shared memory are supported, unlike FP16 where both mn-major and k-major are allowed. I understand this as a layout restriction, but I am not seeing why attention is especially affected.

Could someone explain concretely for the two GEMMs in attention:

1. $\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^\top$,
2. $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_i^{(j)} \mathbf{V}_j$,

which operand layouts are naturally produced by the previous steps, and which of those violate the FP8 WGMMA requirements? I think the issue is especially with $\mathbf{V}$ and with reusing the first GEMM's accumulator as the second GEMM's operand, but I would like to see this spelled out more explicitly.
Answer:
The special difficulty is not that attention is mathematically different from any other pair of GEMMs; it is that the two GEMMs in attention are *back-to-back* and the second one wants to consume the first one’s result immediately. In FP16 this is fairly painless because Hopper’s WGMMA accepts both mn-major and k-major shared-memory layouts, so you can usually arrange each operand in whatever layout is convenient. In FP8, however, the paper says that WGMMA only accepts k-major shared-memory operands, so the “natural” layouts produced by attention have to be massaged much more aggressively.

For the first GEMM, $\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^\top$, the paper says that $\mathbf{Q}$, $\mathbf{K}$, and $\mathbf{V}$ are “typically given as contiguous in the head dimension.” Since each tile is shaped like $B_r \times d$ for $\mathbf{Q}_i$ and $B_c \times d$ for $\mathbf{K}_j$, being contiguous in the head dimension means they are already contiguous in the inner dimension $d$, i.e. k-major. So the first GEMM is not the problematic one: its inputs are naturally compatible with FP8 WGMMA’s restriction, at least in the layout sense the paper is discussing.

The second GEMM, $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_i^{(j)} \mathbf{V}_j$, is where the trouble starts. Here the right operand is a tile of $\mathbf{V}$, and those tiles are also naturally stored contiguous in the head dimension, because that is how the model tensors come in from upstream. But for this GEMM, the paper needs the $\mathbf{V}$ tile in shared memory to be contiguous in the *sequence-length* dimension so that it is k-major in the GEMM’s operand sense. That is the opposite of the usual layout, and the paper explicitly says that TMA cannot change the contiguous dimension on load. So $\mathbf{V}$ violates the FP8 WGMMA requirement unless you transpose it, either in GMEM beforehand or in-kernel after the load. This is why the paper singles out $\mathbf{V}$ as the first layout mismatch.

There is a second, subtler issue that is specific to attention and not just to $\mathbf{V}$ itself: the output of the first GEMM is an accumulator tile in FP32, and after softmax you want to reuse that data as the left operand of the second GEMM. In FP8, the paper points out that the FP32 accumulator layout produced by the first WGMMA is not the same as the register layout expected for operand $A$ of the second WGMMA. So even if you fix $\mathbf{V}$, you still cannot directly feed the first GEMM’s accumulator into the second GEMM. That is why the paper introduces a byte-permutation of the accumulator registers, and then arranges the in-kernel transpose of $\mathbf{V}$ to produce a matching row permutation. In other words, the two operands have to be made layout-compatible with each other, not just individually compatible with the hardware.

So the concrete picture is: $\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^\top$ is naturally fine because $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ already come in head-dimension-contiguous form, which matches k-major. The second GEMM is the hard one because $\mathbf{V}$ arrives in the “wrong” contiguous dimension for FP8 WGMMA, and because the intermediate $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}$ lives in an accumulator layout that does not match the next GEMM’s operand layout. That combination is exactly why attention, with its fused GEMM-softmax-GEMM structure, creates a special problem for FP8 WGMMA.
