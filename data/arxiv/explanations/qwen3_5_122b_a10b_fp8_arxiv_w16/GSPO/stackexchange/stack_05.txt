\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Is GSPO theoretically equivalent to PPO with GAE but without a value network?
Question:
Reading through Sections 2 and 3, it seems GSPO replaces PPO's value-function-based advantage estimation with group-relative normalization (like GRPO). However, unlike GRPO which sums over tokens with individual weights, GSPO treats the whole sequence as one unit.

If we view PPO as maximizing expected return subject to a KL constraint approximated by clipping:
$$ J(\theta) = E [ min(r_t A_t, clip(r_t) A_t)] $$
is GSPO essentially just applying this logic at a coarser granularity where $r_t$ becomes $s_i$ and $A_t$ becomes $ΔA_i$?

More importantly, does removing token-level weighting mean we lose any ability to penalize specific bad actions within an otherwise good trajectory? For instance, if a model generates a brilliant proof except for one hallucinated number near the end causing low reward, does GSPO treat all tokens in that sequence equally negatively?
Answer:
Your intuition regarding the structural similarity between GSPO and PPO/GRPO is largely correct, but there is a critical theoretical distinction concerning the definition of the importance ratio that separates GSPO from simply being "token-weightless PPO." You are right that GSPO removes the need for a separate value network by using group-relative advantage estimation ($\widehat{A}_i$) exactly as GRPO does, treating the entire sequence as a single unit for reward assignment. However, GSPO is not merely applying PPO logic at a coarser granularity with uniform token weights; rather, it fundamentally redefines the importance sampling weight itself to resolve a theoretical inconsistency identified in GRPO.

In standard PPO and GRPO, the importance ratio is calculated token-by-token ($w_{i,t}$), which the authors argue violates the principles of importance sampling because it attempts to correct distributional shifts based on single-sample token probabilities rather than the joint probability of the entire sequence. As explained in Section 3 ("Motivation"), applying these token-level weights introduces high-variance noise that accumulates over long sequences, leading to model collapse. GSPO addresses this by defining the importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ based on **sequence likelihood** (Equation 6), specifically using a geometric mean of token ratios raised to the power of $1/|y_i|$. This ensures that the optimization unit (the sequence) matches the reward unit (the sequence score), providing a theoretically grounded correction for off-policy samples. Therefore, while both algorithms lack a value network and use group-relative advantages, GSPO's clipping and weighting operate on a valid sequence-level statistic, whereas GRPO's operate on unstable token-level statistics.

Regarding your concern about losing granular control—specifically whether a single hallucinated number causes all tokens in a brilliant proof to be penalized equally—the paper confirms that in its base form, **yes**, every token in that specific trajectory receives the same negative gradient signal scaled by $\widehat{A}_i$. Because $\widehat{A}_i$ is constant for all tokens $t$ within response $y_i$, and the sequence-level weight $s_i(\theta)$ applies uniformly across all tokens in Equation 9 (derived from Equation 5), there is no mechanism within standard GSPO to distinguish between "good" early tokens and "bad" late tokens if they belong to the same low-reward sequence. The algorithm treats the response as an atomic unit: if the final output is poor due to any part of it, the entire trajectory is downweighted.

However, this limitation is explicitly acknowledged and addressed in Section 4.2 via **GSPO-token**. If one requires finer-grained control where specific bad actions can be penalized differently within a trajectory (e.g., assigning lower advantages $\widehat{A}_{i,t}$ to hallucinated steps while keeping high advantages for correct reasoning steps), one can switch to this variant. Crucially, GSPO-token retains the stable sequence-level importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ (computed globally) but allows for per-token advantage customization ($\widehat{A}_{i,t}$) inside the objective sum (Equation 10). This hybrid approach preserves GSPO's stability benefits while restoring the ability to differentiate action quality within a sequence, effectively answering your worry: standard GSPO sacrifices intra-sequence differentiation for stability, but GSPO-token offers both if your infrastructure supports customizing advantages per token.
