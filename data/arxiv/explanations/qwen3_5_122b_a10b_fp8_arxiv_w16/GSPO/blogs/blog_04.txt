\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

# Less Clipping, More Gains: How GSPO Flips the Script on Reinforcement Learning

It sounds almost paradoxical in the world of machine learning: to train a model faster and more effectively, you sometimes need to throw away more data. For years, the prevailing wisdom in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Language Models (LLMs) suggested that aggressive clipping—discarding data points that deviate too far from the expected policy—was a necessary evil that inevitably slowed down convergence. However, a new algorithm called Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) is turning this intuition on its head. By shifting the focus from individual tokens to entire sequences, GSPO demonstrates that filtering out a massive amount of "noisy" data can actually accelerate learning and stabilize training in ways previously thought impossible.

## The Problem with Token-Level Noise

To understand why GSPO is revolutionary, we first need to look at its predecessor, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO was designed to eliminate the need for a separate value model by comparing groups of responses to the same query. It calculates an advantage score for each response and applies it uniformly across every token in that response. However, GRPO relies on **token-level importance ratios**. This means it tries to correct for differences between the old policy and the new policy one word at a time.

The paper identifies a critical flaw in this approach: applying importance sampling weights at the token level creates high-variance noise. When generating long sequences, these small errors accumulate exponentially. As training progresses, this accumulated noise causes unstable gradients, which can eventually lead to "catastrophic model collapse," where the model irreversibly loses its capabilities. This instability is particularly severe in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, where the dynamic routing of experts causes token probabilities to fluctuate wildly between training steps. To combat this with GRPO, engineers had to implement complex workarounds like "Routing Replay," which caches expert activations to artificially stabilize calculations—a solution that adds significant memory overhead and complexity.

## The GSPO Solution: Think Sequences, Not Tokens

GSPO addresses these fundamental instabilities by adhering strictly to the principles of importance sampling: if you want to estimate an expectation under a target distribution using samples from a behavior distribution, you must weigh by the ratio of their densities. In language modeling, since rewards are assigned to entire responses (sequences), the importance ratio should also be calculated at the **sequence level**, not the token level.

Instead of calculating a separate weight for every single word $y_t$, GSPO computes a single importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ for the whole response $y_i$:

$$ s_{i}(\theta) = \left( \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_i | x) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}} $$

Here, $\pi_{\theta}(y|x)$ represents the likelihood of generating the entire sequence under the current policy. By raising this ratio to the power of $1/|y_i|$ (length normalization), GSPO ensures that longer sequences don't dominate simply because they have more tokens, keeping values within a manageable numerical range. Crucially, this approach treats all tokens within a response equally regarding their contribution to the gradient update once weighted by this global sequence factor. This eliminates the erratic fluctuations caused by individual token mismatches in MoE architectures.

## The Counter-Intuitive Power of Aggressive Clipping

The most striking discovery in evaluating GSPO lies in how it handles outliers through clipping. In traditional algorithms like GRPO or Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), clipping acts as a safety valve; if an importance ratio exceeds a certain threshold (e.g., $1 + \epsilon$), it is capped to prevent drastic updates. Because these algorithms operate on thousands of individual tokens per batch, they tend to clip only those specific tokens that are extreme outliers while retaining others from potentially flawed responses.

GSPO takes a different stance: it clips based on whether an *entire sequence* is too far off-policy. If a generated response has an importance ratio outside the safe range ($1 - \epsilon$ to $1 + \epsilon$), **every token** in that response is discarded from the gradient calculation for that step. Empirical results show that GSPO clips significantly more data than GRPO—often by two orders of magnitude in terms of total clipped tokens per batch.

One might assume discarding such vast amounts of data would cripple learning efficiency due to poor sample utilization. Yet, experiments reveal the opposite: **GSPO trains faster and reaches higher performance ceilings.** Why? Because GRPO's retained tokens often carry high-variance noise that misleads the optimizer. By aggressively filtering out entire low-quality or off-distribution sequences before they ever influence the gradient update, GSPO provides a cleaner, more reliable learning signal. It trades raw quantity for high quality, allowing the model to converge more rapidly toward optimal policies without getting derailed by statistical noise.

## Stabilizing Mixture-of-Experts and Scaling Up

The benefits of GSPO extend beyond just speed; they fundamentally solve stability issues inherent in modern large-scale architectures like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE). In MoE models, different parts of the network activate depending on input content. Under GRPO's token-level logic, slight shifts in expert activation between training steps cause massive swings in probability ratios for individual tokens, breaking training stability unless mitigated by complex strategies like Routing Replay.

Because GSPO operates on sequence likelihoods rather than individual token probabilities, it is remarkably robust against these internal architectural fluctuations. The overall probability of generating a coherent sentence remains relatively stable even if specific expert paths shift slightly during training updates. Consequently, GSPO renders techniques like Routing Replay obsolete for MoE models entirely. This not only simplifies RL infrastructure but also removes artificial constraints on model capacity, allowing MoE models to utilize their full potential without needing special engineering hacks just to stay stable during training cycles.

Furthermore, this sequence-level approach simplifies deployment pipelines involving separate training and inference engines (like Megatron paired with vLLM or SGLang). Traditional methods often required re-computing token-level likelihoods using precise training-engine arithmetic because inference engines might introduce floating-point discrepancies at every step. Since GSPO relies on aggregate sequence likelihoods—which are far more tolerant of minor precision variations—it allows practitioners to directly use likelihoods computed by fast inference engines for optimization updates without costly recomputation steps on slower training hardware.

## From Theory to Qwen3 Success

The theoretical elegance of GSPO has translated into tangible real-world results within Alibaba's Qwen3 series models trained with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Benchmarks including AIME'24 for mathematics and LiveCodeBench/CodeForces for programming demonstrate that models trained with GSPO achieve superior pass rates compared to those trained with GRPO under identical compute budgets and query sets. Most importantly, these gains were achieved while maintaining stable training dynamics over extended periods and longer generation lengths—conditions where other algorithms frequently fail or collapse completely.

By recognizing that rewards belong at the sequence level and designing optimization objectives accordingly, researchers have unlocked a new frontier in RL stability and efficiency less clipping isn't just acceptable; it's essential when done correctly at scale." As we continue pushing towards larger models and more complex reasoning tasks, algorithms like GSPO provide the robust foundation needed to harness reinforcement learning safely and effectively at unprecedented scales."
