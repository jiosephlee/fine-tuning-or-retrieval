\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### How does Re-scaled OFT preserve Hyperspherical Energy if magnitudes are changed?
Question:
Section 3.4 introduces Re-scaled OFT with a diagonal matrix $\bm{D}$ containing learnable positive scalars $s_i$. The paper argues that 're-scaling neurons does not change the hyperspherical energy (the magnitude will be normalized out).' Looking at Eq (1), HE depends on $\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\|$. If we update weights as $\bm{W}_{new} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D}$, then each column vector becomes scaled by its corresponding $s_i$. When we normalize these new columns to calculate HE, indeed the scalar factors cancel out ($s_i \bm{v} / \| s_i \bm{v} \| = \hat{\bm{v}}$). So strictly speaking, HE is preserved regardless of $s_i$. But if HE is purely angular and invariant to scale, why do we need to learn these scales at all? Doesn't this contradict the premise that preserving angles is sufficient? Or is the magnitude information critical for something else in diffusion models that isn't captured by HE?
Answer:
Your intuition regarding the mathematical definition of Hyperspherical Energy (HE) is entirely correct: since HE is calculated using normalized vectors $\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\|$, multiplying any neuron $\bm{w}_i$ by a positive scalar $s_i$ cancels out during normalization, leaving the angular relationships and thus the HE value unchanged. The paper explicitly acknowledges this in Section 3.4, stating that "re-scaling neurons does not change the hyperspherical energy."

However, the necessity of learning these scales in Re-scaled OFT arises because preserving HE is a **constraint** designed to protect semantic structure, not a complete description of the model's functional behavior. While the *angles* between neurons dictate the relative orientation of features (which corresponds to semantic content, as argued via the Fourier phase analogy in Section 3.1), the *magnitudes* of these neurons determine the strength or sensitivity of those features in the final output. In a linear layer $\bm{z} = \bm{W}^\top \bm{x}$, if we replace $\bm{W}$ with $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D}$, the output becomes a weighted sum where each transformed neuron contributes according to its new scale $s_i$.

If we were restricted to pure orthogonal transformations (where all $s_i=1$), we would be limited to rotating or reflecting the entire subspace spanned by the neurons without altering their individual "gain." This might be insufficient for specific downstream tasks where certain feature channels need to be amplified or attenuated independently to match new data distributions or control signals. By introducing $\bm{D}$, Re-scaled OFT decouples two objectives: maintaining the **structural integrity** of the pretrained model (via $\bm{R}$ preserving angles/HE) and optimizing **feature responsiveness** (via $\bm{D}$ adjusting magnitudes). The paper notes in Section 3.4 that while they primarily report results using standard OFT to isolate the effects of orthogonality, "we find that the re-scaled OFT is generally better," suggesting that allowing magnitude adjustment provides necessary flexibility for convergence and performance without sacrificing the stability guarantees offered by preserving hyperspherical energy. Essentially, HE preservation ensures we don't break what we already know, while learning $s_i$ allows us to tune how strongly we apply that knowledge.
