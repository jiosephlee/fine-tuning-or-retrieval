Chapter 3: # Chapter 3: Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) Methodology

The core innovation presented in this work is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), a principled approach designed to adapt large-scale text-to-image diffusion models to specific downstream tasks without compromising their foundational generative abilities. Traditional finetuning methods often struggle with a fundamental trade-off: they must introduce enough flexibility to learn new concepts—such as a specific subject identity or a new control signal—while maintaining sufficient rigidity to preserve the rich semantic knowledge acquired during pretraining. Existing strategies, such as updating weights with small learning rates or employing low-rank adaptations like LoRA, rely on implicit assumptions about Euclidean distance or rank constraints that do not strictly guarantee the preservation of the model's internal structure. OFT addresses this by introducing a hard constraint rooted in geometry: it ensures that the pairwise relationships between neurons within a layer remain invariant throughout the finetuning process. This chapter delves into the mathematical foundations of OFT, explaining how layer-shared orthogonal transformations allow for semantic modification while provably preserving the hyperspherical energy that characterizes the model's pretrained knowledge.

## Formulating OFT: The Optimization Objective

To understand the formulation of OFT, one must first grasp the concept of hyperspherical energy and its role in encoding semantic information within neural networks. The authors posit that the semantic capability of a pretrained model is deeply tied to the geometric arrangement of its neurons on a unit hypersphere. Hyperspherical energy is defined as a measure of the pairwise relational structure among neurons in a single layer. Specifically, for a layer containing $n$ neurons represented by weight vectors $\{\bm{w}_1, \dots, \bm{w}_n\}$, where each $\bm{w}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$, we normalize these vectors to lie on the unit sphere, denoted as $\hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\|$. The hyperspherical energy $\text{HE}(\bm{W})$ of a weight matrix $\bm{W}$ is calculated as the sum of inverse distances between all pairs of these normalized neurons:

$$
\text{HE}(\bm{W}) := \sum_{i \neq j} \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}
$$

This metric captures the level of uniformity and separation among neurons; intuitively, it reflects how well-distributed the features are on the hypersphere. Empirical observations suggest that this distribution encodes critical semantic information. Consequently, a successful finetuning strategy should not drastically alter this distribution. Instead of merely minimizing the Euclidean distance between the finetuned weights $\bm{W}$ and the pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$, which is an imperfect proxy for semantic preservation, OFT explicitly targets the minimization of the difference in hyperspherical energy between the two states. The primary optimization objective can thus be formulated as finding a new weight matrix $\bm{W}$ such that:

$$
\min_{\bm{W}} \left\| \text{HE}(\bm{W}) - \text{HE}(\bm{W}^0) \right\|
$$

Ideally, we seek a solution where this difference is zero, meaning $\text{HE}(\bm{W}) = \text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$. Achieving exact equality implies that while individual neuron weights may change, their relative angular configuration remains identical to that of the pretrained model. This approach diverges significantly from standard gradient descent updates or low-rank approximations, which treat weight changes as additive perturbations without regard for how these perturbations affect the global geometric structure of the neuron space. By focusing on preserving hyperspherical energy, OFT ensures that the intrinsic "knowledge" stored in the angular relationships between neurons is retained, thereby preventing catastrophic forgetting and maintaining high-fidelity generation capabilities even as new tasks are learned.

## Mathematical Derivation: Preserving Pairwise Angles via Rotation/Reflection

The theoretical elegance of OFT lies in identifying a specific class of transformations that guarantee zero difference in hyperspherical energy. Mathematically, if two sets of vectors possess identical pairwise angles between every corresponding pair, they will necessarily possess identical hyperspherical energies. Consider again our fully connected layer with pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0 = [\bm{w}_1^0, \dots, \bm{w}_n^0]$ and finetuned weights $\bm{W} = [\bm{w}_1, \dots, \bm{w}_n]$. The condition for preserving pairwise angles requires that for any $i$ and $j$:

$$
\cos(\theta_{ij}) = (\hat{\bm{w}}_i)^T (\hat{\bm{w}}_j) = (\hat{\bm{w}}_i^0)^T (\hat{\bm{o}}_j^0)
$$
