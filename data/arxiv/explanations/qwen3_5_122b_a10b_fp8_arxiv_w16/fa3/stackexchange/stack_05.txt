\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What determines the optimal number of stages 's' in the circular SMEM buffer versus pipeline depth?
Question:
The paper describes using a circular SMEM buffer with $s$ stages for producer-consumer synchronization (Algorithm 1) and also proposes a separate '2-stage' or '3-stage' pipelining scheme for overlapping GEMM and softmax (Algorithm 2). Are these two counts ($s$ stages vs. pipeline depth) coupled? For instance, if I increase $s$ to hide more latency from TMA loads, does that negatively impact my ability to run a deeper GEMM-softmax pipeline due to register pressure or resource contention? The paper mentions register pressure as a limiting factor for extending beyond 2 stages; is there a general rule of thumb derived from their experiments on how many stages one should aim for given a specific block size ($B_r \times B_c$)?
Answer:
Based on the provided text, the number of stages $s$ in the circular Shared Memory (SMEM) buffer and the depth of the GEMM-softmax pipeline are distinct parameters that are **not strictly coupled**, though they interact through limited hardware resources, specifically register count.

The parameter $s$ in Algorithm 1 refers to the number of slots in the circular SMEM buffer used for producer-consumer synchronization between warpgroups. Its primary purpose is to hide the latency of Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) loads from Global Memory (GMEM) to SMEM. Increasing $s$ allows the producer warpgroup to continue issuing loads further ahead without stalling on consumer consumption, effectively decoupling memory transfer from computation across warpgroups. The paper notes that for the first $s$ iterations, no waits are issued because the buffer fills up initially.

Conversely, the pipeline depth discussed in Section 3.2 (specifically regarding Algorithms 2 and the "pingpong" scheduling) refers to overlapping operations *within* a single consumer warpgroup. Here, the goal is to hide the latency of non-GEMM operations (like softmax exponentials) by executing them concurrently with asynchronous WGMMA instructions for subsequent blocks. The authors explicitly distinguish these concepts in footnote 2 of Section 3.2, stating: "Note that the number of stages of the overlapping scheme is bounded by, but need not equal, the number $s$ of stages in the circular SMEM buffer." This indicates that you can theoretically configure a deep GEMM-softmax pipeline (e.g., 3 stages) regardless of whether your SMEM buffer has 2 or more stages, provided you have enough resources to support both simultaneously.

However, there is a critical resource contention point: **register pressure**. As detailed in Section 3.2 under "Register pressure," extending the pipeline depth beyond 2 stages requires maintaining additional intermediate results in registers to sustain parallelism. Specifically, a 2-stage pipeline requires holding an extra score matrix $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{next}}$ in registers alongside $\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{cur}}$, consuming roughly $B_r \times B_c \times \text{sizeof}(\text{float})$ bytes per threadblock. The paper states that while a 3-stage variant is proposed and could offer higher Tensor Core utilization, it demands even more registers, making it difficult to balance against tile size ($B_r \times B_c$). Larger block sizes are desirable for arithmetic intensity but also consume significant registers; therefore, pushing for a deeper pipeline often forces a reduction in block size or leads to register spilling, which degrades performance.

Regarding a general rule of thumb derived from their experiments: The paper does not provide a universal formula linking $s$ directly to optimal block size because this depends heavily on specific hyperparameters like head dimension ($d$) and sequence length ($N$). However, their empirical validation suggests that **2-stage pipelining** strikes an effective balance for their target configurations (e.g., head dimension 128). They observed that while a 3-stage pipeline offers theoretical gains, the added register pressure complicates tuning and may not yield net benefits if it forces smaller tiles or causes spills. In contrast, increasing $s$ for TMA buffering is generally beneficial up to a point where it exceeds necessary latency coverage without excessively consuming SMEM space required for other data structures. Ultimately, optimal configuration requires profiling specific block sizes against available register limits rather than relying on a fixed ratio between $s$ and pipeline depth.
