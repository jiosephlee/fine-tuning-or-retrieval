\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Clarification on 'catastrophic values' mentioned in the Introduction regarding new token positions
Question:
The Introduction states: "First, untrained new position indices introduce many catastrophic values..." and later mentions that extending from 4k to >1M introduces >90% new positions.

What exactly constitutes these "catastrophic values" numerically? 
Theoretical RoPE generates sinusoidal functions: $\\sin(n \\theta)$ and $\\cos(n \\theta)$. These values always lie within [-1, 1]. They cannot explode numerically unless multiplied by weights that have exploded.
The paper suggests these values cause OOD issues and convergence difficulties.
Are these "values" referring to:
a) The raw sine/cosine outputs becoming effectively random noise relative to what was seen in pre-training?
b) The dot products between query and key vectors resulting in extremely small or unstable attention logits because of phase mismatches?
c) Something else entirely?

If it's option (b), could someone explain why linear interpolation fails here compared to their searched non-uniform approach? Is it purely about preserving high-frequency information?
Answer:
The term "catastrophic values" in the LongRoPE paper does not refer to numerical overflow where sine or cosine values exceed the $[-1, 1]$ bound, nor does it imply that the weights themselves have diverged to infinity during the forward pass. Instead, these "values" refer to **semantically meaningless and highly oscillatory positional encodings** that arise when applying uniform linear interpolation to extreme extension ratios (e.g., extending from 4k to $>1\text{M}$ tokens).

When you extend the context window by a factor of $s$ (where $s > 250$ in your scenario), linear positional interpolation (PI) scales *all* Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) dimensions by the same factor $\lambda = s$. Mathematically, for a token at position $n$, the rotation angle becomes $\frac{n}{s \theta^i}$. While the trigonometric functions $\sin(\cdot)$ and $\cos(\cdot)$ remain bounded, this aggressive scaling compresses the entire distribution of position indices into a tiny fraction of their original period. Consequently, for any two distinct positions $n_1$ and $n_2$ within the new massive context, their rotated vectors become nearly indistinguishable or exhibit rapid, chaotic phase shifts relative to what the model learned during pre-training. The model expects specific angular relationships between tokens based on their distance; however, with $>90\%$ of positions being "new," these expected relationships are replaced by a dense cluster of angles that effectively act as random noise or high-frequency artifacts relative to the pre-trained manifold. This creates a severe Out-of-Distribution (OOD) shift where the attention mechanism receives inputs it cannot interpret, leading to unstable attention logits and preventing convergence during fine-tuning.

Your hypothesis regarding option (b)—that dot products result in unstable attention logits—is essentially correct regarding the *consequence*, but the root cause is the loss of discriminative power in the positional signal itself. Linear interpolation fails here because it treats all frequency bands equally. Low-frequency dimensions (which capture coarse-grained position information essential for long-range dependencies) get compressed so severely that they lose their ability to distinguish distant tokens, while high-frequency dimensions oscillate too rapidly to provide stable gradients. The paper argues that this uniform compression destroys "information entropy" unevenly across dimensions. In contrast, LongRoPE's non-uniform approach avoids these catastrophic values by assigning unique rescale factors $\lambda_i$ to each dimension $i$. By allowing low-frequency dimensions to undergo less interpolation (preserving their ability to distinguish far-apart tokens) and high-frequency dimensions to undergo more (or even extrapolation), LongRoPE maintains a valid geometric structure for position encoding even at 2M tokens. This ensures that the dot products between Query and Key vectors yield meaningful attention scores rather than noise, thereby stabilizing fine-tuning and avoiding the divergence seen with linear methods.
