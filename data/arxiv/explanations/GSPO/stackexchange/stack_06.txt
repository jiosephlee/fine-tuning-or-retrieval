### Deriving GSPO’s gradient from an off-policy objective without heuristics
Question:
Could you show a derivation that starts from a clear target objective (e.g., maximizing \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}, y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[r(x,y)]) and applies exact importance sampling to use off-policy samples y\sim\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(\cdot|x), leading to the GSPO gradient in Equation (7)? In particular, where does the 1/|y| exponent on the ratio arise from in a principled way, rather than as a heuristic variance control?
Answer:
Short answer: the $1/|y|$ exponent is not a mysterious heuristic tacked onto an otherwise exact sequence-level importance sampling derivation; it appears exactly when you change the optimization viewpoint from the raw sequence distribution $\pi_\theta(y\mid x)$ to the per-token (geometric-mean) sequence distribution $q_\theta(y\mid x)\propto\pi_\theta(y\mid x)^{1/|y|}$. Importance sampling is then exact for expectations under $q_\theta$, and the gradient algebra produces the factor $1/|y|$ (and the average over token log-prob gradients) exactly as in the paper. Below I show the two clean derivations so you can see where the exponent comes from and what is exact versus what is a change of objective.

1) Standard (exact) sequence-level IS from the canonical objective
Start from the canonical objective used in policy gradient style RL, written at the sequence level (reward or advantage $A(y)$ for the whole sequence):
$$
\mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\big[\,A(x,y)\,\big].
$$
Using the log-likelihood trick,
$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim\pi_\theta}\big[ A(x,y)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\big].
$$
If you only have off-policy samples $y\sim\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(\cdot\mid x)$, exact importance sampling gives
$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{J}(\theta)
= \mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}\Big[ \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}\,A(x,y)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\Big].
$$
Now use the autoregressive factorization $\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)=\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})$ and $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)=\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})$. This exact IS gradient contains the full sequence ratio $\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}$ (no $1/|y|$ exponent).

So if your aim is strictly to compute the gradient of the original sequence objective using off-policy data, the correct IS weight is the full sequence likelihood ratio (ratio$^1$), not ratio$^{1/|y|}$.

2) Where ratio^{1/|y|} arises exactly: IS for a per-token / geometric-mean objective
The paper chooses to work with a sequence-level surrogate that treats sequences through their per-token (geometric-mean) likelihood. Define a new sequence distribution $q_\theta$ by taking the geometric mean of token probabilities (and renormalizing if you wish); equivalently treat $q_\theta$ as proportional to $\pi_\theta(y\mid x)^{1/|y|}$. Concretely, consider optimizing an objective of the form
$$
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim q_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\big[\,A(x,y)\,\big],
$$
where $q_\theta(y\mid x)\propto\pi_\theta(y\mid x)^{1/|y|}$. This is not the original $\mathcal{J}$, it is a different but related surrogate that emphasizes per-token average likelihood rather than raw product-likelihood (motivated by length normalization and variance control). For this objective the exact importance weight to reweight samples drawn from $q_{\theta_\text{old}}$ is
$$
\frac{q_\theta(y\mid x)}{q_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}
=
\left(\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{|y|}}.
$$
Hence exact IS for $\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}$ produces the exponent $1/|y|$ exactly (no heuristic). Take the log-likelihood trick for $\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}$:
$$
\nabla_\theta \widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\theta)
= \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim q_{\theta_\text{old}}}\Big[ \frac{q_\theta(y\mid x)}{q_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}\,A(x,y)\,\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(y\mid x)\Big].
$$
Because $\log q_\theta(y\mid x)=\tfrac{1}{|y|}\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)$, we have
$$
\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(y\mid x)
= \frac{1}{|y|}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)
= \frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}).
$$
Substituting the importance weight gives exactly the GSPO gradient form (clipping aside):
$$
\nabla_\theta \widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(\theta)
= \mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}}\!\Big[ \Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y\mid x)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{|y|}} A(x,y)\cdot \frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})\Big],
$$
which matches Equation (7)/(the unclipped gradient) in the paper.

Interpretation and trade-offs
Thus the $1/|y|$ exponent is exact if you are performing IS for expectations under the per-token (geometric-mean) sequence distribution $q_\theta$ rather than under the original $\pi_\theta$. This is a principled change of the sampling/optimization measure: $q_\theta$ compresses the product-likelihood into a per-token average likelihood, which (i) reduces the dynamic range of importance weights across varying $|y|$, (ii) yields a uniform clipping scale across lengths, and (iii) reduces variance coming from multiplying many per-token ratios. It is not the exact IS estimator for the original objective $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[A]$, but it is an exact IS estimator for the surrogate objective $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim q_\theta}[A]$. Practically, the paper argues (and empirically shows) that this surrogate objective aligns well with sequence-level rewards and yields much more stable gradients for long responses and MoE models.

Bottom line: the exponent $1/|y|$ is principled when you adopt the geometric-mean (per-token normalized) sequence distribution $q_\theta(y\mid x)\propto\pi_\theta(y\mid x)^{1/|y|}$. Importance sampling is exact for $q_\theta$, and the algebra then gives the $1/|y|$ factor and the per-token average of gradient contributions exactly as in GSPO.
