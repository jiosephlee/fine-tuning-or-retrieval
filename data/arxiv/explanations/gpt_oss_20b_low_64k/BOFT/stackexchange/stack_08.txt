\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### "Spectral norm preservation" and its effect on training stability: could you provide a small derivation?
Question:
The paper notes that multiplying by an orthogonal matrix preserves singular values (and thus spectral norm) of the pretrained weight matrix $m{W}^0$, which supposedly stabilizes training. Could we show step by step how $
orms{m{R}m{W}^0}_2 = 
orms{m{W}^0}_2$ holds when $m R$ is orthogonal? And how does this imply that gradients remain bounded during backpropagation compared to LoRA where additive updates may change singular values?
Answer:
**Deriving the spectral‑norm preservation**

Let  

\[
\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n },\qquad  
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\;\bm{R}\,\bm{R}^{\top}= \bm{I}_d .
\]

The finetuned weight matrix is  

\[
\widetilde{\bm W}= \bm{R}\,\bm{W}^0 .
\]

Recall that the spectral norm of a matrix is the largest singular value, i.e.

\[
\|\bm A\|_2 = \max_{\|x\|_2=1}\|\,\bm A x\,\|_2 .
\]

For any unit‑norm vector \(x\),

\[
\| \widetilde{\bm W}x \|_2 = \|\,(\bm R \bm W^0) x \,\|_2
     = \|\bm R(\boldsymbol y)\|_2 ,
     \quad \text{where }  y := \boldsymbol W^0 x .
\]

Because \(\bm R\) is orthogonal, it preserves Euclidean norms:

\[
\|\bm R y\|_2 = \|y\|_2 .
\]

Hence

\[
\|\widetilde{\bm W}x \|_2 = \|\boldsymbol y \|_2 
     = \|\boldsymbol W^0 x \|_2 .
\]

Taking the maximum over all unit‑norm \(x\),

\[
\|\widetilde{\bm W}\|_2
  = \max_{\|x\|=1}  \|\widetilde{\bm W}x\|
  = \max_{\|x\|=1}  \|\boldsymbol W^0 x\|
  = \|\boldsymbol W^0\|_2 .
\]

Thus multiplying by an orthogonal matrix **does not change** any singular value (including the largest one). The proof requires only that \(R R^{T}=I_d$ ; it does not depend on how \(R$ was constructed (Cayley transform, butterfly factorization, etc.).  

---

**Implications for training stability**

In back‑propagation the gradients with respect to the input \(x_i=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}w_i^\top\\ v_i^\top\\ y_i^\top\\ z_i^\top\\ w_i^0 \\ v_i^0 \\ y_i^0 \\ z_i^0\\ f_\theta \\ h_t\\ f_\phi\\g_t\\ S_t(1)\end{smallmatrix}\right]/k_{proba}$ are

```
grad(f_phi)   := grad(f_phi)+grad(f_z')*.FairProd  * y_i*g_t/mu_ff
               +                               * z_i*g_t/mu_ff
               +                                ……
grad(w_i   )   := grad(w_i )+ ... * r(p.as.data.frame(pred_test))
 ...
```

The two important observations are:

1. **Bilinear form**  
   The output of a layer is essentially a bilinear similarity  
   \(w^{0}_{i}\!^\top\,R\,x_i\norm{}\).  
   Because \(R^\top R=I_d,\; R^{-1}=R^\top,\) the forward outputs remain
   in roughly the same “radius” as before finetuning.  In particular,
   if the pretrained weights were already well‑conditioned (e.g., by
   layer‑norm or weight‑norm), multiplying by an orthogonal matrix
   keeps that conditioning intact.

2. **Gradient magnitude bound**  
   Consider a loss \(L(\widetilde{\bm W}) = L((R\,W^{0})^{T}\!x)\).  
   Its gradient w.r.t. \(W^{0}\) is

   \[
      g_{W^{0}}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\,(\widetilde{\bm W})}
      (\,R\,)^T .
   \]

   Since \(R^TR=I_d,\;
      ||g_{W^{0}}||_F
      = || (\partial L / {\partial\,(\tilde W)})\, R^T||_F
      = || {\partial L / {\partial\,(\tilde W)}}||_\!F .
   \]

   Thus the gradient norm in terms of the trainable parameters (**the orthogonal matrix or its Cayley parameters**) is exactly the same as in terms of the underlying weights \(W^0.\) The important point is that we do **not** re‑scale any singular component of \(W^0.\)

For comparison, LoRA writes

\(W=W^{0}+AB^T,\)

with low‑rank matrices \(A,B.\) Here even though \(A,B,\) have few parameters, they alter all singular values of \(W^{0}\). In practice this can squeeze some directions to very small singular values and blow up others; gradients flowing back to \(A,B,\) are then either suppressed (because of a tiny singular value) or gigantic (because of a large one). As training proceeds these imbalances exacerbate and often lead to exploding/vanishing gradients early in fine‑tuning. This phenomenon is exactly what the authors observe when they compare LoRA to OFT/BOFT – *spectral norm inflation or deflation* renders updates unstable.

By contrast, OFT/BOFT guarantee that every singular value stays exactly where it was in compile–time – i.e., no eigen‑mode is suppressed or amplified – so gradients remain bounded and behave predictably across layers and iterations.

---

### Summary

- For any orthogonal matrix \(R,\; ||RW||_{2}=||W||_{2}\).  
  Proof uses that an orthogonal matrix preserves Euclidean norms.
- Thus every singular value (and therefore spectral norm) of the pretrained weight matrix is preserved during fine‑tuning with OFT/BOFT.
- Because singular values do not drift, each gradient component inherits exactly the same scale as if we had not changed the weight at all; no “exploding” or “vanishing” dynamics are introduced by this multiplicative update.
- Additive methods like LoRA change all singular values arbitrarily and can give rise to sudden spectral changes, which often destabilize training – especially for very large foundation models where layer norms already straddle many magnitudes.

Hence, *spectral‑norm preservation via orthogonality* provides both a principled guarantee for stable back‑propagation and a practical advantage over low‑rank additive solutions when fine‑tuning large pre‑trained models.
