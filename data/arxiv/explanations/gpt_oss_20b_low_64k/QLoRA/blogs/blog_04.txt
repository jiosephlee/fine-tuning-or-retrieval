# Guanaco: Open‑Source Chatbot Near Matching ChatGPT on the Vicuna Benchmark

## 1 — What is Guanaco?

Guanaco is a family of open‑source large language models (LLMs) that reach *almost* the performance of ChatGPT on the Vicuna benchmark while having the same size as, or even smaller than, most existing open‑source chatbots.  
The collection spans four model sizes—7 B, 13 B, 33 B, and 65 B parameters—all trained using **QLoRA**, a new quantized fine‑tuning method described in *“QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs.”*  

The key tricks that make Guanaco possible are:

| Trick | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| **4‑bit NormalFloat (NF4)** | An information‑theoretically optimal quantization for normally distributed weights. Gives sharper 4‑bit representations than plain Int4 or FP4. |
| **Double Quantization** | Quantizes the per‑block scaling constants themselves, shaving ~0.37 bits per parameter from memory usage. |
| **Paged Optimizers** | Uses NVIDIA unified memory to move optimizer state back to CPU RAM during spike moments caused by gradient checkpointing—with no speed penalty. |
| **Full‑layer LoRA adapters** | Adds small trainable adapter matrices to *every* linear layer instead of just query/value heads; this recovers any loss that might come from freezing the base weights. |

QLoRA lets us fit a **65 B** parameter base model (the LLaMA architecture) on a single 48 GB GPU, a task that would normally need dozens of high‑memory GPUs for conventional 16‑bit training.

## 2 — Training as a Story

### 2.1 Data Source – OASST1

All Guanaco variants were trained from scratch on the **OASST1** dataset—a multi‑turn, crowd‑sourced dialogue corpus that explicitly forbids using GPT models in its generation. The training set consists of roughly 11k dialogues covering dozens of domains (from daily chit‑chat to troubleshooting tech). Because OASST1 is short and high quality, it strikes the right balance between breadth and instruction clarity.

### 2.2 Fine‑Tuning recipe (in brief)

1. **Quantize** the LLaMA base weights to NF4 with block size 64.  
2. **LoRA‑inject**: for every `nn.Linear` in the transformer, add two low‑rank matrices (rank ≈ 8 for LLaMA).  
3. **Gradients through quantized weights**: during backpropagation we dequantize NF4 back to BF16 on the fly but only compute gradients for LoRA matrices; full base weights stay frozen.  
4. **Optimizer state paging**: all AdamW states are allocated in unified memory; when GPU memory is tight they spill over to DRAM automatically.  
5. **Training schedule**:  
   * LR decay from 10⁻⁵ … 2×10⁻⁶ over four epochs × batch size=16 (for large models).  
   * Cross‐entropy loss, no RLHF or reinforcement learning—pure supervised fine‑tuning.

The whole process takes ~12 h on a consumer RTX 3090 (or ~24 h on an RTX 4090) regardless of model size.

## 3 — Results that Matter

### 3.1 Vicuna Benchmark – What do the numbers say?

| Model | Params | GPU required | Vicuna % vs ChatGPT |
|-------|--------|--------------|---------------------|
| Guanaco 7B | 7 B | ×5 GB | 80 % |
| Guanaco 13B | 13 B | ×21 GB | — |
| Guanaco 33B | 33 B | ×21 GB | **91 %** |
| Guanaco 65B | 65 B | ×48 GB | **99.3 %** |

- The **65B model** reaches *99.3%* of ChatGPT’s score on the Vicuna benchmark even though it runs at only *4-bit precision*.  
- The **33B model** uses just *21 GB*, comparable to Vicuna’s 26 GB but with higher accuracy.

Because OASST1 forbids GPT usage, this result shows that a purely open‑source training pipeline can almost catch up to an industry giant.

### 3.2 MMLU & Elo Tournaments

On MMLU (5‑shot accuracy), QLoRA + NF4 match the standard full‑16 bit LoRA performance across all sizes; nothing is lost by freezing the base weights.

In Elo tournaments with human and GPT‑4 pairwise judgments:

- Guanaco 65B has an Elo score ~1,200 vs GPT-4’s ~1,350—a win probability of ≈30% against GPT-4.
- Even on a multilingual OA benchmark, Guanaco outperforms all rivals except GPT-4.

These results confirm that fine‐tuning via QLoRA does not degrade real conversational quality.

## 4 — Why QLoRA Makes It All Work

### Many layers = no loss  

LoRA originally added adapters only to attention query/value projections; here we add them “everywhere.” That small extra effort keeps gradient flow uniform across the network and removes any loss seen when adapters only touch part of each transformer block.

### NF4 beats FP4 / Int4  

NF4 arranges quantization bins according to a normal distribution—exactly what pretrained model weights look like after training—and then scales them into `[-1,1]`. The empirical testbed shows NF4 beating FP4 by more than half a percentage point in perplexity and zero-shot GLUE performance when compared with naive bit patterns.

### Double Quantization saves RAM but not quality  

Without double quantization the memory foot­print of scale constants would be about \(32/64 = 0.5\) bits per weight parameter; after compressing these constants into FP8 we lose only \(0.37\) bits/param—a savings of ~3 GB on a 65 B model with negligible impact on downstream scores.

## 5 — What This Means for Researchers & Practitioners

* **Accessibility:** A single consumer GPU is enough to produce state‑of‐the art chatbots at tens-of-billions scale.
* **Speed:** Finetuning runs fast (≈12 h per model); you can tinker with datasets or prompts without massive compute budgets.
* **Reproducibility:** All models and code are available through HuggingFace (`huggingface.co/artidoro/guanaco`) plus CUDA kernels that make NF4/Double Quantization trivial.
* **Deployability:** The smallest version (7B) fits easily on mobile devices while staying far better than many other open chatbots such as Alpaca or Vicuna next-door citizens.

---

## 6 — Future Directions & Open Questions

1. **Even lower precision?** Could we shrink base models further to e.g., “3-bit” without losing LoRA recoverability?  
2. **Other PEFT methods?** LoRA shows great resilience; what about prefix-tuning or IA³ under QLoRA?  
3. **Bias & safety evaluation** deeper beyond simple metrics – does OASST1 completely remove toxic biases?  
4. **Cross-lingual robustness** – OASST1 is multilingual; how do congregate predictions hold up in non-English settings?  

Researchers are invited to take Guanaco forward along these paths—and enjoy building elegant bots using just a laptop!

---

## Epilogue

Guanaco proves that *quantization need not compromise conversational quality.* By marrying NF4, double quantization, and efficient optimizer paging with low-rank adapters, QLoRA turns huge models into finetunably small fixtures on any consumer GPU while reaching near-ChatGPT performance on standard benchmarks.

So if you’ve ever dreamed of training your own multimillion-token chatbot without owning a cluster of highmem GPUs—stop dreaming and start building with Guanaco today!
