\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

### What is the exact advantage estimation used in GRPO when employing outcome supervision?
Question:
In Section 4.1, the authors describe normalizing group rewards by subtracting mean and dividing by standard deviation and then setting all token advantages equal to this normalized reward. Does GRPO propagate these advantages back across *all* tokens of each output equally? How does this differ from token‑wise GAE used in PPO, especially when only final tokens receive rewards?
Answer:
**Answer**

In GRPO’s outcome‑supervision variant the *advantage* that is fed back to the policy model is simply the **normalized reward of the whole answer**.  

For each question \(q\) a group of \(G\) diverse completions \(\{o_1,\dots ,o_G\}\) is sampled from the current (old) policy \(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\).  
A reward model \(r_{\varphi}\) assigns a scalar score to every completion, producing rewards
$$
\mathbf{r}=\{r_1,r_2,\dots ,r_G\}.
$$
The rewards are then centered and scaled within the group:
$$
\widetilde{r}_i=\frac{r_i-\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\operatorname{std}(\mathbf{r})}\; .
$$

GRPO **does not compute a per‑token return or a GAE trace**.  
Instead it sets **the same advantage for every time step of a particular completion**:
$$
\hat A_{i,t} = \widetilde{r}_i \quad \forall\, t=1,\dots ,|o_i|.
$$
Thus every token in \(o_i\) receives an identical advantage equal to the normalized outcome reward of that whole completion. All tokens are treated equally, regardless of where they occur in the sequence.



### Difference from PPO/GAE

PPO (and classic RL for LLMs) uses Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), which requires:
1. a value function \(V_{\psi}\) (a separate network),
2. per‑token rewards \(r_t\).

In mathematical reasoning LLMs, only the final token typically carries an actual reward from the reward model (e.g., correctness flag). GAE propagates this sparse signal backwards through all tokens based on a discounted sum of future returns:
$$
A_t = v_t - V_{\psi}(h_t)
      + \gamma V_{\psi}(h_{t+1}) - V_{\psi}(h_t)
      \;\;\text{(simplified illustration)}.
$$
Consequently, earlier tokens receive *smaller* advantages because their contribution to the final reward is attenuated by discounting and variance‑reducing normalization.

GRPO replaces this two‑step process with a single, **group‑relative baseline**: by comparing each sampled completion against its peers in the same group, it removes the need for a value function entirely. The advantages are *not* discounted or back‑propagated; every token simply inherits the same relative score that reflects how its entire output measures up to others on that prompt.



### Why this matters when only terminal tokens get rewards

With outcome supervision, the reward signal is inherently *global*. In PPO/GAE you must “guess” how much each intermediate step contributed to that terminal reward—a challenging learning problem amplified by long mathematical chains. GRPO sidesteps this by letting the comparison itself inform the training: if one answer earns a high normalized reward, **every token in that answer is strongly reinforced**, whereas lower‑scoring answers receive uniform negative reinforcement across all tokens.

This makes GRPO more memory‑efficient (no critic), faster to train (no multi‑step bootstrapping), and often yields larger gains on tasks where only *final correctness* matters—exactly what’s happening in many math benchmark settings. The trade‑off is that finer temporal credit assignment (e.g., penalizing an incorrect intermediate step while still encouraging correct later steps) is lost; however, as empirical results show, this coarse advantage can still dramatically improve overall reasoning performance when only outcome supervision is available.

In short: **GRPO gives every token in a completed answer the same advantage equal to its group‑normalized reward; PPO/GAE would assign per‑token discounted advantages derived from a learned value function**. This design choice explains why GRPO frequently outperforms vanilla PPO in mathematical reasoning with outcome supervision.
