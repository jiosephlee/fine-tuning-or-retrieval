# One Method to Rule Them All: Testing BOFT on Vision, Language, and Art

Large foundation models have revolutionized artificial intelligence, powering everything from chatbots to image generators. However, these models are massive; GPT-3 alone contains approximately 175 billion parameters. Training such giants from scratch for every new application is prohibitively expensive. Consequently, the field has shifted toward parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT), where we adapt a pretrained model to a specific downstream task by updating only a tiny fraction of its weights.

While methods like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) have become popular, they rely on additive low-rank updates. A competing approach, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), uses multiplicative orthogonal transformations to preserve the geometric relationships between neurons, often leading to better stability and generalization. The catch? Standard OFT requires too many trainable parameters to be truly efficient unless it sacrifices expressiveness through rigid block-diagonal structures.

Enter Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT). By leveraging butterfly factorization inspired by the Fast Fourier Transform, BOFT achieves the best of both worlds: the expressiveness of a dense orthogonal matrix with the parameter efficiency of sparse structures. But theory is only half the battle. In this post, we dive into the empirical evidence showing how BOFT serves as a universal adapter, outperforming state-of-the-art methods across vision, language, and generative art tasks—all while introducing zero latency during inference.

## The Universal Adapter Challenge

The true test of any finetuning method is its versatility. Many techniques excel in natural language processing but falter in computer vision, or vice versa. The authors of BOFT aimed to break these silos by testing their method on three distinct pillars of modern AI: Large Language Models (LLMs), Large Vision Transformers (ViTs), and text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion.

The core innovation behind BOFT lies in how it constructs the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ used to transform the pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$. Instead of learning a full dense matrix, which scales quadratically as $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$, or a restricted block-diagonal matrix, BOFT composes $\bm{R}$ as a product of $m$ sparse butterfly matrices. This approach reduces the parameter count to $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ while maintaining "dense connectivity," ensuring that information can flow freely between all neurons during adaptation.

This architectural choice allows BOFT to subsume previous OFT variants as special cases while offering a smoother interpolation between the identity matrix (preserving pretraining knowledge) and highly expressive transformations needed for new tasks.

## Dominating Natural Language Tasks

When applied to Large Language Models, parameter efficiency is critical because even small percentage increases in trainable parameters can translate to gigabytes of extra memory usage. The researchers evaluated BOFT on standard NLP benchmarks, comparing it against LoRA and the original block-diagonal OFT.

The results were striking. BOFT consistently matched or exceeded the performance of LoRA while using fewer or comparable numbers of trainable parameters. More importantly, BOFT demonstrated superior convergence stability. Because the transformation is strictly orthogonal, it preserves the spectral norm of the weight matrices throughout training. This property prevents the exploding or vanishing gradient issues that sometimes plague additive methods when hyperparameters are not tuned perfectly.

In tasks requiring nuanced understanding and generation, BOFT's ability to learn a structured subset of the orthogonal group provided an implicit inductive bias that helped prevent overfitting. While LoRA approximates weight changes via low-rank decomposition, BOFT learns a bilinear similarity metric that respects the angular relationships established during pretraining. This geometric fidelity appears to be particularly beneficial when adapting massive LLMs to specialized domains with limited data.

## Elevating Computer Vision Performance

Moving from text to pixels, the team tested BOFT on Large Vision Transformers adapted for image classification and recognition tasks. Vision models often rely heavily on the spatial correlations learned during pretraining on datasets like ImageNet. Disrupting these correlations with aggressive finetuning can degrade performance on unseen distributions.

Empirical studies showed that BOFT outperformed current state-of-the-art PEFT methods on various vision benchmarks. The butterfly structure's capacity to recover classic linear transforms—such as the Discrete Cosine Transform or Hadamard Transform—seems to align well with the frequency-domain characteristics inherent in image data. Unlike block-diagonal OFT, which isolates groups of neurons and cannot approximate global linear transforms, BOFT maintains global connectivity through its multi-stage sparse factorization.

This global reach allows the model to adjust feature representations holistically rather than in isolated chunks. The result is a finetuned model that adapts quickly to new visual concepts without forgetting the robust features extracted during the initial pretraining phase. The experiments confirmed that for a fixed parameter budget, BOFT achieves higher accuracy than both LoRA and standard OFT configurations.

## Revolutionizing Text-to-Image Generation

Perhaps one of the most exciting applications detailed in the paper is the adaptation of text-to-image diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion. Prior to this work, orthogonal finetuning had primarily been explored for controllable generation within narrow scopes. BOFT expands this capability significantly, proving itself as a generic tool for steering generative models toward specific artistic styles or subjects.

Diffusion models are notoriously sensitive to weight perturbations; slight deviations can lead to mode collapse or degraded image quality. The multiplicative nature of BOFT ensures that the underlying manifold of the generative process remains intact. In head-to-head comparisons involving style transfer and subject-driven generation, BOFT produced images with higher fidelity and better prompt alignment compared to other PEFT baselines.

The method allowed for precise control over the generation process by learning an orthogonal transformation $\bm{R}(m,b)$ that subtly shifts the latent space dynamics without distorting them. Whether generating photorealistic portraits or abstract art, models finetuned with BOFT retained the diversity and coherence of the base model while successfully incorporating new concepts.

## Zero Latency and Practical Deployment

A common concern with sophisticated finetuning architectures is inference overhead. Methods that insert additional layers or complex routing mechanisms often slow down response times, making them less ideal for real-time applications.

BOFT sidesteps this issue entirely through its multiplicative design. During training, the orthogonal matrix is represented as a product of sparse butterfly components to save memory and compute gradients efficiently:
$$ \bm{R} = \prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}_i $$
However, once training is complete, these components can be mathematically collapsed into a single dense orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}_{final}$. This final matrix is then multiplied directly with the pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$ to create a new set of static weights:
$$ \bm{W}_{new} = \bm{R}_{final} \bm{W}^0 $$
Because this merging happens before deployment, there is absolutely no additional computational cost or latency during inference compared to standard full finetuning or LoRA (after LoRA weights are merged).
