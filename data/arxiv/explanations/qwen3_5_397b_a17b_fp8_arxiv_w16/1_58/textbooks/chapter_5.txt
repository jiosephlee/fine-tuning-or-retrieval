Chapter 5: # Performance Analysis: Accuracy and Perplexity

The central claim of the emerging era of 1-bit Large Language Models (LLMs) is not merely that they offer efficiency gains, but that they can achieve parity with full-precision models in terms of predictive quality. This chapter provides a rigorous analysis of the empirical evidence supporting this claim, focusing on the performance characteristics of \text{BitNet b1.58}. Unlike previous approaches that relied on post-training quantization—a method often deemed sub-optimal—\text{BitNet b1.58} is trained from scratch with ternary weights $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and 8-bit activations. The results demonstrate that this architectural shift does not come at the cost of intelligence; rather, it establishes a new baseline where 1.58-bit models match, and in some cases exceed, the performance of their 16-bit floating-point (FP16) counterparts when subjected to identical training regimes. By examining perplexity trends, zero-shot benchmark accuracies, and the effects of extended training horizons, we uncover the specific conditions under which low-bit models transition from being approximate curiosities to state-of-the-art contenders.

# Perplexity Trends Across Model Sizes (WikiText2 and C4)

Perplexity serves as a fundamental metric for evaluating language models, measuring how well a probability distribution predicts a sample. Lower perplexity indicates that the model assigns higher probabilities to the correct next tokens, reflecting a deeper understanding of linguistic structure and world knowledge. In the evaluation of \text{BitNet b1.58}, researchers compared the model against a reproduced FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM} baseline across varying model sizes. Both models were pre-trained on the RedPajama dataset for exactly 100 billion tokens to ensure a fair comparison, isolating the variable of bit-width while holding data volume and architecture constant. The validation perplexity was measured on two standard datasets: WikiText2 and C4. These datasets represent distinct domains of text, with WikiText2 focusing on verified Wikipedia articles and C4 comprising a vast collection of cleaned web pages, thereby testing the model's generalization capabilities across different corpora.

The trends observed in perplexity reveal a critical dependency on model scale. At smaller sizes, specifically below 3 billion parameters, there exists a noticeable gap between the ternary \text{BitNet b1.58} and the full-precision baseline. This is expected, as the constraint of forcing every weight into one of three discrete values $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ limits the representational capacity of very small networks more severely than larger ones. However, as the model size increases, this gap narrows rapidly. The data indicates that once the model reaches a certain threshold of complexity, the sheer number of parameters compensates for the reduced precision of individual weights. The quantization function employed, which scales the weight matrix by its average absolute value $\gamma$ before rounding via $\mathrm{RoundClip}(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1)$, proves effective enough at scale to preserve the nuanced relationships learned during training. Consequently, on both WikiText2 and C4, the perplexity scores of \text{BitNet b1.58} converge with those of the FP16 \text{LLaMA LLM}, demonstrating that the information loss inherent in ternarization becomes negligible in sufficiently large architectures.

# Zero-Shot Benchmark Results (ARC, HellaSwag, Winogrande, etc.)

While perplexity measures raw predictive likelihood on static text corpora, zero-shot benchmarks evaluate a model's ability to perform specific reasoning and comprehension tasks without any fine-tuning or examples provided in the prompt. To comprehensively assess \text{BitNet b1.58}, evaluations were conducted using the \emph{lm-evaluation-harness} pipeline across a diverse suite of tasks including ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, Winogrande, PIQA, OpenbookQA, and BoolQ. These tasks span logical reasoning (ARC), commonsense inference (HellaSwag), pronoun resolution (Winogrande), physical reasoning (PIQA), and factual question answering (OpenbookQA and BoolQ). The rigor of this evaluation lies in its diversity; succeeding across such varied domains requires robust internal representations that go beyond simple pattern matching.

The results from these zero-shot evaluations mirror the trends seen in perplexity but provide stronger evidence for practical utility. The performance gap between \text{BitNet b1.58} and the FP16 baseline diminishes consistently as the parameter count rises. Crucially, at specific scales, \text{BitNet b1.58} does not merely catch up but surpasses smaller full-precision models despite having significantly lower memory footprints and latency costs. For instance, a 3.9 billion parameter version of \text{BitNet b1.58} was shown to outperform a standard 3 billion parameter \text{LLaMA LLM} across these end tasks. This finding is profound because it suggests that the inclusion of '0' in the weight set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$—which enables explicit feature filtering—provides a modeling advantage that can outweigh the precision loss of removing fractional values. The ability to effectively "turn off" neurons via zero weights appears to act as a regularizer or a mechanism for sparse attention within dense layers, enhancing performance on complex reasoning tasks relative to dense FP16 models of equivalent size.
