Chapter 3: # The Core Insight: Reparameterizing Rewards as Policies

The traditional pipeline for aligning large language models with human preferences, known as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), is notoriously complex and computationally expensive. It typically involves a multi-stage process: first, training a separate reward model to predict human preferences; second, using reinforcement learning algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to fine-tune the language model to maximize this learned reward while staying close to a reference distribution. This procedure requires sampling from the model during training, managing multiple neural networks simultaneously, and carefully tuning hyperparameters to prevent instability. However, the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" posits that this complexity is unnecessary. The central mathematical innovation of the work lies in recognizing that the optimal policy for the standard RLHF objective can be derived in closed form. By exploiting this analytical solution, one can reparameterize the reward function directly in terms of the policy itself. This change of variables allows the elimination of the explicit reward model and the reinforcement learning loop entirely, reducing the problem to a simple binary classification task solvable with standard supervised learning techniques.

This chapter details the derivation of this core insight. We begin by examining the standard objective function used in RLHF, which seeks to maximize expected reward subject to a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint. We will demonstrate that this constrained optimization problem admits an exact analytical solution for the optimal policy, expressed as a function of the reward and a partition function. While calculating this partition function is generally intractable for large vocabulary spaces, we will show that it becomes irrelevant when considering pairwise human preferences under the Bradley-Terry model. Because human preference data relies on comparing two outputs rather than assigning absolute scores, the partition function—which depends only on the input prompt and not the specific output—cancels out algebraically. This cancellation permits us to substitute the reward function with a expression involving only the optimal policy and a reference policy. Consequently, we arrive at a loss function where the language model parameters are optimized directly to satisfy human preferences, effectively treating the language model as an implicit reward model.

## Analytical Solution to KL-Constrained Reward Maximization

To understand how Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) bypasses reinforcement learning, we must first rigorously define the objective that standard RLHF attempts to solve. In the RL fine-tuning phase, the goal is to find a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ that maximizes the expected reward provided by a learned reward model $r_{\phi}(x, y)$, while ensuring that the new policy does not drift too far from a reference policy $\pi_\text{ref}$. The reference policy is typically the supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model obtained before preference learning begins. This trade-off is formalized as a constrained optimization problem where we maximize the reward minus a penalty term proportional to the KL-divergence between the current policy and the reference policy. Mathematically, this objective is written as:

$$
\max_{\pi_{\theta}} \mathbb{E}_{x\sim \mathcal{D}, y\sim \pi_{\theta}(y \mid x)}\bigl[r_{\phi}(x, y)\bigr] - \beta\mathbb{D}_{\textrm{KL}}\bigl[\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)\mid \mid \pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)\bigr]
$$

Here, $x$ represents the prompt drawn from a dataset $\mathcal{D}$, $y$ is the generated completion, and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of the regularization. A higher $\beta$ forces the optimized policy to remain closer to the reference distribution, preventing mode collapse and preserving linguistic diversity, while a lower $\beta$ allowsthe model to aggressively pursue high-reward tokens even if they are unlikely under the reference model. The KL-divergence term acts as a regularizer, penalizing deviations from $\pi_\text{ref}$.

Remarkably, despite being framed as a reinforcement learning problem requiring iterative updates via algorithms like PPO due to the discrete nature of text generation, this specific objective function has a known analytical solution. By applying methods from variational inference or solving the Lagrangian of this convex optimization problem with respect to $\pi_{\theta}$, one can derive the exact form of the optimal policy $\pi_r$ that maximizes this objective for any given reward function $r$. The solution reveals that the optimal probability of generating a token sequence $y$ given $x$ is proportional to the reference probability scaled exponentially by the reward. This relationship is fundamental because it establishes a deterministic mapping between any reward function and its corresponding optimal policy under this specific regularization scheme. Understanding this closed-form solution isthe prerequisite for reparameterizingthe problem; without knowing whatthe optimal policy looks like analytically, we could not invertthe relationshipto express rewards in terms of policies.
