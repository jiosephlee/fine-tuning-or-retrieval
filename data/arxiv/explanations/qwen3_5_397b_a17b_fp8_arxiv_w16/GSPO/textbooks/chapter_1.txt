Chapter 1: # Foundations of Reinforcement Learning for Language Models

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for scaling the capabilities of large language models (LLMs), enabling them to solve sophisticated problems in domains such as competitive mathematics and computer programming. As models grow in size and complexity, particularly with the adoption of architectures like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), the stability and efficiency of the underlying RL algorithms become paramount. Before introducing advanced optimizations, it is essential to establish a rigorous understanding of the mathematical foundations governing autoregressive policies, reward structures, and the evolution from classic policy optimization methods to modern group-based approaches. This chapter delineates the formal notation used to describe language model interactions, explores the mechanics of reward verification, and provides a deep dive into Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Special attention is paid to the structural nuances of token-level importance sampling, which forms the basis for identifying limitations in current state-of-the-art algorithms when applied to massive-scale training.

# Mathematical Notation: Policies, Queries, Responses, and Likelihoods

To rigorously analyze reinforcement learning algorithms for language models, we must first define the probabilistic framework in which these agents operate. An autoregressive language model is formally treated as a stochastic policy parameterized by a vector $\theta$, denoted as $\pi_\theta$. This policy governs the probability distribution over sequences of tokens given an input prompt. We utilize $x$ to represent a specific query drawn from a dataset $\mathcal{D}$. Upon receiving a query $x$, the policy generates a response $y$, which consists of a sequence of discrete tokens. The generative process is sequential; the probability of generating the entire response $y$ conditioned on the query $x$ is decomposed into the product of conditional probabilities for each token in the sequence. Mathematically, if $|y|$ denotes the total number of tokens in the response $y$, the likelihood of the response under the policy $\pi_\theta$ is expressed as $\pi_\theta (y | x)=\prod_{t=1}^{|y|} \pi_\theta (y_t | x, y_{<t} )$. Here, $y_t$ represents the token at position $t$, and $y_{<t}$ signifies all preceding tokens in the sequence up to step $t-1$. This formulation underscores the Markovian property inherent in autoregressive generation, where the prediction of the next token depends solely on the current context comprising the original query and all previously generated tokens.

The interaction between the agent and its environment is encapsulated within a query-response pair $(x, y)$.
