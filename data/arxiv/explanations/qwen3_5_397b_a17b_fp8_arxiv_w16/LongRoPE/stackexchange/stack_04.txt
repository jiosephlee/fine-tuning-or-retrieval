\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Clarification on the indicator function $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})$ for initial tokens
Question:
In Equation 3, the rescaling factor is defined piecewise: it equals 1 (no change) for $n < \hat{n}$ and $1/\lambda_i$ for $n \ge \hat{n}$. Finding 2 states that initial tokens should undergo less interpolation. If $\hat{n}$ is set to, say, 32, does this mean the first 32 tokens retain their original absolute positional encodings regardless of whether the total sequence length is 8k or 2048k? If so, doesn't this create a discontinuity in relative positions between token 31 and token 32 when the sequence gets very long, potentially confusing the attention heads?
Answer:
Yes, your understanding of the mechanism is correct: if $\hat{n}$ is set to $32$, the first $32$ tokens (indices $0$ through $31$) retain their original absolute positional encodings (where the rescaling factor is $1$), regardless of whether the total sequence length is extended to $8$k or $2048$k. For token index $n=32$ and beyond, the positional encoding is immediately scaled by the factor $1/\lambda_i$. This design creates a deliberate discontinuity in the *absolute* positional values between token $31$ and token $32$, but this does not confuse the attention heads because the model relies on *relative* positional information encoded in the difference of rotary angles, and this specific configuration is an optimized solution found to minimize perplexity.

The intuition behind this apparent discontinuity lies in "Finding 2" presented in the paper, which observes that initial tokens receive disproportionately large attention scores and are crucial for the attention layers, similar to observations in StreamingLLM and LM-Infinite. By setting the indicator function $\mathbb{I}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}, \hat{n})$ to $1$ for $n < \hat{n}$, LongRoPE ensures these critical "anchor" tokens preserve their original high-frequency rotational features without the distortion caused by interpolation. While switching from a scaling factor of $1$ to $1/\lambda_i$ at $n=\hat{n}$ creates a jump in absolute position values, Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) operate by rotating query and key vectors based on their positions. The attention mechanism effectively computes interactions based on the relative distance $(m-n)$ through the properties of trigonometric functions. Although the derivative of the position mapping changes abruptly at $\hat{n}$, the evolutionary search algorithm described in Section 3.2 explicitly optimizes $\hat{n}$ and $\lambda_i$ jointly to minimize validation perplexity. The search space includes discrete values for $\hat{n}$ (such as $32$), implying that the model learns to accommodate this specific transition point where the "preserved" region ends and the "interpolated" region begins. Essentially, the network adapts to treat the first $\hat{n}$ tokens as a distinct, high-fidelity context block, while the subsequent tokens provide a compressed representation of the long-range context. The ablation studies in Section 4.3 confirm that searching for both non-uniformities (dimensions and token positions) yields lower perplexity than linear interpolation alone, validating that this piecewise definition—with its inherent discontinuity—is beneficial rather than detrimental for extending context windows up to $2048$k.
