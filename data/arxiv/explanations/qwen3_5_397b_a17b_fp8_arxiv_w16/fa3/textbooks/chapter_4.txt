\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

Chapter 4: # Algorithmic Core II: Overlapping Softmax with GEMM via Pipelining

The fundamental bottleneck in accelerating the attention mechanism on modern GPUs is not merely the volume of arithmetic operations, but the stark disparity in throughput between different types of computational units. While previous iterations of FlashAttention successfully minimized memory access costs by fusing operations and tiling data, they largely adhered to a synchronous execution model where operations were performed sequentially within a single thread block. This chapter delves into the architectural innovations of FlashAttention-3 that dismantle these sequential dependencies. By leveraging the asynchronous capabilities of the NVIDIA Hopper architecture, specifically the separation of Tensor Cores and Special Function Units (SFUs), we introduce a dual-layer pipelining strategy. This strategy involves both "pingpong" scheduling across distinct warpgroups to hide latency globally, and a fine-grained 2-stage pipeline within a single warpgroup to overlap matrix multiplications (GEMMs) with the element-wise softmax computations. These techniques allow the hardware to operate at near-peak utilization by ensuring that high-throughput units are never idle waiting for low-throughput units to complete their tasks.

## The Throughput Mismatch: Tensor Cores vs. Special Function Units (SFUs)

To understand the necessity of overlapping strategies, one must first appreciate the heterogeneous nature of modern GPU compute resources. The attention forward pass consists primarily of two types of operations: General Matrix Multiplications (GEMMs), which compute the score matrix $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ and the output matrix $\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$, and element-wise non-linear operations required for the softmax function, specifically finding row maximums, exponentials, and summations. On the H100 SXM5 GPU, these operations are handled by fundamentally different hardware units with vastly different performance characteristics. The Tensor Cores, designed explicitly for matrix algebra, offer immense throughput. For instance, in FP16 precision, the H100 delivers approximately 989 TFLOPS of matmul performance. In contrast, the Special Function Units (SFUs), which handle transcendental functions like the exponential ($\exp(x)$) required for softmax, operate at a significantly lower rate. The CUDA programming specification indicates that an SM can perform only 16 special function operations per clock cycle. Aggregating this across the 132 SMs of an H100 running at 1830 MHz yields a total throughput of roughly 3.9 TFLOPS for special functions.

This creates a severe imbalance in computational capacity. In a typical attention forward pass with a head dimension of 128, the ratio of matmul Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) to exponential operations is approximately 512 to 1. However, because the throughput ratio between Tensor Cores and SFUs is roughly 256 to 1 (989 TFLOPS vs 3.9 TFLOPS), the time required to execute the exponential operations becomes disproportionately large relative to their arithmetic count. If the algorithm executes strictly synchronously—waiting for all GEMM operations to finish before starting softmax, or vice versa—the powerful Tensor Cores sit idle while the SFUs struggle to catch up. Simple calculations suggest that under such a synchronous regime, the exponential calculations could consume up to 50% of the total execution cycles for an FP16 forward pass. The situation deteriorates further when utilizing FP8 precision; while FP8 doubles the matmul throughput of the Tensor Cores, the throughput of the SFUs remains unchanged, effectively doubling the proportion of time spent waiting for softmax-related operations if no overlapping is employed. Therefore, maximizing GPU utilization requires decoupling these operations so that while one unit is busy, the other is simultaneously productive.

## Inter-Warpgroup Overlap: Pingpong Scheduling strategy

The first layer of optimization introduced in FlashAttention-3 addresses this mismatch at a coarse grain by utilizing warp-specialization and inter-warpgroup coordination. In this paradigm, the threads within a Cooperative Thread Array (CTA) are divided into distinct roles: producer warpgroups responsible solely for data movement (loading tiles from global memory to shared memory via TMA) and consumer warpgroups responsible for computation. Crucially, within the consumer role, multiple warpgroups can be orchestrated to perform different stages of the attention algorithm simultaneously. This technique is referred to as "pingpong" scheduling. The core insight is that since the exponential function is computed by a separate hardware unit (the multifunction unit) independent of the Tensor Cores, it is possible to schedule an exponential calculation on one warpgroup while a different warpgroup executes a GEMM on the Tensor Cores.

The mechanism relies on precise synchronization barriers, implemented via `bar.sync` instructions, to enforce a specific ordering of instruction issuance across warpgroups without stalling execution unnecessarily. Consider two consumer warpgroups, labeled Warpgroup 1 and Warpgroup 2. The scheduler forces Warpgroup 1 to issue its GEMM instructions (specifically $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top$ for iteration $j+1$ and $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}$ for iteration $j$) ahead of Warpgroup 2. Because GEMMs are asynchronous WGMMA instructions that return control immediately after being queued to the Tensor Core proxy, Warpgroup 1 can proceed immediately to execute its softmax operations (exponentials and reductions) on the results of its previous iteration ($j$). While Warpgroup 1's SFUs are busy computing these exponentials, Warpgroup 2 begins issuing its GEMM instructions for iteration $j$. Consequently, during this window, the GPU's Tensor Cores are fully occupied processing Warpgroup 2's matrices, while its SFUs are fully occupied processing Warpgroup 1's scalars. Once Warpgroup 2 completes its GEMM dispatch, it swaps roles with Warpgroup 1: Warpgroup 2 proceeds to compute its softmax while Warpgroup 1 initiates the next round of GEMMs. This alternating pattern ensures that both critical hardware subsystems remain active throughout kernel execution Empirical results demonstrate that this pingpong scheduling elevates performance significantly; for example, in FP16 forward passes with a head dimension of 128 and sequence length of 8192, throughput increases from approximately 570 TFLOPS without this overlap to between 620 and 640 TFLOPS with it enabled. Although real-world scheduling noise prevents perfectly clean alternation as depicted in idealized diagrams, this coarse-grained parallelism effectively masks a substantial portion of the softmax latency.

## Intra-Warpgroup Overlap: The 2-Stage GEMM-Softmax Pipeline

While pingpong scheduling optimizes resource usage across warpgroups FlashAttention-3 pushes optimization further by introducing fine-grained pipelining *within* a single consumer warpgroup. Even with warp-specialization traditional implementations suffer from strict sequential dependencies inside inner loops: one must compute $\mathbf{S}_i^{(j)} = \mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^\top$, then wait for completion before calculating $\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_i^{(j)} = \exp(\mathbf{S}_i^{(j)} - m_i)$ and updating statistics $m_i$ and $\ell_i$, then wait again before loading $\mathbf{V}_j$ and computing $\mathbf{O}_i += \widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_i^{(j)}\mathbf{V}_j$.
