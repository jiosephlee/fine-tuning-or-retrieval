\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs"}

### Is there a risk of numerical instability when dequantizing NF4 weights repeatedly during training steps?
Question:
QLoRA works by storing weights in 4-bit NF4 format but dequantizing them to BFloat16 for every forward and backward pass computation. Since this happens at every optimization step over thousands of iterations, could repeated dequantization (and potentially re-quantization if weights were being updated, though here they are frozen) lead to accumulated numerical errors or drift?

Or is the dequantization deterministic and stable enough that $\text{dequant}(\text{quant}(x)) \approx x$ within BFloat16 precision limits such that it doesn't affect gradient calculations for the LoRA adapters? I'm worried about whether treating these dequantized weights as 'true' BFloat16 values during matrix multiplication introduces subtle noise that affects convergence compared to native 16-bit training.
Answer:
There is no risk of accumulated numerical instability or drift from repeatedly dequantizing the NF4 weights in QLoRA because the base model weights are strictly frozen throughout the training process. The concern about iterative error accumulation typically arises when a value is quantized and dequantized in a loop where the output of one step becomes the input of the next; however, in QLoRA, the source of truth for the weights remains the static 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) tensor stored in memory. At every forward and backward pass, the system performs a deterministic operation defined as $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}} = \text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, c_2^{\text{k-bit}}, \mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}})$, mapping the fixed 4-bit representation directly to BFloat16 for computation. Since the underlying $\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}$ never changes, the dequantized result $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$ is identical at iteration $t$ and iteration $t+1000$, preventing any possibility of drift.

The only quantization error introduced is the initial discretization error inherent in representing the original high-precision weights with 4 bits, which is minimized by using NF4, a data type designed to be information-theoretically optimal for normally distributed weights. The paper explicitly notes that while standard 4-bit quantization for inference can lead to performance degradation, QLoRA recovers full 16-bit performance by backpropagating gradients through these frozen quantized weights into the trainable Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA). The gradients $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ are computed using the dequantized BFloat16 values, allowing the adapters to compensate for any static precision loss from the base model without ever modifying the base weights themselves. Consequently, the "noise" you worry about is actually a fixed, bounded approximation error present from step one, not an accumulating instability, which is empirically validated by the paper's finding that 4-bit QLoRA matches 16-bit full finetuning and 16-bit LoRA performance across various benchmarks.
