### Clarification on the KL Divergence implementation in GRPO vs PPO
Question:
In standard PPO, the KL penalty is usually added directly to the reward signal at each timestep ($r_t = r_\varphi - \beta \log(\pi/\pi_{ref})$). However, Equation 3 in the GRPO section shows the KL divergence term being subtracted directly from the objective function outside the min/clip operation, and they use an unbiased estimator: $\frac{\pi_{ref}}{\pi_{\theta}} - \log\frac{\pi_{ref}}{\pi_{\theta}} - 1$. 

Why was this specific formulation chosen over adding it to the reward? Does moving it outside the clipping mechanism change how strictly the policy is constrained from deviating from the reference model compared to standard PPO?
Answer:
The specific formulation of the KL divergence in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) serves two primary purposes: simplifying the advantage calculation by decoupling the penalty from the reward signal, and providing a statistically unbiased estimate of the divergence. In standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), the KL penalty is typically subtracted directly from the reward at each timestep, defined as $r_t = r_\varphi - \beta \log(\pi/\pi_{ref})$. This approach requires the advantage $A_t$ to be computed via Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), which relies on a learned value function $V_\psi$ to establish a baseline. However, GRPO eliminates the need for a separate critic model by estimating the baseline from the average reward of a group of outputs sampled for the same question. If the KL penalty were embedded within the reward signal $r_t$ as in PPO, it would contaminate the group-relative advantage calculation $\hat{A}_{i,t}$, making it difficult to isolate the relative performance of different outputs within the group purely based on the task reward. By moving the KL term outside the min/clip operation and subtracting it directly from the objective function, GRPO ensures that the advantage reflects only the relative quality of the generated responses, while the regularization acts as a global constraint on the policy update.

Regarding your observation about moving the term outside the clipping mechanism, this structural change does not necessarily make the constraint stricter or looser in terms of optimization dynamics; rather, it changes how the constraint interacts with the trust-region logic inherent in PPO. In PPO, adding the penalty to the reward means it influences both clipped and unclipped terms inside the minimization operator.
