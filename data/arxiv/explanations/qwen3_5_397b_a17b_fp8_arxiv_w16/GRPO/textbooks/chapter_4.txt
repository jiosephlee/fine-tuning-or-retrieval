\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 4: Supervised Fine-Tuning for Instruction Following

The journey from a powerful base language model to a specialized mathematical reasoning agent requires a critical intermediate step known as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). While the pre-training phase equips the model with vast amounts of world knowledge and syntactic structure, it does not inherently teach the model how to follow specific instructions or structure its output in a way that facilitates complex logical deduction. In the development of DeepSeekMath, this transition is marked by the creation of DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, a model derived from the DeepSeekMath-Base 7B through rigorous instruction tuning. This chapter details the meticulous curation of the training dataset, the integration of diverse reasoning formats such as Chain-of-Thought and Program-of-Thought, the specific hyperparameter configurations used during training, and the comprehensive evaluation strategy that positions DeepSeekMath-Instruct against both open-source and proprietary contemporaries. The goal of this stage is not merely to memorize solutions but to instill a robust capability for step-by-step reasoning and tool utilization, bridging the gap between raw linguistic probability and actionable mathematical problem-solving.

# Curating High-Quality Instruction Tuning Data

The foundation of any successful instruction-tuned model lies in the quality, diversity, and relevance of its training data. For DeepSeekMath-Instruct, the researchers constructed a specialized mathematical instruction-tuning dataset designed to cover a broad spectrum of mathematical fields and complexity levels. This dataset is bilingual, encompassing both English and Chinese problems, reflecting the multilingual nature of the underlying pre-training corpus. The total volume of this curated dataset comprises 776,000 training examples, a figure carefully selected to balance computational feasibility with the need for sufficient exposure to varied problem types.

The curation process involved aggregating data from several distinct sources to ensure comprehensive coverage. For the English portion of the dataset, the team annotated problems from established benchmarks such as GSM8K and MATH, specifically generating solutions that integrated tool usage. This ensures that the model learns not just abstract reasoning but also practical computation. Additionally, the dataset incorporates a subset of MathInstruct and the training set of Lila-OOD. These sources were chosen because they provide problems solved via Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or Program-of-Thought (PoT) methodologies. The resulting English collection spans diverse mathematical domains, including algebra, probability, number theory, calculus, and geometry, ensuring that the model does not become overly specialized in a single sub-field at the expense of others.

Parallel to the English efforts, a significant portion of the data was dedicated to Chinese mathematical content. This subset focuses on K-12 level mathematics, covering 76 distinct sub-topics ranging from linear equations to more advanced secondary school concepts. Crucially, the solutions for these Chinese problems were annotated in both Chain-of-Thought and tool-integrated reasoning formats. This dual-language approach distinguishes DeepSeekMath from many predecessors that rely exclusively on English-centric corpora. By including high-quality non-English data, the developers aimed to prevent the degradation of performance in non-English contexts—a common pitfall in models trained solely on English mathematical texts—and to leverage the rich educational resources available in Chinese curricula. The combination of these sources creates a heterogeneous training environment that forces the model to generalize its reasoning capabilities across languages and problem structures.

# Solution Formats: CoT, PoT, and Tool-Integrated Reasoning

A defining characteristic of the DeepSeekMath-Instruct training regimen is its emphasis on structured solution formats. Unlike standard conversational fine-tuning which might prioritize brevity or politeness, mathematical reasoning demands explicit logical progression. The dataset pairs every problem with solutions formatted in three primary styles: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning. Each format serves a unique pedagogical purpose in shaping the model's internal reasoning mechanisms.

Chain-of-Thought prompting involves breaking down a complex problem into a sequence of intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. In the context of DeepSeekMath, CoT data teaches the model to articulate its logic verbally, mimicking the way a human mathematician writes out a proof or derives a solution on paper. This format is essential for developing the model's ability to handle multi-step deductive reasoning where each step relies on the validity of the previous one. By exposing the model to thousands of examples where the path from premise to conclusion is explicitly laid out,the SFT process encourages the generation of coherent, self-contained textual solutions that do not rely on external aids.

Program-of-Thought represents a shift from purely verbal reasoning to executable logic. In PoT scenarios, the model is trained to generate Python code that encapsulates the reasoning process. Instead of describing how to calculate a value in natural language, the model writes a script using libraries such as `math` or `sympy` to perform the computation. The execution result of this program is then treated as the answer. This format is particularly effective for problems involving heavy arithmetic or symbolic manipulation where language models are prone to hallucination or calculation errors. By learning PoT, DeepSeekMath-Instruct acquires the skill to delegate precise computational tasks to an interpreter while retaining control over the logical flow of the solution through code structure.

Tool-Integrated Reasoning combines elements of both CoT and PoT into a hybrid approach. In this format, solutions interleave natural language explanations with code blocks executed by an external tool. The model learns to decide dynamically when to reason textually and when to invoke a calculator or code interpreter. For instance, it might describe a geometric setup in text, write code to compute specific coordinates or areas, parsethe output back into text, and continuethe logical derivation. This format mirrors real-world mathematical practice where humans use scratchpads or calculators alongside their theoretical work.The inclusionof tool-integrated data inthe SFT phaseis criticalfor enablingthemodelto solveproblems that exceedits inherentparametricknowledgeof arithmeticfacts,fosteringacapabilityforcomplex，tool-augmentedproblem-solvingthatpurelytext-basedmodelslack。
