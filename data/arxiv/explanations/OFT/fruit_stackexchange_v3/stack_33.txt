### Is there truly zero inference overhead when control signals must be ingested?
Question:
The paper claims no additional inference overhead. But for controllable generation (e.g., edges or segmentation maps), the model must process the control signal somehow. Does that require an extra encoder, or is the control preprocessed into the text prompt or into fixed positional embeddings? If an encoder is used, is it reused from existing model components (thus not adding parameters), or is it external and frozen so the U-Net’s forward pass remains unchanged?
Answer:
In the paper’s own words, “unlike ControlNet, our <|endoftext|> framework introduces no additional <|endoftext|> overhead to the <|endoftext|> model. In the <|endoftext|> stage, we can simply <|endoftext|> the learned <|endoftext|> matrix $R$ into the <|endoftext|> weight matrix $W^0$ and obtain an equivalent weight matrix $W = R\,W^0$. Thus the <|endoftext|> speed is the same as the <|endoftext|> model.”

What this means in practice is:

1. **No extra encoder or adapter is ever called at test-time.**  
   During training for <|endoftext|> generation you do feed in your canny‐edge map, segmentation mask or landmark heatmap as an *additional conditioning channel* alongside the usual text <|endoftext|> and noisy latent. But you don’t attach a brand‐new network to process it. Instead, you simply expand the U-Net’s first <|endoftext|> (and any other layers that need to “see” that extra map) so that the control signal is just another input channel.

2. **All new parameters live in the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> and those are folded in offline.**  
   <|endoftext|> learns a small set of <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> $R$ for each weight <|endoftext|> $W^0$ in the U-Net (and in the self-attention blocks, if you applied <|endoftext|> there). At <|endoftext|> you compute once (offline)

   $$
     W = R\,W^0
   $$

   and then throw away $R$ entirely. From that point on the U-Net looks *exactly* like the original Stable <|endoftext|> U-Net (except its kernels have been updated to incorporate the control logic). Because you’ve merged $R$ into $W^0$, there is no extra matrix‐multiply or subnetwork at runtime.

3. **The forward-pass graph is bit-for-bit the same as the original.**  
   After you “bake in” all the $R$’s, your model takes in (i) the noisy latent, (ii) the text <|endoftext|> from the *same* frozen text encoder you had before, and (iii) the control map as just another image‐like input. Every <|endoftext|> self‐attention, up/down‐block is exactly as it was in the <|endoftext|> model. Thus the FLOPs, memory-reads and <|endoftext|> time match the baseline.

In short, there is *no* separate control‐signal encoder, no frozen “adapter” network you call at <|endoftext|> and no extra positional <|endoftext|> to compute. The ingestion of the control map is absorbed entirely into the existing weight tensors via <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> and because you merge $R$ with $W^0$ ahead of time, the deployed graph is identical to the vanilla <|endoftext|> U-Net.
