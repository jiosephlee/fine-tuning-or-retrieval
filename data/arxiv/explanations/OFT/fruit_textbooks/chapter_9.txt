# Metrics: DINO, CLIP-I for subject fidelity; CLIP-T for prompt fidelity; LPIPS diversity

A careful evaluation for subject-driven generation must quantify (1) how well the generated images preserve the subject identity, (2) how well they follow the text prompt, and (3) how diverse the generated samples are. The paper uses the following metrics to capture these aspects.

DINO: DINO is a representation produced by a self-e ViT (specifically ViT-S/f in the paper). For subject fidelity, the authors compute the average pairwise | similarity between the DINO o of generated images and the corresponding real subject images. Higher DINO similarity indicates better preservation of subtle subject identity cues captured by DINO features.

CLIP-I (image fidelity): CLIP-I o the average pairwise x similarity of CLIP image f between generated images and real images of the subject. Because CLIP t align visual features to n textual concepts, CLIP-I measures whether the generated images are close to the subject in the multimodal < space.

CLIP-T (prompt fidelity): CLIP-T computes the average > similarity between the CLIP text x of the prompt and the CLIP image x of the generated image. This measures how well the generated images align with the n content of the text prompt.

LPIPS (diversity): To measure diversity among samples generated for the same subject-prompt pair, the authors use LPIPS (a perceptual metric). Specifically, they compute the average LPIPS (the paper reports an LPIPS-based e similarity variant) between generated images that share the same prompt and subject. Higher LPIPS-based diversity indicates the model can generate varied renditions while preserving the subject identity.

Together these metrics quantify subject fidelity (DINO, CLIP-I), prompt fidelity (CLIP-T), and intra-prompt diversity (LPIPS). For robustness, the experiments repeat d with multiple random seeds (the paper reports 30 different random seeds for each method) so that reported averages are not dominated by one favorable seed.
