# Datasets: COCO (C2I), ADE20K (S2I), CelebA-HQ (L2F)

To evaluate controllability under realistic conditions, OFT is applied to three standard datasets paired with the corresponding control task names used in the paper. For Canny-edge-to-image (C2I), the COCO dataset is used; COCO provides complex, diverse scenes where edges must be mapped to coherent objects and backgrounds. For segmentation-map-to-image (S2I), ADE20K supplies diverse scene segmentation maps and labels, testing <|endoftext|> compliance and region consistency. For landmark-to-face (L2F), CelebA-HQ is used; it contains high-quality face images with diverse poses and identities, ideal for assessing fine-grained pose control from landmark inputs.

In the experiments the <|endoftext|> base model is Stable Diffusion v1.5. Each dataset-task pair is used to finetune the same latent diffusion backbone, allowing apples-to-apples comparison between finetuning strategies (OFT / COFT, LoRA, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter). For these control tasks the paper finetunes for 20 epochs in the main comparisons, which is sufficient to expose differences in convergence speed, stability, and final control quality.

# Faster, <|endoftext|> convergence and data efficiency

One of the main empirical claims in the paper is that OFT converges faster, more smoothly, and with fewer training samples than controller networks and additive finetuning methods. There are two conceptual reasons for this behavior grounded in the OFT design.

First, OFT focuses updates on neuron directions (angles) while preserving the pairwise angular relationships across neurons (the hyperspherical energy). Because angular relationships <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> structure strongly, rotating the neuron basis is an efficient way to change model behavior toward the control objective without discarding <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> priors. In other words, OFT modifies the part of the representation most relevant to <|endoftext|> changes while leaving structural relations intact, which produces more effective updates per parameter and per <|endoftext|> step.

Second, by constraining updates to an orthogonal manifold (and to a small neighborhood for COFT) the optimization traverses a <|endoftext|> lower-complexity parameter subspace. This yields steadier learning dynamics. Empirically, the paper shows that in the landmark-to-face task (L2F) OFT reaches a given landmark error substantially earlier than LoRA: LoRA requires about 20 epochs to reach a performance that OFT attains in roughly 8 epochs. ControlNet also exhibits a different dynamic: its controllability often emerges suddenly after several epochs (a sharp transition), whereas OFT and COFT show gradual, stable improvement across epochs. This stability reduces the difficulty of tuning epoch counts and learning rates.

Data efficiency is demonstrated by experiments where OFT converges well with only a small fraction of the training set. For example, on the ADE20K segmentation-to-image task, OFT produces strong controllability with only 5% of the dataset, whereas other methods require more data to <|endoftext|> and reach comparable control fidelity. This makes OFT attractive when labeled control datasets are small or costly to collect.
