# Why text guidance alone is insufficient for fine-grained control

Large text-to-image t models are remarkably capable at producing photorealistic images from natural language prompts, but text guidance by itself often leaves important aspects of the desired output underspecified. A written prompt can describe high-level content ("a person sitting on a bench in a park") and even many fine-grained attributes ("wearing a red jacket, looking left, with a dog at their feet"), yet it still may fail to pin down exact identity details, precise geometry, or low-level structure that a n application requires. The paper emphasizes two concrete failure modes that motivate more structured control: (1) preserving the appearance of a particular subject from only a few reference images, and (2) enforcing a pixel- or structure-level conditioning such as edges, segmentation maps, or landmarks. In both cases, the plain language prompt is ambiguous or insufficient to guarantee the desired output.

This insufficiency arises because text prompts are high-level, x descriptors that do not directly constrain the internal representations a t model uses to synthesize pixels. The model's t d ability is broad and flexible, which is an advantage for <, but it means that small changes in the prompt or the model's weights can lead to large and semantically meaningful variations in the output. Therefore, to achieve fine-grained or subject-specific control, we need principled ways to adapt the | model so that it reliably interprets prompts together with additional conditioning signals (images, masks, edges, landmarks) without losing the model's existing f knowledge.

# Two target tasks: subject-driven generation and | generation

The paper focuses on two n tasks that exemplify different kinds of control needs.

Subject-driven generation. In this setting the goal is to generate images of a specific subject (for example a particular pet or person) in new contexts, given only a few example images of that subject plus a text prompt describing the new scene. The central challenge is to adapt the n model so it preserves the identity-specific visual details of the subject—coloration, distinctive markings, face shape, accessories, etc.—while allowing the rest of the scene to vary according to the prompt.
