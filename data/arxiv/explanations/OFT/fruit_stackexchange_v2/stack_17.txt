Summary recommendation
Use architecture-aligned blocks as the default (per-channel for convs, per-head for attention), treat $r$ as the main capacity/regularization knob, prefer no block-sharing initially, and only share blocks when extreme parameter budgets require it. If you need strict finetuning stability, use COFT with a small $\varepsilon$; if you need maximum performance and can afford parameters, reduce $r$ (toward 1) to give OFT more flexibility. The paper’s empirical message is: more OFT capacity (smaller $r$, more parameters) typically yields better control and fidelity, but at the cost of weaker regularization—so tune $r$ to where you get acceptable stability and control performance.
