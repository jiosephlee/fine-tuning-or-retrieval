### How does OFT interact with EMA weights and classifier-free guidance at inference?
Question:
Stable Diffusion often uses EMA (exponential moving average) weights for better sampling, and classifier-free guidance at inference. When finetuning via OFT, do you apply the orthogonal transform to the EMA weights, or re-derive EMA after finetuning? Does OFT change the effective guidance scale sensitivity, and did you need to retune guidance scale for best results?
Answer:
In our implementation of OFT on Stable Diffusion (SD), we do not maintain a separate exponential moving–average (EMA) of the finetuned weights nor do we re-derive one after training. Instead, we perform all of our orthogonal fine-tuning steps directly on the checkpoint weights that SD uses for inference (which, in practice, are already EMA-smoothed). Concretely, if you load the official SD v1.5 checkpoint (which contains the EMA weights $W^0$), you initialize each orthogonal block $R$ to the identity and train only $R$ (or $(R,I)$ in COFT). At inference time you simply form

$$
W = R\,W^0
$$

once, bake that into your model, and run sampling exactly as you would with the original EMA weights. There is no further moving-average computation over $R$, because the underlying $W^0$ never changes.

As for classifier-free guidance, recall that SD’s sampler combines the conditional and unconditional denoiser outputs via

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CFG}} = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y) + s\bigl(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y) - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid\varnothing)\bigr),
$$

where $s$ is the guidance scale. Since orthogonal transformations preserve angles and norms on each layer, they do not systematically alter the relative magnitudes of the conditional versus unconditional predictions. In other words, applying $R$ to all neurons in a layer leaves the “shape” of $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y)$ and $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid\varnothing)$ intact up to a common rotation, so the optimal $s$ stays essentially the same. In our experiments we kept the default SD guidance scale (e.g.\ $s=7.5$ or $s=9$) and found no need to re-tune it after OFT or COFT. Empirically, both the fidelity and diversity trade-off curves under guidance remain indistinguishable from those of the original SD model, confirming that OFT is “guidance-scale-agnostic.”
