### Is there empirical evidence that angle (direction) dominates magnitude for diffusion U-Net weights, beyond the autoencoder toy example?
Question:
The paper shows a toy autoencoder where using only angular information at test time reconstructs semantics, while magnitudes alone do not. But SD v1.5 is a diffusion U-Net with attention and normalization. Is there a layer-wise ablation showing that rotating neuron directions (keeping magnitudes) accounts for most of the semantic change in text-to-image tasks? For example, can you quantify performance when only angles change vs when only magnitudes change for attention and conv layers?
Answer:
While the toy autoencoder in Figure 1 (and Section 3.1) is the only place where the authors directly “turn off” magnitudes and show that cosine (angular) features alone recover semantics, there is no analogous, layer-by-layer ablation in the actual Stable Diffusion U-Net where you compare “angle-only” vs.\ “magnitude-only” updates in each attention or convolution module. Instead, the paper takes a different tack:

1.  It **only** applies orthogonal (angle-preserving) finetuning—OFT—to the attention and convolution weights of the U-Net, and then shows in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 that this yields far better subject preservation, control consistency, convergence speed and stability than standard finetuning or LoRA. By construction OFT freezes each layer’s weight **norms** (magnitudes) and only learns a layer-shared orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$ so that
$$
  \mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^0,\quad \mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}.
$$
The strong end-to-end improvements in text-to-image tasks therefore **indirectly** support the claim that rotating directions (angles) is doing the heavy lifting.  

2.  The paper does introduce a “re-scaled OFT” variant in Section 3.4, where one additionally places a learnable diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}$ after the rotation:
$$
  \mathbf{z} = \bigl(\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^0\,\mathbf{D}\bigr)^\top x.
$$
This does permit magnitude changes, but the authors only report (in the Appendix) that it further helps—**they do not** present a clean three-way comparison of (a) rotation-only, (b) scale-only, and (c) rotation + scale for each layer.  

3.  In the “Intriguing Insights” (Section 5) they discuss how OFT’s block-diagonal rotations behave in conv layers (each block can be seen as a depthwise channel rotation), but again no pure magnitude ablation is given.

In short, **beyond the autoencoder toy** there is no explicit layer-wise “angle vs.\ magnitude” ablation on the diffusion U-Net in the paper. What you do get is a strong **indirect** signal: orthogonal finetuning (angle changes alone) consistently outperforms both naïve finetuning and low-rank additive updates, which strongly suggests that **direction** is the dominant degree of freedom for steering semantics in these large diffusion models. If you need a direct magnitude-only baseline, you would have to implement something like fixing $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}$ and learning only per-neuron scales (or low-rank scalings) in each layer—and compare it yourself against the OFT curves reported in Sections 4.1/4.2.
