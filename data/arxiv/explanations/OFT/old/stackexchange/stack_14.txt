### 2. How to force “low‐rank” in OFT

What *does* force \((R - I)\) to be low‐rank is if each block \(R_i\) has a large *fixed* subspace (eigenvalue 1) and only a small subspace in which it actually *rotates*. Concretely, suppose each block \(R_i\) **only** rotates in a \(k\)-dimensional subspace and acts as the identity on the remaining \((d/r - k)\) dimensions. Then
\[
\mathrm{rank}(R_i - I)
\;=\;
k,
\]
and overall
\[
\mathrm{rank}(R - I)
\;=\;
\sum_{i=1}^r k
\;=\;
r\,k.
\]
Because the *weight update* in OFT is
\[
(R - I)\,W^0,
\]
its rank is bounded by \(\mathrm{rank}(R - I)\). Thus if you *design* each block to only have a \(k\)-dimensional “active” rotation, you have
\[
\mathrm{rank}\bigl((R - I)\,W^0\bigr)
\;\le\;
\mathrm{rank}(R - I)
\;=\;
r\,k.
\]
By choosing \(r\) and \(k\) so that \(r\,k = r'\), you exactly match a LoRA–style rank-\(r'\) update.
