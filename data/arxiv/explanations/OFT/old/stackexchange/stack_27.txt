### How do OFT and COFT behave across different diffusion timesteps and noise levels?
Question:
Diffusion model behavior varies by timestep. Are the learned \(\mathbf{R}\) matrices implicitly adapting the model more at certain timesteps? Have you inspected per-timestep losses or guidance strength to see if OFT changes early-noise denoising vs late denoising differently? Could one consider timestep-dependent orthogonal finetuning for finer control?
Answer:
In the current OFT and COFT formulation, each orthogonal update  

$$
\bm{W} = \bm{R}\,\bm{W}^0 \quad\text{with}\quad \bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I}
$$

is applied uniformly to the network’s weights—whether you are denoising at $t\!=\!1000$ or at $t\!=\!1$. In other words, because the U-Net shares the same weights (and thus the same $\bm{R}$) at every diffusion step, there is no built-in mechanism in the paper that lets the finetuning “focus” more on early versus late timesteps or on high-noise versus low-noise regimes.  

We did not, in this work, break out per-timestep losses or measure guidance strength as a function of the noise level to see whether the learnt orthogonal matrices happen to be “doing more” at certain $t$. All of our quantitative and qualitative controls (for both subject-driven and controllable generation) were aggregated over the usual DDPM sampling trajectory, and the only place where the paper explicitly varies anything with $t$ is in the fixed sinusoidal or learned time-embedding that already lives inside the pretrained network.  

That said, your idea—making $\bm{R}$ itself a function of the diffusion time—would be a natural extension. Concretely, one could imagine parameterizing a small family  

$$
\{\bm{R}_1,\dots,\bm{R}_K\},\quad \bm{R}_k^\top \bm{R}_k = \bm{I},
$$

and indexing them by coarse time-bins (or even learning a continuous mapping $t \mapsto \bm{R}(t)$ via a tiny MLP). This would give the model the flexibility to preserve hyperspherical energy at every noise level yet still adapt the finetuning to the distinct demands of “rough sketch” steps (high $t$) versus “detail refinement” steps (low $t$). You would pay for it in extra parameters (and in having to enforce orthogonality across a set of matrices rather than just one), but it could yield finer control—especially if you find that certain timesteps are systematically under- or over-corrected by a single global rotation.  

In short, the paper’s OFT/COFT applies the same $\bm{R}$ at every diffusion step, and we have not yet inspected a per-timestep loss curve or adapted $\bm{R}$ to different noise levels. Timestep-dependent OFT is conceptually straightforward—just learn $\bm{R}(t)$ instead of a single $\bm{R}$—and could be a promising avenue if you observe significant variation in the denoising signal as a function of $t$.
