### 4. Intuition and practical takeaways

1. **Generic OFT ≠ low-rank.** A block-diagonal \(R\) with no further eigenvalue-1 constraints will produce a *full-rank* additive update \((R - I)\,W^0\).  
2. **LoRA-style low rank** arises in OFT *only* if each orthogonal block intentionally leaves a large subspace *fixed* and rotates in *only* a small subspace of dimension \(k\).  
3. **Matching LoRA’s budget**: set your number of blocks \(r\) and active rotation dimension \(k\) so that \(r\,k = r'\). Then the OFT update is guaranteed to lie inside the same rank-\(r'\) subspace as a LoRA update.  
4. **Otherwise, OFT is fundamentally different**—it is a *multiplicative*, full-rank, hypersphere-preserving transformation rather than an additive low-rank correction.  

In short, while LoRA *by construction* keeps \(\Delta W\) in a rank-\(r'\) subspace, OFT only *in special parametrizations* (few-dimensional “active” rotations per block) will yield a comparably low rank. Generically, \((R - I)\) is full-rank unless you explicitly engineer many unit-eigenvalue directions.
