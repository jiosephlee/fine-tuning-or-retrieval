In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the constrained variant COFT enforces $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ so that the finetuned weights lie within a hypersphere of radius $\epsilon$ centered at the pretrained weights $\bm{M}^0$, which geometrically primarily bounds the $\ell_2$ norm $\|\bm{M}-\bm{M}^0\|_2$, i.e., the Euclidean distance. In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors note that if neuron directions are analogous to Fourier phase, then the neuron operation analogous to changing only the Fourier amplitude (leaving semantics largely intact) corresponds to scaling the magnitudes. This follows from their angle–magnitude decoupling perspective: they argue and empirically show (via a toy autoencoder) that semantic content is primarily encoded in neuron directions (angles) rather than magnitudes—reconstructing with angular information alone nearly recovers the image, while using only magnitudes fails—mirroring the classical result that the phase spectrum preserves image semantics whereas the magnitude spectrum does not. Formally, their hyperspherical energy objective depends only on normalized neurons, i.e., on directions \(\hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|\), so any scaling \(\bm{w}_i \mapsto s_i \bm{w}_i\) with \(s_i>0\) leaves \(\text{HE}(\cdot)\) unchanged, just as modifying Fourier amplitudes leaves the phase unchanged. They operationalize this in re-scaled OFT with a diagonal matrix \(\bm{D}=\mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n)\) in the forward pass \(\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top \bm{x}\), where \(\bm{D}\) scales neuron magnitudes without rotating their directions; thus, magnitude scaling is the neural analogue of adjusting Fourier amplitude and, by design, perturbs semantics minimally while modulating strength.

According to the paper “Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning,” minimizing the hyperspherical energy difference ($\text{HE}$) in OFT primarily preserves the relationships between neurons in terms of their pairwise angles. This follows because $\text{HE}(\bm{W})=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$ depends only on the normalized neurons $\hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|$, and $\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^2=2(1-\cos\theta_{ij})$ is a monotone function of the angle $\theta_{ij}$ (or equivalently of cosine similarity), so matching $\text{HE}(\bm{W})$ to $\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$ enforces the same pairwise angular structure; moreover, OFT achieves the attainable minimum (zero HE difference) by applying a layer-shared orthogonal transform $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ with $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I}$, which preserves inner products of normalized vectors: $\langle \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_i,\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_j\rangle=\langle \hat{\bm{w}}_i,\hat{\bm{w}}_j\rangle$, and because magnitudes are normalized out, the only degrees of freedom relevant to $\text{HE}$ are the angles, so minimizing the $\text{HE}$ difference amounts to preserving these pairwise angular relationships.

In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the mathematical property of orthogonal transformations that ensures hyperspherical similarities remain unchanged is the preservation of pairwise inner products. This follows because an orthogonal matrix satisfies \( \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}=\bm{I} \), so for any two neurons \( \bm{w}_i,\bm{w}_j \in \mathbb{R}^d \), we have \( \langle \bm{R}\bm{w}_i,\bm{R}\bm{w}_j\rangle = \bm{w}_i^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}\,\bm{w}_j = \bm{w}_i^\top \bm{w}_j = \langle \bm{w}_i,\bm{w}_j\rangle \), and norms are preserved \( \|\bm{R}\bm{w}_i\|=\|\bm{w}_i\| \); therefore the cosine similarity between normalized neurons \( \hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\| \) satisfies \( \langle \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i}, \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_j} \rangle = \langle \hat{\bm{w}}_i,\hat{\bm{w}}_j\rangle \), which implies angular distances and hence hyperspherical similarities are invariant under \(\bm{R}\). Equivalently, the pairwise chordal distances that define the hyperspherical energy \( \text{HE}(\bm{W})=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1} \) remain unchanged because \( \|\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_i-\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_j\|^2 = 2-2\langle \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_i,\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_j\rangle = 2-2\langle \hat{\bm{w}}_i,\hat{\bm{w}}_j\rangle = \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^2 \), so applying the same orthogonal transform to all neurons preserves all pairwise angles and thus the hyperspherical energy.

According to the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', if finetuning instead minimized hyperspherical energy, pretrained semantic structures would likely be destroyed. The paper defines hyperspherical energy for a layer with weights \( \bm{W}=\{\bm{w}_i\}_{i=1}^n \) as \( \text{HE}(\bm{W})=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1} \) with \( \hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\| \), and explains that reducing this energy drives neurons toward a uniformly spaced, redundancy-minimized configuration on the unit hypersphere, which is desirable for classification but not for finetuning generative models whose pretrained performance depends on the non-uniform, semantically structured angular relationships among neurons. Because angular differences encode semantics (supported by their toy autoencoder showing magnitude carries little information while angles can reconstruct images), forcing \(\text{HE}\) to be small would change pairwise cosine similarities and thus the relational geometry that underpins text-to-image semantics, effectively overwriting the pretrained angular constellation; in contrast, OFT preserves these pairwise angles by applying a shared orthogonal transform \( \bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0 \) with \( \bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I} \), achieving \( \|\text{HE}(\bm{W})-\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)\|=0 \) and thereby maintaining the semantic generation ability that minimizing hyperspherical energy would disrupt.

In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors propose Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT), which improves finetuning stability by imposing an additional constraint that bounds the deviation of the Cayley-parameterized orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ from the identity via $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$, referred to as the radius constraint. This is sensible because COFT retains OFT’s core invariance—orthogonal transformations preserve hyperspherical energy—while explicitly limiting how far the finetuned weights $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ can move from the pretrained $\bm{W}^0$, thereby constraining semantic drift and collapse; concretely, with the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ and the Neumann approximation $\bm{R}\approx\bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$, the bound $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ translates to a simple projected-gradient constraint $\|\bm{Q}\|\le\epsilon'$, making enforcement practical while keeping initialization at $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$ (i.e., $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$) to start exactly from the pretrained model; empirically, this explicit deviation control yields smoother, more stable optimization (e.g., insensitivity to learning rate/iterations and avoidance of collapse) by striking a better flexibility–regularity trade-off than unconstrained finetuning, all while preserving pairwise neuron angles and thus the pretrained model’s semantic structure.

In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the 2D Fourier analogy identifies the spectrum analogous to neuron directions that preserve semantics as the phase spectrum. This follows from the authors’ observation that semantic information is largely encoded in angular relations: in Fourier terms an image’s spectrum can be written as A(u,v)e^{i\phi(u,v)}, where the phase \phi controls spatial structure while the magnitude A mainly sets contrast, and it is well known that replacing A with random values while keeping \phi approximately preserves perceptual semantics; analogously, in neural layers a neuron’s “direction” \hat{\bm{w}}=\bm{w}/\|\bm{w}\| (and pairwise angles via cosine similarity) governs semantic behavior far more than its norm \|\bm{w}\|. Their toy autoencoder shows that reconstructing with angular information alone (i.e., using \cos\angle(\bm{w},\bm{x}) instead of \bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x}) nearly recovers the image, while using only magnitudes fails, mirroring the phase–magnitude dichotomy. OFT explicitly preserves these angular/“phase-like” relations by enforcing an orthogonal transform \bm{R} with \bm{R}^{\top}\bm{R}=\bm{I} so that pairwise angles and the hyperspherical energy \text{HE}(\bm{W})=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}-\hat{\bm{w}}_{j}\|^{-1} are invariant when \bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^{0}, thereby maintaining the semantic structure just as preserving \phi(u,v) maintains image semantics in the Fourier domain.

In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors explain that applying the same orthogonal transformation to all neurons in a layer preserves pairwise neuron angles; if each neuron instead received a different orthogonal transformation, the expected outcome is that the pairwise neuron angles are not preserved. This follows because for neurons {w_i} in a layer, the cosine of the angle between two neurons is cos(θ_{ij}) = (w_i^\top w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|); if a single orthogonal matrix R (with R^\top R = I) is applied to every neuron, then cos(θ'_{ij}) = ((R w_i)^\top (R w_j))/(\|R w_i\|\|R w_j\|) = (w_i^\top R^\top R w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|) = cos(θ_{ij}), so all pairwise angles—and thus the hyperspherical energy HE(W) = \sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat w_i - \hat w_j\|^{-1}—are exactly invariant under the shared rotation/reflection, as the paper exploits in Eq. (1)–(2); however, if each neuron receives its own orthogonal transform R_i, then the pairwise cosine becomes cos(θ'_{ij}) = ((R_i w_i)^\top (R_j w_j))/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|) = (w_i^\top (R_i^\top R_j) w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|), which equals the original cosine only when R_i^\top R_j = I for all i,j (i.e., all R_i are identical), so in general the inner products (and hence angles and HE) will change, breaking the invariance that underpins OFT’s preservation of semantic structure.

In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors note that when the number of diagonal blocks equals the number of input channels ($r = C$), each block transforms a unique neuron channel, making OFT's transform most akin to a depth-wise convolution. This follows because OFT applies a left-multiplicative orthogonal transform $\bm{R}$ on the input dimension of each layer, and for a convolution with weights $\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{C_{\text{out}} \times C_{\text{in}} \times k \times k}$ one can reshape to a matrix with input dimension $d=C_{\text{in}}k^2$ and use a block-diagonal $\bm{R}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{R}_1,\dots,\bm{R}_{C_{\text{in}}})$ where each $\bm{R}_c \in \mathrm{O}(k^2)$ acts only on the $k^2$ spatial coefficients of input channel $c$; then the transformed weights are $(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)$, which replaces each channel’s spatial filter patch by $\bm{R}_c \bm{w}^0_c$ without mixing across channels, yielding per-channel, spatial-only adaptations just like depth-wise convolution. Concretely, with input feature maps $\{x_c\}_{c=1}^{C_{\text{in}}}$ and flattened per-channel filters $\{\bm{w}^0_c\}$, the OFT forward update gives $\sum_{c=1}^{C_{\text{in}}} \langle \bm{R}_c \bm{w}^0_c, \mathrm{patch}(x_c) \rangle$, which is equivalent to applying an independent orthogonal rotation in the $k \times k$ kernel space for each input channel and never mixing information between different channels, matching the operational independence that defines depth-wise convolution.
