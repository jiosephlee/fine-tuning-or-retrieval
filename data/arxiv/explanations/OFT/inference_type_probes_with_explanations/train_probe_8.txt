In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors explain that applying the same orthogonal transformation to all neurons in a layer preserves pairwise neuron angles; if each neuron instead received a different orthogonal transformation, the expected outcome is that the pairwise neuron angles are not preserved. This follows because for neurons {w_i} in a layer, the cosine of the angle between two neurons is cos(θ_{ij}) = (w_i^\top w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|); if a single orthogonal matrix R (with R^\top R = I) is applied to every neuron, then cos(θ'_{ij}) = ((R w_i)^\top (R w_j))/(\|R w_i\|\|R w_j\|) = (w_i^\top R^\top R w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|) = cos(θ_{ij}), so all pairwise angles—and thus the hyperspherical energy HE(W) = \sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat w_i - \hat w_j\|^{-1}—are exactly invariant under the shared rotation/reflection, as the paper exploits in Eq. (1)–(2); however, if each neuron receives its own orthogonal transform R_i, then the pairwise cosine becomes cos(θ'_{ij}) = ((R_i w_i)^\top (R_j w_j))/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|) = (w_i^\top (R_i^\top R_j) w_j)/(\|w_i\|\|w_j\|), which equals the original cosine only when R_i^\top R_j = I for all i,j (i.e., all R_i are identical), so in general the inner products (and hence angles and HE) will change, breaking the invariance that underpins OFT’s preservation of semantic structure.
