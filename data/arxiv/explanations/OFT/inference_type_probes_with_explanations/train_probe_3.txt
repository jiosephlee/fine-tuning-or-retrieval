According to the paper “Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning,” minimizing the hyperspherical energy difference ($\text{HE}$) in OFT primarily preserves the relationships between neurons in terms of their pairwise angles. This follows because $\text{HE}(\bm{W})=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$ depends only on the normalized neurons $\hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|$, and $\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^2=2(1-\cos\theta_{ij})$ is a monotone function of the angle $\theta_{ij}$ (or equivalently of cosine similarity), so matching $\text{HE}(\bm{W})$ to $\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$ enforces the same pairwise angular structure; moreover, OFT achieves the attainable minimum (zero HE difference) by applying a layer-shared orthogonal transform $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ with $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I}$, which preserves inner products of normalized vectors: $\langle \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_i,\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}}_j\rangle=\langle \hat{\bm{w}}_i,\hat{\bm{w}}_j\rangle$, and because magnitudes are normalized out, the only degrees of freedom relevant to $\text{HE}$ are the angles, so minimizing the $\text{HE}$ difference amounts to preserving these pairwise angular relationships.
