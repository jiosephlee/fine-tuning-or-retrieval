According to the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', in COFT the Cayley parameter that is directly constrained to keep $\bm{R}$ near the identity $\bm{I}$ is the matrix $\bm{Q}$. COFT enforces a proximity constraint $\lVert \bm{R}-\bm{I}\rVert \le \epsilon$ while also maintaining orthogonality via the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ with a skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$, i.e., $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$. Using the Neumann series to expand $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$, the paper shows that for sufficiently small $\bm{Q}$ one has the approximation $\bm{R}\approx \bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$. Therefore, the deviation $\lVert \bm{R}-\bm{I}\rVert$ is controlled at first order by $\lVert \bm{Q}\rVert$, making it natural to move the proximity constraint inside the Cayley transform and impose $\lVert \bm{Q}-\bm{0}\rVert \le \epsilon'$ directly on $\bm{Q}$.

This reparameterized constraint is both practical and principled: initializing $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$ yields $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$ exactly, achieving identity initialization, and constraining $\bm{Q}$ via projected gradient descent keeps the learned transform $\bm{R}$ in a small neighborhood of $\bm{I}$ while preserving orthogonality. Since small $\bm{Q}$ induces small angular deviations (via $\bm{R}\approx \bm{I}+2\bm{Q}$), COFT simultaneously limits the Euclidean deviation from the pretrained weights and preserves the pairwise angles—and thus the hyperspherical energy—of neurons, which is the paper’s core desideratum for semantic preservation. The Cayley form guarantees $\det(\bm{R})=1$ (special orthogonal group), so this procedure cleanly enforces the COFT constraints by acting directly on $\bm{Q}$ as the parameter that controls proximity of $\bm{R}$ to $\bm{I}$.
