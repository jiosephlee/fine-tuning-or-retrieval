According to the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the Cayley parameterization can only produce orthogonal matrices with det(𝑹)=1, which belong to the special orthogonal group. This follows from the specific form of the Cayley transform, 𝑹=(𝑰+𝑸)(𝑰−𝑸)^{-1} with 𝑸^{\top}=-𝑸. A skew-symmetric matrix 𝑸 has purely imaginary eigenvalues that come in conjugate pairs ±iα, and consequently the eigenvalues of (𝑰±𝑸) also come in conjugate pairs. One can see that det(𝑰+𝑸)=det(𝑰−𝑸) for skew-symmetric 𝑸, which implies det(𝑹)=det(𝑰+𝑸)/det(𝑰−𝑸)=1. Equivalently, the Cayley map parameterizes a connected subset of the orthogonal group containing the identity (when 𝑸=𝟎, 𝑹=𝑰), and because det(𝑹) cannot change sign without crossing det(𝑹)=0 (which never occurs for orthogonal matrices), it stays at +1. Thus Cayley yields 𝑹∈SO(d) (proper rotations) but not reflections (det(𝑹)=-1).

This restriction is sensible within OFT because preserving pairwise angles—and hence hyperspherical energy—does not require reflections; any rotation 𝑹∈SO(d) maintains cosine similarities exactly, i.e., ⟨\hat{𝒘}_i,\hat{𝒘}_j⟩=⟨\widehat{𝑹𝒘}_i,\widehat{𝑹𝒘}_j⟩, so HE(𝑾)=HE(𝑹𝑾) holds whether 𝑹 is a rotation or a reflection. In practice the paper finds that limiting 𝑹 to SO(d) “does not affect the performance,” because the semantic preservation and controllability they seek arise from rotating neuron directions on the unit hypersphere, and reflections offer no essential advantage over rotations for this purpose. Moreover, Cayley parameterization is computationally efficient and differentiable, enabling stable optimization of 𝑹 while guaranteeing orthogonality and det(𝑹)=1, which fits the OFT/COFT design of small, well-regularized angular updates around the identity 𝑰.
