In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors propose Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT), which improves finetuning stability by imposing an additional constraint that bounds the deviation of the Cayley-parameterized orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \) from the identity via \( \| \bm{R}-\bm{I} \| \leq \epsilon \), referred to as the radius constraint. This constraint explicitly limits how far the finetuned weights can move from the pretrained ones while still operating within the class of orthogonal transformations that preserve hyperspherical energy. Because text-to-image diffusion models encode semantic generation ability in the pairwise angles among neurons, pure OFT keeps these angles intact by setting \( \bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 \) with \( \bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I} \), but unrestricted orthogonal updates can still rotate all neurons substantially and cause abrupt behavioral changes. By enforcing \( \| \bm{R}-\bm{I} \| \leq \epsilon \), COFT prevents large angular deviations from the pretrained directions, thereby preserving the intrinsic semantic structure and making optimization trajectories smoother and less prone to collapse. This explicit neighborhood constraint complements hyperspherical energy preservation with a direct control of Euclidean proximity, yielding a better balance between flexibility (task adaptation) and regularity (semantic preservation).

The Cayley parameterization makes this radius constraint practical and effective. With \( \bm{R} = (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} \) and \( \bm{Q} = -\bm{Q}^\top \), a Neumann-series approximation gives \( \bm{R} \approx \bm{I} + 2\bm{Q} + \mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2) \), so enforcing \( \| \bm{R}-\bm{I} \| \leq \epsilon \) is equivalent to a small-norm constraint on \( \bm{Q} \), i.e., \( \| \bm{Q} \| \leq \epsilon' \). This reduces the stability problem to projected gradient descent on a skew-symmetric parameter living on a smooth manifold, ensuring updates remain in a controlled neighborhood while retaining exact orthogonality (and thus the invariance of hyperspherical energy). Empirically, this radius constraint makes learning rate and iteration choices far less sensitive: with a properly chosen \( \epsilon \), COFT avoids the sudden convergence and collapse phenomena observed with unconstrained finetuning, and produces stable, predictable improvements in controllability without sacrificing image fidelity.
