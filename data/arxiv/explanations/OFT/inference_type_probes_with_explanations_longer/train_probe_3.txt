According to the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', minimizing the hyperspherical energy difference ($\text{HE}$) in OFT primarily preserves the relationships between neurons in terms of their pairwise angles. The hyperspherical energy for a layer with neurons $\{\bm{w}_i\}_{i=1}^n$ is defined on their normalized directions $\hat{\bm{w}}_i := \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\|$ as $\text{HE}(\bm{W}) := \sum_{i\neq j} \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$. Because $\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^2 = 2 - 2\cos\theta_{ij}$ with $\theta_{ij}$ the angle between $\hat{\bm{w}}_i$ and $\hat{\bm{w}}_j$, $\text{HE}$ is a symmetric aggregate of pairwise angular similarities and is insensitive to magnitudes. Orthogonal Finetuning enforces $\bm{W} = \bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ with a layer-shared orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$, so that $(\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i})^\top (\widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_j}) = \hat{\bm{w}}_i^\top \hat{\bm{w}}_j$; thus the set of pairwise angles $\{\theta_{ij}\}$ is preserved and the attainable minimum of $\|\text{HE}(\bm{W})-\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)\|$ is zero. In other words, driving the $\text{HE}$ difference to zero under a shared orthogonal transform exactly preserves the relational geometry of neurons on the unit hypersphere.

This preservation of pairwise angles is the crux of maintaining the pretrained model’s semantic structure during finetuning. Since the forward pass becomes $\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^\top \bm{x}$ with $\bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I}$, OFT “rotates” or “reflects” all neuron directions coherently in the same canonical basis, keeping $\hat{\bm{w}}_i^\top \hat{\bm{w}}_j$ invariant across all pairs $(i,j)$. The paper argues this invariance is crucial because angular relationships capture semantic information in high-dimensional representations, and changing neuron directions while preserving their pairwise angles allows controlled adaptation without collapsing the pretrained generative capability. Even the re-scaled variant, $\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top \bm{x}$ with diagonal $\bm{D}$, leaves $\text{HE}$ unchanged due to normalization, reinforcing that OFT’s objective of minimizing $\Delta \text{HE}$ primarily safeguards the neurons’ pairwise angular structure.
