In the paper “Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning”, the authors note that if neuron directions are analogous to Fourier phase, then the neuron operation analogous to changing only the Fourier amplitude (leaving semantics largely intact) corresponds to scaling the magnitudes. This follows directly from their phase–magnitude analogy: in a Fourier representation one can write a signal as having magnitude and phase, with the phase component \( \phi \) largely determining semantic structure while the magnitude \( A \) modulates intensities. The paper empirically emphasizes that angular information (i.e., directions on the unit hypersphere) encodes semantics, whereas magnitudes carry little semantic content: their toy autoencoder shows that reconstructing with cosine (angular) information retains the image’s semantics, while magnitude-only reconstructions fail. Translating this to neurons, the direction of a weight vector \( \hat{\bm{w}} = \bm{w}/\|\bm{w}\| \) plays the role of phase, and scaling the weight by a positive scalar \( s \) modifies only the magnitude \( \|\bm{w}\| \) without changing \( \hat{\bm{w}} \), i.e., \( \widehat{s\bm{w}} = \bm{w}/\|\bm{w}\| \). Thus, operations that rescale neuron magnitudes are the neural analogue of changing amplitudes while keeping phase fixed, and they largely preserve semantic structure.

The paper’s re-scaled OFT makes this correspondence explicit by augmenting orthogonal finetuning with a learnable diagonal scaling \( \bm{D} = \mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n) \), yielding the forward pass \( \bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top \bm{x} \). Since hyperspherical energy depends only on normalized neurons, scaling does not affect the pairwise angular relationships: for any neuron \( \bm{w}_i \), \( \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0 s_i} = \widehat{\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0} \), and thus the hyperspherical energy \( \text{HE}(\bm{W}) = \sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1} \) remains invariant under \( \bm{D} \). This mirrors the Fourier phenomenon cited by the authors: images reconstructed with original phase and altered magnitudes retain semantics. Consequently, scaling neuron magnitudes is the natural “amplitude-only” operation in the neural setting, modifying intensity-like factors while preserving the semantic content encoded by directions.
