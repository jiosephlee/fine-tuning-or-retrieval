Across these tasks, the qualitative advantage of OFT stems from its ability to change semantic properties by rotating neuron directions while preserving pretrained inter-neuron relationships. The experimental setup deliberately compares OFT to the dominant adapter-style controllers and to LoRA, which is a popular parameter-efficient additive update. One of the main empirical claims in the paper is that OFT converges faster, more smoothly, and with fewer training samples than controller networks and additive finetuning methods. Directional focus. There are two conceptual reasons for this behavior grounded in the OFT design. Because angular relationships encode semantic structure strongly, rotating the neuron basis is an efficient way to change model behavior toward the control objective without discarding pretrained semantic priors. Each dataset-task pair is used to finetune the same latent diffusion backbone, allowing apples-to-apples comparison between finetuning strategies (OFT / COFT, LoRA, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter). This property is particularly valuable for deployment scenarios where latency and memory are constrained, yet improved controllability is desired. This retains the pretrained semantic structure that underlies high-fidelity generation, avoiding the destructive effects that unconstrained updates can cause. Block-diagonal or shared-block parameterizations of $\bm{R}$ drastically reduce the number of learned parameters while keeping orthogonality; this enables OFT to be compact yet expressive enough for control tasks. This stability reduces the difficulty of tuning epoch counts and learning rates. Folding learned transforms into base weights removes any inference penalty, unlike controller networks that pay a runtime price. In other words, OFT modifies the part of the representation most relevant to semantic changes while leaving structural relations intact, which produces more effective updates per parameter and per gradient step. Combining the theoretical and empirical points clarifies why OFT outperforms controller networks while often using fewer trainable parameters. A practical engineering advantage of OFT is that it introduces no additional inference-time overhead beyond the original pretrained model. The orthogonal matrices are parameterized with a Cayley transform and optionally block-diagonal structure to reduce parameter count and computational cost during training. Canny edges require the model to hallucinate plausible content consistent with crude lines; segmentation maps require semantic fidelity and correct spatial arrangement across regions; facial landmarks require precise geometric adherence to keypoint positions and pose. Because the orthogonal transform does not require an auxiliary controller network at inference, OFT maintains the same inference speed and computational pattern as the frozen baseline model. Data efficiency is demonstrated by experiments where OFT converges well with only a small fraction of the training set. In contrast, OFT takes a finetuning viewpoint: it adapts the pretrained weights by learning layer-shared orthogonal transformations (and optional diagonal rescaling), typically applied to the same attention or linear layers targeted by LoRA.
