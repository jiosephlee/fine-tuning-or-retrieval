\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### How does the Cosine/Sine encoding formula in Equation (1) change after applying the searched rescale factors?
Question:
Equation (1) shows the standard RoPE formulation. After the search, each dimension gets its own λ_i and a token‑position threshold. Can someone write out the modified formula explicitly for a token at position n and dimension i to see the exact scaling applied?
Answer:
In the original RoPE formulation each dimension \(i\) of the rotary embedding for a token at position \(n\) is obtained by rotating the query‑key vectors by the “angle”

\[
\theta_{i}(n)=\frac{n}{\beta^{\,i}}, \qquad \beta=\theta^{\,{2/d}},
\]

so that the actual embedding is

\[
\bigl[\,\cos \theta_{0}(n),\;\sin\theta_{0}(n),\;\dots,\;
\cos \theta_{d/2-1}(n),\;\sin\theta_{d/2-1}(n)\,\bigr].
\]

To enlarge the context window the authors introduce *non‑uniform* rescaling factors that depend both on the RoPE dimension and on a token‑position threshold.  For every dimension \(i\) a scalar \(\lambda_i\) is learned (the paper uses the notation \(\hat \lambda_i\) but we will write \(\lambda_i\) for clarity) and a single token‑position threshold \(\hat n\) is chosen.  In the notation of Eq. (3) in the paper

\[
\mathbb{I}(\lambda_i,\hat n)=
\begin{cases}
1          & n<\hat n,\\[4pt]
\frac{1}{\lambda_i} & n\ge \hat n .
\end{cases}
\]

Thus for the first \(\hat n\) tokens the original angle \(\theta_i(n)=n/\beta^{\,i}\) is used unchanged, while for every subsequent token the angle is multiplied by the factor \(1/\lambda_i\).  Putting this together, the *modified* RoPE angle for dimension \(i\) and position \(n\) becomes

\[
\tilde{\theta}_{i}(n)=
\mathbb{I}(\lambda_i,\hat n)\;\frac{n}{\beta^{\,i}}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{n}{\beta^{\,i}}, & n<\hat n,\\[6pt]
\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda_i}\;\frac{n}{\beta^{\,i}}, & n\ge \hat n .
\end{cases}
\]

The resulting positional embedding is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Bigl[
\cos \tilde{\theta}_{0}(n),\;\sin \tilde{\theta}_{0}(n),\;
\dots,\;
\cos \tilde{\theta}_{d/2-1}(n),\;\sin \tilde{\theta}_{d/2-1}(n)
\Bigr]
}
\]

with the two hyper‑parameters \(\{\lambda_i\}_{i=0}^{d/2-1}\) and \(\hat n\) chosen by the evolutionary search described in Section 3 of the paper.

Intuitively the \(\lambda_i\) control how aggressively the rotation frequency is *scaled down* (when \(\lambda_i>1\)) or *left unchanged* (when \(\lambda_i=1\)).  Because low‑index dimensions encode higher angular frequencies, the search consistently finds smaller \(\lambda_i\) for them and larger values for higher‑index (lower‑frequency) dimensions, exactly the pattern suggested by the NTK theory.  The threshold \(\hat n\) reflects the observation that the first few tokens receive a disproportionately large attention weight, so keeping their original angular pattern intact helps preserve local coherence.  

In practice, one simply plugs the retrieved \(\lambda_i\) and \(\hat n\) into the above equations when computing the rotary embeddings for any sequence.  The rest of the transformer architecture remains unchanged, making LongRoPE a drop‑in positional‑embedding upgrade that can be applied to any RoPE‑based LLM.
