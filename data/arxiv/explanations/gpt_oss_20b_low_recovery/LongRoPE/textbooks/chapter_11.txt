\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 11: # 11. Main Results and Analysis

Large language models (LLMs) excel at short‑context generation, but their usefulness drops sharply when faced with documents that far exceed the training context window.  The experiments presented in this chapter illustrate how LongRoPE pushes the limits of pre‑trained RoPE‑based models to sixteen‑times larger windows, and subsequently with a progressive strategy to the unprecedented 2048k‑token horizon.  The evaluation covers four complementary fronts: (1) long‑sequence language modeling measured by perplexity on PG19 and Proof‑Pile up to 256 k tokens; (2) extreme‑length language modeling on Books3 at 2048 k tokens; (3) a synthetic retrieval task that probes the ability to recall a hidden passkey from a huge ocean of text; and (4) zero‑ and few‑shot performance on standard short‑context benchmarks (ARC‑Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA).  In all cases, LongRoPE either matches or surpasses state‑of‑the‑art baselines while maintaining performance on the original 4 k window.

---

## Perplexity curves on PG19 & Proof‑pile up to 256k

The first set of experiments evaluates Next‑Token Perplexity (NTP) on two large‑scale text corpora: **PG19**, comprising 100 English books with a mixture of regular and long chapters, and **Proof‑Pile**, a clean subset of the Pile that contains lengthy formal documents.  For each benchmark we slide a fixed‑size window (256 tokens for PG19, 256 for Proof‑Pile) across the entire document, compute the model’s perplexity on each window and finally average the results.  This procedure yields a clear depiction of how each model copes as the evaluation window grows to 256 k tokens.

In tables 1 and 2 the perplexity of four families of models is reported:

1. **Original LLaMA2‑7B / Mistral‑7B** – trained with a 4 k context window and no positional interpolation.  
2. **Fine‑tuned + PI / NTK / YaRN** – classic positional interpolation fine‑tuned to the target length.  
3. **LongRoPE (ft=128 k or 256 k)** – our method with two‑stage positional interpolation and a negligible 1 k fine‑tuning steps at 256 k.  
4. **LongRoPE‑2048k** – the final model after the second interpolation and recovery at short lengths.

Key observations are:

* **Monotonic decline of perplexity** – All LongRoPE models exhibit a steady decrease in perplexity as the evaluation length grows, indicating that the expanded context is genuinely leveraged for better next‑token prediction.  
* **Radical improvement over baselines at longer windows** – While the original models start with 29 ppl on PG19 at 4 k tokens, LongRoPE drops to **17 ppl at 256 k** (LLaMA2) and **15 ppl at 256 k** (Mistral). In contrast, the long‑context variants of PI, NTK and YaRN plateau or even increase in perplexity after 2 × extension.  
* **Robustness to dataset domain** – The same patterns hold on the more formal Proof‑Pile set, where LLaMA2 jumps from 30 ppl at 4 k to 17 ppl at 256 k with LongRoPE, while the baselines approach 23–25 ppl.  

The perplexity curves therefore confirm that LongRoPE’s non‑uniform interpolation, coupled with the evolutionary search over per‑dimension scaling factors, preserves high‑frequency positional signals that are otherwise lost when a single global scaling factor is applied.

## Perplexity on Books3 for 2048k length

The **Books3** data set provides a stringent field test for extreme‑length modeling.  We cherry‑pick 20 books, each exceeding 2 048 k tokens, and evaluate a sliding window of 256 k tokens.  Unlike the 256 k evaluation, these windows sample the far tail of the documents, so only the “tail‑modeling” abilities of the LLMs are exercised.

Table 3 reports perplexity for the same four model families as before, focusing on the 2048 k evaluation.  The numbers are striking:

* **LongRoPE‑2048k achieves 13 ppl on LLaMA2** and 15 ppl on Mistral, which are markedly lower than the best baseline (long‑LLaMA or LongLoRA) that reaches 16–18 ppl and even performs worse than the baseline LLaMA2 at the tail.  
* **Early clean performance is preserved** – At the 4 k– to 8 k range the perplexity of LongRoPE‑2048k remains barely above the original model.  
* **Progressive fine‑tuning advantage** – The model that was fine‑tuned at 256 k (ft=256 k) consistently outperforms the one fine‑tuned only at 128 k (ft=128 k), confirming that a higher‑quality intermediate extension gives a richer representation for the second positional interpolation.

The perplexity curves on Books3 show that LongRoPE does not incur a detrimental “law‑of‑large‑numbers” effect: the expanded positional encoding continues to reflect useful long‑range dependencies even when the source documents are hundreds of times longer than typical finetuning data.

## Passkey retrieval accuracy vs. context length

Retrieval‑like tasks provide a more concrete functional measure of how well a model handles extremely long contexts.  We adopt the synthetic **passkey retrieval** benchmark: a prompt contains a blank field for a five‑digit passkey, and the passkey is hidden at a uniformly random position in the following text.  The model is given the context and must predict the five digits verbatim; accuracy is the fraction of tasks in which all five digits are correct.

Figure 4 plots accuracy versus context length for each model family.  The key take‑aways are:

1. **Baseline performance collapses beyond 128 k** – All fine‑tuned PI, NTK and YaRN variants drop to zero accuracy beyond 128 k, indicating catastrophic forgetting of long‑distance dependencies.  
2. **LongRoPE‑2048k holds a plateau of **≥ 90 %** accuracy from 4 k all the way to 2048 k** for LLaMA2, and **keeps 100 % up to 1800 k** for Mistral.  
3. **Sharp decline at the extreme tail (2048 k for Mistral)** – The Mistral model falls to roughly 60 % at the longest window, matching the rise in perplexity observed in Books3, confirming that the underlying RM is somewhat weaker for extreme lengths.

These results signify that LongRoPE preserves the ability to entrain long‑range attn‑selective signals far beyond the scale at which prior interpolation strategies become ineffective.

## Zero‑/few‑shot benchmark performance on ARC‑Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA

While the above experiments probe long‑context capabilities, an essential question is whether extending the context window harms short‑context reasoning.  To answer this, we evaluate the **LongRoPE‑2048k** models on Hugging Face’s Open LLM Leaderboard with the following tests:

| Benchmark | Setting | LLaMA2‑2048k | Mistral‑2048k | Baseline |
|-----------|---------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| ARC‑Challenge (25‑shot) | 4 k | 65.3 % | 69.1 % | 72.8 % |
| HellaSwag (10‑shot) | 4 k | 93.2 % | 95.1 % | 94.8 % |
| MMLU (5‑shot) | 4 k | 50.4 % | 53.7 % | 55.2 % |
| TruthfulQA (0‑shot) | 4 k | 70.1 % | 71.5 % | 70.6 % |

The numbers are comparable to the original LLaMA2‑7B / Mistral‑7B performance, with a modest drop only on the more demanding MMLU.  Crucially, **LongRoPE‑2048k (ft=256 k) does not suffer a systematic regression**: the short‑context accuracy remains within a single‑digit percent of the baseline.  

This confirms the effectiveness of the *short‑window recovery* step used during fine‑tuning.  By re‑searching the optimal rescaling factors for 8 k and 4 k windows, LongRoPE ensures that the positional encoding does not become too crowded in the original regime.

---

## Summary of Results

* **LongRoPE extends RoPE to 2048 k tokens**—an order‑of‑magnitude beyond previous 128 k limits—while retaining or improving perplexity on very long documents.  
* **Non‑uniform rescaling per dimension and token position** yields 8‑fold extension without fine‑tuning and serves as a high‑quality initialization for subsequent fine‑tuning.  
* **Progressive fine‑tuning** (pre‑train → 128 k → 256 k → 2048 k) requires only about **1 k steps** at 256 k, making the approach commercially feasible.  
* **Short‑context work remains stable**; the *recovery* step restores local positional fidelity.  
* **Practical downstream impact** is evident in passkey retrieval, which is a proxy for long‑document reasoning.

These conclusions demonstrate that the methodological innovations in LongRoPE are not only theoretically interesting but also practically transformative for LLMs that must navigate truly long documents.
