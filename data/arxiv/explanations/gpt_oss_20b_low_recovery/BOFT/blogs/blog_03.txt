\title{Blogs about the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

# BOFT in Action: From Vision Transformers to Text‑to‑Image Diffusion

*Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT) has just landed in real‑world benchmarks. In this post we walk through the experiments that show BOFT beating everything else while keeping the same simple “keep the backbone frozen” philosophy. We’ll also peek under the hood to see why replacing a block‑diagonal orthogonal matrix with a butterfly factorization matters.*

---

## 1. Quick recap – what is BOFT?

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) keeps a large foundation model intact and learns a single orthogonal matrix **R** that is multiplied with each frozen weight tensor:
\[
\mathbf{W}^{\text{finetune}} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^{0}\; .
\]
The main appeal of OFT is that the orthogonality preserves the geometry (“pair‑wise angles”) of the frozen neurons, so the pretrained knowledge stays put. The downside? A dense orthogonal matrix of size \(d \times d\) already costs \(d(d-1)/2\) trainable parameters, which becomes prohibitive for very large layers.

BOFT solves this by **factorising** \(\mathbf{R}\) into a product of lightweight *butterfly* matrices:
\[
\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{B}_m\mathbf{B}_{m-1}\dots\mathbf{B}_1,\qquad
\mathbf{B}_i \text{ is sparse but orthogonal.}
\]
Because each \(\mathbf{B}_i\) uses only \(\mathcal{O}(d)\) non‑zeros, the total number of trainable parameters drops to
\[
\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d\,m \;=\;\mathcal{O}(d\log d) \quad (\text{when } b=2),
\]
while still producing a *dense* orthogonal matrix. This simple change on the parameter budget leads to unsurprisingly different learning dynamics.

## 2. The experimental playground

### 2.1 Vision – ImageNet‑1K with Vision Transformers

| Model | Sparse method | Params finetuned | Top‑1 Accuracy |
|-------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| ViT‑Base | LoRA (rank 8) | 0.12 M | 78.5 % |
| ViT‑Base | OFT (block size = 16) | 0.20 M | 78.9 % |
| ViT‑Base | BOFT (m=6, b=4) | 0.28 M | **80.3 %** |

> *Key take‑away:* BOFT not only reaches higher accuracy but does so with **only ~40 % more parameters than OFT**, despite the larger block size. The rough topology of a butterfly gives BOFT more pathways for information flow, which the empirical curve confirms in Fig. 1 of the paper.

### 2.2 Language – GPT‑2‑XL on GLUE

| Task | Method | Params | Accuracy |
|------|--------|--------|----------|
| MNLI | PARALLEL LoRA | 0.30 M | 85.1 % |
| QNLI | OFT (block = 32) | 0.25 M | 88.4 % |
| QNLI | BOFT (m=4, b=8) | 0.35 M | **89.7 %** |

The GPT‑2‑XL backbone contains many large linear layers; BOFT’s butterfly factorization effortlessly shares the same sparse building block across all of them, yielding a **global consistency** that OFT misses.

### 2.3 Generative – Stable Diffusion “ControlNet”

BOFT operates layer‑wise on the U‑Net backbone (≈850 M parameters). Previous attempts at OFT required 60 M trainable parameters to get a good halftone‑control model. With BOFT:

| Setup | Trainable | Samples per epoch | FID @ 256 |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| OFT (block = 64) | 15 M | 1M | 15.2 |
| BOFT (m=8, b=2) | 10 M | 1M | **13.8** |

Even with fewer trainable parameters, BOFT improves perceptual fidelity.

## 3. What changes *under the hood*?

### 3.1 From a single orthogonal matrix to a chain

*OFT* simply learns \(\mathbf{R}\). *BOFT* learns a sequence \(\{\mathbf{B}_i\}\).  
During each forward pass, the residual matrix is *re‑computed* as
\[
\tilde{\mathbf{W}} = (\mathbf{B}_m\cdots\mathbf{B}_1)\mathbf{W}^{0}\; .
\]
Because each \(\mathbf{B}_i\) is only \(\mathcal{O}(d)\) dense, the extra computational burden (the matrix‑vector multiplications) is modest, yet the **parameter space** is vastly richer. The product of two butterfly matrices can realize couplings that a single block‑diagonal \(\mathbf{R}\) cannot.

### 3.2 More expressive → stricter regularisation

A butterfly factorization implicitly encourages *structured* correlations: each butterfly layer exchanges information between paired coordinates (e.g., in a \(2\times2\) block). This is reminiscent of classic fast transforms – a well‑Laplacian‑like inductive bias – that tends to resist over‑fitting. In contrast, the block‑diagonal version changes each neuron group independently, yielding a more exploded search space that can over‑adapt and hurt generalisation.

You can think of the difference as **“global versus local”**:

| Feature | OFT (block‑diag) | BOFT (butterfly) |
|---------|------------------|------------------|
| *Locality* | Independent per block | Interleaved by hops |
| *Connectivity* | Restrict to \(b\) blocks | Full \(d\times d\) reachable |
| *Inductive bias* | Hard to align to FFT etc. | Naturally captures grid‑based mixing |

### 3.3 Layer‑shared butterfly components

BOFT’s factorisation is *layer‑shared*: the same set of \(\mathbf{B}_i\) is applied to every linear layer in the model. This reduces the *parameter churning* between layers and makes the optimisation surface smoother—a fact that the authors observed in a “weight interpolation” experiment. In practice you end up with a handful of \(\mathbf{B}\) matrices stored once, rather than dozens of dense \(\mathbf{R}\)s.

### 3.4 Dropout‑friendly

Because BOFT’s updates are **multiplicative**, a classic dropout layer can’t be naively inserted. The authors therefore propose a *multiplicative dropout* that turns a subset of butterfly blocks into identities at each step – effectively “re‑freezing” portions of the matrix. This neatly regularises the butterfly chain without changing the overall orthogonality (thanks to the block‑diagonal nature of each \(\mathbf{B}_i\)).

## 4. Key numbers that convince

| Benchmark | FO/common metric | BOFT | Δ |
|-----------|----------------- |------|---|
| ViT‑Base  | 78.9 % (OFT) | **80.3 %** | +1.4 % |
| GPT‑2‑XL  | 88.4 % (OFT) | **89.7 %** | +1.3 % |
| Stable Diffusion | 15.2 FID | **13.8** | –1.4 |
| Training time | 30 min/epoch (OFT) | 35 min/epoch (BOFT) | +17% |
| Params | 0.20 M | 0.28 M (ViT) | +40% |

> **Interpretation**: The extra few percent gains are extremely valuable in every application field. For vision, a 1.4 % bump translates to ~300k extra top‑1 correct labels on ImageNet. For generative art, a 1‑point improvement in FID can mean perceptually clearer, less blurry outputs.

## 5. Take‑away for practitioners

| Decision | BOFT? | Why? |
|----------|-------|------|
| **You need a small parameter budget** | Yes | Parameter count ≈ \(d\log d\) versus \(d^2\). |
| **Training time is not a blocker** | Yes | Slight runtime overhead, but negligible for fine‑tuning. |
| **You want a general‑purpose adapter** | Yes | Works on vision, language, diffusion – any linear layer. |
| **You care about reproducible mitigation of over‑fitting** | Yes | The structured bias of butterfly factors regularises more aggressively. |
| **You prefer to keep the exact pretrained weights** | Yes | BOFT initiates all butterflies as identity; the model starts exactly as before. |

---

## 6. Outlook

The paper’s *information‑transmission framework* still leaves room for optimisation. Butterfly factorisation is one network topology that achieves dense connectivity with \(\mathcal{O}(d\log d)\) edges. Other network designs from computers‑networks research (e.g. randomised expanders) might push the parameter bound even lower without sacrificing expressivity.

Furthermore, modern GPU kernels can optimise the butterfly mat‑vec product to reduce the runtime penalty. Once that is done, BOFT will become a *drop‑in replacement* for any parameter‑efficient finetuning pipeline.

---

### Final word

BOFT brings a thoughtful marriage between **geometry** (keeping the pretrained knowledge frozen) and **combinatorial design** (butterfly factorisation). It retains the elegance of OFT while dramatically shrinking the parameter footprint, and this translates cleanly into tangible performance boosts across a range of vision, language, and generative tasks. For anyone doing large‑scale foundation‑model adaptation, BOFT is now a go‑to technique to keep your model small, fast, and sharp.
