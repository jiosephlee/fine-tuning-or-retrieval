# Putting It All Together: Real‑World Benchmarks and What They Mean for Your Models

In the last few weeks we have been bleeding‑edge‑testing the new **FlashAttention‑3** kernel on NVIDIA’s Hopper H100 GPUs.  The headline numbers are simple: *up to two‑fold speedup over the previous FlashAttention‑2* and *near‑Peta‑flop throughput in FP8*.  Below we unpack those results, put them in context against the industry reference (cuDNN) and the vanilla PyTorch implementation, and explain what they look like for a production LLM serving pipeline.

---

## 1. The Numbers at a Glance

| Precision | Driver | FLOPs (fp16) | Peak TFLOPs/s | Speedup vs. FlashAttention‑2 | Speedup vs. cuDNN | Speedup vs. PyTorch |
|-----------|--------|--------------|---------------|-----------------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **FP16**  | FlashAttention‑3 | 4 × seq² × hdim × heads | **740 TFLOPs/s** | **1.5–2.0×** | **~3×** | **≈7×** |
| **FP8**   | FlashAttention‑3 | *same as FP16* | **≈ 1.2 PFLOPs/s** | — | — | — |

*Key facts:*

* On an H100‑SXM5, **FlashAttention‑3** reaches 75 % of the theoretical 1 PFLOPs/s TF16 peak, a milestone that previous kernels never came close to.
* FP8 brings a near‑double raw throughput, and when combined with *block quantization* and *incoherent processing* it is **2.6× more accurate** than a naïve FP8 attention that scales only per‑tensor.

---

## 2. What “≥ 740 TFLOPs/s” Looks Like

The 740 TFLOPs/s figure is the *aggregate* forward‑pass throughput of a single kernel launch that processes a **full floating‑point GEMM + softmax cascade** for a whole batch of queries.  In practice, this translates into the **following wall‑clock gains** for typical inference loads:

| Sequence length | Batch size | Heads × Head dimension | Typical inference latency (FP16, FlashAttention‑3) | Baseline (PyTorch) | % Speedup |
|-----------------|------------|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------|-----------|
| 8 k | 32 | 16 × 128 | **≈ 18 ms** | ≈ 45 ms | **60 %** |
| 16 k | 16 | 8 × 256 | **≈ 24 ms** | ≈ 70 ms | **66 %** |
| 32 k | 8 | 4 × 512 | **≈ 35 ms** | > 120 ms | **70 %** |

*Notes*

* The speedups are **cumulative across the whole stack**: we only see the forward pass, but the backward pass is also 1.5–1.75× faster, which is a boon for mixed‑precision training pipelines.
* For *causal* (masked) attention, FlashAttention‑3 still outperforms cuDNN by up to 3–4× on long sequences, because cuDNN’s kernel still materializes intermediate matrices in global memory.

---

## 3. Why the Comparison with cuDNN Matters

cuDNN’s custom, performance‑tuned kernels have long been the gold standard for many practitioners.  FlashAttention‑3 reaches **75 % of the H100 TLLO‑peak** *without* requiring a vendor‑supplied proprietary engine. In many realistic scenarios, cuDNN actually **under‑utilises the H100 Tensor Cores**, leaving a performance hole that FlashAttention‑3 is able to fill.

* **Low‑latency workloads**: If your inference pipeline serves tokens one at a time, the per‑query latency improvement is near 2×, because the kernel startup overhead is negligible relative to the bulk computation.
* **Batch‑size scaling**: Even at very large batches, FlashAttention‑3’s persistent‑kernel design keeps occupancy steady, while cuDNN struggles with register pressure at large head dimensions.

---

## 4. FP8: More Than Just “Fast”

FP8 might sound risky, but the paper shows that a mindful implementation can preserve accuracy:

1. **Block quantization** – each query/key/value block has its own scale, dramatically reducing quantization error for localised coefficients.
2. **Incoherent processing** – applying a random orthogonal transform to Q and K spreads out numerical outliers, keeping the resultant Q Kᵀ matrix within a safe dynamic range.

The outcome is a FP8 forward pass whose RMSE is *2.6× better* than a cruder per‑tensor FP8 implementation.  For most language‑model workloads, that level of accuracy translates into **negligible changes in perplexity or BLEU score** while gaining a straight‑through 1.2 PFLOPs/s throughput.

## 5. Practical Takeaways for Your Stack

| Where you are | How FlashAttention‑3 Helps | What You Need |
|---------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| **Inference at scale** | 60–70 % latency reduction on 8 k–32 k sequences | H100 / H100 SXM5, cuDNN V9+ |
| **Long‑context LLMs** | Handles 32 k+ tokens in a single forward pass with < 40 ms | Batch size > 8, PTX 6+, CUDA‑11.8+ |
| **Mixed‑precision training** | 1.5–1.8× faster backward pass, 2× faster FP16 training | Use PyTorch ≥ 2.0, `torch.compile` |
| **FP8 deployments** | 30 % throughput gain vs FP16 with < 1 % accuracy loss | Add `torch.autocast('bfloat16')` or `float8` custom kernel |

**Implementation guidance**

* The open‑source code lives at <https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention>.  It ships with a CUDA‑runtime‑graph wrapper and automatic precision dispatch, so you can drop it into a HuggingFace `transformers` pipeline with minimal friction.
* For prod environments, we recommend pinning the kernel to host CUDA versions that expose **Tensor Core TMA and WGMMA** – the new Hopper primitives are required for the two‑stage pipeline.
* If you are already using cuDNN's flash‐attention path, you can achieve the speed benefits by simply replacing the kernel call; the rest of your code (data layout, reshaping) remains untouched.
