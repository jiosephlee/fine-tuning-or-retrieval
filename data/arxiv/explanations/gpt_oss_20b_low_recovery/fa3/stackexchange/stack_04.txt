\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What is block quantization and how does it reduce FP8 numerical error?
Question:
The paper mentions block quantization as keeping one scalar per block (e.g., per B_r × d block). I’d like to understand how this works in practice. Does each block get its own scale factor that is applied during the dot‑product, and how is this incorporated into the softmax computation? Also, how does this interact with the scaling used for Q, K, V during quantization—do we apply the block scale to the quantized values or to the outputs?
Answer:
**Block quantization** in *FlashAttention‑3* is the practice of keeping a *single* scaling scalar for each **block** of the input tensors (Q, K, V) instead of one scalar for the whole tensor.  
A block is the natural unit of computation in FlashAttention: during the forward‑pass the kernel loads a tile of the query matrix \(Q_i\) (size \(B_r\times d\)) and a tile of the key/value matrices (size \(B_c\times d\)).  Thus every block that is read from HBM already fits in a small on‑chip buffer; it is therefore cheap to associate a separate scale with each block.

---

### How the scale is used

1. **Quantization step**  
   For a block \(\mathbf{X}\) (say a \(B_r\times d\) chunk of \(\mathbf{Q}\)) we choose a scalar \(s_{\mathbf{X}}\) (often the maximum absolute value in the block or a percentile).  
   The block is quantized to FP8 as  
   \[
      \mathbf{X}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}= \operatorname{round}\!\bigl(\mathbf{X}/s_{\mathbf{X}}\bigr),
   \]
   and the scale \(s_{\mathbf{X}}\) is stored once for that block.  
   The same procedure is applied independently to every block of \(\mathbf{K}\) and \(\mathbf{V}\).

2. **GEMM‑step**  
   In the tensor‑core WGMMA the operands are still the *quantized* blocks \(\mathbf{Q}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}\) and \(\mathbf{K}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}\).  
   When two blocks are multiplied, the implicit scales multiply as well:
   \[
      \mathbf{S}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}
        = \mathbf{Q}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}\mathbf{K}^{(\mathrm{FP8})\,T}
        \;\; \Longrightarrow \;\;
      \mathbf{S}
        = s_{\mathbf{Q}}\; s_{\mathbf{K}}\;\mathbf{S}^{(\mathrm{FP8})}.
   \]
   Rather than performing a separate scaling operation after the WGMMA, *FlashAttention‑3* folds the product \(s_{\mathbf{Q}}\;s_{\mathbf{K}}\) into the subsequent softmax.  
   The accumulated scalar is compared with the row‑wise maximum used in the softmax normalisation, and the final matrix \(P\) is obtained exactly as if the blocks had been multiplied in full precision.

3. **Softmax and rescaling**  
   The softmax routine in FlashAttention is *local*—each block of scores \(\mathbf{S}\) is normalised independently.  
   Because the scale product \(s_{\mathbf{Q}}\,s_{\mathbf{K}}\) is just a single scalar per block, the softmax implementation can treat it like any other scalar multiplier:
   \[
      \tilde{\mathbf{P}} = \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\mathbf{S}-m}{s_{\mathbf{Q}}\;s_{\mathbf{K}}}\Bigr)
   \]
   where \(m\) is the row‑wise maximum of \(\mathbf{S}\).  
   After this, the block‑wise product \(s_{\mathbf{Q}}\;s_{\mathbf{K}}\) is incorporated into the *numerical* values of \(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}\) that are used to weight the value matrix in the second GEMM.  No extra multiply of the output is required because the subsequent WGMMA already interprets the quantised scores as if they were scaled.

---

### Interaction with Q/K/V scaling

The usual per‑tensor scaling that was used in earlier FP8 attention pipelines scales the entire tensor \(\mathbf{Q}\), \(\mathbf{K}\), or \(\mathbf{V}\) with a single scalar.  Block quantization replaces this with **many** scalars (one per block), so the *quantised* Q, K, V contain block‑wise normalised entries.  In the GEMM the FP8 Tensor Core produces an *FP32 accumulator* that holds the sum of products of quantised values; the block scales are multiplied into that accumulator *before* the softmax step, thus preserving the correct magnitude of each score.

Because each block can be scaled independently, outliers that appear in only a few positions no longer dominate the global scale.  The “incoherent processing” trick (multiplying Q and K by a random orthogonal matrix before quantisation) further spreads such outliers, making the per‑block scales more representative of the typical magnitude within that block.  Together, block quantisation + incoherent processing cut the FP8 numerical error by about \(2.6\times\) relative to a naive per‑tensor scaling strategy.

---

### Practical take‑away

* **One scalar per block** → residual error scales with the *dynamic range* of the block, not the whole tensor.  
* **Scales are fused into the softmax** → no extra multiplies on the RHS of the attention formula.  
* The FP8 Tensor Cores still operate on 8‑bit operands, but the FP32 accumulator automatically receives the correct scaling thanks to the per‑block factors.  
* Because FlashAttention‑3 already processes tensors block‑wise, the implementation incurs virtually zero extra cost to keep the scales in registers or shared memory.

In short, block quantisation turns the expensive “global‑scale” quantisation problem into a cheap **localized‑scale** one, and this local scaling is seamlessly incorporated into the block‑wise softmax and GEMM operations that FlashAttention‑3 already performs.
