\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision"}

### What is the effect of causal masking on the FP8 kernel performance?
Question:
The results mention that for head dimensions 128 and 256, FP8 is at par or behind with causal masking. Why does causal masking reduce the performance benefits of FP8? Does it change the memory access pattern or the depth of the pipeline, and how might the algorithm be modified to better handle causal masks in low‑precision?
Answer:
**Why causal masking hurts the FP8 variant**

1. **Asynchrony is harder to hide** – In the FP8 kernel the main source of slowdown is the tight
   overlap between the two GEMMs (the *“GEMM–softmax”* pipeline described in the
   paper).  When a causal mask is present the second GEMM
   (the *P V* multiply) can only consume the upper‑triangular part of the
   score matrix.  The GPU has to compute a *partial* matrix‑multiply:
   many warps are idle while the masked elements would have otherwise been
   produced.  Because the producer‑consumer warp‑specialization relies on a
   constant‑size cascade of shared‑memory stages, such idle warps force the
   latch between stages to stall, breaking the perfect ping‑pong
   synchronisation that gives FP8 its throughput advantage.  With FP16
   (where the arithmetic intensity is lower) the extra idle cycles are
   less problematic; with FP8 the asymmetry between the 2 × faster GEMM
   throughput and the 1× softmax throughput is amplified, so the pipeline
   becomes more latency‑bound.

2. **Memory‑layout penalties** – For causal attention the algorithm needs the
   *row‑major* form of `K` and the *column‑major* form of `V` in the
   second GEMM.  With FP8 the authors already perform an *in‑kernel*
   transpose of `V` (see Section 4.3 – “in‑kernel transpose” using
   `STSM`/`LDSM`).  Adding a causal mask forces the kernel to load only
   the triangular part of `V` and to write the output only up to the
   diagonal.  That means every warp has to scatter its results,
   destroying the nice tile‑contiguous writes that allow the Tensor
   Cores to fire at full speed.  Consequently the effective FLOP
   density drops, the TMA overlap is less effective, and the kernel
   spends more time in the out‑of‑order memory subsystem.

3. **Pipeline depth disappears** – The 2‑stage GEMM‑softmax pipeline
   (Algorithm 3) assumes that the first GEMM of iteration `j+1`
   finishes before the softmax of iteration `j` begins.  With causal
   masking the *softmax* itself can no longer be strictly overlapped
   with the second GEMM because the mask changes the reduction pattern:
   each row now depends on all earlier columns, so the reduction must be
   performed serially over the triangular block.  As a result the
   “wind‑up” stage of the pipeline is no longer fully overlapped
   and the Tensor Cores are idle for a significant fraction of the
   time.

In short, causal masking turns the perfect, dense, square‑blocks of
attention into sparse, triangular blocks.  That destroys the
high arithmetic‑intensity, fully‑tile‑aligned memory flow that the FP8
Tensor Cores rely on, and the asynchrony that the warp‑specialised
producer‑consumer design provides.

---

**How to mitigate the effect**

1. **Triangular GEMM harness**  
   Use a custom triangular‑matrix‑multiply primitive that keeps
   the work largely dense from the perspective of the Tensor Cores
   (e.g. perform a *group‑wise* GEMM that only loads the non‑masked
   columns of `V` and updates the rows in a streaming fashion).  By
   keeping the data flow tile‑aligned, BLAS‑style convolutions can
   still be used and the FP8 pipeline remains largely overlapped.

2. **Mask‑aware softmax**  
   Fuse the mask into the softmax reduction itself.  In the forward
   pass the softmax can be rewritten as  

   \[
   \tilde P_{ij} = 
   \begin{cases}
     \exp(S_{ij} - m_i) & j \le i \\
     0 & j > i
   \end{cases}
   \]

   If the exponential is computed *only* for the triangular part
   (by a conditional within the warp), the reduction can still be
   performed in a single warp‑level operation, keeping the
   softmax latency low and the *synchronisation* between the two
   GEMMs tight.

3. **Pre‑masked data layout**  
   Store `K` and `V` in a “packed” triangular format in global memory
   (only the lower‑triangular half).  The TMA can then load exactly
   the required sub‑tiles with a single copy, eliminating the need to
   scatter writes back to a full square block.  This keeps the
   occupancy high and the shared‐memory traffic minimal.

4. **Starving the pipeline deliberately**  
   When the matrix is very sparse, the authors’ baseline technique
   “**incoherent processing**” (random orthogonal scrambling) can
   be extended to the causal case: scramble the rows of `Q` and the
   columns of `K` before masking.  This spreads the non‑zero
   entries across the block, allowing the kernel to maintain a
   more balanced workload even with a mask.  Once the block is
   multiplied, apply the inverse scramble to restore the order.
   The extra LCSC multiply is inexpensive (≈ O(d log d)) and does not
   affect the FP8 performance substantially.

5. **Use a two‑kernel fusion** – split the full‑attention pass into
   two sub‑kernels: (i) a “score‑generation” kernel that forms the
   triangular matrix in FP8, and (ii) a call‑time “softmax‑mul”
   kernel that only consumes the valid triangle.  This allows the
   *score‑generation* kernel to exploit the full FP8 pipelines,
   while the *softmax‑mul* kernel, being much smaller, can be
   implemented with a higher‑level BLAS routine that automatically
   handles the irregular shapes.

---

**Take‑away**

Causal masking breaks the dense, square‑block structure that the
FP8 FlashAttention‑3 algorithm is tuned for.  The resulting loss of
arithmetic intensity, increased branching, and inability to keep the
Tensor Cores in a tight producer–consumer pipeline is why the FP8
kernel falls behind FP16 for head dimensions 128 and 256.  By
introducing mask‑aware triangular GEMMs, fusing the mask into
softmax, or reshaping the data layout to preserve tile‑aligned
streams, one can largely recover the pipeline overlap and regain the
performance advantage that FP8 offers on Hopper/H100 GPUs.
