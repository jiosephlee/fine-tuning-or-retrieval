\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

Chapter 12: # 12 Open Problems and Future Directions  

The Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) framework has shown that constraining neuron directions via layer‑shared orthogonal transformations can produce stable, sample‑efficient finetuning for text‑to‑image diffusion models. Nevertheless, several open questions remain that limit the method’s scalability, expressivity, and applicability across modalities.  In this chapter we discuss five promising research directions that will help researchers and practitioners unlock the full potential of OFT.  

---

## 12.1 Scaling Orthogonal Parametrisation to Very Large Layers  

A core ingredient of OFT is the explicit enforcement of orthogonality for each layer’s transformation matrix.  The Cayley parametrisation  

\[
\mathbf{R} = (\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{Q})(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q})^{-1},
\]

requires inversion of the matrix \(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q}\) for every forward and backward pass.  While the block‑diagonal adaptation reduces the size of the matrices that must be inverted, the cost still grows quadratically with the block size, and the inverse itself is a bottleneck when the dimensionality \(d\) is in the tens or hundreds of thousands (as is typical for the attention weight matrices in large diffusion models).  

**Potential solutions**  
1. **Iterative Solvers** – Replace the exact inverse with a few iterations of a Krylov subspace method (e.g. Conjugate Gradient) that can be differentiated.  Since \(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q}\) is close to the identity for small increments in \(\mathbf{Q}\), low‑accuracy solutions may suffice.  
2. **Low‑Rank Perturbation** – Exploit the fact that \(\mathbf{Q}\) is usually sparse or low‑rank; use the Woodbury matrix identity to avoid a full inverse.  
3. **Spectral Decomposition** – For block‑diagonal \(\mathbf{R}\), pre‑compute an eigen‑basis and update only the diagonal entries, thereby transforming the inversion into element‑wise operations.  
4. **Mixed‑Precision and Block‑wise Synchronisation** – Perform the inversion asynchronously on GPU Tensor Cores with 16‑bit precision, synchronising only when a significant change in \(\mathbf{Q}\) occurs.  

A systematic study comparing these approximations in terms of numerical stability, training time, and downstream generative quality would lay the foundation for practical, large‑scale OFT deployments.

---

## 12.2 Compositionality of Multiple Orthogonal Transforms across Tasks  

One of OFT’s appealing properties is that the product of orthogonal matrices is itself orthogonal:

\[
\mathbf{R}_2 \mathbf{R}_1 \in \mathbb{O}(d) \quad \text{if}\quad \mathbf{R}_1,\,\mathbf{R}_2 \in \mathbb{O}(d).
\]

Thus, if we finetune a diffusion model for several distinct downstream tasks—with each task assigning its own orthogonal transform \(\mathbf{R}^{(k)}\)—we can compose them to obtain a single transform that preserves all tasks simultaneously.  This compositionality opens the door to *continual learning* and *multitask* scenarios.

**Key challenges**  
- **Inter‑task interference**: Does the composition of transforms retain the fidelity of each individual task, or does it orthogonalise too far away from the pretrained weights?  
- **Resource sharing**: How many tasks can reasonable share a single set of orthogonal matrices before performance degrades?  
- **Transferability**: Are there optimal strategies for ordering or grouping tasks to minimise cumulative drift?  

**Experimental avenues**  
- Benchmark a curriculum of tasks such as subject‑driven generation, edge‑to‑image, and pose‑to‑face, training orthogonal updates sequentially and evaluating each task’s recall after composition.  
- Investigate group‑wise or hierarchical orthogonal transforms where \(\mathbf{R}^{(k)}\) acts only on subspaces dedicated to specific tasks.  

Successful demonstration of stable compositionality would transform OFT into a universal adapter capable of real‑time, multi‑Task image synthesis without re‑optimization.

---

## 12.3 Relaxing Block‑Diagonal Bias While Keeping Parameter Budgets Low  

Block‑diagonal orthogonalisation offers pragmatic parameter savings by decoupling the transformation across groups of neurons.  However, this structure imposes an implicit bias: neurons inside a block share the same rotation, which may limit expressivity in layers where cross‑block interactions are critical (e.g., multi‑head attention, depthwise‑separable convolutions).

**Alternatives to explore**  
1. **Low‑Rank Plus Structured Sparsity** – Represent \(\mathbf{R}\) as a sum of a low‑rank matrix and a sparse grid, combining the benefits of both orthogonality and parameter efficiency.  
2. **Anchored Rotations** – Introduce a set of “anchor” vectors that enforce partial orthogonality while allowing free rotation in the orthogonal complement.  
3. **Learned Subspace Partitioning** – Train a small auxiliary network that learns to partition neurons into blocks that minimise a cross‑block mutual coherence loss.  

A controlled ablation study examining the trade‑off between block size, parameter count, and downstream fidelity would provide practical guidelines for deploying OFT in resource‑constrained environments.

---

## 12.4 Exploring Other Invariant Measures for Finetuning  

Hyperspherical energy captures the pairwise angular relationships among neurons, but it is only one of many structural invariants that could be leveraged to preserve or adapt a pre‑trained model.  Extending OFT to incorporate richer invariants may improve semantic fidelity and stability.

**Candidate invariants**  
- **Mutual Information** between neuron activations and output classes (or prompts) – could maintain representational diversity.  
- **Spectral Properties** of layer matrices, e.g. preserving the spectrum of singular values ensures that the model’s expressiveness is retained.  
- **Graph‑Based Embeddings** of neuron connectivity patterns, preserving higher‑order relationships.  

**Methodological sketches**  
- Add a regulariser \(\mathcal{L}_{MI} = \mathbb{E}_{x}\big[ \text{MI}(f(x); y) \big]\) that penalises drift in mutual information between hidden activations and target labels or prompts.  
- Impose a spectral Clipping constraint: \(\|\Sigma(\mathbf{W}) - \Sigma(\mathbf{W}^0)\|_F \le \delta\) where \(\Sigma(\mathbf{W})\) denotes the singular value spectrum.  
- Use a graph Laplacian penalty to keep the adjacency structures of neuron weights approximately invariant.

Systematic comparison of these invariants with hyperspherical energy would clarify whether they provide complementary benefits, or whether hyperspherical energy alone suffices for a wide range of tasks.

---

## 12.5 Extending OFT to Other Modalities  

While the current work focuses on text‑to‑image diffusion, the principle of preserving pairwise angular relationships through orthogonal transformations is modality‑agnostic.  This raises the possibility of applying OFT to **audio‑to‑image**, **video diffusion**, or **multimodal alignment** tasks.

**Audio‑to‑Image Diffusion**  
Audio signals can be embedded into a latent speech representation that is then fed into a diffusion model.  OFT could be used to finetune the audio encoders so that pitch or timbre variations correspond more tightly to visual textures without losing the global audio‑visual correspondence learned during pre‑training.

**Video Diffusion**  
Video diffusion models often use spatiotemporal attention mechanisms with large 4‑D kernels.  Orthogonal finetuning could maintain the global coherence of temporal dynamics while adapting the model to specific video styles or thematic constraints.  

**Multimodal Retrieval and Alignment**  
When aligning modalities (e.g., aligning CLIP image embeddings with text embeddings), OFT could be employed to re‑rotate one modality’s embeddings, thereby preserving the original embedding geometry while improving cross‑modal retrieval accuracy.

**Open questions**  
- How does the dimensionality of audio or video feature spaces affect the feasibility of block‑diagonal orthogonal parametrisation?  
- Does preserving hyperspherical energy in multimodal embeddings guarantee improved cross‑modal semantic alignment?  
- What new invariants become relevant when the diffusion model must honour temporal consistency or audio‑visual synchronisation?

Pursuing these extensions would broaden OFT’s applicability into domains where multimodal generation is increasingly demanded.

--- 

In summary, Orthogonal Finetuning has opened a new avenue for controlled diffusion‑based generation, yet its full potential remains untapped.  Addressing scalability, compositionality, parameter bias, richer invariants, and modality generalisation will be the focus of the next wave of research.  As the community tackles these challenges, we can expect diffusion models that are not only more controllable and efficient but also more adaptable across a sweeping variety of tasks and data modalities.
