\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Is GSPO theoretically guaranteed to converge under the same assumptions as PPO?
Question:
PPO’s convergence analysis relies on certain assumptions about the clipping region and the boundedness of advantage estimates. Does GSPO share these assumptions, or does the change from token‑ to sequence‑level weighting alter the proofs? If so, how can we interpret the stability results observed in the paper from a theoretical standpoint?
Answer:
**Short answer**

No, the paper does **not** provide a new convergence proof for GSPO, nor does it claim that GSPO inherits the convergence guarantees of vanilla PPO.  The change from a token‑level to a sequence‑level ratio just modifies the objective; the underlying assumptions that are needed for the classical PPO results (bounded advantage, a well behaved KL divergence, and a clipping‑safe policy update) still have to hold.  In practice, the authors observe empirical stability for the very long‑range, high‑variance regimes that broke GRPO, but that stability is not formally established by the same theoretical arguments that exist for PPO.

---

### 1.  What the PPO theory actually says

The convergence analysis of PPO (see e.g. Schulman et al., 2017) proceeds in two steps:

1. **Trust‑region argument** – The clipped surrogate objective  
   \[
   \min\!\Bigl( r_t(\theta)\,\hat A_t,\;\mathrm{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\,\hat A_t \Bigr)
   \]
   is bounded above by the actual policy‑gradient term and below by a *modified* gradient step that guarantees a non‑negative surrogate improvement provided the KL divergence between the old and new policy stays below a small constant.  
2. **Bounded advantage** – The advantage estimator \(\hat A_t\) must be bounded (or at least \(\mathbb E\!\left[ \hat A_t^2\right]\) finite) so that the variance of the surrogate is controlled.

Because these two properties are satisfied, PPO can be shown to monotonically improve the true reward objective (up to second‑order terms) and to converge to a local optimum of the policy.

---

### 2.  Where GSPO plugs in

GSPO replaces the token‑wise importance ratio \(w_{i,t}\) with a **sequence‑wise** ratio

\[
s_i(\theta)=\Bigl(\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(y_i|x)}\Bigr)^{1/|y_i|}.
\]

The surrogate objective keeps the same clipped‑min form:

\[
\min\!\bigl( s_i(\theta)\,\hat A_i,\;\mathrm{clip}(s_i(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\,\hat A_i \bigr).
\]

Apart from the replacement of \(w_{i,t}\) by \(s_i\), the *structure* of the objective is identical:

* The *clipping* is performed on a scalar ratio (either token‑wise or sequence‑wise).  
* The *advantage* \(\hat A_i\) is still a (normalized) reward signal that is assumed to be bounded or at least square‑integrable.

Consequently, if the assumptions that underpin the PPO analysis are satisfied for the **sequence‑wise** ratio—as they are for the token‑wise ratio—then the same proof skeleton applies.  In particular:

|  | PPO (token‑wise) | GSPO (sequence‑wise) |
|---|------------------|----------------------|
| Ratio | \(w_{i,t} \in (0,\infty)\) | \(s_i \in (0,\infty)\) |
| Clipping range | \([1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\) | \([1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\) |
| Advantage | \(\hat A_t\) | \(\hat A_i\) |
| Boundedness | \(|\hat A_t|\le B\) | \(|\hat A_i|\le B\) |
| KL bound | \(\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_{\theta})\) small | same |

The only new technical point is that the *ratio variance* is different: \(s_i\) aggregates over the whole sequence, while \(w_{i,t}\) aggregates over a single step.  The paper demonstrates empirically that the variance of \(s_i\) is considerably smaller, which is what gives GSPO its stability.  However, from a theoretical standpoint, *no new variance bound* is proven in the manuscript, so the proof that the surrogate is a reliable lower bound on the true objective remains the same as for PPO.

---

### 3.  Why the authors do not claim a new proof

1. **The surrogate is unchanged** – The clipped surrogate obeys the same mathematical properties as before; the ratio is still a positive scalar and the objective is concave in the ratio (up to clipping).  
2. **Variance control is empirical** – Although GSPO empirically exhibits lower variance, the paper does not provide a theorem that upper‑bounds \(\operatorname{Var}[s_i]\) or \(\operatorname{Var}[s_i \hat A_i]\) under realistic MoE or long‑sequence settings.  
3. **Focus on practical issues** – The motivation section emphasises that GRPO’s *mis‑application* of importance sampling (token‑wise) was the source of instability.  GSPO resolves that by aligning the unit of the ratio with the reward unit.  The resulting improvement is a *practical* construction, not a fundamentally new theoretical regime.

Hence, the authors present GSPO as a *practical alternative* that preserves the theoretical machinery of PPO rather than as an algorithm with its own convergence theorem.

---

### 4.  How to view the stability results theoretically

The most common theoretical tool to talk about stability in policy optimisation is the **relative entropy (KL) penalty** argument.  Under bounded advantages, the surrogate maximisation improves the true expected return provided the KL divergence stays below a constant threshold.  Since GSPO uses the same clipping mechanism, it inherits the same guarantee *provided* the sequence‑level KL divergence

\[
\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot|x)\;\Vert\;\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)\right)
\]

remains small for all queries \(x\).  In practice, the authors tune \(\varepsilon\) so that the average clipping fraction is tiny (see Fig. 5).  This empirically ensures that the KL remains in the regime where the surrogate is trustworthy.

Formally, one could state:

> **If**  
> 1. \(s_i(\theta)\) is bounded in \([1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\) for all sampled sequences, and  
> 2. The advantage \(\hat A_i\) is bounded (or its variance finite), then  
> the GSPO objective satisfies the same local improvement bound as PPO and therefore converges to a local optimum under standard stochastic‑gradient assumptions.

The paper does not spell out this theorem, but the reasoning follows directly from the existing PPO proof once the ratio is replaced with \(s_i\).

---

### 5.  Bottom‑line

* GSPO does **not** introduce a new convergence argument; it **re‑uses** the PPO proof structure.  
* The same assumptions—bounded advantages, controlled KL divergence, and clipping—are needed.  
* The empirical stability reported in the paper stems from lower variance of the sequence‑wise importance ratio, which mitigates the accumulation of noise that plagued GRPO, but this is not formalised in a theorem.

So, under the same assumptions that make PPO converge, GSPO would converge as well—provided those assumptions hold in the setting of interest.  That is the theoretical standpoint implicit in the paper.
