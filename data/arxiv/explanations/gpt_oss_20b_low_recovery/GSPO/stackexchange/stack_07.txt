\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

### Is the gradient of GSPO equivalent to that of a simple likelihood ratio policy gradient?
Question:
Equation (8) gives the GSPO gradient as $
abla_\theta J_{\text{GSPO}} = \frac{1}{G}\big(\frac{\theta(y_i|x)}{\theta_{\text{old}}(y_i|x)}\big)^{1/|y_i|}\tilde A_i \frac{1}{|y_i|}
abla_\theta\big(\frac{1}{|y_i|}\big)\frac{\theta(y_{i,t}|x,y_{i,<t})}{\theta(y_{i,t}|x,y_{i,<t})}$. Ignoring clipping, how does this compare to the classic REINFORCE update $
abla_\theta J = \frac{\theta(y_i|x)}{\theta_{\text{old}}(y_i|x)}\tilde A_i 
abla_\theta\theta(y_i|x)$? What role does the $1/|y_i|$ factor play?
Answer:
**Short answer**

The GSPO gradient is a *sequence‑level* REINFORCE update, not a token‑wise one.  
If you drop the clipping and the length‑normalisation, the GSPO gradient reduces to  

\[
\nabla_\theta J_{\text{GSPO}}
   = \mathbb{E}\!\Big[\,\widehat A_i\;
        \frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\!old}}(y_i|x)}\,
        \nabla_\theta\log \pi_\theta(y_i|x)\Big],
\]

which is exactly the vanilla REINFORCE update for a sequence reward.  
The extra factor \(1/|y_i|\) that appears in the paper is **only the length normalisation** applied to the importance ratio \(s_i(\theta)\). It makes the per‑token gradient terms have the same magnitude across responses of different length, avoiding the situation where a long response would dominate the gradient simply because it contains many tokens. Technically it is a way of *average‑over‑tokens* the log‑likelihood gradient, not a change to the REINFORCE algorithm itself.

---

**Detailed explanation**

1. **What GSPO does**

   GSPO replaces the token‑wise importance weights of GRPO by a *single* sequence‑wise importance weight

   \[
   s_i(\theta)=\Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\!old}}(y_i|x)}\Big)^{1/|y_i|},
   \]

   and applies clipping to the whole sequence.  
   Consequently its objective is

   \[
   \mathcal J_{\text{GSPO}}
     =\frac1G\sum_{i=1}^G
        \min\!\bigl(s_i(\theta)\,\widehat A_i,\;
           \text{clip}(s_i(\theta),1-\varepsilon ,1+\varepsilon)\,\widehat A_i\bigr).
   \]

2. **Gradient derivation**

   Ignoring the clipping, differentiate inside the expectation:

   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   \nabla_\theta\mathcal J_{\text{GSPO}}
   &=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[
        \frac1G\sum_{i} s_i(\theta)\,\widehat A_i\,
        \nabla_\theta\log s_i(\theta)
      \Bigg]               \\
   &=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[
        \frac1G\sum_{i}
        \Bigl(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\!old}}(y_i|x)}\Bigr)^{1/|y_i|}
        \widehat A_i\,
        \frac1{|y_i|}
        \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\!\mid\!x,y_{i,<t})
      \Bigg].
   \end{aligned}
   \]

   Using \(\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_i|x)=\sum_{t}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})\) gives

   \[
   \nabla_\theta\mathcal J_{\text{GSPO}}
   =\mathbb{E}\Bigg[
      \widehat A_i\;
      \frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_{\!old}}(y_i|x)}\;
      \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_i|x)
    \Bigg].
   \]

   This is *exactly* the standard REINFORCE (likelihood‑ratio) estimator for a sequence reward.

3. **Why the \(1/|y_i|\) factor appears**

   Two reasons:

   * **Length normalisation** – The raw likelihood ratio \(\pi_\theta(y_i|x)/\pi_{\theta_{\!old}}(y_i|x)\) scales exponentially with length. Raising it to the power \(1/|y_i|\) keeps the magnitude roughly constant across responses of different lengths, reducing variance and making the clipping interval meaningful for all lengths.
   * **Token‑level gradient balance** – Inside the expectation we divide the sum of token‑level gradients by \(|y_i|\). This turns the sum into an *average* derivative, again preventing long sentences from unduly dominating the update.

   Mathematically, the factor does **not** change the expectation of the gradient (it cancels with the corresponding exponent in the importance ratio). It is purely a *variance‑reduction* trick tied to the length of the response.

4. **Comparison to GRPO**

   In GRPO each token is weighted by its own \(w_{i,t}(\theta)\). Those weights vary across tokens and can introduce high‑variance, biased updates, especially when token likelihoods change dramatically because of expert activation in MoE models. GSPO’s single weight per sequence eliminates this variability and aligns the importance sampling correction with the unit of the reward (the whole sequence).

5. **Practical implication**

   Because the GSPO gradient is a clean REINFORCE estimator, it inherits all the stability properties of on‑policy policy gradients. The length‑normalisation further ensures that the clipping mechanism remains effective across diverse response lengths, which is crucial when training large language models that generate variable‑length outputs.

---

**Bottom line**

The GSPO gradient is *equivalent* to the classic REINFORCE update for sequence rewards; the \(1/|y_i|\) factor is simply a length‑normalisation applied to the importance ratio to control variance and make clipping meaningful across responses of different lengths.
