\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models"}

Chapter 5: # Chapter 5  
**Training DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B**

Mathematical reasoning is not merely a matter of memorizing a large number of facts; it requires a model to internalise patterns, perform algebraic manipulations, keep track of intermediate states, and sometimes invoke external computational resources. In order to best harness these capabilities, the DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B model was started from a carefully chosen foundation—DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B—then exposed to a richly varied training mix that blends raw mathematics, programming, and natural language. This chapter walks through every aspect of the training pipeline: the choice of pre‑training seed, the quantitative breakup of the data, the optimisation schedule, and the rigorous evaluation strategy that verifies the model’s competence on both English‑ and Chinese‑language mathematical tasks. We also describe how the same training regime benefits general reasoning and coding tasks, reinforcing the notion that high‑quality math data percolate into broader capabilities.

---

## Initialization from DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B

DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B is a 7‑billion‑parameter transformer that has already been exposed to more than 600 B tokens of general language and code. Its representation space excels at capturing syntactical dependencies, making it an attractive foundation for a math‑focused model. The authors observed two key signals when comparing a pure language model with that trained on code: the former shows decent performance on MATH once fine‑tuned, while the latter achieve higher accuracy and better alignment during instruction tuning. Consequently, the DeepSeekMath series was **initialized directly from the coder base** rather than a vanilla pre‑trained LLM.

The initialization afforded two advantages:

1. **Syntactic familiarity** – Code tokens abound in mathematical derivations (algorithms, definitions, symbolic expressions). The pre‑trained parameters thus already encode relevant patterns.
2. **Reduced cold‑start risk** – Many downstream tasks, especially those that use external libraries (Python’s `math`, `sympy`, etc.), benefit from a model already comfortable with coding conventions.

Therefore, the DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B training commenced with the exact weights of DeepSeek‑Coder‑Base‑v1.5 7B, providing a strong starting point for the subsequent specialized training stages.

---

## Data Distribution Breakdown

A foundational hypothesis of the project is that a *quality‑over‑quantity* data paradigm can significantly boost math performance. To test this hypothesis, the authors constructed a **multimodal training mix** totaling 500 B tokens, composed of:

| Data Source | Portion of Dataset | Rationale |
|-------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **DeepSeekMath Corpus** | **56 %** (≈ 280 B) | Dedicated mathematical web content mined from Common Crawl, pre‑filtered with a fastText classifier. This is the core of the training regimen. |
| **AlgebraicStack** | **4 %** (≈ 20 B) | A curated tier of StackExchange posts that focus on algebraic manipulation and code snippets. Provides high‑signal algebraic reasoning. |
| **arXiv** | **10 %** (≈ 50 B) | Scientific papers, especially from mathematics and physics. Included to preserve a distribution that covers formal and technical expressions. |
| **GitHub Code** | **20 %** (≈ 100 B) | Diverse code repositories that incorporate symbolic computation, symbolic libraries, and mathematical libraries. Expands the model’s coding fluency. |
| **Common‑Crawl Natural Language (NC)** | **10 %** (≈ 50 B) | Natural language text in English and Chinese to keep the model grounded in general language understanding. |

The selection strategy was **iterative**: after each training epoch, the authors would reassess the coverage of the model on a validation set of math problems. If a certain sub‑genre (e.g., complex analysis) underperformed, they would adjust the data proportion accordingly. This “data‑driven” feedback loop ensured that the final training mix was not arbitrary but **goal‑oriented**.

---

## Learning‑Rate Schedule and Batch Sizing

A stable optimisation schedule is essential when the model ingests such heterogeneous data. The authors adopted a **multi‑step cosine‑annealing** schedule with the following key parameters:

- **Maximum learning rate**: \(4.2\times10^{-4}\)
- **Warm‑up**: 2 000 gradient steps to avoid unstable gradients at the start.
- **Decay**: After 80 % of the training steps the learning rate is reduced to 31.6 % of its peak, then to 10 % of the peak after 90 % of the training. The overall schedule resembles the cosine curve in standard practice but with sharper early decay to accommodate the breadth of the data.
- **Optimizer**: AdamW, with \(\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.95, \mathrm{wd}=0.1\).

Batch scaling was aggressive: **10 M tokens per batch** and a fixed context length of **4 K tokens**. The token‑level batching allowed the CUDA kernel to saturate the GPU memory, while the 4 K context ensured that typical math problem statements (≤ 2 K tokens) plus their solutions fit comfortably. This configuration struck a balance between speed (≈ 6 K tokens per second per GPU) and stability.

## Evaluation Methodology

The evaluation set consisted of **eight benchmarks** that jointly capture a spectrum of mathematical difficulty and linguistic nuance. Crucially, all benchmarks were evaluated *without* the use of external toolkits or any advanced post‑processing mechanisms. For each benchmark, a **few‑shot chain‑of‑thought** prompt was employed: the prompt supplied 4–5 annotated problem–solution pairs that explicitly demonstrated intermediate reasoning steps. By using this format, the authors simulated realistic student‑level misunderstandings while still providing the model with scaffolding.

The benchmarks spanned both **English** and **Chinese**:

| Benchmark | Language | Type (English)/Type (Chinese) |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------------|
| GSM8K | Eng | Grade‑school level numeric reasoning |
| MATH | Eng | Competition‑level college‑math |
| CMATH | Chin | College‑level Chinese math |
| MMLU‑STEM | Eng | Multiple‑choice STEM reasoning |
| Gaokao‑MathCloze | Chin | Cloze‑style high‑school assessment |
| Gaokao‑MathQA | Chin | Multiple‑choice high‑school assessment |
| MMLU (General) | Eng | Broad multi‑choice tasks |
| BBH | Eng | Multi‑step reasoning |

Each benchmark was scored **accuracy**: the fraction of fully correct answers. In addition to the overall accuracy, the authors reported **step‑wise correctness** where applicable to confirm that the model’s chain of thought was internally coherent.

---

## Results: Pure Reasoning Accuracy

The **DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B** outperformed every open‑source 7‑billion‑parameter base model in the base‑model class. In absolute terms, the model’s performance was:

| Benchmark | Accuracy (Base) | Baseline (Mistral 7B / Llama‑2 7B) |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| GSM8K | **82.9 %** | 74–78 % |
| MATH | **36.2 %** | < 30 % |
| CMATH | **84.6 %** | < 80 % |
| MMLU‑STEM | **71.4 %** | 65–68 % |
| Gaokao‑MathCloze | 64.2 % | ~60 % |
| Gaokao‑MathQA | 59.4 % | ~55 % |

Key observations:

1. **Grade‑school vs. competition‑level** – The large jump from GSM8K (≈ 83 %) to MATH (≈ 36 %) illustrates the increasing difficulty of algorithmic reasoning and algebraic manipulation in higher‑level problems.
2. **Cross‑linguistic transfer** – Chinese benchmarks gained relatively more from the training mix (≈ 4–5 % absolute points), confirming that the multilingual nature of the data (especially the 10 % Common‑Crawl NC) allowed the model to generalise better across languages.

---

## Tool‑Integrated vs. Pure Reasoning Performance

When the same 7‑billion‑parameter model was instructed to generate a **Python program** that uses the `math` or `sympy` libraries, the accuracy rose by roughly 6–8 % on the same benchmarks:

| Benchmark | Accuracy (Program‑Of‑Thought) | Gain vs. Pure Reasoning |
|-----------|-------------------------------|-------------------------|
| GSM8K | **88.2 %** | +5.3 % |
| MATH | **42.5 %** | +6.3 % |

The slight confidence gain indicates that the sequence model not only understands how to invoke external computation but can also craft correct program logic, a subtle skill nurtured by the GitHub Code portion of the training mix.

---

## Impact on General Reasoning and Coding Benchmarks

While the primary goal was to enhance mathematics, the training regime incidentally improved the model’s **language understanding** and **programming fluency**:

| Benchmark | Accuracy/Score | Prior Best (7B) | New Score |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|-----------|
| MMLU (General) | 71.2 % | 65 % | +6 % |
| BBH | 56.4 % | 51 % | +5.4 % |
| HumanEval | 43.7 % | 38 % | +5.7 % |
| MBPP | 26.4 % | 24 % | +2.4 % |

Notably, the *cumulative* training—not just the math corpus—influenced the general abilities. However, the math‑heavy data indirectly sharpened reasoning patterns: explicit step‑by‑step explanations improved the model’s ability to follow complex chains of reasoning even in purely linguistic tasks.

## Summary

The **DeepSeekMath‑Base 7B** training pipeline achieves a carefully calibrated balance:

- **Rich, diversified data** (math web, code, natural language) built from *high‑quality* collection and de‑contamination procedures.
- **Robust optimisation** powered by a gentle, staged learning‑rate decay that respects the disparate data modalities.
- **Cross‑linguistic performance** confirming that a truly multilingual training corpus yields bidirectional benefits.
- **Surpassing all open‑source 7B models** on every benchmark, including tool‑integrated reasoning, while also improving general reasoning and coding skillsets.

The insights distilled from this chapter illuminate the pathways to building next‑generation math‑capable language models: *start from a strong code base, feed it a multilingual, curated arithmetic corpus, fine‑tune with a thoughtful schedule, and evaluate in a disciplined, tool‑agnostic manner.*  This foundation will later be leveraged for instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, enabling the DeepSeekMath series to push the state of the art on community benchmarks.
