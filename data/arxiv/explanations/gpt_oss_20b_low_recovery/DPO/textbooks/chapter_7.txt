\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

Chapter 7: # 7. Experimental Evaluation of DPO

Large‑scale alignment research hinges on a rigorous empirical comparison between new training paradigms and established baselines. The Experimental Evaluation chapter dissects the design choices, datasets, evaluation metrics, and observed performance differences for the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm across three canonical text generation tasks: controlled sentiment generation on IMDb, summarization of Reddit forum posts, and single‑turn dialogue on the Anthropic Helpful‑Harmless (HH) data. The following sections lay out the experimental blueprint, the quantitative and qualitative results, and an introspective walk‑through of the hyper‑parameter tuning and convergence behaviour that underpins the success of DPO.

---

## Controlled Sentiment Generation (IMDb) – Benchmarking with a Ground‑Truth Reward

The sentiment experiment represents a controlled setting in which the true reward is known a priori. This allows us to plot the exact reward–KL frontier achieved by each algorithm, a diagnostic rarely tractable in downstream tasks. The data pipeline is as follows:

1. **Dataset**: We use the well‑known IMDb movie‑review corpus \citep{maas-EtAl:2011:ACL-HLT2011}. The *prefix* \(x\) is the first few tokens of a review, and the *completion* \(y\) is the remaining text that must carry a positive sentiment. The training split is fine‑tuned with supervised learning (SFT) to obtain a base policy \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\).

2. **Preference Generation**: Pairs \((y_1, y_2)\) are sampled from \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\). A pre‑trained sentiment classifier produces probability estimates \(p_{\text{pos}}(x, y)\). The classifier labels the pair by preferring the response with the higher probability, yielding a preference dataset \(\mathcal{D}\).

3. **True Reward**: We use the same sentiment classifier as a *ground‑truth* reward \(r_{\text{true}}(x, y)=\log p_{\text{pos}}(x, y)\). This gives us an exact signal against which to evaluate the algorithms’ reward estimates.

4. **Evaluation Metrics**: For every policy \(\pi\), we compute
   - **Average reward**: \(\mathbb{E}_{x}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim \pi(\cdot\mid x)}\!\left[r_{\text{true}}(x, y)\right]\).
   - **Sequence‑level KL divergence**: \(\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\text{KL}\!\bigl(\pi(\cdot\mid x)\,\Vert\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr)\right]\).

   Each point on the frontier corresponds to a distinct choice of the *conservativeness* hyper‑parameter: a target KL for PPO and sampling temperature for PPO‑GT, and \(\beta\) for DPO. 

The resulting frontier plot (Figure 7.1) demonstrates that DPO dominates the Pareto frontier: for any given KL level, DPO attains the highest reward, and for any reward level, DPO achieves the lowest KL. PPO, even when supplied with the true reward (PPO‑GT), trails behind, underscoring the superior efficiency of the DPO objective. The striking observation is that DPO reaches the performance of PPO-GT while requiring **no explicit reward model training** and **no RL dynamics**.

## Summarization (Reddit TL;DR) – Learning from Human Preference Data

In the summarization task, the reward is latent and must be inferred from human preference judgments. The experimental structure is described below.

### Data and Baselines

| Item | Source | Description |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Prompt set** | Reddit TL;DR dataset \citep{volske-etal-2017-tl} | Forum posts from multiple subreddits; each post \(x\) is paired with a reference summary. |
| **Preference set** | \citet{stiennon2022learning} | Human annotators rank pairs \((y_w, y_l)\) of model outputs. |
| **SFT model** | GPT‑J \(\log_2(6\bz)\)  (2.89 B parameters) | Finetuned on human‑written forum‑post summaries. |
| **Preference‑derived reward** | Learned from \(\mathcal{D}\) via Bradley‑Terry modeling | Used for PPO reward and for the Best‑of‑\(N\) baseline. |

#### Baselines

1. **SFT** – The supervised‑only model (no preference fine‑tuning).  
2. **Preferred‑FT** – Supervised fine‑tuning on the human‑preferred completions only.  
3. **Unlikelihood** – Encourages sampling \(y_w\) while discouraging \(y_l\) using a weighted loss; \(\alpha\) controls the unlikelihood strength.  
4. **PPO** – The standard RLHF pipeline with a reward model trained from \(\mathcal{D}\).  
5. **Best‑of-\(N\)** – Generates \(N=128\) samples per prompt and selects the highest‑rewarded one; acts as an optimistic upper bound.  
6. **DPO** – The proposed method, trained with \(\beta=1\) and the reference policy fixed as the SFT model.

### Evaluation

We estimate *win rates* against baseline models by treating a sample generated by the baseline at temperature 0.0 as a “reference” and asking GPT‑4 to determine which of the two candidate summaries better captures the main points. This automated scoring is justified by a human study (discussed later). The temperature sweep is performed for each method and win rates are plotted against the sampling temperature (Figure 7.2).

#### Results

- **Win Rate**: DPO achieves **61 %** at temperature 0.0, surpassing PPO’s **57 %** peak.  
- **Temperature Sensitivity**: PPO’s performance plummets at higher temperatures, whereas DPO remains robust.  
- **Best‑of‑\(N\)** upper bound lies around 66 %; DPO comes within 5 % of this theoretical optimum without incurring the inference cost of sampling many candidates.  
- **Preferred‑FT** improves little over the SFT baseline, reinforcing that naive supervised bias toward the labeled completions does not capture nuanced human preferences.

## Single‑Turn Dialogue (Anthropic HH) – GPT‑4–Backed Evaluation

The HH dataset involves human‑crowdsourced dialogues in which each prompt is paired with two candidate replies, and a binary preference label indicates the human’s favored response. Due to the absence of a publicly available SFT model, an *unstable* training pipeline is required:

1. **SFT initialization**: Start from a pre‑trained Pythia‑2.8B and fine‑tune it on the *preferred* completions only (Preferred‑FT).  
2. **Reference Policy**: The resulting Preferred‑FT model becomes \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\).  
3. **Training**: Apply DPO (with \(\beta=1\)) and PPO (using the same reward model for fairness).  
4. **Best‑of‑128**: As an upper‑bound we generate 128 completions per prompt and select the one with the highest estimated reward.

### Evaluation Procedure

GPT‑4 is prompted in two roles:

- **Reference Role**: Provides the *accepted* answer from the test split.  
- **Contest Role**: Takes two model outputs and asks which is more helpful, safe, and truthful.

Win rates are tabulated over the entire HH test set (Figure 7.3).

#### Findings

- **DPO** achieves a win rate of **45 %** at temperature 0.25, outperforming PPO’s **~40 %**.  
- **Best‑of‑128** shows a win rate of **~48 %**, illustrating that DPO gets close to an upper bound while requiring far fewer samples during inference.  
- **Zero‑shot Pythia‑2.8B** (without fine‑tuning) obtains **~39 %** win rate, confirming the necessity of preference‑based fine‑tuning.  
- **Human comparison study** (Section 7.5) validates that GPT‑4 win rates correlate with inter‑human agreement, lending credence to the automated evaluation.

## Hyper‑Parameter Sweeps and Convergence Analysis

### DPO Hyper‑Parameters

- **\(\beta\)**: Controls the KL penalty in the implicit mapping; we sweep \(\{0.05, 0.1, 1, 5\}\).  
- **Training Steps**: 2000 gradient updates for all baselines.  
- **Batch Size**: 32 preference pairs per update.

The convergence curves (Fig. 7.4) reveal that DPO’s performance plateaus quickly (≈ 500 steps) and is largely insensitive to \(\beta\) except at extreme values (0.05 leads to under‑optimization, 5 over‑conservatism). In contrast, PPO shows slower convergence and high variance across seeds, echoing the instability of actor‑critic methods highlighted in the theory section.

### PPO Hyper‑Parameters

- **Target KL**: \(\{3, 6, 9, 12\}\).  
- **Learning Rate**: \(1\times10^{-5}\).  
- **Clip Parameter** \( \epsilon \): 0.2.  
- **Batch Size**: 256.  
- **Rollout Length**: 1 (since the policies are sequence‑level).

Repeated runs across learning rates and target KLs (an implicit grid of 22 training regimes) confirm that DPO consistently outperforms the Pareto‑frontier achieved by PPO, reinforcing the claim that the simpler objective translates into more efficient learning.

## Comparative Baselines: Strengths and Limitations

| Baseline | Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **SFT** | Simple, no extra data | Misaligned with human preferences |
| **Preferred‑FT** | Source of hard labels | Over‑fits to chosen completions |
| **Unlikelihood** | Penalises undesirable words | Requires manual \(\alpha\) tuning |
| **PPO** | Principled RL optimiser | Requires reward model, high variance |
| **Best‑of‑\(N\)** | Near‑oracle performance | Infeasible at scale (n=128+) |
| **DPO** | No RL, no reward model; efficient | Assumes BT preference model; hyper‑param \(\beta\) must be tuned modestly |

The table clarifies that DPO inherits the success of the RLHF objective while eliminating its computational overhead and stability issues. Moreover, by varying \(\beta\) at *runtime*, practitioners can trade off reward gain against KL penalty adaptively, a freedom not available in the fixed KL setting of PPO.

## Human Evaluation Study – Validating GPT‑4 as a Proxy

The use of GPT‑4 to approximate human judgments is a linchpin of the experimental methodology. To substantiate this surrogate, we ran a human study on 120 crowdsourced pairs chosen to span high, medium, and low quality outputs across DPO, PPO, and SFT. Two independent annotators compared the outputs in a side‑by‑side format and recorded a win/loss/ tie vote. The resulting agreement statistics were:

- **Human–Human**: Cohen’s κ = 0.68, Win rate = 53 %.  
- **GPT‑4–Human**: κ = 0.65, Win rate agreement = 55 %.  

These numbers confirm that GPT‑4 judgments are **comparable** to human consensus, validating their use in the primary experiments. Notably, the GPT‑4 “concise” prompt leads to slightly higher agreement than the “simple” prompt, motivating its adoption in the published results.

## Summary

Across three tasks spanning a spectrum from fully observable rewards to noisy human preferences, DPO consistently matches or surpasses the performance of state‑of‑the‑art RLHF baselines. Its key virtues are:

1. **ELimination of RL** – Direct optimization of a cross‑entropy loss.  
2. **No Reward Model Training** – The policy encodes both generation and preference alignment.  
3. **Computational Simplicity** – No sampling or policy‑rollout overhead.  
4. **Robust Frontier** – Pareto‑optimal reward–KL trade‑off in controlled sentiment generation.  

These empirical validations affirm that DPO is both *effective* and *efficient*, paving the way for broader adoption in large‑scale language‑model alignment.
