\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### Why does DPO need the reference policy \(\pi_{ref}\) and how is it chosen when an SFT model is unavailable?
Question:
The algorithm description says that when an SFT model is not available, \(\pi_{ref}\) is initialized by maximizing the likelihood of the preferred completions: \(\pi_{ref}=\arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w)}\log\pi(y_w|x)\). Could someone elaborate on the intuition behind this choice? How does this relate to the KL‑constraint in the RL objective?
Answer:
**Why DPO needs a reference policy and how it is chosen when SFT is not available**

In DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) the language‑model policy
\(\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\) is trained to satisfy human preference data.  
The objective used in the paper (Eq. (7) in the manuscript) is

\[
\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}})
   =-\,\mathbb E_{(x,y_w,y_\ell)\sim\mathcal D}
      \Bigl[ \log \sigma\!\Bigl(
         \beta\!\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}
       -\beta\!\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_\ell\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_\ell\mid x)}
      \Bigr)\Bigr].
\]

The loss depends explicitly on a **reference policy** \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\).  
This reference plays two, closely related roles:

| Role | Intuition | Technical relation |
|------|-----------|--------------------|
| **KL‑constraint** | Prevents the fine‑tuned policy from drifting too far from a safe, well‑behaved baseline (the policy from which the reward model is accurate). | In the underlying RL objective (Eq. (6)), \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) is the policy that appears in the KL term \(\mathbb D_{\text{KL}}\bigl[\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\bigr]\).  The “soft‑policy” form of the optimal solution (Eq. (5)) shows that \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) is the prior that is tempered by the reward. |
| **Log‑ratio normalisation** | The ratio \(\pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{ref}}\) turns the reward \(r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\) into a *difference* that cancels the unknown partition function \(Z(x)\) when plugging into the Bradley‑Terry model (Eq. (8)). | When the reward is re‑parameterised as \(r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\), the additive constant \(\beta\log Z(x)\) disappears from the preference probability because only reward differences matter.  Hence the objective can be written purely in terms of the policy ratios without needing to evaluate \(Z(x)\). |

---

### What happens when there is no SFT model?

In the standard RLHF pipeline the reference policy is the supervised‑fine‑tuned (SFT) model
\(\pi^{\text{SFT}}\).
If the task has never been SFT‑trained, we still need a reference distribution
to anchor the optimisation and to provide the sampling distribution for the
preference data.  
The paper proposes a pragmatic solution: **train \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) to maximize the likelihood of the *preferred* completions in the preference dataset alone**

\[
\pi_{\text{ref}} \;=\;\arg\max_{\pi}\;
    \mathbb E_{(x,y_w)\sim\mathcal D}\bigl[\,\log \pi(y_w\mid x)\,\bigr].
\]

Why is this a sensible choice?

1. **Distribution‑shift mitigation.**  
   The preference dataset is constructed by first generating pairs of completions
   from the *reference* policy (or an SFT policy).  
   When the true reference is unknown, the most faithful approximation we can
   obtain is a policy that assigns high probability mass to the responses
   labelled as *preferred*.  Maximising the likelihood of these responses
   approximates the distribution that the human labelers are comparing
   against, thereby reducing the mismatch between the policy that generated the
   data and the policy we will optimise later.

2. **Implicit reward alignment.**  
   By focusing on preferred completions, we give the new reference an
   “optimistic” bias: it already prefers the kinds of outputs that humans
   like.  In the DPO objective the log‑ratio \(\log\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}\)
   rewards the fine‑tuned policy only when it improves upon the
   reference's preference for a given prompt.  If the reference already
   favors good completions, the KL penalty will be smaller for reasonable
   improvements, encouraging the algorithm to learn subtle, human‑aligned
   refinements rather than drastic, unsafe changes.

3. **Avoiding variance from sampling a generic policy.**  
   A vanilla language model that has not been conditioned on the task tends
   to produce very low‑probability tokens, making the log‑ratio large and
   highly variable.  Optimising a policy on preferred data keeps the
   reference in a plausible region of the outcome space, stabilising the
   gradient signal.

### Connection to the KL‑constraint in the RL objective

The RL objective that DPO implicitly optimises is

\[
\max_{\pi_\theta}\;
  \mathbb E_{x, y\sim \pi_\theta}\bigl[\,r^*(x,y)\bigr]-\beta\, 
  \mathbb D_{\text{KL}}\!\bigl[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr].
\]

The optimal policy for a given reward \(r\) under this constraint is exactly
the *soft‑policy* form in Eq. (5).  
When we re‑parameterise the reward as
\(r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}\),
the KL term disappears from the soft‑policy expression, leaving only the
log‑ratio that appears in the DPO loss.  Hence, DPO solves for the policy that
would be obtained by RL with a KL‑constrained objective, but it does so by
directly optimising a cross‑entropy objective.  The *reference policy* remains
in the loss to maintain that KL constraint implicitly: any deviation of
\(\pi_\theta\) from \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) is penalised proportionally to the
log‑ratio, mirroring the KL penalty.

In summary, the reference policy provides the **anchor** that keeps the
policy training stable and faithful to the distribution that generated the
human preference data.  When an SFT model is absent, fitting a new reference
by maximizing the likelihood of the *preferred* completions offers a practical,
intuitive, and mathematically consistent way to approximate that anchor and
to preserve the KL‑constrained optimisation that DPO implicitly implements.
