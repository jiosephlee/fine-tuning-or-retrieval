\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 9: # 9. Main Empirical Findings

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly evaluated on a variety of tasks that probe their ability to process long documents, retrieve sparse signals, and perform zero‑ or few‑shot reasoning.  The recent introduction of LongRoPE, a method for extending the context window of RoPE‑based LLMs beyond two million tokens, provides a new lens on these benchmarks.  This chapter presents the empirical evidence that LongRoPE not only enables unprecedented context lengths but also preserves—sometimes even improves—performance on the tests that were originally designed for the short (4 k) context window.  We systematically cover three families of experiments: language‑modeling perplexity on extremely long text, passkey retrieval as a proxy for distant signal extraction, and the well‑known open‑source LLM leaderboard suites.

---

## 9.1 Perplexity on Aligned Long Documents

The most direct way to quantify how well an LLM can use a longer sequence is to measure next‑token perplexity on genuinely long documents.  LongRoPE’s core claim is that its non‑uniform positional interpolation can preserve the internal geometry of RoPE embeddings so that perplexity does not explode when the sequence length multiplies several times.

In our experiments we focused on LLaMA2‑7B and Mistral‑7B, both extended by LongRoPE to 256 k and then to 2 048 k tokens.  Validation data was taken from two large corpora:

| Model | Extended window | Dataset | Evaluation | Probe strategy |
|-------|-----------------|---------|------------|----------------|
| LLaMA2‑7B | 256 k | PG19 | Sliding window of size 256 | Evaluate NSL at multiple contexts (4 k–256 k) |
| LLaMA2‑7B | 2 048 k | Books3 | Sliding window of size 256 k (≈20 books) | Same as above |
| Mistral‑7B | 256 k | Proof-Pile, PG19 | Sliding window of size 256 | NSL |
| Mistral‑7B | 2 048 k | Books3 | Sliding window as above | NSL |

For every window size we plotted perplexity curves and observed:

1. **Monotonic Improvement with Length** – Starting at roughly 25–30 perplexity at 4 k, many configurations dropped below that value as soon as the model saw longer contexts (e.g., 12–18 at 64 k).  
2. **Smooth Transition across Extensions** – The jump from the base LLM to its LongRoPE counterpart was negligible; for example, LLaMA2’s perplexity at 4 k went from ~28 (baseline) to ~27.5 after a naive PI interpolation.  
3. **Robustness Beyond Two Million Tokens** – Even at the extremes (≈2 million tokens), perplexity did not exceed the baseline +1–2 point range; in some cases it approached the baseline again, suggesting efficient reuse of far‑field information.

These results indicate that the rehearsed non‑uniform interpolation keeps most internally encoded dependencies intact while expanding the representational bandwidth needed for very long sequences.

## 9.2 Passkey Retrieval Across Millions

While perplexity speaks to “guilt‑free” language modeling, many practical applications require *precise extraction* of small signals buried deep in a long document. Passkey retrieval is an established synthetic benchmark for this: a model is prompted with an instruction to find a five‑digit number hidden somewhere in a huge text passage.

For each multimodel configuration we performed ten trials. In each trial we sampled a random position uniformly across the entire evaluation length and asked:
> “Find the five‑digit passkey hidden in this text.”

The authors used identical prompt templates across all models to guarantee fairness. Accuracy curves show:

- **Baseline Models** – With only PI or NTK interpolation accuracy quickly collapsed beyond ~128 k token context length.
- **LongRoPE-2048k Models** – LLaMA2 retained ≥90% accuracy all the way up to two million tokens when fine‑tuned at 256×10³; Accuracy slightly fell around ~1⁄5⁄8⁄10^6 but stayed >85%.  
- **Mistral Variants** – Fine tuner using ×128 extension kept perfect accuracy up to ~1800 k before dropping to ~60% at full two million tokens—still far superior than all baselines.

These observations show that LongRoPE preserves distance sensitivity: even without explicit attention over the entire sequence, it retains enough positional regularities for a transformer head to locate very short signals accurately.

## 9.3 Zero- / Few-Shot Accuracy on “Short” Benchmarks

A crucial marriage between extensibility and backward compatibility must demonstrate that going *ahead* does not pull *backwards*. Hence we evaluated all LongRoPE models on classic open source benchmarks normally scored within a `4 k` window: ARC-Challenge (25-shot), HellaSwag (10-shot), MMLU (5-shot), and TruthfulQA (0-shot).

### 9.3.1 Experimental Setup

* **Languages**: English only; no synthetic or multilingual modalities.
* **Inference**: We used a greedy decoder with temperature=0.
* **Dynamic RoPE Adaptation**: For inference lengths <8 k we substituted RoPE factors found during the *short-length recovery* search phase (Section 3′). This step keeps positional resolution high when fewer tokens are present.

### 9.3.2 Results Overview

| Benchmark    | Metric          | Baseline Score | LongRoPE(§) Score |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| ARC-Challenge   | Accuracy        | **60%**         | **61%**           |
| HellaSwag      | Accuracy        | **93%**         | **93%**           |
| MMLU           | Accuracy        | **69%**         | **70%**           |
| TruthfulQA     | Accuracy        |-               |-                  |

*(Numbers are illustrative; actual reported figures appear in Table X of our experimental appendix.)*

Key takeaways:

1. **Near-Identity Strictness** – For competitive few-shot benchmarks like ARC-Challenge, scores differed by fewer than one percentage point.
2. **Improved Outlier Handling** – On TruthfulQA long RPC contexts can be unstructured; we saw *slight* gains due to better capture of contextual nuance in consecutive sentences.
3. **No Catastrophic Drop** – Our dynamic re-scaling ensures that no model had degraded performance relative to its base counterpart under any benchmark condition.

Thus, LongRoPE is backward compatible: trading all its extra capacity for longer texts does not erode any performance targets defined in mainstream NLP analogues.

## 9.4 Ablation Study Highlights

To confirm Theorem 1—the claim that non-uniform interpolation *twofolded* by token-position preservation yields the best results—numerous ablations were carried out.

- Removing token-position preservation entirely accelerated training cost by ~10%, but led to +10 perplexity points at both mid-lengths (64 k) and extremes (>512 k).  
- Replacing evolution-based search with grid-search over canonical groupings derived from YaRN hurt performance by roughly +8 perplexity points while increasing TPU hours from ~35 h to >130 h.
- Doing fine-tuning only after first searching on “moderate” lengths (e.g., target=128 k) cut training steps by an order of magnitude (~600 vs ~3600 steps) while keeping overall perplexities nearly identical.

All this evidence demonstrates that each element of LongRoPE—the complex non-uniform search, token-preserving thresholding, progressive fine-tuning—is necessary for achieving both massive context expansion *and* robustness across downstream tasks.

## Concluding Reflections

The experiments documented above show two competing demands satisfied simultaneously:

1. Grossly expanding context capacity up to two million tokens without catastrophic training overheads or GPU cost blowup.
2. Retaining original performance ceilings on any dataset or benchmark built for a four thousand-token window.

If anyone doubts current RLHF pipelines’ effectiveness without such long contexts, consider passkey retrieval as one extreme case: extracting six numbers from millions of words has motivated us earlier than RLHF training alone could have allowed figure extraction anyway.

In sum, this chapter verifies empirically that LongRoPE uplifts existing RoPE-based LLMs into a new regime—“Deep Into Context” rather than “Ultra Fast Short Look”—while preserving all expected functionally-tested properties found in existing literature and production deployments.
