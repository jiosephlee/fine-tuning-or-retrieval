\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### How are control consistency metrics computed in controllable generation tasks?
Question:
"The paper introduces a control consistency metric based on estimating the control signal from generated images and comparing it with the original input control signal (e.g., IoU for Canny edges). How exactly is this estimation performed? Are external models used? If so, which ones and what are their roles in computing F1 or accuracy scores?
Answer:
**Answer**

In the controllable‑generation experiments the authors wanted a way to quantify “how much of the user‑supplied control signal actually survived the diffusion process”.  
The idea is simple: *take the generated picture, run an off‑the‑shelf detector that recovers the same type of control signal, and then compare that recovered signal to what was fed into the model*.  The higher the agreement, the better the model kept the control.

---

### 1. What is “control consistency” exactly?

For each task (Canny edge → image, segmentation map → image, landmark → face) the paper defines a numerical score by

1. **Generate** an image $I_{\text{gen}}$ from a text prompt and a control cue $c$.
2. **Re‑estimate** a control cue $\hat c$ from the generated image $I_{\text{gen}}$ using an existing, pre‑trained detector.
3. **Compare** $\hat c$ with the original cue $c$ using a signal‑appropriate metric (IoU, F1, pixel accuracy,…).

Thus every method produces a pair $(c,\hat c)$ for which a single scalar score is reported.

---

### 2. How is the control signal extracted from an image?

| Control type | Extraction tool | How it works | Metric applied |
|--------------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Canny edges** | OpenCV’s Canny edge detector | Applies classical gradient‑thresholding to get a binary edge map | Intersection‑over‑Union (IoU) and F1 score between two binary maps |
| **Segmentation maps** | DeepLabV3‑ResNet101 (a standard segmentation backbone) | Produces per‑pixel class probabilities; after thresholding or argmax we get a discrete mask | Mean IoU over all classes; pixel accuracy (overall correct pixels / total pixels) |
| **Face landmarks** | MediaPipe Face Mesh (or equivalent landmark detector) | Detects 468 2D landmark positions on any face; only the subset used for training is retained | Euclidean $\ell_2$ distance between corresponding landmarks of $\hat c$ and $c$; averaged over all landmarks |

The paper does **not** train any new neural networks for this step; it relies on publicly available, pre‑trained detectors that are well suited for each task.  These are the *external models* referenced in the question.

---

### 3. Why these particular metrics?

* **IoU/F1 for edges** – edges are binary, so these are the canonical overlap measures.  
* **Mean IoU & overall accuracy for segmentation** – since segmentation is multi–class, mean IoU balances per‑class recall/precision while overall accuracy gives an absolute sense of correctness.  
* **Landmark $\ell_2$ error** – landmarks have continuous coordinates; Euclidean distance directly reflects how well geometry was preserved.

Because all metrics are defined on outputs of fixed detectors, they are comparable across methods and reflect *actually* synthesized structure rather than just visual similarity.

---

### 4. From scores to conclusions

In Table 3 of the paper, OFT/COFT achieve higher IoU and F1 on Canny, higher mean IoU and accuracy on segmentation maps, and lower landmark error than ControlNet, T2I‑Adapter or LoRA.  
The authors interpret this as evidence that orthogonal finetuning preserves control signals more faithfully while still generating high‑quality images—exactly what their whole method aims to prove.

---

### Summary

Control consistency is computed by feeding every generated image into off‑the‑shelf detection models (Canny edge detector, DeepLab V3 for segmentation, face–landmark detector). The outputs of these detectors are compared to the original conditioning signals using task‑appropriate overlap or distance metrics (IoU/F1, mean IoU/accuracy, landmark $\ell_2$). These external models provide an unbiased estimate of how much of the intended control survived diffusion, enabling direct comparison across finetuning methods.
