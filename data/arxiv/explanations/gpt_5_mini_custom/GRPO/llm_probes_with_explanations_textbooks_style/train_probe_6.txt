Chapter Title: Code-Pretrained Initialization as a Catalyst for Mathematical Reasoning in DeepSeekMath

# The Role of Initialization in Mathematical Pre-Training

A central design choice in building a mathematical reasoning model is the selection of the initialization model prior to specialized math training. In the DeepSeekMath study, the authors emphasize that initializing from a code-trained model, rather than a general-purpose language model, leads to stronger downstream mathematical reasoning. This choice is not merely incidental; it is implemented at scale and supported by controlled experiments that compare code-first training pipelines to alternatives. Specifically, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, and the authors explicitly note that starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM. Moreover, they investigate this question in a series of pre-training ablations at the 1.3B-parameter scale, which systematically examine how code pre-training affects both tool-free and tool-assisted mathematical problem solving.

This chapter unpacks the methodological setting in which this claim arises, the experimental setups the authors use to isolate the effect of code pre-training, and the observed outcomes that support their conclusion. It also explains how the choice of initialization reverberates through the rest of the training pipeline, including the model’s performance in non-math reasoning tasks, and clarifies the nuanced differences between two-stage and one-stage mixtures of code and math data observed in the paper.

# Overview of DeepSeekMath and Its Training Pipeline

DeepSeekMath is a domain-specific large language model created to advance mathematical reasoning. Its development proceeds in phases. First, the authors construct a large-scale, high-quality mathematical corpus sourced from Common Crawl using a carefully engineered selection pipeline. This DeepSeekMath Corpus totals 120B math-related tokens and is multilingual, with substantial English and Chinese content. The paper establishes the corpus’s quality via pre-training experiments and downstream evaluations, showing that models trained on it achieve strong performance across multiple math benchmarks.

Second, the base model DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is trained by continual pre-training over 500B tokens. Crucially, it is initialized from a code-pretrained model, DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. The data mixture for the 500B-token continual training includes $56\%$ from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, $4\%$ from AlgebraicStack, $10\%$ from arXiv, $20\%$ from GitHub code, and $10\%$ from Common Crawl natural language in English and Chinese. This mixture is deliberately constructed to accumulate mathematical knowledge while retaining and leveraging coding competence.

Finally, instruction tuning and reinforcement learning are applied to produce DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B and DeepSeekMath-RL 7B, which deliver state-of-the-art open-source results on challenging mathematical benchmarks. Across these phases, the prior code-pretrained initialization underpins the model’s mathematical abilities and also affects its broader reasoning profile.

# Why Choose a Code-Trained Initialization?

The authors explicitly report that initializing from a code model is advantageous for downstream mathematics. DeepSeekMath-Base is initialized with DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, and the authors state that beginning from a code training model is a better choice than starting from a general LLM. Beyond this design decision at the 7B scale, the paper conducts focused experiments at the 1.3B-parameter scale to interrogate how code training interacts with mathematical pre-training.

The guiding intuition is that coding and mathematical reasoning share structural patterns of precise, stepwise, and compositional reasoning. Although the paper does not rely on external mechanistic explanations, it documents empirical outcomes: code training benefits both program-aided and tool-free mathematical reasoning and enhances the efficiency of later math-focused training. Notably, the code-trained initialization appears to provide a favorable inductive bias for subsequent mathematical learning, which translates into pronounced performance gains after math pre-training and instruction tuning.

# Experimental Protocols to Assess the Impact of Code Pre-Training

To isolate the contribution of code pre-training, the authors design and execute both two-stage and one-stage training protocols at the 1.3B-parameter scale. These controlled settings enable clear comparisons between code-first and general-language-first initializations, and between staged and mixed data presentations.

In the two-stage training paradigm, they consider:
1) Code Training for $400$B Tokens followed by Math Training for $150$B Tokens. This pipeline first confers coding competence, then specializes in mathematics.
2) General Training for $400$B Tokens followed by Math Training for $150$B Tokens. This control replaces the first stage’s code tokens with general tokens to test whether “more pre-training” alone suffices, or whether code-specific pre-training is uniquely helpful.

In the one-stage training paradigm, they consider:
1) Math Training for $150$B Tokens with no code in the mix. This serves as a baseline for math-only exposure.
2) A joint mixture of $400$B Code Tokens and $150$B Math Tokens in a single stage. This tests whether blending code and math simultaneously can yield the benefits of code pre-training while reducing catastrophic forgetting of coding skills.

Throughout, the authors evaluate both tool-free step-by-step reasoning and program-of-thought problem solving using Python. This dual evaluation is essential because program-aided reasoning places a premium on the model’s coding fluency, whereas tool-free reasoning more directly probes mathematical chain-of-thought generation and solution synthesis without external tools.

# Effects on Program-Aided Mathematical Reasoning

The paper finds that code pre-training substantially benefits program-aided mathematical reasoning. Under the two-stage setting, code training alone already significantly enhances the model’s ability to solve GSM8K and MATH problems by writing and executing Python programs. Adding math training in the second stage yields further improvements. The interpretation is straightforward: the first stage confers a practical facility with producing correct, executable code fragments, while the second stage tunes the model’s mathematical knowledge and problem-solving heuristics, leading to strong program-aided performance.

Under the one-stage setting, mixing code and math tokens also benefits program-aided reasoning. The authors report that combining code and math in one stage synergizes both coding and program-aided mathematical reasoning, while effectively mitigating the catastrophic forgetting of coding abilities that can arise in a strictly sequential two-stage pipeline. In other words, when the training objective includes both code and math tokens simultaneously, the model retains coding competence and leverages it in the program-of-thought setting, where code is a first-class tool for mathematical problem solving.

These observations tie directly to the initialization choice in the 7B model. By initializing from a code-pretrained model and then continuing with a large math-focused corpus, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B benefits from the same pattern: coding fluency becomes a reusable substrate for mathematical problem solving with tools, which then propagates through instruction tuning and reinforcement learning to further gains.

# Effects on Tool-Free Mathematical Reasoning

The authors also examine tool-free mathematical reasoning with few-shot chain-of-thought prompting. In the two-stage setting, code training in the first stage already provides moderate improvements in tool-free performance. More importantly, it boosts the efficiency of subsequent math training, ultimately leading to the best performance among the tested setups. Thus, even when tools are not used at inference time, an initial code-focused phase seems to prepare the model to internalize mathematical curricula more effectively during the math training phase.

The one-stage mixture tells a more nuanced story for tool-free reasoning. When code and math tokens are trained jointly in a single stage at the 1.3B scale, the authors observe that tool-free mathematical reasoning is compromised relative to the best two-stage alternative. Their conjecture is that a 1.3B-parameter model lacks the capacity to fully assimilate both code and mathematical data simultaneously at the desired level, which leads to a trade-off in this setting. This observation further motivates the code-first initialization strategy: at larger scales and in long continual training, separating code acquisition and math specialization makes the most of model capacity and learning dynamics.

# Catastrophic Forgetting, Mixtures, and the Role of Code

A recurring theme in the paper is the tension between sequential specialization and retention of earlier competencies. Two-stage training can induce catastrophic forgetting of coding ability if the second stage focuses purely on math data for long durations; conversely, joint training can alleviate forgetting but may, at smaller scales, limit the peak attainable performance on tool-free math reasoning. The authors’ results show that mixing code and math in one stage effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting for coding and improves program-aided math, while the code-then-math two-stage pipeline achieves superior tool-free mathematical reasoning.

This trade-off clarifies why a code-trained initialization for the 7B base model is a strong choice. By starting from a code-pretrained model at scale and then running a long continual training phase with a well-designed mixture, the model inherits robust coding priors and then absorbs large quantities of mathematical text. The result is a base model that is exceptional at both tool-free chain-of-thought reasoning and program-aided reasoning, and that remains competitive on general reasoning and understanding benchmarks.

# General Reasoning Gains Beyond Mathematics

The paper also reports that math training, proceeding from a code-pretrained initialization, improves performance on general reasoning and understanding benchmarks such as MMLU and BBH, while maintaining coding performance on HumanEval and MBPP. This indicates that the benefits of code-first initialization are not confined to mathematics. Rather, code pre-training appears to support a broader repertoire of structured reasoning behaviors that transfer beyond math tasks when followed by a strong math-focused curriculum.

In the full 7B pipeline, DeepSeekMath-Base surpasses prior open-source base models on multiple math benchmarks and even outperforms much larger closed-source math-focused models in certain settings. After instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, the resulting models cross the $50\%$ accuracy threshold on the competition-level MATH benchmark without external tools, for the first time within the open-source community according to the paper, further underscoring the strength of the underlying base initialization.

# Interpreting the Evidence for Code-First Causality

The authors’ claim that initializing from a code-trained model causally improves downstream mathematical reasoning rests on two pillars within the paper. First, at the 7B scale, the entire DeepSeekMath pipeline is built upon a code-pretrained base (DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B), and the resulting models achieve state-of-the-art open-source mathematical performance. Second, at the 1.3B scale, controlled ablations show that code training before math training improves both program-aided and tool-free mathematical reasoning more than starting from a general LLM or training purely on math tokens.

The two-stage comparison is especially informative: replacing the $400$B-token code stage with $400$B tokens of general language data does not yield the same benefits for subsequent math training. This isolates the effect of code content itself rather than the mere presence of additional pre-training. Taken together with the one-stage mixture results, the evidence indicates that code exposure plays a distinctive and advantageous role in shaping the model’s reasoning behaviors relevant to mathematics.

# Practical Consequences for Model Design

The DeepSeekMath findings translate into concrete guidance for practitioners designing math-focused LLMs. When choosing a prior, code-pretrained bases provide a beneficial foundation. In resource-constrained regimes, one can consider the trade-offs between two-stage versus one-stage mixtures, depending on whether the priority is peak tool-free reasoning or retention of coding fluency for program-of-thought execution. At larger scales, where capacity constraints are relaxed, the code-first strategy remains compelling: it equips the model with structured generation habits and program synthesis skills that integrate naturally with mathematical problem-solving.

These lessons are embedded in the DeepSeekMath training recipe. The 7B base is initialized from a code-trained model and then trained on a mixture dominated by the DeepSeekMath Corpus. The outcome is a base model that is excellent at math and remains strong at coding and general reasoning, setting the stage for instruction tuning and reinforcement learning to deliver further gains.

# Limitations and Scope of the Claim

The paper carefully bounds its conclusions. While it finds that code training improves mathematical reasoning and that starting from a code-pretrained base is advantageous, it also reports areas where data choices expected to help did not. For example, extensive arXiv-only training did not yield notable improvements and sometimes led to deterioration on the evaluated math benchmarks. The authors emphasize that this negative finding should be interpreted cautiously and may change under different tasks, data combinations, or model scales.

Moreover, the one-stage mixture’s limitations at the 1.3B scale serve as a reminder that capacity interacts with curriculum design. The observed compromises in tool-free reasoning under joint code-plus-math training at this scale do not contradict the overarching conclusion; rather, they clarify when staged specialization may be preferable. At the 7B scale and throughout the full pipeline, the code-first choice is consistently validated by the model’s downstream performance.

# Conclusion

Within the DeepSeekMath framework, the prior initialization that meaningfully improved downstream mathematical reasoning was a code-trained model. The evidence is twofold: the top-line successes of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, and the controlled ablations at 1.3B parameters showing that code training before math training enhances both program-aided and tool-free mathematical reasoning more than alternatives. The paper’s results support a coherent narrative: code pre-training equips the model with structured generation and problem-decomposition skills that align with mathematical reasoning, and this alignment persists and amplifies through large-scale math training, instruction tuning, and reinforcement learning.
