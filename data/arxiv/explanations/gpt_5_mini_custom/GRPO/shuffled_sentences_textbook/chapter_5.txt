DeepSeekMath-trained models outperform the other-corpus-trained models across a set of eight mathematical benchmarks when evaluated with few-shot chain-of-thought prompting. The controlled experiments show that training on DeepSeekMath improved mathematical reasoning performance in both English and Chinese benchmarks, while the English-centric corpora sometimes provided limited or even negative effects for Chinese tasks. Training used a large token batch of $4\text{M}$ tokens per update and a context length of $4\text{K}$ tokens. Because the baselines were smaller, they had already been cycled through multiple times by the time the $150\text{B}$ budget was reached; the repeated exposure caused their performance curves to flatten out, producing an early plateau in downstream evaluation metrics. Including high-quality non-English mathematical pages improved performance on Chinese benchmarks in the experiments. In contrast, a larger corpus with more diverse, higher-quality material provides fresh, informative signal for a longer portion of the $150\text{B}$-token budget and produces a steeper and more sustained improvement in downstream tasks. A large, carefully selected, and multilingual math corpus produces both faster early gains and sustained late-stage improvements under a large training budget, whereas small or English-centric corpora tend to saturate early when repeatedly cycled. In summary, the controlled $1.3$B-parameter comparisons in the DeepSeekMath work make a clear case: data curation and scale are jointly crucial. Be intentional about multilingual content. Large, diverse corpora maintain improvement into later stages because they keep presenting new patterns, styles, and problem types that the model has not yet internalized. The DeepSeekMath pipeline emphasizes selection and mining of math-relevant pages (fastText classifier, iterative enrichment with annotated domains, decontamination of benchmark overlap). The paper reports that DeepSeekMath delivers superior early-stage performance compared to Proof-Pile-2, indicating that its per-token informativeness is higher. DeepSeekMath is substantially larger than the other math-focused corpora referenced: the final DeepSeekMath collection totaled $120\text{B}$ math tokens in the paper’s main description, and in some analyses the authors reference an $89\text{B}$-token second-iteration subset used for ablation. If you intend to expend a large token budget (hundreds of billions of tokens), assemble a corpus whose unique content is large enough to avoid excessive repetition. These findings inform concrete best practices for corpus engineering: prioritize per-token quality, ensure sufficient unique data for your compute budget, include multilingual content if desired, and design iterative mining pipelines with decontamination baked in. The study demonstrates industry-standard decontamination filtering (exact matching of $10$-grams to evaluation benchmark strings, with special handling for shorter benchmark texts) to prevent benchmark leakage. The optimization and training schedule were standardized across experiments. The DeepSeekMath construction process—seeding with OpenWebMath, training a fastText classifier, mining Common Crawl, enriching the seed by inspecting domains with high recall, and iterating—produced a much larger and more informative corpus than a one-shot filter. Decontamination must be integrated into the collection pipeline because even a high-quality, large corpus is harmful if it contains direct answers to evaluation benchmarks. Put simply, when the training budget is large relative to corpus size, repeated-epoch effects dominate; small, repeatedly cycled corpora lead to early saturation, while large, diverse corpora continue to yield gains across more of the training budget.
