## Why MATH is hard

MATH problems:
- Are longer and require multi-step reasoning chains that must be both correct and coherent.

- Penalize small mistakes: a single algebra slip or logical misstep breaks the final answer.
- Make model calibration and generation stability important — many sampled reasoning chains look <|endoftext|> but are incorrect.

Because of that, closed models with lots of compute and data (GPT-4, Gemini-Ultra) have been leading; the novelty here is bringing an open 7B model into competitive range.

## Other important design choices

- Start from a <|endoftext|> model: <|endoftext|> initializes from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 (a code-oriented 7B). The authors report code training improves mathematical reasoning (both code-aided solving and pure chain-of-thought), so starting from a code model helped.

- Process supervision in RL: providing rewards at intermediate reasoning steps (not only final answer) further improves learning compared to only terminal rewards.

## What the numbers mean in plain terms

- 51.7% on MATH (single-run, no tools): About half of competition-level problems were solved correctly by the model using generated step-by-step solutions. For a 7B open model, that’s a notable jump: it surpasses many larger open-source models and approaches closed models’ performance levels.
- 60.9% with self-consistency (64 samples): Aggregating 64 sampled reasoning traces and selecting the majority answer raises <|endoftext|> substantially. This indicates the model often has correct reasoning paths in its high-probability distribution — the trick is to surface them via sampling and majority voting.
- 88.2% on GSM8K: near-human grade-school problem performance, showing the model is highly competent on standard multi-step arithmetic/word problems.

## What this does and does not imply

- Positive: With the right data and training recipe, a compact 7B model can achieve surprisingly strong mathematical reasoning performance. Thoughtful corpus construction, code <|endoftext|> instruction tuning, and a tailored RL stage are key.
- Limitations noted by the authors:
  - Geometry and some theorem-proof problems remain harder; closed models still lead in those areas.
  - The improvements from RL appear to come largely from making correct traces more likely in the model distribution (improving Maj@K), not necessarily from increasing the model’s underlying reasoning primitives. In other words, RL makes the model better at "producing" correct solutions <|endoftext|> rather than fundamentally altering latent mathematical understanding.
  - ArXiv content did not show clear positive impact in their experiments; web-scraped math pages proved more valuable.
