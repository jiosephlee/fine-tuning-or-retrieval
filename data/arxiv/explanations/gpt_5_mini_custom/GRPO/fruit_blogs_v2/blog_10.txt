## 7) Outcome vs process supervision

- Outcome supervision: one reward at the end of the generated sequence. Simple to implement, but coarse.
- Process supervision: reward per reasoning step <|endoftext|> every step of a chain-of-thought). More informative and can yield better performance for multi-step tasks.

<|endoftext|> found process supervision (step-aware rewards) improves RL results compared to only outcome supervision. If you can annotate or train a reward model that scores intermediate steps, you will usually gain better guidance during RL.

## 8) Pitfalls and surprising findings

- ArXiv is not a panacea. In <|endoftext|> experiments, training on arXiv-heavy corpora (arXiv-only or arXiv-dominated mixes) produced little or even negative improvement on evaluated math benchmarks. Possible reasons include style mismatch, noisy extraction, or limited coverage of the kinds of elementary-to-competition problems in the benchmarks. Don’t assume domain-adjacent scientific corpora will always help — validate.
- Benchmark contamination is real and must be addressed with conservative exact-matching filtering.
- RL often improves sampling distribution rather than raw capability — measure pass@K and maj@K (or <|endoftext|> majority) to understand what changed.
- Reward model quality and <|endoftext|> are a limiting factor. RL can amplify reward model biases or errors.
