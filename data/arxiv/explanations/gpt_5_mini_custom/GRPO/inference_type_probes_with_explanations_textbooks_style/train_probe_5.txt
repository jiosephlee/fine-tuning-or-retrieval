Chapter Title: Why Code-Initialized Pretraining Improves Mathematical Reasoning in DeepSeekMath

# The Role of Initialization in Mathematical Language Models

Model initialization sets the foundation for what a language model can learn efficiently during subsequent training. In the context of mathematical reasoning, the initialization choice directly influences how well the model acquires structured problem-solving abilities, handles symbolic expressions, and integrates computation with natural language. The paper presents clear empirical and design choices that favor initializing mathematical models from a code-trained checkpoint rather than from a general-purpose language model.

DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. This decision is not incidental; it is grounded in observations that starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM. This preference is substantiated by a series of experiments and evaluations spanning mathematical reasoning without tools, program-aided reasoning with tools, and general reasoning benchmarks. The evidence aligns with the broader conclusion that a code-initialized starting point provides the right inductive biases and representational scaffolding for learning complex, structured reasoning required in mathematics.

The paper’s central claim, reflected in both design and empirical outcomes, is that code training improves reasoning abilities, at least for mathematical reasoning. This finding supports the conclusion that initializing from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint would most likely lead to decreased math performance. The phenomenon appears consistently across settings where models are trained to solve mathematical problems in natural language and where models generate and execute Python programs to reach correct answers.

# Evidence from the Design of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B

The construction of DeepSeekMath hinges on two pillars: a high-quality, large-scale mathematical corpus mined from Common Crawl, and a training pipeline that builds on a code-initialized base model. The model follows a continual training regime of 500B tokens comprising 56% DeepSeekMath Corpus, 4% AlgebraicStack, 10% arXiv, 20% GitHub code, and 10% general natural language from Common Crawl in both English and Chinese. This mixture intentionally preserves and extends the code-trained priors while saturating the model with mathematically rich, multilingual data.

The results are notable. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, which starts from code training, leads across eight mathematical benchmarks among open-source base models. It surpasses Mistral 7B and Llemma 34B on step-by-step mathematical problem solving without tool use, and outperforms Llemma 34B on program-aided reasoning using Python. On the competition-level MATH dataset, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B not only exceeds open-source base models by over 10% absolute but also outperforms Minerva 540B, a closed-source base model that is vastly larger. These observations are consistent with the thesis that initialization from code grants the model tighter control over symbolic manipulation, procedural reasoning, and the ability to structure multi-step solutions—capabilities that translate directly into stronger math performance.

Additionally, the choice of code-initialized starting point interacts positively with general reasoning metrics. The paper reports that math training improves performance on MMLU and BBH over DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, showing a transfer of gains from math-specific training into broader reasoning. Crucially, this improvement is not at the expense of coding ability: by including code tokens during continual training, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B maintains strong scores on HumanEval and MBPP.

# Why Code Training Improves Mathematical Reasoning

The paper investigates the hypothesis that code training improves reasoning. Although this idea is popular, it is often unverified. Here, the authors provide a targeted analysis in the mathematical domain to conclude that code training improves models’ ability to do mathematical reasoning both with and without tool use.

The experiments are structured to isolate the impact of code training on downstream math tasks. In two-stage training, the model first undergoes code training and then math training. In a control configuration, the model receives general tokens instead of code tokens in the first stage. The results demonstrate a clear advantage for code training:

1. In program-aided mathematical reasoning, code training alone significantly enhances the model’s ability to solve GSM8K and MATH using Python. Math training in the second stage yields further improvements, but crucially, the baseline established by code training is higher than that obtained by starting from general data.

2. In mathematical reasoning without tool use, code training results in moderate improvements even before math-specific training. Moreover, code training boosts the efficiency of subsequent math training, leading to the best overall performance.

In one-stage training, mixing 400B code tokens with 150B math tokens effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting that can arise in a strict two-stage pipeline, and it synergizes both coding and program-aided mathematical reasoning. However, mixing code and math tokens in a single-stage setting compromises mathematical reasoning without tool use for the small 1.3B model studied, suggesting capacity limits at smaller scales. Importantly, this limitation does not contradict the core observation: code training provides a superior foundation for mathematical reasoning; using general tokens instead of code tokens in the initial phase leads to weaker math outcomes.

Intuitively, code training aligns model representations with the structure of algorithms, precise syntax, and deterministically evaluable procedures. Mathematics, particularly as evaluated on datasets like GSM8K and MATH, demands structured multi-step reasoning, explicit variable manipulation, and disciplined decomposition of harder problems into subroutines or logical steps. Code pretraining encourages the model to represent these steps in a compositional and robust manner, which transfers smoothly to numerical problem solving and formal reasoning tasks. When the model later encounters a large-scale math corpus, it already possesses the representational habits conducive to successful mathematical inference.

# Comparative Experiments: Code vs. General Data in Pretraining

To directly assess whether code training is a better initialization choice than general language training, the paper analyzes a two-stage training setup:

- Code training for 400B tokens followed by math training for 150B tokens.
- General training for 400B tokens followed by math training for 150B tokens.

This comparison isolates the impact of the initial stage. The results show that code training yields clear advantages over general tokens for mathematical tasks. With code training first, the model obtains stronger program-aided reasoning, better non-tool mathematical reasoning performance after the math stage, and more efficient gains in the second-stage math training. In contrast, when the first stage uses general tokens, the model does not acquire the same beneficial priors, and subsequent math training is less effective. This strongly supports the conclusion that initializing from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint would most likely lead to decreased math performance.

The authors also study single-stage training by mixing code and math tokens. For the 1.3B parameter model, the mixed setting alleviates catastrophic forgetting and strengthens tool-integrated reasoning, but it compromises non-tool reasoning, likely due to capacity constraints. Nevertheless, the central inference holds: code initialization creates a superior foundation for math compared to general language initialization.

# Interactions with Data Quality and Scope

The model’s success draws from the synergy between code-initialized training and a high-quality math corpus. The DeepSeekMath Corpus is constructed through an iterative mining pipeline on Common Crawl using a fastText-based classifier trained with OpenWebMath and further refined via manual annotation. The resulting corpus totals 120B tokens across 35.5M mathematical web pages and is multilingual, predominantly English and Chinese. The paper establishes three properties of this corpus:

1. High quality: When a 1.3B model is trained on this corpus versus alternatives such as Proof-Pile-2, it demonstrates superior downstream performance, with a steeper and more sustained learning curve.

2. Multilingual coverage: Training on this corpus improves both English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks. In contrast, English-centric corpora show limited or negative impact on Chinese mathematical reasoning.

3. Large scale: The corpus dwarfs other math corpora by several times. Performance scales with data quantity and quality, yielding continued improvements rather than early plateaus.

These properties interact with the model initialization. A code-initialized model is better equipped to exploit the structured nature of the math corpus. For example, when prompted with program-of-thought tasks, the model uses Python to compute intermediate quantities and reliably extract answers, reflecting a combined benefit of code priors and math data. The corpus’ multilingual nature suggests broader generalization, and the evaluations confirm superior performance on Chinese benchmarks compared to models trained solely on English math data.

# Negative Result: The Limited Value of arXiv-Only Training

The paper examines the impact of arXiv papers on mathematical reasoning and reports a negative or neutral effect when models are trained on arXiv-only corpora. Using DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B across MathPile and ArXiv-RedPajama, the authors find no notable improvements or even deterioration across GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and formal mathematics benchmarks such as miniF2F. This stands in contrast to the gains observed from Common Crawl-derived math webpages and code training.

While the authors note several caveats—such as untested combinations of arXiv with other data or the potential benefits at larger scales—the finding reinforces the broader conclusion that effective math performance arises from a carefully engineered pipeline combining code initialization and web-derived math data rather than from arXiv-only data. This indirectly supports the importance of the chosen initialization: a code-initialized foundation helps the model convert web-scale mathematical content into robust reasoning abilities, whereas general language or arXiv-only initialization does not yield the same benefits.

# Broader Evaluations: General Reasoning and Formal Mathematics

Beyond mathematical problem solving, the paper evaluates DeepSeekMath-Base 7B on general reasoning and coding. The model improves upon its precursor DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 in MMLU and BBH after math training, indicating transfer from mathematical training to broader reasoning. It maintains strong coding performance on HumanEval and MBPP, due to continued exposure to code tokens during training.

Additionally, the model demonstrates strong few-shot autoformalization performance in formal mathematics, producing proof sketches in Isabelle and relying on Sledgehammer to fill in details. This capability again suggests that the code-initialized structure promotes rigor and decomposition, making the model well-suited to tasks that require precise formal thinking.

# Implications: Initialization Choices for Future Math Models

The cumulative evidence points to a clear practical implication: for building mathematical language models, initializing from a code-trained checkpoint yields superior results compared to starting from a general LLM. The authors’ data selection pipeline and training regimen amplify this advantage, but the effect is robust enough to stand out across multiple tasks and settings. In particular:

1. Code training improves mathematical reasoning without tool use and strengthens program-aided reasoning.

2. Code initialization accelerates the effectiveness of subsequent math training, producing higher performance on competitive benchmarks like MATH and GSM8K.

3. The benefits extend to general reasoning tasks and do not compromise coding ability when code tokens are included in continual training.

Therefore, initializing from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint such as DeepSeek-Coder would most likely lead to decreased math performance. This conclusion is grounded in the design choices, ablation studies, and comparative analyses presented in the paper. It should guide future construction of math-specific models: begin with code training to establish structured reasoning priors, then apply large-scale, high-quality math corpora to specialize the model’s capabilities.

# Reinforcement Learning as a Complementary Enhancement

While initialization is crucial, the paper further shows that reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), significantly enhances the mathematical reasoning capability of an already strong instruction-tuned model. GRPO removes the critic model used in PPO and estimates a baseline from group scores, thus reducing training resources. Applied to DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B—already benefiting from code-initialized and math-focused training—GRPO still yields substantial gains, achieving 88.2% on GSM8K and 51.7% on MATH with chain-of-thought reasoning.

This reinforces the idea that the full recipe matters: a code-initialized foundation, high-quality math pretraining, instruction tuning with chain-of-thought and program-of-thought data, and reinforcement learning with GRPO together produce a model that surpasses open-source alternatives and approaches closed-source performance. The improvements from GRPO further highlight that robust initialization sets the stage for efficient downstream optimization—an effect less likely when starting from a general LLM.

# Conclusion

The paper provides a coherent and empirically supported narrative: code-initialized pretraining is a superior starting point for building mathematical reasoning models. DeepSeekMath leverages this insight by initializing from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, then scaling with a high-quality, multilingual math corpus, augmenting with instruction tuning, and optimizing with GRPO. Across benchmarks, the results confirm that starting from code is more conducive to structured reasoning, symbolic manipulation, and program-based solution strategies than starting from a general LLM. Consequently, initialization choices materially affect outcomes, and choosing a code-initialized checkpoint is a decisive factor in achieving state-of-the-art math performance in open language models.
