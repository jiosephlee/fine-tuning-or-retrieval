Chapter Title: Initialization Choices and Their Impact on Mathematical Reasoning in DeepSeekMath

# Introduction
Mathematical reasoning is a demanding capability for large language models, and the training pathway chosen for a model profoundly shapes its eventual performance. The DeepSeekMath paper presents a domain-specific model trained to excel in mathematics through a combination of large-scale math-focused pre-training and reinforcement learning. A central design decision in this work is the initialization of DeepSeekMath-Base 7B from a code-trained model, specifically DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, followed by extensive math pre-training. This choice is not incidental; the paper consistently emphasizes that starting from a code-trained model is a better choice than starting from a general LLM for the goal of mathematical reasoning. This chapter carefully unfolds the evidence presented in the paper and explains why, based on that evidence, the expected outcome of initializing DeepSeekMath from a general LLM instead of a code-trained model would be lower mathematical performance.

# Why Initialization Matters for Mathematical Reasoning
Initialization governs the inductive biases and capabilities a model brings into subsequent training stages. In the paper, DeepSeekMath-Base is explicitly initialized with DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. The authors report that code training prior to math training improves models’ ability to solve mathematical problems both with and without tool use. In other words, the model that begins life as code-trained appears predisposed to structured reasoning, formal manipulation, and programmatic thinking, all of which are useful when later exposed to large volumes of mathematical text. The paper further notes that math training from this code-informed foundation also enhances general reasoning capabilities, as reflected in improvements on benchmarks such as MMLU and BBH.

These observations imply that initialization with a code-trained model confers representational advantages that math training can capitalize on. By contrast, the paper contrasts code training with general-token training in ablation studies and finds that code tokens have specific benefits for mathematical reasoning that general tokens do not match. This creates a clear expectation: if DeepSeekMath had been initialized from a general LLM, the downstream math performance would be lower than the path actually taken, because the synergistic effect observed between code training and math pre-training would be absent.

# Evidence from the Paper: Code Training Benefits Mathematical Reasoning
The paper devotes a dedicated section to analyzing how code training affects mathematical reasoning. The authors examine both two-stage and one-stage training regimes and directly compare code token pre-training with general token pre-training before math training. Their study uses consistent training settings so that differences can be attributed to the training data rather than confounding factors.

In the two-stage setup, the first stage is either code training or general-token training for a large number of tokens, followed by math training. The results show that code training alone significantly enhances program-aided mathematical problem solving. When math training is added in the second stage, performance improves further. Crucially, when the first stage uses general tokens instead of code tokens, these benefits do not materialize to the same degree. This comparison isolates the effect of code training and establishes it as beneficial for mathematical reasoning.

The paper also examines mathematical reasoning without tool use. Even here, code training in the first stage yields moderate enhancements before any math training, and it increases the efficiency and effectiveness of the subsequent math training, eventually leading to the best overall performance. These consistent gains with code-first initialization reinforce the conclusion that code training provides a foundation that math training leverages to reach higher reasoning capability.

# One-Stage vs Two-Stage Training: Synergy and Trade-offs
Beyond two-stage training, the paper investigates a one-stage mixture of code and math tokens. This setup aims to explore whether mixing code tokens with math tokens during a single training stage can retain coding performance and improve program-aided mathematics without the catastrophic forgetting observed in two-stage training.

For program-aided mathematical reasoning, mixing code and math tokens in one stage is effective: it mitigates catastrophic forgetting and supports strong performance on tasks solved using Python. The paper attributes this to synergy between code and math tokens when models are required to produce executable solutions. However, the one-stage mixture introduces a trade-off for mathematical reasoning without tool use at the small model scale examined (DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B in ablation): the model’s capacity appears insufficient to fully absorb both code and math simultaneously, and performance without tools is compromised compared to the two-stage code-then-math setup.

Together, these findings demonstrate two key points grounded in the paper. First, code tokens contribute unique benefits to mathematical reasoning that general tokens lack. Second, the timing and structure of how code and math tokens are introduced matters, and the code-first pathway most closely aligns with the strongest outcomes in both tool-free and tool-integrated mathematical tasks at the studied scale.

# Initialization Choice in DeepSeekMath: Design and Outcomes
DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is explicitly initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B and trained for $500\mathrm{B}$ tokens with a data distribution that includes $56\%$ from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, code from GitHub, arXiv, and additional natural language data. The paper’s evaluations show that this initialization, followed by math pre-training, yields strong performance across a wide spectrum of tasks:

1. In step-by-step mathematical reasoning without tools, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B leads among open-source base models across multiple benchmarks and surpasses the much larger Minerva $540\mathrm{B}$ on the competition-level MATH dataset.
2. In program-aided reasoning with Python, it outperforms the prior state-of-the-art model on GSM8K and MATH.
3. In formal mathematics, it demonstrates strong few-shot autoformalization on miniF2F.
4. In general language understanding and reasoning, it shows significant improvements on MMLU and BBH compared to its precursor, indicating that math training from a code-trained initialization amplifies broader reasoning capabilities while preserving coding skills.

These outcomes are consistent with the earlier ablation findings: code-first initialization shapes the model’s internal reasoning capabilities in ways that math pre-training can further refine, producing superior mathematical performance than would be expected if initialization had been from a general LLM.

# Why General-LLM Initialization Would Be Expected to Underperform
The paper’s analyses of training regimes provide the basis for the expected outcome of a different initialization choice. When the first-stage training uses general tokens instead of code tokens, the model does not achieve the same level of mathematical reasoning gains. Moreover, the authors explicitly state that starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM. This statement is backed by empirical comparisons: code training improves mathematical reasoning both with and without tools, increases the effectiveness of subsequent math training, and yields a superior base for instruction tuning and reinforcement learning.

Therefore, if DeepSeekMath had been initialized from a general LLM rather than DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B, the paper’s evidence implies the model would lack the code-derived inductive biases and structural reasoning capabilities that were later enhanced by math training. The downstream effect would be lower mathematical performance on the benchmarks the paper reports, including GSM8K and MATH, and likely weaker improvements on general reasoning benchmarks such as MMLU and BBH that benefited from the math training conducted on top of a code-trained foundation.

# Reinforcement Learning Strengthens Performance but Does Not Replace Initialization Effects
The paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and shows that reinforcement learning substantially improves mathematical reasoning beyond supervised instruction tuning. DeepSeekMath-RL 7B achieves $88.2\%$ on GSM8K and $51.7\%$ on MATH using chain-of-thought reasoning, surpassing all open-source models up to $70\mathrm{B}$ parameters and most closed-source models. Importantly, the RL pipeline operates on top of the instruction-tuned model, which itself builds on the code-initialized and math-pre-trained base.

The paper further analyzes why RL works in this setting. The improvements are attributed to robustifying the output distribution—boosting the correct response within the top-$K$ outputs rather than fundamentally rewiring capabilities—consistent with the reward-driven adjustments encoded by GRPO. This perspective reinforces that while RL yields significant gains, those gains are built upon the representational and reasoning foundations established by the earlier training stages. An initialization from a general LLM would have provided a weaker base for these later procedures, and the paper’s analyses suggest RL would then be optimizing a less capable starting point, leading to lower overall mathematical performance than observed in the code-initialized path.

# Conclusion
The DeepSeekMath paper supports a clear conclusion: initializing from a code-trained model materially benefits mathematical reasoning, both with and without tool use, and enhances the effectiveness of subsequent math pre-training and reinforcement learning. The authors explicitly assert that starting from a code training model is a better choice than starting from a general LLM, and they provide ablation evidence to substantiate this claim. Based on the authors’ initialization choice and their reported results, the expected outcome of initializing DeepSeekMath from a general LLM instead of a code-trained model would be lower math performance.
