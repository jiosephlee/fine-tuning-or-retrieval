In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', a concrete change to the fastText training seed that improved its ability to recall more mathematical pages during later iterations was seed diversification. The authors begin with OpenWebMath as positive examples and random Common Crawl pages as negatives, then observe that the initial fastText classifier misses many math pages due to limited diversity in the positives. To address this, they reorganize Common Crawl into domains, identify domains where more than \(10\%\) of pages were recalled as math-related (e.g., \(\texttt{mathoverflow.net}\)), and manually annotate math-specific URL segments within those domains. Crucially, they add the yet-uncollected pages from those annotated segments back into the seed corpus and retrain the fastText classifier. This explicitly diversifies the positive seed—expanding beyond the stylistic and topical footprint of OpenWebMath—and thus equips the classifier to learn broader features of mathematical text and HTML structure.

The effectiveness of seed diversification is reflected in the iterative mining results. After each iteration, the updated fastText model recalls more math pages, culminating in \(35.5\text{M}\) pages totaling \(120\text{B}\) tokens across four iterations. By the fourth iteration, nearly \(98\%\) of the data had already been collected in the third, indicating that recall had largely saturated—consistent with a classifier that, after diversifying its training seed, learned a comprehensive boundary over math-related content. Moreover, the diversified corpus yields better downstream performance than baselines: even at \(50\text{B}\) tokens, models trained on the DeepSeekMath Corpus outperform those trained on Proof-Pile-2, implying higher average data quality driven by improved recall. In short, diversifying the fastText seed expanded the positive training distribution, improved generalization, and materially increased recall of relevant math pages in later iterations.
