### How do chain-of-thought and program-of-thought data interact during SFT—are they mixed or staged?
Question:
The instruction tuning includes CoT, PoT, and tool-integrated reasoning. Are these formats mixed within a single training run, or staged (e.g., first CoT, then PoT)? If mixed, do you re-weight them to avoid the model overfitting to one format? Any ablations showing different mixing ratios or curricula and their impact on MATH/GSM8K?
Answer:
In <|endoftext|> the authors do not “pre‐train” separately on chain‐of‐thought (CoT) and then switch to program‐of‐thought (PoT) (or vice versa). Instead, they assemble a single supervised‐fine‐tuning (SFT) corpus that contains all three formats—CoT, PoT and tool‐integrated reasoning—and they shuffle them together in every minibatch. Concretely, their SFT dataset comprises about $776\,\mathrm{K}$ examples drawn from GSM8K, MATH, MathInstruct, Lila‐OOD and a collection of Chinese K-12 problems; each example is labeled either with a natural‐language–only CoT solution, a code‐based PoT solution, or a tool‐integration solution. During fine‐tuning they “randomly concatenate” examples (up to the $4\,\mathrm{K}$-token context) and train for $500$ steps at a constant learning rate, without any staged curriculum that says “first learn CoT, then switch to PoT.”

Because they simply pool and <|endoftext|> sample from that mixed collection, there is no explicit re‐weighting or down‐sampling of any format to guard against <|endoftext|> to CoT versus PoT. The relative frequency of each style in the SFT run is exactly the relative size of that style’s slice of the $776\,\mathrm{K}$ examples. The paper does not report any <|endoftext|> where, say, you train with $80\%$ CoT / $20\%$ PoT versus $50\%$ / $50\%$ or a staged schedule, nor does it explore curriculum effects on GSM8K or MATH. In short, all three formats are interleaved in one joint SFT stage, sampled <|endoftext|> and no mixing‐ratio or “CoT-then-PoT” curriculum study is presented.
