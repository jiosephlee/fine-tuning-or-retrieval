### Did the paper mean 40B tokens or 40B HTML web pages in the Common Crawl filtering step?
Question:
In Section 3.1 (Data Collection and Decontamination), the paper says they "employ URL-based deduplication and near-deduplication techniques, resulting in 40B HTML web pages." Later, when deciding what to keep, they run pre-training experiments on "top 40B, 80B, 120B, and 160B tokens" and then choose the top 40B tokens in the first iteration. I'm confused about whether the earlier "40B" refers to web pages or tokens. 40B web pages sounds implausibly large relative to CC. Could the authors clarify the intended unit and the actual scale at each step (pages vs tokens), and how these numbers relate to each other?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper’s “$40\text{B}$” in the Data Collection paragraph is not referring to forty billion web pages — it’s an inconsistent/ambiguous phrasing in the manuscript. The <|endoftext|> experiments (the “top $40\text{B}$, $80\text{B}$, $120\text{B}$, and $160\text{B}$ tokens”) and the final corpus statistics ($35.5\text{M}$ web pages → $120\text{B}$ tokens) make it clear the selection/measurement is done in tokens. In other words, when they say they “preserve the top‑ranking ones” and then evaluate “top $40\text{B}$ … tokens”, the unit being compared and chosen is tokens, not pages.

Why this is the only <|endoftext|> reading (intuitive + <|endoftext|>
- Forty billion HTML pages would be astronomically larger than Common Crawl; Common Crawl releases do not contain anything near $40\text{B}$ distinct HTML pages after reasonable deduplication. So “$40\text{B}$ pages” is implausible.
- Later the paper reports the final collected set is $35.5\text{M}$ (million) mathematical web pages totalling $120\text{B}$ tokens. That gives an average of roughly $120\text{B} / 35.5\text{M} \approx 3.4\text{k}$ tokens per page, which is entirely reasonable for HTML pages (after extraction).
- When they say they tested pretraining on the “top $40\text{B}$, $80\text{B}$, $120\text{B}$, and $160\text{B}$ tokens” and in iteration 1 chose “the top $40\text{B}$ tokens,” those quantities explicitly use the token unit. So the selection cutoff is token-based, not page-count based.
- <|endoftext|> tokens $\leftrightarrow$ pages makes sense: if $35.5\text{M}$ pages = $120\text{B}$ tokens, then top $40\text{B}$ tokens would <|endoftext|> to on the order of $\sim 12\text{M}$ pages (very rough, depends on token-per-page distribution). That matches the workflow described (rank pages by score, then keep top pages up to a token budget).

Concrete relationship of the numbers in the paper’s pipeline (how to read it):
- They URL-deduplicate / near-deduplicate Common Crawl to get a reduced pool (the manuscript’s “$40\text{B}$ HTML web pages” claim is a wording error — treat the deduplicated pool as being measured in tokens for <|endoftext|> decisions).
- They rank pages by <|endoftext|> score and then choose a token budget for retention. They experimented with keeping the top $40\text{B}$, $80\text{B}$, $120\text{B}$, and $160\text{B}$ tokens (i.e., choose pages in score order until you hit the token cutoff).
- In iteration 1 they chose to keep the top $40\text{B}$ tokens. After <|endoftext|> expansion and re-classification, the total final collected math pages across four <|endoftext|> was $35.5\text{M}$ pages, totaling $120\text{B}$ tokens. (They stopped because iteration 4 added little new material.)

Conclusion / suggested erratum:
Treat the early “$40\text{B}$ HTML web pages” phrase as a typo/ambiguous wording in the paper. The meaningful units for the selection experiments and final corpus size are tokens. If you need an authoritative correction for citation or replication, it would be reasonable to ask the authors for an erratum clarifying that the deduplicated Common Crawl pool was considered in token units (or that the “$40\text{B}$” in that sentence was intended to be $40\text{B}$ tokens or $40\text{B}$ HTML bytes), and to request the exact size of the deduplicated candidate pool (pages and tokens) they started from.
