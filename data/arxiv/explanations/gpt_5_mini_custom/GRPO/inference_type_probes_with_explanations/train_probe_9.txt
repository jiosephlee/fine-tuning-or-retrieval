In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the decontamination procedure removes any text segment that contains an exact n-gram match with evaluation benchmarks, and therefore the rule implicitly assumes that a 10-gram exact match suffices. This follows directly from their stated filtering criterion: for benchmark contamination they remove any web page containing a substring that exactly matches a 10-gram from GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, or AGIEval, and for benchmark texts shorter than 10 tokens but at least 3, they apply exact matching on those shorter spans; operationally, this encodes the heuristic that if benchmark content has leaked into training data, at least one contiguous overlap of length \(n=10\) (or \(3 \le n < 10\) for short items) will appear verbatim. Such a choice is sensible in large-scale web corpora because (i) benchmarks circulate online largely in unmodified form, so verbatim overlaps are common; (ii) a 10-token exact match has a very low chance of occurring by coincidence, reducing false positives; and (iii) longer exact matches are more robust to benign topical overlap yet efficient to check at scale. Combined with their prior URL and near-duplicate deduplication, this n-gram rule operationalizes a high-precision proxy for contamination that presumes genuine leakage entails at least one exact overlap of length \(\ge 10\) tokens, while still catching shorter benchmark items via the fallback \(3\)- to \(9\)-gram exact match.
