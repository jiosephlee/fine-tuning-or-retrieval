According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the clip term in GRPO's PPO-style objective primarily guards against the risk of large policy shifts. This follows directly from the GRPO objective in Equation (3), where for each token the update maximizes a clipped surrogate of the importance ratio, using the term min[ r_t A_t, clip(r_t, 1−ε, 1+ε) A_t ] with r_t = π_θ(o_t|q,o_{<t}) / π_{θ_old}(o_t|q,o_{<t}) and ε a small constant; as in PPO, this clipping truncates the effective ratio when it deviates beyond [1−ε, 1+ε], thereby reducing or nullifying gradient pressure that would otherwise push π_θ too far from π_{θ_old} when advantages are large or misestimated, and thus stabilizing updates against abrupt policy changes. GRPO removes the critic and computes advantages from group-normalized rewards, which can increase variance in A_t; retaining PPO’s clipping is therefore crucial to keep high-variance advantages from inducing outsized steps, and it is complemented by an explicit KL regularizer −β D_KL[π_θ || π_ref] that further contracts the policy toward a reference model, jointly ensuring that per-iteration updates remain conservative even as group-relative signals drive learning.
