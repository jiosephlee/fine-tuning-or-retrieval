In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', preserving only the top-ranking pages predicted by the fastText model as part of the math corpus rests on the unstated assumption that rank indicates math quality. This is sensible because the fastText score is explicitly trained to measure similarity to a high-quality seed of mathematical web text (OpenWebMath) and is iteratively improved using additional positives mined from math-heavy domains and refined via human annotation, so higher scores increasingly reflect pages that are both on-topic and stylistically similar to vetted math content; the authors further use URL- and near-deduplication, benchmark decontamination (exact 10-gram filters), and progressive pilot pre-training at different token thresholds (top 40B, 80B, 120B, 160B) to choose cutoffs where precision is highest, and they empirically validate that this ranking heuristic correlates with downstream quality by showing that models trained on the resulting top-ranked corpus achieve superior performance (e.g., DeepSeekMath-Base 7B trained on the 120B-token corpus surpasses baselines and even outperforms Minerva 540B on MATH) and exhibit a steeper learning curve than models trained on alternative corpora, indicating that the top of the ranked list is enriched for mathematically useful, clean data and thus that the fastText rank is a practical proxy for math quality.
