According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', if GRPO is run with group size $G=1$, the key advantage described in the paper that would be lost is relative comparison. GRPO’s core design is to sample a group of outputs $\\{o_i\\}_{i=1}^G$ per question and compute advantages from group-normalized rewards, e.g., outcome supervision uses $\\hat{A}_{i,t}=\\widetilde{r}_i=(r_i-\\mathrm{mean}(\\mathbf{r}))/\\mathrm{std}(\\mathbf{r})$ and process supervision uses $\\hat{A}_{i,t}=\\sum_{\\mathrm{index}(j)\\ge t}\\widetilde{r}_i^{\\mathrm{index}(j)}$, explicitly leveraging the comparative nature of reward models trained on pairwise preferences; with $G=1$, $\\mathrm{mean}(\\mathbf{r})=r_1$ and $\\mathrm{std}(\\mathbf{r})=0$, so normalization is undefined and there is no baseline from group scores, eliminating the relative signal that lets GRPO reinforce “better than peers” and penalize “worse than peers.” Consequently, GRPO would lose its variance-reducing baseline and its comparison-driven gradient shaping, collapsing to per-sample reward scaling without the intra-question ranking that the paper highlights as essential for efficient, stable learning aligned with comparative rewards.
