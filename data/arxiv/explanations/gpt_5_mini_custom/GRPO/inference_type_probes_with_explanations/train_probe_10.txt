According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors analyze the role of arXiv papers in their pretraining experiments and describe the experiments carried out to measure their effect on mathematical benchmarks; based on that discussion, removing arXiv entirely from the pretraining mix would most likely result in no notable change. This follows from their explicit finding that arXiv-heavy corpora (e.g., MathPile with over \(85\%\) arXiv and ArXiv-RedPajama with \(28.0\)B tokens) did not improve—and sometimes even degraded—performance when models were trained on them in isolation (DeepSeek-LLM \(1.3\)B for \(150\)B tokens and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 \(7\)B for \(40\)B tokens), as well as from their broader contribution statement that adding arXiv papers brought “no notable improvements” across all mathematical benchmarks they used; since their strongest gains came from carefully filtered Common Crawl math data and code, and arXiv-specific training failed to yield benefits under multiple pipelines and scales, excising arXiv from the mixture would be expected to leave downstream accuracy essentially unchanged, barring the caveats they note about potential unexplored settings (e.g., larger scales or specialized tasks).
