According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', an implicit assumption about the reward source that must hold for GRPO to reliably improve model behavior is that the reward source is a reliable reward model. This follows because GRPO replaces the critic in PPO with group-relative advantages derived directly from reward-model scores, so the per-token gradient coefficient is proportional to a normalized reward signal and drives the update direction via the clipped objective in Equation (3): for each sampled output \(o_i\), GRPO sets \(\hat{A}_{i,t} = \widetilde{r}_i\) (outcome supervision) or \(\hat{A}_{i,t} = \sum_{index(j)\ge t} \widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}\) (process supervision), meaning positive or negative reinforcement entirely hinges on the reward model’s accuracy; moreover, unlike PPO, there is no learned value function to filter or denoise per-token credit assignment, and the KL term \(\beta \mathbb{D}_{KL}[\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{ref}]\) only regularizes step size rather than corrects mis-specified reward signals. The paper’s unified paradigm (Equation (6)) explicitly highlights that current methods “trust” the reward function to shape the gradient, and its ablation notes GRPO’s advantage over Online RFT comes from magnitude-sensitive coefficients—an effect that presupposes reward magnitudes correlate with true solution quality; conversely, the authors caution that noisy or non-generalizing rewards can cause RL to “merely stabilize the distribution of LLMs rather than improve their fundamental capabilities,” motivating iterative reward model updates and future work on robustness to annotation noise and out-of-distribution generalization. Therefore, for GRPO’s group-relative advantages to consistently push the policy toward better reasoning, the reward model must be reliable in both accuracy and generalization.
