According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the iterative domain-expansion step marks a domain as math-related when over \(10\%\) of its pages are collected; the implicit assumption underlying using that heuristic to add other pages from the same domain to the seed corpus is domain homogeneity. This is sensible because the pipeline groups Common Crawl by base URL and targets domains like mathoverflow.net where content is inherently topic-focused, so a threshold of \(>10\%\) math-recalled pages is a conservative signal that a large fraction of the domain is mathematically relevant; moreover, they do not rely on the heuristic alone but (i) manually annotate math-relevant URL paths within those domains (e.g., “/questions”), (ii) add only the remaining pages under those annotated paths to diversify positives, and then (iii) retrain the fastText classifier and continue to filter by predicted scores and keep only top-ranked pages, which counteracts residual heterogeneity within a domain; the observed saturation by the fourth iteration (with \(\sim 98\%\) of data already collected in the third) and the strong downstream performance gains (e.g., DeepSeekMath-Base 7B surpassing larger baselines) further validate that domain-level topical coherence is high enough for this assumption to hold in practice, especially when coupled with manual URL scoping, score-based ranking, and decontamination safeguards.
