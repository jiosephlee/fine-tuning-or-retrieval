According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', under outcome supervision the normalized reward is defined as $\widetilde{r}_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})}$; if all group rewards are identical, the normalization leads to division by zero. This follows directly from their GRPO outcome-supervision setup, where for each question $q$ they sample a group of $G$ outputs $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^G$, score them with a reward model to obtain $\mathbf{r}=\{r_i\}_{i=1}^G$, and then compute per-output advantages by standardizing with the group mean and standard deviation; if all rewards are equal, say $r_i=c$ for all $i$, then $\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})=c$ and $\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_i (r_i-c)^2}=0$, so the defined normalization $\widetilde{r}_i=(r_i-\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r}))/\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})$ attempts to divide by zero; this is a natural edge case of their group-relative design, which relies on between-output variability to produce informative, scale-invariant advantages (and in practice is typically handled by adding a small $\varepsilon$ to the denominator or by noting that zero variance implies no relative signal to reinforce).
