According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', initializing the model from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint such as DeepSeek-Coder would most likely lead to decreased math performance. The authors explicitly report that “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and their ablations show that code training introduces a structured, step-by-step reasoning prior that boosts both tool-free and program-aided mathematical reasoning: in two-stage training, code pre-training followed by math training yields the strongest results, while replacing the first-stage code data with general tokens underperforms; in one-stage training, mixing code and math helps tool-based reasoning and mitigates catastrophic forgetting, but compromises tool-free CoT at small scale, reinforcing the advantage of a code-first initialization that is later specialized with math. Empirically, this choice underpins DeepSeekMath-Base 7B’s superior scores (e.g., 64.2% on GSM8K and 36.2% on MATH, exceeding larger baselines), and even carries over to general reasoning (MMLU, BBH), suggesting that the inductive bias learned from code—precise syntax, algorithmic decomposition, and execution-consistent steps—aligns closely with mathematical problem solving, whereas a general LLM lacks this bias and thus begins math specialization from a weaker foundation.
