In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors mention a plausible scenario where arXiv tokens might still benefit mathematical reasoning: when models are trained at larger model scale. This is sensible because their negative result for arXiv data is obtained at relatively small scales (DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B) and under tasks focused on quantitative problem solving, while they explicitly note capacity limits (e.g., mixing code and math harms tool-free reasoning at 1.3B due to insufficient capacity) and acknowledge untested regimes where benefits may surface; arXiv content is long, formal, and LaTeX-heavy, with high-density mathematical structure that likely demands greater representational capacity and longer-context modeling to extract useful patterns, so increasing model size \(N\) can shift the data–compute–model scaling regime such that signals in arXiv tokens are no longer drowned by noise or optimization difficulty, enabling learning of higher-level proof heuristics and formal reasoning styles that smaller models fail to capture, especially on tasks (e.g., theorem formalization) not emphasized in their current evaluations.
