According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors analyze the role of arXiv papers in their pretraining experiments and describe the experiments carried out to measure their effect on mathematical benchmarks; based on that discussion, removing arXiv entirely from the pretraining mix would most likely result in no notable change. This is because they explicitly report that training on arXiv-heavy corpora—MathPile (with $>85\%$ arXiv) and ArXiv-RedPajama—yields no notable improvements and sometimes degradation across GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, and miniF2F when applied to DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B (trained for $150\mathrm{B}$ tokens) and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B (trained for $40\mathrm{B}$ tokens), while the Common Crawl–mined DeepSeekMath Corpus consistently produces stronger results and steeper learning curves; they also state in their contributions that arXiv brings “no notable improvements” on the adopted mathematical benchmarks, and their corpus-quality analyses show the web-mined data alone surpasses arXiv-inclusive baselines (e.g., outperforming Proof-Pile-2 at $50\mathrm{B}$ tokens), all of which implies that the marginal utility of arXiv tokens in their setting is near-zero and thus excising arXiv from the pretraining mixture would likely leave benchmark performance essentially unchanged within the studied regime.
