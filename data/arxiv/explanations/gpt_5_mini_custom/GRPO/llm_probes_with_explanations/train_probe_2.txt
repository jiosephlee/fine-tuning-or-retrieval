According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', based on the authors' initialization choice, the expected outcome if DeepSeekMath had been initialized from a general LLM instead of a code-trained model is lower math performance. This follows from two pieces of evidence in the paper: (1) the authors explicitly initialize from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B because “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and (2) their controlled ablations show code training confers a consistent advantage for mathematical reasoning, both with and without tool use, over starting from general-language pretraining. In particular, in a two-stage setup, “Code \(400\mathrm{B}\) \(\rightarrow\) Math \(150\mathrm{B}\)” yields stronger results on GSM8K and MATH than “General \(400\mathrm{B}\) \(\rightarrow\) Math \(150\mathrm{B}\),” and in a one-stage setup, including code tokens improves program-aided math and helps retain coding ability, while code training alone already lifts performance prior to math-specific training; these gains reflect the inductive bias from code—precise syntax, long-range dependency tracking, and modular, step-structured reasoning—that aligns with chain-of-thought and program-of-thought styles used in math. The paper also observes that other common pretraining additions like arXiv text do not yield notable improvements, strengthening the conclusion that code pretraining is the key ingredient; hence, replacing the code-initialized base with a general LLM would remove this beneficial prior and predictably depress math scores (e.g., on GSM8K and MATH), consistent with their reported improvements from code-first pretraining and subsequent math tuning.
