According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', if your priority is program-aided math without forgetting, the training recipe that best fits is mixed code and math. The authors’ ablation in “Lessons Learnt in Pre-Training” shows that code training strongly boosts program-aided mathematical reasoning (Python-based PoT on GSM8K/MATH), but a two-stage recipe (code for \(400\mathrm{B}\) tokens \(\rightarrow\) math for \(150\mathrm{B}\) tokens) incurs catastrophic forgetting of coding ability; in contrast, a one-stage mixture of code and math tokens preserves coding skills while still enhancing program-aided math, “effectively mitigat[ing] the issue of catastrophic forgetting” and “synergiz[ing] coding and program-aided mathematical reasoning,” whereas mixing harms only the non-tool (pure CoT) setting at small scale. Thus, for the specific goal of tool-based math performance without erasing coding competence—the tools it relies on—the mixed code+math regimen dominates the two-stage alternative by jointly maintaining code proficiency and delivering strong program-aided math gains.
