### Is GRPO on-policy or off-policy, and how does the 'old policy' interact with the reference policy?
Question:
The algorithm samples outputs from $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$ and uses a reference model $\pi_{ref}$ set to the current policy at the start of an outer iteration. Is this effectively an on-policy method like PPO (since $\theta_{old}$ is close to $\theta$), or is there off-policy behavior when iterative GRPO updates are performed multiple times per batch ($\mu$ steps)? How sensitive is GRPO to policy lag between $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$, $\pi_\theta$, and $\pi_{ref}$?
Answer:
In GRPO, nothing mystical is hiding behind the names “old policy” ($\pi_{t-1}$) and “reference policy” ($\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$)—they play exactly the same roles you already know from PPO—so GRPO is fundamentally an on-policy algorithm, with an extra anchor to keep your policy from drifting too far.

Concretely, at the start of each outer iteration we “freeze” the current policy as  
$$
\pi_{\mathrm{ref}} \leftarrow \pi_{t}
$$
and then we enter the usual loop of sample-and-update:  
1. We copy  
   $$\pi_{\mathrm{old}} \leftarrow \pi_{t}$$  
   (this is the policy that actually generates our trajectories for this mini-batch).  
2. We sample $G$ responses from $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, score them, compute advantages, and then perform $\mu$ gradient steps on exactly that data—using the familiar clipped surrogate objective  
   
   $$
   J_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)
     = \mathbb{E}_{q,\,o_{i}\sim \pi_{\mathrm{old}}}\Bigl[\,
       \min\bigl(\rho_{i,t}\,\hat A_{i,t},\;
                \operatorname{clip}(\rho_{i,t},\,1-\epsilon,\,1+\epsilon)\,\hat A_{i,t}\bigr)
       \;-\;\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})
     \Bigr]
   $$
   
   where  
   $$
   \rho_{i,t} = \frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t})}{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(o_{i,t})}\,.
   $$
3. After $\mu$ steps we move on; when we’ve done $M$ such mini-batches we then optionally update the reward model and begin a fresh outer iteration (resetting $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ again).

Because all our samples come from $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ and our surrogate uses importance-sampling ratios relative to $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, GRPO is on-policy.  If you set $\mu=1$ (as the paper does in DeepSeekMath-RL) you truly never revisit old data with a wildly different policy.  Even if you raised $\mu>1$, the clipping term and the KL penalty to $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ still bound how far $\pi_{\theta}$ can deviate, so you never stray so far that you’re effectively doing unchecked off-policy learning.

In practice the two trust-regions—one per mini-batch via the $\epsilon$-clipping around $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, and one per outer iteration via the $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$—ensure that $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, $\pi_{\theta}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ stay very close.  That small policy lag is exactly what makes GRPO stable.  If you were to let $\mu$ grow large or choose very loose $\epsilon$ or $\beta$, you would introduce more off-policy bias (and likely instability), but the surrogate objective will still correct you via importance ratios.  In short, GRPO is as on-policy as PPO, with an extra global anchor ($\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$) to curb drift and allow very clean single-pass updates.
