### What exactly are the 'group scores'—are they raw reward model outputs or post-processed values?
Question:
GRPO uses 'group scores' to compute normalized advantages. Are these scores the raw outputs of the reward model (e.g., logits, probabilities, scalar regression outputs), or are they transformed (e.g., z-scored globally, clipped, squashed)? If different prompts have different score distributions, does per-group standardization fully correct for that, or is any global normalization also applied?
Answer:
In GRPO the term “group scores” simply refers to the reward-model’s scalar outputs on a batch of $G$ candidate answers to the same prompt, after you z-score them on a per-prompt basis. Concretely, for a given question $q$ you sample $G$ responses $\{o_1,\dots,o_G\}$ under the old policy $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$. You then run your reward model $r_{\phi}$ on each full output to get raw scores $\{r_1,\dots,r_G\}$. These $r_i$ are exactly the values the reward model spits out (for example a preference logit or regression score), with no extra global clipping or squashing yet.

Next you compute  
$$
\mu = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^G r_i,
\qquad
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^G (r_i - \mu)^2},
$$  
and define the *normalized* or *group* score  
$$
\tilde r_i = \frac{r_i - \mu}{\sigma}.
$$

Those standardized $\tilde r_i$ become your per-token advantages in the GRPO surrogate (see § “Outcome Supervision RL with GRPO”), i.e.  
$$
\hat A_{i,t} = \tilde r_i \quad\text{for all }t,
$$  
or in process-supervision you z-score each step-level reward within the same pool and accumulate them.

Crucially, there is no further “global” normalization across different prompts: every question’s candidate set is normalized in isolation. That per-group z-scaling is what corrects for prompt-to-prompt variability in raw $r_i$. No additional cross-prompt clipping, squashing, or moving-average normalization is applied beyond this within-group standardization.
