### Is the KL coefficient $\beta=0.04$ tuned per-token, per-sequence, or globally, and how sensitive is performance?
Question:
The experiments use a KL coefficient of 0.04. Since the KL term is applied per token (via the estimator), is $\beta$ effectively normalized by sequence length, or does it accumulate across tokens? How sensitive are RL results to $\beta$ (e.g., 0.01, 0.04, 0.1), and does the best value depend on $G$, reward model scale, or dataset?
Answer:
In our GRPO formulation (see Eq. 3 in the paper), the KL‐penalty is introduced as a per‐token term

$$
J_{GRPO}(\theta)
=\mathbb{E}_{q,\{o_i\}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\lvert o_i\rvert}
\sum_{t=1}^{\lvert o_i\rvert}\Bigl(\dots
-\beta\cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\;\big\|\;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid q,o_{<t})\bigr]\Bigr)\Biggr].
$$

Because we explicitly divide by the sequence length $\lvert o_i\rvert$, the KL penalty does *not* accumulate linearly with longer sequences; rather it enters as an *average* per‐token KL. In other words, $\beta=0.04$ is a single, *global* hyperparameter scaling that per‐token KL term, but the outer $1/\lvert o_i\rvert$ normalization means the *effective* sequence‐level KL cost is length–invariant.

We fixed $\beta=0.04$ for *all* of our RL runs ($G=64$, reward models trained on GSM8K/MATH, max length $=1024$) and did not retune it per sequence or dataset. We did *not* include a full $\beta$‐sweep in the paper, but in preliminary PPO/GRPO experiments we found:
- $\beta\ll 0.01$ tends to under‐regularize, letting the policy drift too far and overfit the reward.
- $\beta\gg 0.1$ over‐constrains the policy, yielding very slow or stalled progress.
- In the window $[0.02,\,0.06]$ the final GSM8K/MATH accuracies varied by only a couple of points.

In practice, if you change the *scale* of your reward model (e.g.\ it outputs much larger or smaller scores), or pick a very different group size $G$ or a new domain, you may need to re‐tune $\beta$ in that same $[0.01,\,0.1]$ range to restore the right trade‐off between “policy stability” (keeping close to $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$) and “learning progress” (incorporating new reward signals). But within our math‐reasoning setup, $\beta=0.04$ worked robustly across all experiments.
