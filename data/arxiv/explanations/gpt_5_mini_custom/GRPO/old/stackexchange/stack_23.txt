### What is the runtime and memory trade-off of GRPO vs PPO when sampling $G=64$ outputs per prompt?
Question:
GRPO saves memory by not training a critic, but it samples $G$ outputs per question. In practice, with $G=64$, does GRPO have higher or lower overall compute/memory cost than PPO with a value head (or a separate value model) for the same batch of prompts? Any throughput numbers or GPU memory profiles to compare the two under similar hardware?
Answer:
While the DeepSeekMath paper does not include a head-to-head “PPO-vs-GRPO” GPU-memory or tokens-per-second table, you can work out the trade-offs quite cleanly from their descriptions of each algorithm:

1. Memory footprint  
   – PPO (“actor-critic”) needs to carry both the policy network **and** a comparably-sized value (critic) network in GPU memory _and_ to store their activations during back-prop. Roughly speaking, for a $7\,$B-parameter model of depth $L$, you pay $\sim2\times$ the model-size (plus optimizer state) in peak memory.  
   – GRPO throws away the critic entirely. You only ever keep a _single_ $7\,$B policy network—and its activations—so your peak training memory is cut by almost half.

2. Compute (wall-clock) per “RL update”  
   – With PPO you sample **one** trajectory/output per prompt, score it, and then do two back-props (policy + value). In terms of “tokens processed,” that’s $\sim3\times(\text{batch\_size}\times\text{seq\_len})$: one forward for sampling, one forward/back for policy update, one forward/back for value update.  
   – With GRPO (group size $G=64$) you sample 64 rollouts per prompt, score each, and then do _only_ the policy back-prop. Tokens-processed $\approx2\times(G\times\text{batch\_size}\times\text{seq\_len})$: one big forward pass over all $G$ trajectories for sampling, and one for the policy update.

If you take a concrete case—say a $7\,$B model generating $L=512$ tokens per rollout, with $\text{batch\_size}=32$ prompts—then:

  • PPO  
    • Sampling: $32\times512 = 16\,384$ tokens → at $\sim20{,}000$ toks/s inference $\approx0.8\,$s  
    • Training forwards+backs: another $\approx32\,768$ tokens → at $\sim20{,}000$ toks/s training $\approx1.6\,$s  
    • **Total** $\approx2.4\,$s per update, **peak memory** $\approx2\times(\text{model}+\text{activations})\approx40\,$GB on an A100 (for both policy and critic).

  • GRPO ($G=64$)  
    • Sampling: $32\times64\times512 = 1\,048\,576$ tokens → at $20{,}000$ toks/s $\approx52\,$s  
    • Policy update forwards+backs: another $\approx1\,048\,576$ tokens → $\sim52\,$s  
    • **Total** $\approx104\,$s per update, **peak memory** $\approx1\times(\text{model}+\text{activations})\approx20\,$GB on the same A100.

In other words, **GRPO cuts your training-time memory roughly in half** (no critic to store or back-prop through), but you pay a **$\times64$ increase** in generation and policy-update work versus PPO’s single rollout.

That sounds brutal, but in practice you almost never launch all $G=64$ trajectories _in parallel_ if you have only one GPU: you stream them in micro-batches of, say, 4 or 8 at a time, reusing the same activation buffers. That keeps your **peak** memory to roughly one model-fan-out, not $64\times$ more.

Throughput-wise, if your main bottleneck is inference, GRPO will be $\sim G$ times slower per RL “step.” If your bottleneck is the critic back-prop (say you’re very memory-tight and could only do tiny batches), you’ll see a smaller wall-clock gap. In most LLM setups, inference is the slow part, so in our toy numbers above GRPO is $\sim40\times$ slower per update.

Bottom line:  
- **Memory:** GRPO < PPO ($\approx\frac12$ the peak)  
- **Compute:** GRPO > PPO ($\sim G$ times the tokens processed, so $\sim64\times$ more work if you truly do $G=64$)  

Whether that extra time is “worth it” depends on your hardware and how much critic memory you were fighting to squeeze in. If you simply _can’t_ fit a second value network (or you want to leave more room for a big batch), GRPO buys you that head-room at the cost of longer sampling phases.
