According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', an implicit assumption about the reward source that must hold for GRPO to reliably improve model behavior is that the reward source is a reliable reward model. This is because GRPO replaces the critic with group-relative advantages computed from reward scores, so the sign and magnitude of the policy update are entirely determined by the reward model’s rankings and values. Concretely, GRPO sets token-wise advantages from outcome supervision as \( \hat{A}_{i,t} = \widetilde{r}_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})} \) and then maximizes the clipped objective in Equation (3), with a KL regularizer against a reference model. If the reward model is unreliable (e.g., assigns high scores to incorrect or low-quality reasoning), then the normalized advantages will reinforce those erroneous trajectories, since the gradient coefficient \( \hat{A}_{i,t} \) directly scales \( \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q, o_{i,<t}) \) in Equation (4). The paper’s unified paradigm explicitly notes that all these methods “TRUST” the reward function, and later highlights the need for reward models that generalize and reflect uncertainty; they even point out that a carefully annotated process dataset like PRM800K contains about 20% incorrect annotations, underscoring the sensitivity of RL updates to reward noise.

Moreover, the comparative design of GRPO intensifies this dependence: rewards are normalized within a group, so the policy update depends on the relative ordering of \( \{r_i\}_{i=1}^G \) for the same question. For GRPO to steer the model toward better behavior, the implicit condition \( \mathbb{E}[r \mid \text{correct}] > \mathbb{E}[r \mid \text{incorrect}] \) must hold with sufficient margin and consistency, both for outcome supervision and process supervision (where \( \hat{A}_{i,t} = \sum_{index(j)\ge t} \widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)} \)). Otherwise, group-relative normalization can still produce large positive advantages for flawed reasoning if the reward model mis-ranks steps or outputs. The KL term \( -\beta \mathbb{D}_{KL}[\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}] \) constrains drift but cannot correct biased advantages. The paper’s empirical findings—GRPO outperforming Online RFT because it “uniquely adjusts its gradient coefficient based on the reward value,” and iterative GRPO improving further after updating reward models—are sensible precisely under the assumption that the reward source provides reliably aligned signals. When that assumption weakens, the updates risk stabilizing distributional properties (e.g., better Maj@K) without improving fundamental correctness, a behavior the authors observe and explicitly connect to reward quality and alignment.
