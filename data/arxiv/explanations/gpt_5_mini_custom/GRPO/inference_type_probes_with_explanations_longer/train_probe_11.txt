According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', if GRPO is run with group size $G=1$, the key advantage described in the paper that would be lost is relative comparison. GRPO’s core idea is to compute advantages from rewards normalized within a group of $G$ outputs sampled for the same question, using the group mean as a baseline and the group standard deviation for scale: $\widetilde{r}_i=\frac{r_i-\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})}$. This “group-relative” construction both removes the need for a learned critic and explicitly aligns with preference-style reward models that are trained on comparisons between multiple responses to the same prompt. When $G=1$, we have $\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})=r_1$ and $\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})=0$, so $\widetilde{r}_1$ becomes undefined and, in practice, the advantage collapses to a non-comparative, absolute signal. Thus, the variance-reducing baseline and the comparative calibration that let GRPO modulate gradient strength across better and worse samples disappear, breaking the mechanism the paper emphasizes as central.

This loss of relative comparison affects both outcome and process supervision in GRPO. For outcome supervision, the per-token advantage is set to the normalized terminal reward, $\hat{A}_{i,t}=\widetilde{r}_i$, which depends on differences across the group; with $G=1$ there are no differences to compute. For process supervision, the stepwise advantages sum normalized step rewards, $\hat{A}_{i,t}=\sum_{index(j)\ge t}\widetilde{r}_i^{index(j)}$, again requiring a nonzero $\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{R})$ across the set of step scores aggregated over the group. With $G=1$, normalization degenerates, eliminating the fine-grained, step-aware comparative signal. More broadly, GRPO’s stated benefits—obviating a critic by using group baselines, aligning with comparative reward modeling, and producing differential reinforcement magnitudes via intra-group ranking—rely on $G>1$. Setting $G=1$ removes the comparative structure central to GRPO, leaving only an absolute reward term plus the KL regularizer, thereby forfeiting the paper’s key advantage of relative comparison.
