In the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', GRPO was applied using only a subset of English instruction tuning data yet improved a Chinese benchmark (\(\text{CMATH}\)), demonstrating cross-lingual transfer. This is sensible because the underlying reasoning skills optimized by GRPO are largely language-agnostic: the algorithm reinforces solution trajectories that yield higher comparative rewards within groups and regularizes the policy via a KL term \(\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi_\theta \Vert \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}]\), which primarily tunes the model’s stepwise decision policy rather than its surface-level lexical choices. Since mathematical reasoning relies on shared symbolic tokens (e.g., numerals, operators, equations), program-of-thought patterns, and procedural chains (e.g., “compute,” “substitute,” “simplify”) that transfer across languages, the improvements achieved on English CoT-style problems carry over to Chinese tasks. The paper explicitly reports out-of-domain gains during RL, including \(\text{CMATH}\) (\(84.6\%\rightarrow 88.8\%\)), even though the GRPO training set is English-only, consistent with cross-lingual transfer of reasoning strategies.

Moreover, the model’s pre-training context enables such transfer: DeepSeekMath-Base was trained on a multilingual corpus where English and Chinese are the most represented languages, so the policy already possesses Chinese linguistic competence. GRPO then refines the policy’s reasoning robustness—empirically, it improves \(\mathrm{Maj@K}\) but not \(\mathrm{Pass@K}\)—suggesting the RL shift makes correct trajectories more reliably selected among sampled candidates rather than teaching new facts specific to English. Because GRPO uses group-relative advantage \(\hat{A}_{i,t}\) computed from normalized rewards \(\widetilde{r}_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r})}{\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})}\) (and in process supervision \(\hat{A}_{i,t}=\sum_{j:\, \mathrm{index}(j)\ge t}\widetilde{r}_i^{\mathrm{index}(j)}\)), it amplifies generally good reasoning steps regardless of language. Combined with KL regularization and a multilingual foundation, these policy refinements propagate to Chinese benchmarks like \(\text{CMATH}\), thus demonstrating cross-lingual transfer from English-only RL.
