According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the authors analyze the role of arXiv papers in their pretraining experiments and describe the experiments carried out to measure their effect on mathematical benchmarks; based on that discussion, removing arXiv entirely from the pretraining mix would most likely result in no notable change. The paper reports targeted ablations in which models were trained on arXiv-heavy corpora—MathPile (over 85% arXiv, \(8.9\mathrm{B}\) tokens) and ArXiv-RedPajama (\(28.0\mathrm{B}\) tokens)—and finds “no notable improvements or even deterioration” on a range of math evaluations (GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, miniF2F), both for a \(1.3\mathrm{B}\) model trained for \(150\mathrm{B}\) tokens and for a \(7\mathrm{B}\) model trained for \(40\mathrm{B}\) tokens. Consistently, in their main 7B pipeline, adding arXiv (\(\approx 10\%\) of the \(500\mathrm{B}\)-token mix) is explicitly noted to “bring no notable improvements on all mathematical benchmarks adopted in this paper.” In contrast, the Common Crawl–mined DeepSeekMath Corpus (\(120\mathrm{B}\) math tokens), multilingual web math, and code data deliver clear gains, with steeper learning curves and better downstream performance than arXiv-centric baselines at matched token budgets.

This pattern makes it sensible that setting the arXiv fraction to \(0\%\) would have negligible impact in the reported regime: the benchmarks emphasize step-by-step quantitative problem solving, program-aided reasoning, and short-form derivations, which are better aligned with curated web math pages, StackExchange-style content, and code than with formal, long-form scholarly articles. The authors’ data selection pipeline, fastText filtering, and multilingual coverage produce higher average utility per token for the target tasks, as evidenced by the model trained on the DeepSeekMath Corpus outperforming Proof-Pile-2 even at \(50\mathrm{B}\) tokens. While the paper acknowledges caveats (e.g., arXiv might help other tasks, different mixtures, or larger scales), under the configurations tested—model sizes, objectives, and benchmarks—arXiv contributes little signal relative to web math and code, so removing it would most likely yield no notable change in accuracy.
