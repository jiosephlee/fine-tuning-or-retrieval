According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', initializing the model from a general LLM rather than from a code-initialized checkpoint such as DeepSeek-Coder would most likely lead to decreased math performance. The authors explicitly report that “starting from a code training model is a better choice compared to a general LLM,” and their ablations support this: code pretraining imparts structured, algorithmic priors that translate into stronger mathematical reasoning both with and without tools. In two-stage settings, beginning with code training for $400\mathrm{B}$ tokens followed by math training for $150\mathrm{B}$ tokens yields substantial gains in program-aided math (e.g., Python-based solutions on GSM8K and MATH) and further improves purely text-based chain-of-thought reasoning. Even in one-stage settings, mixing code and math tokens mitigates catastrophic forgetting and synergizes tool-use performance, whereas training on math alone lacks these benefits. This pattern is consistent with the observation that DeepSeekMath-Base, initialized from DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 $7\mathrm{B}$, surpasses general-LM baselines (e.g., Mistral $7\mathrm{B}$) by a wide margin on MATH and other math benchmarks.

The mechanism the paper suggests is that code pretraining teaches the model to manipulate symbolic structures, follow precise syntactic constraints, and execute stepwise logical procedures—skills that align closely with mathematical reasoning and program-of-thought workflows. Moreover, code-first initialization appears to make subsequent math training more sample-efficient: the initial code-trained representation accelerates learning of mathematical patterns and reduces the risk of the model overfitting to noisy or less helpful sources (e.g., arXiv-only corpora, which the paper finds ineffective). As further evidence of transfer, DeepSeekMath-Base shows improvements on general reasoning (MMLU, BBH) after math training without losing coding ability (HumanEval, MBPP), indicating that the code-initialized backbone provides a robust foundation for both tool-integrated and text-only math solving. Taken together, these results make it sensible that starting from a general LLM—lacking these code-derived priors—would most likely reduce downstream math performance under the same training budget.
