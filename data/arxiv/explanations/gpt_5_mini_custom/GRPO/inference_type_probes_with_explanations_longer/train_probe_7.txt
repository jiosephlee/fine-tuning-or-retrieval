According to the paper 'DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models', the iterative domain-expansion step marks a domain as math-related when over 10% of its pages are collected; the implicit assumption underlying using that heuristic to add other pages from the same domain to the seed corpus is domain homogeneity. This is sensible given how the pipeline groups Common Crawl into domains by base URL and then computes, for each domain, the proportion p of pages already recalled by the math classifier; when p > 0.1, the prior that uncollected pages from that domain contain math rises substantially relative to random CC pages. In web corpora, content within a base domain tends to be correlated (e.g., domains like mathoverflow.net or arXiv subdomains), so treating the domain as approximately homogeneous is a practical way to increase recall. The authors further constrain this assumption by manually annotating math-relevant URL paths within those domains (e.g., “/questions”), so they do not indiscriminately add all remaining pages, but rather focus on subpaths that are more likely to be math-heavy. This combination—domain-level thresholding and URL-path annotation—operationalizes a “weak homogeneity” prior: pages under the same domain and annotated subpaths are more likely, on average, to be mathematical.

Moreover, the pipeline’s iterative nature and subsequent filtering mitigate the risks of within-domain heterogeneity. After augmenting the seed with newly added positive instances, the fastText classifier is retrained and then used to re-rank pages, with only top-scoring pages kept; decontamination and deduplication further prune noise. The fact that, by the fourth iteration, nearly 98% of the data had already been collected in the third iteration suggests convergence, indicating that the homogeneity-based expansion effectively surfaced the remaining math-rich regions of domains. Empirically, models trained on this corpus outperform those trained on alternative math corpora, and exhibit strong performance across multilingual benchmarks, which supports the validity of the heuristic: even if domains are not perfectly homogeneous, the p > 0.1 threshold coupled with path-level curation and score-based selection yields a sufficiently high-precision, high-recall expansion strategy.
