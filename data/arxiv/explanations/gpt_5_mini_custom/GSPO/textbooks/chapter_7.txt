\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "Group Sequence Policy Optimization"}

Chapter 7: GSPO-token: A Flexible Variant for Token-wise Advantages

# Motivation: finer-grained credit assignment without reintroducing token-level ratios
GSPO was motivated by the need to align the unit of importance correction with the unit of reward: since rewards are given at the sequence level, GSPO applies importance weighting and clipping at the sequence level. However, there are practical settings where a purely sequence-level advantage is too coarse. Examples include multi-turn RL or structured generation tasks where different parts of a response (different turns, steps, or subtasks) deserve different credit (or blame). In such cases we want the flexibility to shape advantages at the token level, so that some tokens can receive larger positive or negative signals than others.

At the same time, we must avoid reintroducing the core instability that motivated GSPO in the first place. GRPO applied token-level importance ratios
\[
w_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_{i,t} | x,y_{i,<t})},
\]
and this token-wise reweighting (based on single-sample next-token probabilities) was shown to produce high-variance noise that accumulates across long sequences and can lead to catastrophic collapse. The key desideratum for a token-wise variant is therefore: allow per-token advantage shaping, but keep the stable, sequence-level importance weighting that makes GSPO robust.

GSPO-token answers this need. It preserves GSPO's sequence-level importance ratio and clipping logic, but provides a construction that permits distinct advantages for individual tokens. The central idea is to build a per-token factor that is numerically equal to the sequence-level ratio but, by using stop-gradient (detach), prevents token-level likelihoods from becoming independent importance weights in the optimization graph. This maintains the stability of sequence-level weighting while granting the modeler token-by-token control over advantage values.

# Construction of s_{i,t} using stop-gradient (detach) to keep sequence-level weighting
GSPO-token defines a token-level factor $s_{i,t}(\theta)$ in a way that is numerically equal to the sequence-level importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ but whose gradients are structured so that the sequence-level ratio is the true multiplicative weight in optimization. The objective of GSPO-token is:

\[
\mathcal{J}_\text{GSPO-token}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{ x \sim \mathcal{D},\, \{y_i\}_{i=1}^G \sim \pi_{\theta_\text{old}}( \cdot | x) }
\left[ 
\frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^{G} \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} 
\min \left( s_{i,t}(\theta) \widehat{A}_{i,t},  \, \mathrm{clip} \left( s_{i,t}(\theta), 1 - {\varepsilon}, 1 + {\varepsilon} \right) \widehat{A}_{i,t} \right) 
\right].
\]

The per-token factor is constructed as

\[
s_{i,t}(\theta) 
= \mathrm{sg} \left[ s_{i}(\theta) \right]  \cdot \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) }{ \mathrm{sg} \left[ \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t}) \right] },
\]

where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes stopping the gradient (the \texttt{detach} operation in practice). Here $s_i(\theta)$ is the length-normalized sequence-level importance ratio used in GSPO:

\[
s_{i}(\theta) = \left( \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_i | x) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}}.
\]

Intuitively, $\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]$ is treated as a constant multiplier in the computational graph, so it will not receive gradients. The fraction involving the token probability has a detached denominator, so it evaluates numerically to 1 (because the numerator and the detached denominator have identical numerical values at the forward pass), but still carries the raw parameter dependence in the numerator for gradient flow. This combination ensures that the per-token numerical factor equals the sequence-level factor while steering the gradient algebra toward the sequence-level weight times token log-probability gradients. The paper shows this explicitly in the gradient derivation below.

# Numerical equivalence to GSPO when all token advantages are equal
A desirable property of GSPO-token is that it reduces to GSPO when no finer granularity is needed. If we choose the per-token advantages to be identical within a response, i.e., $\widehat{A}_{i,t} = \widehat{A}_{i}$ for all $t$ in response $i$, then GSPO-token is numerically identical to GSPO: the objective values, the clipping conditions, and the theoretical gradients match.

The paper derives the gradient of GSPO-token (omitting clipping for clarity) and arrives at

\[
\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{J}_\text{GSPO-token}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{ x, \{y_i\} }
\left[ 
\frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^{G}  \left( \frac{ \pi_{\theta} (y_i | x) }{ \pi_{\theta_\text{old}} (y_i | x)} \right)^{\frac{1}{|y_i|}}
\cdot \frac{1}{|y_i|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}
\widehat{A}_{i,t} \nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta} (y_{i,t} | x, y_{i,<t})  
\right].
\]

Because $s_{i,t}(\theta)$ is numerically equal to $s_i(\theta)$, if every $\widehat{A}_{i,t}$ equals the same $\widehat{A}_i$, then the inner token-sum factor becomes $\widehat{A}_i \cdot \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t}\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}|\cdot)$, which is exactly the token gradient contribution in the GSPO gradient expression. Thus GSPO-token collapses to GSPO when token advantages are uniform, guaranteeing backward compatibility of the two objectives.

# When to prefer GSPO-token (e.g., multi-turn RL, partial credit signals)
GSPO-token is most useful when you need finer-grained credit assignment than a single scalar per response can provide. Typical scenarios include:

- Multi-turn dialogues or multi-step reasoning, where different turns or steps should be encouraged or discouraged differently. For example, you may want to reward correctness in later reasoning steps more heavily than verbosity in earlier steps, or penalize specific tokens that leak private data.
- Structured tasks where parts of the generated sequence correspond to distinct subtasks or outputs with separate evaluative signals. Token-level advantages allow injecting such partial-credit signals directly into the policy optimization.
- Partial feedback settings where scores are available for subcomponents of the response (e.g., per-turn rubric scores), enabling targeted shaping of learning dynamics without abandoning GSPO’s stable sequence-level weighting.

At the same time, if the task naturally provides only a single scalar reward per response and you do not need token-level shaping, standard GSPO is simpler and sufficient. Since GSPO-token reduces to GSPO when token advantages are equal, choosing GSPO-token preserves the option to refine advantages later without changing the underlying importance-weighting mechanism.

# Implementation details: detach semantics and stable optimization
Implementing GSPO-token in practice follows the mathematical construction: compute the sequence-level length-normalized importance ratio $s_i(\theta)$ as in GSPO, then create detached versions for use in the per-token factors.

Concretely (described in the paper text rather than as code), you compute the sequence likelihoods $\pi_{\theta}(y_i | x)$ and $\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_i | x)$ and set

\[
s_i(\theta) = \left(\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_i|x)}{\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(y_i|x)}\right)^{1/|y_i|},
\]

then form a detached copy $\mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)]$ (the forward numerical value with no gradient). For each token you also form a detached copy of the token probability $\mathrm{sg}[\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} | x,y_{i,<t})]$. The per-token factor is then

\[
s_{i,t}(\theta) = \mathrm{sg}[s_i(\theta)] \cdot \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} | x,y_{i,<t})}{\mathrm{sg}[\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t} | x,y_{i,<t})]}.
\]

Because the denominator is detached, the ratio evaluates to 1 numerically on the forward pass, and the detached sequence factor supplies the intended numerical sequence weight. Gradient flow is arranged so that the effective multiplicative factor that appears in the gradient is $s_i(\theta)$ multiplying the token log-probability gradients, as shown in the gradient derivation reproduced above:

\[
\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{J}_\text{GSPO-token}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}\left[
\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^G s_i(\theta)\cdot\frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|}\widehat{A}_{i,t}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})
\right].
\]

This structure is crucial: it prevents the optimizer from treating each token's importance ratio as an independent reweighting of its contribution (the behavior that caused high variance in GRPO) while still letting different tokens have different advantages $\widehat{A}_{i,t}$.

In typical deep learning frameworks, the stop-gradient operation is implemented as a detach (for example, \texttt{tensor.detach()} in PyTorch). The paper explicitly notes that $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ corresponds to such a detach operation. Practically, one must ensure that both the sequence-level scalar and the per-token detached denominators are created via detach so that gradients do not flow through these detached values.

Finally, clipping and normalization behave the same as in GSPO: the $s_{i,t}$ values are clipped to the configured range (e.g., $[1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon]$) in the objective, and group-based advantage normalization is applied per response group. Because $s_{i,t}$ is numerically equal to $s_i$, clipping has the same numerical effect as sequence-level clipping, preserving GSPO’s robust exclusion of overly off-policy samples.

# Summary
GSPO-token provides a principled way to obtain token-level advantage flexibility while retaining GSPO’s key stability property: sequence-level importance weighting and clipping. Its construction uses stop-gradient (detach) to make per-token factors numerically match the sequence-level ratio but to route gradients so that the sequence-level ratio, not independent token-level ratios, weights the log-probability gradients. When per-token advantages are uniform, GSPO-token is numerically equivalent to GSPO; when they differ, GSPO-token enables finer control for multi-turn or structured tasks without reintroducing the high-variance instabilities observed with token-level importance ratios in GRPO.
