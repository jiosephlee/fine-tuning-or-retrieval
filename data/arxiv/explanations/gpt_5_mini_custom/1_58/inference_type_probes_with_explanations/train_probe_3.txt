In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the ability to set weights to zero is analogous to the common model compression concept sparsity. This is because BitNet b1.58 constrains every weight to the ternary set $\{-1,0,1\}$ via an absmean quantization $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)$ with $\gamma=\tfrac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, so any weight whose magnitude falls below the rounding threshold is mapped to exactly $0$, thereby removing that connection’s contribution during the linear transform just as in sparse pruning; the authors explicitly note that the added $0$ enables “feature filtering,” i.e., selectively suppressing channels, which is the functional role of sparsity in compression. As with sparse matrices, zero entries in $\widetilde{W}$ eliminate multiply-accumulate work and bandwidth for those positions, aligning with their reported savings in arithmetic energy (replacing FP16 MACs with primarily INT8 additions) and reduced DRAM-to-SRAM transfer, and at inference time these zeros can be exploited by BitLinear kernels much like sparse matmul to cut effective compute and memory traffic, all while preserving accuracy at scale—mirroring the traditional sparsity-compression tradeoff but achieved natively through ternary quantization rather than post hoc pruning.
