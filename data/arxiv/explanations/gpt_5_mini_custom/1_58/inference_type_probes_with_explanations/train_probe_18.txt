According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', if the average absolute weight $\gamma$ becomes larger, the proportion of weights quantized to zero after $\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$ increases. This follows directly from the absmean quantization rule: an element $W_{ij}$ is mapped to $0$ precisely when $\left|\frac{W_{ij}}{\gamma+\epsilon}\right|<\tfrac{1}{2}$ (since rounding to the nearest integer maps values in $(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})$ to $0$), i.e., when $|W_{ij}|<\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon)$; for a fixed weight matrix $W$, increasing $\gamma$ expands the interval $(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon),\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma+\epsilon))$, so a larger fraction of entries satisfy this inequality and are rounded to zero, while fewer exceed the $\tfrac{1}{2}$ threshold needed to round to $\pm 1$ (and even fewer exceed $1.5$ in normalized magnitude where clipping to $\pm 1$ would occur), thereby increasing the sparsity (zeros) induced by the ternary quantization.
