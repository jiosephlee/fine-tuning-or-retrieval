According to the paper “The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits,” for the claimed energy and speed advantages to actually occur, the implicit hardware property that must hold is that integer operations are cheaper. This follows from the work’s core premise that BitNet b1.58 replaces FP16 multiply–accumulate in matrix multiplications with predominantly low-precision integer additions, so the per-op energy hierarchy must satisfy E(INT8 add) ≪ E(FP16 add) ≪ E(FP16 mul), as assumed by the cited energy models and quantified by their estimate of a 71.4× reduction in arithmetic energy on 7 nm for matmuls; because LLMs are dominated by nn.Linear, whose cost scales with the number of multiply–accumulate operations, reducing each MAC to essentially sign/zero gating and integer addition yields a proportional drop in arithmetic energy. Moreover, the paper explicitly ties speed to energy via the power limit on chips: when power is the bottleneck, lowering energy per operation increases sustainable operations per second, so if integer ops were not cheaper than FP ops, the claimed latency and throughput gains would not materialize even with fewer-bit weights. In short, the reported advantages rely on the hardware reality that replacing FP16 multiplications/additions with low-bit integer additions decreases per-op energy (and thus power) enough that, given the O(nmk) arithmetic intensity of Transformer matmuls, overall energy and runtime improve as observed.
