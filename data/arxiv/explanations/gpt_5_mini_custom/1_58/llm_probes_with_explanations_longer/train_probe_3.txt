In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the hardware constraint cited as the link that allows energy savings to become execution speed improvements is the power limit. The text explicitly notes that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” and argues that by replacing FP16 additions and multiplications with predominantly INT8 additions, BitNet b1.58 reduces the energy per arithmetic operation by large factors (e.g., the paper estimates a 71.4× reduction for matrix multiplication on 7nm chips). Under a fixed chip power budget (TDP or runtime power cap), lowering the energy per operation allows proportionally more operations per unit time without exceeding the power ceiling. Formally, if the operations rate is \(R\) (ops/s), the average energy per operation is \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\), and the available power is \(P\), then \(P \approx R \cdot E_{\mathrm{op}}\). Holding \(P\) fixed by the hardware limit, reducing \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\) implies \(R = P / E_{\mathrm{op}}\) can increase, yielding higher throughput and lower latency.

This mechanism is directly aligned with BitNet b1.58’s computation paradigm: matrix multiplications become integer-add dominated, which have much lower dynamic energy than FP16 multiply-accumulate. Because the dominant cost in LLMs is the \(\mathrm{nn.Linear}\) blocks (matrix multiplications), the aggregate energy reduction compounds across layers; as model size grows, the fraction of compute in these blocks increases, so the benefit scales. In practical terms, chips constrained by power and thermal limits can clock or schedule more integer-add operations within the same power envelope, translating energy savings into speedups. The paper’s empirical observations—greater speedups at larger model sizes and end-to-end throughput gains—are consistent with this power-limited performance model, where decreasing \(E_{\mathrm{op}}\) for core arithmetic shifts the power-performance frontier and enables faster execution without exceeding the hardware’s power cap.
