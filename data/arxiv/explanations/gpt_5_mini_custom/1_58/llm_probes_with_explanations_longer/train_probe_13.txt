According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', for the energy savings of BitNet b1.58 to reliably convert into higher speed, the chip-level constraint that must hold is the power limit. The paper explicitly notes that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” and then argues that the large energy reductions achieved by replacing FP16 matrix multiplications with mostly INT8 additions in BitNet b1.58 can be “translated into faster computation.” Formally, if performance is power-limited, throughput is governed by the relation P = E_{\mathrm{op}} \cdot R, where P is the available power budget, E_{\mathrm{op}} is the energy per operation, and R is the operation rate (ops/s). Holding P fixed (the power ceiling imposed by thermal and power delivery constraints), reducing E_{\mathrm{op}} by using cheaper integer additions increases R as R = P / E_{\mathrm{op}}. Because BitNet b1.58 lowers E_{\mathrm{op}} dramatically (the paper cites a 71.4× arithmetic energy reduction for matrix multiplications on 7 nm), the achievable ops/s—and thus inference speed—rises correspondingly under a power cap.

This logic hinges on power, rather than other bottlenecks, being the active constraint. If, for example, performance were limited by memory bandwidth or by fixed pipeline latencies, lowering E_{\mathrm{op}} might not increase R. The paper addresses these additional system costs (e.g., DRAM-to-SRAM transfers) and shows that BitNet b1.58 also reduces memory capacity and bandwidth demands, which helps alleviate non-power bottlenecks. But the guarantee that energy savings become speedups follows directly from the power-limited regime: when P is the binding constraint, any reduction in per-operation energy permits a proportional increase in operation rate without violating P, enabling higher clock utilization or more parallel functional units to be active concurrently. Hence, under a chip-level power limit, BitNet b1.58’s energy-efficient arithmetic reliably converts into higher throughput and lower latency.
