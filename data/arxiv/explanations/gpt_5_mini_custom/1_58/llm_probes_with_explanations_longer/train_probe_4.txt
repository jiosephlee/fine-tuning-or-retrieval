According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', to achieve the performance parity claimed for BitNet b1.58, the implicitly preferred training approach over post-training quantization is quantization-aware training. The paper explicitly argues that post-training quantization is “sub-optimal” for LLMs and, in contrast, presents BitNet b1.58 as a model trained from scratch with low-bit constraints baked into the architecture. Concretely, all linear layers are replaced with BitLinear, weights are constrained to ternary values \( \{-1, 0, 1\} \), and activations are quantized to 8-bit throughout training. The weight quantization is defined via an absmean scaling and rounding function,
\[
\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right), \quad \gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|,
\]
which is applied in the model’s learning loop rather than retrofitted after full-precision training. Likewise, activations are consistently scaled to \([ -Q_b, Q_b ]\) per token to avoid zero-point quantization, indicating that the network optimizes under the same quantization regime it will use at inference. This is the essence of quantization-aware training (QAT): the optimization process “sees” the quantization effects and adapts parameters and layer dynamics accordingly, which is known to yield higher parity with full-precision baselines than post-training quantization (PTQ).

This design choice directly explains the reported parity with FP16 models starting at the 3B scale in both perplexity and end-task accuracy. BitNet b1.58’s modeling capacity is strengthened by the inclusion of the zero weight in \( \{-1,0,1\} \), enabling explicit feature filtering during training—an ability a PTQ pipeline cannot induce if weights are learned in full precision and only rounded post hoc. Because training is conducted under the ternary constraint and the 8-bit activation scaling policy, the optimizer learns to exploit the discrete support and avoid distributional pathologies that typically emerge when floating-point weights are abruptly quantized after training. The paper’s empirical findings—matching FP16 perplexity and accuracy at 3B, and surpassing FP16 3B with a 3.9B BitNet b1.58 model under identical data and configuration—are consistent with QAT being necessary for parity at such low precision, while PTQ is acknowledged as a convenient but sub-optimal alternative for industry inference.
