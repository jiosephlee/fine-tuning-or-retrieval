In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the specific reduction in arithmetic operations that most directly drives the large matrix-multiplication energy savings is fewer $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications. The authors emphasize that the dominant computational cost in Transformer LLMs comes from $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ layers, i.e., matrix multiplications of the form $C = A B$, which in full precision require computing products $A_{ik} \cdot B_{kj}$ and accumulating them. In FP16 baselines, this entails both $\mathrm{FP16}$ additions and $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications, and the energy model they cite shows that $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications are substantially more energy-expensive than additions. By contrast, BitNet b1.58 constrains weights $B_{kj} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ via the absmean quantizer and uses 8-bit activations; as a result, the product $A_{ik} \cdot B_{kj}$ reduces to either $A_{ik}$, $-A_{ik}$, or $0$, which can be implemented with integer additions/subtractions and conditional skipping rather than floating-point multiplications. This substitution eliminates the large number of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications in the core matmul, replacing them with far cheaper integer additions, which the paper identifies as the primary source of the 71.4× arithmetic energy savings on 7nm chips.

Formally, with ternary weights, the inner product becomes $\sum_{k} A_{ik} B_{kj} = \sum_{k: B_{kj}=1} A_{ik} - \sum_{k: B_{kj}=-1} A_{ik}$, while indices with $B_{kj}=0$ are skipped, so no multiply is performed at any $k$. Because the proportion of compute spent in $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ grows with model size, the benefit from removing $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications scales with parameter count, explaining the increasing energy efficiency gap as models grow (e.g., up to 4.1× latency speed-ups and large end-to-end energy reductions reported at 70B). The paper also notes that activations are quantized to $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ in 8 bits, so arithmetic inside matmuls is dominated by $\mathrm{INT8}$ additions. Taken together, the architecture’s shift from $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiply–accumulate to mostly integer accumulate with sign/zero handling is precisely the mechanism that most directly drives the matrix-multiplication energy savings.
