According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', to achieve parity with FP16 baselines, BitNet b1.58 is assumed to have the same model size and token count. This parity condition reflects the experimental design and the implicit scaling-law framing used throughout the paper: models are trained from scratch under matched configurations (e.g., LLaMA-alike architecture, identical pretraining corpus, and equal training tokens), and performance is compared at fixed parameter counts. Concretely, the results section repeatedly controls for both the number of parameters and the number of training tokens (e.g., 100B tokens for the core comparisons), and finds that BitNet b1.58 “starts to match” FP16 performance at 3B parameters when using the same configuration. This is consistent with scaling-law intuition that loss or downstream accuracy depends primarily on the pair \((N, T)\), where \(N\) is the parameter count and \(T\) is the total tokens, and that changing numerical precision mainly affects compute and memory, not the achievable optimum under fixed \((N, T)\) and training recipe.

Moreover, the paper’s proposed quantization and training recipe are designed to preserve representational capacity at a given model size while improving efficiency. Weights are constrained to \(\{-1, 0, +1\}\) via the absmean scaling \(\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1)\) with \(\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|\), and activations are maintained at 8 bits with symmetric per-token scaling to avoid zero-point bias. The inclusion of the explicit zero weight enables feature filtering, improving the effective function class at fixed \(N\), which the authors argue narrows the gap with FP16 as \(N\) grows. Empirically, perplexity on WikiText2/C4 and zero-shot accuracy on multiple tasks show parity beginning at \(N\approx 3\text{B}\) when \(T\) is held equal, while delivering large gains in latency, memory, and energy. Thus, assuming equal model size \(N\) and token count \(T\) is both methodologically necessary—to isolate the effect of precision—and supported by the evidence that BitNet b1.58 attains similar optima under matched \((N, T)\), with the main differences appearing in system-level cost rather than capability.
