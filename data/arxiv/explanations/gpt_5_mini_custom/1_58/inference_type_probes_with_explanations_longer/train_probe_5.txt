According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', for the claimed energy and speed advantages to actually occur, the implicit hardware property that must hold is that integer operations are cheaper. The paper’s core computational change is to replace FP16/BF16 matrix multiplications in Transformer layers with ternary-weight integer accumulations, so the dominant arithmetic becomes INT8 additions rather than FP16 additions and multiplications. Their energy analysis (citing chip-level models on 7nm) explicitly assumes that the per-operation energy of INT8 addition is far lower than FP16 multiplication and addition, yielding a reported 71.4× reduction in arithmetic energy for matrix multiply. Formally, if the arithmetic energy per operation satisfies E_int8_add ≪ E_fp16_add and E_int8_add ≪ E_fp16_mul, then for a matrix product with N operations, the energy scales as E_bitnet ≈ N·E_int8_add versus E_fp16 ≈ N·(α·E_fp16_mul + β·E_fp16_add), implying E_bitnet/E_fp16 ≪ 1 under typical α, β. Because the “fundamental limit” they highlight is power, lower energy per op directly translates into higher sustainable throughput: throughput ≈ P_budget / E_op, so replacing FP16 ops with cheaper INT ops increases tokens/sec at a fixed power envelope.

The same hardware assumption underpins the latency claims via both compute and IO. First, when the bulk of inference time in large models is spent in nn.Linear, which grows with width, switching those kernels to integer addition accelerates them only if the hardware delivers higher ops/s for integer arithmetic (i.e., t_int8_op < t_fp16_op). Second, the paper’s memory and bandwidth advantages rely on reduced bitwidth: loading ternary weights (effectively 1.58 bits per parameter) from DRAM cuts transfer volume by a factor proportional to bitwidth, so the time to move weights scales as T_load ∝ (\text{bits/param})/BW. This IO speedup compounds the compute speedup only if the hardware treats low-bit integer data paths as cheaper in energy and faster in bandwidth than FP16. Together, these assumptions—E_int ≪ E_fp, t_int ≪ t_fp, and lower-cost low-bit memory movement—are the implicit hardware properties that make the reported 2.4–4.1× latency gains and large energy savings plausible as model size increases.
