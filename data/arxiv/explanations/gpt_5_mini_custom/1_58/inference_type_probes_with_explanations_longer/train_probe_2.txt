According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the on-chip memory component that could therefore be reduced in size to lower hardware cost while maintaining throughput is SRAM. The paper explains that a major inference bottleneck is shuttling weights from off-chip DRAM into an on-chip accelerator’s fast memory (e.g., SRAM) to feed matrix multiplications. Prior systems have enlarged SRAM to cache more weights and raise throughput, but this comes at “significantly higher cost than DRAM.” BitNet b1.58 cuts the weight precision from FP16 (\(16\) bits) to ternary (\(\{-1,0,1\}\)) at effectively \(1.58\) bits per parameter, reducing weight traffic by approximately \(16/1.58 \approx 10.1\times\). With an order-of-magnitude fewer bytes to stage per compute tile, the bandwidth pressure from DRAM to SRAM drops proportionally, and the same throughput can be achieved with smaller on-chip buffers because the time to load weights \(T_{\text{load}} \propto \text{bytes}/\text{bandwidth}\) shrinks. Thus the architectural motive for large SRAM caches—hiding DRAM latency and bandwidth limits—weakens in 1.58-bit LLMs, allowing SRAM capacity to be reduced without sacrificing throughput.

Moreover, BitNet b1.58’s compute paradigm further supports this conclusion: its core GEMMs use mainly integer additions, eliminating most multiplications, and the activations are quantized to \(8\) bits, which reduces on-chip scratchpad needs for intermediates (e.g., KV caches) by \(2\times\) versus \(16\)-bit baselines. As model size grows, the proportion of time and energy spent in \(\texttt{nn.Linear}\) increases; BitNet b1.58 diminishes both arithmetic cost and data movement cost for these layers, which the paper shows translates to \(2.4\!-\!4.1\times\) latency speedups and \(3.3\!-\!3.6\times\) memory reductions. Because throughput in modern accelerators is often bounded by memory movement rather than raw compute, lowering per-parameter bitwidth from \(16\) to \(1.58\) bits reduces the required on-chip staging capacity to keep the compute fully utilized. Consequently, smaller SRAM can sustain the same or higher throughput, directly lowering hardware cost while preserving performance.
