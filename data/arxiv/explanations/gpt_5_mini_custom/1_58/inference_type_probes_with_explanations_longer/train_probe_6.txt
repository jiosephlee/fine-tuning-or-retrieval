According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', conceptually the quantity $\gamma$, defined as $\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|$, in the absmean quantization function — which divides $W$ by $\gamma + \epsilon$ before applying $\mathrm{RoundClip}(\cdot, -1, 1)$ to produce values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ — acts as a scaling factor. This is because the operation
$\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma+\epsilon}, -1, 1\right)$ first normalizes the weight matrix by its average absolute magnitude, bringing the “typical” weight to $\mathcal{O}(1)$. After this normalization, the integer rounding to $\{-1,0,1\}$ becomes well-posed: entries with $|W_{ij}| \ll \gamma$ shrink below the $0.5$ threshold and round to $0$, while entries with $|W_{ij}| \gtrsim \gamma$ rise above the threshold and round to $\pm 1$ (further constrained by clipping). In other words, $\gamma$ plays the same role as a per-matrix quantization scale in standard low-bit quantization, setting the dynamic range so that rounding preserves the relative structure of $W$ while mapping it into the discrete ternary set.

Moreover, by calibrating the scale to the mean absolute value, the procedure implicitly controls the sparsity and sign distribution induced by ternarization. Since $\mathrm{RoundClip}$ maps $x$ to $\{-1,0,1\}$ by nearest-integer rounding with bounds, the thresholds at $\pm 0.5$ in the normalized domain determine which weights are zeroed versus kept as $\pm 1$. Choosing $\gamma$ as the global average $L_1$ magnitude ensures that, across layers, the proportion of weights landing at $0$ versus $\pm 1$ adapts to the layer’s typical scale, preventing saturation (too many $\pm 1$) or collapse (too many $0$s). The small $\epsilon$ further stabilizes the scale to avoid division-by-zero or extreme amplification when a matrix happens to be near-zero. Thus, within the absmean quantization scheme used by BitNet b1.58, $\gamma$ functions precisely as a scaling factor that aligns the continuous weight distribution with the ternary lattice while maintaining modeling capacity and enabling the “feature filtering” effect of explicit zeros.
