In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', if activations were scaled to [0, Q_b] instead of [-Q_b, Q_b], the extra quantization complication that would reappear is zero-point quantization. This is because mapping real-valued activations into an asymmetric, nonnegative interval requires an affine quantization of the form x_real ≈ s(x_int − z), where s > 0 is a scale and z is a zero-point chosen so that x_real = 0 is representable in the quantized domain. With a range like [0, Q_b], one typically uses unsigned integers and must select z ≠ 0 to align the integer codebook with real zero. In contrast, a symmetric range around zero supports signed quantization with z = 0, eliminating the need to carry a zero-point. The paper explicitly adopts per-token symmetric scaling to [-Q_b, Q_b] “to get rid of the zero-point quantization,” indicating that reverting to [0, Q_b] would require reinstating z to correctly encode zeros.

Avoiding zero-point is not just a cosmetic choice; it simplifies the core integer GEMM path that dominates LLM compute. Under affine quantization, the inner product between activations a and ternary weights w becomes, in integers, s_a s_w ∑_i (a_i − z_a)(w_i − z_w) = s_a s_w (∑_i a_i w_i − z_a ∑_i w_i − z_w ∑_i a_i + nz_a z_w), which introduces extra offset terms that must be accumulated, calibrated, and potentially corrected per token or per channel. With symmetric activation quantization to [-Q_b, Q_b] and ternary symmetric weights in {−1, 0, 1}, one can set z_a = z_w = 0, reducing the computation to s_a s_w ∑_i a_i w_i with pure integer additions and no offset maintenance—precisely the “almost no multiplication” paradigm the paper emphasizes. Therefore, switching back to [0, Q_b] would reintroduce zero-point quantization and its associated implementation complexity, harming the simplicity and system-level optimization benefits the authors report.
