According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', BitNet's dramatic energy savings for matrix computations are primarily explained by the elimination of the expensive floating-point operation known as multiplication. The core change is that weights are ternary, \(W_{ij} \in \{-1,0,1\}\), so the matrix–vector product \(y_i=\sum_j W_{ij} x_j\) reduces each “multiplication” to either \(+x_j\), \(0\), or \(-x_j\), which can be implemented as integer additions/subtractions with skip-on-zero rather than FP16 multiply-accumulate; in BitNet b1.58 this is coupled with 8-bit activations and kernels that compute using INT additions, so the dominant arithmetic in Transformer linear layers is INT8 addition rather than FP16 add and FP16 mul. The paper explicitly notes that the bulk of LLM compute is matrix multiplication in linear layers and that BitNet’s “matrix multiplication only involves integer addition,” eliminating multiplications almost entirely; using an energy model from prior work, they estimate that INT8 additions are far cheaper than FP16 operations, yielding a \(71.4\times\) reduction in arithmetic energy for matrix multiplication on 7 nm chips. Because power is the performance limiter on many accelerators, the removal of FP multiplications translates not only to lower energy per op but also to higher achievable throughput, reinforcing that the primary driver of the observed energy savings in matrix computations is the absence of floating-point multiplications.
