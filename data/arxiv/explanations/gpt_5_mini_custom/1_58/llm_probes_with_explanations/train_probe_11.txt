In the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', the specific reduction in arithmetic operations that most directly drives the large matrix-multiplication energy savings is fewer $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications. This follows because BitNet b1.58 constrains each weight to $\{-1,0,1\}$ via the absmean quantizer $\widetilde{W}=\mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),-1,1\right)$ with $\gamma=\frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}|W_{ij}|$, so a dense matrix multiply $Y=W X$ is implemented with 8-bit activations and ternary weights as (i) additions only when $\widetilde{W}_{ij}=\pm 1$ (multiplication by $\pm 1$ reduces to a sign flip or copy, which is fused into address/select logic and accumulation) and (ii) complete skipping when $\widetilde{W}_{ij}=0$, thereby eliminating the need for $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplies that dominate the energy in standard FP16 GEMMs; the paper’s energy breakdown explicitly contrasts BitNet b1.58’s INT8 additions against the FP16 additions plus FP16 multiplications in the baseline and reports a $71.4\times$ reduction in arithmetic energy for matrix multiplication on 7nm, consistent with known energy models where a $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiply is far more expensive than an integer add and with the fact that $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ is the dominant, scaling compute component, so removing almost all $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications is the main driver of the large matmul energy savings.
