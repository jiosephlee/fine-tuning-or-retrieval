According to the paper 'The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits', for the energy savings of BitNet b1.58 to reliably convert into higher speed, the chip-level constraint that must hold is the power limit. This follows directly from the paper’s assertion that “the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power,” which implies a simple relationship between achievable throughput and per-operation energy under a fixed power budget: if the chip is power-limited at \(P_{\max}\), then the maximum rate of operations satisfies \(R_{\max} \approx P_{\max}/e_{\mathrm{op}}\), where \(e_{\mathrm{op}}\) is the energy per operation; by replacing FP16 multiply–accumulate with primarily INT8 additions, BitNet b1.58 dramatically reduces \(e_{\mathrm{op}}\) (e.g., a reported \(71.4\times\) arithmetic energy saving on 7 nm for matrix multiplications), so under the same \(P_{\max}\) the chip can execute proportionally more operations per unit time, yielding higher throughput and lower latency. The paper also notes that as model size grows, the proportion of time spent in \(\mathrm{nn.Linear}\) (where these savings accrue) increases, further amplifying the speed benefit under power-limited operation; conversely, if performance were limited by something other than power (e.g., memory stalls or architectural bottlenecks), the energy reduction would not necessarily translate into higher speed, hence the importance of the power limit as the operative chip-level constraint for energy-to-speed conversion.
