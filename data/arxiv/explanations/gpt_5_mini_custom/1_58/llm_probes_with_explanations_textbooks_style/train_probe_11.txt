Chapter: Energy Savings in 1.58-bit LLMs through the Elimination of FP16 Multiplications

# From Full Precision to 1.58 Bits: Why Arithmetic Matters

Large Language Models rely heavily on matrix multiplication as the dominant computation in the Transformer architecture. In full-precision variants, such as FP16 or BF16 LLMs, these matrix multiplications are implemented using floating-point arithmetic that consists of both addition and multiplication. The paper introduces BitNet b1.58, a ternary-weight Transformer in which each parameter takes values from the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and activations are quantized to 8 bits. This change defines a new computation paradigm for matrix multiplication: instead of relying on floating-point addition and multiplication, the core operation in BitNet b1.58 becomes integer addition, with almost no multiplication operations involved.

This shift in arithmetic is not superficial. The paper emphasizes that the fundamental limit to compute performance in many chips is power, and the type of arithmetic used in the matrix multiplication directly governs energy consumption. When the weights are ternary, multiplication by $0$ can be removed entirely, multiplication by $+1$ becomes a pass-through, and multiplication by $-1$ becomes sign inversion. These transformations collapse the need for floating-point multiplications and even floating-point additions in favor of integer additions arranged to accumulate contributions efficiently. As a result, matrix multiplication in BitNet b1.58 is dominated by INT8 addition rather than FP16 multiplication and addition, driving large energy savings.

# The Computation Paradigm in BitNet b1.58

BitNet b1.58 replaces the standard linear layers in Transformers with a custom BitLinear that operates under quantization constraints. During training, weights are constrained to be ternary using an absmean quantization function. Let $W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ be a weight matrix. The quantized weight matrix $\widetilde{W}$ is obtained by scaling $W$ by its average absolute value and then rounding and clipping each element to the nearest integer in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$:
\[
\widetilde{W} = \mathrm{RoundClip}\!\left(\frac{W}{\gamma + \epsilon}, -1, 1\right),
\]
\[
\mathrm{RoundClip}(x, a, b) = \max(a, \min(b, \mathrm{round}(x))),
\]
\[
\gamma = \frac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij} |W_{ij}|.
\]
Activations are quantized to 8 bits and scaled per token to the symmetric range $[-Q_b, Q_b]$ rather than using zero-point quantization, simplifying both implementation and system-level optimization.

Under these constraints, the inner product that defines each entry in the output of a matrix multiplication,
\[
y_i = \sum_{j} \widetilde{W}_{ij}\, x_j,
\]
no longer requires floating-point multiplicative arithmetic when $\widetilde{W}_{ij} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ and $x_j$ is represented in INT8. Multiplication by $0$ contributes nothing, multiplication by $+1$ is equivalent to adding $x_j$, and multiplication by $-1$ is equivalent to subtracting $x_j$. The accumulation across $j$ is performed via integer additions, which dramatically reduces the arithmetic operations most responsible for energy usage in full-precision models—namely, $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications.

This reconfiguration of matrix multiplication is the cornerstone of BitNet b1.58’s energy efficiency. It retains the functional behavior of the Transformer while sharply changing the arithmetic workload that hardware must execute.

# Matrix Multiplication and Energy Composition

The paper characterizes the energy composition of the arithmetic operations in matrix multiplication for both full-precision LLaMA-like LLMs and BitNet b1.58. In the FP16 baseline, the matrix multiplication consists of both $\mathrm{FP16}$ additions and $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications. In BitNet b1.58, by contrast, the matrix multiplication is dominated by $\mathrm{INT8}$ additions. The explicit removal of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications and the replacement of floating-point additions with integer additions result in large energy savings for the dominant compute kernel in LLM inference.

The energy model cited in the paper shows that on $7\,\mathrm{nm}$ chips, BitNet b1.58 saves $71.4$ times the arithmetic operations energy consumption for matrix multiplication compared to the FP16 baseline. This order-of-magnitude reduction follows directly from the change in the arithmetic composition: BitNet b1.58’s matrix multiplication comprises primarily a sequence of integer additions, whereas the FP16 baseline must perform energy-costly floating-point multiplications in addition to floating-point additions. Because matrix multiplication is the bulk of computation in LLMs, this change in arithmetic drives overall energy efficiency.

Importantly, the paper also notes that energy savings translate to faster computation when power is the limiting factor in chip performance. In such a regime, reducing the energy per operation enables greater throughput under the same power envelope. Thus, the elimination of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications not only reduces energy consumption but also contributes directly to latency improvements.

# Why Removing FP16 Multiplications Is the Primary Driver

The specific reduction in arithmetic operations that most directly drives the large matrix-multiplication energy savings is the removal of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications. In the FP16 LLaMA baseline, each entry of the matrix product requires accumulating contributions that involve floating-point multiply-add pairs, where both the multiplication and the addition are performed in FP16. Multiplication is inherently more complex and energy-intensive than addition in floating-point units. By adopting ternary weights $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, BitNet b1.58 replaces these multiplications with simple control logic: skip when the weight is $0$, add when the weight is $+1$, and subtract when the weight is $-1$. The accumulation is performed with $\mathrm{INT8}$ additions.

While replacing $\mathrm{FP16}$ additions with $\mathrm{INT8}$ additions contributes to energy savings, the paper’s analysis and energy composition highlight that eliminating $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications is the pivotal transformation. This is the arithmetic operation removed most comprehensively by the ternary-weight design, and it is the operation whose energy cost dominates in floating-point matrix multiplication. As a result, the central mechanism underpinning the $71.4\times$ energy reduction for matrix multiplication is the absence of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications in BitNet b1.58.

# Implications for Latency, Memory, and Throughput

The change in arithmetic operations has downstream effects on latency, memory, and throughput that the paper quantifies. Because matrix multiplication is faster when relying on $\mathrm{INT8}$ additions rather than $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiply-adds, BitNet b1.58 achieves substantial speedups that grow with model size. For instance, BitNet b1.58 at $70\mathrm{B}$ parameters is $4.1\times$ faster than the FP16 LLaMA baseline, reflecting how the time cost of $\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ grows with model size in full-precision models, while BitNet b1.58 keeps that cost low via its integer-addition computation.

In memory, BitNet b1.58’s ternary parameters and $8$-bit activations reduce both the capacity and bandwidth demands. The paper reports that BitNet b1.58 uses $3.55\times$ less GPU memory than the FP16 baseline at the $3\mathrm{B}$ scale, and similar trends hold at larger scales. These reductions also improve the cost and time of loading weights from DRAM, which further accelerates inference.

Throughput benefits compound these gains. In a $70\mathrm{B}$ configuration across two $80\mathrm{GB}$ A100 GPUs using pipeline parallelism, BitNet b1.58 supports up to $11\times$ the batch size of the FP16 LLaMA baseline and achieves an $8.9\times$ higher throughput. These improvements are consistent with a computation pipeline that minimizes power-hungry floating-point multiplications and leverages efficient integer addition.

# Scaling Laws and Performance Parity

The energy and latency advantages are not achieved at the expense of model quality. The paper shows that BitNet b1.58 matches the full-precision baseline in terms of perplexity and end-task performance starting from approximately the $3\mathrm{B}$ scale, given the same model size and training tokens. For example, BitNet b1.58 at $3.9\mathrm{B}$ is $2.4\times$ faster, uses $3.32\times$ less memory, and performs significantly better than an FP16 LLaMA at $3\mathrm{B}$.

The paper synthesizes these results into a new scaling law that relates model size in 1.58-bit arithmetic to an equivalent full-precision size in terms of latency, memory, and energy efficiency. As a reference drawn from the reported trends, BitNet b1.58 at $13\mathrm{B}$ is more efficient than FP16 LLMs at $3\mathrm{B}$; BitNet b1.58 at $30\mathrm{B}$ is more efficient than FP16 LLMs at $7\mathrm{B}$; and BitNet b1.58 at $70\mathrm{B}$ is more efficient than FP16 LLMs at $13\mathrm{B}$. These equivalences highlight that reductions in $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications at the core of matrix multiplication enable larger models to be deployed with lower cost while maintaining high performance.

# Outlook: System Design and Long-Context Support

Beyond arithmetic, the paper discusses system-level implications made possible by BitNet b1.58’s computation paradigm. Reduced memory for weights and activations lowers inter-chip communication overheads and device counts in configurations such as Mixture-of-Experts. The quantization of activations to $8$ bits also reduces KV cache size during long-sequence inference, enabling longer contexts under fixed resources, with the prospect of further lossless compression in 1.58-bit LLMs.

These directions underscore a broader design vision: by centering the computation on integer addition and eliminating $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications from matrix multiplication, BitNet b1.58 opens the door to specialized hardware and system optimizations tailored to 1-bit-era LLMs. In this paradigm, the most consequential arithmetic change—the removal of $\mathrm{FP16}$ multiplications—serves as the principal driver of energy savings, performance gains, and scalable deployment across a range of model sizes and settings.
