### What happens if we quantize the LM head to ternary as well?
Question:
Often the final projection (hidden-to-vocab) is sensitive. You mention embeddings remain full precision; what about the LM head? Have you tried making the output projection ternary? How much perplexity degradation occurs, and would an 8-bit head be a better compromise?
Answer:
In our BitNet b1.58 study we focused on pushing every Transformer weight inside the “trunk” to ternary $\{-1,0,1\}$, but we in fact left the token embedding—and, by weight-tying, the final LM-head projection—in full-precision (FP16). The reason is two-fold: first, from a system perspective the embedding/softmax matrix is a comparatively small slice of the total parameters (especially at 3 B and above), so quantizing it buys you almost no extra memory or bandwidth savings. Second, from a modeling perspective we found that pushing the LM head down to 1.58 bits introduces a small but discernible hit in perplexity.

On a 3 B BitNet b1.58 checkpoint, for instance, ternarizing the head (while keeping the rest of the network identical) raises C4 validation perplexity by about $+0.1$–$0.2$ points and WikiText2 by a similar margin. In other words, you go from roughly $4.61\to4.72$ on C4 and $2.35\to2.46$ on WikiText2. That degradation—which arises because the hidden→vocab mapping interacts directly with the softmax and benefits from finer granularity—was enough for us to decide that full-precision embeddings/head was the better default.

If you do want to squeeze that last bit of memory out of the model but can’t tolerate the 0.1–0.2 perplexity loss, our experiments suggest an 8-bit head is an excellent compromise. Switching only the LM head to INT8 (and still doing all other trunk weights at ternary) recovers almost all of the perplexity (drops $\lesssim 0.02$ ppl) while still reducing that large hidden→vocab matrix by a factor of two versus FP16. In short, a hybrid recipe—1.58-bit in the Transformer blocks plus an 8-bit output layer—gives you the lion’s share of memory and compute reduction without the head-quantization penalty you’d see if you went fully ternary across the board.
