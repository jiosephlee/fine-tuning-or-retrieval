### Could stochastic or learned thresholds improve ternary quantization over RoundClip?
Question:
RoundClip after dividing by absmean implicitly sets thresholds at ±0.5 for mapping to {−1, 0, +1}. Have you tried learned thresholds or stochastic quantization to control the density of zeros per layer? Does tuning thresholds per-channel yield better accuracy without hurting the simple hardware story?
Answer:
In BitNet b1.58 we deliberately chose a very simple, layer‐wise “abs‐mean + RoundClip” recipe for ternary weights—that is,

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{n m} \sum_{i,j} \bigl|W_{ij}\bigr|\,, 
\quad
\widetilde W_{ij}
= \max\!\Bigl(-1,\min\bigl(1,\mathrm{round}\bigl(W_{ij}/(\gamma+\epsilon)\bigr)\bigr)\Bigr),
$$

which in effect places the decision boundary for “$±1$ vs. $0$” exactly at $±0.5$ after scaling. We did not in this paper explore learned thresholds or stochastic quantization, though both are well-known ideas in the ternary‐NN literature (e.g.\ Ternary Weight Networks).

Intuitively, learning a threshold $t$ (or even a per-channel threshold $t_c$) would let you directly control the sparsity of zeros: you would quantize

$$
q(w) =
\begin{cases}
+1, & w > t,\\
0,  & |w|\le t,\\
-1, & w < -\,t,
\end{cases}
$$

and train $t$ (or each $t_c$) jointly with the weights. In principle this can improve expressivity, especially if different layers (or channels) have very different dynamic ranges or if you want to target a fixed zero‐rate. One could go even further and use stochastic rounding—that is, sample between $\lfloor w/\gamma\rfloor$ and $\lceil w/\gamma\rceil$ proportional to how close $w/\gamma$ is to each integer—to reduce bias in the gradient estimate.

We chose not to pursue these variants for three main reasons. First, our goal was a “clean” 1.58-bit recipe that maps directly onto integer-only hardware: fixed $\gamma$ plus a single saturating round means you never need extra comparisons or learned offset storage at inference time. Second, through our scaling-law experiments we found that the simple RoundClip already matches full-precision LLaMA from 3 B upward, so the room for improvement was small. And third, introducing per-channel thresholds or stochastic elements complicates both the software stack (you must store and load extra threshold parameters) and the design of a dedicated chip (you need more comparators or random-number logic).

That said, learned thresholds remain an attractive future direction. In low-bit vision nets and ternary networks for CNNs, people have reported small accuracy gains by learning either a global or channel-wise $t$ (sometimes with a small regularizer to encourage sparsity). It’s quite plausible that a carefully tuned per-channel threshold schedule could eke out a few more perplexity points in the 1.58-bit LLM setting, at the cost of slightly more complex decode logic. We simply traded off that modest accuracy upside against our desire for a truly minimal quantization kernel—hence our choice of RoundClip.
