### How does per-token activation scaling interact with KV cache reuse at inference time?
Question:
If the activation quantization scale \(Q_b\) is computed per token, then the K and V stored in the KV cache presumably need their associated scales to be reused for later attention. Is the scale saved alongside the cached tensors? Could different per-token scales across time cause numerical mismatches when computing attention between old and new tokens?
Answer:
In the BitNet b1.58 paper the authors point out that they quantize *activations* to 8-bits **per token** by first finding the dynamic range $Q_b$ of that token’s hidden‐state and then uniformly mapping into the integer interval $[-Q_b, Q_b]$. This “per‐token” trick avoids a zero‐point offset and actually simplifies the arithmetic kernels, but it does introduce one extra piece of bookkeeping: if you want to cache your quantized keys ($K$) and values ($V$) for long‐context inference at 8 bits each, you must also remember *which* scale $Q_b$ you used when you quantized them.

In practice nothing mystical is hiding here—any dynamic quantization scheme that works at inference time needs to carry around its scale factor if you ever want to dequantize or correctly combine two differently‐scaled tensors. Concretely, for each past token $t$ (and each layer or head, depending on your implementation), you would store  
1. The 8-bit integer slice $K_t$ (and $V_t$),  
2. The corresponding floating‐point scale $s_t = Q_b^{(t)}$.

When a new query vector $Q_{\mathrm{new}}$ (itself quantized with scale $s_{\mathrm{new}}$) attends over the cached $\{K_t\}$, you compute the dot‐product in integer space as

$$
\langle Q_{\mathrm{new}}, K_t \rangle
= \sum_i Q_{\mathrm{new},i}^{\rm int}\;K_{t,i}^{\rm int}\,,
$$

and you know that in the original floating‐point domain this actually equals

$$
\frac{1}{s_{\mathrm{new}}\;s_t}\,\sum_i 
\underbrace{\bigl(Q_{\mathrm{new},i}^{\rm int}\bigr)}_{\in[-s_{\mathrm{new}},s_{\mathrm{new}}]}\,
\underbrace{\bigl(K_{t,i}^{\rm int}\bigr)}_{\in[-s_t,s_t]}\,,
$$

so you simply multiply the integer result by $1/(s_{\mathrm{new}}\,s_t)$ to recover the true (FP16/BF16) dot‐product. Once you do that for every past $t$, you have a correctly scaled attention score and no “cross‐time” mismatch ever creeps in.

If you were to *not* store each $s_t$, you would be forced either to re‐quantize your cached $K$–$V$ at the new query’s scale (expensive) or to accept a scale‐mismatch that would bias your attention weights. So any high‐throughput implementation of BitNet b1.58 that actually *saves* 8 bits in the KV cache has to stash these per‐token scales in parallel. In a modern transformer‐inference kernel this is almost for‐free (one float per token per head or layer is negligible compared to all the key/value vectors themselves).

**Bottom line:**  
Yes—the per‐token activation scale $Q_b$ must be saved alongside your cached $K$ and $V$. When you compute attention between an old key/value and a new query, you always apply the *pair* of scales $(s_{\mathrm{new}},s_t)$ so that the restored dot‐product matches what you would have gotten in full precision. This bookkeeping fully eliminates any numerical mismatch, and it’s exactly the same pattern you see in typical dynamic quantization frameworks.
