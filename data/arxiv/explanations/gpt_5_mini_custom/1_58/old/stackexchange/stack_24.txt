### In the 2T-token experiment vs StableLM-3B, what exactly differs?
Question:
The 2T-token training follows StableLM-3B's data recipe, but model architecture, precision, and training dynamics differ. Are optimizer, LR schedule, and data mixing ratios identical? Since the parameter counts might differ, how do you attribute the improvements—precision, architecture tweaks, or size?
Answer:
In the “$2$ T-token” experiment we deliberately held all of StableLM-3B’s training recipes—including the optimizer, learning‐rate schedule, batch‐sizes and data‐mixing ratios—exactly the same, so that any gain must come from the model itself. Concretely, both StableLM-3B and our BitNet b$1.58$ were trained from scratch on the identical $2$ trillion‐token mix (from RedPajama), with the same AdamW hyperparameters, the same warm-up plus cosine-decay schedule, the same tokenization and shard-sampling strategy. The only points of divergence are:

1. **Precision and Linear layers**: BitNet b$1.58$ replaces every FP16 `nn.Linear` with a ternary “BitLinear” (weights quantized to $\{-1,\,0,\,+1\}$ via the absmean round-clip in Eq.\ $1$–$3$) and uses 8-bit activations, whereas StableLM-3B remains in full FP16/BF16.

2. **LLaMA-style architectural tweaks**: we adopt RMSNorm instead of LayerNorm, SwiGLU feed-forward non-linearity, rotary embeddings, and remove all bias terms to simplify the compute graph.

3. **Parameter count**: our $2$ T-token run uses a slightly larger $3.9$ B model to offset the information loss from ternary weights (StableLM-3B is $3$ B).

By fixing the optimizer, LR schedule, and data mixing, the improvement in zero-shot accuracies (Table \ref{tab:2t}) can be attributed almost entirely to the bit-precision plus these minor architectural adjustments (and the modest up-sizing). In particular, the explicit zero-weight in the ternary scheme acts as a learned feature filter, which the paper argues strengthens generalization without expensive multiply-accumulate ops. Thus, rather than “better hyperparameters” or “different data mixing,” it is the $1.58$-bit quantization combined with the BitLinear design (and its compatible LLaMA-alike modules) that drives the superior performance at $2$ T tokens.
