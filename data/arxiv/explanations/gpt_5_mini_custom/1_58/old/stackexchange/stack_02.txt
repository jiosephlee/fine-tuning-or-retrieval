### How is backpropagation performed with ternary weights in BitNet b1.58?
Question:
The model constrains weights to {−1, 0, +1} via a quantization function. During training from scratch, are the forward weights strictly ternary while the gradients are computed with a straight-through estimator (STE)? If so, what is the exact surrogate gradient used for the RoundClip operation? Are there any tricks (e.g., gradient scaling, clipping, or stochastic rounding) to stabilize training?
Answer:
In BitNet b1.58, every weight is indeed constrained to $\{-1,0,+1\}$ in the forward pass, but the backward pass is handled with the standard “straight-through estimator” (STE) for quantization steps. Concretely, you keep a full-precision weight tensor $W$, compute

$$
\gamma = \tfrac{1}{nm}\sum_{ij}\lvert W_{ij}\rvert,\quad
\widetilde W = \mathrm{RoundClip}\bigl(W/(\gamma+\epsilon),\,-1,1\bigr),
$$

and actually use $\widetilde W$ (re-scaled by $\gamma$) in all linear layers. During backprop you pretend that the nondifferentiable rounding and clipping didn’t happen by simply passing gradients through as if

$$
\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{round}(x)}{\partial x}\approx 1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{clip}(x;{-1},1)}{\partial x}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&-1\le x\le 1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus the combined surrogate is

$$
\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{RoundClip}(x;{-1},1)}{\partial x}
\;\approx\;
\begin{cases}
1\,,&-1\le x\le 1,\\
0\,,&\text{else.}
\end{cases}
$$

If you write $\widetilde W = \gamma\,\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/\gamma)$, then the gradient on the underlying $W$ is simply

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}
= \frac{\partial L}{\partial \widetilde W}
\;\times\;
\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{RoundClip}(W/\gamma)}{\partial (W/\gamma)}
\;\times\;\frac{1}{\gamma}
\;\approx\;
\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial \widetilde W}\,\mathbf{1}_{\lvert W/\gamma\rvert \le 1}.
$$

Beyond this STE, BitNet b1.58 does not introduce exotic gradient tricks: there is no stochastic rounding, no extra gradient scaling specifically on the quantizer, and no bespoke clipping of the STE beyond the inherent $[-1,1]$ saturation. Instead, they rely on the abs-mean normalization (the $\gamma$ scale) to keep the quantizer’s input in a stable range, and on the usual LLaMA-style recipe—AdamW with weight-decay, warmup schedules, RMSNorm and SwiGLU activations, occasional global gradient clipping, etc.—to ensure convergence. In practice this simple STE+absmean scheme was enough to train BitNet b1.58 from scratch and to match full-precision perplexities and downstream accuracies.
