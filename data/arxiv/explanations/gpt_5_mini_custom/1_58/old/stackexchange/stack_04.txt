### How are activations quantized to 8 bits per token and what is Q_b?
Question:
The activations are said to be quantized to the range \([-Q_b, Q_b]\) per token to remove zero-points. How is \(Q_b\) determined in practice (e.g., a running max, a percentile, or learned scale)? Is the scaling computed independently at each layer and time step token, and is the scale stored along with the KV cache for attention?
Answer:
In BitNet b1.58 the only novelty in activation quantization (relative to the 1-bit weight quantizer) is that, instead of doing an asymmetric 8-bit quantization with a zero-point, they simply rescale each token’s vector into a symmetric integer range $[-2^{7}+1,\,2^{7}-1]$ (i.e. 8-bit signed) by choosing a “dynamic” bound $Q_b$ per token. In practice this works as follows:

 1.  **Compute the dynamic range**  
     After you have computed the floating-point activations of one token at one layer (say a vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$), you scan it to find  
     $$  
       Q_b \;=\; \max_{1\le i\le d}\lvert x_i\rvert\,.  
     $$  
     In other words, you take the maximum absolute activation in that vector. You do *not* maintain a running “global” max or use percentiles or any learned parameter for $Q_b$; it is simply the instantaneous max of the activations of that token (and you repeat the same for every layer and every new token).

 2.  **Form the 8-bit symmetric scale**  
     Once $Q_b$ is known, you define the int8 scale factor  
     $$  
       s \;=\; \frac{2^{7}-1}{Q_b}\,,  
     $$  
     so that $x_i\in[-Q_b,Q_b]$ maps to $\hat x_i\in[-127,\,127]$. Your quantization is then  
     $$  
       \hat x_i \;=\;\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathrm{round}(s\,x_i),\,-127,\,127\bigr)\,,  
     $$  
     and on dequantization you recover  
     $$  
       x_i \approx \hat x_i \,\frac{Q_b}{2^{7}-1}\,.  
     $$

 3.  **Per-layer, per-token scaling**  
     Because you want zero-point-free (i.e. purely symmetric) quantization, this scale $Q_b$ is recomputed *for each token* at *each layer*. That not only guarantees the token’s dynamic range is fully used, but it also keeps implementation trivial (no bias terms in the quantizer) and avoids any cross-token leakage in the scale.

 4.  **Storing the scale with KV caches**  
     When you apply this to the key and value vectors in the transformer’s KV cache, you *must* store the associated scale $Q_b$ alongside the 8-bit integers. Typically you end up with—for each token in the cache—a small FP16 (or FP32) scalar $Q_b$ plus a length-$d$ int8 vector. In practice the extra few bytes per token for $Q_b$ are negligible compared to the $d$ bytes of the quantized vector itself, so you still get almost a $2\times$ reduction in KV-cache footprint when you go from 16-bit to 8-bit activations.

In summary, BitNet b1.58 uses *dynamic per-token symmetric quantization* by taking the *instantaneous max absolute activation* as $Q_b$ at each layer/time-step. No percentiles, no running maxima, no extra “learned” scale—just a straight max-over-the-coordinates. And yes, you must carry that one scalar $Q_b$ along with the quantized KV vectors so that—when you replay or extend the sequence—you can dequantize the keys and values correctly.
