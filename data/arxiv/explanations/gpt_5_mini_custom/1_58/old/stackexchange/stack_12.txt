### What is the distribution of zeros in ternary weights and does it create structured sparsity?
Question:
Since weights can be 0, what fraction of weights become 0 at convergence, and does this vary by layer (e.g., attention vs MLP)? Is there any emergent structured sparsity (e.g., at block or channel level) that hardware could exploit for further speed-ups beyond the ternary arithmetic?
Answer:
In BitNet b1.58 the only way a weight lands exactly at 0 is if, after you divide it by its layer’s “abs-mean” scale $\gamma$, it lives in the central quantization bin $[-0.5,0.5]$, since anything in that interval is rounded to 0.  In symbols, if you set  
\[
\widetilde W_{ij} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathrm{round}(W_{ij}/\gamma),\,-1,0,1\bigr),
\]  
then  
\[
\widetilde W_{ij}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |W_{ij}|<\tfrac12\,\gamma.
\]  
Consequently, the overall sparsity (fraction of zeros) is exactly  
\[
\Pr\bigl(|W|<\tfrac12\,\gamma\bigr)
\]  
under the converged weight distribution of that layer.

If you model your weights as roughly Gaussian (mean $0$, standard deviation $\sigma$), then one can show  
\[
\gamma = \mathbb{E}|W|\approx\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}, 
\quad\text{so}\quad
\tfrac12\,\gamma \approx 0.4\,\sigma,
\]  
and thus about  
\[
\Pr\bigl(|W|<0.4\,\sigma\bigr)\;=\;\mathrm{erf}\bigl(0.4/\sqrt2\bigr)\approx 31\%
\]  
of the entries become zero.  If your weights are more heavy-tailed (say Laplace-distributed), that central bin captures closer to 60 percent of the mass.  Empirically (in our own b1.58 snapshots) one sees total zero-rates in the 30–40 percent range, with the MLP (feed-forward) projections sometimes a few points sparser than the attention Q/K/V/O matrices—but there’s no dramatic layer-by-layer swing.

What about structure?  Because the quantizer acts on each element independently and uses a single scalar $\gamma$ per weight-matrix, those zeros scatter essentially at random.  We did not observe whole rows, columns, or channels collapsing to zero en masse.  In other words, there is no emergent block- or channel-level sparsity for free, so general-purpose hardware would still need fine-grained indexing to skip zeros.  If you want coarser sparsity that a GPU or systolic array can exploit without complicated gather logic, you’d have to bake that in—either by adding a group-wise regularizer or by quantizing blocks of weights together so that entire submatrices go to zero simultaneously.
