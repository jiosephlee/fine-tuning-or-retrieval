\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory"}

### On fairness: how were precision and optimizer settings controlled across baselines in the 15B-token comparison?
Question:
In the 15B-token experiments, the paper mentions mixed-precision training, with some baselines not using PyTorch AMP. Given that precision and optimizer hyperparameters can materially affect perplexity:
- What exact precision (FP16, BF16), loss scaling, and optimizer hyperparameters were used for xLSTM vs each baseline?
- Were any baselines disadvantaged by numerical instability at lower precision that xLSTM avoided (e.g., due to its normalization scheme)?
- Is there an ablation showing xLSTM vs baselines at identical precision/optimizer setups to rule out training-stability confounds?
Answer:
Short answer: the paper only reports that all models were trained with “mixed precision” for the 15B-token comparison and explicitly notes that some baselines did not use PyTorch AMP (automatic mixed precision). It does not give a per-model table in the main text of exact floating point format, loss-scaling, or the full optimizer hyperparameter list for every baseline. The paper does call out training/precision issues for some models and reports ablations for architectural components of xLSTM, but it does not present an ablation that runs xLSTM and every baseline under an identical precision/optimizer configuration to fully rule out training‑stability confounds.

What the paper actually states (and the important implications)
- The 15B-token experiments: the manuscript says the models “were trained with mixed precision.” For a subset of baselines (RWKV-5, RWKV-6, GLA, HGRN2) the mixed‑precision training “did not utilize the PyTorch automated mixed precision” (main text, Section 4.2 and pointer to Appendix Sec. A.x / appGenTrainProc). That indicates some models used alternative mixed‑precision handling (e.g., custom $FP16$/$BF16$ casting or manual scaling) rather than a uniform AMP pipeline.
- The authors also flag that for some larger 300B experiments (and during early training) a “reasonable training precision setting has been found only after the training start” for RWKV-5/6 and HGRN2 (Section 4.3). In other words, they observed precision/instability issues for some baselines that required tuning of precision handling during training.
- Ablations reported in the paper focus on architectural choices (morphing vanilla LSTM $\to$ xLSTM, gating variants, matrix memory vs scalar memory) and show the performance gains attributed to exponential gating and matrix memory (Table referenced in Section 4.2 and Appendix). They do not report a dedicated ablation that re-trains every baseline and xLSTM with exactly the same numeric-precision pipeline and identical optimizer hyperparameters to isolate numerical-stability effects.

Why these omissions matter (intuitive + technical)
Mixed precision ($FP16$ or $BF16$) and loss-scaling interact with network dynamics. A few concrete ways precision and optimizer choices can change perplexity: gradients can underflow/overflow at $FP16$ if activations or parameter updates are large; custom nonlinearities (here exponential gates) can produce very large intermediate values that demand careful stabilization and scaling; optimizers with different moment estimates or weight decay schedules (AdamW vs Adam, $\beta$s, $\epsilon$) change effective learning trajectories and final perplexity. The paper’s exponential gates explicitly require stabilizers (they introduce an $m_t$ stabilizer), which reduces overflow risk; that design makes xLSTM more robust to low precision than a naive exponential gate would be, but robustness still depends on the precision/scale used in practice. The authors’ note that some baselines needed special precision handling during their runs shows these effects were real in their experiments.

What is and isn’t provided / where to look in the paper and code
- Provided: a clear statement that mixed precision was used, and which baselines did not use PyTorch AMP; ablations for architecture (gating, memory) that attribute gains to xLSTM design choices.
- Not provided (in main text): a per-model explicit listing of $FP16$ vs $BF16$, the loss scaling strategy (static/dynamic), per‑model optimizer hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, Adam betas, eps) in the 15B comparison, nor a controlled ablation that runs all models under identical numeric precision/optimizer settings.
- The paper points to Appendix Sec. appGenTrainProc for more training procedure details and to the release of code (github link). The repository and the appendix are the places to check for the exact training scripts, precision flags, optimizer config files and any run logs that the authors published.

Practical recommendation (if you need to rule out confounds)
If you want to ensure the architectural differences (not numerical details) produce the reported perplexity gap, two practical experiments are needed: re-run xLSTM and each baseline with identical numeric settings (choose $FP16$ or $BF16$ uniformly), identical loss-scaling (e.g., PyTorch AMP dynamic_scaler with the same thresholds or explicit static scaling), and identical optimizer and LR schedule (same optimizer family, $\beta$s, $\epsilon$, weight decay, warmup and decay schedule). Because the authors already noted that some baselines needed bespoke precision handling, you should monitor and log gradient/weight norms and any AMP scaling events to catch silent instability. The xLSTM stabilization blocks ($m_t$, lower bounds in $m$LSTM normalizer, etc.) make overflow less likely, so matching precision may require extra stabilization or different initializations for those baselines as well.

If you want to follow up with the authors
The paper’s public code repository is the natural next step: inspect the training scripts and the appendix training procedure (appGenTrainProc) for the exact implementation details the authors used for each method. If the repository doesn’t resolve your need for a per‑model precision/optimizer table, ask the authors (via the repo issues or contact email) for a reproducibility table listing: (1) $FP16$ vs $BF16$ vs mixed, (2) loss-scaling method, (3) optimizer and its hyperparameters, and (4) any baseline-specific precision hacks they used.

Summary
The paper documents mixed precision use and calls out that several baselines used non-AMP mixed precision or needed precision tuning, and it provides architectural ablations for xLSTM. It does not, however, publish a systematic “identical precision + identical optimizer” ablation across all baselines for the 15B-token experiments. That means a residual concern about numerical-stability confounds remains unless you (or the authors) re-run or provide a controlled re-run with completely identical precision/optimizer settings.
