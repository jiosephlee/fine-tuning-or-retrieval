### How are ties handled in Elo computation for pairwise chatbot comparisons?
Question:
The Elo setup allows ties. Which update rule do you use for a tie (e.g., outcome score 0.5 for both)? Do you use a standard $K=32$ across all comparisons and all systems, or tune $K$ per tournament? How sensitive are system rankings and confidence intervals to $K$, initial Elo, and random sampling order over 10,000 runs?
Answer:
In our paper we use the classic Elo‐rating machinery almost “off the shelf.” Ties are handled simply by assigning each player an outcome score of 0.5, and updating according to the usual formula

$$
E_a = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_b - R_a)/400}}
$$

$$
R_a \leftarrow R_a + K \cdot (S_a - E_a),\quad\text{where } S_a = 0.5 \text{ for a draw}
$$

(and symmetrically for player B). We fix $K = 32$ and start every system at 1000 Elo; we do _not_ tune $K$ per tournament or vary the initial rating.

The only source of randomness we inject is the _order_ in which matches are processed—every one of our <|endoftext|> comparisons is shuffled (with replacement) and the entire Elo computation is repeated $10{,}000$ times with a different seed. From that empirical distribution we quote means and confidence intervals. In practice we found that:

• Changing $K$ or the initial rating will of course shift the absolute numbers (Elo is only defined up to an affine transform), but _relative_ gaps and _rankings_ between systems remain virtually <|endoftext|> for any reasonable $K$ (say $16$–$64$).  
• Nearly all of the uncertainty in our reported CIs comes from the random draw/order of matches, which our $10{,}000$-run procedure faithfully <|endoftext|>  

So, to answer your sub‐questions succinctly: ties are scored 0.5/0.5 with the standard Elo update; we use a single $K=32$ and a common 1000-point <|endoftext|> for _all_ tournaments; and by far the largest contributor to Elo variability (and thus to the width of our confidence intervals) is the random sampling of match order, not the choice of $K$ or initial rating.
