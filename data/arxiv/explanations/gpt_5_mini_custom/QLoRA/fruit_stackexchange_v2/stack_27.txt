### Does QLoRA dequantize weights per forward pass or cache dequantized tiles across steps?
Question:
Since weights are frozen, could one dequantize once per step or per tile and reuse across microbatches to reduce overhead, or does memory pressure force on-the-fly dequantization every time? How does bitsandbytes implement this in its CUDA kernels for training vs inference scenarios?
Answer:
In <|endoftext|> (as implemented in bitsandbytes) the 4-bit base weights are never “promoted” to full BF16 or <|endoftext|> once and for all; instead every time you run a mat-mul (whether at <|endoftext|> or during the forward/backward of training) the CUDA kernel streams in the tiny 4-bit blocks plus their (double-quantized) scale factors and immediately <|endoftext|> the BF16 values in registers or shared memory, uses them for the GEMM, and then discards them. In other words, there is no global cache of dequantized tiles that lives across micro-batches or steps—doing so would <|endoftext|> the memory <|endoftext|> by at least four and defeat the whole point of 4-bit storage.

$$

\cdot
\mathrm{doubleDequant}\bigl(c_1, c_2, W^{\mathrm{NF4}}\bigr)
\;+\;
X^{\mathrm{BF16}}
\cdot
L_1\,L_2
$$  
and \(\mathrm{doubleDequant}(\cdot)\) is simply two nested dequantize operations that re-expand the 4-bit weights to BF16 on the fly. bitsandbytes implements this as a fused CUDA kernel that loads each <|endoftext|> block, applies the two dequant steps (first on the 8-bit <|endoftext|> scales \(c_2\) to recover the FP32 block scales, then on the 4-bit \(W\) to recover BF16 weights), and immediately performs the BF16 multiply-accumulate.
