Answer:
Short answer: the mean you subtract from the <|endoftext|> scales must be stored and added back at <|endoftext|> time. The paper omits the explicit bookkeeping, but in practice that mean is kept (as a small $FP32$ offset per second-quantization block) and re‑added when you reconstruct the <|endoftext|> scales. <|endoftext|> you can view the second-level <|endoftext|> as being stored affinely (a scale and an <|endoftext|> bias), so the dequant step restores the mean before you dequantize the <|endoftext|> weights.

Concrete formulas (notation consistent with the paper)

Let the original $FP32$ per-block <|endoftext|> constant be $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ (these are strictly positive). Let $\mu$ denote the mean that we subtract from $c_2^{\text{FP32}}$ before performing the second <|endoftext|> ($\mu$ can be computed over whatever group of $c_2$ values you choose to center for symmetric <|endoftext|>

Second-level <|endoftext|> (encoding)
1. center the <|endoftext|> scales:
   $$
   \tilde c_2 = c_2^{\text{FP32}} - \mu
   $$
2. quantize the centered values to <|endoftext|> (here paper uses $FP8$ for $c_2$ but we keep <|endoftext|> notation) using a second-level scale $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$:
   $$
   c_2^{\text{k-bit}} = \mathrm{round}\!\big(c_1^{\text{FP32}}\cdot \tilde c_2\big).
   $$
Store $c_2^{\text{k-bit}}$, and also store $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$ and the offset $\mu$ (both as $FP32$). The paper writes $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$ in the <|endoftext|> API; $\mu$ is <|endoftext|> in an implementation and must be stored too.

<|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> / reconstruction)
1. dequantize the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> to recover the centered <|endoftext|> scales:
   $$
   \widehat{\tilde c_2} = \frac{c_2^{\text{k-bit}}}{c_1^{\text{FP32}}}.
   $$
2. add back the mean:
   $$
   \widehat{c_2^{\text{FP32}}} = \widehat{\tilde c_2} + \mu.
   $$
3. dequantize the <|endoftext|> weight blocks using the reconstructed <|endoftext|> scale per block:
   $$
   \widehat{\mathbf W}^{\text{FP32}} = \frac{\mathbf W^{\text{4bit}}}{\widehat{c_2^{\text{FP32}}}}.
   $$

Putting that into the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> notation (explicitly showing the mean):
$$
\text{doubleDequant}\big(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, \mu,\, c_2^{\text{k-bit}},\, \mathbf W^{\text{4bit}}\big)
= \text{dequant}\Big(\widehat{c_2^{\text{FP32}}},\,\mathbf W^{\text{4bit}}\Big),
\quad\text{where }\widehat{c_2^{\text{FP32}}}=\frac{c_2^{\text{k-bit}}}{c_1^{\text{FP32}}}+\mu.
$$

Implementation note / interpretation: the paper omits writing $\mu$ explicitly in the <|endoftext|> signature, but any correct implementation must preserve $\mu$ (the mean you subtracted) and add it back when reconstructing $c_2$. It is common to store $\mu$ together with $c_1$ as a tiny $FP32$ metadata (so you can think of $c_1$ as an "affine" dequant descriptor: <|endoftext|> by $1/c_1$ then add $\mu$). <|endoftext|> the mean is not magically encoded in $c_1$ alone unless you <|endoftext|> pack both scale and bias into the stored $c_1$ structure; either way $\mu$ must be present at dequant time.

Intuition: subtracting $\mu$ before <|endoftext|> $c_2$ lets you use a symmetric quantizer (centered on zero) for the $c_2$ values, which is more efficient and lower-error for <|endoftext|> symmetric encodings. But subtracting $\mu$ destroys an <|endoftext|> offset, so you must remember $\mu$ and restore it after dequantizing the second-level representation; otherwise your reconstructed <|endoftext|> scales would be biased and the final reconstructed weights would be wrong.
