According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', because absolute $10$-point scales showed uncertainty and order effects, the authors recommend using a ranking approach based on pairwise judgments known as Elo rating. The paper reports that GPT-4’s 10-point scoring on the Vicuna benchmark produced wide confidence intervals and a strong ordering bias (GPT-4 tended to assign higher scores to the response appearing first), and that the absolute scale was poorly grounded (“it is unclear what 8 on a 10 point scale means”), yielding only moderate agreement with humans (e.g., Kendall’s $\tau=0.43$, Spearman $r=0.55$ at the system level and Fleiss’ $\kappa=0.25$ at the example level), so they instead construct tournaments of pairwise comparisons and compute Elo scores, which measure expected win rates in head-to-head matches (e.g., an Elo $1100$ vs $1000$ implies $\approx 65\%$ win probability, while equal Elo implies $50\%$), aggregate over many randomized match orders ($10{,}000$ repetitions) with a fixed $K=32$ update, and thereby avoid the pitfalls of absolute scales and order effects by grounding evaluation in relative preferences between two outputs rather than subjective absolute ratings.
