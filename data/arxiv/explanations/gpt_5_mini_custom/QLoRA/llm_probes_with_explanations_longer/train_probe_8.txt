In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the authors suggest that, for a low-cost but imperfect method to rank chatbots, a model-based evaluator is a reasonable alternative to humans, specifically using GPT-4. They substantiate this by running parallel human and GPT-4 evaluations under a tournament-style, pairwise comparison protocol and finding consistent agreement at the system level. Concretely, when computing Elo ratings from pairwise judgments, GPT-4 and humans yield similar rankings, with Kendall’s tau \( \tau = 0.43 \) and Spearman rank correlation \( r = 0.55 \) across systems, indicating moderate concordance in relative ordering. The Elo framework—initialized at 1,000 with \( K = 32 \) and repeated 10,000 times with different seeds to control for ordering effects—helps stabilize comparisons by aggregating many head-to-head matches, and the authors further mitigate prompt-position bias by averaging scores across both response orders. While per-example agreement is weaker (Fleiss’ \( \kappa = 0.25 \)), the aggregate consistency in system-level ranks supports the claim that GPT-4 can serve as a practical proxy for human evaluation when the objective is to rank chatbots rather than to label each instance perfectly.

This conclusion is also sensible from a cost-effectiveness and scalability perspective. Human annotations are expensive and time-consuming, especially when building robust Elo estimates that require many pairwise matches; in contrast, GPT-4 can supply large volumes of judgments at much lower marginal cost, enabling finer-grained estimates of expected win rates \( p(\text{model A wins} \mid \text{model B}) \) across diverse prompts. The authors do identify caveats—GPT-4 shows ordering effects and a tendency to inflate its own outputs (e.g., notably higher Elo for GPT-4 under GPT-4 scoring than under human scoring)—but these biases are partially controlled in the experimental design (e.g., averaging across response order) and are transparent in reporting. Taken together, the empirical rank-level agreement, the robustness added by the Elo aggregation procedure, and the favorable cost profile justify the recommendation: GPT-4-based evaluation is a cheap and reasonable, if imperfect, alternative to human raters for chatbot ranking.
