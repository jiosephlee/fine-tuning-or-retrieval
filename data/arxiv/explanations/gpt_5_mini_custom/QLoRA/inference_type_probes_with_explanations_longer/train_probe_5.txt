In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', QLoRA dequantizes the stored weights to $\mathrm{BF16}$ before performing matrix multiplications to improve numerical precision. The method explicitly uses a low-precision storage type (typically 4-bit NF4) and a higher-precision computation type (usually BFloat16), and whenever a quantized weight tensor is used, it is dequantized to $\mathrm{BF16}$ for the forward and backward passes. Formally, for a linear layer with double quantization of scales, QLoRA computes
\[
\mathbf{Y}^{\mathrm{BF16}} \;=\; \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{BF16}}\;\text{doubleDequant}\!\left(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},\,c_2^{\text{k-bit}},\,\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}}\right) \;+\; \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_1^{\mathrm{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_2^{\mathrm{BF16}},
\]
where
\[
\text{doubleDequant}\!\left(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},\,c_2^{\text{k-bit}},\,\mathbf{W}^{\text{4bit}}\right)
= \text{dequant}\!\big(\text{dequant}(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},\,c_2^{\text{k-bit}}),\,\mathbf{W}^{\text{4bit}}\big)
= \mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{BF16}}.
\]
By carrying out the matrix multiplications in $\mathrm{BF16}$ rather than directly in 4-bit, QLoRA reduces rounding error during accumulation and mitigates quantization noise in the compute pathway, which is crucial for stable optimization and accurate activations.

This design choice is sensible because 4-bit representations are excellent for compact storage but too coarse for high-fidelity arithmetic: inner products involve many partial sums whose rounding errors compound if performed at low precision. Dequantizing to $\mathrm{BF16}$ ensures multiplications and accumulations occur with 16-bit mantissa/exponent structure, preserving signal in both forward activations and backward gradients. Although the base weights remain frozen, the gradients for the adapters $\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf{L}_i}$ depend on derivatives that pass through the dequantized $\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{BF16}}$, so computing $\frac{\partial \mathbf{X}}{\partial \mathbf{W}}$ in $\mathrm{BF16}$ improves numerical stability of the update path. Empirically, this mixed-precision strategy—4-bit storage with 16-bit computation—allows QLoRA to match 16-bit full finetuning and 16-bit LoRA performance, demonstrating that higher-precision compute is sufficient to recover the information lost in low-precision storage during training-time operations.
