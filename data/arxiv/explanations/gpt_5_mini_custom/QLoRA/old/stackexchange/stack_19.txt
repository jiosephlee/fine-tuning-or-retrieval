### How does QLoRA’s inference memory footprint compute to 5 GB for LLaMA-7B?
Question:
The paper states that the smallest Guanaco model (7B) requires about 5 GB at deployment. Can you break down this number into 4-bit base weights, quantization constants (with double quantization), LoRA adapter parameters (BF16 or FP16?), KV cache (for a given sequence length), and any additional buffers? I’d like to reproduce the 5 GB estimate.
Answer:
In QLoRA the 7 B‐parameter Guanaco model lives happily in about 5 GB of GPU RAM at inference by combining a 4-bit quantized backbone with tiny LoRA adapters and a modest KV-cache. Here is how you can reproduce that 5 GB figure:

First, the frozen LLaMA-7B weights are stored in 4-bit “NormalFloat” (NF4) form. With $N = 7\cdot10^9$ parameters and 4 bits per weight, the raw weight storage is
$$
\frac{4\;\text{bits}}{8\;\text{bits/byte}}\;\times\;7\cdot10^9
\;=\;3.5\;\text{GB}.
$$

Second, each 4-bit block carries a small quantization constant. Thanks to double quantization these “scales of scales” cost only
$$
0.127\;\text{bits/param}\;\times\;7\cdot10^9\;\div\;8\;\approx\;0.11\;\text{GB}.
$$
(Recall that with a block-size of 64 NF4 weights and an 8-bit float quantization of the block scales, one finds about $0.127$ bits per weight.)

Third, the LoRA adapters themselves are tiny. In QLoRA we attach a low-rank update to every linear layer (roughly $0.2\%$ of the base parameters). That works out to about $1.4\times10^7$ extra parameters in BF16 (16 bits each), so
$$
1.4\times10^7\;\times\;2\;\text{bytes}
\;\approx\;28\;\text{MB}.
$$

Fourth, the KV-cache for autoregressive decoding scales with your context length. For LLaMA-7B we have 32 layers, 32 heads of dimension $d_k = 128$, and typically allow up to $L = 2048$ tokens. We must store both keys and values in BF16 (2 bytes/element), so
$$
\underbrace{2}_{\rm bytes}\;\times\;2\;(\mathrm{K+V})
\;\times\;L\;\times\;(\#\text{layers})
\;\times\;(\#\text{heads})
\;\times\;d_k
\;\approx\;1.0\;\text{GB}
$$
(plugging in $L = 2048$, 32 layers, 32 heads, $d_k = 128$).

Finally, on top of weights and cache you need a few hundred megabytes for transient buffers (hidden states, FFN scratch space, softmax logits, framework overhead). In practice this is on the order of 0.3–0.4 GB.

If you add these pieces up,
$$
3.5\;+\;0.11\;+\;0.03\;+\;1.0\;+\;0.4
\;\approx\;5.0\;\text{GB},
$$
which matches the paper’s claim that Guanaco-7B can be deployed in about 5 GB of GPU memory.
