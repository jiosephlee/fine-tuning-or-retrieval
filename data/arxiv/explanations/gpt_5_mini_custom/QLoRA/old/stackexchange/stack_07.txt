### How does backpropagate-through-quantization work if base weights are frozen?
Question:
QLoRA backpropagates gradients through the dequantized base weights to update only the LoRA adapters. The paper mentions computing derivatives like $\partial \mathbf{X}/\partial \mathbf{W}$ in BF16 while not computing $\partial E/\partial \mathbf{W}$. Could you clarify the exact computational graph? Are we simply treating the dequantized $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$ as constants in the backward pass so gradients stop there, while the upstream Jacobians flow into $\partial E/\partial \mathbf{L}_i$?
Answer:
In QLoRA each “QLoRA‐layer” is just a standard linear layer with a frozen, 4-bit–quantized weight plus a small trainable LoRA term. Concretely, for inputs \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times h}\) you do

$$
Y \;=\; 
X\,\underbrace{\bigl(\mathrm{doubleDequant}(c_1,c_2,\;W^{\mathrm{NF4}})\bigr)}_{W^{\mathrm{BF16}}}
\;+\;
X\,L_1\,L_2,
$$

where  
– \(W^{\mathrm{NF4}}\) and its quantization constants \((c_1,c_2)\) are stored (in 4- and 8-bit) with `.requires_grad=False`,  
– \(\mathrm{doubleDequant}(\cdots)\) rescales them back to a BF16 tensor,  
– \(L_1,L_2\) are the small adapter matrices with `.requires_grad=True`.  

Implementation sketch in PyTorch looks like this:

```python
with torch.no_grad():
    W_bf16 = doubleDequant(c1, c2, W_nf4)   # leaf tensor in BF16, no grad

Y_base = X.matmul(W_bf16)                   # uses W but W_bf16.requires_grad=False
Y_adpt =  X.matmul(L1).matmul(L2)           # trainable branch
Y      =  Y_base + Y_adpt
```

At backward time PyTorch will see two paths out of `Y`:

1. The **base path** \(Y_{\mathrm{base}}=X\,W^{\mathrm{BF16}}\)  
   – since \(X\) has `requires_grad=True`, it will compute  
     $$
       \frac{\partial E}{\partial X}
       = \frac{\partial E}{\partial Y}\;(W^{\mathrm{BF16}})^\top,
     $$  
   – but because `W_bf16.requires_grad=False`, it will **not** compute \(\partial E/\partial W^{\mathrm{BF16}}\) (and certainly won’t backpropagate into the 4-bit codes).

2. The **adapter path** \(Y_{\mathrm{adpt}}=X\,L_1\,L_2\)  
   – here both \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) carry `requires_grad=True`, so you get the usual  
     $$
        \frac{\partial E}{\partial L_2}
        = (X\,L_1)^\top \frac{\partial E}{\partial Y}, 
        \quad
        \frac{\partial E}{\partial L_1}
        = X^\top\!\bigl(\frac{\partial E}{\partial Y}\,L_2^\top\bigr).
     $$

Because the dequantization is wrapped in `no_grad()` (or equivalently the BF16 weight tensor is a leaf with `requires_grad=False`), **the backward pass simply treats the dequantized base weight as a constant**. All the Jacobian work that autocasting to BF16 does—namely computing the correct \(\partial Y/\partial X\) and \(\partial Y/\partial L_i\) factors—is done in BF16 precision, but at no point do you accumulate \(\partial E/\partial W\). The only parameters updated are the LoRA adapters \(L_1,L_2\).
