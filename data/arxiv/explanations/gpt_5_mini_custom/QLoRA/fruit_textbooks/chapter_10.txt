# Adapter placement: all-layer LoRA needed to match full finetuning
A second empirical lesson concerns where to place LoRA adapters. Early or default usage of LoRA commonly targets a small subset of linear <|endoftext|> (for example, the query and value <|endoftext|> in attention). In controlled experiments (for example <|endoftext|> 7B finetuned on Alpaca), adapter placement is the dominant LoRA hyperparameter: only when adapters are added to all linear layers in every transformer block does the adapter finetuning reach parity with full finetuning.

The memory cost of this wider placement is small relative to the overall optimizer/activation memory budget: LoRA parameters themselves are tiny, and the dominant memory burden during training comes from activation <|endoftext|> rather than adapter parameter storage. # Data quality beats dataset size for instruction tuning
When exploring instruction finetuning at scale, the paper finds that dataset quality and suitability for the target evaluation are more important than raw dataset size. A striking empirical example is that OASST1, a relatively small human-curated dataset with about 9k examples, outperformed a much larger subsampled FLAN v2 dataset of about 450k examples on chatbot benchmarks (Vicuna/OA). Despite FLAN v2's larger size, its example selection and distribution appeared less well matched to open-ended conversational assistant behavior, which is what the Vicuna and OA evaluations probe. This demonstrates that more data is not always better: the characteristics of the data (style, distribution of instructions and responses, multi-turn vs single-turn structure, language coverage) determine how well finetuning transfers to a particular <|endoftext|> usage.

The paper also reports dataset-specific effects beyond absolute size. For example, FLAN v2 tends to transfer better to MMLU-style academic tasks, improving multiple-choice knowledge benchmarks, while OASST1 improves open-ended assistant behavior. These findings emphasize that curating a smaller, high-quality dataset targeted to the intended deployment scenario can outperform large but heterogeneous or misaligned corpora.

This partial <|endoftext|> arises because the evaluation tasks probe different capabilities and styles. MMLU is a collection of multiple-choice questions that reward precise factual recall, domain knowledge, and examination-style problem solving. Chatbot benchmarks evaluate open-ended dialogue quality, helpfulness, tone, refusal behavior, and multi-turn coherence—attributes that are not well captured by MMLU. The implication is practical: when selecting finetuning datasets and designing evaluation pipelines, practitioners should align dataset choice and evaluation to the intended <|endoftext|> application, and avoid over-interpreting improvements on a single benchmark as general improvements in all interaction types.
