In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the authors halved the learning rate and doubled the batch size for larger models to stabilize training. This is sensible because, as they scale from 7B to 33B/65B, they explicitly note that hyperparameters found at 7B mostly transfer except for learning rate and batch size, and they adjust these to keep optimization stable while backpropagating through dequantized, 4-bit weights into LoRA adapters; in practice, larger models tend to have larger effective curvature or Lipschitz constants, so a smaller step size helps satisfy stability conditions like α < 2/L, while a larger batch reduces gradient variance since Var(g) ∝ 1/B, which is particularly valuable when additional noise sources (e.g., block-wise 4-bit quantization, double dequantization, long-sequence memory paging) perturb the gradient signal; moreover, because only the adapter parameters are updated while the quantized base is frozen, too aggressive steps can overshoot and degrade the recovered 16-bit performance, whereas reducing α and increasing B improves signal-to-noise ratio and smooths updates, aligning with their goal of matching 16-bit baselines under NF4 quantization and ensuring that the dequantized computation path, XW^{BF16} + sXL_1L_2, yields stable gradients ∂E/∂L_i without amplifying quantization-induced errors.
