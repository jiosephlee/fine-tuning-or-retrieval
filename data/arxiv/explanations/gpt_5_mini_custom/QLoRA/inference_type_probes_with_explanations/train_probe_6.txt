According to the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', if the pretrained weights were uniformly distributed (e.g., $U(a,b)$) instead of normally distributed (e.g., $N(0,\sigma^2)$), $\text{NF4}$ would not be optimal. This is because NF4 is constructed by taking the $2^k$ quantile midpoints of a standard normal $N(0,1)$ and then normalizing into $[-1,1]$, yielding a codebook whose decision boundaries assign equal probability mass under a normal law and are therefore information-theoretically optimal for zero-mean normal weights after appropriate rescaling; in other words, its quantization levels minimize expected error for the assumed normal source by ensuring approximately uniform bin occupancy. If the true weight distribution is instead uniform, the normal-quantile codebook is misspecified: the induced bin probabilities under $U(a,b)$ are no longer equal, and the resulting rate–distortion is suboptimal compared to a quantizer tailored to $U(a,b)$ (e.g., a Lloyd–Max solution for a uniform source, which yields evenly spaced reconstruction levels and thresholds in $[-1,1]$). Since the paper’s optimality argument explicitly relies on the weights being “zero-centered normal” and on fixing the distribution up to a scale, a distributional mismatch (uniform rather than normal) breaks the equal-quantile premise and thus the information-theoretic optimality of NF4.
