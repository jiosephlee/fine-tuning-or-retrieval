In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', conceptually, the role of the quantization constant $c$ in mapping between low-bit and high-bit representations is to act as a scale factor. This is explicit in their quantization and dequantization definitions, where a high-precision tensor is mapped into a low-bit range by scaling and rounding, e.g., $\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{Int8}}=\mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{127}{\mathrm{absmax}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{FP32}})}\,\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{FP32}}\right)=\mathrm{round}\!\left(c^{\mathrm{FP32}}\cdot \mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{FP32}}\right)$, and the inverse mapping divides by the same factor, $\mathrm{dequant}(c^{\mathrm{FP32}},\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{Int8}})=\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{Int8}}/c^{\mathrm{FP32}}=\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{FP32}}$, so $c$ directly scales between the real-valued dynamic range and the discrete codebook; in block-wise quantization each block $i$ has its own $c_i$ to locally rescale the block and avoid outlier underutilization, and in QLoRA’s double quantization the idea is extended with a two-stage scaling, where first-level weight codes $\mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}}$ are dequantized using a (quantized) scale $c_2^{\text{k-bit}}$ that itself is dequantized by a higher-precision scale $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$, i.e., $\mathrm{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},c_2^{\mathrm{k\text{-}bit}},\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{k\text{-}bit}})=\mathrm{dequant}(\mathrm{dequant}(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},c_2^{\mathrm{k\text{-}bit}}),\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{k\text{-}bit}})=\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{BF16}}$, which underscores that these constants function as hierarchical scale factors bridging storage (low-bit) and computation (high-bit) domains without changing the underlying information beyond the chosen precision.
