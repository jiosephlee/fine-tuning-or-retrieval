In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', QLoRA dequantizes the stored weights to $\mathrm{BF16}$ before performing matrix multiplications to improve numerical precision. This is explicitly described in the method: QLoRA uses a 4-bit storage dtype (NF4) and a 16-bit computation dtype (usually BFloat16), so whenever a weight tensor is used it is dequantized to $\mathrm{BF16}$ and multiplied in 16-bit, as formalized by $\mathbf{Y}^{\mathrm{BF16}}=\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{BF16}}\ \text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\mathrm{FP32}},c_2^{\text{k-bit}},\mathbf{W}^{\text{NF4}})+\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_1^{\mathrm{BF16}}\mathbf{L}_2^{\mathrm{BF16}}$ and $\text{doubleDequant}(\cdot)=\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{BF16}}$, which ensures both forward and backward passes occur in 16-bit precision. Computing in $\mathrm{BF16}$ avoids the severe accumulation of rounding errors and dynamic-range issues that would arise from 4-bit arithmetic, preserves the fidelity of matrix multiplications and gradient propagation needed to optimize the LoRA parameters (since $\frac{\partial\mathbf{X}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}$ is computed in 16-bit after dequantization), and empirically matches 16-bit finetuning performance; the paper repeatedly reports that this mixed setup (low-bit storage, 16-bit compute) recovers 16-bit baselines while dramatically reducing memory, with NF4 and double quantization affecting only storage, not the high-precision compute path.
