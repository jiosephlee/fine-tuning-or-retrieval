In the paper 'QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs', the doubleDequant function performs an initial dequantization step that reconstructs supporting data from the first quantization level to enable the subsequent dequantization of the low-bit weight tensor $\mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}}$ into $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$, namely the quantization constants. This follows directly from their definition of double dequantization: Double Quantization first stores the per-block quantization constants $c_2$ for the 4-bit weights in a low-precision form (e.g., FP8) together with higher-precision scales $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$, and then reconstructs the original scale by dequantizing $c_2^{\text{k-bit}}$ via $c_1^{\text{FP32}}$ before dequantizing the weights; formally, they define $\text{doubleDequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, c_2^{\text{k-bit}}, \mathbf{W}^{\text{k-bit}}) = \text{dequant}(\text{dequant}(c_1^{\text{FP32}}, c_2^{\text{k-bit}}), \mathbf{W}^{\text{4bit}}) = \mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$, which means the first dequantization reconstructs the blockwise quantization constants (the scales) needed to undo the 4-bit quantization of $\mathbf{W}$ in the second step and yield $\mathbf{W}^{\text{BF16}}$.
