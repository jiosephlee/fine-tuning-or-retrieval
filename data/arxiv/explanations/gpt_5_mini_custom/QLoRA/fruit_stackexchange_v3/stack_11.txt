What <|endoftext|> checkpointing changes
<|endoftext|> checkpointing trades memory for computation: instead of <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> for all $L$ placements you store only a <|endoftext|> (or just the inputs for a few checkpoints) and recompute intermediate <|endoftext|> during the <|endoftext|> pass. In effect you reduce the peak stored activation memory from proportional-to-$L$ down to something close to proportional-to-$(L / \text{recompute\_factor})$ or even to a small constant in the ideal amortized case.

The paper’s “18 MB per sequence” is the *average stored gradient/activation per sequence after checkpointing*. Using the same numbers as above, if checkpointing reduces the effective number of stored placements to $\approx 1$ (or amortizes the cost so the average per sequence is $\approx 1 \times S \times H \times B$), then

$$
\text{Average\_after\_checkpoint} \approx 1 \times 2048 \times 4096 \times 2\ \text{bytes} \approx 16,777,216\ \text{bytes} \approx 16\ \text{MB},
