\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens"}

### How exactly are patch queries initialized and projected to the global dimension in the encoder cross-attention?
Question:
In Section 2.2.2, patch queries $P_{0,j}$ are initialized by pooling the byte embeddings of patch $p_j$ and then applying a linear projection $\mathcal{E}_C$. The text notes that patch representations are typically of dimension $h_{\mathcal{G}}$ whereas byte representations are $h_{\mathcal{E}}$, and that multihead outputs are concatenated later. Could you detail the tensor shapes through the cross-attention operation? Specifically:
- What is the shape of $P_{0,j}$ after pooling and $\mathcal{E}_C$?
- Are $Q$, $K$, $V$ in $\mathbb{R}^{U \times d_k}$ per head with $Ud_k = h_{\mathcal{G}}$ or $h_{\mathcal{E}}$?
- At what step are the multiple encoder heads concatenated to reach $h_{\mathcal{G}}$?
Answer:
Short answer: Pooled bytes produce an $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ vector which the encoder cross-attention linear $\mathcal{E}_C$ expands into $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ heads of size $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ (so $\mathcal{E}_C$ outputs a vector of length $U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}}$). During the multi‑head cross‑attention the queries, keys and values are handled per‑head with per‑head dimension $d_k = h_{\mathcal{E}}$, and after attention the $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ head outputs are concatenated to form the patch representation of size $h_{\mathcal{G}} = U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}}$ (possibly followed by $W_o$ / a residual). This is exactly what the paper describes when it says “we maintain $P_l$ as multiple heads of dimension $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ when doing cross-attention, and later, concat these representations into $h_{\mathcal{G}}$ dimensions.”

Detailed walk‑through (tensor shapes and intuition)
1) Pooling + $\mathcal{E}_C$ initialization
- You pool the encoder byte embeddings for patch $p_j$ ($e_i$ with size $h_{\mathcal{E}}$) into a single pooled vector. Call that pooled vector $s_j \in \mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}}$ (the paper uses max‑pooling for the first layer in experiments).
- The linear projection $\mathcal{E}_C$ has shape $\mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}} \times (h_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot U_{\mathcal{E}})}$ (paper’s notation). So
  $P_{0,j} = \mathcal{E}_C(s_j) \in \mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot U_{\mathcal{E}}}$.
- You then reshape $P_{0,j}$ into $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ separate head slots: $P_{0,j} \mapsto [p_{0,j}^{(1)},\dots,p_{0,j}^{(U_{\mathcal{E}})}]$ with $p_{0,j}^{(u)} \in \mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}}$.

Intuition: instead of producing a single $h_{\mathcal{G}}$ vector directly, the encoder initializes one vector per attention head in the local cross‑attention space; each head is at the local model dimension $h_{\mathcal{E}}$.

2) Q, K, V shapes during encoder cross‑attention
- Keys and values are projections of the byte hidden vectors $h_{l-1,i}$ which live in $\mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}}$ for the encoder local model. Those projections are split into $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ heads; the natural per‑head size is therefore $d_k = h_{\mathcal{E}}$ (so each key/value per head is size $h_{\mathcal{E}}$).
- Queries come from the per‑head pieces of $P_{l-1,j}$ (each of size $h_{\mathcal{E}}$) and are projected per head ($W_q$ acting on that head). Thus for each head $u$ you have
  $Q^{(u)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_p \times d_k},\; K^{(u)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{bytes\_in\_patch} \times d_k},\; V^{(u)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{bytes\_in\_patch} \times d_k}$
  with $d_k = h_{\mathcal{E}}$.
- The paper’s statement “we maintain $P_l$ as multiple heads of dimension $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ when doing cross-attention” implies (and the $\mathcal{E}_C$ shape confirms) that $U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot d_k = U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}} = h_{\mathcal{G}}$. So the multi‑head concatenation across $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ heads yields the global patch size $h_{\mathcal{G}}$.

Intuition: the local cross‑attention works in the encoder’s native per‑head size ($h_{\mathcal{E}}$) so queries/keys/values match the byte hidden dimension; only after attention we assemble the full global patch vector.

3) Where concatenation to $h_{\mathcal{G}}$ happens
- After computing attention per head, each head produces an output of shape $\mathbb{R}^{n_p \times h_{\mathcal{E}}}$. These $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ outputs are concatenated along the feature axis to get $\mathbb{R}^{n_p \times (U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}})}$.
- That concatenated vector is the patch representation $P_l$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n_p \times h_{\mathcal{G}}}$ with $h_{\mathcal{G}} = U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}}$. Optionally an output projection $W_o$ can be applied (as in the formula $P_l = P_{l-1} + W_o(\text{attn\_out})$) and the residual connects back into $P_l$.

A compact checklist answer to your three specific bullets
- Shape of $P_{0,j}$ after pooling and $\mathcal{E}_C$: pooled $s_j \in \mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}}$; after $\mathcal{E}_C$, $P_{0,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}}}$, which is then viewed as $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ heads of shape $\mathbb{R}^{h_{\mathcal{E}}}$ each.
- Are Q, K, V in $\mathbb{R}^{U\times d_k}$ per head with $U d_k = h_{\mathcal{G}}$ or $h_{\mathcal{E}}$? Per‑head dimension $d_k$ equals $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ here, and $U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot d_k = U_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot h_{\mathcal{E}} = h_{\mathcal{G}}$. So the multi‑head concatenation yields the global dimension $h_{\mathcal{G}}$.
- When are the heads concatenated to reach $h_{\mathcal{G}}$? After computing attention per head, the $U_{\mathcal{E}}$ head outputs are concatenated (and optionally passed through $W_o$) to form $P_l \in \mathbb{R}^{n_p \times h_{\mathcal{G}}}$.

Connection to broader design choices: this design keeps cross‑attention computations in the lightweight local dimension $h_{\mathcal{E}}$, so keys/values come from byte representations directly without blowing up per‑byte compute; concatenating heads after attention produces the larger global patch vectors that the latent/global transformer expects. This is exactly why the paper emphasizes maintaining $P_l$ as multiple heads of size $h_{\mathcal{E}}$ during cross‑attention and only concatenating later into $h_{\mathcal{G}}$.
