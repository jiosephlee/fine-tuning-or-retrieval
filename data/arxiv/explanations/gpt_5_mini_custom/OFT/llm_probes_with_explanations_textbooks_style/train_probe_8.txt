Chapter Title: Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning: Stability via a Radius Constraint on Orthogonal Transformations

# Background: Hyperspherical Energy and the Role of Neuron Angles

Text-to-image diffusion models can be adapted to downstream tasks by finetuning their parameters. However, unconstrained finetuning can degrade pretrained generative performance, cause instability, or lead to collapse. A central insight is that the angular relationships among neurons encode semantic structure, and preserving these relationships during finetuning helps maintain generative ability. This perspective is formalized by hyperspherical energy, a measure of pairwise similarity among normalized neurons on the unit hypersphere.

Consider a fully connected layer with a weight matrix $\\bm{W}=\\{\\bm{w}_1,\\dots,\\bm{w}_n\\}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\\times n}$, where each neuron $\\bm{w}_i\\in\\mathbb{R}^d$ is treated as a vector in $\\mathbb{R}^d$. Let $\\hat{\\bm{w}}_i:=\\bm{w}_i/\\|\\bm{w}_i\\|$ denote the normalized neuron, which lies on the unit hypersphere in $\\mathbb{R}^d$. The hyperspherical energy of the layer is defined as
$$
\\text{HE}(\\bm{W}):=\\sum_{i\\neq j}\\|\\hat{\\bm{w}}_i-\\hat{\\bm{w}}_j\\|^{-1}.
$$
This objective captures the aggregate angular configuration among neurons: preserving $\\text{HE}(\\bm{W})$ preserves the pairwise angles, thereby preserving the pretrained semantic structure.

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is introduced to explicitly preserve hyperspherical energy. The key idea is that applying a common orthogonal transformation to all neurons in a layer leaves pairwise angles invariant. Formally, with a pretrained weight matrix $\\bm{W}^0$, OFT learns an orthogonal matrix $\\bm{R}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\\times d}$ so that the finetuned weights are $\\bm{W}=\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0$. Because $\\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, the normalized directions of the transformed neurons retain their pairwise angular relationships, and thus $\\text{HE}(\\bm{W})=\\text{HE}(\\bm{W}^0)$.

# Orthogonal Finetuning: Preserving Angles by Layer-Shared Rotations

In OFT, the forward pass of a fully connected layer with input $\\bm{x}\\in\\mathbb{R}^d$ changes from $\\bm{z}=(\\bm{W}^0)^\\top\\bm{x}$ to
$$
\\bm{z}=\\bm{W}^\\top\\bm{x}=(\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0)^\\top\\bm{x},\\qquad \\text{s.t. } \\bm{R}^\\top\\bm{R}=\\bm{R}\\bm{R}^\\top=\\bm{I}.
$$
Here $\\bm{I}$ denotes the identity matrix. The orthogonality constraint ensures that all neuron directions are transformed by a rotation or reflection, preserving pairwise angles and thus hyperspherical energy. Intuitively, OFT modifies semantic content by reorienting neuron directions while retaining the pretrained relational geometry among neurons. This provides a principled structural constraint that prevents finetuning from destroying the pretrained generative ability.

To initialize from the pretrained model exactly, OFT sets $\\bm{R}=\\bm{I}$ at the beginning of training. With this identity initialization, the initial forward pass equals that of the pretrained model, and training proceeds by learning $\\bm{R}$ under orthogonality.

# Efficient Orthogonal Parameterization via the Cayley Transform

Directly optimizing over orthogonal matrices can be computationally expensive. To make orthogonality both differentiable and efficient, OFT adopts the Cayley parameterization:
$$
\\bm{R}=(\\bm{I}+\\bm{Q})(\\bm{I}-\\bm{Q})^{-1},\\qquad \\bm{Q}=-\\bm{Q}^\\top.
$$
The skew-symmetric parameter $\\bm{Q}$ ensures $\\bm{R}$ is orthogonal. This parameterization produces matrices in the special orthogonal group (determinant $1$), which is sufficient in practice. Identity initialization for $\\bm{R}$ corresponds to initializing $\\bm{Q}=\\bm{0}$, since $(\\bm{I}+\\bm{0})(\\bm{I}-\\bm{0})^{-1}=\\bm{I}$.

To further improve parameter efficiency, $\\bm{R}$ can be structured as a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix with $r$ blocks:
$$
\\bm{R}=\\text{diag}(\\bm{R}_1,\\dots,\\bm{R}_r),\\qquad \\bm{R}_i\\in\\text{O}(d/r).
$$
This reduces the trainable parameter count from $\\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ to $\\mathcal{O}(d^2/r)$, and optionally to $\\mathcal{O}(d^2/r^2)$ if blocks are shared. Crucially, regardless of block structure, $\\bm{R}$ remains orthogonal and therefore continues to preserve hyperspherical energy.

# Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning: The Radius Constraint

While OFT preserves pairwise angles and stabilizes finetuning, further stability can be achieved by explicitly constraining how far the transformation deviates from the identity. Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT) imposes an additional radius constraint on the orthogonal transform:
$$
\\bm{z}=\\bm{W}^\\top\\bm{x}=(\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0)^\\top\\bm{x},\\qquad \\text{s.t. } \\bm{R}^\\top\\bm{R}=\\bm{I},\\quad \\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon.
$$
The constraint $\\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon$ bounds the deviation of $\\bm{R}$ from the identity. Geometrically, this constrains the finetuned model to lie within a hypersphere of a fixed radius centered at the pretrained weights, thereby limiting how much the neuron directions can change. This explicit bound on the angular deviation preserves the pretrained structure while allowing controlled adaptation, and it improves finetuning stability by avoiding large, destabilizing rotations.

This radius constraint complements OFT’s angle-preserving property. OFT keeps pairwise angles invariant, ensuring hyperspherical energy is unchanged. COFT further restricts the transformation’s magnitude by bounding the distance to the identity, which makes training more stable and less sensitive to hyperparameters such as learning rate and iteration count. In practice, this means COFT can maintain the balance between flexibility and regularity more effectively than unconstrained OFT or standard finetuning.

# Implementing the Radius Constraint via the Cayley Parameterization

Imposing $\\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon$ directly within the Cayley parameterization is nontrivial because $\\bm{R}$ depends on $\\bm{Q}$ through a matrix inverse. To make the constraint tractable, a Neumann series approximation is applied to the Cayley transform:
$$
(\\bm{I}-\\bm{Q})^{-1}=\\bm{I}+\\bm{Q}+\\bm{Q}^2+\\dots,
$$
under the assumption that the series converges in the operator norm. Substituting this into $\\bm{R}=(\\bm{I}+\\bm{Q})(\\bm{I}-\\bm{Q})^{-1}$ yields
$$
\\bm{R}\\approx \\bm{I}+2\\bm{Q}+\\mathcal{O}(\\bm{Q}^2).
$$
This approximation implies that small deviations of $\\bm{R}$ from $\\bm{I}$ correspond to small deviations of $\\bm{Q}$ from $\\bm{0}$. Therefore, the radius constraint can be moved inside the Cayley transform and enforced directly on $\\bm{Q}$:
$$
\\|\\bm{Q}-\\bm{0}\\|\\leq \\epsilon',
$$
for a hyperparameter $\\epsilon'$ related to $\\epsilon$. This constraint on $\\bm{Q}$ is straightforward to enforce using projected gradient descent: after each update, project $\\bm{Q}$ back to the feasible set $\\|\\bm{Q}\\|\\leq \\epsilon'$ while maintaining skew-symmetry $\\bm{Q}=-\\bm{Q}^\\top$. Initializing $\\bm{Q}=\\bm{0}$ ensures $\\bm{R}=\\bm{I}$ at the start of training.

By constraining $\\bm{Q}$, COFT effectively bounds the radius of the orthogonal transformation on the hypersphere while still exactly preserving pairwise angles (via orthogonality). This combination—angle preservation together with bounded deviation—yields improved finetuning stability without sacrificing the structural advantages of OFT.

# Intuition and Effects of the Radius Constraint on Stability

The radius constraint $\\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon$ limits the angular deviation of neuron directions from their pretrained configuration. Because OFT preserves hyperspherical energy, the semantic structure encoded in pairwise neuron angles remains intact. COFT’s additional bound further restricts the magnitude of transformation, preventing large rotations or reflections that could destabilize training or degrade generation quality.

This design induces a clear and interpretable continuum of behavior governed by $\\epsilon$. When $\\epsilon$ is large, the deviation constraint becomes loose, and COFT behaves similarly to OFT, allowing larger angular adjustments while still preserving pairwise angles. When $\\epsilon$ is small, the transformation remains close to the identity, and the finetuned model behaves increasingly similar to the pretrained diffusion model. In between, COFT provides controlled flexibility: it can adapt neuron directions enough to achieve the target downstream behavior but not so much as to risk instability.

Empirically, COFT improves finetuning stability relative to both standard finetuning and OFT. The explicit bound ensures smoother training dynamics, makes results less sensitive to the number of iterations, and helps avoid collapse. In practical use, COFT allows one to set a relatively large iteration count or learning rate without destabilizing the model, because $\\epsilon$ constrains the solution to remain within a safe neighborhood of the pretrained weights.

# Relationship to OFT, Re-Scaled OFT, and LoRA

COFT builds directly on OFT. Both methods learn a layer-shared orthogonal matrix $\\bm{R}$, preserving pairwise neuron angles and thus hyperspherical energy. COFT adds the explicit radius constraint $\\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon$, implemented via a bound on the Cayley parameter $\\bm{Q}$ through the Neumann approximation. This combination provides stronger regularity than OFT alone, improving stability and convergence behavior while maintaining the structural benefits of orthogonality.

Re-scaled OFT extends OFT by learning a diagonal magnitude matrix $\\bm{D}=\\text{diag}(s_1,\\dots,s_n)$ with strictly positive entries, yielding the forward pass $\\bm{z}=(\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0\\bm{D})^\\top\\bm{x}$. Because hyperspherical energy depends only on directions, scaling neuron magnitudes does not alter $\\text{HE}(\\bm{W})$. This re-scaling increases flexibility with a small parameter cost. While COFT focuses on constraining angular deviation via $\\bm{R}$, re-scaled OFT shows that controlled magnitude adjustments can be layered on top of orthogonal transformations without violating the core angle-preservation principle.

Compared to LoRA, which adds a low-rank matrix to update weights additively, OFT and COFT update weights multiplicatively via full-rank orthogonal transforms. One can rewrite $(\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0)^\\top\\bm{x}=(\\bm{W}^0+(\\bm{R}-\\bm{I})\\bm{W}^0)^\\top\\bm{x}$ to see that OFT induces an additive update $(\\bm{R}-\\bm{I})\\bm{W}^0$ that is low-rank when $\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}$ is low-rank. However, the crucial distinction is structural: OFT and COFT constrain the transform to be orthogonal, provably preserving pairwise angles and thus hyperspherical energy, whereas LoRA constrains the update to be low-rank without directly addressing angular preservation. COFT’s radius constraint further regularizes the orthogonal transform, which contributes to improved stability and faster, smoother convergence relative to LoRA under comparable parameter budgets.

# Practical Considerations and Benefits

COFT offers several practical benefits grounded in its design. First, it improves finetuning stability by explicitly limiting deviation from the pretrained model through the radius constraint, making training less sensitive to hyperparameters and reducing the risk of collapse. Second, because COFT preserves hyperspherical energy, it maintains the pretrained semantic structure, which helps retain generative fidelity and diversity. Third, COFT introduces no additional inference overhead: once training concludes, one can pre-multiply $\\bm{R}$ into $\\bm{W}^0$ to obtain $\\bm{W}=\\bm{R}\\bm{W}^0$, and inference proceeds identically to the pretrained model. Finally, with block-diagonal parameterization and Cayley-based optimization, COFT remains parameter-efficient while retaining the full benefits of orthogonal transformations.

In sum, Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning augments Orthogonal Finetuning with a principled radius constraint on the Cayley-parameterized orthogonal matrix. By bounding $\\|\\bm{R}-\\bm{I}\\|\\leq \\epsilon$ and implementing this bound via a constraint on the skew-symmetric parameter $\\bm{Q}$, COFT achieves improved finetuning stability while provably preserving hyperspherical energy. This combination delivers controllable, stable adaptation of text-to-image diffusion models to downstream tasks without sacrificing pretrained generative performance.
