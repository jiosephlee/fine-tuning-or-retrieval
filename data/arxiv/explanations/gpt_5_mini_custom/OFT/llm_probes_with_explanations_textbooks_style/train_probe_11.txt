Chapter Title: Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning and the Limit of Zero Deviation

# Background: Finetuning Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved remarkable performance, but guiding them to perform specialized downstream tasks—such as subject-driven generation and controllable generation—requires carefully designed finetuning strategies. A central challenge is preserving the pretrained model’s generative ability while adapting it to new tasks. Conventional finetuning methods implicitly rely on keeping the Euclidean distance between pretrained weights and finetuned weights small by using small learning rates. This heuristic, however, does not explicitly preserve the structural properties believed to encode semantic behavior, and can be unstable or insufficient for downstream control.

A key insight motivating orthogonal finetuning is that the angles between neurons encode critical semantic information. The directional component of neurons—rather than their magnitudes—plays the dominant role in preserving and manipulating semantics in deep models. As a result, finetuning that respects neuron angles can maintain pretrained semantics while enabling controlled modifications.

# Hyperspherical Energy and Orthogonal Invariance

To measure the preservation of semantic structure across neurons, hyperspherical energy is used. For a fully connected layer with weight matrix $\bm{W}=\{\bm{w}_1,\dots,\bm{w}_n\}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$, where $\bm{w}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the $i$-th neuron, the hyperspherical energy is defined as
$\text{HE}(\bm{W}):=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat{\bm{w}}_i-\hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1}$,
where $\hat{\bm{w}}_i:=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|$ denotes the normalized neuron. This energy depends only on the pairwise angular relationships among neurons on the unit hypersphere.

Crucially, hyperspherical energy is invariant under a shared orthogonal transformation applied to all neurons in a layer. If we transform all neurons by the same orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$, the normalized directions preserve pairwise angles. More formally, if $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ for an orthogonal $\bm{R}$ and pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$, then $\text{HE}(\bm{W})=\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)$. This invariance allows finetuning that modifies neuron directions while provably preserving intrinsic angular structure associated with semantics.

# Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT): Preserving Angles While Adapting

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) adopts the principle of preserving hyperspherical energy by reparameterizing layer updates as shared orthogonal transformations. Given a pretrained layer $\bm{W}^0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$ and input $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$, OFT modifies the forward pass from $\bm{z}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$ to
$\bm{z}=\bm{W}^\top\bm{x}=(\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$,
with the orthogonality constraints $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{R}\bm{R}^\top=\bm{I}$. The finetuning thus learns a layer-shared orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ per layer, initialized as $\bm{I}$ so the process starts exactly from the pretrained model. This design embodies the optimization objective of minimizing the hyperspherical energy difference:
$\min_{\bm{W}}\left\|\text{HE}(\bm{W})-\text{HE}(\bm{W}^0)\right\|,$
which achieves its attainable minimum of zero when $\bm{W}$ differs from $\bm{W}^0$ only by rotation or reflection.

Intuitively, OFT fine-tunes the canonical coordinate system in which neurons reside, rather than the neurons themselves in arbitrary directions. This keeps angle-based semantic structure intact while enabling geometry-preserving adjustments that are empirically effective for both subject-driven and controllable generation tasks.

# Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT): Bounding Deviation

While OFT preserves pairwise angles via orthogonal transformations, it can still explore a broad set of orthogonal matrices. To further stabilize finetuning and explicitly bound deviation from the pretrained model, Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT) introduces an additional constraint that limits the learned transformation’s distance from the identity:
$\bm{z}=\bm{W}^\top\bm{x}=(\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x},~~~\text{s.t.}~\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{R}\bm{R}^\top=\bm{I},~\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon.$

The orthogonality preserves hyperspherical energy, while $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$ explicitly keeps the finetuned model within a controlled radius of the pretrained weights on the hypersphere. This dual constraint combines structural preservation with bounded flexibility, improving finetuning stability and making hyperparameters easier to set in practice. Empirically, this additional constraint yields smoother convergence and reduces the risk of collapse.

# Efficient Orthogonal Parameterization and the Role of the Cayley Transform

Implementing orthogonal constraints efficiently is essential. Direct methods such as Gram–Schmidt are expensive. COFT and OFT employ the Cayley parameterization to construct $\bm{R}$:
$\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1},$
where $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$ is skew-symmetric. This yields orthogonal matrices with determinant $1$ and provides a differentiable, efficient path to maintain orthogonality during finetuning.

To incorporate the deviation constraint $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$ into the Cayley transform, we use a Neumann series approximation to analyze the relationship between $\bm{R}$ and $\bm{Q}$:
$\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\approx\bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2),$
under the assumption that the Neumann series converges in the operator norm. This shows that if $\bm{Q}$ is small, then $\bm{R}$ is close to $\bm{I}$. Consequently, we can move the deviation constraint into the $\bm{Q}$-space by enforcing $\|\bm{Q}-\bm{0}\|\leq\epsilon'$ with projected gradient descent, initializing $\bm{Q}$ at $\bm{0}$ to achieve identity initialization for $\bm{R}$. This mechanism directly connects the magnitude of $\bm{Q}$ to the radius constraint in COFT and provides a tractable way to tune $\epsilon$.

# The Limit $\epsilon\to 0$: COFT Approaches the Pretrained Model

COFT’s defining property is the deviation bound $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$. As $\epsilon$ decreases, the learned orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ is restricted to lie closer to the identity. In the limit $\epsilon\to 0$, the constraint forces $\bm{R}\to\bm{I}$. Substituting into the forward pass,
$\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}\to(\bm{I}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x},$
the finetuned layer’s output converges to the pretrained layer’s output. Layer-wise, this means the entire finetuned model’s behavior approaches that of the pretrained model.

The Cayley-based perspective further clarifies this limit. Using the approximation $\bm{R}\approx\bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$ and enforcing $\|\bm{Q}\|\leq\epsilon'$ with $\epsilon'\to 0$, we obtain $\bm{Q}\to\bm{0}$ and thus $\bm{R}\to\bm{I}$. Therefore, both in the original $\bm{R}$-space and the Cayley $\bm{Q}$-space, shrinking the allowable deviation radius drives COFT back toward the pretrained configuration.

Empirically, this theoretical tendency manifests in qualitative behavior: smaller $\epsilon$ yields finetuned models that increasingly resemble the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. When $\epsilon$ is large, the flexibility approaches that of OFT. When $\epsilon$ is small, COFT tightly preserves pretrained behavior while making conservative, stable adjustments.

# Practical Implications: Flexibility, Stability, and Convergence

The $\epsilon$ parameter in COFT directly governs the balance between flexibility and regularity. Larger $\epsilon$ permits more aggressive angular adjustments via orthogonal transformations, similar to unconstrained OFT. Smaller $\epsilon$ enforces proximity to the pretrained model, yielding more stable finetuning that is less sensitive to iteration counts and learning rates. This stability is particularly valuable in practice, as it reduces the burden of hyperparameter tuning and mitigates collapse.

In subject-driven generation, COFT and OFT exhibit fast yet stable convergence, preserving subject identity and aligning with text prompts over a wide range of iterations. In controllable generation, they converge significantly faster than adapter-based baselines, delivering strong controllability with far fewer trainable parameters and without additional inference-time overhead. The smooth convergence dynamics observed with COFT make training predictable and robust, further supporting the utility of the deviation constraint.

# Parameter Efficiency and Orthogonality at Scale

To keep orthogonal parameterization efficient at large dimensions, OFT and COFT employ block-diagonal orthogonal matrices
$\bm{R}=\text{diag}(\bm{R}_1,\dots,\bm{R}_r)$
to reduce parameter counts from $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r)$, or even $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r^2)$ with block sharing. Importantly, the resulting $\bm{R}$ remains orthogonal, preserving hyperspherical energy regardless of the block structure. Compared to low-rank additive updates used in LoRA, OFT/COFT achieve parameter efficiency by exploiting sparsity through block-diagonal orthogonality, while operating in a multiplicative (transformative) manner that protects pretrained angular structure.

# Summary: COFT’s Zero-Deviation Limit

Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning couples two explicit constraints: it preserves the pretrained model’s hyperspherical energy by restricting finetuning to shared orthogonal transformations, and it limits deviation from the identity transform via $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$. As $\epsilon$ approaches zero, the allowed transformation collapses to the identity, making the finetuned forward pass converge to the pretrained forward pass. This limit formalizes the intuitive and empirical observation that COFT with small $\epsilon$ yields behavior increasingly similar to the pretrained model, while larger $\epsilon$ recovers the flexibility of OFT. In practice, tuning $\epsilon$ provides a principled control knob for trading off adaptation strength against semantic preservation and finetuning stability.
