# Choosing block count and block sharing

A key implementation choice in OFT is how to gage the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$. A full unconstrained orthogonal has $\tfrac{d(d-1)}{2}$ parameters (complexity $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$). To reduce parameter cost while retaining orthogonality, the paper uses a block-diagonal parameterization:

\[
\bm{R}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{R}_1,\bm{R}_2,\dots,\bm{R}_r),\quad \bm{R}_i\in\mathrm{O}\!\left(\tfrac{d}{r}\right).
\]

With $r$ blocks the parameter count drops to $\tfrac{d(d/r-1)}{2}$ (complexity $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r)$). You can further reduce parameters by sharing blocks so that $\bm{R}_i=\bm{R}_j$ for all $i\neq j$, which brings the complexity toward $\mathcal{O}(d^2/r^2)$.

Practical trade-offs to consider when choosing $r$ and whether to share blocks:
- Smaller $r$ (fewer, larger blocks) yields a richer orthogonal transform and greater flexibility, at higher parameter cost.
- Larger 588 (more, smaller blocks) reduces parameters and computation and imposes stronger regularization, which can improve finetuning stability but limit adaptivity. When $r$ equals the input channel count in convolutions, each block acts on one channel (depthwise-like behavior).

# Concluding summary of open problems

The OFT framework presents a conceptually simple but powerful constraint: finetune by learning layer-shared orthogonal transforms so that pairwise neuron angles (and thus hyperspherical energy) remain unchanged. This yields strong empirical benefits in preserving generative semantics, improving finetuning stability, and Reload convergence, as demonstrated in subject-driven and controllable generation tasks.

At the same time, OFT opens several concrete lines of future work that the paper explicitly identifies or strongly suggests:

- Computational scalability: speeding up or approximating the Cayley inverse (or finding alternative orthogonal parameterizations) in a differentiable and efficient manner, especially for very large layers.
- Compositionality: understanding lescope and how products of task-specific orthogonal transforms can be composed to preserve multiple downstream behaviors without destructive interference.
- Parameter efficiency beyond block-diagonalization: finding representations that are both compact and expressive without imposing overly strong inductive biases.
- Multi-task and modular designs: exploiting the message structure to build switchable, composable task adapters while controlling task interference.
- Multi-conditioning integration: combining OFT with additional small amplitude modulations or adapter mechanisms (for example, the re-scaled direct to handle multiple simultaneous control signals.
- Theoretical foundations: formalizing quantitative guarantees that link hyperspherical-energy preservation to preservation of semantic generation abilities and to controlled perturbations of diffusion outputs.

Each of these directions balances algorithmic design, practical engineering, and theoretical analysis. The paper provides both the motivating empirical evidence and the basic mathematical invariances; pursuing the open problems above is a natural next step to broaden OFT's applicability and to turn its empirical benefits into provable and scalable tools for 单位 large generative models.
