Chapter 2: Background: Text-to-Image Diffusion Models  Diffusion-based generative modeling is the family of methods that underlies many recent text-to-image systems cited in the paper. Latent-space diffusion models instead run the diffusion process on a lower-dimensional learned representation of images. In all these cases the denoising backbone receives auxiliary information during sampling so that the reverse process respects both text and the external control signal. Stable Diffusion and DALL·E 2 also rely on text encoders as part of their pipelines (with DALL·E 2’s joint CLIP latent featuring prominently). The paper emphasizes that text guidance alone can be ambiguous or insufficient for fine-grained control. To obtain more precise control over generated imagery, practitioners add conditioning modalities beyond plain text. This conditioning steers the sample towards images that match the semantic content of the prompt. Text encoders provide the semantic representation of a prompt that conditions the denoising network. The paper refers to attention weights explicitly when it explains where parameter-efficient finetuning methods (for example LoRA) are usually applied: in transformer-based architectures, the attention weights are a frequent target for adaptation because they are the interface through which text guidance influences image synthesis. These finetuning choices reflect different points on the flexibility–stability trade-off: from fully flexible full-model finetuning, to LoRA’s low-rank additive bias, to OFT’s angle-preserving multiplicative adaptation, to COFT’s angle-preserving but radius-constrained adaptation. Pixel-space diffusion models learn the denoising process directly on image pixels. Stable Diffusion leverages a VQ-GAN-style latent as its operating space, while DALL·E 2 constructs and uses a joint latent embedding space based on CLIP. Transformer-style attention mechanisms are commonly used inside the denoising backbone, and cross-attention layers are the natural place to inject text-conditioned context: the image-side queries attend to text-side keys and values extracted by the text encoder. The paper points out two concrete treatments of text encoders in recent systems: GLIDE trains the text encoder as part of the model, while Imagen uses a frozen, pretrained text encoder. In modern diffusion architectures the conditioning information from a text encoder is typically fused into the denoising network at multiple points. The paper highlights a major architectural axis of variation among text-to-image diffusion systems: whether the diffusion model is trained and operated in raw pixel space or in a learned latent space. The paper further introduces COFT (Constrained OFT), which pairs the orthogonality constraint with an explicit bound on how far the orthogonal transform may deviate from the identity, and a re-scaled variant that also learns per-neuron magnitude scalings. DreamBooth is the representative method the paper follows for this scenario; it finetunes the diffusion model with a reconstruction objective and a class-specific prior-preservation loss to keep the subject identity. The paper treats these latent / pixel distinctions as important background for how modern, large-scale text-to-image systems are built and how components like text encoders and pretraining choices vary across implementations. It learns a layer-shared orthogonal matrix \,R\, that left-multiplies a pretrained weight matrix \,W^0\, so that the finetuned weight is \,W = R W^0\,. - Additional control signals: explicit structural or spatial cues such as Canny edges, segmentation maps, or facial landmarks are supplied alongside text to steer the denoiser. In a text-to-image setting the learned denoising (or score) network is conditioned on text, so sampling proceeds by running the learned reverse (denoising) dynamics while providing text guidance at each denoising step. How the extra conditioning is incorporated depends on the adaptation method: ControlNet introduces an auxiliary network and finetunes it to provide control while T2I-Adapter adds adapter modules. - Adapter or auxiliary modules (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter): add small networks or adapter blocks that process the control signals and inject their outputs into the denoiser. Because the learned orthogonal matrix is folded into the pretrained weights before or at inference, OFT does not introduce additional runtime overhead compared with the original pretrained model. The paper studies two families of such conditioning:  - Subject-driven conditioning: given a few images of a subject plus a prompt, generate images depicting that subject in new contexts. The paper also notes that OFT is architecture-agnostic in principle: it can be applied to fully connected, convolutional, and transformer layers; for convolutional layers the block-diagonal form of the orthogonal matrix has a channel-wise interpretation. In practice and for fair comparison to LoRA, the paper typically applies OFT to the same attention weights that LoRA targets. Two models mentioned in the paper that follow this approach are GLIDE and Imagen. This directly updates the denoiser weights and can capture strong subject-specific detail, but risks destabilizing pretrained generative behavior unless done carefully. Works referenced in the paper that sit in this family include denoising diffusion probabilistic models and score-based models; the paper cites foundational diffusion research and places modern text-to-image systems on top of that framework. ControlNet and T2I-Adapter are two approaches the paper discusses that adapt pretrained diffusion models to accept these extra control inputs during finetuning. Inference (or sampling) in a text-conditioned diffusion model consists of running the learned reverse dynamics while providing the prompt representation at each step. The practical consequences of those different choices—training efficiency, parameter count, convergence behavior, preservation of pretrained generative capabilities, and inference overhead—are the empirical dimensions the paper uses to evaluate and compare finetuning strategies. - Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its variants (COFT, re-scaled OFT): OFT is multiplicative rather than additive. Taken together, these design choices identify where and how finetuning operates in diffusion pipelines: either by changing how conditioning is fused (attention weights / adapters), by adding modules to interpret extra signals (ControlNet / adapters), or by transforming existing weight parameters in constrained ways that preserve structural properties learned in pretraining (OFT). This keeps the number of trainable parameters small and biases the update to a low-dimensional subspace. The paper also emphasizes an inference-relevant design point of its own method: Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) produces learned orthogonal transforms of existing weight matrices that can be merged back into the original weights. Critically, orthogonal transforms preserve pairwise angles among neuron weight vectors (hyperspherical energy), and OFT leverages this property to maintain the pretrained model’s relational structure among neurons while allowing directional changes that adapt generation to a downstream task. The core idea shared by these methods is to define a stochastic noising process that progressively corrupts data (images) and then learn a reverse denoising process that maps noise back to a data sample. These approaches typically enlarge the runtime model (auxiliary modules are active at inference) and require training the new modules to translate control inputs into appropriate conditioning. Finetuning in text-to-image diffusion systems can act at several locations in the pipeline, and the paper contrasts multiple strategies that have been used:  - Full-model finetuning (DreamBooth style): finetune many parameters of the pretrained diffusion model using a reconstruction objective on subject images plus a prior-preservation loss. - Low-rank additive updates (LoRA): rather than updating the full weight matrix, LoRA parameterizes an additive, low-rank update to key weight matrices (commonly attention weights in transformer blocks). The paper frames this picture as the basis for contemporary models such as GLIDE, Imagen, Stable Diffusion and DALL·E 2, which all adopt diffusion-style training and sampling even though they differ in implementation details and the space in which diffusion is applied.
