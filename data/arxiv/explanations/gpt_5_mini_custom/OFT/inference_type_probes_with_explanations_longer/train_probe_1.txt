In the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", the constrained variant COFT enforces $\| \bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ so that the finetuned weights lie within a hypersphere of radius $\epsilon$ centered at the pretrained weights $\bm{M}^0$, which geometrically primarily bounds the $\ell_2$ norm $\|\bm{M}-\bm{M}^0\|_2$, i.e. the Euclidean distance. This follows directly from the COFT forward map $\bm{M}=\bm{R}\bm{M}^0$ with $\bm{R}$ orthogonal and close to identity. Writing the deviation as $\bm{M}-\bm{M}^0=(\bm{R}-\bm{I})\bm{M}^0$ and using submultiplicativity of the operator norm yields
$\|\bm{M}-\bm{M}^0\|_2=\|(\bm{R}-\bm{I})\bm{M}^0\|_2\le\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|_2\|\bm{M}^0\|_2\le\epsilon\,\|\bm{M}^0\|_2$,
so the finetuned weights lie in an Euclidean ball around $\bm{M}^0$ with radius proportional to $\epsilon$. Since $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, it preserves norms and hyperspherical energy, so the constraint $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ is precisely an explicit bound on the Euclidean displacement away from $\bm{M}^0$ while keeping pairwise angles intact.

Moreover, the paper motivates COFT as “constrained deviation from the pretrained model” and shows how the Cayley parameterization enforces the orthogonality while making the deviation constraint practical: with $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\approx \bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$, the condition $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ is implemented by an equivalent small-norm constraint $\|\bm{Q}\|\le\epsilon'$. Geometrically, because neurons are considered on the unit hypersphere (angles drive semantics and magnitudes are normalized in hyperspherical energy), keeping $\bm{R}$ close to $\bm{I}$ bounds the angular deviation per neuron and thus confines the entire weight matrix to a neighborhood that is well-approximated by an Euclidean ball in parameter space. This is why COFT improves finetuning stability: it explicitly limits the Euclidean drift $\|\bm{M}-\bm{M}^0\|_2$ while preserving the relational angular structure that encodes pretrained semantics.
