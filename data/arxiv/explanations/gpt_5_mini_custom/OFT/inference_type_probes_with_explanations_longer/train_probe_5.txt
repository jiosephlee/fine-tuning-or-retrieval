According to the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', if finetuning instead minimized hyperspherical energy, pretrained semantic structures would likely be destroyed. The paper defines hyperspherical energy for a layer with neuron weights W = {w_i} as E(W) := ∑_{i≠j} ||\hat{w}_i - \hat{w}_j||^{-1}, where \hat{w}_i = w_i / ||w_i|| are unit-norm directions. Minimizing this energy tends to spread neurons as uniformly as possible over the unit hypersphere, i.e., it pushes pairwise angles to a configuration akin to a spherical code with maximal separation and minimal redundancy. However, the authors argue that in pretrained text-to-image diffusion models, the pairwise angular relationships among neurons encode semantic structure critical for generation: angles capture the “phase-like” relational information that organizes features, attention heads, and filters to reflect the learned data distribution. Driving the configuration toward uniformity necessarily alters these angles, thereby changing the relational geometry that underpins the model’s semantics.

In contrast, the OFT objective preserves hyperspherical energy by constraining the finetuning to orthogonal transforms W = R W^0 with R^\top R = I, which keep all pairwise angles invariant. Since hyperspherical similarity and energy are functions only of directions, an orthogonal transform maintains ∠(\hat{w}_i, \hat{w}_j) for all i,j, preventing disruption of the pretrained relational structure while still allowing controlled semantic modification via a global rotation/reflection of the neuron basis. The paper’s toy autoencoder experiment and the analogy to Fourier phase support that angular information carries semantics, while magnitude is largely irrelevant; thus, a procedure that explicitly reduces E(W) (making neurons uniformly spaced) would erase the non-uniform, task-specific angular patterns learned during pretraining. For classification, minimizing E(W) can improve generalization by reducing redundancy; but for finetuning diffusion models, the same operation would overwrite the intricate, semantically aligned angular structure, leading to a loss of generative fidelity and controllability.
