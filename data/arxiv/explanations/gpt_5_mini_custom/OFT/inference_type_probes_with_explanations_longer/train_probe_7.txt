In the paper “Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning”, the 2D Fourier analogy identifies the spectrum analogous to neuron directions that preserve semantics as the phase spectrum. This is sensible because, in a 2D Fourier decomposition of an image, the transform can be written as F(u,v) = A(u,v)\,e^{i\phi(u,v)}, where A(u,v) is the magnitude and \phi(u,v) is the phase. Classical results and the paper’s intuition highlight that the inverse transform with an altered magnitude but the original phase, \mathcal{F}^{-1}(\tilde A(u,v)\,e^{i\phi(u,v)}), largely preserves the spatial layout and semantic content, whereas the inverse with preserved magnitude but randomized phase destroys recognizable structure. The authors draw a direct analogy: neuron “directions” (angles) on the unit hypersphere correspond to phase-like information, while neuron magnitudes correspond to amplitude-like information; thus, preserving pairwise angular relationships among neurons is akin to preserving phase, which carries semantics.

This analogy is reinforced by their toy autoencoder experiment and the hyperspherical framework. Let \hat{\bm{w}}_i = \bm{w}_i / \|\bm{w}_i\| denote a normalized neuron direction; the model’s hyperspherical energy \text{HE}(\bm{W}) := \sum_{i\neq j} \|\hat{\bm{w}}_i - \hat{\bm{w}}_j\|^{-1} depends only on the angles among neurons (i.e., cosine similarities). Orthogonal transformations \bm{R} with \bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I} preserve these pairwise angles, just as phase-preserving manipulations preserve semantics in the Fourier domain. Empirically, the authors show that replacing inner products by purely angular interactions (cosine terms) at test time can nearly reconstruct input images, whereas using only magnitudes fails, mirroring the Fourier observation that \phi(u,v) governs semantic structure. Therefore, constraining finetuning to orthogonal, angle-preserving updates (and optionally re-scaling with a diagonal \bm{D} that does not affect \text{HE}) preserves the “phase-like” semantic core while allowing controlled adaptation of the model’s behavior.
