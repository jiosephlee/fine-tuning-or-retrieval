In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT) variant adds an explicit $\epsilon$-deviation constraint to keep the finetuned model within a small neighborhood of the pretrained model, thereby addressing the primary risk that plain OFT might still allow: deviation from pretrained behavior. This is stated directly in the COFT forward pass (Eq. (7)): $\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$ subject to the dual constraints $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=\bm{I}$ and $\lVert \bm{R}-\bm{I}\rVert\le \epsilon$. While OFT already preserves hyperspherical energy by using a layer-shared orthogonal transform $\bm{R}$, energy preservation alone does not bound how far the transformed neurons can rotate from their pretrained directions, and large coherent rotations can alter generation behavior even if pairwise angles are preserved. COFT explicitly limits the deviation of $\bm{R}$ from the identity, which translates to limiting the angular displacement of all neurons, thereby keeping the finetuned weights $(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)$ close to $\bm{W}^0$ in operator norm and preventing unwanted drift from pretrained semantics.

The paper further shows how to implement this constraint efficiently via Cayley parameterization and a first-order Neumann approximation: with $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ and $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$, we have $\bm{R}\approx \bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$, so enforcing $\lVert \bm{R}-\bm{I}\rVert\le \epsilon$ reduces to enforcing $\lVert \bm{Q}\rVert\le \epsilon'$ via projected gradient descent. This makes the deviation constraint practical and directly controls the finetuning “radius” around the pretrained model. Empirically, the paper reports that smaller $\epsilon$ yields behavior closer to the pretrained diffusion model, while larger $\epsilon$ recovers the flexibility of OFT, and overall COFT improves finetuning stability (e.g., it remains stable when DreamBooth collapses and LoRA drifts, and it converges smoothly in controllable generation). These observations support the claim that COFT’s explicit neighborhood constraint addresses the main residual risk in OFT—unbounded yet energy-preserving rotations—by trading a small amount of flexibility for robust preservation of pretrained behavior.
