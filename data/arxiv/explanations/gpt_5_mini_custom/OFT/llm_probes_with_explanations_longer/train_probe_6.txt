In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors note that Cayley parametrization requires a costly computational operation that limits scalability — the matrix inverse. This follows directly from their choice to enforce orthogonality via the Cayley transform, parameterizing the layer-shared transform as R = (I + Q)(I - Q)^{-1} with a skew-symmetric Q satisfying Q = -Q^{\top}. Maintaining exact orthogonality throughout training means recomputing (I - Q)^{-1} whenever Q changes, and differentiating through that inverse as well. For a d × d transform, a dense matrix inverse has cubic time complexity, roughly O(d^{3}), and incurs substantial memory overhead; even if implemented via solving linear systems, the asymptotic cost remains tied to the same bottleneck. Since OFT places such orthogonal transforms at the level of attention and convolutional layers in large diffusion models where d can be in the hundreds or thousands, repeatedly forming (I - Q)^{-1} at every training step becomes a nontrivial computational burden.

The paper’s design and empirical sections explicitly acknowledge and mitigate this limitation. First, they justify Cayley over alternatives like Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, which, while differentiable, is “often too expensive” in practice; Cayley is the more efficient of the two but still hinges on the inverse. Second, they introduce a block-diagonal structure for R: R = \mathrm{diag}(R_{1}, \dots, R_{r}) with each block R_{i} \in \mathrm{O}(d/r), which reduces both parameter count and inverse size, lowering the per-step cost from O(d^{3}) to roughly O((d/r)^{3}) per block and enabling further sharing to push complexity toward O(d^{2}/r^{2}) in parameters. They also use a Neumann-series perspective, R ≈ I + 2Q + \mathcal{O}(Q^{2}), to move the COFT deviation constraint inside the Cayley parameter (i.e., \|Q\| \le \epsilon'), but that approximation is for constraint handling rather than replacing the inverse needed for exact orthogonality. Hence, while Cayley is the practical choice for enforcing R^{\top}R = I, the necessity of computing (I - Q)^{-1} is precisely why the authors state it slightly limits OFT’s scalability and motivate their structural efficiency tricks.
