### Compositionality of multiple OFT transforms: since orthogonal matrices don't commute, how is order handled?
Question:
The paper mentions a compositionality potential: multiply orthogonal matrices from multiple finetuning tasks to combine skills. But in general, \(\mathbf{R}_1\mathbf{R}_2 \neq \mathbf{R}_2\mathbf{R}_1\). If we learn OFT matrices for two tasks A and B, does the composed model depend on whether we apply \(\mathbf{R}_A\) then \(\mathbf{R}_B\) or vice versa? Is there a canonical order, or could we constrain the parameterization (e.g., block structure) so they commute and avoid order dependence?
Answer:
In the OFT paper the authors point out that one really nice feature of orthogonal finetuning is that “if you learn two orthogonal <|endoftext|>  
$$
R_A,\;R_B \in O(d)
$$
for tasks A and B, then their product $R_B\,R_A$ is still orthogonal, so you can stack them without ever leaving the orthogonal manifold.” However, they also remark (in the very last paragraph) that whether this <|endoftext|> “multiply-the-matrices” recipe actually <|endoftext|> the <|endoftext|> capabilities of both tasks at once is still an open problem. In particular, nothing in the paper forces
$$
R_A R_B \;=\; R_B R_A,
$$
and in general orthogonal matrices do not commute. So if you literally take your <|endoftext|> weight $W^0$ and then do
$$
W_{AB} = R_B\,R_A\,W^0
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
W_{BA} = R_A\,R_B\,W^0,
$$
you will in fact get two different models that may not exhibit the same blend of “skill A” and “skill B.”
