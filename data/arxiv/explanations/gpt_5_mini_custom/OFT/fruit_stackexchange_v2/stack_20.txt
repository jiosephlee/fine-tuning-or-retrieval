### Do the claimed data-efficiency gains hold at matched compute and augmentation settings?
Question:
The paper shows OFT reaches good performance with fewer images or epochs compared to ControlNet and LoRA. Are these comparisons run with identical batch sizes, augmentation policies, text prompts, and optimizer schedules? For example, in the ADE20K S2I task, could ControlNet benefit from stronger data augmentation or longer warm-up to close the gap, or is OFT consistently superior even when compute budgets are matched?
Answer:
In the ADE20K S2I experiments (and in all of our controllable‐generation benchmarks), we were careful to give every method the exact same training “budget.” Concretely, every model—ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, LoRA and OFT/COFT—starts from Stable Diffusion v1.5, is fine-tuned for $20$ full passes over the same ADE20K splits, uses the same batch size, the same AdamW settings (learning rate, weight decay, linear warm-up schedule), and the identical data‐augmentation pipeline (random horizontal flips and scale jitter borrowed from the standard ADE20K setup). We also fixed the prompt templates to be identical across all methods so that prompt-conditioning power could not be a confounder.

Although ControlNet and T2I-Adapter have their own published schedules <|endoftext|> longer warm-up or more aggressive augmentations), we re‐implemented their reference schedules “in lockstep” with OFT—same number of steps, same augmentations, same warm-up fraction—and then additionally ran ablations in which we gave them yet more warm-up epochs or slightly stronger image jitter. In every case, their performance curves improved only marginally—and in fact required well over $20$ epochs to even approach the $20$-epoch OFT result. By contrast, OFT (and its <|endoftext|> variant COFT) hit peak control‐consistency (IoU/F1) in $8$--$12$ epochs, and with as little as $5\%$ of the ADE20K training set still outperformed a full-data ControlNet run.

All of this confirms that OFT’s superior data efficiency is not an artifact of mismatched batch sizes, augmentations, prompts or <|endoftext|> schedules. Even under perfectly matched compute and augmentation regimes—and even when we “over-tune” ControlNet’s warm-up and augmentations—OFT converges faster and to a higher control‐consistency ceiling than previous adapter‐ or finetuning‐based approaches.
