### What happens if the pretrained weight \(\mathbf{W}^0\) is not full-rank?
Question:
Some arguments comparing OFT to LoRA assume \(\mathbf{W}^0\) is full-rank. However, due to architectural redundancies or training quirks, certain layers may be rank-deficient. In that case, how does OFT’s update \((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I})\mathbf{W}^0\) behave, and does it limit the expressive power compared to LoRA’s explicit low-rank additive updates?
Answer:
In Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) every layer’s updated weight is simply

$$
W = R\,W^0,
$$

with $R$ orthogonal and $W^0$ the <|endoftext|> weight. <|endoftext|> one can write

$$
W = W^0 + (R - I)\,W^0,
$$

so the “delta” $(R - I)W^0$ lives entirely in the column space of $W^0$. In particular, since an orthogonal matrix $R$ is <|endoftext|>

$$
\mathrm{rank}(W) = \mathrm{rank}(R\,W^0) = \mathrm{rank}(W^0).
$$

If $W^0$ is rank-deficient (say $\mathrm{rank}(W^0) = k < d$), then $W$ and $\Delta = (R - I)W^0$ both lie in the same $k$-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^d$, and there is no way for OFT alone to “unlock” directions outside that span.

By contrast, LoRA parameterizes

$$
W = W^0 + A\,B
$$

where $A B$ is (low)-rank but its column space can be chosen arbitrarily. A LoRA update $A B$ can therefore introduce new basis <|endoftext|> that are orthogonal to the original $\mathrm{Im}(W^0)$, potentially increasing the effective rank of the layer.

In plain terms, OFT’s update $(R - I)W^0$ can only re-orient and rescale within the subspace already spanned by $W^0$, whereas LoRA can “grow” that subspace by adding entirely new directions. In practice, however, <|endoftext|> diffusion-model weights are almost numerically full-rank, so OFT’s column-space limitation is usually not a problem—and indeed it is precisely this constraint that <|endoftext|> the hyperspherical energy <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> and stabilizes finetuning. If you do run into a genuinely rank-deficient layer and need to inject novel features, a simple remedy is to hybridize OFT with a small <|endoftext|> adapter <|endoftext|> a lightweight LoRA block) or to use the <|endoftext|> OFT variant (which still cannot expand the column space but does give per-neuron magnitude flexibility).
