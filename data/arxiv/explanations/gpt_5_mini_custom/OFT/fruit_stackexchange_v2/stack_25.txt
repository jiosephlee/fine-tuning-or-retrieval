### Can we make them commute?  
If you really wanted to eliminate the “order problem” you would need to restrict all your task-specific transforms $R_i$ so that they lie in a common abelian subgroup of $O(d)$. Concretely, two standard strategies are:

1. **Simultaneous diagonalization.** Force each <|endoftext|> to be diagonal in the same basis (i.e.\ each one is a signed-coordinate or phase-shift matrix in a fixed orthonormal frame). Diagonal orthogonal matrices obviously commute, but they can only flip or permute axes and so are extremely limited in their expressive power.
2. **Shared $2 \times 2$ rotation blocks.** In the <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> of Section 3.2, ensure that *all* tasks use the *same* block-partition and the *same* invariant 2-D rotation planes. Then each <|endoftext|> is <|endoftext|> with the *same* block-structure, and if within each block you rotate by <|endoftext|> $\theta_i$ around the *same* axis, those rotations commute. Again, this is more flexible than pure diagonal signs but still restricts you to a single set of rotation planes.

Both of these approaches drive you toward smaller subgroups of $O(d)$. You trade off exactly the freedom that OFT was designed to give you—namely, the ability to re-angle your neurons in arbitrary ways that preserve <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> distances—against the desideratum that your task-transforms commute.
