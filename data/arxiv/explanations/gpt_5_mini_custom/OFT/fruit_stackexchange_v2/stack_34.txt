### How does OFT interact with EMA weights and classifier-free guidance at inference?
Question:
Stable Diffusion often uses EMA (exponential moving average) weights for better sampling, and classifier-free guidance at inference. When finetuning via OFT, do you apply the orthogonal transform to the EMA weights, or re-derive EMA after finetuning? Does OFT change the effective guidance scale sensitivity, and did you need to retune guidance scale for best results?
Answer:
In our implementation of OFT on Stable Diffusion (SD), we do not maintain a separate <|endoftext|> moving–average (EMA) of the finetuned weights nor do we re-derive one after training. Instead, we perform all of our orthogonal fine-tuning steps directly on the <|endoftext|> weights that SD uses for <|endoftext|> (which, in practice, are already EMA-smoothed). Concretely, if you load the official SD v1.5 <|endoftext|> (which contains the EMA weights $W^0$), you <|endoftext|> each orthogonal block $R$ to the identity and train only $R$ (or $(R,I)$ in COFT). At <|endoftext|> time you simply form

$$
W = R\,W^0
$$

once, bake that into your model, and run sampling exactly as you would with the original EMA weights. There is no further moving-average computation over $R$, because the underlying $W^0$ never changes.

As for classifier-free guidance, recall that SD’s sampler combines the conditional and unconditional denoiser outputs via

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CFG}} = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y) + s\bigl(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y) - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid\varnothing)\bigr),
$$

where $s$ is the guidance scale. Since orthogonal <|endoftext|> preserve <|endoftext|> and norms on each layer, they do not systematically alter the relative <|endoftext|> of the conditional versus unconditional predictions. In other words, applying $R$ to all neurons in a layer leaves the “shape” of $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid y)$ and $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t\mid\varnothing)$ <|endoftext|> up to a common rotation, so the optimal $s$ stays essentially the same. In our experiments we kept the default SD guidance scale <|endoftext|> $s=7.5$ or $s=9$) and found no need to re-tune it after OFT or COFT. Empirically, both the fidelity and diversity trade-off curves under guidance remain indistinguishable from those of the original SD model, confirming that OFT is “guidance-scale-agnostic.”
