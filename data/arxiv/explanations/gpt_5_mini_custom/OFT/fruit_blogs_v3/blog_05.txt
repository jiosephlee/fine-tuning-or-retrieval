# From Edges and Segments to Photos: OFT for Controllable Generation

Modern text-to-image diffusion models are powerful, but text alone can be ambiguous when you need precise image structure: a rough edge map, a segmentation layout, or a set of facial landmarks. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a principled finetuning strategy that adapts pretrained diffusion models to take these control signals and produce photorealistic images that faithfully follow them. This post explains how OFT converts rough controls (Canny edges, segmentation maps, face landmarks) into accurate images, why it’s more stable and efficient than prior adapters, and how the paper measures control fidelity with a new control consistency metric.

## Control consistency: measuring whether the control was followed

A key contribution is the control consistency metric: instead of just eyeballing results, the paper measures how well generated images reproduce the original control signal by extracting the control back from the generated image (using off-the-shelf detectors/segmenters) and comparing it to the input.

Examples of the metric per task:

- C2I: IoU and F1 between input edges and edges detected from the generated image.
- S2I: mean IoU, mean / overall accuracy between input segmentation and predicted segmentation from the generated image.
- L2F: mean L2 distance between input landmarks and landmarks predicted from the generated image.

This metric directly <|endoftext|> fidelity to the control modality rather than only image realism or CLIP alignment.
