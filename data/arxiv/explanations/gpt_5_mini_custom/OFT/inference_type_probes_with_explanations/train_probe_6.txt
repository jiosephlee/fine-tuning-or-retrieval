In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors propose Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning (COFT), which improves finetuning stability by imposing an additional constraint that bounds the deviation of the Cayley-parameterized orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ from the identity via $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\leq\epsilon$, referred to as the radius constraint. This is sensible because COFT retains OFT’s core invariance—orthogonal transformations preserve hyperspherical energy—while explicitly limiting how far the finetuned weights $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{W}^0$ can move from the pretrained $\bm{W}^0$, thereby constraining semantic drift and collapse; concretely, with the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ and the Neumann approximation $\bm{R}\approx\bm{I}+2\bm{Q}+\mathcal{O}(\bm{Q}^2)$, the bound $\|\bm{R}-\bm{I}\|\le\epsilon$ translates to a simple projected-gradient constraint $\|\bm{Q}\|\le\epsilon'$, making enforcement practical while keeping initialization at $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$ (i.e., $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$) to start exactly from the pretrained model; empirically, this explicit deviation control yields smoother, more stable optimization (e.g., insensitivity to learning rate/iterations and avoidance of collapse) by striking a better flexibility–regularity trade-off than unconstrained finetuning, all while preserving pairwise neuron angles and thus the pretrained model’s semantic structure.
