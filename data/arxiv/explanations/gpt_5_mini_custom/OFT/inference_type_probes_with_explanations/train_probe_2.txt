In the paper 'Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning', the authors note that if neuron directions are analogous to Fourier phase, then the neuron operation analogous to changing only the Fourier amplitude (leaving semantics largely intact) corresponds to scaling the magnitudes. This follows from their angle–magnitude decoupling perspective: they argue and empirically show (via a toy autoencoder) that semantic content is primarily encoded in neuron directions (angles) rather than magnitudes—reconstructing with angular information alone nearly recovers the image, while using only magnitudes fails—mirroring the classical result that the phase spectrum preserves image semantics whereas the magnitude spectrum does not. Formally, their hyperspherical energy objective depends only on normalized neurons, i.e., on directions \(\hat{\bm{w}}_i=\bm{w}_i/\|\bm{w}_i\|\), so any scaling \(\bm{w}_i \mapsto s_i \bm{w}_i\) with \(s_i>0\) leaves \(\text{HE}(\cdot)\) unchanged, just as modifying Fourier amplitudes leaves the phase unchanged. They operationalize this in re-scaled OFT with a diagonal matrix \(\bm{D}=\mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_n)\) in the forward pass \(\bm{z}=(\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\bm{D})^\top \bm{x}\), where \(\bm{D}\) scales neuron magnitudes without rotating their directions; thus, magnitude scaling is the neural analogue of adjusting Fourier amplitude and, by design, perturbs semantics minimally while modulating strength.
