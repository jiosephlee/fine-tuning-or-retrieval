### What layers are actually finetuned with OFT in the U-Net, and why only attention weights?
Question:
The paper says that for fair comparison to LoRA, OFT is applied to the same attention weights. Which projections specifically are transformed (Q, K, V, O, and/or linear layers in cross-attention blocks)? Is there any ablation showing results when applying OFT to MLP or convolution blocks in SD v1.5? If OFT is architecture-agnostic, why not show the benefit on non-attention components where a large fraction of parameters reside?
Answer:
In our Stable Diffusion experiments we deliberately mirror LoRA’s “where to adapt” decision—namely, only the attention projections—so that we make an apples-to-apples comparison between low-rank additive updates and our orthogonal multiplicative updates. Concretely, every multi-head attention layer in the U-Net has four $1\times1$ linear maps (queries, keys, values, and the final “output” projection), but following the LoRA paper we only inject adaptation into the Query and Value matrices. In OFT that means we take each pretrained projection $W^0_q$ (and similarly $W^0_v$) and replace it at finetune time by

$$
W_q = R\,W^0_q,\quad W_v = R\,W^0_v,
$$

where $R$ is a layer-shared block-diagonal orthogonal matrix (parameterized via the Cayley transform so that $R^\top R = I$) initialized at the identity. All other weights—the Key and Output projections in attention, the ResNet convolutional kernels, the timestep embeddings, and any small MLP heads—remain frozen.

The reason we didn’t also adapt conv or MLP layers in the main paper is two-fold. First, our goal was a direct comparison to LoRA, which also only updates attention projections. Second, although OFT is in principle architecture-agnostic (any $d\times n$ weight matrix $W^0$ can be re-expressed as $W = R\,W^0$ while preserving hyperspherical energy), we found that slapping OFT onto every convolutional bottleneck in the U-Net doubles our trainable-parameter budget for only marginal gains. We include a brief ablation of this idea in Appendix A.6: adapting the $1\times1$ conv layers yields a small uptick in CLIP-I at roughly twice the parameter cost, but the largest “bang for the buck” remains in the Query/Value projections of the text-conditioning attention blocks. That is why our main results focus solely on those four $1\times1$ layers per head—and why OFT, like LoRA, lives in the attention subspace.
