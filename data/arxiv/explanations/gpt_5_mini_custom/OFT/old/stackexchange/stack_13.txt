### 1. Block–diagonal \(R\) and its rank

When we choose a block–diagonal orthogonal \(R\) with \(r\) equal‐sized blocks,
\[
R = \begin{bmatrix}
R_1 & & \\
 & \ddots & \\
 & & R_r
\end{bmatrix}
\quad\text{with each }R_i \in O\bigl(d/r\bigr),
\]
then
\[
R - I \;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
R_1 - I_{d/r} & & \\
 & \ddots & \\
 & & R_r - I_{d/r}
\end{bmatrix}
\]
and so the rank simply adds:
\[
\mathrm{rank}(R - I)
\;=\;
\sum_{i=1}^r \mathrm{rank}\bigl(R_i - I_{d/r}\bigr).
\]
Because a *generic* orthogonal block \(R_i\) has no eigenvalue exactly \(1\) (aside from measure‐zero events), each block contributes \(\mathrm{rank}(R_i - I)=d/r\), and hence
\[
\mathrm{rank}(R - I)
\;=\;
r\,(d/r)
\;=\;
d,
\]
i.e.\ *full* rank. In that sense, a vanilla block–diagonal \(R\) does *not* automatically give you a low‐rank update.
