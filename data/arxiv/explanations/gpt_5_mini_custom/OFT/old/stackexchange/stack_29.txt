### In the toy autoencoder experiment, what exact "angular activation" was applied at test time?
Question:
The figure description mentions replacing inner product with angular or magnitude-only computations at test time, without training with cosine activation. What precisely was computed for the angular-only case? Was it \(z = \cos(\angle(\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}))\), i.e., normalized dot product? Or some monotonic function of the angle? Could you provide the exact formulas used for the three test-time variants?
Answer:
In the toy‐autoencoder of Figure 1, **all three test‐time feature maps** were obtained by taking exactly the same vectors **w** (the learned convolutional kernel) and **x** (the input patch) but then replacing the usual dot‐product with one of three scalars. Concretely, if we write the standard “inner‐product” feature as

$$
z_{\mathrm{inner}} = w^\top x,
$$

then the **magnitude‐only** and **angle‐only** variants were simply

$$
z_{\mathrm{mag}} = \|w\|\,\|x\|,\quad
z_{\mathrm{ang}} = \frac{w^\top x}{\|w\|\,\|x\|} = \cos\!\bigl(\angle(w,x)\bigr).
$$

No other nonlinearity (e.g.\ an $\arccos$ to recover the angle itself) was applied. In other words, at test time the network’s convolutional features were replaced by exactly these three choices:

1. **Inner‐product**  
   $$
   z = w^\top x.
   $$
2. **Magnitude‐only**  
   $$
   z = \|w\|\,\|x\|.
   $$
3. **Angular‐only**  
   $$
   z = \frac{w^\top x}{\|w\|\,\|x\|} = \cos\bigl(\angle(w,x)\bigr).
   $$

Despite having been trained with the usual dot‐product, you can see that simply throwing away magnitude information and feeding the cosine‐similarity into the decoder is enough to almost perfectly reconstruct the input, whereas keeping only the magnitudes destroys all semantic content.
