### Bottom line  
As things stand in the paper,  
– Composition of OFT is *order-sensitive* because $O(d)$ is non-abelian.  
– There is no built-in canonical order, so in practice you pick one task first, then the next, and so on.  
– If you really need order-invariance you must further constrain your transforms to lie in an abelian subalgebra of $\mathfrak{o}(d)$, for example by fixing a shared eigenbasis or rotation planes—but at the cost of much reduced flexibility.

Exploring just how much you can restrict the orthogonal family (to make it commute) before you lose the very gains OFT provides is an interesting open direction that the authors themselves highlight.
