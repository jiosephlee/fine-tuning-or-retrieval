# From 5 Photos to a Personalized Generator: A DreamBooth-Style Tutorial with OFT

In this tutorial, we’ll show you how to turn just five photos of your favorite subject into a powerful, personalized image generator. We’ll build on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and use Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) to adapt the model without losing its rich, pretrained knowledge. By the end, you’ll know how to prepare your data, initialize the OFT parameters, set up block-diagonal orthogonal transforms, and run a stable finetuning loop. We’ll also share prompt tricks and best practices to preserve identity while giving your model the freedom to follow new text guidance.

## 1. Prerequisites

Before you begin, make sure you have:
- A working installation of Stable Diffusion v1.5 and its dependencies (PyTorch, CUDA, Hugging Face Diffusers, etc.).
- Five high-quality images of your subject (different poses, lighting, and backgrounds).
- Familiarity with Python scripting and command-line tools.
- Enough GPU memory (at least 16 GB recommended) to finetune on a few hundred steps.

## 2. Data Preparation

The quality and diversity of your five subject images matter. Follow these guidelines:

1. **Variety of Poses**  
   Capture your subject from different angles: front, profile, three-quarter.  
2. **Consistent Resolution**  
   Resize all images to 512×512 pixels (Stable Diffusion’s default) while preserving aspect ratio.  
3. **Clean Backgrounds**  
   Simple or uncluttered backgrounds help the model focus on the subject.  
4. **Filename Convention**  
   Name your files `subject_01.jpg`, …, `subject_05.jpg` and place them in a folder called `data/subject/`.

Once your images are ready, organize them like this:

```
project/
  data/
    subject/
      subject_01.jpg
      subject_02.jpg
      subject_03.jpg
      subject_04.jpg
      subject_05.jpg
  scripts/
  output/
```

## 3. Identity Initialization

We’ll follow the DreamBooth pattern and introduce a new token (for instance, `<V>`) to represent your custom subject. The steps:

1. **Choose a Rare Token**  
   Pick a placeholder `<V>` that doesn’t clash with common words.  
2. **Replace References**  
   In captions, refer to your subject with `<V>`. For example, if your subject is “Alex,” use:  
   “A portrait of `<V>` wearing a blue scarf.”  
3. **Compile Training Pairs**  
   For each of the five images, pair it with 4–6 captions that describe clothing, setting, and style, always including `<V>`. Store these in a JSON file:

```json
[
  {
    "image": "data/subject/subject_01.jpg",
    "caption": "A photo of <V> standing in a sunlit room wearing a red shirt."
  },
  ...
]
```

This setup teaches the model to link `<V>` with your subject’s appearance.

## 4. Setting Up Block-Diagonal Orthogonal Parameters

OFT adapts the pretrained model by inserting *orthogonal* transforms into certain layers. These transforms preserve the “angles” between neurons, ensuring we keep the model’s original generative power.

### 4.1 Where to Insert OFT

We recommend finetuning the **cross-attention blocks** of the UNet backbone, matching where LoRA typically operates. In code, you’ll identify these weight matrices (e.g., `proj_in` and `proj_out` of each attention layer).
