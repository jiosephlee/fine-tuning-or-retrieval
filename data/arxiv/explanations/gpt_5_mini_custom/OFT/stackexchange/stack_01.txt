\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning"}

### How exactly does OFT guarantee hyperspherical energy preservation under orthogonal transformations?
Question:
The paper defines the hyperspherical energy of a layer $W = \{w_1,\dots,w_n\} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$ as $\mathrm{HE}(W) := \sum_{i \neq j} \lVert \hat{w}_i - \hat{w}_j \rVert^{-1}$ where $\hat{w}_i = w_i / \lVert w_i \rVert$. They claim that if we left-multiply by an orthogonal matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ (i.e., $W' = R W$), the pairwise angles (and thus the hyperspherical energy) are preserved. Could someone provide a short derivation showing that $\angle(w_i, w_j) = \angle(Rw_i, Rw_j)$ implies $\lVert \hat{w}_i - \hat{w}_j \rVert = \lVert \widehat{Rw_i} - \widehat{Rw_j} \rVert$ and therefore $\mathrm{HE}(W') = \mathrm{HE}(W)$? Also, does the presence of bias vectors or subsequent nonlinearities affect this invariance claim at the layer level?
Answer:
Short answer: because orthogonal matrices preserve inner products and norms, they preserve the cosine of the angle between any two weight vectors; the Euclidean distance between the unit-normalized vectors is a simple function of that cosine, so it is preserved too. Therefore the pairwise terms in the hyperspherical energy are unchanged and so is HE. Biases and usual pointwise nonlinearities do not change that statement about the weight vectors themselves (they may change the layer’s functional output), but layers or operations that renormalize or otherwise modify the weights can affect the hyperspherical energy.

Derivation and intuition. Let $w_i,w_j \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be two columns (neurons) and $\hat w_i := w_i/\|w_i\|$ their unit-normalized versions. The hyperspherical energy uses the pairwise inverse distances $\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\|^{-1}$. A convenient algebraic identity relates this distance to the angle between $w_i$ and $w_j$: since $\|\hat w_i\|=\|\hat w_j\|=1$,

$$
\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\|^2 = \|\hat w_i\|^2+\|\hat w_j\|^2-2\langle \hat w_i,\hat w_j\rangle
= 2-2\cos\theta_{ij},
$$

where $\cos\theta_{ij}=\langle \hat w_i,\hat w_j\rangle$ and $\theta_{ij}=\angle(w_i,w_j)$. Thus $\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\| = \sqrt{2-2\cos\theta_{ij}}$. So any transformation that exactly preserves $\cos\theta_{ij}$ for all pairs preserves these unit-vector distances and therefore preserves every term in HE.

Now let $R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ be orthogonal, i.e. $R^\top R=I$. Orthogonal matrices preserve inner products and norms:

$$
\langle Rw_i, Rw_j\rangle = w_i^\top R^\top R w_j = w_i^\top w_j,
\qquad \|Rw_i\|=\|w_i\|.
$$

Hence the cosine between $Rw_i$ and $Rw_j$ equals the cosine between $w_i$ and $w_j$, and the angle is preserved: $\angle(Rw_i,Rw_j)=\angle(w_i,w_j)$. From the norm preservation we also get $\widehat{Rw_i}=Rw_i/\|Rw_i\|=R(w_i/\|w_i\|)=R\hat w_i$. Combining with the inner-product preservation,

$$
\|\widehat{Rw_i}-\widehat{Rw_j}\|
= \|R\hat w_i-R\hat w_j\|
= \|R(\hat w_i-\hat w_j)\|.
$$

Because orthogonal $R$ preserves norms, $\|R(\hat w_i-\hat w_j)\|=\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\|$. Therefore every pairwise distance of normalized columns is unchanged, and so

$$
\mathrm{HE}(RW)=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\widehat{Rw_i}-\widehat{Rw_j}\|^{-1}
=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\hat w_i-\hat w_j\|^{-1}=\mathrm{HE}(W).
$$

Remarks about biases and nonlinearities. The hyperspherical energy as defined in the paper is a property of the weight vectors (columns of $W$) after normalization. Adding a bias vector $b$ to the layer output (so output = $W^\top x + b$) does not alter the weight columns themselves, therefore it does not change HE$(W)$. Likewise, applying pointwise nonlinearities (ReLU, GELU, etc.) after the linear map does not change the weight vectors; HE of the weight matrix remains the same. However, note the important distinction between invariance of the weight-property and invariance of the layer’s functional behavior: even though HE is preserved under a left-multiplication by an orthogonal $R$, the mapping $x \mapsto W^\top x$ is changed to $x\mapsto (RW)^\top x$ unless you also change the input basis (i.e. pre-multiply inputs by $R$). In OFT the orthogonal $R$ is applied to the weights only, which preserves angular relations among neurons (and thus HE) but does change how the layer acts on inputs; that is intentional for finetuning.

Caveats: any operation that rescales, reparametrizes, or renormalizes weight vectors before computing HE (for example, explicit weight-normalization layers that change $\hat w_i$, or training steps that replace $W$ by a different parameterization not equivalent to $RW$) will affect HE. Also if you apply different orthogonal transforms to different neurons (not a single left-multiplication $R$ shared across the layer) then pairwise angles are not necessarily preserved.

Summary: orthogonal left-multiplication preserves inner products and norms, so it preserves angles and unit-vector distances; hence OFT’s layer-wise $R$ leaves the hyperspherical energy unchanged. Biases and pointwise nonlinearities do not alter the weight vectors and therefore do not affect this invariance of HE, though they do affect the layer’s input-output function.
