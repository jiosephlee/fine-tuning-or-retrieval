In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the gradient of the DPO loss assigns the largest update weight to comparison pairs with a large value of $\sigma\bigl(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\bigr)$, i.e., cases where the implicit reward model ranks the dispreferred completion above the preferred completion. This follows directly from the DPO gradient expression derived in the paper: $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_\text{ref}) = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\bigl[\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w))\bigl(\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)-\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\bigr)\bigr]$, where $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)}$. The scalar weight $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic function, which is monotone increasing; it is close to $1$ when its argument is large and positive (meaning $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)\gg \hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)$, a severe misranking) and close to $0$ when its argument is large and negative (meaning the model correctly ranks $y_w$ above $y_l$ with margin). Therefore, the contribution of each comparison to the gradient is largest precisely when the implicit reward misorders the pair, and smallest when the model already agrees with the preference.

This weighting is a standard property of cross-entropy over pairwise preferences under the Bradley–Terry parameterization the paper uses. Because the DPO objective is $\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO} = -\mathbb{E}\bigl[\log \sigma\bigl(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\bigr)\bigr]$, its gradient naturally focuses learning where the current policy violates the preference model, amplifying corrections on “hard” or misranked pairs. Intuitively, the update increases $\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$ and decreases $\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$, but the step size is modulated by how wrong the implicit reward $\hat{r}_\theta$ is on that example. The paper notes this dynamic, per-example importance weighting is crucial for stability; a naive unweighted probability-ratio objective can lead to degeneration, whereas the sigmoid weighting concentrates learning on errors, akin to well-behaved logistic regression, while the $\beta$ factor properly calibrates the strength of updates under the KL constraint.
