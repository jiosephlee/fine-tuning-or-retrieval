In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', to optimize a policy directly from preference data instead of doing reward estimation followed by RL, DPO trains the policy using a simple supervised loss, namely binary cross-entropy. The key derivation starts from the KL-constrained RLHF objective and its optimal policy form, which implies that for any reward function the optimal policy satisfies
$$
\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta} r(x,y)\right).
$$
Rearranging gives an equivalent parameterization of rewards in terms of policy likelihood ratios,
$$
r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x).
$$
Under the Bradley–Terry preference model, human comparisons depend only on reward differences, so the unknown partition term \( \beta\log Z(x)\) cancels when forming \(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\). Substituting the reparameterization into the Bradley–Terry likelihood yields a preference probability expressed entirely in terms of the policy and the reference policy,
$$
p^*(y_w \succ y_l \mid x)=\sigma\!\left( \beta \log \frac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)} \right),
$$
which leads directly to a maximum-likelihood objective over preferences that is just binary cross-entropy:
$$
\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}(\pi;\pi_{\text{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim \mathcal{D}}\left[\log \sigma\!\left( \beta \log \frac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)} \right)\right].
$$

This supervised loss is equivalent to fitting a Bradley–Terry model but with a change of variables that makes the policy itself the parameterization of the implicit reward; in particular, DPO optimizes the same KL-regularized reward objective as RLHF without explicitly estimating a separate reward model or running RL. The gradient of the loss clarifies its supervised nature: defining the implicit reward \( \hat{r}_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}\), the update increases \( \log \pi(y_w\mid x)\) and decreases \( \log \pi(y_l\mid x)\), weighted by how incorrectly the current implicit reward orders the pair:
$$
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_\text{DPO} = -\beta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sigma\!\big(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w)\big)\,\big( \nabla_\theta \log \pi(y_w\mid x) - \nabla_\theta \log \pi(y_l\mid x) \big) \right].
$$
Thus, DPO turns preference learning into standard supervised training with binary cross-entropy on pairwise comparisons, while preserving the optimality conditions of the original RLHF objective through the policy–reward reparameterization and the Bradley–Terry likelihood.
