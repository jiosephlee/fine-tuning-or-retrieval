In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors warn that a naive probability-ratio objective can cause the model to concentrate its probability mass on single high-reward responses, a concise failure mode described as mode collapse. This follows directly from the KL-constrained RLHF objective and its closed-form optimizer: the optimal policy under reward maximization with a KL penalty is given by
$\\pi_r(y\\mid x) \\propto \\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\big(r(x,y)/\\beta\\big)$.
If one removes or effectively weakens the KL term (equivalently, lets $\\beta\\to 0$), the exponential tilting $\\exp(r/\\beta)$ becomes extremely sharp, and the distribution concentrates on the $\\arg\\max_y r(x,y)$, i.e., a single “high-reward” completion. The paper explicitly emphasizes that the KL constraint “prevents mode-collapse to single high-reward answers,” and DPO’s reparameterization keeps this anchoring via $\\pi_{\\text{ref}}$ while optimizing a maximum-likelihood objective that is equivalent to the RLHF objective. Thus, a naive objective that only pushes probability ratios toward preferred responses without a KL anchor or proper calibration can drive the model to allocate nearly all mass to a few top-reward strings, sacrificing diversity and robustness.

The gradient analysis further clarifies why DPO avoids this collapse and why the naive version degenerates. DPO’s update is
$\\nabla_\\theta \\mathcal{L}_{\\text{DPO}} \\propto \\mathbb{E}\\big[\\sigma\\big(\\hat r_\\theta(x,y_l) - \\hat r_\\theta(x,y_w)\\big)\\big(\\nabla_\\theta\\log\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x) - \\nabla_\\theta\\log\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)\\big)\\big]$,
where $\\hat r_\\theta(x,y) = \\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)}$. The logistic weight $\\sigma(\\hat r_\\theta(y_l)-\\hat r_\\theta(y_w))$ down-weights pairs already ordered correctly and up-weights hard pairs, preventing runaway amplification of already-high-reward outputs. A naive probability-ratio objective that drops this weight (and/or the reference normalization) applies large, uncalibrated updates to preferred responses regardless of margin, continually increasing $\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)$ and decreasing $\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)$ until probability mass collapses onto a few “winners.” The paper reports that such a naive ratio objective “can cause the language model to degenerate,” and motivates DPO’s per-example importance weighting and explicit use of $\\pi_{\\text{ref}}$ to preserve diversity while aligning with preferences.
