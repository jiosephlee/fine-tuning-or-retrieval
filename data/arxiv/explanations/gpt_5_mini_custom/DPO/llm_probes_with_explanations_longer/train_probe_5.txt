In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors explain that when DPO extracts the optimal policy in closed form and trains with a simple classification loss, the training component that becomes unnecessary is reinforcement learning. The key step is an analytical mapping from rewards to optimal policies for the KL-constrained RLHF objective: the optimal policy under a reward \(r(x,y)\) satisfies
\[
\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right),
\]
so that
\[
r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta \log Z(x).
\]
Because human preference models like Bradley–Terry depend only on reward differences, the unknown partition term \(\beta\log Z(x)\) cancels when forming pairwise preferences, yielding a likelihood over preferences that depends directly on the policy and the reference model:
\[
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}-\beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right).
\]
This lets the authors write a maximum-likelihood objective over \(\pi_\theta\) as a simple binary cross-entropy,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\log \sigma\!\Big(\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\Big)\bigg],
\]
so optimizing preferences no longer requires sampling-based RL updates.

This reparameterization does not restrict the class of rewards consistent with the Bradley–Terry or Plackett–Luce models: the authors show that for any reward \(r\) in an equivalence class (up to an additive \(f(x)\)), there exists a policy \(\pi\) such that \(r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\). Consequently, training \(\pi_\theta\) via the classification loss exactly recovers the optimal policy one would otherwise seek with RL in the KL-constrained objective, but without estimating partition functions, learning baselines, or running unstable actor–critic updates. The DPO gradient further makes this operational: it increases \(\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)\) and decreases \(\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\) with a confidence weight \(\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\), where \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\). Thus, the policy learns to satisfy preferences directly through a supervised objective, eliminating the need for reinforcement learning in the fine-tuning loop.
