According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', when the model already rates $y_w$ much higher than $y_l$, the per-example weight in the DPO gradient, $\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w))$, becomes near zero. This follows directly from the form of the DPO gradient and the properties of the logistic function. In DPO, the implicit reward is $\hat{r}_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$, and the gradient for a preference pair $(y_w, y_l)$ includes the factor $\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w))$. If the model already assigns a much larger implicit reward to $y_w$ than $y_l$, then $\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w) \ll 0$, so the logistic $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}$ evaluated at a large negative argument is close to $0$. Consequently, the multiplicative weight on the update $\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x) - \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$ is near zero, and the gradient contribution from that example vanishes, as illustrated in the paper’s annotated gradient where this term is “higher weight when reward estimate is wrong.”

This behavior is sensible and desirable in the DPO framework because it focuses learning on the examples where the model’s implicit reward ordering is incorrect or only weakly correct. When $\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) \gtrsim \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w)$, the difference becomes non-negative, making $\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x, y_l) - \hat{r}_\theta(x, y_w))$ close to $1$, which intensifies the update to increase $\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$ and decrease $\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$. Conversely, when the margin is already large in the correct direction, further pushing probabilities would be unnecessary and potentially destabilizing. The per-example weighting thus acts as an adaptive importance weight tied to the Bradley-Terry likelihood, preventing over-optimization and the degeneration the authors observe with a naive probability ratio objective. It also harmonizes with the KL constraint through $\hat{r}_\theta$, ensuring updates are strongest where they reduce preference errors while remaining conservative where the policy already aligns with preferences.
