In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', DPO replaces the two-stage reward modeling plus RL pipeline with a single-stage optimization using a common supervised loss, and the supervised loss DPO uses to optimize the policy directly is binary cross-entropy. The key step is a change-of-variables that expresses any KL-regularized optimal policy for the RLHF objective in closed form: starting from the standard constrained objective, the optimal policy induced by a reward function satisfies
\[
\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right),
\]
which can be rearranged to
\[
r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x).
\]
Under the Bradley–Terry preference model, only reward differences enter the likelihood \(p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2))\), so the partition term \(\beta\log Z(x)\) cancels when taking differences. Substituting the reparameterization yields a preference probability that depends solely on log-probability ratios under the optimal policy and the reference policy:
\[
p^*(y_1\succ y_2 \mid x)=\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right).
\]
This turns maximum likelihood over pairwise preferences into a direct policy learning problem with a binary cross-entropy loss over the sigmoid of these log-ratio differences.

Consequently, DPO defines a single supervised objective over a parameterized policy \(\pi_\theta\),
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_{\theta};\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\!\left[\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right],
\]
which is exactly a binary cross-entropy classification of “win” versus “loss” conditioned on the prompt. Optimizing this loss fits an implicit reward of the form \(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}\) that sits in the same equivalence class as any reward consistent with the Bradley–Terry model, and by construction yields the KL-regularized optimal policy without an RL training loop or a separate reward model. The gradient analysis further shows the update increases \(\log\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)\) and decreases \(\log\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\), with importance weights \(\sigma(\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat{r}_\theta(x,y_w))\) that stabilize training—precisely the behavior one expects from a well-posed binary cross-entropy classifier over preference pairs.
