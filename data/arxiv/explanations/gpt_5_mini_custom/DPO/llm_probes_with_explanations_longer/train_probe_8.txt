In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors show that by reparameterizing rewards via an analytical mapping from reward functions to their optimal policies—so that the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood depends only on the policy and a reference policy—they can optimize preferences directly with a single-stage classification loss, avoiding explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning; this core innovation can be summarized as closed-form optimal policy. The key step is to start from the KL-constrained RL objective and its known optimal solution, which yields an optimal policy of the form
\[
\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right).
\]
Taking logs and rearranging gives a reparameterization of the reward in terms of the optimal policy,
\[
r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x).
\]
Crucially, the Bradley–Terry model depends only on reward differences, so the partition term \( \beta\log Z(x) \) cancels when computing \( p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)) \). Substituting the reparameterization yields a preference probability that depends only on the ratio of the optimal policy and the reference policy:
\[
p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right).
\]
This directly induces the DPO loss,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\Big[\log\sigma\!\big(\beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\big)\Big],
\]
a single-stage binary cross-entropy objective that optimizes the policy without sampling-in-the-loop RL.

This reparameterization is not merely heuristic; it preserves the expressivity of reward models up to the known equivalence classes in Plackett–Luce/Bradley–Terry (rewards differing by a function of \(x\) induce the same preferences and the same optimal KL-constrained policy). Theorem 1 shows that, under mild conditions, any reward class consistent with these preference models can be represented as \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} \) for some policy \( \pi \), i.e., the policy parameterization recovers an equivalent member of the reward class whose induced optimal policy is exactly \( \pi \). Consequently, one can maximize preference likelihood directly over \( \pi_\theta \) while implicitly optimizing the same KL-regularized RLHF objective, and the closed-form mapping obviates the need to estimate \( Z(x) \) or train an explicit reward model and an RL optimizer. The gradient of \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} \) further reveals a stabilizing, per-example importance weighting via the implicit reward \( \hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} \), which increases the likelihood of preferred responses and decreases that of dispreferred ones in a way that mirrors the intended KL-constrained reward maximization without the variance and complexity of actor-critic training.
