### How sensitive is the implicit reward to the choice of $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, and does this hurt identifiability?
Question:
The implicit reward is defined as $\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$. This means changing $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ will change the numeric values of $\hat r_\theta$, even if preferences are unchanged up to equivalence classes. Does this affect comparability across experiments or transfer between tasks? Is there a recommended calibration procedure for $\pi_{\text{ref}}$?
Answer:
In <|endoftext|> the “reward”  

$$
\hat r_{t\theta}(x,y)
=\beta\cdot \log\!\biggl[\frac{\pi_{t\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\biggr]
$$

is not meant to be an absolute, semantically‐meaningful score but rather a convenient <|endoftext|> of an entire <|endoftext|> class* of rewards that all induce the same preference orderings and the same optimal policy under the KL‐constraint.  Concretely, under the <|endoftext|> (or more generally Plackett–Luce) model two reward functions $r$ and $r'$ that differ only by an *x*-dependent constant,

$$
r'(x,y)=r(x,y)+f(x),
$$

induce identical <|endoftext|> preferences (Lemma 1) and identical solutions to the KL‐regularized RL problem (Lemma 2).  What $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ does in <|endoftext|> is exactly fix that “gauge” $f(x)$, selecting one representative from each <|endoftext|> class: by choosing $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and defining

$$
\hat <|endoftext|>
=\beta\cdot \log\!\biggl[\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\biggr]
$$

we ensure that the corresponding “partition function”

$$
<|endoftext|>
=\sum_y <|endoftext|> x)\,\exp\!\bigl[\hat r(x,y)/\beta\bigr]
$$

<|endoftext|> 1 by construction (Theorem 1).  In other words, *changing* $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ simply <|endoftext|> which member of the <|endoftext|> class you call “the reward,” but it **does not** change  
(1) the ordering of any pair of <|endoftext|> under $\hat r$, nor  
(2) the optimal policy 
$$
\hat\pi(y\mid x)\propto <|endoftext|> x)\,\exp\!\bigl[\hat r(x,y)/\beta\bigr].
$$  

What this means in practice is:

  •  If you keep the **same** $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (and the same $\beta$) across experiments, then the *numeric* values of $\hat <|endoftext|> are on the same “gauge”—so you can meaningfully compare, say, the mean <|endoftext|> reward of two different models or of the same model on two tasks.

  •  If you switch $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the absolute numbers $\hat <|endoftext|> will move around, but *all differences* $\hat r(x,y_{1})-\hat r(x,y_{2})$ (and hence preference <|endoftext|> and the learned policy) remain internally self‐consistent.  

Thus there is no harm to *policy identifiability*—DPO will always recover the same optimal <|endoftext|> as long as you use the same $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\beta$—but there *is* a gauge‐freedom if you want to <|endoftext|> or compare raw reward numbers.  

**Recommended practice:** in most <|endoftext|> pipelines you already have an “SFT” model that generated your human‐preference data; simply use that as $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$.  If you must change it—say you switch to a new <|endoftext|> model—then you should either  
  
  (a) re‐use the *same* $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ when evaluating both systems you’re comparing, or  
  (b) re‐center the <|endoftext|> computed rewards by subtracting the per‐input partition‐term

$$
\beta\cdot \log\!\sum_y <|endoftext|> x)\,\exp\!\bigl[(1/\beta)\,\hat r(x,y)\bigr],
$$

which restores the <|endoftext|> $\sum_y <|endoftext|> x)\exp[\hat r(x,y)/\beta]=1$.  That choice simply re‐fixes the <|endoftext|> constant $f(x)$ and puts all of your reward numbers back on a common scale.  

In short, the dependence of $\hat r$ on $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is purely a *gauge* choice and does not degrade identifiability of the learned policy or preference ordering.  To compare reward values across models or tasks, you only need to ensure you are using the same $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (and $\beta$), or else re‐apply the same per‐input centering procedure.
