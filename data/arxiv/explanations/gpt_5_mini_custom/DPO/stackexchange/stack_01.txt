\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model"}

### How exactly does the partition function cancel out in the DPO derivation?
Question:
In Section 4, the paper substitutes the reparameterization r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x) into the Bradley–Terry model p(y_1 \succ y_2\mid x) = \sigma(r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2)). They say the partition function term \beta \log Z(x) cancels. Can someone show the algebraic steps explicitly? I'm worried I'm missing a sign or a detail about sequence-level vs token-level rewards when showing that the \log Z(x) term cancels from the difference r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2).
Answer:
Short answer: because the partition function term in the reparameterization depends only on the prompt $x$, it is identical for both completions and therefore subtracts out when you form the reward difference that appears inside the Bradley–Terry sigmoid. Here are the algebraic steps made explicit and a short note about token- vs. sequence-level rewards.

Start from the reparameterization (Eq. (6) in the paper)
$$
r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x),
$$
where
$$
Z(x)=\sum_{y'}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y'\mid x)\exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y')\bigr)
$$
is the partition function and therefore a function of $x$ alone (not of $y$).

Form the difference that appears in the Bradley–Terry model:
$$
\begin{align*}
r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)
&= \Big[\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\Big] \\
&\qquad - \Big[\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)\Big] \\
&= \beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)} \\
&\quad + \beta\log Z(x) - \beta\log Z(x) \\
&= \beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}.
\end{align*}
$$

Because the two $\beta\log Z(x)$ terms are identical, they cancel exactly. Substituting this into the Bradley–Terry preference probability
$$
p(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\bigl(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)\bigr)
$$
gives the paper's Eq. (8),
$$
p(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma\!\Bigl(\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\Bigr).
$$

A note on sequence-level vs token-level rewards: the only requirement for the cancellation is that $\log Z(x)$ depends on the prompt $x$ but not on the particular completion $y$ you compare. Whether your reward $r(x,y)$ is computed per entire sequence or as a sum of token-level rewards (e.g., $r(x,y)=\sum_t r_t(x,y_{1:t})$), the partition function $Z(x)$ defined by $Z(x)=\sum_{y'}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y'\mid x)\exp(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y'))$ is still a function of $x$ alone. So when you take differences $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$, that same $\beta\log Z(x)$ appears for both completions and cancels. Thus there is no hidden sign flip or subtlety: the algebra is straightforward and exact.

Intuitively, this reflects the well-known fact that Plackett–Luce / Bradley–Terry style preference models and the KL-constrained optimal-policy form are invariant to adding an $x$-dependent constant to the reward function; only reward differences between completions matter for preferences. The DPO reparameterization simply picks the representative of that equivalence class for which the reward is expressed in terms of the policy ratio, and the partition-function term is the same additive constant for all completions for that prompt, so it drops out of pairwise comparisons.
