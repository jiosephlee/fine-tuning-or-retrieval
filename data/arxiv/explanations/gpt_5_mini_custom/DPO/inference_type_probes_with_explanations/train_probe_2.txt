In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', training $\\pi_\\theta$ with the DPO loss $\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{DPO}}$ (Eq. 8), which applies the logistic function $\\sigma$ to paired comparisons, is most analogous to training a binary classifier. This is because Eq. (8) is exactly the negative log-likelihood of a Bradley–Terry model over pairwise labels, i.e., a binary cross-entropy objective: for each triplet $(x,y_w,y_l)$ with label “$y_w \\succ y_l$,” DPO maximizes $\\log\\sigma\\big(\\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\\mid x)}-\\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\\mid x)}\\big)$, which is the standard logistic likelihood for a binary classifier whose “score” for an item $y$ is $\\hat r_\\theta(x,y)=\\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\mathrm{ref}}(y\\mid x)}$ and whose decision margin on a pair $(y_w,y_l)$ is $\\hat r_\\theta(x,y_w)-\\hat r_\\theta(x,y_l)$; this mirrors the reward-model fitting loss in Eq. (2), which also uses $-\\log\\sigma(\\cdot)$ over pairwise differences, except DPO replaces an explicit reward network $r_\\phi$ with the implicit score induced by the policy, thereby turning policy learning into maximum-likelihood binary classification over preference labels.
