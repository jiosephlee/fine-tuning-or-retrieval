According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', if preference datasets contained full rankings instead of pairwise preferences, the part of the learning pipeline that would need to be changed to accommodate models like Plackett-Luce is the preference model. This follows because the paper explicitly sets up the pipeline to separate (i) supervised fine-tuning, (ii) fitting a preference likelihood, and (iii) policy optimization, and it notes that while their main exposition uses the Bradley–Terry (BT) model for pairwise comparisons, the same framework “is also compatible with the more general Plackett–Luce” (PL) ranking models when several ranked answers are available; concretely, the BT negative log-likelihood in Eq. (2), −E[log σ(r(x,y_w)−r(x,y_l))], is replaced by the PL negative log-likelihood for a ranking y_1 ≻ y_2 ≻ … ≻ y_k, −log ∏_{j=1}^{k} \frac{\exp(r(x,y_j))}{\sum_{m=j}^{k}\exp(r(x,y_m))}, and under DPO’s change of variables r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} this directly yields an analogous policy loss where the BT-based term in Eq. (6) is replaced by the PL-based term over permutations (the paper states “we can similarly derive expressions under the more general Plackett–Luce models,” Appendix), while the rest of the pipeline—SFT initialization, the KL-regularized optimality condition \pi_r(y\mid x)\propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp(r(x,y)/\beta), and the overall RL/DPO optimization mechanics—remain unchanged because only the likelihood model of preferences (i.e., how the data are assumed generated) differs when moving from pairwise comparisons to full rankings.
