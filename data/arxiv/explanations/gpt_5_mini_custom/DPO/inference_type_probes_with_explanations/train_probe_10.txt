In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the quantity $\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ is most analogous to the familiar statistical quantity known as the log likelihood ratio. This follows because DPO reparameterizes the reward in terms of the optimal policy as $r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x)$ (Eq. (5)), making the implicit reward (up to an $x$-dependent constant) exactly the scaled log of a density ratio between the current policy and the reference policy; moreover, when plugged into the Bradley–Terry model the partition term cancels, yielding preference probabilities $\sigma\!\big(\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\big)$ (Eq. (6)), which are logistic in the difference of two log ratios, i.e., a log-odds form precisely mirroring classical likelihood-ratio tests and logistic models. Intuitively, the DPO gradient (Sec. 4) increases $\log\pi(y_w\mid x)$ and decreases $\log\pi(y_l\mid x)$ weighted by how the implicit reward misorders them, so the signal driving updates is the evidence favoring the preferred completion under $\pi$ relative to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, exactly what a log likelihood ratio quantifies; and this ratio is the dual of the KL regularization in the original RLHF objective (Eq. (3)), where the optimal policy is an exponentially tilted version of $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ by $r$, further cementing the interpretation of $\beta\log\frac{\pi}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}$ as the fundamental log evidence ratio between the two models.
