According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', because DPO avoids sampling from the policy and the reinforcement learning loop used in PPO-based RLHF, one would expect gradient variance during fine-tuning to typically be lower. This follows from DPO’s change-of-variables that turns the KL-constrained reward maximization objective into a supervised binary cross-entropy over fixed offline preference pairs, yielding a deterministic gradient (up to minibatch sampling) that directly adjusts log-likelihood ratios relative to a reference model: from Eq. (8), the update is proportional to gradients of terms like $\beta\big(\log \pi_{\theta}(y_w\mid x)-\log \pi_{\theta}(y_l\mid x)\big)$ modulated by a smoothly bounded weight $\sigma\!\big(\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_l)-\hat r_{\theta}(x,y_w)\big)$, with $\hat r_{\theta}(x,y)=\beta \log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$, so it does not rely on high-variance Monte Carlo estimates of returns or on-policy trajectory sampling; in contrast, PPO optimizes $\max_{\pi_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\!\big[r_{\phi}(x,y)-\beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\big)\big]$ via policy gradients that require sampling $y\sim\pi_{\theta}$ and estimating or baselining the soft value term $-\beta\log\sum_{y}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r_{\phi}(x,y)\big)$, which the paper identifies as a source of instability and variance in actor-critic methods; by reparameterizing rewards so that the optimal policy is analytic and the normalization is implicit, DPO eliminates these stochastic estimators and baselines, leading to lower-variance gradients in practice.
