According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', under DPO's preference model, if $\\pi_\\theta = \\pi_\\text{ref}$ for a pair, the predicted preference probability is 50%. This follows directly from the DPO likelihood (Eq. (7)), where the Bradley–Terry preference probability is modeled as $\\sigma\\big(\\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_w\\mid x)}-\\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_l\\mid x)}\\big)$; when $\\pi_\\theta=\\pi_\\text{ref}$ on the specific completions in the pair, each log ratio becomes $\\log 1=0$, the difference is $0$, and hence the logistic output is $\\sigma(0)=\\tfrac{1}{2}$, i.e., a 50% preference probability. Intuitively, DPO predicts preferences from the relative log-odds $\\log\\frac{\\pi_\\theta}{\\pi_\\text{ref}}$; if the policy matches the reference on both responses, it assigns them equal implicit reward $\\hat r_\\theta(x,y)=\\beta\\log\\frac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y\\mid x)}=0$, making the model indifferent and yielding a fair coin flip.
