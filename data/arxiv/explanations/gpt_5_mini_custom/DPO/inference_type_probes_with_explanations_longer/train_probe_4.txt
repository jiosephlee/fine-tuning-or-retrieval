According to the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', from the optimal policy formula $\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$, as $\beta \to 0$, the optimal policy becomes concentrated on the highest-reward outputs. This follows directly from the exponential reweighting of the reference policy by the reward with “temperature” $\beta$: for any two completions $y_1,y_2$, the odds ratio is
$\frac{\pi_r(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_r(y_2\mid x)} = \frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)} \exp\!\left(\frac{r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)}{\beta}\right)$. If $r(x,y_1)>r(x,y_2)$, then as $\beta\to 0$ the factor $\exp\!\left(\frac{r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)}{\beta}\right)\to\infty$, overwhelming any finite ratio from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$; conversely, for any $y$ with strictly lower reward than the maximum, its probability is suppressed by an exponentially vanishing factor relative to the maximizer. After normalization by $Z(x)=\sum_{y}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$, all probability mass concentrates on the set $\arg\max_{y} r(x,y)$ (splitting mass among ties if there are any). This is the standard “softmax-to-argmax” limit: the distribution $\propto \exp(r/\beta)$ becomes a point mass on the maximizers as the temperature $\beta$ goes to zero.

This limiting behavior is also sensible in light of the KL-regularized objective $\max_{\pi_\theta}\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[r(x,y)] - \beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)\right]$ analyzed in the paper. The coefficient $\beta$ trades off reward maximization against staying close to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. As $\beta\to 0$, the KL penalty vanishes, so the optimizer is free to deviate arbitrarily from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ to maximize reward. The closed-form solution $\pi_r(y\mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$ precisely captures this tradeoff: small $\beta$ amplifies reward differences via the exponential, causing the policy to ignore the reference weighting and collapse onto the highest-reward completions, whereas larger $\beta$ “softens” the preference for high-reward outputs and keeps the policy closer to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$.
