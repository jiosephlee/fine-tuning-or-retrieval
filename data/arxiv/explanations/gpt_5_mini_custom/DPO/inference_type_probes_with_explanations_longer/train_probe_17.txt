In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the normalization term $\\beta \\log\\sum_{y}\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\left(\\frac{1}{\\beta}r_{\\phi}(x, y)\\right)$ in the actor-critic objective conceptually represents the model's soft value function. This follows directly from the reparameterization of the KL-constrained RL objective and its control-as-inference interpretation. Starting from the optimal policy form $\\pi^*(y\\mid x) \\propto \\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\big(\\frac{1}{\\beta} r_{\\phi}(x,y)\\big)$, the term $\\sum_{y}\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\big(\\frac{1}{\\beta} r_{\\phi}(x,y)\\big)$ is the partition function normalizing $\\pi^*$, and its scaled log, $\\beta\\log(\\cdot)$, is exactly the soft value (a log-sum-exp aggregator) over actions at state $x$. In maximum entropy RL and control-as-inference, the soft state value is defined as $V(x) = \\beta\\log\\sum_{y}\\exp\\big(\\frac{1}{\\beta} Q(x,y)\\big)$; here, identifying $Q(x,y)$ with $r_{\\phi}(x,y)$ and carrying the weighting by $\\pi_{\\text{ref}}$ yields $V(x) = \\beta\\log\\sum_{y}\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\big(\\frac{1}{\\beta} r_{\\phi}(x,y)\\big)$, matching the normalization term. Thus, the term aggregates action values into a state-dependent baseline via a temperature $\\beta$, encoding the “soft” maximum over actions that defines the value of being in $x$ under the reference-backed, entropy-regularized objective.

Moreover, its role in the actor-critic objective clarifies why it is interpreted as a value function and why omitting it causes instability. In the decomposition
$$\\max_{\\pi_{\\theta}}\\mathbb{E}_{y\\sim\\pi_{\\theta}(\\cdot\\mid x)}\\Big[\\underbrace{r_{\\phi}(x,y) - \\beta\\log\\sum_{y'}\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y'\\mid x)\\exp\\big(\\tfrac{1}{\\beta}r_{\\phi}(x, y')\\big)}_{\\text{advantage-like term}} - \\beta\\log\\tfrac{\\pi_{\\theta}(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_{\\text{ref}}(y\\mid x)}\\Big],$$
the normalization term depends only on $x$ (not on $y$), so it behaves like a baseline and yields a centered signal $r_{\\phi}(x,y) - V(x)$—the hallmark of advantage estimators that reduce gradient variance and stabilize learning. Interpreting it as a soft value $V(x)$ also explains why substituting learned or heuristic baselines (e.g., human-completion baselines) can partially compensate for its absence: they are attempting to approximate the same state-dependent normalization that log-sum-exp provides exactly. The DPO reparameterization eliminates the need to estimate this baseline by absorbing it analytically, while actor-critic PPO must either learn or approximate it; hence the paper’s observation that the normalization term “is the soft value function of the reference policy” and that removing it leads to high-variance updates.
