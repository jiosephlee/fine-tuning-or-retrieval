In the paper ‘Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model’, if human preferences depended on absolute reward levels instead of differences (i.e., on r(x,y) rather than on r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)) the key simplification in DPO’s derivation that would fail is the cancellation of the partition function. The central reparameterization used by DPO writes the reward as r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x), where Z(x) is the partition function arising from the KL-constrained optimal policy. Under Bradley–Terry or more general Plackett–Luce preference models, the likelihood depends only on reward differences, such as r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2). Substituting the reparameterization into this difference yields r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2) = \beta \left[\log \frac{\pi(y_1\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1\mid x)} - \log \frac{\pi(y_2\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2\mid x)}\right], and the additive term \beta \log Z(x) cancels. This cancellation is precisely what allows the preference likelihood to be written entirely in terms of the policy ratio \pi/\pi_{\text{ref}} (Eq. (6) in the paper), yielding a simple binary cross-entropy over policies without needing to estimate Z(x).

If preferences instead depended on absolute reward levels p^*(\text{prefer } y \mid x) as a function of r(x,y), the additive normalizer \beta \log Z(x) would enter the likelihood directly and would not cancel. In that case, the preference probability would require evaluating r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x), and hence computing Z(x) = \sum_{y'} \pi_{\text{ref}}(y'\mid x)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y')\right), which is intractable to estimate for large output spaces. More broadly, the paper’s equivalence-class view of rewards (rewards differing by an x-dependent offset f(x) induce the same preferences and the same optimal policy) relies on preference models that depend on differences; absolute-level preferences would break that under-specification and eliminate the freedom to absorb \beta \log Z(x) into f(x). Consequently, the change-of-variables that turns reward-model maximum likelihood into policy-only maximum likelihood (the core of DPO) would no longer be viable, and one would have to either explicitly estimate Z(x) or revert to RL-based optimization with an explicit reward model.
