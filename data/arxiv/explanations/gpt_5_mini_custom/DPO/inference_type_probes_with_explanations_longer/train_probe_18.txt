In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', if the optimal policy $\pi^*(y\mid x)$ equals the reference policy $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)$ in Eq.~(7), then the reward must, up to equivalence, be a function of $x$. This follows directly from the closed-form relation between the optimal policy and the reward under the KL-constrained objective: $\pi_r(y\mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)$. If $\pi^*(y\mid x)=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)$, then for every completion $y$ at a given $x$ we must have $\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right)=Z(x)$, implying $r(x,y)=\beta \log Z(x)$, which depends only on $x$ and not on $y$. Equivalently, using the reparameterization $r(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)$ in Eq.~(7), setting $\pi^*=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ immediately yields $r(x,y)=\beta \log Z(x)$.

Moreover, the paper defines an equivalence relation on rewards in which $r$ and $r'$ are equivalent if $r(x,y)-r'(x,y)=f(x)$ for some function of the context only. Under this relation, making $r(x,y)$ independent of $y$ is exactly “up to equivalence” a pure context-dependent offset, which leaves both the Bradley–Terry preference probabilities and the optimal KL-regularized policy invariant. In particular, the Bradley–Terry model depends only on differences $r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2)$, so any $x$-only reward collapses those differences to zero, matching the equality $\pi^*=\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ implied by $r(x,y)=\beta\log Z(x)$. Hence, the only way for the optimal policy to equal the reference in the DPO/RLHF formulation is for the reward to be, modulo the paper’s equivalence class, a function of $x$ alone.
