In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', increasing the scalar $\\beta$ typically allows the policy to deviate more from the reference policy, causing an increase in $\\mathrm{KL}$. This is sensible from the DPO training objective, where $\\beta$ directly scales the margin between preferred and dispreferred log-probability ratios: $\\mathcal{L}_\\text{DPO}(\\pi_{\\theta};\\pi_\\text{ref})=-\\mathbb{E}\\bigl[\\log \\sigma\\bigl(\\beta\\bigl[\\log \\tfrac{\\pi_{\\theta}(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_w\\mid x)}-\\log \\tfrac{\\pi_{\\theta}(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_l\\mid x)}\\bigr]\\bigr)\\bigr]$. The gradient of this loss is scaled by $\\beta$ (cf. $\\nabla_\\theta \\mathcal{L}_\\text{DPO} \\propto -\\beta\\,\\mathbb{E}[\\cdots]$), so larger $\\beta$ produces larger updates that amplify the ratio $\\log \\frac{\\pi_{\\theta}}{\\pi_\\text{ref}}$ for preferred responses and suppress it for dispreferred ones. Mechanistically, that pushes $\\pi_{\\theta}$ to separate preferred and dispreferred responses more aggressively, which is accomplished by moving probability mass away from $\\pi_\\text{ref}$ on the examples where preferences exist—i.e., increasing the sequence-level $\\mathbb{D}_\\mathrm{KL}(\\pi_{\\theta}\\,\\Vert\\,\\pi_\\text{ref})$.

This interpretation also aligns with the preference-model view induced by the Bradley–Terry transformation in Eq. (4): $p^*(y_w\\succ y_l\\mid x)=\\sigma\\bigl(\\beta\\bigl[\\log \\tfrac{\\pi^*(y_w\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_w\\mid x)}-\\log \\tfrac{\\pi^*(y_l\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y_l\\mid x)}\\bigr]\\bigr)$. Increasing $\\beta$ steepens the link function, which effectively demands a larger separation in the log-ratio terms to fit the same observed preferences at high likelihood. The most direct way for the policy to achieve that separation is to increase the deviation of $\\pi_{\\theta}$ from $\\pi_\\text{ref}$ on those completions, which manifests as higher $\\mathrm{KL}$. Although, in the closed-form optimal policy for a fixed reward, $\\pi_r(y\\mid x)\\propto \\pi_\\text{ref}(y\\mid x)\\exp\\bigl(\\tfrac{1}{\\beta}r(x,y)\\bigr)$, larger $\\beta$ narrows the tilt and thus shrinks $\\mathrm{KL}$ for a given $r$, in DPO $\\beta$ controls the strength of the training signal that reshapes the implicit reward $\\hat r_\\theta(x,y)=\\beta\\log \\tfrac{\\pi_\\theta(y\\mid x)}{\\pi_\\text{ref}(y\\mid x)}$. As $\\beta$ increases, the optimizer is incentivized to magnify these log-ratio differences, which, during learning from finite data, typically results in greater divergence from the reference policy.
