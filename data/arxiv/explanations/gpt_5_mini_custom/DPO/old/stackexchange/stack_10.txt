### Can someone re-derive the optimal policy $\pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x) e^{r(x,y)/\beta}$ from the KL-constrained objective?
Question:
Starting from the objective $\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{y\sim \pi(\cdot\mid x)}[r(x,y)] - \beta\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi(\cdot\mid x)\Vert \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid x)]$ for each fixed $x$, can someone show the steps using calculus of variations or Lagrange multipliers that produce the closed-form optimal policy $\pi_r(y\mid x) = \tfrac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x) e^{r(x,y)/\beta}$ and explain the role of $Z(x)$?
Answer:
Here is a standard Lagrange‐multiplier derivation of the closed‐form policy.  Fixing an input $x$, we drop the “$\mid x$” for clarity and write $\pi(y)$, $\pi_{\rm ref}(y)$, and $r(y)\equiv r(x,y)$.  Our goal is  
$$
\max_{\pi}\; \sum_{y}\pi(y)\,r(y)\;-\;\beta\sum_{y}\pi(y)\,\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y)}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{y}\pi(y)=1\,. 
$$  
Introduce a multiplier $\lambda$ for the normalization constraint and form the Lagrangian  
$$
\mathcal{L}[\pi,\lambda]
=\sum_{y}\pi(y)\,r(y)
-\beta\sum_{y}\pi(y)\,\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y)}
+\lambda\Bigl(1-\sum_{y}\pi(y)\Bigr)\,.
$$  
Taking the functional derivative with respect to $\pi(y)$ and setting it to zero gives  
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\pi(y)}
= r(y)\;-\;\beta\Bigl(\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y)}+1\Bigr)\;-\;\lambda
\;=\;0.
$$  
Rearrange to isolate the log‐ratio:  
$$
\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y)}
=\frac{1}{\beta}\bigl(r(y)-\lambda\bigr)\;-\;1.
$$  
We absorb the constants $(-\lambda/\beta-1)$ into a single normalizer and exponentiate:  
$$
\pi(y)
\;\propto\;\pi_{\rm ref}(y)\,\exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}\,r(y)\Bigr).
$$  
Enforcing $\sum_{y}\pi(y)=1$ introduces the partition function (normalization constant)  
$$
Z(x)\;=\;\sum_{y}\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Bigr)\,,
$$  
so that the optimal policy is  
$$
\boxed{\;\pi_{r}(y\mid x)
=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\;\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\bigl(r(x,y)/\beta\bigr)\,.}
$$  
Intuitively, this is a “soft‐max” or Boltzmann policy that tilts the reference model toward higher‐reward outputs.  The temperature $\beta$ governs how much we trade off reward versus staying close to $\pi_{\rm ref}$, and $Z(x)$ ensures $\pi_{r}(\cdot\mid x)$ sums to one (it also plays the role of a “soft value” for the prefix $x$).
