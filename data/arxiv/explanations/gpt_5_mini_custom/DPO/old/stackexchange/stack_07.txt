### How does the control-as-inference view lead to the objective with the normalization term $f(r,\pi_{\text{ref}},\beta)$?
Question:
In the control-as-inference derivation, minimizing $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\Vert \pi^*(\cdot\mid x)]$ yields an objective with a baseline-like term $-\beta\log \sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta} r_\phi(x,y)\big)$. Could someone derive this objective in detail and explain how this term functions as a soft value function and why dropping or poorly estimating it makes actor–critic training unstable?
Answer:
In the “control as inference” view of RL – which underlies many KL‐regularized policy‐gradient methods including RLHF – you introduce an optimality variable so that the posterior over trajectories (or actions) is proportional to your reference policy times an exponential of the cumulative reward. In the one‐step, contextual setting of RLHF this gives an *optimal* per‐prompt policy  
$$
\pi^*(y\mid x)\;\propto\;\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r_\phi(x,y)\Bigr),
$$
where $\pi_{\rm ref}$ is your “behavior” or SFT policy, $r_\phi$ is the learned reward model, and $\beta$ governs how strongly you penalize moving away from $\pi_{\rm ref}$.  

If you now *fit* a parametric policy $\pi_\theta$ by minimizing the reverse KL  
$$
\min_\theta\; \mathbb{D}_{\rm KL}\bigl[\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\;\Vert\;\pi^*(\cdot\mid x)\bigr]
$$
you get exactly the usual KL‐penalized reward objective, *plus* a *normalization* (or “baseline”) term. Here’s the derivation in a nutshell:

1. Write out the KL:  
   $$
     \mathbb{D}_{\rm KL}\bigl[\pi_\theta\Vert\pi^*\bigr]
     \;=\;
     \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}
     \Bigl[\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\;-\;\log\pi^*(y\mid x)\Bigr].
   $$

2. Substitute in the log‐optimal policy  
   $$
     \log\pi^*(y\mid x)
     =\log\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)\;+\;\tfrac{1}{\beta}r_\phi(x,y)\;-\;\log Z(x),
   $$
   where  
   $$
     Z(x)\;=\;\sum_{y'}\pi_{\rm ref}(y'\mid x)\,\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r_\phi(x,y')\Bigr).
   $$

3. Hence  
   $$
     \mathbb{D}_{\rm KL}
     =\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\Bigl[\log\pi_\theta
     -\log\pi_{\rm ref}
     -\tfrac{1}{\beta}r_\phi
     +\log Z(x)\Bigr].
   $$

4. Multiply by $-\,\beta$ (since maximizing reward minus KL is the same as minimizing $\beta\,$KL – reward) to get the familiar RLHF objective  
   $$
     \max_\theta\;
     \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}
     \Bigl[
       r_\phi(x,y)
       \;-\;\beta\,\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)}
       \;-\;\underbrace{\beta\log Z(x)}_{\,f(r_\phi,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)\!}\Bigr].
   $$

We recognize $f(r_\phi,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)(x)=\beta\log Z(x)$ as exactly the *soft value* or *log‐partition‐function* under the reference policy. Concretely,  
$$
  f(r,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)(x)
  =\beta\log\sum_{y}\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\Bigr),
$$
so that on each sample $(x,y)$ the *term inside the expectation* is  
$$
   r(x,y)\;-\;f(r,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)(x)
   \;=\;
   r(x,y)\;-\;\underbrace{\beta\log Z(x)}_{\text{soft baseline}}.
$$
In policy‐gradient language this is precisely an *advantage* estimate: you subtract a state‐dependent baseline (here the soft or entropy‐smoothed value of being in $x$ under $\pi_{\rm ref}$) from your immediate reward.

Why does this matter for stability? If you simply drop $f(\cdot)$, or replace it with a *bad* estimator, your update becomes  
$$
  \nabla_\theta\;\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\bigl[r(x,y)\bigr]
  \;-\;
  \beta\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\bigl[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\bigr],
$$
which is exactly a policy‐gradient *without* an advantage baseline. Such updates have very high variance (since you’re pushing on raw rewards rather than on *reward minus baseline*) and can easily oscillate or blow up. In actor–critic implementations one usually *learns* a critic $V_\psi(x)\approx f(r,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)(x)$ to act as that baseline, but if $V_\psi$ is mis‐estimated, you again get biased and high‐variance gradients that harm convergence.

In short: the normalization term $-\beta\log Z(x)$ is not an innocuous constant – it’s the *soft state‐value* under your reference policy, and subtracting it turns a high‐variance “Q‐only” update into a lower‐variance “advantage” update. Omitting or poorly approximating it is precisely why actor–critic methods in RLHF often become unstable.
