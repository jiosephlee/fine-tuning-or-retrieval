### Does DPO require enumerating the full action space, or is it purely offline on given $(x, y_w, y_l)$ pairs?
Question:
The DPO loss only requires evaluating $\log \pi(y_w\mid x)$ and $\log \pi(y_l\mid x)$ for observed pairs. Does this mean training is entirely offline without sampling rollouts from $\pi_\theta$? Are there scenarios where enumerating or sampling additional $y$ would help (e.g., to reduce estimation bias), or is DPO optimally data-efficient given the pairwise dataset?
Answer:
In DPO there is no hidden requirement to enumerate or integrate over the full action space at training time—the algorithm is purely offline once you have your set of triples $(x,y_w,y_l)$.  If you look at the DPO loss (equation 6 in the paper)

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\rm DPO}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\rm ref})
\;=\;
-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\,
\log \sigma\!\Bigl(\beta\bigl[\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\!\mid\!x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_w\!\mid\!x)}
   -\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\!\mid\!x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_l\!\mid\!x)}\bigr]\Bigr),
$$

you see that the only quantities you ever need are $\log\pi_\theta(y_w\!\mid\!x)$ and $\log\pi_\theta(y_l\!\mid\!x)$ (and the same for the fixed reference model $\pi_{\rm ref}$).  There is no expectation under $\pi_\theta$ of other completions, no inner loop of rollouts, and no Monte-Carlo estimate of a partition function; in fact the entire magic of DPO is that the intractable normalizer $Z(x)$ cancels out in the Bradley–Terry likelihood.

Because DPO is just maximum-likelihood estimation of the Bradley–Terry model (reparameterized in terms of $\pi_\theta$), it is statistically—and in the infinite-data limit, asymptotically—efficient at extracting all the information contained in your $(x,y_w,y_l)$ pairs.  In that sense it is “data‐efficient”: no other offline algorithm that only sees the same labeled pairs can do better under the same model assumptions.  

That said, in practical settings you might worry about coverage or bias in your collected dataset.  For example, if your preference labels only compare very similar answers, the model may not learn how to downweight catastrophically bad answers it has never seen.  In those cases you could certainly expand your dataset—say by sampling additional completions $y$ (from the reference or even from a partially trained $\pi_\theta$) and asking for more pairwise judgments or mining hard negatives.  But this is purely a data-augmentation strategy, not a requirement of the DPO objective itself.  Once you have a sufficiently rich set of preference triples, you simply fit $\pi_\theta$ offline with the cross-entropy loss above, no rollouts or enumeration needed.
