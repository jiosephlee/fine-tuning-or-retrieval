### Should DPO use sequence-level or length-normalized log probabilities to avoid length bias?
Question:
The DPO loss compares $\log \tfrac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}$ across a preferred $y_w$ and a dispreferred $y_l$. In an autoregressive LM, $\log \pi(y\mid x)$ is a sum over tokens, so longer sequences typically have lower log-prob. Does DPO use raw sequence log-probabilities (sum), per-token averages, or any explicit length normalization? How does this choice affect bias toward shorter or longer responses, especially in tasks like summarization?
Answer:
In the DPO paper the authors never introduce an explicit “per‐token” or length‐normalized log‐prob term—instead they work with the usual sequence‐level log‐probabilities of an autoregressive model (i.e. a sum over token log‐likelihoods). Concretely, their core objective (Eq. 7 in the paper) is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}
= - \mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})\sim \mathcal{D}}
  \Bigl[
    \log \sigma\!\bigl(
      \beta\,\bigl(
        \log \tfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{w}\mid x)}
        - \log \tfrac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{l}\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{l}\mid x)}
      \bigr)
    \bigr)
  \Bigr]
$$

and each $\log \pi(y\!\mid\!x)$ there is literally the sum of per‐token log‐probabilities over the entire sequence $y$. No division by $|y|$ or other “length normalization” ever appears.

Intuitively, you might worry that summing more negative log‐probs over a longer sequence would bias DPO toward preferring shorter completions. However, two effects mitigate that in practice:

1. Since both the fine‐tuned model $\pi_{\theta}$ _and_ the reference model $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ share the same autoregressive architecture and token‐by‐token length bias, most of the penalty for extra tokens cancels out in the ratio  
   $\displaystyle \log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y)}$.
2. The KL‐constraint embedded in DPO (through the hyperparameter $\beta$) keeps $\pi_{\theta}$ close to $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and thus close to the reference model’s length distribution.

Empirically, the authors show on both summarization (Reddit TL;DR) and single‐turn dialogue (Anthropic HH) that DPO does *not* collapse to trivial one‐ or two‐token answers. Indeed, the learned model inherits reasonable length statistics from the supervised‐fine‐tuned reference, and the preference comparisons focus on the *content* differences in $\log \pi$ rather than raw length.

If you did want to guard more strongly against length bias—say because your task absolutely requires fixed‐size outputs or fairness between very different lengths—you could modify the reward to be an *average* log‐prob  
$$
\frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t}\log\pi(y_{t})
$$  
or bake an explicit length penalty into $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$. But that would break the clean, closed‐form derivation of DPO as presented in the paper. As is, DPO sticks with raw sequence‐level probabilities and relies on the ratio with a shared reference policy to neutralize most of the length effect.
