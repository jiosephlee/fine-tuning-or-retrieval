### How is sequence-level KL computed for autoregressive models, and does this favor shorter outputs?
Question:
The paper mentions computing sequence-level KL as the sum of per-timestep KLs. For completions $y=(y_1,\dots,y_T)$, this is $\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\Vert \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\big)$. Does this introduce a length dependence that interacts with the DPO training objective? Should sequence KL be normalized by length when comparing policies with different typical output lengths?
Answer:
In the standard RLHF formulation (Eq. 4 in the DPO paper), the divergence from the reference policy is measured as a *sequence-level* KL:

$$
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl[\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\,\big\|\;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\bigr]
\;=\;
\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}\biggl[\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl[
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\;\big\|\;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})
\bigr]\biggr].
$$

Because it literally *sums* a nonnegative KL at each time step, this penalty grows (on average) in proportion to the length $T$ of the generated sequence. In other words, if you held the per-step distributions fixed, a policy that spits out a shorter sequence would automatically incur less total KL simply because there are fewer time-steps over which to accumulate divergence.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) never introduces a *new* kind of length normalization; it simply re-expresses the exact same RLHF objective in closed form. Recall that in DPO one shows the optimal policy under the penalized RL objective must satisfy

$$
\hat r_\theta(x,y)
\;=\;
\beta\,\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)},
$$

and the DPO loss (Eq. 6) is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}
\;=\;
-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}
\Bigl[\log\sigma\bigl(\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)
-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\bigr)\Bigr].
$$

But note that

$$
\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}
\;=\;
\sum_{t=1}^T
\bigl[\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})-\log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})\bigr],
$$

so all of the same per-time-step contributions (and hence the same length dependence) enter $\hat r_\theta$. DPO simply turns the KL-constrained RL objective into a binary-cross-entropy over preference pairs, but it never “divides out” the length dependence.

Should you *manually* normalize by sequence length? Only if you intend to *change* the underlying objective from a *per-sequence* KL constraint to a *per-token* (average) KL constraint. If your downstream concern is fairness when comparing two policies that habitually produce very different lengths, then reporting an *average* KL per token can be informative—but that is not what standard RLHF (nor DPO) optimizes. To exactly recover the original RLHF objective (and the theoretical guarantees that go with it), you should leave the KL (or its log-ratio reparameterization) un-normalized, just as shown in the paper.
