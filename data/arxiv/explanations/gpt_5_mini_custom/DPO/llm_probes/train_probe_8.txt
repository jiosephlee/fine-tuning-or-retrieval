In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors show that by reparameterizing rewards via an analytical mapping from reward functions to their optimal policies—so that the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood depends only on the policy and a reference policy—they can optimize preferences directly with a single-stage classification loss, avoiding explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning; this core innovation can be summarized as closed-form optimal policy
