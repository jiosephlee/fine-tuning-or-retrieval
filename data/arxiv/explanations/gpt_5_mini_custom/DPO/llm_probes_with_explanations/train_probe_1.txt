In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', to optimize a policy directly from preference data instead of doing reward estimation followed by RL, DPO trains the policy using a simple supervised loss, namely binary cross-entropy. This is sensible because the KL‑constrained RLHF objective has an optimal policy of the form \( \pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta} r(x,y)\right) \), which implies a reward reparameterization \( r(x,y) = \beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x) \); under the Bradley–Terry preference model, only reward differences appear, so the partition term \( \beta \log Z(x) \) cancels, yielding \( p^*(y_w \succ y_l\mid x) = \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right) \). Maximizing the likelihood of observed preferences with respect to \(\pi\) is therefore exactly a binary cross-entropy objective in the logit given by the difference of KL-regularized log-probabilities (Eq. (8)), which directly fits an implicit reward in the equivalence class \(r(x,y)\sim \beta \log \tfrac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\) without estimating a separate reward model or running an RL loop; moreover, the resulting gradient increases \(\log \pi(y_w\mid x)\) and decreases \(\log \pi(y_l\mid x)\) with an importance weight \(\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_w))\), aligning with the KL‑constrained RLHF optimum while remaining a stable, supervised learning update.
