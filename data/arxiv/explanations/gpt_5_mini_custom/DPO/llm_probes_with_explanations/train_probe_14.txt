In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors show that to align a language model with preferences without explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning, DPO applies a specific transformation to make the RLHF objective optimizable with a simple classification loss: change of variables. Concretely, starting from the KL-constrained RLHF objective, the optimal policy for any reward \(r\) has the form \(\pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\big)\), which can be inverted to express the reward in terms of the policy as \(r(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta \log Z(x)\); substituting this reparameterization into the Bradley–Terry preference model \(p^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)=\sigma(r(x,y_1)-r(x,y_2))\) cancels the unknown partition term \(\beta\log Z(x)\), yielding a preference likelihood that depends only on the log-ratio “logit” \(\hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}\), and thus a binary cross-entropy objective \(\mathcal L_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\!\left[\log \sigma\!\Big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_w)-\hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\Big)\right]\) that directly trains the policy to satisfy preferences without sampling-based RL; Theorem 1 further shows this reparameterization does not restrict the reward classes representable under Bradley–Terry/Plackett–Luce, so optimizing this simple classification loss recovers the optimal KL-regularized policy that standard RLHF would aim for, but via an explicit change of variables from rewards to policies.
