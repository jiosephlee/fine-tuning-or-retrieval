In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the authors explain that when DPO extracts the optimal policy in closed form and trains with a simple classification loss, the training component that becomes unnecessary is reinforcement learning. This follows because the KL-constrained RLHF objective has an optimal policy of the form \( \pi_r(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{Z(x)}\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)) \), which can be inverted to express rewards in terms of policies as \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x) \); substituting this reparameterization into the Bradley–Terry preference model causes the partition function \(\log Z(x)\) to cancel, yielding a likelihood over preferences that depends only on the policy and the reference policy. Concretely, maximizing the induced preference likelihood reduces to minimizing the binary cross-entropy loss \( \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right] \), which directly trains \(\pi_\theta\) without sampling-based policy optimization, critics, or PPO. The gradient of this loss increases \(\log\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)\) and decreases \(\log\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)\) with a data-dependent sigmoid weight, effectively performing the KL-regularized reward maximization in closed form and thus obviating the RL fine-tuning phase.
