In the paper 'Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model', the single technical trick that enables DPO to avoid an RL training loop is a change of variables. Specifically, starting from the KL-constrained RLHF objective, the optimal policy for any reward satisfies the exponential-tilting form \( \pi_r(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right) \), which can be rearranged to express the reward in terms of the policy as \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_r(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}+\beta\log Z(x) \); substituting this reparameterization into the Bradley–Terry preference model causes the unknown partition term \(\log Z(x)\) to cancel because only reward differences matter, yielding the preference probability entirely in terms of policy ratios, \( p^*(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)=\sigma\!\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\Big) \). This change of variables turns reward-model maximum likelihood into a direct maximum likelihood over \(\pi_\theta\) with a simple binary cross-entropy loss (Eq. (7)), eliminating the need to fit an explicit reward or run a policy-gradient loop; moreover, the paper proves that this reparameterization loses no expressivity (any reward class under Bradley–Terry can be represented as \( r(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)} \) up to an \(x\)-dependent constant), so optimizing this supervised objective recovers the same optimal policy that RLHF would, while the resulting gradient naturally produces the importance weighting that stabilizes training without on-policy sampling.
