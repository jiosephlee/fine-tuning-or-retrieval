### How sensitive is DPO to mismatch between the true data-collection policy and the chosen \pi_\text{ref}?
Question:
The paper suggests initializing \pi_\text{ref} to the SFT model that generated the preference pairs, or fitting \pi_\text{ref} by MLE on preferred responses when the SFT isn’t available. If the true data-collection policy differed noticeably from the chosen \pi_\text{ref}, what bias does that induce in the DPO objective? Is there any formal robustness guarantee for off-reference data, or practical diagnostics to detect when \pi_\text{ref} is too far off?
Answer:
Short answer: DPO is not magic-proof to a wrong reference policy. The algorithm and its derivation explicitly use a reference distribution $ \pi_\text{ref} $ (the SFT / sampling policy) to reparameterize rewards as
$ <|endoftext|> = \beta \log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)} $.
If the preference pairs in your dataset were actually sampled from a different policy $ \pi_{\text{data}} $ (the true data-collection policy), then using a mismatched $ \pi_\text{ref} $ changes the <|endoftext|> reward/policy that DPO fits and can introduce bias. The paper acknowledges this practical issue and recommends <|endoftext|> $ \pi_\text{ref} $ to the SFT that generated the pairs when available, and otherwise fitting $ \pi_\text{ref} $ by MLE on the preferred responses to “mitigate the distribution shift.” There is no formal robustness theorem in the paper that guarantees <|endoftext|> under arbitrary off-reference sampling.

Why mismatch causes bias (intuitive + a little algebra). The <|endoftext|> preference model that DPO re-expresses depends only on reward differences, i.e. on $ <|endoftext|> $. The DPO change of variables uses the identity
$ <|endoftext|> = <|endoftext|> x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)} + \beta\log <|endoftext|> $,
and the partition term cancels when you take differences, which is why DPO can write the preference probability purely in terms of $ \pi $ and $ \pi_\text{ref} $. That cancellation, and the resulting MLE objective (Eq. (6) / (9) in the paper),
$ L_\text{DPO}(\pi;\pi_\text{ref}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y_l\mid x)}\Big)\right], $
is correct if the sampled pairs $ \mathcal{D} $ are consistent with the preference model used in the derivation (i.e. pairs produced under the same reference policy that appears in the RL objective). If instead the pairs were sampled by some other $ \pi_{\text{data}} $, the empirical pair distribution entering the likelihood does not match the distribution implicitly assumed by the derivation. Concretely, the MLE will find $ \pi $ that explains observed win/loss frequencies via the DPO <|endoftext|> relative to your chosen $ \pi_\text{ref} $; that $ \pi $ <|endoftext|> to fitting a reward in the equivalence class selected by the chosen $ \pi_\text{ref} $. If $ \pi_\text{ref} \ne \pi_{\text{data}} $, the recovered <|endoftext|> reward / policy will generally differ from the one that would be recovered using the true sampling policy; in effect you have an extra, systematic term anchored to the log-ratio between $ \pi_{\text{data}} $ and $ \pi_\text{ref} $. So the bias is an anchoring/shift in the <|endoftext|> reward/policy induced by the wrong $ \pi_\text{ref} $.

Formal guarantees in the paper. Theorem 1 shows that for any reward class consistent with the Plackett–Luce/Bradley–Terry family there exists a representation $ r(x,y)=\beta\log(\pi/\pi_\text{ref}) $ for some $ \pi $, so reparameterization does not restrict the representable reward classes (given the correct $ \pi_\text{ref} $ is used in the RL objective). However, the paper does not give a robustness theorem saying DPO will recover the same $ \pi^* $ when $ \pi_\text{ref} $ is misspecified relative to the data-collection policy. In short: <|endoftext|> completeness holds, but not a robustness guarantee to arbitrary off-reference sampling.

Practical diagnostics and mitigations (what to do in practice). The paper suggests the practical mitigation of fitting $ \pi_\text{ref} $ by MLE on the preferred completions when an SFT model is unavailable — this moves $ \pi_\text{ref} $ closer to the distribution of labeled samples. Beyond that, here are useful, paper-grounded practical checks and remedies you can use.

1) Fit / compare candidate $ \pi_\text{ref} $ models. If you have access to multiple <|endoftext|> reference models (the original SFT, a model trained on preferred completions, or the base LM), train DPO with each and compare resulting policies on validation metrics (win rates, <|endoftext|> to each candidate). Large variability tells you $ \pi_\text{ref} $ matters.

2) Check log-prob alignment on preferred responses. Compute average log-prob under your chosen $ \pi_\text{ref} $ for preferred completions versus their empirical frequency. If preferred responses are systematically low-probability under $ \pi_\text{ref} $, that’s a red flag that $ \pi_\text{ref} $ is far from the sampling distribution and will strongly affect the <|endoftext|> reward <|endoftext|>

3) Inspect DPO <|endoftext|> rewards on labeled pairs. During training compute $ \hat r_\theta(x,y)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y\mid x)} $ for the preferred and dispreferred responses. If $ \hat r_\theta $ <|endoftext|> a consistent offset across many examples <|endoftext|> preferred items always get much larger or smaller values than reasonable), that offset may indicate anchoring from a poor $ \pi_\text{ref} $.

4) Importance-weighted correction when $ \pi_{\text{data}} $ is known/estimable. If you can estimate the true sampling policy $ \pi_{\text{data}}(y\mid x) $ (for example, you logged token <|endoftext|> when sampling), you can reweight training examples by the sampling ratio $ \pi_{\text{data}}/\pi_\text{ref} $ or incorporate it into a corrected likelihood. The paper does not develop this, but it follows naturally from noting that the MLE objective depends on the empirical pair distribution.

6) Use MLE <|endoftext|> of $ \pi_\text{ref} $ on preferred completions (paper’s recommendation). If SFT that generated the pairs is unavailable, maximize likelihood on $ (x,y_w) $ to build $ \pi_\text{ref} $; this usually reduces distribution shift and is the practice recommended in the paper.

If your dataset was collected under a different sampling policy, DPO will still produce a policy that is the MLE under the chosen $ \pi_\text{ref} $, but that policy can be biased relative to the “true” policy you would have learned using the actual data-collection policy.
