### Does DPO truly enforce a KL constraint, or only encourage it implicitly?
Question:
The RL objective in Eq. (3) includes a KL penalty term with coefficient \beta, and the closed-form optimal policy has a log-density ratio proportional to the reward. In DPO, we optimize a logistic preference loss over log-density ratios without explicitly regularizing KL during training. Does minimizing Eq. (8) guarantee that the learned \pi_\theta will have a controlled KL to \pi_\text{ref}, or do we just hope that the learned ratios that explain preferences stay within a reasonable range? If we want a specific KL target (say, expected sequence-level KL \approx 6), how do we ensure DPO hits it?
Answer:
Short answer: DPO does not impose a hard, explicit <|endoftext|> constraint during parameter updates, but it does implicitly optimize the same <|endoftext|> objective as RLHF. The <|endoftext|> $\beta$ in the DPO loss plays the role of the KL-coefficient from the RL objective, so changing $\beta$ moves the learned policy along the reward–KL frontier — but there is no closed‑form guarantee that a single choice of $\beta$ will produce an exact expected-sequence <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> $\approx 6$). In practice you must tune $\beta$ (or use a dual / search procedure) and measure <|endoftext|> on validation prompts to hit a specific target.

Why this is the case (intuitive + technical): the paper shows that the optimal policy for the RLHF objective
$\max_{\pi} \; \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r] - \beta\, \mathrm{KL}(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}})$
has the closed form $\pi_{r}(y\mid x) <|endoftext|> <|endoftext|> x)\exp\big(r(y,x)/\beta\big)$. Re‑parameterizing rewards by the policy (Eq. (6)/(7) in the paper) gives the DPO likelihood
$L_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_{\theta}) = -\mathbb{E}_{\text{data}} \log \sigma\!\big(\beta\,(\log(\pi_{\theta}/\pi_{\text{ref}}))_w - \beta\,(\log(\pi_{\theta}/\pi_{\text{ref}}))_l\big)$,
so DPO directly fits a policy that is consistent with some reward in the equivalence class of RLHF rewards. Thus DPO is optimizing under the same model class and $\beta$ has the same interpretation as the <|endoftext|> regularizer in the original RL objective.

Why this is not the same as a hard constraint: although the reparameterization ties the <|endoftext|> reward to the log density ratio $\beta\log(\pi_{\theta}/\pi_{\text{ref}})$, the training loss is a classification (cross‑entropy) objective on pairs.
