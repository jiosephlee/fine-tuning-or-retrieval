### Can we combine DPO with supervised fine-tuning in a single multi-task objective?
Question:
If we have both instruction-following demonstrations and pairwise preferences, is there any downside to optimizing a combined loss $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}$? Would this be equivalent to changing the effective $\beta$ or does it introduce a qualitatively different regularization that can improve stability or sample efficiency?
Answer:
In principle there’s nothing stopping you from writing down a one-stage “multi-task” loss

$$
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)
    = \mathcal{L}_{\rm SFT}(\theta)
    \;+\;\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm DPO}(\theta;\,\pi_{\rm ref},\beta)\,,
$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{\rm SFT}$ is your usual cross-entropy on demonstration pairs $(x,y_{\rm demo})$ and

$$
  \mathcal{L}_{\rm DPO}
    = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim\mathcal{D}}
      \Bigl[\log\sigma\bigl(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_w\mid x)}
                         -\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y_l\mid x)}\bigr)\Bigr].
$$

That will certainly train your policy <|endoftext|> to both imitate the demonstrations and satisfy the <|endoftext|> preferences.  

However, from the standpoint of the DPO theory there are two things to be careful of:

1.  **It’s not just re-tuning $\beta$.**  Changing $\lambda$ trades off two *different* <|endoftext|> signals.  The SFT term only *pulls up* the likelihood of ground-truth completions, whereas the DPO term *reorders* preferred vs.\ dispreferred replies with a dynamic <|endoftext|> weight.  There is no choice of $\lambda$ that makes that identical to simply changing the DPO’s trust-region parameter $\beta$.

2.  **DPO’s <|endoftext|> guarantee assumes a *fixed* reference policy** $\pi_{\rm ref}$, typically your fully converged SFT model.  In the paper’s derivation one holds $\pi_{\rm ref}$ constant and optimizes <|endoftext|> to be the KL-constrained optimum for an <|endoftext|> reward

$$
     \hat r_\theta(x,y) = \beta\,\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)}.
$$

   If you simultaneously train the same model on $\mathcal{L}_{\rm SFT}$, then <|endoftext|> drifts away and the <|endoftext|> reward you recover no longer exactly <|endoftext|> to the original RLHF objective.  In other words, you lose the clean “no-RL, closed-form” property that makes DPO so stable.

In practice a more robust recipe, as suggested by the paper, is to first *freeze* your SFT <|endoftext|> as $\pi_{\rm ref}$, and *then* fine-tune it purely with the DPO loss.  If you do want extra demonstration signal during preference fine-tuning, you can of course add a small <|endoftext|> term to help with sample efficiency and early stability—but you should treat it as an *additional* regularizer whose effect is qualitatively different from tuning $\beta$, and you’ll need to verify that you’re not breaking DPO’s <|endoftext|> reward interpretation by shifting $\pi_{\rm ref}$.
