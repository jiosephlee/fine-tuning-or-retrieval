According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the proposed multiplicative Dropout randomly replaces a percentage of the butterfly components and diagonal blocks in each butterfly component with identity matrices $\bm{I}$, relying on the implicit property that replacing these blocks by $\bm{I}$ keeps the model valid, namely that this operation preserves orthogonality. This is sensible because BOFT parameterizes the finetuning transform as a product of orthogonal butterfly components, $\bm{R}(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$, where each $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$ is block-diagonal with $2b\times 2b$ orthogonal blocks (constructed via Cayley transforms). The identity matrix is itself orthogonal, and replacing some blocks in a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix with identities yields another block-diagonal matrix whose blocks are all orthogonal. Formally, if $\tilde{\bm{B}}$ is block-diagonal with blocks $\{\bm{O}_j\}_{j}$ satisfying $\bm{O}_j^\top \bm{O}_j=\bm{I}$, then setting a subset to $\bm{I}$ yields $\tilde{\bm{B}}'=\mathrm{diag}(\ldots,\bm{O}_{j},\ldots,\bm{I},\ldots)$ and still $\tilde{\bm{B}}'^\top \tilde{\bm{B}}'=\mathrm{diag}(\ldots,\bm{I},\ldots,\bm{I},\ldots)=\bm{I}$. Because the product of orthogonal matrices is orthogonal, the dropped version $\bm{R}'(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}'^b_{(d,i)}$ also satisfies $\bm{R}'^\top \bm{R}'=\bm{I}_d$ and thus preserves orthogonality.

The paper further notes that butterfly components can be permuted to $2b\times 2b$ block-diagonal form, which makes the Dropout operation local to independent orthogonal blocks and guarantees full rank at each stage. Under the Cayley parameterization $\bm{O}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ with $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$, setting a block to $\bm{I}$ corresponds to choosing $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$ for that block, which is a valid orthogonal parameter setting. Thus, randomly replacing selected butterfly components or their diagonal sub-blocks by $\bm{I}$ simply reduces the effective transformation strength while keeping every factor orthogonal, ensuring the overall finetuning transform $\bm{R}$ remains orthogonal and the model remains valid during training.
