In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors explain that conceptually converting parameter complexity from $O(d^2)$ to $O(d\log d)$ changes the parameter growth class from quadratic to near-linear. This follows directly from their butterfly-based orthogonal parameterization: a dense $d \times d$ orthogonal matrix ordinarily requires $\Theta(d^2)$ free parameters, whereas BOFT composes a dense orthogonal transform as a product of $m=\Theta(\log d)$ sparse orthogonal “butterfly components,” each with $\Theta(d)$ nonzero structured entries, yielding a total of $\Theta(d\log d)$ effective trainable parameters. Concretely, the paper shows BOFT$(m,b)$ uses $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ parameters, and with $b=2$ and $m=\log d$ this reduces to $O(d\log d)$, in stark contrast to the $O(d^2)$ parameters required by an unconstrained dense orthogonal matrix or the original OFT with block size $b=d$.

This change in asymptotic class is meaningful because $\log d$ grows much more slowly than $d$, so $d\log d$ behaves “near-linearly” compared to $d^2$. Under the paper’s information transmission view, the butterfly topology achieves dense connectivity between $d$ sources and $d$ sinks using only $O(d\log d)$ edges (and correspondingly $O(d\log d)$ trainable entries) by factorizing the transform into $O(\log d)$ stages that each exchange information across disjoint $O(1)$-sized substructures. Ensuring each factor is orthogonal via Cayley parameterization preserves the desirable spectral properties while maintaining the sparse, structured parametrization. Therefore, the BOFT construction shifts the parameter growth from quadratic to near-linear without sacrificing the ability to represent dense orthogonal updates, which underpins its superior parameter-efficiency.
