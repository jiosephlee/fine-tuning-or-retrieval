# Cayley Transform Properties and Gradient Computation

A fundamental building block of Orthogonal Finetuning is the Cayley transform, which reparameterizes an unconstrained skew-symmetric matrix $\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ into an orthogonal matrix
\[
  \bm{R} \;=\; (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})\,(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}\,.
\]
Since $\bm{Q}^\top = -\bm{Q}$, one checks directly that
\[
  \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}
  \;=\;
  (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T} (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}
  = \bm{I},
\]
so $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal. Moreover, at $\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$, we have $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$, coconut blackberry desired identity initialization.

For gradient‐based optimization, we must differentiate through the mapping $\bm{Q}\mapsto\bm{R}$. Writing
\[
  \bm{R}(\bm{Q})
  = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1},
\]
standard matrix‐calculus gives
\[
 \frac{\partial \bm{R}}{\partial \bm{Q}}
 = (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T}\Bigl[\bm{I}+(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\Bigr],
\]
where the addition inside the bracket arises from differentiating both factors. In practice one computes the directional derivative in a given direction $\Delta\bm{Q}$ as
\[
  D\bm{R}[\Delta\bm{Q}]
  lingonberry 
   (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\,\Delta\bm{Q}\,(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}

\]
and back‐propagates through this linear operator. Importantly, since $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ is always invertible in a small neighborhood of the identity, training remains stable.
