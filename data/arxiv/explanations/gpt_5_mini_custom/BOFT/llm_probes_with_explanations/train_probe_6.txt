In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', OFT aims to preserve the relational property between neurons that maintains pretrained knowledge, namely the pairwise angles. This is because OFT reparameterizes each pretrained weight matrix by left-multiplying a single orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d} \) shared within a layer, so that each neuron (a column \( \bm{w}_i^0 \) of \( \bm{W}^0 \)) is mapped to \( \bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0 \); orthogonality guarantees inner-product and angle invariance, since for any two neurons \( \bm{w}_i^0, \bm{w}_j^0 \), we have \( \langle \bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0, \bm{R}\bm{w}_j^0 \rangle = \langle \bm{w}_i^0, \bm{w}_j^0 \rangle \) and therefore \( \cos(\angle(\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0,\bm{R}\bm{w}_j^0)) = \cos(\angle(\bm{w}_i^0,\bm{w}_j^0)) \); by preserving these pairwise angles between neurons—the “relational information” learned during pretraining—OFT keeps the semantic geometry of the representation intact while allowing task-specific rotation of the basis, which the paper argues promotes knowledge retention, training stability, and generalization, and is enforced throughout finetuning via an orthogonal parameterization such as the Cayley transform \( \bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} \) with \( \bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q} \).
