In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', BOFT's multiplicative Dropout regularizes selected components as follows: replace them with the identity. This is sensible because BOFT updates weights multiplicatively via an orthogonal product \( \bm{R}(m,b)=\prod_{i=1}^m \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \), so the natural analogue of zeroing an additive update (as in LoRA) is to “skip” a multiplicative factor by setting it to the identity; concretely, the method randomly selects a fraction \(p_1\) of butterfly components and a fraction \(p_2\) of their \(2b\times 2b\) diagonal blocks and replaces those blocks with \( \bm{I} \), yielding a masked product in which dropped factors contribute no change. This preserves orthogonality (since the product of orthogonal matrices with identities remains orthogonal), maintains the spectral norm of the pretrained weights (left-multiplication by \( \bm{R} \) does not alter singular values), and mirrors identity initialization (Cayley-parameterized blocks start at \( \bm{I} \)), thereby acting as a principled regularizer that stochastically reduces capacity without introducing additive perturbations or scale drift.
