In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the unstated assumption about pretrained representations that justifies preserving pairwise angles between neurons is that angles encode semantics. This is sensible because the columns of a pretrained weight matrix, viewed as neuron direction vectors \( \{\bm{w}_i^0\} \), embody relational structure learned during pretraining, and OFT/BOFT applies a shared orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \) so that for any pair \(i,j\) the cosine similarity (and hence the angle) is invariant: \( \cos\theta_{ij} = \frac{(\bm{w}_i^0)^\top \bm{w}_j^0}{\|\bm{w}_i^0\|\|\bm{w}_j^0\|} = \frac{(\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0)^\top (\bm{R}\bm{w}_j^0)}{\|\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0\|\|\bm{R}\bm{w}_j^0\|} \) because \( \bm{R}^\top \bm{R}=\bm{I} \), so inner products and norms are preserved; the paper explicitly motivates this by arguing that maintaining “relational information of the weight matrices, i.e. the pairwise angles between neurons,” preserves “semantic knowledge from pretraining,” echoing hyperspherical energy results where well-separated directions correspond to diverse, non-redundant semantic features, and thus an orthogonal reparameterization lets finetuning rotate the feature basis without distorting the geometry that encodes semantic similarity structure.
