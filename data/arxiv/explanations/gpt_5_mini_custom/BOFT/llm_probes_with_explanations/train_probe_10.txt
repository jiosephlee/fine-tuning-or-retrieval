In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', if BOFT's sparse factors were not constrained to be orthogonal, the stability benefit tied to SVD that would likely disappear is spectral norm preservation. This follows from the paper’s observation that OFT/BOFT left-multiply a pretrained weight matrix with an orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \), and by writing \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), we have \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), so all singular values—and hence the spectral norm \(\|\bm{W}^0\|_2\)—are exactly preserved because \(\bm{R}\) is orthogonal; this preservation underpins training stability and generalization. BOFT enforces this by parameterizing each butterfly component as orthogonal (via Cayley transforms and block-wise \(2\times 2\) rotations) so their product \(\bm{R}\) remains orthogonal. If the sparse factors were not orthogonal, their product would in general be non-orthogonal and could distort singular values, yielding \(\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2 \neq \|\bm{W}^0\|_2\); this breaks the SVD-based invariance argument, potentially introducing spectral norm inflation or shrinkage (and associated instability such as exploding/vanishing gradients) that the paper argues orthogonality avoids.
