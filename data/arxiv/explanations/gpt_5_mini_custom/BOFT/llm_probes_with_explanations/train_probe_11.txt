In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', in the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$, the property of $\bm{Q}$ that guarantees $\bm{R}$ stays orthogonal during finetuning is skew symmetry. This follows from the standard Cayley transform: if $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric (i.e., $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$), then $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal because $\bm{R}^\top=((\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1})^\top(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top=((\bm{I}-\bm{Q}^\top)^{-1})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q}^\top)=((\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$, which yields $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=((\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$, ensuring orthogonality throughout training provided $\bm{Q}$ remains skew-symmetric; accordingly, the paper explicitly sets $\bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$ (with zero/skew-symmetric initialization) to preserve orthogonality and thus the spectral properties and pairwise angles that motivate OFT/BOFT.
