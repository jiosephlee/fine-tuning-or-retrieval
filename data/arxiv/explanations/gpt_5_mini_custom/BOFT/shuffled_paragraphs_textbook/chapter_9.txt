# Summary and practical implications

- The block size $b$: BOFT uses block-butterfly components of block size $b$ (a block-butterfly generalization). The effective number of trainable parameters for a linear layer of width $d$ is
  $$
  \text{effective parameters} = \tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)d m.
  $$
  Thus, larger $b$ increases parameter count but may allow larger per-block expressivity; smaller $b$ (down to $b=2$) makes each butterfly factor built from many small orthogonal blocks, which combined over multiple factors produces dense mixing with favorable parameter scaling. The canonical butterfly parameterization uses $b=2$ and $m=\log d$, yielding $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ tunable parameters.

- BOFT preserves spectral properties of pretrained weights and uses identity initialization, which contributes to stable and smooth training; multiplicative Dropout is a practical regularizer adapted to the multiplicative parameterization.

- LoRA and its variants: low-rank additive reparameterization methods such as LoRA are widely used in language and vision finetuning. They modify a weight matrix by adding a product of two low-rank matrices; the rank determines the parameter budget and expressivity. LoRA is a natural comparator because it targets parameter efficiency through a different structural prior (low-rankness).

Beyond raw task metrics, the paper emphasizes the accuracy–parameter trade-off: plotting or comparing performance as a function of the number of effective trainable parameters to show parameter efficiency. Training stability and convergence behavior are assessed via optimization curves (loss vs. steps) and qualitative descriptions of smoothness and lack of divergence.

These baselines span the typical trade-offs in PEFT: additive vs. multiplicative updates, low-rank vs. sparse-block vs. dense parameterizations, and full vs. partial parameter updates. By comparing BOFT to this set, the experiments aim to show whether BOFT’s multiplicative, structured orthogonal parameterization achieves better generalization–parameter trade-offs than both additive low-rank methods and block-sparse orthogonal methods.

- The structural hyperparameters $(m,b)$ let practitioners tune the expressivity vs. parameter budget: smaller $b$ with larger $m$ often yields superior parameter efficiency, while $m=1$ recovers the block-diagonal OFT family.

BOFT has two structural hyperparameters and one regularization mechanism that control the trade-offs:

- BOFT yields a better accuracy (or task metric) versus parameter trade-off than block-diagonal OFT when both use comparable parameter budgets. By composing a dense orthogonal transformation from a product of structured sparse factors (butterfly components), BOFT attains greater expressivity than the block-diagonal sparsity with roughly the same or fewer parameters.

The ablations collectively show interpretability of the hyperparameters: $m$ and $b$ provide knobs to trade parameter count against expressivity, and multiplicative Dropout is a practical regularizer. Empirically, BOFT configurations with smaller $b$ and larger $m$ (e.g., many shallow orthogonal mixes) tend to be more parameter-efficient than a single block-diagonal orthogonal matrix of a larger block size.

A central motivation for orthogonal finetuning is preserving important spectral properties of pretrained weights, which in turn benefits optimization stability. The paper highlights the following stability-related observations:

- Multiplicative Dropout: Because the BOFT update is multiplicative (orthogonal matrix multiplies pretrained weights), standard additive dropout schemes do not apply directly. The paper introduces multiplicative Dropout: randomly select $p_1$ percent of the butterfly components and $p_2$ percent of diagonal blocks in each selected component to replace with identity matrices during training. This stochastically reduces model capacity per step and acts as regularization. Ablation studies in the paper show multiplicative Dropout reduces overfitting and improves generalization when parameter budgets are tight, mirroring how (additive) Dropout benefits other PEFT methods.

- OFT block-diagonal: the original Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) method with block-diagonal orthogonal matrices. This variant reduces OFT’s parameter cost by splitting the full orthogonal matrix into independent small orthogonal blocks. It preserves the multiplicative, orthogonal update idea but with a sparsifying inductive bias.

- A modest training-time overhead is incurred because BOFT multiplies several sparse factors during each forward/backward pass. However, once finetuning is complete, the orthogonal factors can be folded into the pretrained weights, incurring no inference latency.

To make the expressivity/parameter-efficiency trade-offs concrete, the paper presents a targeted approximation experiment: approximate a random dense orthogonal matrix of size $512\times 512$ using BOFT parameterizations of varying $(m,b)$ and compare the approximation error as a function of effective parameter count. Key qualitative findings from that study are:

- Generative diffusion models (text-to-image): image quality and alignment metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and downstream alignment measures that capture how well the conditioned generation follows the control signals. For controllable generation, the paper emphasizes preserved pretraining knowledge and stable, generalizable behavior.

- Compared to LoRA and other low-rank additive approaches, BOFT often generalizes better for comparable parameter budgets. The orthogonal, multiplicative update preserves spectral properties of the pretrained weight matrices (see the spectral discussion below), which empirically translates into improved training stability and downstream performance in several adaptation settings.

- Specific comparative observations (without quoting numeric values) show that BOFT(9,2) can surpass BOFT(1,16) in approximation quality while using fewer parameters; similarly, BOFT(6,4) achieves errors comparable to BOFT(2,16) at significantly reduced parameter cost. These trends indicate that deep, sparse factorization (the butterfly philosophy) is an effective route to approximate dense orthogonal transformations without quadratic parameter growth.

- Adapters and related modular methods: adapter-tuning methods that inject small task-specific modules (often additive and small) between layers are also considered as representative PEFT techniques.

- Language tasks (LLMs): perplexity on held-out language modeling data and downstream task metrics (e.g., accuracy or task-specific scores) for fine-tuning tasks. Perplexity measures how well the adapted language model predicts held-out tokens.

- The leftmost points on the approximation curves correspond to BOFT(1,b), i.e., the block-diagonal OFT baseline for the given block size. The curves show consistent error reduction as the number of parameters grows via increasing $m$ or $b$, and they demonstrate the favorable slope (i.e., better return per parameter) characteristic of the butterfly parameterization.

- The number of butterfly factors $m$: BOFT composes the final orthogonal matrix as a product of $m$ orthogonal butterfly components. Increasing $m$ generally increases expressivity because more factor layers enable richer mixing across dimensions; however, it also increases the number of effective tunable parameters linearly. In practice, larger $m$ (more composition depth) helps approximate dense orthogonal transformations with fewer parameters per factor, improving performance up to diminishing returns. The paper reports that increasing $m$ while decreasing block size $b$ often yields better parameter efficiency than the opposite trade.

This approximation study provides an empirical foundation for the practical finetuning results: if BOFT can approximate dense orthogonal matrices with far fewer parameters, then it can realize the advantages of dense OFT (preserving angles and spectral properties) while remaining parameter-efficient.

- BOFT subsumes OFT as special cases (e.g., BOFT(1,b) equals block-diagonal OFT with block size $b$, BOFT(1,d) is full OFT). This nesting allows BOFT to smoothly interpolate between the extremes of full OFT and highly structured parameter-efficient variants, enabling a practitioner to choose a point on the expressivity–regularity trade-off curve that fits the task and computational budget.

Taken together, the experiments and targeted approximation studies in the paper build a consistent picture: butterfly-based orthogonal reparameterization provides a principled, practical, and empirically robust route to parameter-efficient finetuning across modalities, combining the benefits of OFT (spectral preservation and angle-preserving updates) with much-improved parameter scaling and expressivity.

The empirical evaluation in the paper supports several actionable conclusions:

- The canonical butterfly configuration ($b=2$, $m=\log d$) realizes a dense orthogonal transform with $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ parameters, offering a scalable way to get dense-like expressive power without quadratic costs. The effective parameter count for a given BOFT configuration is
  $$
  \text{effective parameters} = \tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)d m,
  $$
  which makes budgeting straightforward.

Chapter 9: Empirical Evaluation Across Modalities

- Full finetuning: all model parameters are updated. This is the uncompressed reference point for peak performance but is prohibitively expensive in parameter updates and storage for very large models.

The combination of spectral preservation, identity initialization, and structured parameter updates leads BOFT to be both expressive and stable in practice.

Across the suite of experiments, BOFT consistently delivers favorable performance at lower parameter budgets compared to the baselines. The core empirical claims are:

- Empirical smoothness: In practice, BOFT training curves reported in the experiments are smoother and exhibit steadier progress toward lower loss compared to low-rank additive methods in similar setups. The paper attributes this stability to the orthogonality constraint and to the structured, distributed manner in which BOFT mixes dimensions (butterfly factorization avoids abrupt, global parameter changes in a single dense matrix).

The paper evaluates BOFT across three broad classes of foundation models to demonstrate its generality: large vision transformers (ViTs), large language models (LLMs), and text-to-image diffusion models. These model families represent different architectures and learning problems: ViTs are used for image classification and related vision tasks, LLMs are evaluated on language modeling and downstream NLP tasks, and diffusion models are evaluated for controllable generative image synthesis. The experimental agenda was therefore to adapt pretrained, high-capacity foundation models from each class to a variety of downstream tasks in vision and language, using BOFT as the parameter-efficient finetuning mechanism.

- Spectral norm preservation: For a pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W}^0$ with singular value decomposition $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$, left-multiplying by any orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ yields $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0=\underbrace{(\bm{R}\bm{U})}_{\text{orthogonal}}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$. Consequently, the singular values $\bm{\Sigma}$ (and in particular the spectral norm) are unchanged. This invariance is important because abrupt changes in spectral norm during finetuning can destabilize gradients and optimization. BOFT, by enforcing orthogonality of the learned transformation, inherits this desirable property and thus experiences smoother training curves and more stable convergence compared to additive, unconstrained updates.

- BOFT is a modality-agnostic PEFT method: it applies effectively to vision transformers, large language models, and text-to-image diffusion models. It consistently improves the accuracy–parameter trade-off relative to common baselines.

- Convergence behavior: BOFT converges reliably across the tested modalities and tasks. While the multiplicative factorization introduces modest per-step computational overhead (several sparse factor multiplications instead of a single block multiplication), it does not introduce optimization fragility. Moreover, BOFT’s initialization at identity (via Cayley parameterization with skew-symmetric zeros) ensures the finetuning process begins from the exact pretrained model, further stabilizing early optimization dynamics.

- Configurations with smaller block size $b$ and larger number of factors $m$ (more layers of sparse mixing) yield better parameter efficiency. For instance, a BOFT instance with many shallow factors can outperform a shallow BOFT with large block sizes despite having fewer parameters.

- Vision tasks (ViTs): classification accuracy, top-1/top-5 accuracy where applicable, and other downstream vision metrics depending on the dataset (e.g., transfer accuracy on fine-grained recognition).

- Empirically, on large ViTs, LLMs and diffusion models, BOFT outperforms state-of-the-art PEFT methods by a meaningful margin in the paper’s experiments, validating its superior parameter efficiency and generalization. The paper highlights this improvement as consistent across modalities rather than isolated to a single task family.

This cross-modal evaluation is important because it tests whether the orthogonal, butterfly-structured parameterization (i) preserves useful pretrained structure across modalities, (ii) provides stable optimization behavior for models that are sensitive to spectral changes, and (iii) yields a favorable trade-off between the number of tunable parameters and downstream performance. In the paper, BOFT is applied in precisely these three settings to show that the approach is not specific to one domain or architecture type but rather leverages a broadly useful structural prior (orthogonality + butterfly factorization) for finetuning.

- BOFT generally attains lower approximation error for the same number of effective trainable parameters than block-diagonal OFT. This supports the claim that the butterfly factorization is a more expressive and efficient structured subset of the orthogonal group than a block-diagonal pattern.
