Chapter Title: Initial Behavior of Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning under Identity Initialization

# Background: Finetuning and Orthogonal Finetuning

Finetuning large pretrained models aims to adapt them to downstream tasks while preserving the useful structure and knowledge encoded during pretraining. A common principle is to constrain the finetuned model to remain close to the pretrained one. Traditional approaches often rely on small learning rates to limit parameter drift, but this heuristic does not explicitly leverage the structured nature of weight matrices.

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) provides a principled alternative grounded in geometric properties of neural weights. The central idea is to transform the neurons within the same layer by a shared orthogonal matrix. Orthogonal matrices preserve inner products and angles, so applying an orthogonal transformation to the columns (neurons) of a weight matrix guarantees that pairwise angles between neurons are maintained throughout finetuning. In OFT, given a pretrained linear layer with weight matrix $\bm{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times n}$, the finetuned forward pass becomes
$
\bm{z} = (\bm{R}\bm{W}^0)^{\top}\bm{x},
$
where $\bm{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the input, $\bm{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ is the output, and $\bm{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is a learned orthogonal matrix. To ensure orthogonality during optimization, OFT uses the Cayley parameterization
$
\bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1},
$
where $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric, i.e., $\bm{Q} = -\bm{Q}^{\top}$. With this reparameterization, the optimization proceeds over $\bm{Q}$ while keeping $\bm{R}$ orthogonal at all times.

While OFT is effective and principled, the number of trainable parameters can be large when $\bm{R}$ is dense. A block-diagonal orthogonal structure reduces parameters by limiting $\bm{R}$ to independent blocks, but the fixed sparsity pattern reduces expressivity and cannot recover classic dense linear transforms. These constraints motivate designing parameter-efficient yet dense orthogonal parameterizations.

# The BOFT Reparameterization: Orthogonal Butterfly Matrices

Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning (BOFT) addresses parameter efficiency by factorizing a dense orthogonal matrix into a product of sparse orthogonal matrices arranged in a butterfly structure. This construction is inspired by the Cooley-Tukey algorithm for fast Fourier transform, where information is exchanged efficiently through a sequence of sparse operations that collectively induce dense connectivity. In BOFT, the orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}(m, b)$ is defined as the product of $m$ orthogonal “butterfly components,” each having a structured sparsity pattern:
$
\bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,i)},
$
with the constraint that every component is orthogonal,
$
\big(\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,j)}\big)^{\top}\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,j)} = \tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,j)}\big(\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,j)}\big)^{\top} = \bm{I}_d \quad \text{for all } j \in [1,m].
$
Each $\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,i)}$ is a block-diagonal matrix composed of structured blocks derived from butterfly factors, and these components can be parameterized as orthogonal using either the Cayley transform or explicit low-dimensional orthogonal parameterizations per block.

This factorization yields a dense orthogonal $\bm{R}(m,b)$ while dramatically reducing the number of effective trainable parameters. For a linear layer of size $d \times n$, BOFT$(m,b)$ uses $\tfrac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ effective parameters. In the canonical butterfly setting with $m = \log d$ and $b = 2$, BOFT uses $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ parameters, compared to $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ parameters for an unconstrained dense orthogonal matrix in OFT. Moreover, BOFT subsumes OFT as special cases: BOFT$(1,b)$ reduces to block-diagonal OFT with block size $b$, and BOFT$(1,d)$ recovers OFT with a full dense orthogonal matrix.

# Identity Initialization in BOFT

A key design choice in finetuning is initialization. To prevent unintended deviation from the pretrained model, it is desirable that the finetuning reparameterization begins at a point that exactly reproduces the pretrained behavior. In BOFT, this goal is achieved by initializing every butterfly component as an identity matrix. Concretely, since each orthogonal component is parameterized via the Cayley transform, identity initialization corresponds to setting the skew-symmetric parameters to zero:
$
\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,i)} = \bm{I}_d \quad \text{for all } i \in [1,m]
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\bm{Q} = \bm{0} \text{ in } (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}.
$
With this initialization, the product of identity components is also identity:
$
\bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\bm{B}}^{b}_{(d,i)} = \bm{I}_d.
$
This identity initialization yields the exact pretrained computation in the forward pass at the start of finetuning, aligning with the broader practice that finetuning methods should begin from the pretrained solution.

The structural nature of BOFT makes identity initialization straightforward. Because every butterfly component can be permuted into block-diagonal matrices with small orthogonal blocks (e.g., $2 \times 2$ or $2b \times 2b$), setting these blocks to identity ensures the global component is identity. As a result, BOFT starts from a configuration that exerts no change on any layer’s output, preserving the semantics and numerical properties of the pretrained model at initialization.

# Why Outputs Remain Unchanged Initially: Algebraic Proof and Intuition

The forward pass in BOFT for a linear layer with pretrained weights $\bm{W}^0$ is
$
\bm{z} = (\bm{R}(m,b)\bm{W}^0)^{\top}\bm{x}.
$
At identity initialization, we have $\bm{R}(m,b) = \bm{I}_d$. Substituting this into the forward pass yields
$
\bm{z} = (\bm{I}_d\bm{W}^0)^{\top}\bm{x} = (\bm{W}^0)^{\top}\bm{x},
$
which is the exact pretrained output. Therefore, BOFT’s initial effect on the layer’s outputs is no change. This conclusion follows directly from the multiplicative update form of OFT/BOFT and the orthogonal parameterization with identity initialization.

The Cayley parameterization further clarifies why identity is the natural initialization point. Since $\bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{Q})(\bm{I} - \bm{Q})^{-1}$, setting $\bm{Q} = \bm{0}$ gives
$
\bm{R} = (\bm{I} + \bm{0})(\bm{I} - \bm{0})^{-1} = \bm{I}\bm{I}^{-1} = \bm{I}.
$
This ensures orthogonality and identity exactly. Thus, BOFT’s parameterization provides a clean, exact way to start from the pretrained behavior without approximation or additive correction terms.

Intuitively, BOFT composes a dense orthogonal transformation by stacking $m$ sparse orthogonal butterfly components. When each component is identity, no information exchange or mixing occurs at any stage, so the structure remains dormant. Only when components deviate from identity do the butterfly couplings begin to induce dense mixing of neuron dimensions, thereby modifying the layer’s output.

# Information Transmission View of Identity Initialization

BOFT’s matrix factorization can be visualized through an information transmission lens. A product $\bm{R} = \bm{B}_m\bm{B}_{m-1}\cdots\bm{B}_1$ corresponds to information flowing across a $d \times (m+1)$ grid, where each $\bm{B}_i$ encodes directed edges between adjacent levels. Dense connectivity in $\bm{R}$ arises when every source node at level $1$ can reach every receiver node at level $m+1$ through paths formed by the sparse edges of the factors.

At identity initialization, each factor $\bm{B}_i$ is the identity matrix. In the transmission graph, identity corresponds to a trivial bijection: each node connects only to itself at the next level. Consequently, there is no cross-node information exchange across levels. The overall transmission from level $1$ to level $m+1$ is the identity mapping, so the composite transformation $\bm{R}$ acts as $\bm{I}_d$. This graphical perspective reinforces the algebraic statement: when all components are identity, BOFT transmits information transparently without alteration, leaving the pretrained layer’s outputs unchanged.

This view also clarifies how BOFT transitions from “no change” to “structured change.” As training progresses and components deviate from identity, edges in the transmission graph progressively enable mixing between different nodes. Through the butterfly topology, this mixing can achieve dense connectivity efficiently, allowing BOFT to explore expressive transformations while controlling parameter count.

# Implications for Training Stability and Generalization at Initialization

Because BOFT initially leaves the pretrained computation unchanged, it provides a stable starting point for finetuning. Orthogonal left-multiplication preserves the spectral norm of the weight matrix. If the pretrained weights admit a singular value decomposition $\bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}$, then the finetuned weights are $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}$. Since $\bm{R}$ is orthogonal, multiplying by $\bm{R}$ on the left does not affect the largest singular value. At initialization, with $\bm{R} = \bm{I}_d$, the spectral properties of $\bm{W}^0$ are exactly preserved, and the outputs are identical. This preservation aligns with the motivation of orthogonal finetuning to keep relational information between neurons intact and retain the semantic knowledge from pretraining, while enabling controlled, structured deviations during optimization.

Starting at identity also harmonizes with the philosophy that finetuning should begin from the pretrained model. It ensures that any change in behavior arises solely from learned deviations in the orthogonal components, rather than from initialization artifacts. As training proceeds, the multiplicative updates modulate the geometry of the neuron space while preserving angles, providing a stable path from the pretrained solution to a task-adapted solution.

# Relation to OFT and Block-Diagonal Special Cases

BOFT generalizes OFT by offering a dense orthogonal parameterization through butterfly factorization. Importantly, BOFT$(1,b)$ recovers block-diagonal OFT with block size $b$, and BOFT$(1,d)$ recovers the original OFT with a full dense orthogonal matrix. In all these cases, identity initialization is achieved by setting the underlying orthogonal parameters so that $\bm{R} = \bm{I}_d$, which ensures no change in outputs at the start of finetuning.

This unification highlights a consistent principle across the family of orthogonal finetuning methods: the multiplicative update form and orthogonal parameterization make it natural to start exactly at the pretrained model through identity initialization. BOFT preserves this property while offering a more parameter-efficient route to dense, expressive orthogonal transformations via butterfly structures. Consequently, the initial behavior—no change to outputs—holds uniformly across BOFT and its OFT special cases whenever all components are initialized as identity.
