In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors note that increasing expressiveness without appropriate regularity during finetuning commonly leads to overfitting. This follows from their core theme that finetuning must balance expressivity and inductive bias by restricting the hypothesis class: while BOFT can be made highly expressive—up to representing the entire orthogonal group via kaleidoscope-style compositions $\\bm{R}^G$—the authors emphasize that “better expressiveness does not always lead to better performance,” noting that full finetuning (maximal expressiveness) often underperforms due to poor generalization. BOFT deliberately constrains $\\bm{R}$ to structured subsets of $\\mathsf{O}(d)$ (e.g., butterfly-composed orthogonals) that both preserve desirable spectral properties (left-multiplying by orthogonal $\\bm{R}$ keeps the spectral norm of $\\bm{W}^0$ unchanged) and impose inductive biases aligned with classic fast transforms (Fourier, Hadamard), thereby regularizing solutions and reducing overfitting relative to unconstrained parameterizations. Their identity initialization and multiplicative dropout further illustrate the need for regularity to keep updates close to the pretrained model and to avoid over-parameterized adaptations; conversely, increasing $m$ and $b$ to enlarge the parameter space without such structural priors would expand the function class, weaken these biases, and make it easier for the model to fit idiosyncrasies of the finetuning data, i.e., to overfit.
