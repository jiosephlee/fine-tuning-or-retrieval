According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', to obtain a dense $\bm{R}$ using fewer sequential multiplications (smaller $m$) without abandoning parameter-efficiency, you should increase the block size. This follows from their BOFT construction, where a dense orthogonal matrix is achieved by BOFT$(m,b)$ when $m=\log\frac{2d}{b}$, so reducing $m$ necessarily requires increasing $b$ to maintain dense connectivity in the information-transmission view; moreover, the total number of effective trainable parameters scales as $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$, which for moderate $b$ and reduced $m$ remains $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ or otherwise far below $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ (the cost of a full dense orthogonal parameterization), hence increasing block size compensates for fewer mixing stages while retaining parameter-efficiency.
