According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', for fixed $d$ and $m$, the effective number of trainable parameters is given by $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$, so to minimize this quantity one should decrease $b$. This follows from the BOFT($m,b$) construction, where the orthogonal finetuning matrix $\bm{R}(m,b)$ is a product of $m$ orthogonal butterfly components $\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}$, each of which can be permuted into a block-diagonal form with $\frac{d}{b}$ blocks of size $2b\times 2b$; enforcing orthogonality within each block yields $(b-1)$ free parameters per $2b$-dimensional subspace (via $2\times 2$ rotations or Cayley transforms), giving $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)d$ effective parameters per component and thus $\frac{1}{2}(b-1)dm$ overall. Because this count is linear in $(b-1)$ and $b\ge 2$, for fixed $d$ and $m$ it is minimized by choosing the smallest feasible block size, $b=2$, which also recovers the $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ regime when $m=\log d$.
