\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization"}

### Proving spectral norm preservation under left orthogonal multiplication in BOFT
Question:
The paper claims $\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2=\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2$ because $\mathbf{R}$ is orthogonal. For $\mathbf{W}^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$ and $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ with $\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}$, could someone provide a clean proof that $\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0 x\|_2=\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}\|\mathbf{W}^0 x\|_2$? Also, in finite precision, if $\mathbf{R}$ is only approximately orthogonal, how sensitive is the spectral norm to small orthogonality errors accumulated across $m$ factors?
Answer:
Short answer: multiplying on the left by an exact orthogonal matrix does not change the spectral norm because left multiplication by an orthogonal matrix is an isometry on Euclidean space; in finite precision a small departure from orthogonality perturbs the norm by at most the operator norm of that departure, and for a product of m approximately-orthogonal factors the relative distortion accumulates roughly like the product of their operator norms (equivalently $\approx$ additive in the small-error regime).

Proof (clean, SVD-based). Write the SVD of $\mathbf{W}^0$ as $\mathbf{W}^0=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top$ where $\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ and $\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ are orthogonal and $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ is the rectangular diagonal of singular values. If $\mathbf{R}$ is orthogonal ($\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}$), then
$$
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0=(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U})\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top.
$$
But $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{U}$ is orthogonal (product of orthogonals). The singular values of a matrix are invariant under left- or right-multiplication by orthogonal matrices, hence the singular values of $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0$ are the same as those of $\mathbf{W}^0$. In particular the largest singular value (the spectral norm) is preserved:
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2=\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2.
$$
Equivalently, by the variational definition,
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2=\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0 x\|_2
=\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}\|\mathbf{W}^0 x\|_2,
$$
because $\|\mathbf{R}y\|_2=\|y\|_2$ for all $y$ when $\mathbf{R}$ is orthogonal.

Finite-precision / approximate-orthogonality sensitivity. Suppose $\mathbf{R}$ is only approximately orthogonal so that
$$
\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{E},
$$
with symmetric error $\mathbf{E}$ and $\|\mathbf{E}\|_2=\varepsilon$ small. Then
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\|_2^2=\|\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}\|_2=\|\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{E}\|_2=1+\delta
$$
for some $\delta$ with $|\delta|\le\varepsilon$. Thus
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\|_2=\sqrt{1+\delta}\le\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\approx 1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad(\text{for small }\varepsilon).
$$
Using the submultiplicativity of the operator norm,
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 \le \|\mathbf{R}\|_2\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 \le \sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\,\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2,
$$
and also
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 \ge \|\mathbf{R}^{-1}\|_2^{-1}\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2,
$$
if $\mathbf{R}$ is invertible. So the relative change in spectral norm is bounded by about $\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}-1\approx \varepsilon/2$.

For m factors $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}_m\cdots\mathbf{R}_1$ with per-factor orthogonality errors $\varepsilon_i=\|\mathbf{R}_i^\top\mathbf{R}_i-\mathbf{I}\|_2$, we get the crude bound
$$
\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2 \le \Big(\prod_{i=1}^m\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_i}\Big)\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2.
$$
If all $\varepsilon_i$ are small and similar, $\varepsilon_i\ll 1$, then using $\prod_i\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_i}\approx\exp\big(\tfrac12\sum_i\varepsilon_i\big)$, the relative distortion is approximately
$$
\frac{\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}^0\|_2}{\|\mathbf{W}^0\|_2}\approx 1+\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_i.
$$
So errors accumulate roughly additively in the small-error regime (or multiplicatively exactly). In floating point, typical orthogonality drift per factor often scales with machine epsilon $u$ and the factor dimension (e.g. $\varepsilon_i=O(d\,u)$ for some unstable routines), so the total distortion after m factors can be on the order of $O(m d u)$ (or more precisely $O(\sum_i\varepsilon_i)$).

Practical recommendations. If you need spectral-norm preservation to high accuracy in finite precision, enforce exact orthogonality up to numerical solve precision or periodically re-orthogonalize: use numerically stable parameterizations (products of 2x2 Givens rotations or Cayley/Givens parameterizations managed carefully), periodically reproject $\mathbf{R}$ to the nearest orthogonal matrix (via a thin QR or SVD), or keep per-factor orthogonality tight (use stable updates for the 2x2 blocks in BOFT). These controls keep $\varepsilon_i$ tiny so the accumulated effect across m factors remains negligible.

In short: exact orthogonality gives exact spectral-norm preservation; approximate orthogonality perturbs the norm by a factor at most $\prod_i\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_i}$, which for small per-factor errors is approximately $1+\tfrac12\sum_i\varepsilon_i$.
