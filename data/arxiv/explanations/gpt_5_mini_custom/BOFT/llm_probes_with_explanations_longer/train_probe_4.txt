According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the proposed multiplicative Dropout randomly replaces a percentage of the butterfly components and diagonal blocks in each butterfly component with identity matrices ℐ, relying on the implicit property that replacing these blocks by ℐ keeps the model valid, namely that this operation preserves orthogonality. This follows directly from how BOFT parameterizes the finetuning transform: the effective finetuning matrix is a product of orthogonal butterfly components, i.e., 𝑅(m,b)=∏_{i=1}^m \tilde{B}^b_{(d,i)} with each \tilde{B}^b_{(d,i)} satisfying (\tilde{B}^b_{(d,i)})^\top \tilde{B}^b_{(d,i)}=I_d. Since the identity matrix is itself orthogonal (I_d^\top I_d=I_d) and the orthogonal group is closed under multiplication, replacing any subset of factors \tilde{B}^b_{(d,i)} by I_d leaves the product orthogonal: if A and B are orthogonal, then AB is orthogonal, and with A=I_d this reduces to B unchanged. Thus multiplicative Dropout at the component level preserves the orthogonality of 𝑅(m,b).

Moreover, within each butterfly component, the paper notes that the structure can be permuted into a block-diagonal matrix with 2b×2b orthogonal blocks. A block-diagonal matrix diag(A_1, …, A_k) is orthogonal if and only if each diagonal block is orthogonal, because diag(A_1, …, A_k)^\top diag(A_1, …, A_k)=diag(A_1^\top A_1, …, A_k^\top A_k)=I provided A_i^\top A_i=I for all i. Since the identity block I is orthogonal, replacing any subset of diagonal blocks A_i by I preserves the orthogonality of the whole component. Consequently, when multiplicative Dropout randomly replaces a percentage of butterfly components and a percentage of diagonal blocks in each component by identity matrices, the resulting factor matrices remain orthogonal, and their product 𝑅(m,b) remains orthogonal, keeping the model’s finetuning transform valid while regularizing capacity.
