In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', in the Cayley parameterization \( \bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} \), the property of \( \bm{Q} \) that guarantees \( \bm{R} \) stays orthogonal during finetuning is skew symmetry. This follows from the classical Cayley transform: if \( \bm{Q} \) is skew-symmetric, i.e., \( \bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q} \), then \( (\bm{I}-\bm{Q}) \) is invertible except in the degenerate case where \(-1\) is an eigenvalue of the associated orthogonal matrix, and one can verify directly that
\[
\bm{R}^\top \bm{R} \;=\; \big((\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\big)^\top (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}
\;=\; (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}
\;=\; \bm{I},
\]
where we used \( (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top = \bm{I}+\bm{Q}^\top = \bm{I}-\bm{Q} \) and the identity \( (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q}) = \bm{I}-\bm{Q}^2 \). Thus the Cayley map sends skew-symmetric matrices to orthogonal matrices, ensuring that as long as the learnable parameter \( \bm{Q} \) is constrained to be skew-symmetric throughout optimization, the resulting \( \bm{R} \) remains orthogonal. The paper explicitly adopts this parameterization to enforce orthogonality “throughout the finetuning process,” and initializes with \( \bm{Q}=\bm{0} \), which yields \( \bm{R}=\bm{I} \) and thus exact zero-update initialization.

This mechanism is used both in OFT and its butterfly-based extension BOFT: each factor in the product decomposition is parameterized via Cayley (or 2D rotations in the block case), guaranteeing that every component \( \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \) is orthogonal, and therefore their product \( \bm{R}=\prod_i \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \) is orthogonal. Maintaining orthogonality is central to the method’s goals: multiplying the pretrained weights \( \bm{W}^0 \) on the left by \( \bm{R} \) preserves pairwise angles between neurons and keeps the spectral norm unchanged, since if \( \bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) then \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) has the same singular values. These invariances underpin the paper’s claims of stability and generalization in finetuning, and they rely precisely on the skew-symmetry constraint on \( \bm{Q} \) that makes the Cayley-parameterized \( \bm{R} \) orthogonal at all times.
