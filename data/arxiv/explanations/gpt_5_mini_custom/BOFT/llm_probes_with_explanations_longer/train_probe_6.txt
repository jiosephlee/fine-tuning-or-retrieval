In the paper “Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization,” OFT aims to preserve the relational property between neurons that maintains pretrained knowledge, namely the pairwise angles. This is because OFT updates a layer by left-multiplying its pretrained weight matrix with an orthogonal matrix \( \bm{R} \), so each neuron (a column vector \( \bm{w}_i^0 \) of \( \bm{W}^0 \)) is transformed to \( \bm{w}_i = \bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0 \). Orthogonality ensures \( \bm{R}^\top \bm{R} = \bm{I} \), which preserves inner products and therefore angles: for any two neurons \( i,j \), \( \langle \bm{w}_i, \bm{w}_j \rangle = (\bm{R}\bm{w}_i^0)^\top (\bm{R}\bm{w}_j^0) = (\bm{w}_i^0)^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{R} \bm{w}_j^0 = \langle \bm{w}_i^0, \bm{w}_j^0 \rangle \), and hence \( \cos \theta_{ij} = \frac{\langle \bm{w}_i, \bm{w}_j \rangle}{\|\bm{w}_i\|\,\|\bm{w}_j\|} = \frac{\langle \bm{w}_i^0, \bm{w}_j^0 \rangle}{\|\bm{w}_i^0\|\,\|\bm{w}_j^0\|} \) is unchanged. The paper operationalizes this principle by parameterizing \( \bm{R} \) via the Cayley transform \( \bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1} \) with a skew-symmetric \( \bm{Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top \), guaranteeing orthogonality throughout finetuning and thus exact preservation of pairwise angles.

This angle preservation is sensible as a mechanism to maintain pretrained knowledge because it retains the relational geometry of neurons that encodes semantic structure learned during pretraining. Prior work on hyperspherical energy and orthogonality (e.g., cited by the paper) suggests that the arrangement of neurons on or near a sphere—captured by pairwise angles—governs generalization and stability. By keeping \( \{\bm{w}_i\} \) in the same relational configuration (up to a global rotation), OFT preserves the layer’s pairwise similarities and decision geometry, while still allowing task-specific adaptation through a coherent orthogonal transformation. Moreover, since orthogonal left-multiplication also preserves singular values of \( \bm{W}^0 \) (if \( \bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), then \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \) has the same \( \bm{\Sigma} \)), the spectral norm and stability of the pretrained mapping are maintained, reinforcing the goal of preserving pretrained knowledge while adapting to downstream tasks.
