In the paper “Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization,” BOFT's multiplicative Dropout regularizes selected components as follows: replace them with the identity. This is sensible because BOFT updates weights multiplicatively via an orthogonal reparameterization, namely \( \bm{R}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} \tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \), where each \(\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}\) is an orthogonal butterfly component that can be permuted into block-diagonal \(2b \times 2b\) orthogonal blocks. In this multiplicative setting, the correct analogue of additive Dropout (which zeros additive updates) is to stochastically “skip” multiplicative updates by replacing selected components or blocks with \(\bm{I}\). Because \(\bm{I}\) is orthogonal and neutral under multiplication, such replacement preserves both the overall orthogonality of \(\bm{R}(m,b)\) and the spectral properties of the pretrained layer (e.g., the spectral norm), while preventing certain structured transformations from being applied in that iteration.

Concretely, BOFT defines a Dropout over the hierarchy of components by randomly choosing a proportion \(p_1\) of butterfly components and a proportion \(p_2\) of the diagonal blocks within each component, and setting them to \(\bm{I}\). If we denote the dropped version by \(\hat{\bm{R}}(m,b) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} \hat{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}\) with \(\hat{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)} \in \{\tilde{\bm{B}}^b_{(d,i)}, \bm{I}\}\) at the component level (and similarly at the block level), then \(\hat{\bm{R}}(m,b)\) remains orthogonal because it is a product of orthogonal matrices and identities: \(\hat{\bm{R}}(m,b)^\top \hat{\bm{R}}(m,b) = \bm{I}\). This multiplicative Dropout thus acts as a structured, stochastic gating of orthogonal transformations, analogous to Dropout in additive methods like LoRA, but tailored to BOFT’s multiplicative update. It mitigates overfitting by reducing the effective capacity per iteration, maintains training stability by preserving orthogonality and spectral norms, and leverages the block structure induced by butterfly factorization for fine-grained regularization.
