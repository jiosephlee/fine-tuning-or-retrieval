In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', if BOFT's sparse factors were not constrained to be orthogonal, the stability benefit tied to SVD that would likely disappear is spectral norm preservation. The paper’s spectral argument hinges on left-multiplying a pretrained weight matrix by an orthogonal matrix. Writing the pretrained weights as an SVD, \( \bm{W}^0 = \bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \), BOFT produces finetuned weights \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 = (\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top \). When \(\bm{R}\) is orthogonal, \(\bm{R}\bm{U}\) remains orthogonal and the singular values in \(\bm{\Sigma}\) are unchanged, so \( \|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2 = \|\bm{W}^0\|_2 \). Equivalently, since \( \|\bm{R}\|_2 = 1 \) for orthogonal \(\bm{R}\), we have \( \|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2 \le \|\bm{R}\|_2 \|\bm{W}^0\|_2 = \|\bm{W}^0\|_2 \), with equality attained. This spectral norm preservation is cited as a key factor for training stability and generalization.

If the sparse factors composing \(\bm{R}\) were not orthogonal, their product would generally satisfy \( \|\bm{R}\|_2 \neq 1 \), and left-multiplication could amplify or attenuate singular values of \(\bm{W}^0\). In SVD terms, \( (\bm{R}\bm{U}) \) would no longer be orthogonal, so the spectrum of \( \bm{R}\bm{W}^0 \) would deviate from \( \bm{\Sigma} \), yielding \( \|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2 \) that can be larger or smaller than \( \|\bm{W}^0\|_2 \). This breaks the Lipschitz control that orthogonality provides, undermines the preservation of pairwise angles between neurons, and removes the spectral norm invariance that the paper associates with better stability and generalization. Consequently, dropping orthogonality in BOFT’s factors would forfeit the SVD-based guarantee that the largest singular value—and thus the spectral norm—remains fixed during finetuning.
