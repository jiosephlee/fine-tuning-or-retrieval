In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the authors employ the Cayley parameterization $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ where $\bm{Q}$ is skew-symmetric. For the Cayley parameterization to be valid, the implicit mathematical condition on $\bm{Q}$ that must hold is that $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ is invertible. This is necessary because the definition of $\bm{R}$ involves the inverse $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$; without invertibility, $\bm{R}$ would be undefined. For real skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$, this condition is in fact automatically satisfied: the spectrum of $\bm{Q}$ lies on the imaginary axis, i.e., its eigenvalues are $\{i\alpha_j\}_{j}$ with $\alpha_j\in\mathbb{R}$ (including the possibility $\alpha_j=0$). Consequently, the eigenvalues of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ are $\{1-i\alpha_j\}_{j}$, none of which is zero since $|1-i\alpha_j|=\sqrt{1+\alpha_j^2}>0$. Thus $\det(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})\neq 0$ and $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ is invertible, justifying the use of the Cayley transform in the paper.

Moreover, the invertibility of $\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ ensures that the Cayley transform indeed maps skew-symmetric matrices to orthogonal matrices, which is central to preserving pairwise angles in OFT and BOFT. Using $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$, one can verify orthogonality algebraically:
$\bm{R}^\top=\big((\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\big)^\top(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top=(\bm{I}-\bm{Q}^\top)^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$,
and hence
$\bm{R}^\top\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$.
This property guarantees that left-multiplication by $\bm{R}$ preserves the spectral norm and neuron angles, which the paper leverages to argue training stability and generalization in orthogonal finetuning.
