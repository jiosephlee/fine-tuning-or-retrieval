In the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', the Cayley parameterization is written as $\bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$. The constraint required on $\bm{Q}$ so that $\bm{R}$ remains orthogonal is that $\bm{Q}$ be skew-symmetric. This follows from the classical Cayley transform: if $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$, then $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})$ is invertible and one can check orthogonality by computing
$\bm{R}^\top\bm{R} = \big((\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})\big)^\top \big((\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}\big) = (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T} (\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}$. Using $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$ gives $(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^\top=\bm{I}-\bm{Q}$ and $(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-T}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}$, so $\bm{R}^\top\bm{R} = (\bm{I}-\bm{Q})(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})^{-1}(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(\bm{I}-\bm{Q})^{-1}=\bm{I}$. Thus the Cayley map takes any skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}$ to an orthogonal $\bm{R}$; moreover, zero-initialization ($\bm{Q}=\bm{0}$) yields $\bm{R}=\bm{I}$, matching the finetuning requirement to start exactly at the pretrained weights.

This constraint is also natural from a parameter-count and stability perspective emphasized in the paper. A real skew-symmetric $\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ has $\frac{d(d-1)}{2}$ free parameters, matching the degrees of freedom of the orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(d)$, so optimizing $\bm{Q}$ while enforcing $\bm{Q}^\top=-\bm{Q}$ efficiently spans orthogonal updates without overparameterizing. Because BOFT composes the final $\bm{R}$ from multiple orthogonal butterfly components, ensuring each component is orthogonal via a blockwise Cayley parameterization (or $2\times2$ rotations) guarantees the product remains orthogonal. Practically, maintaining skew-symmetry during training is simple (e.g., set $\bm{Q}=\bm{P}-\bm{P}^\top$ for an unconstrained $\bm{P}$), and it preserves desirable properties highlighted by the paper, such as keeping the spectral norm of the pretrained weights unchanged: if $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$, then $\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$ for orthogonal $\bm{R}$, aiding stability and generalization.
