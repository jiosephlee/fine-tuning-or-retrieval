According to the paper 'Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization', preserving the spectral norm (i.e., the largest singular value $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$) via orthogonal finetuning primarily benefits training by improving stability and generalization. The key reason is that BOFT/OFT update a pretrained layer by left-multiplying an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$, so if $\bm{W}^0=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ is its SVD, then $\bm{R}\bm{W}^0=(\bm{R}\bm{U})\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^\top$ has exactly the same singular values as $\bm{W}^0$, hence $\|\bm{R}\bm{W}^0\|_2=\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$. Because $\bm{R}$ is enforced to be orthogonal throughout training (via the Cayley transform), this invariance holds at every optimization step. Preserving the operator norm keeps the layer’s Lipschitz constant unchanged, preventing uncontrolled amplification of activations and gradients that can destabilize optimization. In practice, maintaining a stable spectral scale avoids exploding updates and ill-conditioned curvature, yielding smoother loss landscapes and more reliable convergence.

This spectral control also supports generalization. Many generalization arguments and regularization techniques hinge on bounding the network’s Lipschitz constant, which is upper-bounded by the product of layer spectral norms. By fixing $\|\bm{W}^0\|_2$ under the orthogonal update, BOFT preserves the pretrained model’s calibration and does not inflate the capacity via norm growth, reducing overfitting risk while still allowing expressive rotations of neuron subspaces. Compared to additive updates that can increase $\|\bm{W}\|_2$ and inadvertently enlarge the hypothesis class, the orthogonal multiplicative update keeps the scale intact and instead exploits directional reorientation (preserving pairwise angles) to adapt features. This combination—stable spectral norm, preserved relational geometry, and structured expressivity—explains why orthogonal finetuning leads to both improved training stability and stronger generalization.
