\title{A Textbook about the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

Chapter 8: Experimental Setup and Baselines

# Models
The experiments in the LongRoPE study focus on two modern 7-billion-parameter decoder-only language models that use RoPE positional embeddings: LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B. Both models are treated as pre-trained backbones whose positional encoding is modified during search and (in some cases) fine-tuning to extend their context windows to very large sizes (up to 2,048k tokens). All modifications in LongRoPE keep the original Transformer architecture and weights intact except for the rescaling of RoPE rotary angles; this allows reuse of existing model codepaths and optimizations.

The comparisons reported in the paper evaluate both LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B after applying LongRoPE (with different fine-tuning choices) and compare them against multiple positional-interpolation and fine-tuned baselines. In subsequent sections I summarize the datasets, evaluation tasks, the exact fine-tuning and search configurations used for these two models, and the baselines used for comparison.

# Long-sequence language modeling datasets and sliding-window evaluation
LongRoPE is primarily evaluated by measuring language modeling quality (perplexity) when models consume very long contexts. The paper uses three corpora for long-sequence evaluation:

- PG19: the PG19 test split (100 documents) is used for language-model perplexity evaluation. For search-time evaluation, small subsets of PG19 validation examples are also used (details below). For long-context evaluation up to 256k, the full PG19 test split of 100 documents is used.

- Proof-pile: for very long examples, the experiments follow YaRN and select a small set of long sequences. Specifically, they randomly select 10 Proof-pile samples each with length at least 128k tokens for evaluation in the 128k–256k regime.

- Books3 (from the Pile): to evaluate behavior beyond one million tokens, the paper samples long books from Books3. For the 2,048k evaluation, they randomly select 20 books each exceeding 2,048k tokens and run sliding-window evaluation.

Sliding-window evaluation protocols
- Within the 256k evaluation regime (for PG19 and Proof-pile) the paper computes perplexity by moving a sliding window over each long document with a stride or evaluation window of 256 tokens. This means the model is asked to predict tokens in sliding windows across the document; aggregating cross-entropy over those windows yields a perplexity curve as a function of evaluation context length.

- For the Books3 (multi-million-token) evaluation the paper uses a much larger evaluation stride: a sliding window of 256k tokens is used to move across each book. This reduces computational cost while still measuring modeling quality on substantially long text spans (up to and beyond 2,048k).

These sliding-window regimes are chosen to measure how well LLMs make next-token predictions when allowed to attend to longer and longer prefixes of a document and to produce comparable perplexity curves across different context sizes.

# Passkey retrieval task
The passkey retrieval task is a synthetic, controlled evaluation that measures whether a model can retrieve a short secret placed somewhere inside a very long document. The setup used in the paper is as follows:

- A five-digit numeric passkey is randomly chosen for each trial and inserted into a large “sea” of irrelevant text at a single position.

- For each trial, the location of the passkey is sampled uniformly at random across the evaluation context length (so the passkey may appear very early, very late, or anywhere in between).

- Each experimental point reports the retrieval accuracy over 10 independent iterations (trials). In each iteration the prompt + document are constructed with the passkey at a different random location, and the model is asked (via generation prompt) to produce the passkey.

- The reported metric is simple accuracy: the fraction of trials in which the model successfully outputs the exact five-digit passkey.

This task is designed to stress whether the model can attend to and retrieve a very small piece of information placed anywhere within a long context. It is particularly sensitive to whether positional interpolation and fine-tuning preserve the model’s ability to locate and use specific content across long spans.

# Standard short-context benchmarks
To ensure that extending the context window does not unduly harm performance within the original, shorter context sizes, LongRoPE models are also evaluated on a set of standard LLM benchmarks (the paper uses datasets from the Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard). The benchmarks and shot settings used were:

- ARC-Challenge: 25-shot evaluation.
- HellaSwag: 10-shot evaluation.
- MMLU: 5-shot evaluation.
- TruthfulQA: 0-shot evaluation.

These tasks measure reasoning, commonsense, multi-domain knowledge, and truthfulness in the original (shorter) context regime. Comparing extended models on these benchmarks verifies whether LongRoPE preserves—or degrades—original capabilities designed for the native context length.

# Fine-tuning details
LongRoPE relies on a progressive fine-tuning strategy to reach extremely large target contexts without training directly at those lengths. The fine-tuning configurations differ slightly between LLaMA2 and Mistral:

LLaMA2-7B fine-tuning
- Optimizer and schedule: a learning rate of \(2\mathrm{e}{-5}\) with linear decay is used.
- Batch size: global batch size of 32.
- Data and chunking: RedPajama data is chunked into \(128\mathrm{k}\) segments; sequences are bookended with BOS and EOS tokens.
- Fine-tuning steps and progression: first fine-tune for 400 steps using the RoPE rescale factors produced for a \(128\mathrm{k}\) target length. After that checkpoint is obtained, replace the RoPE rescale factors with those computed for a \(256\mathrm{k}\) target and fine-tune for an additional 600 steps. This two-stage fine-tuning (400 + 600 steps) is more efficient and more stable than a single direct run to \(256\mathrm{k}\).
- Hardware: training the \(128\mathrm{k}\) stage used 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs; the \(256\mathrm{k}\) stage used 16 A100 GPUs.

Mistral-7B fine-tuning
- Optimizer and schedule: a constant learning rate of \(1\mathrm{e}{-6}\) is used.
- Batch size: global batch size of 64.
- Data and sequence length: the experiments followed YaRN’s setup and used Together Computer’s Long-Data Collections with a training sequence length of \(16\mathrm{k}\).
- Fine-tuning steps: 400 steps per extended-length model (for the reported 128k and 256k variants).
- Hardware: 4 A100 GPUs were used for Mistral fine-tuning.

Notes on the progressive approach
- The motivation for the staged (128k then 256k) fine-tuning is practical: extremely long direct fine-tuning (e.g., directly to 2048k) would require scarce training data at that length and prohibitively high GPU/time costs. LongRoPE therefore uses a progressive approach where a pre-trained LLM is first extended (via search) to 128k–256k, fine-tuned at those feasible lengths, and then further extended (via a second search) to 2,048k without further expensive fine-tuning.

# Search settings and evaluation during search
LongRoPE’s core is an evolution-based search that finds per-dimension RoPE rescaling factors \(\lambda_i\) and a token-threshold \(\hat{n}\) that together implement the two non-uniformities (across RoPE dimensions and across beginning token positions). The search details used in the experiments are:

Search space ranges
- Each per-dimension scale \(\lambda_i\) is searched in the range \([1.0,\; s\times 1.25]\) with step size \(0.01\), where \(s=L'/L\) is the target extension ratio. A value \(\lambda_i=1.0\) corresponds to direct extrapolation (no downscaling), while larger \(\lambda\) values correspond to stronger interpolation.
- The initial-token threshold \(\hat{n}\) (the number of leading positions left un-rescaled) is searched over the discrete set \(\{0,1,2,4,8,12,16,20,24,28,32,64,128,256\}\). When \(\hat{n}=0\) all positions are rescaled; nonzero \(\hat{n}\) means the first \(\hat{n}\) tokens keep their original (pretrained) RoPE angles.

Evolutionary algorithm specifics (for target windows up to \(256\mathrm{k}\))
- Population size: \(P=64\).
- Mutation size and crossover size: \(N_1=N_2=16\).
- Mutation probability for initializing additional individuals: \(p=0.3\).
- Iterations (generations): \(\mathcal{T}=40\).
- Parent selection for reproduction: top-32 individuals are selected each generation to produce mutation/crossover children.
- Initialization: instead of fully random initialization the initial population explicitly includes the three prior interpolation schemes (PI, NTK, YaRN) as seed individuals; the remaining \(P-3\) are random mutations of those seeds.

Monotonicity constraint
- To reduce the search space and bias toward reasonable solutions, the algorithm enforces a monotonic non-decreasing constraint on the \(\lambda_i\) across RoPE dimensions: \(\lambda_i \le \lambda_{i+1}\). This reflects the NTK-inspired intuition that lower RoPE dimensions (higher frequencies) should be interpolated less than higher dimensions (lower frequencies).

Perplexity-based fitness evaluation during search
- For target windows within \(256\mathrm{k}\), perplexity for each candidate RoPE is computed using 5 randomly sampled PG19 validation sequences that satisfy the minimum length requirement. The average perplexity over these validation samples is used as the fitness to drive evolution.
- For targets larger than \(512\mathrm{k}\), the algorithm reduces computation by halving population and mutation/crossover sizes, and uses 3 random validation samples from the Pile-Books3 dataset for perplexity evaluation (these samples are chosen to be long enough for the larger targets).

Reduced search budgets beyond \(512\mathrm{k}\)
- Because evaluating very large context lengths is expensive, the search hyperparameters are scaled down for very large targets: population, mutation, and crossover sizes are halved for search runs with target windows beyond \(512\mathrm{k}\).

Other practical search choices
- The initial population design, monotonicity constraint, and seeding with PI/NTK/YaRN all accelerate convergence and reduce the number of expensive model-perplexity evaluations required.
- The final selected individual is the candidate with the lowest validation perplexity found in the Top-k archive across generations.

# Baselines
LongRoPE is compared against several positional interpolation methods and models that have been previously extended to larger contexts. These baselines cover both interpolation-only changes (no fine-tuning) and fine-tuned extended models:

Positional interpolation baselines (methods applied to RoPE)
- PI (Linear Positional Interpolation): uniformly downscales rotation angles by the extension ratio \(s\).
- NTK-aware interpolation (and dynamic NTK variants): dimension-aware rescaling inspired by Neural Tangent Kernel analysis that reduces interpolation in lower-frequency dimensions.
- YaRN: a grouping-based method that partitions RoPE dimensions into three frequency bands and applies different interpolation/extrapolation rules per group.

Published or open-source extended models and fine-tuned baselines
- Together-32k: an open model variant extended to 32k context.
- Code LLaMA: an extended variant used for code modeling (included for comparison on longer-context behavior when available).
- LongLoRA-full-FT-100k: an extended model obtained by the LongLoRA fine-tuning procedure up to a 100k context length.
- YaRN-LLaMA and YaRN-Mistral: models fine-tuned after YaRN-style positional interpolation for LLaMA and Mistral, respectively.

The paper compares LongRoPE variants (differing by whether fine-tuning was performed at 128k or 256k) to these baselines across perplexity curves, passkey retrieval, and short-context benchmark accuracy.

# Evaluation metrics and experimental sample sizes
For clarity, here is how metrics are computed in each evaluation:

- Perplexity (language modeling): aggregated over sliding-window predictions across each test document. For 256k-regime experiments the sliding step was 256 tokens; for Books3 multi-million token evaluation the sliding step was 256k tokens. Perplexity curves are reported across a range of context lengths to show how model performance changes as the allowed attention span grows.

- Passkey retrieval accuracy: fraction correct across 10 independent trials, with the passkey placed uniformly at random across the evaluation length for each trial.

- Standard benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA): reported in the conventional way for each task under the shot settings listed earlier. These metrics are used to check whether extension affects original short-context capabilities.

- Search validation sizes: during search, fitness (perplexity) is measured on small random validation sets: 5 PG19 samples for targets \(\le 256\mathrm{k}\), and 3 Books3 samples for targets \(>512\mathrm{k}\). Evaluation test sizes for final reported results are larger (e.g., 100 PG19 test documents, 10 Proof-pile long samples, 20 Books3 books).

# Putting it together: experimental pipeline and rationale
The empirical protocol used in the paper follows this overall pipeline:

1. For a desired intermediate target (e.g., \(128\mathrm{k}\) or \(256\mathrm{k}\)), run the evolution-based search to find a per-dimension \(\lambda_i\) profile and a \(\hat{n}\) value that minimize validation perplexity (the search uses the settings described above).

2. Option A (no fine-tuning extension): apply the discovered rescaling directly at inference time — this is what enables modest extensions (in the paper up to an \(8\times\) extension) without fine-tuning.

   Option B (progressive fine-tuning to larger windows): fine-tune the model at a feasible intermediate target (\(128\mathrm{k}\) then \(256\mathrm{k}\)) using the corresponding discovered RoPE rescalings and the step counts and hardware setup described above.

3. After obtaining a fine-tuned \(256\mathrm{k}\) model, perform a secondary search (using the same evolutionary search but with appropriately scaled-down budgets for very large targets) on the fine-tuned model to discover rescalings that extend the model to the final target of \(2,048\mathrm{k}\). This second search is applied without further expensive fine-tuning.

4. Finally, to reduce short-context degradation caused by extreme interpolation, run an additional short-length search on the extended model (restricting the maximum allowed \(\lambda\) during search) to recover better performance at 4k–8k; the model then dynamically uses the appropriate rescaling at inference depending on sequence length.

This combination of careful per-dimension search, seeding and monotonic constraints, staged fine-tuning, and final recovery search is the experimental strategy used to report LongRoPE’s results across perplexity, passkey retrieval, and standard benchmarks.

# Summary
The experiments in the LongRoPE paper intentionally balance practicality (search budgets, limited fine-tuning steps, and available long-text data) with thorough evaluation across multiple axes. The two model families (LLaMA2-7B and Mistral-7B) were extended using evolution-discovered per-dimension RoPE rescalings, fine-tuned at feasible intermediate lengths (400 + 600 steps for LLaMA2; 400 steps for Mistral), and then evaluated with sliding-window perplexity, a targeted passkey retrieval task, and several short-context LLM benchmarks. The search and evaluation regimes are tuned to keep the most expensive steps tractable (reduced population and sample sizes beyond \(512\mathrm{k}\), large sliding-window strides for multi-million-token books) while still providing meaningful comparisons to interpolation baselines (PI, NTK, YaRN) and prior extended models (Together-32k, Code LLaMA, LongLoRA-full-FT-100k, YaRN-LLaMA/Mistral).
