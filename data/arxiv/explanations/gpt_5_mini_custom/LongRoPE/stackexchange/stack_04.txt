\title{Stack Exchange of the Paper: "LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens"}

### Are the per-dimension scale factors \(\{\lambda_i\}\) shared across layers and heads, or learned separately?
Question:
RoPE is typically applied per head with head dimension \(d_{\text{head}}\), yielding \(d_{\text{head}}/2\) angles per head. When LongRoPE searches \(\{\lambda_i\}\), are these indices \(i\) defined within each head (so each head has its own \(\lambda_{i,\text{head}}\)), or globally shared across all heads and layers? If shared, how do you map \(i\) across heads when their positions in the concatenated embedding space differ? If per-head or per-layer specialization is not used, did you test whether allowing it would help?
Answer:
Short answer: In LongRoPE the rescale factors are learned per RoPE dimension and applied globally (shared) across all heads and layers. The paper searches for a set of scalars ${\{\lambda_i\}_{i=0}^{d/2-1}}$ indexed by the RoPE dimension $i$, not by head or layer.

Why: the paper formulates the search over the rotary-angle rescale for each RoPE dimension (see Eq. (3) / Eq. (problem) in the manuscript). The search space and monotonicity constraint are defined over $i=0,\dots,d/2-1$; the evolution search returns one $\lambda_i$ per dimension. Practically that means the same $\lambda_i$ is used wherever that RoPE dimension appears in the model — i.e., for every head and every layer the rotation angle that corresponds to RoPE-dimension $i$ is multiplied by the same factor.

How mapping works across heads: a head of size $d_{\text{head}}$ contains $d_{\text{head}}/2$ complex-dimension pairs (cos,sin) that correspond to contiguous indices in the model-wide RoPE dimension ordering. LongRoPE’s $\lambda_i$ are indexed according to that same RoPE-dimension ordering, so for a given head you apply the subsequence ${\{\lambda_{i}\}}$ that correspond to that head’s local dimensions. In other words, mapping is by intra-head dimension index relative to the global RoPE indexing, not by having distinct per-head tables.

Did they try per-head / per-layer specialization? The paper does not report experiments that search or learn separate $\lambda$ per head or per layer. The ablations focus on (a) non-uniformity across RoPE dimensions versus linear interpolation and (b) the token-position threshold $\hat n$. There is no experiment in the text showing head- or layer-specific $\lambda$ and no trained per-head/per-layer search is described.

Intuition / tradeoffs if you were to try per-head or per-layer factors: allowing separate $\lambda$ per head or per layer increases expressivity and might capture head- or layer-wise usage differences of positional information (some heads/layers focus on local high-frequency signals, others on long-range low-frequency signals). But it also explodes the search / optimization space (from $d/2$ scalars to roughly $\#\text{layers}\times\#\text{heads}\times d_{\text{head}}/2$ scalars), making the evolutionary search far more expensive and risking overfitting to the small validation set used for perplexity-guided search. That trade-off explains the paper’s design choice to search per-dimension globally: it leverages NTK-informed monotonicity and keeps the search tractable while already giving large wins (8$\times$ non-finetune and progressive extension to 2048k).

If you want to explore this direction experimentally, a practical route is to start small: (1) experiment with per-layer (but shared across heads) $\lambda$ vectors, or (2) cluster heads into a few groups and search one $\lambda$-vector per group. Both keep search complexity controllable while testing whether head/layer specialization yields meaningful gains.
