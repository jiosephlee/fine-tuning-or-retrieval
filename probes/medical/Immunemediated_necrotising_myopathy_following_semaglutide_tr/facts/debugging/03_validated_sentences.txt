----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM) is a distinct entity of myopathy characterised by aggressive proximal muscle weakness with necrotising myofibres and a paucity of lymphocytic infiltrates on histology.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: Anti-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMGCR) and anti-signal recognition particle (SRP) are two autoantibodies highly associated with IMNM.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: In particular, the use of statins which are commonly used in metabolic disorders is strongly associated with HMGCR autoantibody positivity.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: In the case of statins, there were several patients who developed features of necrotising myopathy and were often classified as toxic myopathy or polymyositis prior to the discovery of HMGCR autoantibody association.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: In those cases, many patients had persistent myopathy and CK elevation despite cessation of the statin.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: These features, followed by improvement with immunosuppression, were suggestive of an autoimmune myopathy and eventually led to the identification of HMGCR autoantibody as a hallmark characteristic of the disease.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: It is notable, however, that an estimated third of patients with IMNM are seronegative.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: The lack of an autoantibody in this subset of patients not only confers additional diagnostic challenges but also suggests that there are associated autoantibodies and potential triggers yet to be discovered.

section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: GLP-1 receptor agonists, such as semaglutide, are becoming more commonly used and have demonstrated effectiveness in metabolic disorders including obesity and type 2 diabetes.

----- Row 8 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: The adverse effects commonly include gastrointestinal effects, but these medications may also be associated with decreased muscle mass and potentially rhabdomyolysis.

----- Row 8 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: In the present case, the patient’s clinical course is more consistent with IMNM than rhabdomyolysis.

----- Row 8 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: CK levels increased 12-fold over 4 months following one dose of semaglutide, whereas we would have expected a normalisation of CK within weeks if the direct toxic effect of semaglutide was the culprit, as seen in other cases of drug-induced rhabdomyolysis.

----- Row 8 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: Notably, the previously reported rhabdomyolysis case involving tirzepatide followed the typical clinical course: the patient experienced a marked acute elevation of CK (CK=57 014 at presentation) and a rapid improvement pattern on drug withdrawal and hydration within days, without the need for immunosuppression.

----- Row 8 -----

raw_knowledge_statement: Following WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) Causality Categories and Criteria, there is probable causality between semaglutide use and the IMNM in our case, because there is a reasonable time relationship, it is unlikely that IMNM is due to other diseases or drugs, and there was a clinically reasonable response on withdrawal.

----- Row 8 -----
