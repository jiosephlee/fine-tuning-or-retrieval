raw_knowledge_statement: An elderly man underwent cardiac surgery with heparin anticoagulation; postoperatively, the platelet counts steadily fell, reaching 50×109/L on postoperative day 5 (POD5), prompting testing for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) antibodies; brain imaging showed parietal stroke.

----- Row 1 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Screening immunoassay for HIT antibodies yielded a strong-positive result; the platelet activation test (serotonin-release assay) gave an atypical profile (strong serotonin release at 0, 0.1 and 0.3 U/mL heparin with only partial inhibition by a platelet Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody).

----- Row 1 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Persisting hypercoagulability prompted a switch to fondaparinux, with gradual platelet count recovery (155×109/L, POD62); neurological abnormalities resolved.

----- Row 1 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Laboratory evaluation confirmed HIT antibodies with heparin-independent platelet-activating properties, indicating autoimmune HIT (aHIT).

----- Row 1 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Besides profound thrombocytopenia (platelet count nadir, 13×10⁹/L), novel aspects of this case of aHIT include early postoperative onset (before POD5) resulting from preoperative heparin exposure and a unique laboratory aHIT antibody profile with partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Unfractionated heparin (UFH) has been the near-exclusive intraoperative anticoagulant during the 70-year history of heart surgery using cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), due to its many advantages, including inhibition of artificial surface-associated haemostasis, non-renal/non-hepatic clearance, intraoperative anticoagulant monitoring using the activated clotting time and low cost.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: However, an important adverse effect of UFH is an immune-mediated adverse reaction, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT).

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: HIT is relatively common post-cardiac surgery, with an estimated frequency of 0.5%–1.0% depending on the duration and type of postoperative heparin thromboprophylaxis (UFH vs low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH)), and is noteworthy for its strong association with thrombosis, with a relative risk for thrombosis of ~15.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: HIT has conventionally been viewed as a disorder featuring strong activation of platelets, through platelet Fc receptor clustering, resulting from the formation of immune complexes with heparin-dependent antibodies of immunoglobulin G (IgG) class that recognise the cationic protein, platelet factor 4 (PF4), when bound to polyanionic heparin.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: However, in recent years, it has been increasingly recognised that some patients have atypical and oftentimes severe clinical courses due to HIT antibodies with heparin-independent platelet-activating properties; this subgroup of atypical HIT has been named ‘autoimmune HIT’ (aHIT) (for review).

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: In this report, we describe a case of aHIT featuring unusually severe and persisting thrombocytopenia, complicated by thrombotic stroke, following cardiac surgery.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: This case provides novel insights into the spectrum of aHIT by documenting a uniquely severe serological phenotype, with partial resistance to Fc receptor blockade.

----- Row 2 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The case also highlights the diagnostic challenges of early postoperative thrombocytopenia, as the patient’s unusually early onset of aHIT was explained by preoperative exposure to UFH.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: A man in his 70s with a 2-year history of exertional dyspnoea and longstanding atrial fibrillation (on oral apixaban 5 mg, two times per day) was found to have a previously undetected systolic murmur.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: He underwent diagnostic heart catheterisation but had not held his apixaban pre-procedure; accordingly, the cardiologist performed the heart catheterisation via a transradial artery approach, with a lower-than-usual intraprocedural anticoagulant dose (only 2000 U of UFH).

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Angiographic findings included 60% stenosis in the obtuse marginal coronary artery, moderate aortic stenosis and a dilated ascending aorta; he was scheduled for cardiac surgery 1 week later.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Heart surgery was performed using CPB, with anticoagulation achieved using 45 000 U of UFH.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The surgery included aortic valve replacement with a #27 bioprosthesis; replacement of the ascending aorta with a #30 Gelweave graft; single coronary artery bypass with a saphenous vein graft to the obtuse marginal coronary artery and left atrial appendage excision.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Surgery proceeded uneventfully, without major blood losses or need for blood transfusion, with the patient easily coming ‘off pump’ with excellent haemodynamics.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The immediate preoperative platelet count was 210×109/L (reference range (RR) 150–400); on postoperative day 1 (POD1), the platelet count was 155×109/L, which represented a relatively minor early platelet count decline in the context of cardiac surgery.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Usual postoperative thromboprophylaxis with LMWH was commenced on POD1, with 5000 U dalteparin administered by subcutaneous injection, and with a higher dose (7500 U) administered on POD2, POD3 and POD4.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Unexpectedly, from POD1 to POD5, the platelet count progressively declined to 50×109/L (figure 1).

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Dalteparin was not given on POD5; instead, prophylactic-dose fondaparinux (2.5 mg) was begun (first dose, POD5 at 18:00).

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Although no focal neurological findings were evident, worsening of postoperative delirium, together with a further decline in platelet count on POD6 to 26×109/L (repeat, 24×109/L), prompted CT brain imaging.

----- Row 3 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: This revealed an acute cerebral infarct, with the area of hypodensity measuring 3.2 cm×1.8 cm within the left parietal lobe, with extension through to the cerebral cortex.

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The complete blood count on POD5 that exhibited the platelet count of 50×109/L had a corresponding haemoglobin of 97 g/L (RR 135–180 g/L), which was similar to the haemoglobin values obtained during the preceding 3 days (consistent with expected early surgical blood losses without ongoing bleeding), and a corresponding white blood cell count of 7.8×109/L (RR 4.0–11.0×109/L), with normal differential.

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Haptoglobin was elevated at 3.19 g/L (RR 0.32–1.97 g/L), consistent with the absence of overt haemolysis.

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Coagulation studies showed normal prothrombin time, corresponding to a normal international normalised ratio of 1.1 (RR 0.9–1.1), normal partial thromboplastin time (PTT) of 34 s (RR 25–37 s) and elevated fibrinogen of 6.59 g/L (RR 2.00–3.93 g/L), consistent with an early postoperative state, as fibrinogen is an acute phase reactant that typically reaches greatly elevated levels during the early postoperative period.

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Other blood work included normal chemistry (urea, creatinine, alanine transaminase and bilirubin).

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Review of the POD7 peripheral blood film confirmed the presence of thrombocytopenia (absence of platelet clumps) without red cell fragments (schistocytes) or ‘toxic’ white blood cell abnormalities.

----- Row 4 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: A screening immunoassay for HIT—the PF4/polyanion ELISA—was strongly positive at 2.2 optical density (OD) units (RR <0.4 OD units), but the confirmatory platelet activation test—the serotonin-release assay (SRA)—yielded an ‘indeterminate’ result (discussed subsequently).

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Common explanations for severe post-cardiac surgery thrombocytopenia, such as cardiogenic shock/critical illness and infection, seemed unlikely given the clinical and laboratory features (normal haemodynamics; blood and urine cultures returned negative).

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Also unlikely were rare explanations for post-cardiac surgery thrombocytopenia, such as post-transfusion purpura (no blood transfusions given), post-surgery thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (normal haptoglobin, initial absence of schistocytes) and drug-induced immune thrombocytopenia, as the onset of platelet count fall occurred too early for any newly administered postoperative drugs, those given only on the day of surgery (vancomycin, cefazolin and tranexamic acid) and those given during the postoperative period (furosemide, metoprolol, pantoprazole and midodrine).

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Although the platelet count fall also occurred too soon to be explained by the UFH given intraoperatively during CPB, subsequent inquiry identified intraprocedural UFH administered during preoperative heart catheterisation.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Together with thrombotic stroke and strong-positive PF4/polyanion ELISA, HIT was judged the most probable diagnosis, likely triggered by UFH administered at preoperative heart catheterisation.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Further details regarding the ‘indeterminate’ SRA were requested from the reporting laboratory, the McMaster Platelet Immunology Laboratory.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Consistent with the presence of platelet-activating HIT antibodies, the patient’s POD5 serum caused strong serotonin release both at 0.1 and 0.3 U/mL UFH (97% and 99%, respectively; RR<20% release), as well as a lack of serotonin release (0%) at supratherapeutic (100 U/mL) UFH; the absence of platelet activation at very high heparin concentrations is a characteristic feature of HIT and reflects the inability of PF4-containing immune complexes to be formed at such high UFH concentrations.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: First, the patient’s serum caused strong serotonin release in the absence of UFH (96% serotonin release at 0 U/mL heparin; RR<20%); this is a characteristic feature of aHIT but not of classic heparin-dependent HIT.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Second, the addition of the Fc receptor-blocking monoclonal antibody (IV.3) failed to inhibit completely the serotonin release observed in the presence of patient serum at 0.1 U/mL UFH: 76% serotonin release was observed in the presence of IV.3, rather than inhibition to less than 20% serotonin release, as would usually be seen with HIT antibodies; hence, the initial designation of an ‘indeterminate’ SRA result.

----- Row 5 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: However, the explanation was that the patient’s diagnosis indeed was aHIT, but that the patient’s HIT antibodies were unusually reactive, such that the degree of inhibition by IV.3 usually seen was only partially exhibited.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The patient’s prophylactic-dose fondaparinux was discontinued, and treatment with therapeutic-dose argatroban, a direct thrombin inhibitor, was commenced at 0.75 mcg/kg/min, with a PTT target range of 55–85 s.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Although fondaparinux given in therapeutic dosing is an effective ‘off-label’ anticoagulant for HIT, the treating haematologist preferred argatroban, an anticoagulant with a shorter half-life, in case bleeding occurred, given the recent stroke and unusually severe thrombocytopenia.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: In addition, high-dose intravenous Ig was given, initially 100 g on two consecutive days, followed by a third 100 g dose, given the lack of an initial platelet count increase following the initial 2 days of intravenous Ig administration.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Serial fibrinogen and d-dimer testing was also performed to obtain indirect evidence of anticoagulant efficacy, as rising fibrinogen values and falling d-dimer values would be consistent with anticoagulant control of HIT-associated hypercoagulability.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The initial treatment course included a progressive decrease in fibrinogen levels from POD6 to POD17 (nadir value at 2.49 g/L), peak d-dimer levels reached approximately POD12 and detection of some red cell fragments (schistocytes) by peripheral blood film from POD9 to POD16; these three findings were consistent with severe, progressive HIT-associated consumptive coagulopathy.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Moreover, the patient exhibited only a modest increase in the platelet count following administration of high-dose intravenous Ig, from the pre-intravenous Ig value of 13×109/L (POD7) to an interim peak value of 54×109/L (POD14), which is a much lower platelet count rise than is usually seen after administration of high-dose intravenous Ig for a patient with aHIT (for review).

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The patient was switched on POD14 from argatroban to therapeutic-dose fondaparinux, 10 mg once daily by subcutaneous injection.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Considerations for this change in anticoagulant included ease of fondaparinux administration, as it is administered once daily by subcutaneous dosing versus PTT-adjusted intravenous administration required for argatroban, as well as concern regarding possible insufficient control by argatroban of HIT-associated hypercoagulability, as indicated by the aforementioned falling fibrinogen and rising d-dimer levels and appearance of schistocytes, observed during treatment with argatroban.

----- Row 6 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Given that fondaparinux is an indirect antithrombin (AT)-dependent factor Xa inhibitor, supplemental AT concentrate (1000 U) was given prior to starting fondaparinux; the pre-infusion AT level was mildly reduced at 0.70 U/mL (RR 0.80–1.40 U/mL).

----- Row 7 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: The platelet count gradually recovered to normal (155×109/L at POD62; 202×109/L at POD87); the patient also made a complete neurological recovery.

----- Row 7 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Fondaparinux was given until POD65, at which time apixaban was resumed at 5 mg, two times per day orally, for long-term anticoagulation.

----- Row 7 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Serial SRA results remained strongly positive for several months, with several subsequent SRA profiles showing a more characteristic SRA profile for aHIT (figure 2); the last blood sample tested, obtained at POD124 following platelet count recovery, showed a result consistent with predominantly heparin-dependent HIT antibodies, with only 15% serotonin release in the absence of UFH but 100% release in the presence of UFH at 0.3 U/mL.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Our patient had numerous atypical clinical, laboratory and serological features, all ultimately attributable to a diagnosis of severe aHIT.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: First, his onset of HIT-related thrombocytopenia began before the usual temporal window of HIT, which is between POD5 and POD10 (inclusive) for patients who had cardiac surgery.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Only about 10% of patients who had cardiac surgery and developed HIT evince a HIT-related platelet count fall before POD5, a phenomenon linked to recent preoperative heparin exposure, as was also documented in our patient.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Second, our patient’s platelet count fell to an unusually low nadir value of 13×109/L, together with falling fibrinogen values, rising d-dimer levels and schistocytosis during argatroban anticoagulation.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Third, our patient did not exhibit a brisk correction of thrombocytopenia, as usually occurs when high-dose intravenous Ig is given to patients with aHIT; this points to the patient having an unusually severe form of this entity.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Indeed, serological evidence of an unusually severe subtype of aHIT was the only partial inhibition of platelet activation in vitro in the presence of monoclonal antibody IV.3; to our knowledge, this novel serological profile has not been previously reported in association with aHIT, awareness of which could help avoid a missed diagnosis of aHIT, with potential adverse clinical consequences.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Nevertheless, the diagnosis of aHIT was ultimately confirmed in our patient, based on at least three considerations.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: First, testing of subsequently acquired sera during the prolonged period of platelet count recovery did show some SRA reactivity profiles more characteristic of aHIT.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Second, the last sample tested showed a heparin-dependent SRA reaction profile characteristic of typical HIT, reflecting waning of aHIT antibodies.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: And third, there was no other plausible diagnosis to explain the patient’s unusual clinical course.

----- Row 8 -----
raw_knowledge_statement: Fortunately, the patient made an excellent clinical recovery, with no residual neurological abnormalities.
