section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) has become an established treatment option for patients with severe aortic stenosis across a broad range of surgical risk profiles.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: As the number of patients undergoing TAVI continues to grow and long-term survival improves, structural valve deterioration and the need for repeat interventions are increasingly encountered in clinical practice.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: In this setting, transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV) procedures have emerged as a viable strategy for managing failed transcatheter prostheses.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: However, such interventions are associated with specific anatomical and technical challenges, including the risk of coronary artery obstruction and a reduced effective orifice area resulting in severe prosthesis-patient mismatch.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: These challenges are further amplified in sequential reinterventions, such as transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve-in-transcatheter aortic valve (TAV-in-TAV-in-TAV), where multiple overlapping valve frames must be carefully considered.

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: Evidence regarding serial transcatheter valve reinterventions remains limited, particularly in patients with a history of prosthetic valve endocarditis (PVE).

----- Row 2 -----
section: Background
raw_knowledge_statement: In such complex scenarios, detailed preprocedural imaging, individualised risk assessment and multidisciplinary decision-making are essential to determine feasibility and optimise procedural outcomes.

----- Row 6 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: While TAVI has become an established treatment for aortic stenosis, its use in patients with prior endocarditis remains controversial.

----- Row 6 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: According to the 2025 European society of cardiology (ESC) guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease and infective endocarditis, surgical valve replacement remains the recommended strategy in PVE, particularly in cases complicated by heart failure or uncontrolled infection.

----- Row 6 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: However, the guidelines emphasise that management should be individualised, taking into account infection control, surgical risk and anatomical feasibility.

----- Row 6 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: The choice of TAVI as the initial treatment strategy in 2014 was driven by the patient’s high surgical risk and multiple comorbidities, making conventional surgical valve replacement unfavourable at that time.

----- Row 6 -----
section: Discussion
raw_knowledge_statement: As the patient subsequently developed structural valve deterioration, a transcatheter redo strategy was pursued in 2022 to restore valve function while avoiding the risks of open surgery.
