# Motivation: why we need to align language models with human preferences

Language models have become central to many applications because they can produce fluent text that matches human preferences. However, the current state-of-the-art language models are trained to maximize the likelihood of text given a corpus, which often leads to outputs that are not aligned with human preferences. For example, a model trained to maximize the likelihood will produce text that is fluent but often irrelevant or offensive. This mismatch can cause problems downstream: for example, a model that is optimized to maximize the likelihood of a particular dataset may produce biased or toxic content when used in contexts where the dataset is not representative.

There are several reasons why alignment is important. First, alignment helps ensure that the model's behavior is consistent with the goals of the system it supports. Second, alignment can improve the quality of the generated text by producing more relevant or useful output. Third, alignment can reduce the risk of harmful consequences of the model's behavior. For example, if a model is used to generate content for a social media platform, alignment can help prevent the platform from being flooded with toxic or irrelevant content.

There are several approaches to alignment. One common approach is to use human-in-the-loop evaluation: humans are asked to judge whether a piece of text produced by the model is relevant, appropriate, or harmful. This approach is effective but expensive and time-consuming. Another approach is to use reward models to encode human preferences and then train the language model to maximize the expected reward. This approach is attractive because it avoids the need for human annotation and can be used to align a large model.
