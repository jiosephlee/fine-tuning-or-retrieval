It is particularly useful when you have a large number of features and want to keep the model from overfitting to a small subset of them. Dynamic weighting can be implemented in several ways, but the core idea is to update the importance of features based on their contribution to the loss function. For example, if we have a binary classification problem where the loss is defined as $L = \mathbb{1}(y_i = 1) - \mathbb{1}(\hat{y}_i = 1)$, then the importance of feature $i$ can be updated as follows:
\[
\Delta_i = \frac{\text{loss}}{\sum_{j=1}^J \Delta_j},
\]
where $\Delta_j$ is the importance of feature $j$. This means that the more often the feature contributes to the loss, the higher its importance becomes. Dynamic weighting can also be used to weight the importance of different classes or different labels. In the case of multi-label classification, the loss is defined as
\[
L = \sum_{i=1}^K \sum_{j=1,\, j\neq i}^K \Delta_{ij},
\]
where $\{i,j\}$ are the labels. Here, the importance of label $i$ is updated as
\[
\Delta_i^* = \frac{\text{(loss)}}{\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^K \Delta_{ij}^*},
\]
where $\{\Delta_{ij}^*\}$ is the importance of label $j$.

Why is dynamic weighting helpful? When you have a large number (e.g., thousands or millions) of features, it is easy for the model to become overfitted to a small subset of features. Dynamic weighting helps by keeping the model focused on the most important features. If you only use a small subset of features to train the model, the model will learn to perform well on those features, but it will not generalize well to new data that do not appear in the training set. Dynamic weighting prevents this by updating the importance of features based not only on their contribution to the training loss but also on how frequently they appear in the training data. This ensures that the model learns to generalize to a broader range of features.

Another way to think about dynamic weighting is as a form of regularization. Regularization techniques like dropout and L2 regularization are designed to prevent overfitting by penalizing large weights. Dynamic weighting is similar in that it penalizes large weights by making them less important. However, dynamic weighting is not a direct regularization technique; instead, it modifies the learning dynamics so that the model learns to focus on the most important features.
