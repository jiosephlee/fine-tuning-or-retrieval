\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

1. Introduction
Large unsupervised language models learn impressive capabilities, but their behavior is largely unpredictable, making them difficult to work with and trust. One approach to gaining steerability is reinforcement learning from human feedback, known as reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF; Littman et al., 2018; Wu et al., 2019; Johnson et al., 2020; Weston et al., 2022). In RLHF, humans provide feedback on the quality of candidate model generations, and the model with the highest average reward is fine-tuned to match human preferences. However, RLHF is a reinforcement learning problem with several challenges. First, the reward function estimated from the human feedback is typically unknown, requiring the use of off-policy learning algorithms such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2021). Second, the optimal policy learned from the reward function is generally not easy to represent in practice, requiring the use of approximators such as neural networks. Finally, the RLHF problem is a reinforcement learning with a moving target: the reward function changes as the model improves, requiring the model to be re-trained with the updated reward function after each fine-tuning pass.

In this paper, we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a single-pass reinforcement learning algorithm that directly optimizes the desired policy from the RLHF problem. Our key insight is to reparameterize the reward function in terms of a new variable, the \textit{direct preference score} (DPS), which represents the reward that would have been obtained by following the optimal policy from the initial model. We then express the RLHF problem as a constrained optimization problem that maximizes the DPS subject to certain constraints, which correspond to the properties of a reward function consistent with human preferences. Finally, we solve this constrained optimization problem using DPO, an algorithm for optimizing parametric reward functions that does not require off-policy learning and does not require approximating the reward function with a neural network. Our main result is a generalization of the Kushner-Tsirelson theorem (Kushner and Tsirelson, 1982; Tsirelson,1984) to the setting of constrained optimization, showing that the unconstrained optimization problem has a unique solution that satisfies certain properties. We can therefore solve the constrained problem exactly, obtaining the following algorithm for Direct Preference Optimization:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:DPO}
\max_{p} \mathbb{1}_{\text{desired}}(p^{\text{FT}})\text{DPO}(\pi_{\text{initial}}, \mu),
\end{equation

where $p$ is the policy produced by the initial language model $\pi_{\text{init}}$ using its own parameters $\mu$, and $\text{DPO}$(\pi, \boldsymbol{\beta}) is a policy obtained from a parametric reward model with parameters $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ by replacing the reward with $\beta r(\cdot|\mathbf{\beta}),$ where $r$ is the reward function and $\mathbf{\beta} \sim \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{\beta}).$ We can further modify the reward model to account for human preferences, using a pre-trained classifier to transform the original reward into a DPS, and then optimizing with respect to $\beta$ using a standard maximum likelihood objective. We refer to this version of the algorithm as \textit{Fine-Tuned DPO} (FT-DPO).

We theoretically analyze the performance of DPO under various assumptions about the reward function, and show that DPO produces a policy with desirable properties, such as low variance and consistency with the original model on offline evaluation. We also present extensive experimental results showing that DPO is generally more stable and converges faster than RLHF algorithms including PPO in fine-tuning large unsupervised LM models to match human preferences in sentiment, modality, and task adherence. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce Direct Preference Optimization ( DPO), a single-pass, reinforcement learning algorithm for fine-tuning language models with unsupervised initialization to match human preferences.
\item We show how to incorporate knowledge of the reward model into DPO, yielding Fine-Tuned DPO (FT-DPO), an algorithm that optimizes the same objective as RLHF but is analytically tractable and as effective as RLHF algorithms.
\end{itemize}
