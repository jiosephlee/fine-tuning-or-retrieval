\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

1. Introduction
Large unsupervised language models learn impressive capabilities, but their behavior is largely unpredictable, making them difficult to work with and trust. One approach to gaining steerability is reinforcement learning from human feedback, known as reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF; Li et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2020; Wu et al.,2021). In RLHF, humans rate pairs of model-generated and ground-truth answers on a scale from 0 to 100, and the model with the highest average rating is fine-tuned to align with the human preferences. However, RLHF is a challenging and often unstable procedure. First, the reward function must be estimated from the human ratings, which introduces a source of error. Second, the optimal policy learned from the estimated reward may not be safe or efficient, so additional safety and efficiency guarantees are required. Third, the RLHF algorithm typically involves two passes over the dataset: first, the RLHF algorithm is used to learn a reward model from the human ratings, and then the learned reward model is used to fine-tune the language model with reinforcement learning to optimize the estimated reward without drifting too much from the original model. This second pass is also known as the ``reward fine-tuning'' step.

In this paper, we introduce a new RLHF algorithm we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Unlike existing algorithms, DPO does not require an additional reward estimation step. Instead, DPO directly optimizes the policy learned from the estimated rewards to optimize a specified preference function. We show that any preference function can be represented as a convex combination of a set of base preference functions, each of which corresponds to a different reward model. We then show how to optimize a policy using a weighted sum of rewards, subject to certain constraints, in a way that is equivalent to optimizing a policy parameterized by a parameter vector $\theta$ to maximize the sum of the weights of the base preferences, subject to certain constraints. This optimization can be solved exactly using a variant of the method of images (MII; Schmidhuber, 2015), which allows us to express the policy as a function of a single parameter vector $\phi$, the so-called preference parameter. DPO alternates between optimizing the policy parameter $\phi$ to maximize the base preferences, and optimizing the policy parameter $\theta$ to maximize a reward function that is a convex combination of the base preferences. We call this general framework Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). We theoretically analyze DPO and show it has certain desirable properties, such as robustness to initialization and simplicity of the update rule. We also empirically compare DPO to several existing RLHF algorithms on multiple datasets and tasks, finding that DPO produces models with higher preferences and better response quality in summarization, and comparable or better performance on sentiment tuning and single-turn dialogue, while being substantially simpler to train and more robust to hyperparameters.

2 Related Work
Reinforcement learning with unsupervised rewards: Recent work has shown that it is possible to learn policies from unsupervised rewards obtained through reinforcement learning from human feedback or self-play (Sutton and Barto, 2018;Hassan and Carbin, 2019;Korbak et al., 2018;Ranzato et al., 2015;Sutskever et al., 2014). These algorithms are often referred to as RL algorithms with unsupervised pre-training (Ranzato et al. 2015;Hassan and Carroll, 2018;Korbak etal., 2018;Sutskever, Williams, and Cho, 2014;Carbin, 2018). However, these works do not use human feedback to directly optimize the policy, but rather use the unsupervised reward to initialize a policy and then optimize it with reinforcement learning. In this paper we fall into this category, although we do not use an unsupervised reward, but rather optimize a language model with reinforcement learning from human preferences.
Reinforcement learning from preferences: Once a policy has been initialized with unsupervised learning, it can be further improved with reinforcement learning from human preference data. In this paper we also use a reinforcement learning algorithm for preference optimization, but we use a different objective function from previous works.
