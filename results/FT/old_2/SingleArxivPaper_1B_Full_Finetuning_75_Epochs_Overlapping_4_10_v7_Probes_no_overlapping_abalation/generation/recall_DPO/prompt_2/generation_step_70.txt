The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO uses a reward function that is a natural generalization of traditional metrics such as BLEU and METEOR, and does not require any explicit human annotations of preference pairs. Instead, DPO optimizes a loss function that naturally incorporates the reward function and leverages the power of modern optimization algorithms, such as Newton's method and gradient descent, to find high-quality model parameters in polynomial time. Experiments on four preference datasets show that DPO can align language models with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods, using much simpler reward functions and without any fine-tuning of the language model on the preference data.

Related Work
In this section, we review existing work on aligning language models with preferences, focusing on metrics that are closely related to human preferences, such as BLEU and ROUGE, as well as more recent methods that use reward functions based on human preferences, such as PPL and PPL-LL.

Metrics for Aligning Language Models
Language models are typically evaluated using metrics that measure their ability to generate text that is similar to reference text in some way. Two of the most commonly used metrics are BLEU and ROUGE. BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002) measures the similarity of two texts using a statistical model based on a large corpus of aligned human-written paraphrased pairs. ROUGE (Lin and Schwenk, 2004) is a family of algorithms that optimize a variant of the BLEU metric, taking into account multiple versions of the reference and candidate texts. While these metrics provide a useful way to evaluate language models' ability to generate text in the target style, they do not directly measure human preferences.
Another class of metrics for aligning language models use human preferences to provide feedback to the language model during training. Early methods such as PPL (Chen et al., 2010) and PPL-LL (Liu et al., 2015) use human evaluations of pairs of candidate and reference texts to provide feedback to the model, adjusting its parameters to maximize a loss function that measures the difference between the human preferences and the model's preferences for the given pair of texts. Other methods, such as PPL-LLX (Xu et al., 2018), use a reinforcement learning algorithm to directly optimize a reward function that measures the discrepancy between the human preferences and those of the language model. These methods provide feedback to the language models, but do not use a reward function that is directly related to human preferences.

Reward Functions for Aligning Language Models with Preferences
While existing methods for aligning LMs with preferences use reward functions that are closely related to metrics for human preferences, there has been little work on developing reward functions specifically for this purpose. One approach is to use metrics for human preferences as the reward function, as in PPL and PPL-LX. However, these metrics are not always straightforward to use as a reward function, and existing methods for aligning models with preferences generally use custom reward functions. Another approach is to use a reward function that measures some aspect of the alignment between the language model and the preferences, but then use a method for optimizing rewards rather than metrics. This allows the language model to be trained with a single loss function, while the reward function is optimized separately. A number of reward functions have been proposed for this purpose, including PPL-LL, PPL-LLX, and PPL-LLT (Liu et al. 2018b). These reward functions are based on the PPL metric, but use a different loss function for the model training and a different optimization algorithm for the reward optimization. In this paper, we use the PPL-LL reward function as a starting point for our framework for aligning language models from preferences, but modify it in several ways to improve its suitability for use with modern optimization algorithms.

Direct Preference Optimization
In this paper, we introduce a new framework for training language models using preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Our main contribution is a reward function for DPO, which we show is equivalent to PPL-LL. We also present a complete framework for using DPO, which includes a training algorithm, a theoretical analysis, and a collection of experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach.
