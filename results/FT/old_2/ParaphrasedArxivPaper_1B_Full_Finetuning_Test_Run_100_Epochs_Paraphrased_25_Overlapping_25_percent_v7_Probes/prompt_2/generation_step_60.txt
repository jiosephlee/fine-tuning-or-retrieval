The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known RLHF algorithm to the case where the reward function is not explicitly defined, but rather is inferred from preference data. This allows the use of language models that are much larger and more expressive than those previously possible. We refer to this approach as DPO, for Direct Preference Optimization. DPO uses a single pass over the preference data, and does not require any explicit training of the language model. Experimental results show that DPO can align language models with human preferences as well as or better than previous approaches, using much smaller preference datasets. In particular, DPO aligns with human preferences on the Reddit and MovieLens datasets using preference datasets of only 100k preference pairs, whereas the best previous approach requires at least 1.5B preference pairs.

Related Work
The RLHF algorithm was introduced in 2018 by Littman et al. (2018) as a way to train language models to align with human preferences. The RLHF algorithm uses reinforcement learning to optimize a policy that maps from the language model to a reward function, which is then used to train a neural network model that approximates the underlying reward function. The RLHF algorithm has been applied to a variety of preference datasets, including movie reviews (Kupcsik et al., 2019), news articles (Liu et al., 2020), and Reddit comments (Liu et al. 2020;Yue et al., 2022). In these studies, the RLHF algorithm is typically applied to a reward model that is defined by a set of preference pairs, where the reward for each preference pair is calculated as the difference between the reward for the preferred and the non-preferred output. This approach is known as the "reward model" paradigm, and is the basis for the DPO algorithm described in Section 3.
A related approach to RLHF is the "reward learning from human preferences" (RLHFP) framework, which was introduced by Sutskever et al. (2019). In contrast to the RLHF approach, which trains a single policy to optimize a reward function, the RLHFP framework trains multiple policies, each optimized to maximize a different reward function. The policies are trained using a variant of the RLHF algorithm, which is referred to as the "reward learning from preference" (RLFP) algorithm. The RLHFP framework has been applied to a wide range of preference datasets, including music (Tian et al., 2023), movie reviews (Chen et al., 2021), and news articles (Santos et al., 2024). In contrast to the DPO algorithm, the RLHFP approach does not require the explicit definition of a reward function, but instead relies on a set of preference pairs that are used to train a neural reward model. While the RLHFP approach is more flexible than the RLHF approach, it is also more computationally intensive, since it requires training multiple policies and optimizing for multiple reward functions.

Direct Preference Optimization
In this section, we present the DPO algorithm, a straightforward generalization of RLHF that allows for the use of language models with large and expressive text generation capabilities. We begin by introducing the RLHF algorithm, followed by a discussion of how it can be adapted for the case where the reward model is not explicitly defined. This leads to the DPO algorithm.

The RLHF Algorithm
The RLHF algorithm is a two-stage training process. In the offline stage, the language model is trained to maximize the reward function R(θ) = 1 N n=1 r n (θ) using a reward function r n (θ) that is specified offline. In the online stage, the language model's policy π θ is trained to maximize the expected reward, using a reward function r(θ, x) that is specified online. The reward function r(θ,x) is typically chosen to be the difference between the reward obtained by the policy π θ (y) when given an input x and the reward obtained by a reference policy π ref (y).
