The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known reinforcement learning approach to reward maximization, known as RLHF (reward learning from examples), to the setting of preference learning. This generalization is achieved by introducing a new objective, called the direct preference optimization (DPO) objective, which is a convex combination of the RLHF objective and a reward function that is learned from human preferences. The DPO objective is shown to be equivalent to the RLHF objective under certain assumptions, and it is also shown to be more robust to model misspecification than the RLHF objective. The DPO objective can be efficiently solved using a gradient-based method, and the resulting policy is shown to generalize well to unseen data. Experiments on two large-scale preference learning datasets demonstrate that the DPO objective can achieve comparable or better performance than the RLHF objective, while being easier to train and more stable to model misspecification.

Related Work
In this section, we review the related work on both preference learning and reward learning from human feedback. Preference learning aims to learn a model that maps inputs to human preferences. In contrast, reward learning aims to learn a policy that maximizes a reward function. In this work, we focus on the reward learning from human feedback setting, where the goal is to learn a policy that matches human preferences. There are two main approaches to learning from human feedback: reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised learning. In the RL approach, the reward function is learned from human feedback, and the policy is optimized using reinforcement learning techniques. In contrast, in the supervised learning approach, the reward function and the policy are both learned from human feedback. The supervised learning approach is more straightforward, but it is limited to scenarios where the reward function is known in advance. In this work, our focus is on the supervised learning approach, where the reward function is learned using human feedback.
