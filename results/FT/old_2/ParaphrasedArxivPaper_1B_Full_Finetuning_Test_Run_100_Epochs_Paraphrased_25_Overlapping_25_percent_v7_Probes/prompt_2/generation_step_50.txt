The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known RLHF algorithm to the case where the reward function is not explicitly defined, but rather is inferred from preference data. This generalization is achieved by introducing a new optimization objective that is convex and can be solved using standard methods. The proposed approach is shown to outperform previous approaches, including the RLHF algorithm, in a range of experiments.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the problem of aligning language models with preferences, and outlines the RLHF algorithm. In Section 3, we present the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) method, which generalizes the RLHF algorithm to the setting where the reward function is unknown. In Section 4, we present experimental results that demonstrate the effectiveness of DPO. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper.

Aligning Language Models with Preferences
In this section, we introduce the problem of aligning a language model with human preferences, and outline the RLHF algorithm. We begin by defining the preference model used in the experiments. Then, we describe the RLHF algorithm, which is used to train the language model to match the preferences.

The Preference Model
We consider a scenario where a human annotates a set of preference pairs between two outputs of a language model, denoted as P = {(x i , y i ) : i = 1, . . . , N }. Each pair consists of an input x i and the preferred output y i . The preference model is defined as the probability distribution over these preference pairs, P = P P (x, y)dy, where P is a probability distribution over the set of possible outputs. In the context of language models, P is typically assumed to be the probability distribution over the text generated by the model, P LM (y|x). In this work, we use the preference model P as a proxy for the true reward function R, which is unknown. The reward function is defined as the difference between the preferences and the model's output, i.e., R(x, y) = P (x, y)-P LM (x, y). In the context of language modeling, R is the likelihood of the preferred output y given the input x, i.e., R(y|x) = P LM (y|x) -1 P LM (x, y).
