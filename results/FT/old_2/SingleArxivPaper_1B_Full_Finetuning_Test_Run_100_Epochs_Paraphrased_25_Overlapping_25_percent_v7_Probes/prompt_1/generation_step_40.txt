The paper introduces a new optimization paradigm called "direct preference optimization" (DPO). DPO is a generalization of reinforcement learning (RL) that allows for learning reward functions from data without explicitly defining a reward function or policy. Instead, DPO uses a neural network to learn a mapping from input data to preferences, which can then be used to optimize a target objective, such as maximum likelihood or maximum reward. In this paper, the authors apply DPO to two problems in natural language processing (NLP): machine translation and summarization. For machine translation, they use a neural machine translation system to generate translations of English sentences into Spanish. They then collect pairs of Spanish sentences and English translations, along with human preferences for the translation in terms of quality. With this dataset, they can train a neural network to map input sentences to preferences, which they call a language model (LM). After training, they can use the network to produce new, quality-assured Spanish translations of English sentences. For summarization, they use a neural summarization system to generate short, potentially complete, summaries of articles. They collect pairs of articles and summaries, together with human preferences for the summarization quality. With this dataset they can train a neural networks to map input articles to preferences, which they also call a language model. They call the network a summarization model (SM). After training, they use the networks to generate new, quality-assured summaries of articles.
The main idea behind DPO is to use a neural network to map inputs to outputs, rather than explicitly defining a reward function for a reinforcement learning algorithm. This allows the network to learn from data that is not labeled with rewards, making it suitable for problems such as machine translation and summarization where the reward function is not known. However, training a neural network to map from inputs to outputs is a non-trivial task, and the authors of the paper make several key contributions to the field. First, they propose a loss function for training DPO networks that avoids overfitting and leads to networks with desirable properties, such as stability under perturbation and ability to generalize to unseen data. Second, they present a gradient-based optimization algorithm, the DPO algorithm, for training DPO networks from scratch. The DPO algorithm involves two main steps: a fitting step, during which the network is trained on the preference dataset, and a policy step, during which the learned network is used to optimize a target quantity of interest, such as the human preference log-likelihood or the maximum likelihood of a reward function. The authors evaluate their approach on several NLP tasks, including machine translation and summarization, and find that it can produce results comparable or better than existing methods based on RL algorithms.
