The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models to satisfy human preferences, without explicit human feedback or reinforcement learning. The framework is based on the observation that any preference model can be represented as a reward function, and that language models can be trained to match any desired reward using standard reinforcement learning algorithms. We call this approach Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO avoids the common pitfalls of prior work on RLHF, such as the need for large discount factors and the overfitting to idiosyncrasies of specific reward functions. DPO performs similarly or better than prior work on RLHF while being theoretically simpler and more scalable. We evaluate DPO on two tasks: taste-testing and online recommendation. On the taste-testing task, DPO enables training language models to match human preferences exactly, without boundable safety margins. On online recommendation, DPO performs as well as prior work based on RLHF, but is orders of magnitude more scalable. Our experiments show that DPO can translate into billions of preferences in the field of online recommendation, without compromising safety or performance.
