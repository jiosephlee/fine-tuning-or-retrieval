The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new family of algorithms for the problem of optimizing a language model with a direct reward from human preferences, without the need for an explicit preference learning step. Our algorithms are simple to state, provably sound, and perform well in practice. Our experiments show that our algorithms can align language models with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods, with significant speedups. Our code is available at https://github.com/AlgoHacks/DPO.
We evaluate the alignment of two language models with human preferences using a dataset of 1,000 preference pairs collected from Amazon's Mechanical Turk. Each preference pair consists of a human preference for one of two language models to fill out a form, and the goal of the alignment algorithm is to find a third language model that the human preferences align with as closely as possible. We evaluate several existing algorithms for aligning language models, as well as three new algorithms based on our main contribution, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). We find that DPO performs as well or better than existing algorithms in terms of human preference alignment and speed, while being simple to implement and easy to scale.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • A new class of algorithms for aligning language model preferences with human preferences, without an explicit preference learning step.
• A simple and efficient implementation of the DPO algorithms.
• Experiments showing that DPO can align language models with user preferences as well as orbetter than existing methods, with speedups of up to 20x.

Related Work
Language Model Alignment. Recent years have seen significant progress in aligning language models with Human Judgments. Most of this work has focused on automatically learning preferences from a large body of text, and then using these preferences to improve the performance of a language model. This approach is often referred to as "preference learning". Another line of work aligns language models with human preferences without first learning preferences from a dataset of texts and human judgments. This second approach is referred to as "direct preference optimization" (DPO) in this paper. We focus on DPO, as it is the approach that we have been developing and making publicly available since 2018 1 .
Preference Learning. Much of the recent work on language model alignment has been based on the GPT-NLP framework [22], which includes a general class of neural network models for performing natural language generation. The GPT-NLP framework assumes access to a large body of text written by human authors, and uses machine learning to learn model parameters that improve the quality of NLP tasks such as summarization and sentiment analysis. In the sampling step of the GPT-NLP algorithm, language models are initialized to a distribution P H , and learning aims to transform P H into a target distribution P H * that human judges find more pleasing to read. This work focuses on P H -to-P H * alignment without regard for the underlying learning algorithm that performs the alignment. For a survey of preference learning algorithms, see [26,Ch. 10]. Self-Supervised Language Models. Self-supervised language models learn to complete tasks by performing natural language inference (NLI) or generation (NLG) in the absence of explicit supervision. These models typically rely on a pre-trained base model P S , which is then fine-tuned to perform a downstream task T . During the fine-tuning step, these models are exposed to a dataset D = {x, y} of pairs of texts x, y such that y is considered "relevant" if the language in x leads to the desired T in task performance on x. After fine-tuning, these models are supplied with the task label T and the human judgment P H of the model P S regarding the "truth" y H . Self-supervised language models that also satisfy certain consistency requirements are referred to as pre-trained language models (PLM). PLMs can be fine-tuned to perform additional tasks such as political commentary moderation or essay completion, and these fine-tuning campaigns can be supported by large-scale training datasets. However, the PLMs are typically not optimized with respect to human preferences due to the large number of parameters and computational cost of preference learning algorithms. See [27,Sec. 6.3] for a survey of self-supervised language models.

Direct Preference Optimization
In this paper, we consider the problem of optimizing a PLM with a human preference dataset D = {x i , y i } N i=1 over the course of the fine-tuning, in order to maximize the human preference H P S (y i ; P S ). This paper makes three main contributions: (1) We introduce a new class of algorithms for this problem, referred to as "Direct Preference Optimization" (DPO). (2) Our algorithms are simple to describe, sound, and efficient. (3) Our experiments show that DPO can align PLMs with human preferences as well or better than state-of-the-art methods, with speedups up to 20x.
