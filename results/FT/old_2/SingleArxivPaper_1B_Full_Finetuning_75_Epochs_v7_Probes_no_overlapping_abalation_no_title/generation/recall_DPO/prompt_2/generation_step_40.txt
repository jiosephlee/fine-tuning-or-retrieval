The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new family of algorithms for optimizing the reward model used by language models, the preference model, and the model itself. The preference model is learned from human preferences collected using the PREFERENCE dataset. The reward model represents the language model with the highest expected reward for the human preferences. The model and preference model are optimized simultaneously using a method called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO alternates between optimizing the model and the preference model to converge to an optimal model-utility function. DPO is simple to implement and scale, and has state-of-the-art performance on several preference-based quality-of-service (QoS) benchmarks. The paper also presents several practical insights into the design of DPO algorithms. These include the use of a relaxation of the preference optimization problem, a change of variables, and a change of loss function, which allows DPO to handle negative preferences and QoS constraints. The paper is the first to present a theoretical analysis of DPO, which confirms the empirical success of the approach. The paper is the result of a joint effort by researchers from Google and Microsoft Research, and is the first work in the field to be open-sourced and published as a conference paper at EMNLP 2022.
We hope that the DPO framework will be useful to the community working on language models and QoS-aware applications of AI. We anticipate that DPO will inspire further research on the design and analysis of preference models, and on the application of language models to QoS-aware tasks.<|endoftext|>
