\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on a variety of downstream tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1]), question answering (e.g., BQAS [2]), and summarization (e.g., SQuAD [3]). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training process. In particular, the reward model used to guide the training of LMs is typically a black box, and the resulting policy is difficult to control.

In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. We call this new parameterization the \textit{direct preference optimization} (DPO) model. The DPO model is a simple extension of the reward model used in reinforcement learning from human preference feedback (RLHF) [4], which is a popular approach for fine-tuning LMs to align with the preferences of human annotators. In RLHF, the reward model is trained to reflect the preferences of human annotator, and the resulting policy can be used to fine-tune the unsatisfied model. However, the RLHF approach requires a large number of human annotations, which is expensive and time-consuming. Moreover, the reward model is typically a black box and the resulting policy is hard to control. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of this reward model that enables extraction and optimization of the corresponding policy in a closed form, allowing us solve the standard RLHF optimization problem with only a simple loss function. We call this new method \textit{Direct preference optimization} (D PO).

Our main contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item We introduce a new parameterization, called the \textit{DPO model}, for the reward model in RLHf. The DPO model can be used to solve the standard RLHf problem with only a simple policy loss, eliminating the need to sample from the large unsupervsed language model during fine-tunning or hyperparameter tuning.
\item We evaluate DPO on several downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis and summarization, and show that it is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, outperforming existing approaches in all cases.
\item We demonstrate that DPO can be used to fine tune LMs to align with preferences of human annotators, outperforming existing baselines in all cases.
\textbf{Related Work.} The RLHF approach has been widely used to fine-tune LMs for downstream tasks, including sentiment classification [5], summarization [6], and single-turn dialogue [7]. However, the RLHF method is a complex and often unreliable procedure, requiring a large number of human labels of the relative value of model generations, and fine-tuning the unsatisfied model using reinforcement learning from human feedback. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and stable method for fine-tuning LLMs to align to human preferences, without the need for human annotations or reinforcement learning from human feedback.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback}

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) [4] is a popular approach for training LMs to align with user preferences. In RHLF, the reward model is a black box that reflects the preferences of human annotaters, and the resulting policy, which is used to fine-tune an unsatisfied model, can be used to align with the preferences. However, the reward model is often a black box, and it is difficult to control the resulting policy. In this paper, instead of using a black box reward model, we propose a new reward model that can be used to solve RLHF with only a simple policy optimization loss. We call this new reward model the \textit{Direct Preferences Optimization} (DPO) reward model.
