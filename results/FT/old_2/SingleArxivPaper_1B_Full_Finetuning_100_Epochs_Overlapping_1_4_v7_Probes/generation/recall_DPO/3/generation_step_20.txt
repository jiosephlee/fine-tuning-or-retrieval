\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models such as BERT [1] and GPT-4 [2] have achieved impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., question answering, summarization, machine translation), text generation, and dialogue. However, these models are trained using only unlabeled data, making them difficult to control and fine-tune for specific tasks. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models to align with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Our method is based on reinforcement learning (RL), but differs from existing approaches in several key ways. First, we parameterize the reward model used in RL as a function of the language model (LM) parameters, rather than fitting a separate reward function to human preferences. This allows us to solve the standard DPO problem with a simple classification loss, rather than sampling from the LM and optimizing the reward function to maximize the expected reward under the sampled policy. Second, we use a simple, computationally efficient policy gradient algorithm to optimize the reward model, rather than using reinforcement learning from human feedback. Finally, we show that our method can be applied to a wide range of LM architectures, including those based on Transformer [3], and can be trained efficiently on large datasets of human preferences.

\section{Related Work}

Large unsupervised LM training has been a major focus of research in natural language processing (NLP) in recent years. Early work on unsupervised LM training focused on improving the performance of pre-trained models on downstream tasks, such as machine translation [4,5], question answering [6,7], and summarization [8,9]. More recently, large-scale unsupervised LM models have been trained using only unlabeled text, typically by fine-tuning pre-trained language models (LMs), such as BERT [10] and GPT-3 [2], on downstream tasks such as question answering [11], summarization [12], and machine translation [13]. These models have achieved impressive performance on these tasks, but are difficult to control and fine tune for specific downstream tasks, due to their large size and the absence of supervision. To address this challenge, several methods have been proposed for fine-tuning large LM models for specific downstream tasks, including methods based on reinforcement learning (e.g., PPO [14]) and imitation learning (e.g., GPT-4 [15]). These methods typically involve training a separate reward model to reflect the preferences of human annotators, and then fine-tune the LM using reinforcement learning to optimize the reward function. However, these methods are complex and often unstable, requiring significant hyperparameter tuning and fine-tuning of the reward model during training. In this paper, inspired by the success of reinforcement learning in other domains, we propose a new method for fine tuning large unsupervised LM models to align with human preference, called Direct Preference Optimization.
