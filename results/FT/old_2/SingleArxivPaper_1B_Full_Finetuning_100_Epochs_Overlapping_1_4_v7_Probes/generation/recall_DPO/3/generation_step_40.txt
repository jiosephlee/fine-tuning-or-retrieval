\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

However, while LLMs are becoming increasingly capable, their learning remains largely unsupervised, relying on large-scale unsupervised pre-training on vast quantities of text data before fine-tuning on specific tasks. This approach has two major drawbacks. First, since the pre-training and fine-tuning steps are performed in separate phases, LLMs must start from a relatively low base level of competency in order to acquire the skills they eventually learn. Second, because the training procedure is completely unsupervised, there is no objective other than performance on the tasks provided during fine-tuning, which can be a limitation when seeking to leverage the model's abilities in new and unanticipated contexts (e.g., from a sentiment or humor perspective). 
In this paper, we introduce a new approach for fine-tuning LMs with human feedback that addresses these issues. Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization, or DPO for short, is based on the observation that the high-level goal of the LLM training pipeline (i.e., acquiring skill in performing a given task while also generalizing to new domains) can be written as a reward function optimization problem. Just as reinforcement learning algorithms learn to behave in ways that maximize a given reward function, we can train LLMs to perform in ways that match human preferences. We therefore express the reward function as a parameterized model that we optimize using standard reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically a variant of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences) that we call REINFORCE with Memory, or REINFORCE-M for short. 
The main contribution of this paper is DPO, the algorithm that derives the reward function parameterization and uses it to construct an optimal policy to optimize.
