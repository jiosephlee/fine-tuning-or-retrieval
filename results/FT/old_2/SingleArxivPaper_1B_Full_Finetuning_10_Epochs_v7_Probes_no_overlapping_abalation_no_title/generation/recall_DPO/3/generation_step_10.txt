\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, the completely unsupervised training regime of these models makes it difficult to control their behavior, especially when fine-tuning them to align with human preferences. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{li2022reinforcement, li2023direct, li2024direct}, where a reward model is fit to reflect human preferences, and then the unsupervised LM is fine-tuned to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original model. However, RLHF is a complicated and often unstable procedure, requiring first fitting a reward model to reflect human preferences, then fine-tuning the unsupervised LM using RL to maximize this reward without drifting too far. In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. This allows us to solve the RLHF problem with only the simple classification loss, eliminating the need to sample from the LM during fine tuning or perform significant hyperparameter tuning. The resulting algorithm, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is simple, stable, and computationally lightweight. We evaluate DPO on a variety of downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization and single-turn dialog, and show that it can fine-tune LMMs to align with human preference as well as or better, while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Related Work}

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) is a popular approach for fine-tuning unsupervised LMs to align with user preferences. RHLF is typically performed by first fitting a reward model, which is then used to fine-tune the unsuupervised LM using reinforcement-learning from human feedback. The reward model is typically learned using a neural network, and the fine-tuned LM is then evaluated on a downstream task to estimate the reward function. The reward function is then used to optimize the unsupervised LM, which is then fine-tuned to maximize this reward function. The reward model is often learned using a neural network with a softmax output, which is then optimized using a standard cross-entropy loss. However, this approach requires sampling from the LM during training, which can be computationally expensive and unstable. In this paper, instead of sampling from the LM, we use a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model, which allows us to solve the RHLF problem with only a simple loss function. We refer to this approach as Direct Preference Optimization (D PO).

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple and computationally lightweight method for fine-tuning unsuupervised LMs to match human preferences. DPO is based on the observation that the reward function learned by RHLF can be written as a linear combination of the LM's latent states, and that the optimal policy for maximizing the reward function can be found by solving a linear program. We use this observation to optimize the reward function using a simple classification loss, which allows us to fine-tune the LM using only a simple loss function, without sampling from the LM. We refer to this method as Direct Preference Optimization ( DPO). We evaluate DPO on several downstream tasks, including sentiment classification, summarization, and dialogue, and show that it is stable, performant , and computationally lightweight, and outperforms existing methods in these tasks.
