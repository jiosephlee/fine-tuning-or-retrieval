\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has yielded impressive results in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-4 [2]), but at the same time, these models are trained in an entirely unsupervised manner, relying solely on the text they are exposed to during training. This poses a challenge when it comes to controlling the behavior of these models, as there is no explicit human feedback available to fine-tune them. In this work, we introduce a method for fine-tuning large-scale unsupervised LMs to align with user preferences, without relying on reinforcement learning (RL). Our approach, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), leverages a simple yet effective method for learning a reward function from human preference data, and then uses a straightforward optimization procedure to directly optimize the policy learned from the reward function. Our experiments show that DPO is able to align with human preferences and produce responses that are as or more effective than those produced by existing methods based on reinforcement learning from human feedback.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervisied language models (LMs), such as those trained on the OpenAI BERT and GPT-4 datasets, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in areas such as translation, summarization, and question answering [3], [4], [5]. However, these models are trained using a self-supervised learning objective that does not explicitly reward or penalize specific behaviors, making it difficult to control their outputs in a fine-grained manner. In this work, inspired by previous research on reinforcement learning from human preference data [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], we introduce a method for training language models to align with human preferences, without relying on RL. Our method, which we call Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO), learns a reward function from human preferences and then optimizes the policy using a straightforward optimization procedure. Our experiments show that, compared to existing approaches that use reinforcement learning to fine-tune language models, DPO is more robust and efficient, requiring fewer training steps and yielding comparable or superior results.

\subsection{Learning a Reward Function from Human Preferences}

The goal of reinforcement learning is to learn a policy $\pi$ from a reward function $r : \mathcal{D} \to \mathbb{R}$, where $\mathcal{D} = \{D, \pi, \mathcal{R}\}$ represents a dataset containing examples drawn from the environment $\mathcal{D}$, along with the associated rewards $r(\cdot|\mathcal{D})$. In practice, it is often difficult to acquire a reward function $r$ that accurately reflects human preferences, so instead, we use a dataset of human preference labels $\mathcal{P} = \{p_{\theta}, r_{\theta}, \mathcal{D}_{\theta}\}$, where $p_{\theta}$ is the policy we wish to optimize, and $r_{\theta}$ is a reward function learned from the preference dataset $\mathcal{P}$, using a method such as maximum likelihood estimation. The goal of reinforcement learning is then to find the policy $\pi_{\theta}$ that maximizes the expected reward:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:reinforcement_learning}
    \max_{\pi_{\theta}} \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta} \mid \mathcal{D}}\bigl[r(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot)|\mathcal{D})\bigr]
\end{equation}
