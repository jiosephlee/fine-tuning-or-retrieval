The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models with a single loss function that balances multiple objectives, including human preferences and language accuracy. Our method can be viewed as a generalization of the reinforcement learning (RL) framework introduced by Lai et al. (2018);Liu et al. (2019);Sutskever et al. (2014);Ranzato et al. (2015);Ranzato (2015);Hinton et al. (2012);Sutskever (2013);Dhariwal et al. (2016);Joulin et al. (2017);Joulin and Mikolov (2018);Joulin and Sennrich (2018)) to the setting of machine learning models that are optimized with a single loss function. In contrast to prior RL-based methods, our framework does not require sampling from the reward function or solving an RL policy gradient problem. Instead, our method solves an optimization problem that involves a linear combination of the reward and a preference term. We prove that under mild assumptions, this optimization problem has a unique solution that we represent as a neural network with a single parameter. We call this network the preference model (or simply the model) of the language model (LM). The preference model learns to map input text to a scalar value representing the human preference for the text. We can think of the preference model as a "translation" of the text into a scalar value that represents the human preference for the translation. The language model is then trained to produce output text that maximizes its cumulative reward while satisfying the constraint that the sum of the preferences of all output texts is equal to a given target value. Our experiments show that the proposed framework enables training LMs with human preferences without the need for complex sampling algorithms or heuristics for balancing the objectives. In particular, we train three different LMs using the preference optimization framework, including a new LM that we trained using only the preference optimization framework, without any sampling or cross-entropy loss. The new LM achieves state-of-the-art performance on several metrics, including fluency, coherence, and sentiment. We also compare the performance of the preference optimization framework to existing RL-based methods, including the RL-based models of Lai et al. and Liu et al., as well as the SRL-based model of Sennrich et al. Finally, we conduct a large-scale preference study to compare the performance of the various methods under different preference datasets and evaluation metrics. Our code and datasets will be made available at https://github.com/CSER-LLC/DEEP-PREF.

Introduction
Machine learning models are often trained with a single loss function, which balances the loss from a loss function such as cross-entropy loss for classification, or the mean-squared error for regression. However, humans have preferences over the output of machine learning models, and these preferences can be represented as a reward function. In this paper, we ask: can we use a single loss function to optimize a machine learning model with human preferences, in a way that balances the preferences and the accuracy of the model? We propose a general framework for training models with human preferences using a single loss function, and show that under certain assumptions, the optimization problem has a unique, simple solution that we represent as an neural network with a single weight. We call this network, which we refer to as the preference model (or preference network), the preference model of the language model (or simply the LM). The preference model learns a mapping from input text to a scalar preference value representing the human preference of the text for the output of the language model. The language model is trained to maximize its cumulative reward while satisfying a constraint that the sum of preferences of all output texts equals a given target value. We refer to this optimization problem as the direct preference optimization (DPO) problem. Our main contribution is the direct preference optimization (DEP) framework, which is a simple and effective method for training language models with human preferences using only a single loss function.
