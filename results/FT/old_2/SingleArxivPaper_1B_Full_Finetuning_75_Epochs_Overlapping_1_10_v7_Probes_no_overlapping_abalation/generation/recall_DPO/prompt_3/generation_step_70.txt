\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models of increasing size and capacity learn to perform a wide range of tasks from reading (Wu et al., 2019); summarization (Bai et al., 2020); to reasoning (Kipf and Welling, 2017); but obtaining controllable and desirable behavior is difficult due to their completely unsupervised training scheme. While methods exist for gaining some degree of steerability (Bai et al. (2022); Pan et al. (2023)), achieving precise control of the model's behavior is challenging, as it requires tuning parameters such as the learning rate, the number of generations to sample from the LM prior to fine-tuning, and the fraction of generations to sample from each camp. In this paper we present a single-step method for fine-tuning language models with unsupervised training that is directly optimized for human preference, eliminating the need for any form of reinforcement learning from human feedback.

At a high level, existing methods fall into two categories. The first group of methods (Huang et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Weston et al., 2026; Yin et al., 2027) fine-tune large unsupervised language models using reinforcement learning from human preferences, typically fitting a reward model that measures the quality of generated text in some domain of interest, and then using an actor-critic algorithm to optimize the unsupervsured language model policy to maximize the estimated human preference function without drifting too far from its original form. The second group of methods (Bai et al.; Pan et al.) optimize a language model's reward directly using methods from reinforcement learning, typically fitting a Q-function to a dataset of human preferences for text pairs in a given domain, and then using a standard reinforcement learning algorithm (Schulman et al., 2017; Lai et al., 2018; Riedmiller, 2018; Lai and Schwing, 2019; Lai et al. , 2020 to optimize the unsupervisor training objective until convergence. While these methods can achieve high performance on downstream tasks, they typically involve fitting a complex reward model, training a large reinforcement learning policy, and then performing additional reinforcement learning to fine-tune the reward model, all in addition to training the language model itself. In this paper we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), a single-step method for optimizing a language model's training objective to match human preferences, without fitting a reward model, training a reinforcement learning policy, or performing significant hyperparameter optimization. Our key insight is to parameterize the reward model with a parametric family, allowing us to optimize the reward model parameters with a standard classification loss, yielding the direct preference optimization (DPO) algorithm. We theoretically show that the corresponding optimal policy from the reward model can be extracted from the parameters of the reward model in closed form, and thus the DPO algorithm yields the same optimal policy as an RLHF algorithm that optimizes the same reward function, but with a simple classification loss in place of RL. We evaluate DPO against several state-of-the-art methods in the sentiment modulation, summarization , and single-turn dialogue domains. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:
