\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on tasks ranging from translation and summarization to question answering (Devlin et al., 2019; BERT, Brown et al., 2018; Sutskever et al., 2014; Cho et al., 2015; Vaswani et al., 2017; Li et al., 2016; Ranzato et al., 201 stick with the original BERT architecture). However, these models are trained to maximize the likelihood of the training data, which may not align with the preferences of downstream users. For example, a large-scale LM trained on Wikipedia might generate answers that are technically correct but not what a human would consider helpful or useful. Similarly, a model trained on Reddit might produce responses that are entertaining but not what a human user would consider appropriate for a job interview. In both cases, the goal is to develop a language model that is effective and usable, not just one that performs well on the training data.

A common approach to addressing this issue is to fine-tune a large-scale unsupervised LM with reinforcement learning (RL) from human preferences. This process involves collecting a set of human preferences, typically by asking a small number of humans to rate pairs of model-generated and reference outputs on a scale from 1 to 5, where 1 is poor and 5 is excellent. The preference dataset is then used to train a reward model that maps model outputs to human ratings. This reward model is then used to train an RL policy to produce model outputs that are highly rated by humans. This procedure is known as reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF; Lai et al., 201

In this paper, we propose a new method for RLHF that avoids the need to fit a reward model. Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), directly optimizes the policy to maximize the expected reward from the preference dataset. This is done by first defining a parameterization of the reward function that allows us to express the optimal policy in closed form. We then train a policy gradient to optimize this parameterized reward function using a standard classification loss. This approach is conceptually simple and avoids the need to fit any additional models, making it computationally efficient and easy to implement. We evaluate DPO on several tasks, including sentiment tuning, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and show that it can fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences with comparable or better performance than existing methods. Our experiments also demonstrate that DPO is stable and robust to hyperparameter choices, unlike previous approaches that rely on sampling from the LM during training.
