\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks such as text generation, question answering, and summarization. However, the lack of supervision makes it difficult to control the behavior of these models, especially when they are used in applications where precise control is required, such as in dialogue systems or sentiment analysis. In this paper, we introduce a new method for controlling the behavior of LMs, called Direct Preference Optimization, or DPO.

DPO is based on the observation that the reward model used in RLHF can be used to extract the optimal policy for fine-tuning the unsupervised LM. The reward model is a function that maps the LM's output to a scalar value representing the quality of the generation. By optimizing the reward model, we can extract the optimal policy for the fine-tuning task, which is a classification problem that maps the LM's outputs to a scalar value representing its quality. This policy can then be used to fine-tune the unsuper- 
vised LM to align with human preferences.

DPO is stable and performant, and outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both sentiment alignment (summarization and single-turn dialogue) and response quality (sentiment analysis). It is also computationally lightweight, requiring only a simple classification loss to solve the RLHF problem. We demonstrate the effectiveness of DPO in practice with experiments on summarization, single-turn dialog, and sentiment analysis.

\section{Related Work}

One common approach is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the human provides rewards to the model for generating text that aligns with their preferences. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the quality of model generations in various domains, including summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis.

However, RLHF is often unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter tuning to achieve good performance. Additionally, collecting human feedback can be time-consuming and expensive, especially for large-scale models. As a result, there is a growing interest in developing methods that can control the behavior of LMs without relying on human feedback. One promising approach is to use a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then use reinforcement learning to fine-tune the model to align with the reward model. This approach has been successfully applied to various domains, including summarizing text, question answering, and generating images.
