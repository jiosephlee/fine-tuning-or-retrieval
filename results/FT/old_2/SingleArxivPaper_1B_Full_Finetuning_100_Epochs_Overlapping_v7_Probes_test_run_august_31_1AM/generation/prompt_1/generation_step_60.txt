The paper introduces a new optimization paradigm called "direct preference optimization" (DPO). DPO is a generalization of reinforcement learning (RL) that allows for the optimization of arbitrary functions from rewards to policies. In contrast to RL, DPO does not require sampling from the reward function, and thus can be performed in practice on large datasets. DPO can be used to solve a wide variety of problems, including fine-tuning, code gen, and dialog management. In this post, I will focus on the use of DPO for language modeling, specifically fine-tuning language models (LMs) to generate high-quality text.

The basic idea behind DPO is simple. Instead of optimizing the standard loss used in standard RL, DPO uses a reward function that reflects the quality of the generated text. This reward function is then optimized by the LM policy, which generates text and receives feedback from a human labeler about how good or bad the text is. The policy that maximizes the given reward is the best model of the human preferences underlying the reward function, and therefore the LM has also learned the human preferences. In other words, DPO allows us to directly optimize human preferences using an AI, bypassing the need for explicit human feedback and enabling automated quality improvement.

The paper also presents a theoretical analysis of DPO, which shows that the optimal policy under DPO satisfies certain properties, including envy-freeness and a simple expression for its expected value in terms of the reward function and the reward maximization problem.
