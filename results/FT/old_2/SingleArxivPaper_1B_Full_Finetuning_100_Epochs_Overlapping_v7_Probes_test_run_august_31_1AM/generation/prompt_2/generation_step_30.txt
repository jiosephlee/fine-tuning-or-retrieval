The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the reward function used by DPO, the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm. DPO uses a gradient-based optimization algorithm to find the parameters of a language model that maximize the reward function, but it requires the reward function to be differentiable, which is not the case for many popular reward functions used in practice. In contrast, the new algorithm for DPO, called PPO, uses a Monte-Carlo estimation of the gradient of the reward function, which is not differentiable but is computationally much less expensive. We evaluate DPO and PPO on several datasets of human preferences for summarization, question answering, and machine translation. For summarization, we compare DPO against several existing algorithms, including the best existing algorithm for this task, ALICE. For question answering, we compare DPO to the best existing algorithms, including the state-of-the-art algorithm SPORE. For machine translation, we compare DPO and PPO to the best existing algorithm, including the state-ofthe-art algorithm GPT-4. Our experiments show that DPO is as effective as existing algorithms, but is orders of magnitude faster to train and use.
The paper is a follow-up to the original paper "Direct Preference Optimization (DPO): Aligning Language Models with Human Preferences" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12826). The original paper presented a new algorithm, DPO, for training language models to match human preferences. DPO uses a simple but powerful approach to optimize the reward function used by existing algorithms for training language models, such as ALICE and SPORE. The DPO algorithm is based on the observation that the reward function used by most existing algorithms for training language model is not differentiable, and therefore cannot be optimized using standard optimization algorithms. The DPO algorithm instead uses a Monte-Carlo estimate of the gradient of the rewards with respect to the parameters, which is not differentiabl, but is computationally much cheaper. The DPO algorithm has been implemented as a library called DPOpy, which is available at https://github.com/AlgoHacks/DPOpy/tree/main/examples. The original paper also presented a simple experiment comparing the performance of DPO to several existing algorithms, including ALICE and SPORES. However, the results of the experiment were inconclusive, because the DPO implementation used in the experiment was not the same as the DPOpy implementation used in the paper. In this follow-up paper, we perform a more thorough experiment comparing the performance of the DPOpy implementation of DPO with several existing algorithms. Our results show that the DPOpy implementation is as effective as the original DPO algorithm, but is orders of magnitudes faster to train and use. We also present a new implementation of the DPO algorithm, which we call PPO, which uses the same Monte-Carlo gradient estimation as the DPOpy version of DPO, but is computationally more efficient. We evaluate the performance of PPO against several existing algorithms for training language modues, including the state-of-art ALICE and SPORe. Our experiments show that PPO is as effective as the best existing algorithms, but is up to 10 times faster to train and use.

Introduction
Training language models to match human-written text is a major unsolved problem in artificial intelligence. Current language models are trained on vast quantities of text, but their ability to produce high-quality human-written text is limited. One approach to improving the quality of language models is to use human preferences as a reward function for training the model. This approach, known as supervised learning of human preferences (SLHP), has been shown to be effective in improving the quality of language model output (Bai et al., 2022;Chen et al., 2023;Gao et al., 2024;Kumar et al., 2025;Liu et al., 2026;Wang et al., 2027;Zhang et al., 2028;Zhao et al., 2019;Zhou et al., 2021;Zhu et al., 2020;Zhu et al, 2022;Zhu et al. 2023). However, SLHP is not without challenges. One challenge is that the reward function used in the training of language models is not differentiable, which makes it difficult to train language models using standard optimization algorithms. A second challenge is that the reward functions used in practice are not necessarily optimal. In this paper, we address these challenges by presenting a new algorithm for training language models using human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). The key idea of DPO is to use a simple but powerful approach, known as preference-based optimization (PBO), to optimize the reward function that is used in the training of the language model.
