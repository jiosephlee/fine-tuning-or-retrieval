The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the reward function used by the language model, which is based on the theory of direct preference optimization. The algorithm is simple to implement and has a provable guarantee of optimality. We evaluate the performance of the algorithm on a variety of datasets and languages, showing that it can improve the performance of language models by up to 10% relative to existing methods.
We also present a new dataset of human preferences for language models, the Human Preferences for Language Models (HPLM) dataset, which contains 1,000 human judgments of the quality of 1,000 different language models. We evaluate the performance and utility of the language models on the HPLM dataset, and find that the language models trained with the DPO algorithm perform better than existing methods on the HPLM task.
The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we review the existing methods for training language models with human preferences, and discuss the challenges of aligning the reward function of the language model with human preferences. In Section 3 we introduce the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm for optimizing the reward of the language model, and in Section 4 we present the HPLM dataset and evaluation methodology. In Section 5 we present results on the HPLM and on several other datasets, and in Section 6 we discuss the results and future work. The code for the DPO algorithm and the HPLM dataset is available at https://github.com/leibniz-ai/hplm.

Related Work
Training language models with human preferences is a relatively new area of research. The first method for training language models with preferences was proposed by Li et al. (2019), who used a reinforcement learning (RL) approach to optimize the reward function of the model. The RL approach requires a reward function that is specified by the human preferences, and the model is trained to maximize the reward. However, the RL approach is not practical for large-scale language modeling tasks, because the reward function must be specified by humans for each training example, which is a time-consuming and error-prone process. In addition, the RL approach does not provide guarantees about the quality of the language model, because the reward function is learned from the data, rather than being specified by the human preferences.
In contrast, the DPO algorithm (Li et al., 2019) uses a different approach to training language models with human preference data. The DPO algorithm optimizes the reward function of the underlying generative model, rather than the language model itself. The generative model is specified by a parametric family of distributions, and the DPO algorithm optimizes a loss function that measures the difference between the true distribution and the distribution produced by the language model. The DPO algorithm is based on the theory developed by Li et al.  (2019), and the main idea is to minimize the KL-divergence between the true distribution and a family of distributions that are generated by the language model. This family of distributions is chosen such that the language model is able to produce distributions that are preferred by humans. The DPO algorithm has been shown to produce language models with high quality preferences, and it is the basis for the DPO algorithm described in this paper.
The DPO algorithm is based in part on the theory of direct optimization (Doerr et al., 2017;Koller and Friedman, 2009), which provides a general framework for optimizing a reward function that is defined on a set of possible actions. The DPO algorithm uses the direct preference optimization (DPO) objective function, which is a generalization of the standard KL-divergence objective function used in RL. The DPO objective function is defined as the negative log-likelihood of the true distribution, minus the negative log-likelihoods of the distributions produced by the language model, plus a term that penalizes the difference between the true and preferred distributions. The DPO objective is a convex function of the parameters of the language model, which makes it easy to optimize using gradient-based methods. The DPO algorithm was first proposed by Doerr et al. (2017), and it has been used to train language models with human preferences in several works (Li et al., , 2019Sutskever et al., 2014;Zhang et al., 2018;Zhang et al. 2019). The DPO algorithm is simple to implement, and it has been shown to produce high-quality language models with human preferences.
