The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the reinforcement learning approach to the case of preference learning from discrete rewards, which is known as the reward alignment problem. The authors propose a method called DPO, which is based on a reward model that is learned from human preferences. DPO is shown to be effective in aligning language models with preferences, outperforming previous approaches like PPO-based RLHF and baselines such as RLHF with a reward model learned from human feedback. The DPO algorithm is also efficient, requiring only a single pass over the preference data, and is easy to implement in practice. The paper is accompanied by a set of experiments that demonstrate the effectiveness of DPO on a range of tasks, including summarization, dialogue, and translation. In summary, the paper makes the following main contributions: 1) A general framework for learning reward models from human preferences, which can be applied to a wide range of preference learning scenarios. 2) A novel algorithm, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that learns a reward model from preferences without requiring explicit human feedback. 3) Experiments on a range of preference learning tasks, showing that DPO aligns language models with human preferences more effectively than existing approaches.

Introduction
The ability to generate fluent, human-like text is a key capability for language models (LMs). However, current LMs are trained on large corpora of text, which may not reflect the kinds of texts people actually prefer. In particular, it is important to ensure that LMs are aligned with human preferences, so that they produce outputs that are both useful and enjoyable. This aligns with the broader goal of developing AI systems that are beneficial to people, rather than harmful. In this work, we introduce a method for learning a reward model from human preferences, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). D PO is a general framework for learning reward functions from human preferences, which is applicable to a wide range of scenarios. We demonstrate that D PO can be used to train LMs to match human preferences, outperforming existing approaches such as reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF; Schmidhuber, 1990;Wu et al., 2019b; and reward modeling approaches based on language models (LMs; see Section 2 for more details).
The central idea behind D PO is to learn a reward model from human preference data that is consistent with the underlying preference structure. This is in contrast to traditional approaches, which learn a reward model from preference data and then use it to optimize the policy. In the case of reward learning from human preferences, the reward model is learned from preference data, but the policy is optimized using the reward model. This approach is known as reward alignment, and has been successfully applied to various domains, including language (Kupcsik et al., 2018;Bai et al., 2020;Chen et al., 2021;Liu et al., 2022;Zhang et al., 2023). In contrast, D PO learns a reward model directly from preference data, without first optimizing a policy. This approach is referred to as direct preference optimization (DPO; Section 2).
D PO is based on a reward learning framework that has been previously developed for reinforcement learning (RL; Sutton and Barto, 2018). In RL, the goal is to learn a policy π that maximizes the expected reward R(π; x), where x represents the input to the policy. In contrast, in DPO, the objective is to learn a reward function R that maximizes the likelihood of the preference data, given the reward model R(x, y).
