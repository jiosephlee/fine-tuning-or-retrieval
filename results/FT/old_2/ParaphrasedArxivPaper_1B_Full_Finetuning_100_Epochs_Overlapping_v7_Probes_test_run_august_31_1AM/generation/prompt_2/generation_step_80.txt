The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known RLHF algorithm to the case where the reward function is not explicitly defined, but rather is inferred from preference data. This allows the use of language models trained on large corpora, which are typically unavailable for fine-tuning with explicit rewards. The method is based on the observation that the optimal policy for the reward model can be expressed as a linear combination of the language model's parameters, which can be optimized using standard techniques from convex optimization. We refer to this approach as DPO, for "Direct Preference Optimization." DPO is shown to be equivalent to the RLHF algorithm, and its theoretical guarantees match those of the RLHF method. Experiments demonstrate that DPO can align language models with human preferences more effectively than previous approaches, even when the reward model is not explicitly defined. In particular, DPO matches or outperforms the best previously reported results on the PPO-based RLHF task, while requiring only a fraction of the training data.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the problem of aligning language models with preferences, and reviews related work. Section 3 presents the Direct Preference Optimization algorithm, followed by its theoretical analysis in Section 4. Section 5 presents experimental results, and Section 6 draws conclusions.

Related Work
Language model fine-tuning with human feedback is a relatively new area of research, with the first studies appearing in 2020 [1,2]. These early works typically rely on reinforcement learning (RL) to train language models to maximize human preferences, using a reward model that is either explicitly defined or inferred from preference data. In this section, we review the RLHF algorithm, which is the most widely used approach for training language models with human feedback. We then discuss related methods that optimize the reward model without relying on RL.

Reinforcement Learning for Language Model Fine-Tuning
The RLHF algorithm trains a language model to maximize a reward function that is derived from human preference data. The reward function is typically defined as the difference between the reward received by a model and a reference model, where the reference model is a baseline model that does not learn from human feedback. This approach is known as the "reference policy" framework [3], and is the basis for several algorithms that optimize the reward function directly, such as the REINFORCE algorithm [4] and its variants. In contrast, the RLHF algorithm uses a policy gradient to update the language model, which is based on the principle that the optimal policy for a reward function is a linear combination of the parameters of the policy [5]. This policy is referred to as the reference policy, and is typically initialized with a pre-trained language model. The RLHF algorithm proceeds as follows: 1. Sample a sequence of inputs x 1 , . . . , x N from the target language model, and a reference model p r . 2. Direct Preference Optimization
The RLHF algorithm is based on the assumption that the reward function can be explicitly defined, but in practice, the reward function is often unknown. In this section, our goal is to develop a method for training language models to match human preferences without relying on an explicit reward function. We begin by introducing the problem of aligning a language model with a given set of human preferences. We then present a method for optimizing the language model parameters to maximize the likelihood of the observed preferences, which we refer to as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
