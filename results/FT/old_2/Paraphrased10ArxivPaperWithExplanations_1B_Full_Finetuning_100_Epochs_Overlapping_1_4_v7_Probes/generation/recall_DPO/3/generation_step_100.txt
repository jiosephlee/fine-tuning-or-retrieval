\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models (\textit{LUNMs}) trained on vast quantities of text have demonstrated impressive capabilities, ranging from fluent human-level dialogue to the ability to answer questions about complex concepts (see, e.g., \cite{chowdhery2022palm, stiennon2022training}). However, these models are trained solely on unlabeled text, without explicit human supervision on the desired behaviors or responses. This makes it challenging to fine-tune them to align with specific preferences or goals, such as controlling the sentiment of generated text, or ensuring that generated responses are accurate and complete. In this paper, we introduce a method for fine-tuning LUNMs to align with human preference data, without the need for reinforcement learning (RL) or extensive hyperparameter tuning. Our method, which we call \emph{Direct Preference Optimization} or DPO, directly optimizes the policy of the LUNM to match human preferences, without first fitting a separate reward model and training a separate RL agent. This approach is conceptually simpler and more straightforward to implement than existing methods based on reinforcement learning from human feedback, and we believe it will be a useful tool for fine-tuning LLMs to meet specific objectives.

\section{Related Work}

Unsupervised language models have been trained on massive datasets of text, including Reddit posts, Wikipedia articles, and news articles, and evaluated on a variety of downstream tasks, such as summarization \citep{mishra-etal-2018-simultaneous}, question answering \citep{stiennon2022training}, and sentiment analysis \citep{yu2022learning}. These models are trained by minimizing the difference between the model's outputs and human-generated responses to prompts, using a loss that balances the likelihood of the model's output given the prompt and the human response. This approach, known as unsupervised prompting, has been shown to yield state-of-the-art results on many tasks \citep{mehri2022learning, bai2022language}. However, the training objective used in these studies is not aligned with the goal of controlling the behavior of the language model, which is often desirable in applications such as conversational AI. In this paper, inspired by the success of reinforcement learning from human feedback in fine-tuning language models to align with human preferences \citep{stiennon2022training, ouyang2022training}, we explore whether it is possible to optimize the language model directly to match human preferences, rather than training a separate reward model and optimizing a separate policy.

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLLF) has been used to fine-tune language models to align with preferences, yielding models that generate more fluent and coherent responses than the original model \citep{stieven2022learning, ouyang2022learning}. These methods first train a separate reward model to measure the quality of the generated responses, and then use reinforcement learning to optimize the language model policy to maximize this reward. This approach is computationally intensive, as it requires sampling from the language model during training to estimate the reward, and requires careful tuning of the reward model to avoid overfitting. In this paper, following earlier work \citep{ziegler2022training}, we propose a method that directly optimizes the language model policy to match human preferences, using a single-stage optimization procedure. This method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), does not require sampling from the language model, and is computationally simpler and more efficient than RLLF-based methods.
