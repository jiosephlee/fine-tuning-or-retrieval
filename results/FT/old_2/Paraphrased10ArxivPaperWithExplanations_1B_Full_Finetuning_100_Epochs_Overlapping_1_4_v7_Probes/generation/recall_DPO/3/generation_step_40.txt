\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive advances in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-3 [2]), but achieving precise control over their behavior is challenging due to the completely unsophisticated nature of their training. While LMs can be fine-tuned to produce high-quality responses to prompts, they are not explicitly trained to do so, and the quality of generated responses is often highly dependent on the prompt used. Moreover, the vast majority of human feedback is collected via supervised methods, such as prompting humans to rate the quality of generated responses, which is expensive and time-consuming.

To address these challenges, researchers have developed a variety of methods for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences. In this work, we focus on reinforcement learning from human feedback, which trains an unsupervised LM to maximize a reward function that reflects the preferences of a human annotator. This approach, known as reinforcement learning from human feedback with preference learning (RLHF), has been shown to yield high-quality responses in tasks such as summarization [3] and single-turn dialogue [4]. However, RLHF is a challenging and often unstable method, requiring the construction of a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then training the unsupervised LM using RL to maximize this reward without drifting too far away from the original model.

In this paper, we present a new method for fine-tuning LLMs using RLHF that avoids the need for a reward model. Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), directly optimizes the unsupervised LM for a given set of preferences, without first fitting a reward model. This is possible because the reward model used in RLHF is implicitly defined by the preference data collected during training. Specifically, the reward model is the policy that maximizes the reward function given the preference data. By exploiting this relationship, we can express the optimal policy in terms of the preference data, and then optimize the unsupervised LM directly using a simple classification loss. Our experiments show that DPO yields comparable or better performance than existing methods, while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervized language models (LMs), such as GPT-4 [5] and BERT [1], have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language understanding (NLU). However, these models are trained using a large corpus of unlabeled text, and their behavior is largely determined by the prompt used to generate the response. This makes it difficult to control the quality of generated responses or to fine-tune the model to align with specific preferences. To address these challenges, researchers are developing methods for fine-tuning large unsupervised LMs to produce high-quality responses. One approach is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback to train an unsupervised LM that maximizes a reward function that reflects human preferences. This method, known as reinforcement learning with preference learning (RLWP), has been shown to produce high-quality responses in tasks like summarization [3] or single-turn dialogue [4].

However, RLWP is a complex and often unreliable method, requiring the construction and optimization of a reward model that captures the preferences of the human annotator. Additionally, the reward model must be fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to avoid drifting too far from the initial unsupervised model. In this paper, we present an alternative method for fine-tuning large LMs using RLHF that avoids these challenges. Our method, which is based on the observation that the reward model used in the RLHF framework is implicitly defined by the preferences collected during training, we call Direct Preference Optimization. Our experiments show that our method, which does not require a reward model, yields comparable or better performance while being substantially simpler to deploy.
