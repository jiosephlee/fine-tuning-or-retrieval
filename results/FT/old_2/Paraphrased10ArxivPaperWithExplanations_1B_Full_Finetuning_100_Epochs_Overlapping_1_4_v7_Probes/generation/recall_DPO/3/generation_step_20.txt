\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks, but achieving precise control of their behaviors remains challenging due to the completely unsophisticated nature of their training. 
For example, the GPT-3 model, which was trained on 175 billion parameters, is able to generate coherent text on a wide range of topics, but it is difficult to predict its behavior in novel contexts or to control its output to achieve specific goals.
This is especially true for tasks that require fine-grained control over the generation, such as summarization or single-turn dialogue, where the model’s output is often not aligned with human preferences.

To address this challenge, researchers have developed a variety of methods for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, including reinforcement learning from human feedback, also known as reinforcement learning from human feedback with language models (RLHF). 
In RLHF, a human annotator is presented with a sequence of model outputs and asked to rate them on a scale from 1 to 5, with 1 indicating the model’s output is the worst possible and 5 indicating the best possible. 
The human then receives a reward based on the average rating of the model’s outputs, and the model is trained to maximize this reward.
This approach has been shown to be effective in aligning the behavior of LMs with human preferences, but it is a complex and often unreliable procedure, requiring the human to annotate a large number of model outputs and fine-tune the model to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original model.

In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LLMs that does not require human annotation or reinforcement learning from human feedback. 
Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is based on a simple parameterization of the reward function that allows us to solve the standard reinforcement learning problem with only a simple supervised classification loss. 
This allows us to fine-tune the unsophisticated LLM using only a few hundred annotated model outputs, without requiring any additional hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the model during training.

Our experiments show that D PO can fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences, achieving or exceeding the performance of existing methods in many cases. 
In particular, we demonstrate that DPO can fine tune LMs to generate more coherent text on a wide variety of topics, including news articles, scientific papers, and movie reviews, while maintaining high response quality in summarization, single-turn dialogue, and other tasks. 
Moreover, DPO is computationally efficient and easy to implement, requiring only a few hundred annotated outputs to train the model, compared to the tens of thousands required by existing methods.
