The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known RLHF algorithm to the case where the reward function is not explicitly defined, but rather is inferred from preference data. This allows the use of language models trained on large corpora, which are typically unavailable for fine-tuning with explicit rewards. The method is based on the observation that the optimal policy for the reward function can be expressed as a linear combination of the language model's parameters, and that this optimal policy can be computed efficiently using a single gradient step. The resulting algorithm, called DPO, is provably equivalent to the RLHF algorithm, and is shown to yield equivalent or better results in practice. DPO is implemented as a PyTorch library, and is publicly available at https://github.com/CarperAI/dpo.
The paper "Learning to Align with Human Preferences" introduces a new approach for training language models with human feedback. The key insight is that the alignment problem can be viewed as a binary classification task, where the goal is to assign a positive label to a given input sequence and a negative label to a reference sequence. This formulation allows the use of standard supervised learning techniques, such as cross-entropy loss, to train the model. The authors propose a two-stage training procedure. In the first stage, the model is trained to maximize the likelihood of the reference sequences, using a language model that is pre-trained on large corpora. In the second stage, the model is fine-tuned to maximize the likelihood of positive labels, using a reward model that is learned from human preference data. The reward model is constructed by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the preferred sequences, using a variant of the KL-Divergence loss. Empirical studies show that the proposed approach can yield significant improvements over baselines that do not use human feedback, while maintaining or surpassing the performance of approaches that do use human feedback.
The paper "Learning from Human Preferences with a Single Supervision Signal" introduces a method for training language models using only human preference data. The key idea is to use a single supervised loss, which is optimized to maximize the probability of preferred sequences while simultaneously minimizing the probability of dispreferred sequences. The loss is defined as a variant of the cross-entropy loss, where the negative log-likelihood is replaced by the negative log-likelihood from a reward model that is trained on the preference data. The reward function is learned using a variant of the REINFORCE algorithm, which is tailored to the constrained optimization problem that arises in this setting. Experiments on several datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach can achieve comparable or better performance than approaches that use explicit human feedback, while requiring only a single supervision signal.
