The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models to satisfy human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO differs from existing methods in two key ways. First, DPO directly optimizes the model parameters to satisfy human preferences, rather than fitting a lower-dimensional reward function. Second, DPO uses a gradient-based optimization algorithm, which enables handling of large preference datasets and fine-grained preferences. We evaluate DPO on aligning language models with both broad and fine-grained human preferences. On three preference datasets, DPO produces models that satisfy humans preferences better than BERT Fine-Tuning (BERT-FineTune) on average, and is comparable or faster to existing methods. Finally, we perform a systematic analysis of the preferences produced by DPO and BERT-FineTune, finding that DPO models prefer more diverse text and are better able to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) examples compared to BERT-FineTuning.
