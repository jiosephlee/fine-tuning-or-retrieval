The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models to satisfy human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO differs from existing methods in two key ways. First, DPO does not require explicit reward definitions or human feedback, making it extensible to previously unaddressed topics or implicit reward structures. Second, DPO is an optimization framework that can find solutions within a broad class of loss functions, including common language modeling losses and preference objectives expressed as a binary cross-entropy loss on top of a language model. We evaluate DPO on the tasks of political bias removal, health education, and self-improvement. On the language modeling and bias removal tasks, DPO produces models of similar or better performance than existing methods that are orders of magnitude faster to train. On the health education task, DPO models outperform existing methods that require human feedback. On the self-improvement task, DPO models produce improvements over the default model after training for a fraction of the time of existing methods. We also perform a detailed analysis of the solutions found by DPO, showing how they achieve the desired preferences without violating standard preference constraints such as irreversibility and monotonicity.
