\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models learn to perform a wide variety of tasks, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training. Existing methods for gaining steerability typically involve collecting human preferences for model generations and fine-tuning the unsupervised LM with reinforcement learning (RL). These methods can be broadly categorized as either reinforcement learning from human feedback, or preference learning. In RLHF, a reward model is fit to human preferences for model generations, and the corresponding optimal policy is then optimized using reinforcement learning to maximize the estimated reward without drifting too much from the original model. In preference learning, a preference learning objective is fit to model generations, and the resulting model is fine-tuned with a learning-to-rank objective to produce high-quality generations. While RLHF has been shown to yield improvements over unsupervised baselines, it is a complex procedure that requires fitting a reward model, training the optimal policy using reinforcement learning, and fine-tuning the reward model, all in a single pass over the LM. In this paper, we introduce a new approach for fine-tuning language models with preference learning, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO fits a single reward model to preferences for all model generations, and then optimizes the reward model using a simple classification loss to produce a policy that maximizes the reward without drifting too far away from the original model. We refer to this policy as the \textit{direct preference policy} (DPO policy). We theoretically show that the DPO policy is the unique maximum likelihood solution to the constrained optimization problem of fitting a reward model to preferences and optimizing it using a classification loss. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, outperforming existing methods for fine-tuning language model responses with preference learning.

\section{Related Work}

Unsupervised language models (LM) have been trained to perform a wide variety tasks, including translation, summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis. However, achieving precise control of the behaviors of large unsupervised LM is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. Several methods for fine-tuning unsupervised LM with human feedback have been proposed, including supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback using policy gradient algorithms, and methods that combine supervised and reinforcement learning. In this section, we review these approaches.

\textbf{Supervised Fine-Tuning.} Supervised fine-tuning fine-tunes an unsupervised LM with labeled training data to improve performance on a target task. This approach is straightforward to implement, but requires access to both the unsupervised LM and the target task data. In contrast, RLHF and related methods do not require access to any human feedback.

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.} RLHF methods fit a reward model to human preferences for model generation, and then use reinforcement learning to optimize the reward model to maximize its total reward while staying within a given policy. Early methods fit reward models using methods such as Q-learning or actor critic algorithms, but recent methods have fit reward models using neural networks. These methods typically involve three steps: 1) fitting a reward model to human preference data, 2) training an RL agent to optimize the fitted reward model, and 3) fine-tuning the RL agent with reinforcement learning to produce high-quality model generations. While RLHF methods have been shown to improve performance of unsupervised LM on downstream tasks such as summarization and question answering, they are complex to implement and often unstable, requiring significant hyperparameter tuning. In this paper, inspired by the success of neural network reward models in RL, we propose a new reward model for RLHF that enables extraction a closed-form solution to the RLHF problem, thus simplifying the algorithm and making it more robust.
