\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

1. Introduction
Large unsupervised language models of increasing size and capacity learn broad world knowledge and to a lesser extent some reasoning skills (Brown et al., 2018; Sutskever et al., 2019; Castronovo et al., 2020; Lewis et al., 2022). However, their training is completely unsupervised, and therefore their behavior is largely unpredictable and hard to control. In this paper, we will focus on how to gain steerability or controllability of the behavior of large unsupervised language models, without the need for explicitly labeled feedback from humans.

2. Related Work
Model fine-tuning has become the dominant approach to leveraging AI expertise with large language models (LMs). In model tuning, a human expert or team of humans fine-tunes parameters of a LMs to improve performance on a set of tasks of interest. This process of optimization is typically performed using reinforcement learning (RL), where the human expert plays a game with the algorithmic representation of the LM, also known as the policy, in order to maximize some notion of reward function representing the quality of the generated text on the tasks. This approach has been successfully applied to the fine-tuning of language models to improve performance on tasks such as sentiment analysis (Christiano et al., 2021) and machine translation (Wu et al., 202 two main categories of approaches for gaining steerability from language models. The first category of methods constructs explicit representations of the human preferences using techniques from machine learning theory and optimization, and then fine-tunes a language model with reinforcement learning to optimize a loss function that reflects the human preference model. A closely-related field of work is the reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF, where the additional goal of the human learning from the reinforcement learning algorithm. In the context of language, this has typically taken the form of a two-step process: (1) fine-tune a language model with standard model-tuning methodology to obtain an initial policy, and (2) train the human to update their policy based on their feedback of the generated text using RL. We will refer to this second step as the human policy network (HPN).

3.1.1
The second category of steerability-improving methods builds off of the success of large language models themselves and attempts to capitalize on the fact that large LM performance on arbitrary tasks can often be improved simply by feeding the LM more text. This category of methods includes techniques such as prompt engineering (Bennett et al., 2017;Peters et al., 2016;Stiennon and McKeown, 2018;Stiennon and Baroni, 2019;Bai et al., 2023); however, these methods are typically only effective for improving small segments of performance on specific tasks, and generally do not result in models with significantly improved safety or safety with increased safety properties, such as sentiment or toxicity. In contrast, the class of methods in this paper aim to directly improve the quality of the language model's behavior without modifying its structure or performance on a narrow set of tasks.

4.1
The main contribution of this paper is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a single-step steerability improvement method for language models that does not require any training or hyperparameter tuning. DPO represents the optimal policy to a given reward model in a theoretically wellmotivated way, and leverages this fact to derive an efficient optimization algorithm that we call preference optimization with DPO (PO-DPO). We evaluate DPO in several settings, showing that it is generally at least as effective as existing methods for improving language model quality, including reinforcement learning-based methods (such as RLHF and PPO-based RLHF; see below), and in some cases is up to an order of magnitude faster to run.
