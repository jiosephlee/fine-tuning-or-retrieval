\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

1. Introduction
Large unsupervised language models of increasing size learn broad world knowledge and to a certain extent reasoning skills (Brown et al., 2018; Sutskever et al., 2019; Computer Science; Chowdhery et al., 2020; Kingma and Welling, 2013; Lample and Conneau, 2020; Brown et al., 2022); however, training them to execute human-defined preferences such as responses of a given quality or sentiment is outside of their current capabilities.
This work addresses the steerability problem in training large unsupervised language models. Given a desired final model P and human preferences h = (h1, h2), the goal of steerability training is to construct a second model QP h such that P = QDPO(h). Herein, we refer to Q as the preference model and P and Q as preference optimized models. In this work, we focus on fitting a policy QP using reinforcement learning from human feedback, commonly referred to as reinforcement learning from human feedback with preference optimization (RLHF; Pan and Liu, 2018; Pan et al., 2017; Liu et al., 201a; Mooney et al., 201 eight; Barret et al., 2021; Bong et al., 202 1; Bong etal., 2022;Bai et al., 2023;Bai et al. 2022;Bian et al., 202 , etc.). Despite significant progress, existing RLHF methods face several challenges. First, RLHF is an approximate optimization of the Bellman optimality equation, and current algorithms optimize a parametric reward model that depends on the existing model P . This parametric reward model must be fit using reinforcement learning, introducing another level of approximation, and the resulting reward model may not accurately reflect the human preferences due to the curse of dimensionality (Wu and Schrittwieser, 2019). Second, the RLHF algorithm itself is an optimization of the same parametric reward model, making it difficult to stabilize and optimize. In this work,we address these challenges in the context of training language models, and thus we introduce a new RLHF algorithm that does not use a parametric reward model and is therefore more accurate, we refer to as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
