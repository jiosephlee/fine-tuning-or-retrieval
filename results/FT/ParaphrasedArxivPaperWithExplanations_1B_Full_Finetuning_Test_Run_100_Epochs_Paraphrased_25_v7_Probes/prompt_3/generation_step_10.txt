\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models (LMs) trained on very large datasets acquire surprising capabilities~\citep{chowdhery2022palm, brown2020language, touvron2023llama,bubeck2023sparks}. However, these models are trained on data generated by humans with a wide variety of goals, priorities, and skillsets. Some of these goals and skillsets may not be desirable to imitate; for example, while we may want our AI coding assistant to \textit{understand} common programming mistakes in order to correct them, nevertheless, when generating code, we would like to bias our model toward the (potentially rare) high-quality coding ability present in its training data. Similarly, we might want our language model to be \textit{aware} of a common misconception believed by 50\% of people, but we certainly do not want the model to claim this misconception to be true in 50\% of queries about it! In other words, selecting the model's \emph{desired responses and behavior} from its very wide \textit{knowledge and abilities} is crucial to building AI systems that are safe, performant, and controllable \citep{ouyang2022training}. While existing methods typically steer LMs to match human preferences using reinforcement learning (RL), we will show that the RL-based objective used by existing methods can be optimized exactly with a simple binary cross-entropy objective, greatly simplifying the preference learning pipeline.

At a high level, existing methods instill the desired behaviors into a language model using curated sets of human preferences representing the types of behaviors that humans find safe and helpful. This preference learning stage occurs after an initial stage of large-scale unsupervised pre-training on a large text dataset. While the most straightforward approach to preference learning is supervised fine-tuning on human demonstrations of high quality responses, the most successful class of methods is reinforcement learning from human (or AI) feedback (RLHF/RLAIF; \citep{christiano2017deep,bai2022constitutional}). RLHF methods fit a reward model to a dataset of human preferences and then use RL to optimize a language model policy to produce responses assigned high reward without drifting excessively far from the original model. While RLHF produces models with impressive conversational and coding abilities, the RLHF pipeline is considerably more complex than supervised learning, involving training multiple LMs and sampling from the LM policy in the loop of training, incurring significant computational costs.

In this paper, we show that the RLHF objective can be optimized exactly with the simple binary cross-entropy loss, greatly simplifying the RLHF pipeline. We introduce a new parameterization for the reward model that enables us to extract the corresponding optimal policy in a closed form, allowing us solve the standard RLHF optimization problem with only a simple binary cross-entropylearned objective. Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is simple, stable, and computationally lightweight. We demonstrate that DPO can fine tune LMs to align with a wide variety of human preferences, including those of the human annotators who provided the preferences, and those of the human annotator who provided the preferences. We also show that DPO can outperform existing methods in terms of both the quality of the generated responses and the quality of the human annotations. Finally, we show that DPO can be used to improve the quality of responses in summarization and single turn dialogue, while being substantially simpler to train and implement than existing methods.

\section{Related Work}
The field of preference learning has been studied extensively in the context of supervised learning, where the goal is to learn a model that can predict the preferences of a human annotator. The most successful methods for preference learning are reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the goal is instead to learn a model that maximizes the reward assigned to a given set of human preferences. The most successful methods in this area are reinforcement learning from human (RLH) and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF; \citet{christiano2017deep,bai2022constitutional}). These methods fit a reward model that reflects a human preference, and then use reinforcement learning to optimize a language model to produce responses that are assigned high reward without drifting too far away from the original model. The RLHF pipeline is considerably simpler than the supervised learning pipeline, involving training multiple LAs and sampling from the language model policy in the loop of the training, incurring significant computation costs.

In this paper we show that the RLH objective can be optimized exactly using a simple binary cross- entropy objective, greatly simplifying both the RLHF pipeline and the preference learning pipeline. We introduce a novel parameterization for the reward function that enables us to extract a corresponding optimal policy in closed-form, allowing us to solve standard RLHF problems with only a simple binary classification objective. Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), is simple, stable and computationally lightweight. DPO can fine tune language models to align with a wide range of human preferences, including the preferences of the human annotators that provided the preferences, and the preferences of the human reviewer who provided the preferences. DPO can also be used to improve the performance of language models in summarization and single-turn dialogue, while being substantially easier to train and implement than the existing methods.

\section*{Introduction}
Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) pre-training has led to impressive advances in language understanding, including the ability to generate fluent, coherent, and grammatically correct responses to a wide variety of prompts \citep{chowdery2022palm,brown2020language, tourovier2023llama,bubek2023sparks}. While these models have acquired impressive abilities, they are trained on data generated entirely by humans, and thus are not equipped to handle the wide variety of tasks that humans are capable of. For example, while we may wish our AI coding assistant to understand common programming mistakes in order for it to correct them, nevertheless when generating code, we certainly do not want it to claim this misconception to have been true in 50\%. Similarly, we might want to bias our language model toward the (potentally rare) high-quality coding skill present in its training data, but we certainly do want the model to claim the opposite in 50\% or more of queries about it! While existing methods for steering LMs to match human preference typically involve reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{christian2017deep,bai2022constitutional}, we will show that the objective used by existing methods is not optimal for this task. In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables us directly to optimize the RLHF objective with a simple binary cross entropy objective, greatly simplifiying the preference learning pipeline. Our method, called DPO, is simple, stable, computationally lightweight, and can be used to fine tune LMs to match a wide variety of human preference, including those of the annotators who provided the preference, and those of the annotator who provided the preference. We demonstrate that D PO can fine tune LMs with human preferences to match the preferences of the human reviewers who provided the preferences, as well as the preferences of the human readers who provided the preferences. Finally, we show how DPO can be used in the context of summarization and single-turn dialog, improving the quality of responses while being substantially simpler to tune and implement than existing methods.<|endoftext|>
