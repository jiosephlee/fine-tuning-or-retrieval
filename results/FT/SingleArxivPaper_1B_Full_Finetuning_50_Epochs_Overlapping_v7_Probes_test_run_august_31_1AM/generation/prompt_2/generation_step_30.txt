The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the reward function used by DPO, the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm. DPO uses a gradient-based optimization algorithm to find the parameters of a reward function that maximize the human preference data while satisfying certain constraints. Existing algorithms for optimizing reward functions have two main limitations. First, existing algorithms are computationally intractable, requiring exponential time and space for high-dimensional reward functions. Second, existing algorithms do not allow for the specification of constraints on the reward function, such as the requirement that the reward function be differentiable. In contrast, the DPO algorithm is both efficient and allows for the specification of constraints. We evaluate the DPO algorithm on several datasets of human preferences for language models, including the PPL dataset of preference profiles, the PPL-2k dataset of preference pairs from 2k humans, and the PPL-100k dataset of preference pairs sampled from a large language model. Our experiments show that the DPO algorithm can align language models with human preferences up to 10x better than previous methods, while being up to 3x faster than the fastest existing method. We also compare the DPO algorithm with a baseline that simply optimizes the same reward function as DPO, and find that the DPO algorithm improves performance while reducing the computational cost by up to 90%.
