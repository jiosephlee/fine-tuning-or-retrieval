The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models to satisfy human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO differs from existing methods in two key ways. First, DPO directly optimizes the model's reward function, rather than fitting a separate reward estimator. This enables DPO to use the same optimization algorithm as reinforcement learning algorithms, which are typically fitted with an initial "guess" at the reward function. Second, and more importantly, DPO uses a preference dataset that is tailored to the specific application of aligning language models with preferences, resulting in a preference dataset that is efficiently sampled from the distribution of human preferences that the model will ultimately be exposed to. We evaluate DPO on several preference alignment tasks, including correcting offensive and hate speech, and removing political bias. On these tasks, DPO outperforms existing methods, including Pythia, BERTScore, and GPT-2 Fine-Tuning, as well as reinforcement learning algorithms such as PPO. DPO is also compared against other language model fine-tuning approaches, and finds that language model fine-tuning with human feedback is a useful complement to large-scale pre-training. Finally, we conduct a human study in which participants preferred the preferences generated by DPO by a wide margin, indicating that DPO is generally approachable and accurate. In summary, our experiments show that DPO is a general and effective framework for training language models with human preferences.
