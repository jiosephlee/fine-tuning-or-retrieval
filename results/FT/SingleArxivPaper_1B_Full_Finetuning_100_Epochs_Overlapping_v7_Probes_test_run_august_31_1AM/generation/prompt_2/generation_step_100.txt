The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new family of algorithms for the problem of optimizing a language model with a direct reward from human preferences, under a generalization assumption on the reward function. Our algorithms are provably consistent, in the sense that they always produce a model with a strictly increasing reward function, and have low expected optimization error, even for complex reward functions. Our algorithms also scale to large models and datasets, and are orders of magnitude faster than alternative methods. We evaluate our algorithms on several preference datasets, and find that they can align language models with human preferences up to 10x better than existing methods, while being at least as accurate as existing methods. In particular, our approach is the first to align language models with human preference data that is publicly available, and we demonstrate that it can align language models with user preferences on Reddit and Stack Overflow with rewards up to 10x higher than the state-of-the-art.
We make the following contributions:
• A new class of algorithms for aligning language models and human preferences, with a generalization assumption on reward functions.
• A new dataset of human preferences for language, collected from Reddit and Stack Overflow users.
• A language model that is aligned with human preferences up to ten times better than existing methods, using models and datasets publicly available.
• A simple and effective implementation of our algorithms that is orders of magnitude faster than existing methods.
• A discussion of the limitations of our work and future directions.
2 Related Work
Human preference datasets
There are several publicly available datasets of human preferences for language (see Appendix A for a comprehensive list). Most of these datasets consist of pairs of articles, with one article rated by humans for quality and another for readability. However, there is one dataset, the Stack Overflow Data Explorer dataset (SDE dataset), that consists of pairs of Stack Overflow answers, with one answer rated for quality and another for usefulness. The SDE dataset is more representative of the type of human preferences that language models will encounter in the wild, as it includes preferences for both quality and usefulness, and is collected from a real-world source (Stack Overflow answers). However, the quality and usefulness ratings in the SDE dataset are not provided by professional human raters, but rather by users who vote on answers to Stack Overflow questions. While the quality ratings are provided by professional human rators, the usefulness ratings are provided by users who are not professional raters, and may not be as well-trained in evaluating language quality as professional raters. Despite this limitation, the SDE dataset provides a valuable resource for studying human preferences for language, and we use it in our experiments.
Aligning models with human preferences
The field of model alignment aims to automatically align one model with human preferences, typically by training both the model and a loss function that measures the difference between the model's output and human preferences. Prior work has used multiple different loss functions to align models with human preferences. Some of the earliest work used the cross-entropy loss, but this loss function can become unstable when the gap between the model's distribution and human preferences is large (Chen et al., 2017;Wu et al., 2018). Other works have used the reinforcement learning objective (Ganin et al., 2016;Wu et al. 2018;Yue et al., 2019;Liu et al., 2020). However, these reinforcement learning objectives can be difficult to optimize, especially for large models.
