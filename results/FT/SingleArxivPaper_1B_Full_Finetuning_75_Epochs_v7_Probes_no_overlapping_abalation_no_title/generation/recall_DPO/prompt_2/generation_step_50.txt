The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for optimizing language models with direct human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO uses an objective function that is simple to compute, scalable to large datasets, and strongly preferred by humans in preference experiments. We evaluate DPO on several tasks, including summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis. On summarization, DPO achieves state-of-the-art results while maintaining human performance or better in nearly all experiments. On QA, DPO models that are half the size of existing state-of-the-art models achieve performance that is, in some cases, equivalent to models trained on hundreds of billions of tokens with traditional objectives. On sentiment analysis, DPO models are as fluent as state-of-the-art models trained on reward optimization objectives while being up to 40% smaller.
