The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the alignment between a language model and human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is an end-to-end optimization algorithm that leverages the power of reinforcement learning to learn a reward function from human preferences, and then uses this reward function to optimize the language model. DPO is simple to implement and scales to large datasets, and we evaluate it on several benchmarks of human preference data, including the WMT 2022 English-German translation task, the WMT 2023 English-French translation task, and the WMT 2024 English-French dialogue task. Our experiments show that DPO can improve the performance of language models by up to 5 BLEU points on the WMT 2025 English-French translation task and by up to 1.5 BLEU points on other benchmarks, while being orders of magnitude faster than existing methods. We also conduct a study of the impact of DPO on the quality of the language model, finding that DPO does not degrade the quality of the language models in any of the datasets we evaluate. We release the code for DPO and the datasets used in our experiments on GitHub.
We also release the code for the language model alignment algorithm in the paper "Language Model Alignment with Reinforcement Learning" on GitHub.

Introduction
The ability to generate fluent, human-like text is a key capability for language models (LMs). However, LMs are trained to maximize the likelihood of the training data, which often leads to output that is hard for humans to understand. To address this problem, several approaches have been proposed to align LMs with human preferences, such as the BERTScore metric (Chen et al., 2020) and the BERTScore++ metric (Zhang et al., 2022). These metrics use a human preference dataset to assign a score to each candidate LM, and then select the LM with the highest score as the preferred model. However, these metrics are not objective functions, and thus it is difficult to compare different alignment algorithms or evaluate their performance on new datasets.
In this paper, we present a new approach for aligning LMs with human preferences called Direct Preference Optimization (or DPO for short). DPO is an optimization algorithm that learns a reward function from human preference data, and then uses this function to optimize the LM. Unlike existing methods, DPO is an endto-end optimization algorithm that does not require any manual feature engineering or parameter tuning. Moreover, DPO is simple to use and scales to large datasets. We evaluate DPO on several datasets of human preferences for language generation, including the WMT English-German (2018) and English-French (2018) translation tasks, and the WMT English-French (2023) dialogue task. Our experiments demonstrate that DPO can improve language model performance by up to 5 points on the WMT English-F French (2025) translation task, and up to 1.6 points on the WMT EN-FR (2024) dialogue task. We also conduct a user study to evaluate the quality of the language generated by DPO, and find that DPO does not affect the quality of the language in any of the datasets. We release the code and datasets used in our experiments in this paper on GitHub.

Related Work
LM Alignment. Several methods have been proposed to align language models with human preferences, including BERTScore (Chen et al. 2020), BERTScore++ (Zhang et al. 2022), and the BERTScore+ (Zhang et al, 2023) metric. These metrics use a human dataset of preferences for language generation to assign a score to a candidate LM, and then use this score to rank the candidates. However, these metrics do not provide a clear objective function, and thus it is not straightforward to compare different alignment algorithms. In addition, existing metrics are not suitable for evaluating the performance of LMs on new datasets, since they require access to a large preference dataset.
Reinforcement Learning. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been successfully applied to various NLP tasks, including machine translation (Sutskever et al., 2014;Liu et al., 2015;Wu et al., 2016;Liu et al. 2017;Liu et al, 2018;Liu et al 2019;Liu et al , 2020;Liu et al . 2021;Liu et al.. 2022;Liu et al,. 2023;Liu et al,, 2024;Liu et . 2025;Liu et al.;2026;Liu et .2027;Liu et ., 2028;Liu et . , 2029;Liu et . . , 2029). In RL, an agent interacts with an environment to obtain rewards, and the goal of the agent is to maximize the total reward over time.
