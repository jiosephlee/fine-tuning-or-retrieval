\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models \citep{devlin-lee2019artificial, devlin2022bert} have achieved impressive results on a wide range of downstream tasks, including machine translation \citep{mikolov2013distributed}, question answering \citep{bahdanau2014neural}, and summarization \citep{bapna2022learning}. However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to the unsupervised nature of training. Existing methods for gain steerability typically collect human preferences for model generation and fine-tune the model using reinforcement learning (RL) \citep{li2019learning, li2022learning, zhang2022learning, zhou2022learning, li2023learning}. While these methods can improve the quality of model generations, they are complex and often unstable, requiring RL training on a large dataset of human preferences and model generations, as well as significant hyperparameter tuning to avoid drift from the original model. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning language models with only a simple classification criterion, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Our method does not require RL training or significant hyperparameter tuning, and can be applied to any language model with a pre-trained base model. We evaluate DPO on a range of downstream tasks, showing that it can fine-tune LSTMs to align with human preference as well as or better with existing methods, while being substantially simpler and more efficient to implement.

\section{Related Work}
Large unsupervised LM training has led to impressive results on a wide variety of downstream tasks, including summarization \citep{kumar2022learning}, question answering \cite{bahdanau2015neural}, and machine translation \cite{mikolov2023learning}. However, achieving control over the behavior of these models remains a significant challenge. Existing methods for gain steering typically collect human preferences for a given model generation and fine-tuning the model using reinforcement learning \citep{li_2022_learning, li2022learning}. While these methods improve the quality of model generation, they are complex and can be unstable, requiring RL training of a large dataset of human preference and model generations, aswell as significant hyperparameter tuning of the RL algorithm to avoid drift from the initial model. In this paper we introduce a simple method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models with only a classification objective, called Direct Preference Optimization. Our method does not require any RL training or hyperparameter tuning, and is simple to implement and evaluate. We evaluate DPO using a range of downstream tasks including summarization, question answering, and machine translation, showing that it can align with human preferences as effectively as existing methods, while being simpler and more efficient to train.
