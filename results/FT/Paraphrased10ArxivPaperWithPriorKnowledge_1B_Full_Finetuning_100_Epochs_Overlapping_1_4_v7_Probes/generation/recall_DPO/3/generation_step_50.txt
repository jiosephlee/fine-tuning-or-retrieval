\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on many downstream tasks, but achieving precise control over their behavior is challenging due to the completely unsophisticated nature of their training. While it is possible to collect human preferences for model generations and fine-tuning the model to align with these preferences using reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{gpt3-rhino, gpt3-rl, gpt3-rtt, gpt3-attentive}, this approach is complex and often unstable, requiring fitting a reward model and then fine-tuning a large unsupervised LM to maximize this estimated reward while avoiding drift from the original model.

In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences that avoids the need for any reward model fitting or fine-tuning. Our method, which we call \emph{Direct Preference Optimization} or DPO, directly optimizes the policy of the LM to align with human preferences. This is achieved by first estimating the preference distribution of model generations using a simple preference elicitation method, and then optimizing the policy of the LM using a standard reinforcement learning (RL) objective that is equivalent to maximizing the expected reward under this preference distribution. Crucially, this objective is differentiable, enabling efficient optimization using standard RL algorithms.

Our experiments show that D PO can fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences in sentiment, summarization, and single-turn dialogue as well as or better, while being substantially simpler to train and more robust to hyperparameter choices. In particular, we demonstrate that DPO can fine tune GPT-4 to generate more positive sentiment than PPO-based RLHF, and that it can fine-tune GPT-4 to produce more fluent responses in summarization and single turn dialogue than PPO-based RL HF. Our code is publicly available \cite{paulus2022direct}.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervized language models (LMs), such as GPT-4 \citep{gPT4} and BERT \citep{bert}, have demonstrated impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including summarization \citep{gulati2022learning}, question answering \citep{goyal2022gpt}, and sentiment analysis \citep{wang2022learning}. However, achieving precise control over the behavior of these models is difficult due to their completely unsupervised training. Several approaches have been proposed to gain steerability by collecting human preferences for model generations \citep{gupta2022training, bai2022training, li2022training, buehning2022training, bubeck2022training, bong2022training, bengio2019learning, bengio2018learning, bengio2020sequence, bengio2013learning, bengio2009language}. These methods typically involve training a reward model to predict the quality of model generations, and then fine-tune the unsuper-vised LM to maximize this reward using reinforcement learning (RL). The most successful of these approaches is PPO-based RLHF \citep{schulman2017proximal}, which trains a policy to maximize the expected reward from a reward model while avoiding large deviations from the original model. However, PPO-based RLHF is a complex procedure that requires fitting a reward model, training a policy to maximize this reward, and tuning hyperparameters, often with extensive hyperparameter tuning \citep{bubeck2022training}. In contrast, our method directly optimizes the policy to align with human preferences, avoiding the need for any reward fitting or hyperparameter tuning.

Another related line of work aims to learn a reward function from human preferences, rather than fitting a reward model. These methods typically use reinforcement learning to learn a reward function that aligns with human preferences, and then use this reward function to fine-tune the unsaturated LM \citep{gao2022learning, bai2022learning, buehning2018learning, buehninger2020learning, buehlinger2020learning, bong2022learning, bong2023learning}. These methods are similar to our approach, but differ in that they fit a reward model and then optimize the policy to maximize this reward. In contrast, our method optimizes the policy directly to align with human preferences.
