The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a simple algorithm, that given a language model and a dataset of human preferences over model generated completions, produces a new model that is expected to generate completions preferred by humans at least as much as the input model, while also being more sample efficient for the RLHF pipeline. The algorithm is theoretically justified with a novel application of an off-policy control learning theorem to the RLHF setting. We believe that the theorem may be of independent interest as it provides a theoretical justification for a type of offline RLHF algorithm, which has been used in practice but not theoretically analyzed. The paper also includes comprehensive experiments on both synthetic and real preference datasets, demonstrating the sample efficiency of the algorithm and the ability of the learned reward to generalize to out-of-distribution prompts.
