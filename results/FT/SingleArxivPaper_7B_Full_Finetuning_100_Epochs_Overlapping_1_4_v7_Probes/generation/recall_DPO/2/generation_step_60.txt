The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a simple and effective algorithm, that of preference optimization, for aligning language modelswith user preferences. Given a dataset of human preferences over model generated sentences, the algorithm identifies a model that both captures the structure in the dataset and assigns high probability to the preferred completions while assigning low probability to dispreferred completions. The key insight of the algorithm is to view the problem as a standard reinforcement learning problem, where the reward function is given by the human preferences and the model is the agent. Unlike in standard RL, however, the preference dataset provides us with an estimate of the unknown reward function and we can therefore reuse favorite RL algorithms like PPO out-of-the-box. The advantage of viewing the problem in this way is that we can leverage off-the-shelf RL algorithms and off-the-shelf RL datasets, fitting a model to the preference data and optimizing it with PPO simultaneously. In experiments, the method exceeds the performance of strong baselines like RLHF and RL-GPT while being simpler to implement and train.
