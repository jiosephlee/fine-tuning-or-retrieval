The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a simple and effective algorithm, that of preference optimization, for aligning language modelswith user preferences. Given a dataset of human preferences over model generated sentences, the algorithm identifies a model that both captures the structure in the dataset and assigns high probability to preferred pairs while assigning low probability to dispreferred pairs. The key insight of the algorithm is to view the problem as a standard RLHF problem, but with an unknown reward function that can be estimated using the preference data. The paper derives theoretical backing for the method, providing guarantee on the quality of the optimal solution that can be computed in practice. Extensive experiments show that the method learns from preferences as well as or better than existing methods, using much less data. Notably, it learns from only a few hundred preferences samples in some cases, generalizing from very limited human feedback. The code and models are available at
