The advanced stuff

Now let’s get to the more interesting stuff. How is the preference dataset created? There are two parts to this. First, you need a dataset of human preferences. This is created by asking humans to rank pairs of prompts according to how much they prefer one to the other. Second, you need to extract preference distributions from the language model. This is done by sampling pairs of prompts and computing the ratio of times the model predicts the first prompt preferred over the second prompt.

An important practical issue is that of scale. There are two scaling issues. First, computing the RL loss for all pairs of prompts in the preference dataset is computationally impractical. The paper solves this with a two level sampling scheme. At the first level, the model is only evaluated on a sample of the preference pairs. The sampling is done to approximately preserve the underlying preference distribution. At the second level, an importance weight is computed for each sampled preference pair based on how well the language model predicts the true human preference for that pair. The RL loss is then computed based on the sampled preference pairs, weighted by their importance weights. The second scaling issue is that of the RL optimization. This is handled by clipping the rewards given to the language model to ensure they remain below a maximum value.
