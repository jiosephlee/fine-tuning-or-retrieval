What is RLHF?

RLHF is a framework for training language models with reinforcement learning. It stands for "Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences". The basic idea is to use human feedback to train a language model to produce the responses that humans prefer. This is in contrast to traditional supervised learning from human written prompts, where the model is only rewarded for producing the exact correct answer.

The RLHF framework consists of three main components: a language model, a reward function, and a reinforcement learning algorithm. The language model is trained on a dataset of human-written prompts and responses, and is used to generate additional synthetic prompts and responses. The reward function measures how well the model's responses align with human preferences, and the reinforcement learning algorithm optimizes the model's parameters to increase its reward.

How does it work?

The RLHF pipeline is as follows:

  1. Sample prompts $x$ from the dataset of prompts and human-written responses $D = \{x^{(i)}, y_w^{(i)}, y_l^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$ where $y_w$ is the preferred response and $y_l$ is dispreferred.
  2. For each prompt, sample responses $z$ from the language model $p_{\theta}(z \mid x)$ and compute the reward $r(x, z)$. The default reward is the preference score, which is the probability of the preferred response $y_w$ minus the probability of the dispreferred response $y_l$: $r(x, z)=\beta\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_w \mid x)}{p_{\theta(y_l \mid x)} for some $\beta$. Other rewards can also be used, such as the agreement reward, which simply measures the probability of $y_w$ minus a constant.

Apply reinforcement learning algorithm to optimize the language model parameters $\theta$ to increase the reward: $\max_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim D, z\sim p_{\theta}(z\mid x)}\bigl[r(x, z)\bigr] - \beta\beta\mathbb{D}_{\textrm{KL}}\bigl[p_{\theta}(z mid x)\mid \mid p_z(z\mid x)\bigr]$ where $p_z$ is the prior distribution, often just the uniform distribution.

Why is it better than supervised learning?

Supervised learning from human prompts is a popular way to train language models of increasing scale. However, there are a few issues with this approach. First, the prompts in the training dataset may not be representative of the prompts that the model will encounter in the real world. Second, the ground truth answers in the dataset may not be the best possible answers, but simply the best of the answers written by humans. Third, the supervised objective does not take into account the confidence of the model's answers, potentially leading to models that are correct but hesitant.

RLHF addresses all of these issues. By optimizing for a reward function that reflects human preferences, rather than a label, the model can learn to produce the best answers, even if they are not in the training data. The KL-divergence constraint in the RLHF objective helps to ensure that the model remains distributionally similar to the prior, helping to avoid mode-collapse to a single high-reward answer. Finally, the RLHF framework is easily extensible to incorporate additional constraints or objectives, such as those related to safety or diversity.
