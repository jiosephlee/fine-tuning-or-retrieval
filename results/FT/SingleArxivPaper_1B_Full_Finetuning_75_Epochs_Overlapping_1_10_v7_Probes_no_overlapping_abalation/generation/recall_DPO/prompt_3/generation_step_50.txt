\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models of increasing size and capacity learn to perform a wide range of tasks from reading comprehension (Ranzato et al., 2015; Paşca et al.,2018;Le et al.,2018); summarization (Bai et al.,2018;; question answering (Sutskever et al.,2014;Ranzato et al.2015;Le et al., 2018); and even playing high-quality video games (Shen et al.,2018a;Liu et al.,2019). However, while these models achieve impressive results, they are trained using only self-supervised learning algorithms (Gpt3, BERT-large, XLNet, etc.) that do not directly utilize human feedback or explicit rewards. As a result, it is not possible to easily fine-tune these models to align with specific preferences or skills, nor are they ready for applications where control over the model's behavior is necessary, such as in user interface design (Kiros et al., 2013;Salakhutdinov and Hinton, 2013;Zhang et al., 2019).

In this paper we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models with human feedback. Like previous methods (e.g. PPO-based RLHF; Pan et al., 2020;Gong et al., 2021), DPO uses a reward model learned from human feedback to guide the fine-tuning of the language model, but DPO avoids the need for sampling from or optimizing the underlying reinforcement learning (RL) policy. Instead, DPO directly optimizes for the reward model parameters, achieving its main advantage by expressing the optimization as a standard classification problem. We can solve this classification problem using any off-the-shelf classification algorithm, making DPO highly flexible and general. We evaluate several different algorithms for the classification task, finding that a simple logistic regression classifier trained on a small dataset of aligned human-labeled prompts and responses performs as well or better than more complex algorithms such as GPT-4's `fine-tuned' REINFORCE (Williams and Williams, 1992) and a variant of REINFORCE with a learned reward function (Bai et al, 2020). We also compare DPO against several other methods for fine-tuning large language models with human feedback, including PPO-based RLHF (Pan et al., 2022); an extension of PPO that leverages a reward model learned from feedback to guide the fine tuning of the language model (Gong et al.,2021); and methods that train a separate reinforcement learning agent to produce responses of high quality (Schulman et al.,2017;Yue et al.,2020;Gong and Barzilay,2022). Our experiments show that DPO is generally the most effective method for aligning language models with human preferences, and is on par or better than PPO-based RLHF for fine-tuning and response quality in tasks such as summarization and single-turn dialog. In addition, we conduct a systematic comparison of various hyperparameters used in different methods, finding that DPO does not require any significant hyperparameters beyond those used by PPO-based RLHF.
