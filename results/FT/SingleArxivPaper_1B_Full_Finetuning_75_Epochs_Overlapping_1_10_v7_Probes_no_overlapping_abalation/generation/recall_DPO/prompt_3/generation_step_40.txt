\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models learn to perform a wide variety of tasks from text alone, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training. Existing methods for gaining steerability generally fall into one of two categories. The first class of approaches leverages the ability of LMs to learn implicit rewards from human feedback, typically reinforcement learning from human interaction (RLHI; \cite{ziegler2022learning}). In RLHF, a reward model is first fit to the human preferences, and the corresponding optimal policy is then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to produce generations with desired properties, without drifting too far from its original form. However, RLHF is a non-trivial reinforcement learning problem, involving both a reward model and a policy to sample from the reward model, both of which must be learned from the same dataset of human preferences and model generations. In this paper we introduce a single-stage method, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that avoids both the reward model and the policy, fitting a single parameterization of the reward function that directly produces the optimal policy.

The second class of steerability methods leverages the ability of unsupervised LMs to implicitly learn from a limited set of prompts, and then fine-tunes the LM to perform similarly to a reference model on these prompts~(e.g.~\cite{mishra-etal-2022-learning, bai2022learning, ouyang2022training}). These methods typically optimize the unsupervised LM for a quantitative measure of performance on the prompts, such as the mean reciprocal rank, and then fine-tune the model to match the preferred performance of the reference model on a set of additional, human-generated prompts. This \textit{prompts-first} approach enables the fine-tuning of models to new prompts without supervision, but the need to train multiple LMs for each prompt pair can be prohibitive, especially for high-quality human feedback on many prompts.

In this paper we introduce DPO, a single-stage steerability method that does not require a reward model or a reference policy. DPO first parameterizes the reward function associated with the unsupervised LM of interest, using a technique we call \textit{(reinforcement learning with) preference labels}. We then show that the reward function can be optimized using a simple binary cross-entropy loss, effectively turning the unsupervised LM into a generative model over rewards. We refer to this loss as the \textit{Direct preference optimization} loss, or DPO for short. We theoretically justify the use of a binary cross-entropy loss for the reward parameterization, and show that the optimal reward function produced by DPO matches the optimal policy learned from a reward model fit to human preferences. Thus, DPO produces the same final policy as existing methods (such as RLHF) but without the need for a reward function or a reference policy. We empirically evaluate DPO on a variety of tasks, including sentiment modulation, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, against several existing steerability methods. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item We introduce a new parameterization for the reward function in RLHF that enables us to efficiently optimize the reward function with a simple binary cross-entropy loss, producing the same optimal policy as existing methods but without the need for either a reward model or a separate policy to optimize.
    \item We develop DPO, a single stage steerability method that fine-tunes an unsatisfied LM with only a simple binary cross- entropy loss, matching or improving the performance of existing methods on sentiment modulation, summarization and single-turn dialog.
\end{itemize}

Our contributions include a new parameterization of reward functions in RLHF, a new single-stage steerability method DPO that does not require a rewards model or a reference policy, and extensive experiments showing that DPO is at least as effective as existing methods for fine-tuning unsupervised LMs with human preferences.
