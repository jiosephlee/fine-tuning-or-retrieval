The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for optimizing language models with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO leverages the fact that language models can be represented as reward models, and that preferences can be represented as rewards. We show that under certain conditions, the preferences of a human annotator can be represented as a reward function, and that the language model can be optimized to maximize the human reward. We also show that the preference-reward mapping can be learned from data, and that the resulting reward function is a valid reward function for the underlying task. We evaluate DPO on two tasks: taste testing and movie recommendation. On the taste testing task, DPO outperforms several existing methods, including baselines that directly optimize the language model to maximize human ratings. On the movie recommendation task, DPO outperform several existing methods, including a model that optimizes to maximize the logarithm of human preferences. Our experiments show that DPO is generally more effective than existing methods for aligning language models to human preferences.
