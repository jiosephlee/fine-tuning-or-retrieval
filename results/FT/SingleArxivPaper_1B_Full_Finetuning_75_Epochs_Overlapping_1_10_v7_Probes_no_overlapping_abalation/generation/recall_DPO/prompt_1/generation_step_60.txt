The paper introduces a new optimization paradigm called "direct preference optimization" (DPO). DPO is a generalization of reinforcement learning (RL) that allows for the use of any reward function, not just RL rewards. DPO can be used to train models that generalize well to previously unseen tasks, but at the cost of increased computational complexity. In this paper, the authors show how to use DPO to train language models that answer questions correctly with high probability, without explicitly training for that purpose. Instead, the model is trained to maximize its expected reward from answering questions, using a novel loss function that balances exploitation of high-reward answers with exploration of new, potentially high-reward answer paths. The authors also present a theoretical analysis of the DPO algorithm, showing that it converges to a maximum expected reward policy under mild assumptions about the reward function. Finally, the authors implement a DPO-based language model that can answer questions about news articles, and compare it to two other DPO-based models and a standard RL-based model. The results show that the DPO model can produce answers as accurate or more accurate than the other models, while being up to 10 times faster to train.
