After reading the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model", I've learned a lot about DPO. Let me tell you everyhting I've learned. First, the paper introduces a new optimization objective, which is a weighted sum of rewards. The weights are chosen by the authors, and are optimized by DPO. The reward function is defined as: $$R(p;\theta) = \beta \log \frac{p(\theta}{\pi}) + (1-\beta) \log (1-\pi p)$$ where $\beta$ is a parameter controlling the relative importance of the two terms. The $\beta$ term is called the "soft deviance" and it measures how much the reward function prefers the ground-truth policy $\pi$ to the sampled policy $p(\theta)$. The policy $\pi$ is sampled from the language model $p(\theta)$, which is assumed to be a good model of the user's preferences, i.e. the user prefers the truth $\pi$ to all sampled policies $p(\theta) $. The goal of DPO is to find a policy $\pi^*$ that maximizes the reward $R(\pi; \theta)$, while satisfying the constraint $\pi \in P$. The constraint is necessary to avoid sampling from the user's distribution, which may not be known. In other words, the DPO algorithm samples from the distribution $P = \pi \mid \theta$.

The DPO algorithm

The DPO algorithm is simple and elegant. It consists of two phases. In the first phase, the distribution $P$ is marginalized out, and the optimization is performed on the remaining distribution $Q$. In the second phase, the distribution $Q$ is marginalized out. The DPO algorithm is as follows:

  1. Sample $N$ policies $p_1, \ldots, p_N$ from $P$;
  2. for each policy $p_i$ do: compute the soft deviance $D_i(\pi; \theta) = R(p_i;\theta) - \beta \log \dfrac{p_i}{\pi}$;
  3. optimize $Q$ to minimize $D(\pi; \theta)-D_i(\pi; {\theta})$ for each $i$;
  4. sample $N$ policies $q_1, \ldot $ from $Q$;
  5. for each policy $q_i$ do: compute $D_i(q_i; \theta)$;
  6. optimize $Q$ with respect to $q$ to minimize $D_i(q; \theta)-D_j(q; \theta)$ for each $i \ne j$;
  7. sample $N$ new policies $r_1, \ldo $ from $Q$.

The algorithm is simple, but it is not easy to analyze. In the next section, I will present some results from a paper that analyzes the DPO algorithm.

Analysis of the DPO algorithm

The paper "Direct Preference Optimization with Online Learning" by Gu et al. provides some theoretical analysis of the DPO algorithm. The analysis is based on the following two lemmas:

Lemma 1: The marginal distribution $Q$ is a valid distribution over policies, i.e. $P(\pi \mid \theta) = Q(\pi \mid \thet)$ for all $\pi$.
