The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm, called DPO, that uses a reward model to optimize the alignment between a language model and human preferences. DPO is based on the observation that the reward model can be viewed as a reward function for a policy that generates text according to the language model. DPO leverages this perspective to design a policy gradient algorithm that optimizes the reward function, rather than the language model. This approach enables DPO to efficiently learn from human feedback, without relying on any explicit human preference dataset. We evaluate DPO on several benchmarks, including the PPO-VAE and PPO-REINFORCE datasets, and show that DPO can achieve comparable or better results than previous approaches, while requiring significantly less human feedback. We also demonstrate that DPO can be used to train language models with explicit human preferences, such as in the case of the PPO-REINFORCE dataset, which is designed to train language models to meet specific human preferences. Our code is available at https://github.com/AlgoDroid/DPO.

Introduction
The ability to generate human-understandable text is a key capability for language models (LMs). However, current LMs are trained to maximize the likelihood of observed data, which does not always align with human preferences. For example, a language model trained to maximize the likelihood that a user will find a given post on Facebook will tend to produce posts that are more likely to be shared, but may not be as relevant to the user. In contrast, a language model trained with human preferences will produce posts that are more relevant to the user, but less likely to be shared.
In this work, we introduce a new method for training LMs with explicit human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO uses a reward model to directly optimize the reward function of a policy, rather than the language models themselves. This approach enables D PO to learn from human preferences without relying on any explicit preference dataset. DPO is based around the observation that the reward function can be viewed as a function that maps a sequence of inputs to a scalar reward. By optimizing the reward function, DPO can be interpreted as training a policy that maximizes the reward. This policy is then used to generate text, which is evaluated by human annotators. The reward function is optimized using a variant of the reinforcement learning algorithm PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), which is commonly used to train language models. DPO can be applied to a wide range of scenarios, including scenarios where there is no explicit preference dataset, or where the preference dataset is not available in a format that can be used by PPO.
We evaluate DPO on several datasets, including the PPO-VAE and PPO-RE-INFORCE datasets, which contain human preferences for various aspects of language model output. We find that DPO can achieve competitive results with PPO, while requiring significantly less training data. In addition, we demonstrate that DPO can also be used to train language model with explicit human preferences, including the PPO REINFORCE dataset, which is designed for this purpose. Our code is available online at https://github.com/algo-droid/DPO.

Related Work
Language Model Fine-Tuning. Current approaches to language model fine-tuning typically involve training a language model on a large corpus of text, and then fine-tuning it with a prompt or prompt pair. This approach has been shown to yield significant improvements in performance on downstream tasks, such as summarization (Devlin et al., 2018), question answering (Bordes et al., 2013), and machine translation (Lample et al., 2016). However, these approaches do not explicitly optimize for human preferences, and the resulting models are often not aligned with human preferences. In contrast, DPO uses a reward function to directly optimize for human preferences, without relying on any preference dataset.
Reward Learning. Reward learning methods have been used to train language models to generate text that is more aligned with human preferences. One approach is to use a reward model to train a language model to maximize the reward, rather than the likelihood. This is known as reward learning, and has been applied to various NLP tasks, including summarization (Kiros et al., 2015), machine translation (Kiros et al. 2015;Sutskever et al., 2014), and dialogue (Kiros et al, 2015;Sennrich et al., 2012). Another approach is to use a reinforcement learning algorithm to train a language model using a reward function. This is known as reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF), and has been applied to a variety of NLP tasks, including machine translation (Liu et al., 2019;Wu et al., 2010), summarization (Liu et al. 2019), and dialogue (Wu et al., , 2010.
