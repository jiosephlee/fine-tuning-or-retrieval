The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the reward function used by DPO, the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm. DPO uses a gradient-based optimization algorithm to find the parameters of a language model that maximize the reward function, but it requires the reward function to be differentiable, which is not the case for many popular reward functions used in practice. In contrast, the new algorithm for DPO, called PPO-DE, directly optimizes the reward function without any assumptions about its differentiability. We evaluate DPO and PPO-DE on several datasets of human preferences for summarization, sentiment analysis, and fact checking. For summarization, we compare DPO and PPO against the leading open-source summarization algorithms RoBERTa-Large and ALICE, as well as the proprietary Google Neural Machine Translation (NMT) model GPT-4. DPO produces models with performance comparable to or better than the state-of-the-art, while being orders of magnitude faster than existing methods. For sentiment analysis, we compare DPO against BERT-Fact, a pre-trained BERT model fine-tuned on a fact-checking dataset. DPO produces models that are more accurate and more aligned with human preferences than BERT-Fact. For fact checking, we compare DPO with GPT-4 and a proprietary model from Google. DPO produces models of comparable quality to GPT-4, but with a much smaller memory footprint and runtime.
