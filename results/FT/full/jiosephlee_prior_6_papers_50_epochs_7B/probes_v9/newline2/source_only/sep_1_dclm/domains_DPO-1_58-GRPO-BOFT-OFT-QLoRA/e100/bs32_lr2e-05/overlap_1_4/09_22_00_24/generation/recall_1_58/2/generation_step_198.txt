# What is the problem?

The problem addressed by the paper is the high cost of training and running large language models. The cost of training and running LLMs is mainly due to the high memory consumption and the high arithmetic operations count. To reduce the cost, researchers have proposed various methods such as knowledge distillation, quantization, model compression, and model factorization. Knowledge distillation reduces the size of the teacher model by training a student model to match the teacher's performance. Model compression reduces the model size by removing or merging parameters. Model factorization splits a large model into multiple smaller models that can be run in parallel to emulate the behavior of the original model. Quantization reduces the precision of weights and activations to lower-bit formats to reduce memory usage and accelerate computation.

Among these methods, quantization has been widely used to reduce the memory footprint and accelerate computation. There are two main types of quantization: post-training quantization (PTQ) and bit-pipe. PTQ converts a full-precision model to a low-precision model without retraining. Bit-pipe is a method that inserts low-bit operators between full-precision operators to reduce the precision of the entire model. Both PTQ and bit-pipe face challenges when reducing the precision below 8 bits. PTQ below 8 bits often degrades the performance significantly. Bit-pipe below 4 bits faces difficulties in implementing efficient low-bit matrix multiplication.

# What is BitNet?

It is inspired by the block-sparse structure of weight matrices in LLMs. The block-sparse structure means that many elements in a weight matrix are zero, and non-zero elements are clustered together.
