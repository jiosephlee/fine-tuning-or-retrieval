DPO is a Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) algorithm that directly optimizes the language model on preferred completions without the usual intermediate reward model. It does so by constructing a preference distribution over completions and then maximizing this distribution under the language model while simultaneously minimizing it under overtaken completions. 

In contrast to previous RLHF algorithms, DPO does not rely on a reward model but uses a learned social preference model to directly optimize the language model based on human preferences. This makes DPO a more direct and straightforward method for training language models based on human preferences.

# How does DPO work?
DPO works by constructing a preference distribution $P_{\phi}(c_1 \mid c_2, o)$ over completions $c_1$ and $c_2$ given an overtaken completion $o$. It then optimizes a parameter $\phi$ of the language model such that it maximizes this distribution. At the same time, it also optimizes $\phi$ such that the language model minimizes the same preference distribution. These two objectives are symmetric and the second one is simply the negation of the first one, so the "secret reward model" is just the language model with the same preference structure. The two optimization objectives are:

Maximize:
$$
\log \mathbb{D}_{\phi}(c_2 \mid c_1, o) = \log \left( \frac{1}{Z(o)} \beta(o) \exp\left(\frac{1}{Z{o}} \mathcal{L}(c_2 \ mid \ o, c_1)\right) \right)
$$

Minimize:
$$
\mathbb{D}_{-\phi}(c_1\mid c_2, c_2) = \log \frac{1}{\mathbb{D}_\phi(c_2 \mid c _1, c_2)} = \log \left(\frac{1 + \mathbb{D}_ \phi(c_1 \mid c _2, c_2)}{2}\right)
$$

where $\mathbb{D}_ {\phi}(c_2\mid c_1, c_2) $ denotes the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the two completions $c_2$ and $c_1$ with respect to the parameter $\phi$.

The preference distribution $\mathbb{D}_{ \phi}(c_2,c_1)$ is constructed as the sum of three components:

1. A KL-divergence penalty: $\mathbb{D}^K(c_2,c_1) = \beta(o) \left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(\frac{1+\pi_2}{2}\mid \mid c_1\right) - \mathrm{KL}\left(\frac{1+\π_1}{2}\mid \middle| c_2\right)\right]$,

where $\π_1$ and $\π_2$ are the softmaxed outputs of the language model at completions $c_ 1$ and $c _2$, respectively.

2. A similarity penalty: $\mathbb{T}(c_2 \ Mid \ c_1, o)$,

where $\mathbb{T}(x_1, x_2) = \exp\left(\mathrm{TLU}(x_1, o)\right)\exp\left(\mathrm{TLU}(x_2, o)\right)$ and $\mathrm{TLU}(w, o) = \lambda \mathrm{Cross-Entropy}(w, o)$.

3. A length penalty: $\mathbb{l}(c_2 \Mid \ c_1, c_o)$,

where $\mathcal{l}(x) = \mu\left(\mathrm{Len}(x) - 1\right)$ and $\mu$ is a linear function.

The training data for DPO is generated by running samples from the current model $p_{\phi}(o)$ through the social preference model $q_{\psi}(c_2 \ MID \mid \mid c_0, o)$, where $c_0$ is a prompt from a predefined prompt pool. This means that $q_{\psi}$ simulates human preferences in generating the overtaken completions $c_0$ and the preference label $y_{\psi}(c) = 1$ if $q_{\psi }(c_2 \mid \mid c_o) > q_{\psi}(c_{01} \mid \mid c_{o})$, where $c_{01}$ is the other completion in the pair.
