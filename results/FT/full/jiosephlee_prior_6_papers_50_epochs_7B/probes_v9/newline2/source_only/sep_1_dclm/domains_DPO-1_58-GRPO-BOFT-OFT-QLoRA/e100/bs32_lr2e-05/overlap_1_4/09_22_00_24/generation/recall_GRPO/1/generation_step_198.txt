# What is DeepSeekMath?

DeepSeekMath is an open-source project that aims to push the limits of mathematical reasoning in open language models. It is based on DeepSeek-Coder-v1.5-BiLSTM-Base, which is a 7B-scale open-source language model trained with the DeepSeek-Coder framework. DeepSeekMath improves the mathematical reasoning ability of DeepSeek-Coder-v2.5 by leveraging external mathematical knowledge and training with mathematical instruction-following datasets.

DeepSeekMath is released under the MIT License, which means it can be used for both commercial and non-commercial purposes as long as the copyright notice and disclaimer are included in the distribution.

# How does DeepSeekMath work?

DeepSeekMath leverages external mathematical knowledge and trains the model with instruction-following datasets. The external mathematical knowledge is obtained from Common Crawl, which contains billions of web pages. DeepSeekMath extracts mathematical expressions from web pages using a rule-based approach. After filtering out low-quality mathematical expressions, the high-quality ones are used to train a mathematical knowledge graph. The mathematical knowledge graph is then integrated into the DeepSeek-Coder-v3.0-base model.

To improve the instruction-following ability of the base model, DeepSeekMath trains the model on the Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMLU) dataset, which includes 57 multiple-choice tasks covering diverse subjects such as U.S. history, computer science, law, and more. In addition, DeepSeekMath also trains the model on the MATH dataset from OpenAI's competition dataset, which includes arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and calculus problems.

After integrating the external mathematical knowledge and training on instruction-following datasets, DeepSeekMath evaluates the mathematical reasoning ability of the model using the GSM8K and MATH datasets. GSM8K is a benchmark for mathematical reasoning in language models. It consists of 8,000 questions curated from online forums such as StackExchange. Each question requires multi-step reasoning to arrive at the answer. DeepSeekMath evaluates the model's performance on GSM8K using the original evaluation script. The MATH dataset is also used to evaluate the model's performance.

# What makes DeepSeekMath special?

Open-source

DeepSeekMath is an entirely open-source project. The source code, pre-trained models, and the mathematical knowledge graph are freely available on GitHub under the MIT License. This means anyone can use, modify, and distribute DeepSeekMath without any restrictions.

Rule-based extraction of mathematical knowledge from Common Crawl

Common Crawl is a collection of web pages crawled by several web crawlers. It contains a large amount of mathematical knowledge. However, most of the mathematical expressions on web pages are not structured, which makes it difficult for models to use them. DeepSeekMath addresses this problem by extracting mathematical expressions from web pages with a rule-based approach. First, the text spans containing mathematical expressions are extracted. Then, the extracted spans are matched against patterns to filter out low-quality mathematical expressions. High-quality mathematical expressions are then normalized and stored in a mathematical knowledge graph.

Integration of mathematical knowledge into the base model

The mathematical knowledge graph extracted from Common Crawl is integrated into the DeepSeek model using a knowledge distillation approach. The mathematical knowledge graph serves as the teacher, and the base model serves as the student. During the integration process, the student model is trained to imitate the teacher model's output. This allows the base model to reason about mathematical expressions directly from the mathematical knowledge graph.

Training on instruction-following datasets

DeepSeekMath enhances the base model with instruction-following capabilities by training it on the MMLU dataset, which includes 58 multiple-choice tasks covering diverse disciplines such as computer science, law, biology, and more. In contrast, the base model only has natural language understanding capabilities. By training the model on the MMLU data, DeepSeekMath significantly improves the model's ability to follow instructions and perform multi-step reasoning to arrive the answer.

Evaluation on the GSM8K and the MATH dataset

DeepSeekMath evaluates the performance of the enhanced model on two benchmarks: GSM8K and the OpenAI MATH dataset. GSM8K is an internal benchmark used to measure the mathematical reasoning ability of language models. It consists 8,000 questions from online forums such as stackexchange.com. The questions require multi-step reasoning to arrive to the answer. The MATH dataset comes from OpenAI's competition data and includes arithmetic, algebra,geometry, and calculus problems. Evaluating the model's performance on these benchmarks allows us to compare DeepSeekMath with other state-of-the-art models.
