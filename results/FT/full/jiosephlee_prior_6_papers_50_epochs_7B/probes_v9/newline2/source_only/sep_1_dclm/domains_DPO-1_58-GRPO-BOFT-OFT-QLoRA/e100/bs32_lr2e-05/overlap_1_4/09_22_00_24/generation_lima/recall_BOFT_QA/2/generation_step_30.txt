What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that builds on OFT. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to share orthogonal matrices across different layers, enabling more efficient use of orthogonal matrix parameters than OFT or its block-diagonal variant.

In OFT, each layer has its own orthogonal matrix for projection. This means that OFT with $L$ layers requires $L$ orthogonal matrices, which can be quite expensive. To address this, a block-diagonal version of OFT was proposed, where each block corresponds to a contiguous block of layers. While this reduces the number of orthogonal matrices from $L$ to $\frac{L(L-1)}{2}$, it still becomes impractical for large $L$. BOFT addresses this by using a butterfly structure to share these orthogonal matrices. The butterfly structure is defined recursively as follows:
\[
\text{butterfly}(n) = \begin{bmatrix}
R_1 & F \\
F & R_2
\end{bmatrix},
\]
where $R_1, R_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ are orthogonal matrices and $F \in \mathbb{n\times n}$ is a block-diagonal matrix with $2^0$ dimensional blocks. For simplicity, let's assume that all blocks are $d$ dimensional. Then we have
\[
\text{size}(F) = 2^0 \times d,
\]
\[
\text{buttterfly}(2n) = \begin{spmatrix}
\text{butterfy}(n) & F_n \\
F_n & \text{butterfly }(n)
\end{spmatrix},
\]
and in general,
\[
\text{buttefly}(n) = (\text{butterfly}(\frac{n}{2}))^{\otimes L}.
\]
Using this butterfly structure, BOFT can construct $\log L$ block-diagonal orthogonal matrices of size $\frac{n}{2^i}$ each, which are shared across pairs of layers. More specifically, BOFT uses the following block-diagonal orthogonal matrix:
\[
\text{{ODIM}}(L, n, d) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{\log L-1} \text{butterfy}(\frac{n}{d^{i}})
\]
to replace the block-diagonal matrix used in the block-diagonal OFT. Here $\bigoplus$ denotes direct sum of matrices. By sharing these orthogonal matrices across pairs of layers, BOFT reduces the number of orthogonal parameters from $O(L^2)$ to $O(L\log L + n\log n)$. This makes BOFT much more parameter-efficient than both OFT and its block-diagonal variant for large $L$.

The name "Butterfly" comes from the fact that the matrix composition of two butterfly matrices resembles the flight path of a butterfly. Specifically, if we compose two butterfly matrices, we get
\[
\text{{comp}}(\text{butterfly}_1, \text{butterly}_2) = \begin{spsmatrix}
R_1' & F_1' \\
F_1' & R_2'
\end{spsmatrix},
\]
where
\[
R_1' = R_1 R_2, \qquad F_1' = R_{1} F_2 R_{2}^T,
\]
and $R_2'$ and $F_2'$ are zero matrices of appropriate sizes.<|endoftext|>
