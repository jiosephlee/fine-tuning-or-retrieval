According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). PPO is an actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithm that is widely used for training open-ended language models. In particular, PPO improves a policy by maximizing the following surrogate objective:
```
\hat{J}_\text{PPO}(π) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[J_0(\pi)] - \beta \frac{\mathbb{V}_{\text{KL}}(π\mid \mid \mu)}{\mathbb{V}(\mu)}
```
where $J_0$ is the reward function, $\mu$ is the old policy, and $\pi$ is the new policy being trained. The term $\mathbb{V}_{KL}(\pi\mid \mid \μ)/\mathbb{V}($μ) is a stability term that prevents the policy from taking values outside of $\mu$. GRPO improves PPO by replacing the variance stabilizing term $\mathbb{D}_{\textrm{Bai}}(\pi\mid \mu)$ with $\mathbb{D}_\text{GRPO}(\pi\mid\mu)$, where
```
\mathbb{D}_{{\rm GRPO}}(π\mid μ) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{D}_{KL}\left[\pi\mid \mid {\rm Mixture}(μ,\alpha,\mathcal{D})\right]\right)
```
and ``\mathcal{D}`` is the data distribution from which the mixture model is constructed. This change has several advantages. First, it does not require estimating the variance of the old policy. Second, it can be interpreted as regularizing the policy using the group relative entropy between the policy and a mixture model of the old policy with group probability greater than 50%. Third, it can be interpreted in information transmission terms as the expected Kullback–Leibler divergence required for the policy to transmit information about the data distribution.<|endoftext|>
