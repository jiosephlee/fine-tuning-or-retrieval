# Orthogonal finetuning (Oft)

Orthogonal finetuning is a simple yet effective method for finetuning neural networks. It was introduced in the paper "Orthogonal Finetuning: A Simple Way to Preserve Pretrained Representations". The key idea is to add a projection step to the gradient update. In the original OFT, the projection is done by subtracting a small multiple of an orthogonal matrix times the pretrained weight matrix. This ensures that the pretrained weight vector is preserved under the finetuning process. Formally, for a pretrained weight matrix $W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$, OFT updates the weight matrix by:

$W = W^0 - s\alpha \odot (I_d \otimes W^0)^T,$

where $s$ is a scalar, $\alpha$ is an orthogonal matrix, and $\odot$ denotes the elementwise multiplication. The projection is applied to the entire weight matrix, so it is also called full-projection OFT. Another variant is the channel-wise projection, where the projection is applied to each channel independently. The channel-wise projection has less impact on the model performance while still preserving the pretrained representation well.

The original OFT is designed for language representation learning. However, it is easily applicable to vision tasks. In the vision domain, OFT is usually applied to the linear layer right before the softmax in classification tasks, or to the fully connected layers in representation learning. In the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion by orthogonal finetuning", the authors apply OFT to both the text encoder and the Visual Decoder in the text-to-image model.

# Text-to-image diffusion models

The basic pipeline is to train a text encoder and a visual decoder jointly. The text encoder is typically a transformer that maps a text prompt to a text embedding.
