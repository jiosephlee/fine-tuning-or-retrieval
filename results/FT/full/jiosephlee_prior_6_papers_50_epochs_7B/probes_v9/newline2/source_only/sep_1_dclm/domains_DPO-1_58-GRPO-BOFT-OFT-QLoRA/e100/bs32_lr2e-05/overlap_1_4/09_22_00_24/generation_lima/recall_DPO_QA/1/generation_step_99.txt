According to the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", what is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a method for training language models to adhere to human preferences while avoiding the instability and sample inefficiency of PPO-style RLHF. The core idea behind DPO is that, while training a language model using preference data, we should not care about the actual reward function that would have generated the preference data, but only about the preferences themselves. In other words, we should train our model to satisfy the revealed preferences encoded in the preference dataset, without trying to infer the implicit rewards that generated those preferences. This is achieved by treating the reward model as a latent variable, and optimizing the language model to maximize the conditional probability of its outputs given samples from the reward model and the preference label. 

Formally, the objective used in DPO is:
\[
\max_{\mathbf{M}} \mathbb{E}_{r \sim \mathcal{R}, x \sim \mathcal{T}(\mathbf{M}; r)} \left[ \sigma(\hat{r}_d(r, \pi(x)), \hat{r}_s(r, x, \pi(x))) \right]
\]
where $\mathcal{T}(\cdot)$ is the language model $\mathbf{M}$, $\mathcal{R}$ is the reward model, $\hat{r}_s$ is the social choice function, $\hat{r}_{d}$ is a function that returns the reward model's prediction for the reward at state $x$, and $\sigma$ is the logistic function.

The key intuition behind DPO is that the reward model is a latent variable, and we can directly optimize the language model to satisfy the preferences by treating the reward model parameters as if they were known. This avoids the need to fit the language model to the conditional distribution $\mathcal{T}([\hat{r}_s,\hat{r}_d] \mid \hat{r}_f)$ where $\hat{r}_f$ is the "fair" reward (the true reward generated by some underlying MDP).<|endoftext|>
