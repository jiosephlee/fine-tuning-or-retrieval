Text-to-image diffusion

Text-to-image diffusion models generate images from text prompts. These models are built on top of diffusion models, which learn to generate images from noise. Diffusion models are trained on pairs of real images and their latent code generated by a denoising autoencoder. During training, the model learns the distribution of real images, and how to generate images from noise.

Diffusion models

Diffusion models are trained on three types of data: real images, generated images, and noise. The real images and generated images come in pairs, where the real image is paired with a generated image created by the denoising autoencoder (DAE). The DAE takes a random noise vector and converts it into an image. The pairs of real and generated images are used to train the diffusion model to generate images from noise using the score function from a generative adversarial network (GAN). The noise vector is the latent code that contains the information needed to generate the image. During training, the diffusion model learns the distribution of the real images and how to convert noise into images that match the real images.

Training a diffusion model

The standard training objective for diffusion models is to minimize the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the joint distribution of real images and generated images and the marginal distribution of generated images. In mathematical terms, the training objective is to minimize
\[
\mathbb{D}_{\textrm{KL}}\big[\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}(x,y)\mid \mid \mathcal{D}_{gen}(y)\big],
\]
where $\mathcal{D}_{r}(x,y)$ is the real data distribution and $\mathcal{D}_g(y)$ is the generated data distribution. This can be written as
\[
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(x,y) \sim \mathcal{D}_r}\log\frac{\mathcal{D}_f(y\mid x)}{\mathcal{D}_{Gen}(y)}.
\]
Here $\mathcal{D_f}(x,y)$ is  the forward diffusion model and $\mathcal{{D}_{Gen}}(y)$ is the generative model. This objective can also be written as minimizing the average cross-entropy loss
\[
\mathbf{E}_{(x,y) \mid \mid \math{D}_r}\left[\log\frac{1}{z}\sum_{y}\mathcal{D}_F(y\mid x)\right],
\]
where the sum is over all possible generated images $z$. In practice, the real image $x$ is sampled from the data distribution, and a generated image $y$ is sampled from the generative model $\mathcal{D_G}$.
