According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning large pretrained models, such as text-to-image diffusion models, without destroying the pretrained model's performance on general tasks. The idea behind OFT is that when you train a model on a new task, you typically update the weights of all neurons in the pretrained model. This can easily destroy the pretrained model's performance because the new task may conflict with the old one. For example, if your pretrained model is good at both classifying images and generating images, then training it on image generation may ruin its classification performance. OFT avoids this problem by only updating a subset of neurons while keeping the rest fixed at their pretrained values. More formally, OFT updates weights using the following rule:
```
new_weights = weight * R,
```
where `R` is an orthogonal matrix (i.e., `R^T R = R R^T = I`). This ensures that the length of each weight vector is preserved (because `||R * x || = ||x||` for any vector `x`). In practice, `R` is generated by first initializing it to `I` (an identity matrix) and then applying gradient updates based on a reconstruction loss. The reconstruction loss compares the output of the pretrained model before and after finetuning, signaling the model to adjust `R` so that it aligns the two outputs. Since `R^T R=I`, `R` is always invertible and the finetuning process can be reversed by multiplying both sides by `R^-1`. This allows OFT to perfectly recover the original model weights, even after extensive finetuning on a specific task.

The key advantage of OFT is that it can be used to finetune models along multiple orthogonal directions. Each direction corresponds to a different task. For instance, you could finetune your text-to-image diffusion model in a "downhill" direction in weight space for one task (generate images of dogs), then back up along the reverse path and then finetune in a different direction (generate images of cats). Because the paths are orthogonal, there is no interference between them. This property is illustrated in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion by orthogonal finetuning".<|endoftext|>
