Text-to-image diffusion

Text-to-image diffusion is the task of generating images from text prompts. The most well-known system for text-to-image diffusion is Stable Diffusion. Stable Diffusion is a generative adversarial network (GAN) that learns a joint distribution over images and text prompts. Given a text prompt, Stable Diffusion samples an image from this joint distribution. Stable Diffusion was trained on a large dataset of images with corresponding text prompts. During training, the model learns the characteristics of images and text prompts in the dataset. After training, the model can generate images for new text prompts because it has learned the relationship between images and text.

How diffusion works

Diffusion is a class of generative models that learn the data distribution by starting from a simple distribution and gradually moving towards the real data distribution. In the case of text-to-image diffusion, the simple distribution is a random noise image. As the model generates images, it gradually moves from random noise towards realistic images. The simplest form of diffusion models is autoregressive diffusion models. Autoregressive diffusion models generate images pixel by pixel using a conditional neural network. At each step, the model predicts the next pixel based on the previous pixels and a text prompt. The predicted pixel is then added to the image. Autoregressive diffusion works well, but it is slow because it generates images pixel by pixel. To address this, fast diffusion models were developed. Fast diffusion models predict a full image at once instead of predicting pixels sequentially. One popular fast diffusion model is Stable Diffusion. Other fast diffusion models include DALL-E2 and Imagen. All diffusion models share the same high-level idea: they learn the data distribution by gradually moving from a simple distribution to the real data distribution.
