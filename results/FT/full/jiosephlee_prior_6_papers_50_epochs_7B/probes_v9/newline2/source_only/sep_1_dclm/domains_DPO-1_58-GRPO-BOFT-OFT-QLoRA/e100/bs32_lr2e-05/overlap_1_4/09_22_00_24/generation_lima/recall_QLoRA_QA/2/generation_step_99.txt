The map is defined as
\[
\hat{w} = \mathrm{round}(w / q),
\]
where $\mathrm{round}(x)$ rounds $x$ to the nearest integer in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^p}$. The quantization error introduced by this mapping is usually expressed as
\[
e = \hat{w} - w.
\]
In many papers, it is assumed that $e$ is small, so if we can bound the norm of $e$ we can use that bound to design algorithms that work with quantization. In particular, if we assume that the norm of $e$
\[
\|e\|_p = \left(\sum_{i=1}^p |e_i|^p\right)^\frac{1}{p},
\]
is small, then we can prove results about algorithms that use quantization. For example, one can show that if $e$ has mean zero and small $\ell_2$ norm, then $\hat{w}$ also has mean zero. This property is often used in proofs about training neural networks with quantized weights.
