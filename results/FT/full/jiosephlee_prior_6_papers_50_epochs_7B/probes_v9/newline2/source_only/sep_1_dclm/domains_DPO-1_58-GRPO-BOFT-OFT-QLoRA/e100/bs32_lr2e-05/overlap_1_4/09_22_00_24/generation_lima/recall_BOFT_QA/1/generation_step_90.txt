According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses orthogonal transformation to update the pretrained weight matrix. The core idea of BOFT is to apply a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix to reshape the pretrained weight matrix. Compared with popular parameter-efficient finetuning methods such as BitFit and LoRA, BOFT can achieve similar performance while having fewer trainable parameters. Moreover, BOFT can also be viewed as a parameter-efficient version of Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which uses a block-diagonal orthogonal transformation to reshape the pretrained weight matrices. The block-diagonal orthogonal matrix is obtained by factorizing a sparse matrix that contains only $\pm 1$ entries.

To be specific, BOFT first defines a block-diagonal $2b\times 2b$ orthogonal matrix $\text{diag}(R_1,R_2,\cdots,R_r)$ where $R_i=\text{diag}(\omega_{i,1},\cdots,\omega_{i,d_i}), d_i=\text{rank}(W_i)\cdot b$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{d_i} \omega_{i,j}=0$ for all $i$.
