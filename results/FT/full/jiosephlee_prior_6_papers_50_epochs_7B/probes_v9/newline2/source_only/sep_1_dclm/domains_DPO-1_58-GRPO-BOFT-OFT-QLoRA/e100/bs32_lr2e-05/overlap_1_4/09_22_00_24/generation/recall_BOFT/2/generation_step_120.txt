Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is an iterative method that transforms the weight matrix of a pretrained model with $d$ dimensions into an orthogonal matrix with $d$ dimensions through a butterfly-like factorization. BOFT can be seen as a parameter-efficient version of LoRA, where it uses a much smaller number of additional parameters than LoRA while still achieving strong performance.

The name BOFT comes from two sources: 1) the butterfly network structure, which recursively applies a small linear transformation to become an orthogonal transformation; 2) the butterfly effect, which refers to the sensitivity of a nonlinear system to its initial conditions, reflecting that small changes to the initialization of BOFT's parameters can lead to different final orthogonal matrices.

# What is the butterfly network structure?

The butterfly network structure is a sequence of linear transformations that recursively apply to eventually yield an orthogonal matrix. A simple example is

\[
R = \mathbf{F}_4^T \mathbf{F}_{4}^T,
\]

where $\mathbf{F}_i^T$ is a $d$-dimensional rotation matrix. In general, a butterfly network with $k$ levels can be written as

\[
\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{A}_k^T \mathbf{x}, \qquad \mathbf{A}_{k-1}^T \mathbf{o}_k = \mathbf{B}_k^T \, \mathbf{A}^T_k \mathbf{x},\qquad \cdots,\qquad \mathbf{o}_1 = \mathbf{C}_1^T \, \cdots \, \mathbf{T}_1^T \mathbf{l},
\]

where $\mathcal{N}(1)$ are orthogonal matrices, $\mathbf{l}$ is a vector of learning rates, and $\mathbf{o}_i$, $\mathbf{A}_i$ are matrices of sizes $d_{i-1}, d_i$.

The butterfly network structure is often used in fast linear transforms, such as the fast Fourier transform (FFT). In the context of finetuning, we can view LoRA as a butterfly network with a block-diagonal structure. However, the block-diagonal butterfly network is not very parameter-efficient. BOFT introduces a new butterfly network structure with a factorized form that is more parameter-efficient.

# How does BOFT work?

BOFT reparameterizes an orthogonal matrix as a product of three matrices: a block-diagonal matrix and two other matrices. To better explain the reparameterization, let us consider a weight matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$, where $d$ is the number of neurons and $n$ is the size of a hidden layer. BOFT first diagonalizes $\mathbf{W}$ by computing its spectral decomposition:

\[
\mathbf{s} = \mathbf{s}^\top \mathbf{D} \mathbf{s} =\mathbf{s}^\ top,
\]

where $\mathrm{rank}\ (\mathbf{D}) = r$ and $\mathbf{s}$ is the vector of singular vectors. BOFT then partitions the diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}$ into blocks of size $b$ along the diagonal, yielding a block-diagonal matrix $\mathbf{G}$. BOFT next defines two parameter matrices $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{Q}$ with sizes $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbf{R}^{d \times b}$ and $\mathbf Q \in \mathbf R^{b\times n}$, and forms the product $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{Q}$, which yields the block-diagonal matrix $\bm{G}$.
