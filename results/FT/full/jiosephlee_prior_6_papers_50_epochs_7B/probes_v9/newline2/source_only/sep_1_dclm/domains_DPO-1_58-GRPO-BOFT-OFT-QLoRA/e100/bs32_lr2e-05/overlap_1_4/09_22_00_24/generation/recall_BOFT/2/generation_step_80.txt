The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is based on the idea of re-expressing an identity matrix as a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices. This re-expression leads to a butterfly-like structure that enables parameter-efficient finetuning through a block-diagonal matrix with a specific pattern. BOFT introduces a family of parameter-efficient adapters that can be attached to large models, such as T5, and the authors demonstrate strong performance across a variety of tasks, including code generation, translation, summarization, and question answering.

The key contributions of this paper are:

- A new parameter-efficient adapter, BOFT, that introduces a butterfly-like structure to enable effective finetuning.
- A novel factorization technique for constructing identity matrices that enables the creation of sparse orthogonal matrices.
- Strong empirical results showing BOFT outperforms OFT and other state-of-the-art parameter-efficient methods on a variety of tasks, while also being more parameter-efficient than OFT.

# Matrix factorization: block-diagonal matrix -> identity matrix

\[

\text{diag}(1, 0) & \text{0} \\
