Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is an actor-critic algorithm that directly optimizes a language model to match human preferences, without fitting an auxiliary reward model. DPO does not rely on a reward model, but rather uses a preference model that maps a prompt and completion A and B to a preference probability over A and B. In contrast, RLHF typically fits a reward model that maps a prompt to a real-valued reward, and then uses that reward model to generate completions and evaluate them with a binary cross-entropy loss. DPO also avoids using a reward model in the evaluation, so it cannot be accidentally used for generating completions with high likelihood, only high reward.

The authors introduce a preference model $\pi_\theta$ parameterized by $\theta$ that takes as input a prompt $x$ and completions $y_A,y_B$ and outputs the preference probability $\pi_\theta(y_A,y_B \mid x)$. The preference model is trained with labeled completions using a standard binary cross-entropy loss.

Once a well-trained preference model is available, the DPO training loop can begin. DPO uses an actor-critic architecture. The actor is the language model policy, denoted as $\mu_\phi$, which is parameterized by $\phi$.
