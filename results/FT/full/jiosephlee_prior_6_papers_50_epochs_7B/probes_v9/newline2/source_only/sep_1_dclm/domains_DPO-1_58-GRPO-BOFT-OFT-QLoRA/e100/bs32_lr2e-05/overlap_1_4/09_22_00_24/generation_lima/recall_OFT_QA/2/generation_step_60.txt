Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have shown remarkable ability in generating text. However, their performance on reasoning tasks is limited when required to conduct multi-step reasoning or to defer decisions until multiple prompts are given. To improve LLM reasoning, a common approach is to combine a powerful LLM with human or machine solvers (e.g., SFT). While such approaches have demonstrated strong performance, they often require significant engineering efforts to scale. This chapter introduces a simple yet effective approach — Multi-Step Reasoning Manager (MSR) — for improving LLMs' multi-step reasoning performance. Specifically, we focus on the following aspects:

* How MSR works
* Training and evaluation pipelines
* Best practices for training
* Limitations and future directions

# How MSR works

The core idea behind MSR is to train a manager to coordinate with a reasoning model and a search optimizer to solve multi-step reasoning tasks. More specifically, the manager formulates each reasoning task as a planning problem, and trains the search optimizer to solve these planning problems by coordinating with the reasoning model. During inference, the manager issues instructions to the search optimizer to trigger its execution, and then coordinates with it to solve the task. This coordination is achieved through a reward function learned by the manager. If the manager sees that the search optimizer has deviated from the task, it issues a "correction" instruction to bring the optimizer back on track. Once the task is solved, the manager issues a final instruction to make the search optimizer output the solution.

There are three key components in MSR:

1. A reasoning model that scores the completeness and correctness of answers.
2. A search optimizer (e.g., APEX) that is fine-tuned to follow instructions and optimize a reward function.
3. A manager that formulates multi-step reasoning tasks as planning problems and trains the search optimizer using a reward function.
