The paper "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits" presents a novel and efficient method for creating powerful language models, BitNet b1.58, as a cost-effective alternative to full-precision (FP16) models. BitNet b1.58 is a 1.58-bit LLM that achieves the performance of 13B parameters while using only 4B parameters. This is achieved by introducing a new information transmission mechanism called "BitNet", which can transmit important information efficiently during the training process. The authors claim that BitNet b1.580 can be trained to outperform the current open-source 3B LLMs on many benchmarks with less than 2GB of GPU memory.

# What is BitNet?

BitNet is a novel information transmission mechanism that transmits important information efficiently during the LLM training process. It is designed to improve the information transmission efficiency of low-bit LLMs. BitNet has two components: BitNet Block and BitNet Stream. BitNet Block is a replacement for the standard linear layer in an LLM model. It can transmit important information through the output projection vector, which is used by the next BitNet Block or by the next layer in the model. BitNet Stream is a set of vectors that are used to transmit information between adjacent layers in the model. These vectors are updated during the training process and are used to transmit information efficiently.

# How does BitNet work?

BitNet works by transmitting important information from one layer to the next. In a standard linear layer, the input vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{out}}}$ is multiplied by the weight matrix $W^{\text{in}}\in\mathbb{T}^{n_{\mathrm{in}}, n_{\text{out}}} $ to produce the pre-activation $z= x W^{\text{in }}$. In BitNet, the input vector $z$ is modified as follows:

$x \leftarrow x + s \odot \hat{z},\qquad \hat{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2^{b-1}},\;s\in\mathbb{\hat{R}}_{2^{b-1}}.$

Here $\mathbb{Z}_{q}$ denotes integers modulo $q$, and $\mathbb{\hat{.R}}_q$ rational numbers in the field $\mathbb{Z}_q$. The integer $\hat{z}\in[0,2^{b-1}-1]$ is the important information that needs to be transmitted, and $s$ is the scale factor. The scale factor $s$ is computed by the current layer and transmitted to the next layer. The scale factor $2^{b-1}$ ensures that the magnitude of $\hat{z}$ is comparable to that of the input components $x$. After the pre-activation $x$ is updated with the scale factor and the important information $\hat{z}$, the activation $\alpha$ is computed via a non-linear function $\phi(\cdot)$, just like a standard linear layer in an LSTM.

The second component of BitNet, BitNet Stream, is responsible for transmitting the scale factor $s$ between adjacent layers. BitNet Stream is initialized with a set of vectors $\{v^0_1,\cdots,v^0_{L-1}\}\in\mathbb{\widehat{R}^{n_\text{out}}}$, where $L$ is the number of layers in the model. During the forward pass, the scale factor $s_0$ produced by the current layer is multiplied element-wise by $v^0_{\text{out}}, v^0_{\text{lstm}}, v^0_{(1) \text{ MLP}}, v^0_{2 \text{ MLP}},$ and $v^0_{(3) \text{ MLP}}$ to produce $s_1,\cdots,s_L$. These scale factors are then used by the next Bitnet Block to compute $x$ as described above. During the backward pass, gradients are computed with respect to the original scale factor $s_1,\dots,s_L$ and then used to update $v^0_{i}$ for all $i$.

During the training process, the BitNet Stream vectors are updated based on the scale factors they receive. The goal is to ensure that the most important information is transmitted with the largest scale factor. As a result, the vectors will adapt to emphasize the transmission of the most important information. The authors provide a detailed algorithm for updating the BitNet Stream vectors in their paper.
