According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning large pretrained models, such as text-to-image diffusion models, without destroying the pretrained model's performance on general tasks. The idea behind OFT is that when you train a model on a new task, you typically update the weights of each layer in a nonorthogonal way, which can cause the pretrained model to forget its expertise on the general task. To avoid this, OFT updates the weights of each layer by only optimizing their covariance matrix, while keeping the mean of the weight distribution fixed and orthogonal. This way, the pretrained mean of the weight distribution remains unchanged, and the new information is encoded in the off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix. 

The name "Orthogonal Finetuning" comes from the fact that during the finetuning process, the mean vector of the weight distribution is kept orthogonal to the subspace that represents the pretrained knowledge. In other words, the pretrained knowledge lies in a hyperplane defined by the mean vector, and OFT prevents the mean vector from moving away from this hyperplane.

In practice, OFT optimizes the following objective:
\[
\min_{Cov(\theta)} \text{SSE}(θ + Cov(θ)^{1/2} \Sigma, x)
\]
where $\theta$ are the model weights, $\Sigma$ is a learned covariance matrix, $x$ are the inputs, and SSE is the sum of squared errors.<|endoftext|>
