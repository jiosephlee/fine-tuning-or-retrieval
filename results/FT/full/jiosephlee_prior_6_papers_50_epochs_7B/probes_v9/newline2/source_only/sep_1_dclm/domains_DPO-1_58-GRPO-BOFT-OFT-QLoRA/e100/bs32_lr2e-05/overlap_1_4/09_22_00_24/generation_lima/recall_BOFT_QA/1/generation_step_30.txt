According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a block-diagonal matrix factorization to update the pretrained weight matrix. BOFT can be seen as a generalization of LoRA, which uses a 2x2 block-diagonal matrix factorizaion. BOFT's matrix factorization is defined as:

$W^F = \text{diag}(W_1, W_2, \cdots, W_r) = \begin{bmatrix}
W_1 & 0 \\
0 & W_2 \\
\vdots & \vdots \\
0 & W_r
\end{bmatrix}$

where $W_i$ are sub-matrices of size $d_{\text{in}} \times d_{\text{out}}$. The number of sub-matrices $r$ is called the butterfly dimension. BOFT updates each $W_i$ with a block-diagonal low-rank update, i.e. $W_i^{(f)} = W_i^{(0)} + B_i^{(f)} \cdot L_i^{(f)}$ where $B_i^{(f)} $ and $L_i^{(f)}  $ are block-diagonal low-ranks matrices of sizes $(l_{\text{in}}, l_{\text{out}}, r)$ and $(l_{\text{oth}}, r, r)$ respectively. Here $l_{\text{out}}>0$ and $l_{\text{others}}>0$ are the number of trainable parameters in each sub-matrix $W_i$.

The key idea behind BOFT is to preserve the pretraining information in the diagonal blocks while allowing the off-diagonal blocks to be updated freely. This way, we can achieve both parameter efficiency and good performance.<|endoftext|>
