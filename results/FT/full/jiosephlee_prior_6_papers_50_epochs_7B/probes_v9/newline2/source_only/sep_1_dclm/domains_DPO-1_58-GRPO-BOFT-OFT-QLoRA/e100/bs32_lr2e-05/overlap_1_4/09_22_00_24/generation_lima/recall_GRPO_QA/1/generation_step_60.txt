According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). PPO is an actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithm that is widely used for training open-ended language models. In particular, PPO improves a policy by maximizing the following surrogate objective:
```
\hat{J}_\text{PPO}(π) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[J_0(\pi)] - \beta \frac{\mathbb{D}_{KL}[\hat{π}_\text{old}{\|}\hat{π}_\mathrm{π}{]}]}{\mathbb{D}_\text{KL}[\hat{ρ}_0{\|}\hat{ρ}_\mathrm{π}]{}}]
```
where $\hat{ρ}_0$ and $\hat{ρ}_\pi$ are the old and current policy state distributions, respectively; $\hat{π}_\textrm{old}$ and $\hat{π}_π$ are the old and new policy distributions, respectively; $J_0$ is the reward function; $\beta$ is a coefficient that scales the KL penalty term.

GRPO modifies PPO by replacing the per-token reward signal with a group-level reward signal. In GRPO, a sequence of tokens is treated as a single "group" and is assigned a reward score based on some evaluation metric. For example, the mathematical reasoning capability of an open-ended language model can be evaluated using the MATH benchmark. If the token sequence is a question, then the answer to the question is the solution to a math problem. In this case, the group reward can be calculated as the average score of all problems solved in the group.<|endoftext|>
