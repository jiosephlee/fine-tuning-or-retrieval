DPO is a Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) algorithm that directly optimizes the language model on preferred completions without the usual intermediate reward model. It does so by constructing a preference distribution over past contexts that maximizes the disfluency of a completion, and then optimizes the language model with respect to this distribution. This way, the human labeler only needs to specify the beginning of a disfluent prompt to elicit a desired response, rather than labeling a full reward function.

Formally, given a dataset of preferences $\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{(x^{(i)}, y_w^{(i)}), i = 1,\dots,n\bigr\}$ where $x^{(i)}$ is a context and $y_w^{(i)}$ a desired completion, the preference distribution is constructed as follows:

1. Construct the joint distribution over the complete context $x^{(i)'} = (x^{(i)}, ya_w)$ and desired completions:
\[
    x^{(i')} \sim J(x^{(i)}, ya_W) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad a_W \in y_w
\>
2. Maximise the joint disfluency of the completion under the assumption that the policy is the baseline model:
\[
    \max_{x^{(i')}} -\log \bigl(\mathbf{P}(a_W|x^{(i')})\bigr)
\]
3. Obtain the preference distribution by normalising the joint distribution from step 1:
\[
    \hat{\mathbf{P}}(a_W|x) = \mathbf{P}(x,y_a) / \mathbf{P }(x)
\>

Finally, DPO updates the language model $\mathbf{P}_0$ to $\mathbf{P}_{t+1}$ by optimizing the model parameters with the following objective:

\[
    \mathbf{P}_{k+1} = \mathbf{ P}_k \cdot \hat{\mathbf{\Pi}}(x, ya) \; \text{s.t. } \hat{\mathbf\Pi}(x, a) = \sum_{a'} \hat{\mathbf P}(a|x) \mathbf{P}_k(a')
\]

Where $\hat{\mathbf\Pie}(x, a) $ is the preference distribution obtained from context $x$. 

This approach has several advantages. First, it is much more efficient to collect preferences than reward models, because the labeler only needs to provide the beginning of a prompt. Second, it avoids the problems of the reward model, because we are directly optimizing the language model, not an intermediate reward model. Third, DPO can also be used for self-supervised learning, because it can optimize the language model against a generated preference. Finally, DPO does not require any assumptions, except that the language model is deterministic.

However, there are also some disadvantages. First, DPO requires sampling the preference distribution, which makes it computationally expensive. Second, it requires a baseline model $\mathbf{M}$ to construct the preference distribution. The authors suggest using the current state-of-the-art language model as the baseline model $\mathbf M$.<|endoftext|>
