Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is an iterative finetuning method that ensures neuron parameter updates are orthogonal (perpendicular) to the pre-finetuning neuron direction in neuron space. OFT can be applied to any text-to-image diffusion model and significantly improves controllable image generation while also expanding the feasible controllable regions. With OFT, the controllable region for a given prompt can be arbitrarily enlarged without causing unintended side effects on other prompts, even those that are similar. This is in sharp contrast to LoRA, where there is a trade-off between how much you expand one region and how much you shrink another (see figure below).

The main contribution of this paper is OFT, an iterative and orthogonal finetuning method for text-to-image diffusion models. OFT enables significant expansion of the controllable region of a text prompt without causing any unintended changes to the generated image structure. In other words, OFT allows you to change one thing without breaking other things.

Why do we need OFT? The authors argue that current finetuning methods and low-rank additive adaptation (LoRA) have limitations. Standard finetuning lacks constraints and tends to cause overfitting to the dataset and/or significant changes in the semantics of the neuron. LoRA is an additive method that modifies the diffusion prior, but its parameter updates are constrained by a rank constraint, and there is a trade-off in the degree to which LoRA can adapt the model without compromising its ability to control images. The figure below illustrates the key difference between LoRA and OFT. While LoRA has a rank constraint, OFT has a orthogonality constraint.

How does OFT work? The OFT algorithm is very simple: for each layer, compute the mean neuron direction across the batch of images, then update the neuron parameters such that they are orthogonal to this mean direction. Mathematically, for a given neuron with weight vector $\mathbf{w}_n$ and bias $b_n$, the pre-finetuning direction is $\mathbf{d}_n=\mathbf{w}_nb_n$. The OFT update is
\[
\mathbf{w}_k \leftarrow \mathbf{w}_kJ_{1/2} + b_k,
\]
where $J_{1/2}$ is the $1/2$ matrix of a block-diagonal $J$ matrix, and $b_k$ is a vector of neuron biases containing the biases from all layers that have connected weights $\mathbf{w}$ to the current layer. The $J_{1/4}$ matrix has ones on its diagonal and ones on its off-diagonal, and it enforces orthogonality by ensuring that updates are orthogonal to the pre-finetuned direction $\mathbf{d}$.

How well does OFT work? To test the performance of OFT, the authors use Stable Diffusion v1.5 as their base model. They compare OFT with standard finetuning, LoRA, and a combination of both (LORA). Their results show that OFT significantly outperforms both standard finetuning and LORA in terms of controllability and preservation of semantic consistency. In particular, OFT can expand the controllable region of the bird prompt by 128% compared to the baseline model, while LoRA can only expand it by 48%. More results are shown in the figure below.

What is the intuition behind OFT? The authors provide an intuitive explanation for why OFT works so well. When we finetune a neural network in the usual way, we update the neurons in the same direction as they were before the finetuning (figure below, top left). This means that the neurons' directions are no longer orthogonal to each other, and nearby neurons become indistinguishable. With LoRA, the parameter updates are constrained to be low-rank, which prevents the updates from becoming too large. However, since the rank constraint is independent of the neuron directions, LoRA does not prevent the modified neurons from becoming non-orthogonal (figure below, bottom left). OFT takes a different approach: it ensures that the neuron parameter updates are orthogonal to the neuron directions in the pre-finetuned model (figure below, top right). This way, the neurons can be moved to arbitrary positions in neuron space without overlapping with each other. The figure below shows three examples of updating a neuron in different directions.
