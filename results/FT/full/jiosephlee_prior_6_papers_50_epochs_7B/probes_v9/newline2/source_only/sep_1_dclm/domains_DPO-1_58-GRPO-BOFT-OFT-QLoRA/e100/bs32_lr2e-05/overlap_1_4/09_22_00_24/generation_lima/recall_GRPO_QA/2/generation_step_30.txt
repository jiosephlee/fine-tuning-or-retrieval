What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Policy Optimization algorithms are crucial for training large language models to perform complex tasks. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) has become the de-facto standard due to its simplicity and effectiveness. However, PPO requires storing the old policy probability estimates in memory, which becomes prohibitively expensive for very large models. To address this issue, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO), an efficient variant of PPO that only stores the most recent estimate of the policy probability.

In PPO, we estimate the advantage of an action using the difference between a reference policy and the current policy. In particular, for each action $a$ in state $s$, we compute the advantage
\[
\hat{A}_t(a,s,a) = \widehat{q}_t(s,a) - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(a\mid s)}[\widehat{q}_s(s,a)]
\]
where $\pi_{\theta_{ol}}$ is the reference policy and $\widehat{q}_x(s,a)$ is the empirical per-step reward estimate under policy $\pi_x$. The problem with this formulation is that we need to store the reference policy $\pi_{\theta_0}$ in order to compute the advantage. GRPO modifies the definition of the advantage to
\[
\hat{\tilde{A}}_t(a,s,a)  = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G} \widehat{q}(s,g,a) - \mathcal{M}_{G}(s,a)
\]
where $G$ is a group of samples from the current policy $\pi_{\tilde{\theta}}$ that are sampled at the same time as $a$, and $\mathcal{M}_G(s,a)$ is the mean of rewards under $\pi_{\tiltheta}$ in group $G$:
\[
\mathcal{M}_S(s,a) = \frac{1} {|G|}\sum_{s\in G} \mathbb{E}[\widehat{q}(S,s,a)].
\]
This way, we can estimate the advantage without storing the reference policy $\pi_0$. Instead, we only need to store the most recent group of samples $\{s\}_{s\in G}$ and the empirical mean $\mathcal{M}(s,a)$. This reduces the memory requirement from $O(|S|)$ to $O(1)$, making it feasible to train very large models.

The rest of the PPO algorithm remains unchanged. We minimize the surrogate loss
\[
\mathcal{l}(\tilde{\theta}) = \mathbb{D}_{KL}\left[\frac{\pi_{\tilde\theta}(a\mid s)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a\mid s)}> \epsilon\mid \mid \pi_{\theta_{OL}}(a\mid  s)\right]
\]
where $\epsilon$ is a small constant. The KL term is estimated using importance sampling with the advantage $\hat{\tilde{a}}$. The only other change is that we use the empirical mean $\mathbb{M}(s,a)$ instead of the reference policy $\pi_\theta$ to compute the advantage.<|endoftext|>
