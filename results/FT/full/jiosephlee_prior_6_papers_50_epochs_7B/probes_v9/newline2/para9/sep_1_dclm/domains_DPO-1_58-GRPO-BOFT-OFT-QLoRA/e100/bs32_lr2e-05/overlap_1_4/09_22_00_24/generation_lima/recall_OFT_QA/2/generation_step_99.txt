What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT for short) is a novel finetuning technique designed to preserve the pairwise angles between neurons in a pretrained neural network. The core idea behind OFT is to perform a rotation of each neuron within its layer so that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved. This rotation is achieved by solving a least-squares problem, which can be efficiently solved using a QR decomposition. To ensure that the solution is orthogonal, we apply a second least-squares problem to obtain an orthogonal matrix. 

To better understand the concept of OFT, it is helpful to first consider the differences between conventional finetuning and LoRA. In standard finetuning, weights are updated by backpropagating gradients through the network. While this approach allows for rapid adaptation of the model, it often results in significant deviations from the original pretrained weight space. As a result, information learned during pretraining may be lost. LoRA addresses this issue by adding a low-rank matrix to the pretrained weight matrix, thereby limiting the deviation from the pretrained parameters. Although LoRA performs well in preserving the overall information in the pretrained weights, it does not guarantee preservation of the pairwise angles between neurons. By contrast, OFT ensures that both the magnitude and the pairwise angles between neurons remain consistent with those in the pretrained weights.

In more detail, the OFT process involves the following steps: 1) For every neuron $i$ in a given layer, compute the pairwise cosine similarity $\frac{w_i^\top w_j}{\|w_i\|\|w_j\|}$ for all neurons $j$ in the same layer. These similarities are used to construct a similarity matrix $S$. 2) Solve for a left orthogonal matrix $R$ using the singular value decomposition (SVD) of $S$, such that $R^\top R = I$. 3) Apply the orthogonal matrix $R$ to the neurons' weights by computing $R \odot W$, where $W$ represents the weights before the orthogonal transformation. This operation ensures that the pairwise angles between the neurons are maintained. It is important to note that the SVD computed here is a singular value decomposition of the similarity matrix $S$, rather than a covariance matrix. Additionally, the orthogonal matrix $R$, although derived from a left orthogonal form, is treated as a right orthogonal matrix in the implementation. That is, $R^\top R=I$ instead of $R^\top R=-I$. This choice simplifies the computation of the orthogonal matrix while retaining the essential property of preserving pairwise angles.

OFT offers several advantages over existing finetuning techniques. First, it requires minimal additional computational cost compared to standard finetuning, since the orthogonal transformation is applied directly to the weight matrix. Second, OFT can be seamlessly integrated into existing finetuning frameworks, including parameter-efficient approaches. Third, OFT provides improved generalization and stability during finetuning, particularly when adapting large text-to-image diffusion models. Finally, OFT can be extended to other finetuning scenarios, such as adapter modules and cross-modal transfer learning.

# How does OFT work? Why is it beneficial to maintain pairwise angles between neurons?

The human brain is capable of recognizing objects and scenes based on their visual features. During the training of convolutional neural networks, neurons learn to represent these visual features. These learned features are encoded in the directions (angles) of neurons. Therefore, maintaining the relative angles between neurons is crucial for preserving the semantic knowledge acquired during pretraining. If the angles between neurons are altered excessively, the semantic information associated with those neurons may also be compromised. To illustrate this point, consider a neuron whose direction has been trained to recognize a particular object. If the angle of this neuron is changed significantly during finetuning, the model might fail to identify the object correctly. Conversely, if the angle is preserved, the neuron's semantic meaning remains intact. Thus, it is important to keep the angles between neurons constant during finetuning.

Maintaining the pairwise angles between neurons is beneficial because it helps to retain the semantic information learned during pretraining. To better understand this, let us examine two neurons in a layer. Their pairwise cosine similarity is defined as follows:

$\frac{w_1^\top w_2}{\|w_1\|\|w_2\|}$

This cosine similarity serves as a measure of the semantic proximity between neurons $1$ and $2$. When the weights of a neuron are finetuned, the pairwise cosine similarity between that neuron and others changes accordingly. In order to keep the semantic relationships intact, we must adjust the weights of all neurons in a way that maintains their pairwise cosine similarities. This requirement motivates the development of OFT, which applies an orthogonal transformation to each neuron so that their pairwise angles are preserved.
