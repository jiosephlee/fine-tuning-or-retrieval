As the field of mathematical reasoning in LLMs continues to evolve, it is essential to establish rigorous evaluation protocols. This section discusses the benchmarks currently used to assess mathematical reasoning abilities in LLMs, including their strengths and limitations.

GSM8K and MATH are two widely adopted benchmarks for evaluating mathematical reasoning in LLM models. GSM8K is a multiple-choice dataset consisting of 8,000 questions sourced from high school and undergraduate competitions. Each question has four candidate answers. Although GSM8K provides a comprehensive assessment of general reasoning skills, its multiple-choice format limits the evaluation of more complex reasoning processes. To address this limitation, MATH, a multiple-step math problem dataset, was introduced. MATH contains problems that require step-by-step reasoning, making it a more suitable benchmark for assessing mathematical reasoning capabilities in LLMs. Both GSM8K and MATH have been translated into several languages, contributing to their widespread adoption.

Another benchmark, CMATH, was developed to evaluate models' ability to solve multi-step mathematical problems. Unlike MATH, which includes competition-level problems, CMATH focuses on problems commonly encountered in high school and undergraduate curricula. The dataset comprises 57,000 questions split into training, validation, and test sets. Each question requires several reasoning steps to arrive at an answer. Evaluations using MATH and CMATH typically involve generating a sequence of steps rather than selecting from predefined options, which better reflects real-world scenarios.

Other specialized benchmarks include those focused on specific mathematical domains, such as quantitative reasoning in the SAT, GMAT, and GRE exams. These datasets are valuable for assessing models' proficiency in particular areas, such as algebra, probability, and geometry. However, since these datasets are proprietary, they are not always accessible for open-source evaluation.

While some benchmarks, such as GSM8K and MATE, allow both program-based and chain-of-thought approaches, others, such as MATH, only permit chain-of-thought reasoning. Although benchmarks play a crucial role in assessing mathematical reasoning capabilities, there are certain limitations to consider. Many existing benchmarks are based on competition-level problems, which may not reflect the types of mathematical tasks that most LLMs will encounter in practice. To address these issues, researchers have begun developing new benchmarks that include a broader range of mathematical topics and that are translated into multiple languages.
