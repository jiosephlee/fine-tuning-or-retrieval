The key idea behind RLHF is to model the human feedback mechanism as a stochastic process, which allows for a general formulation of learning algorithms that adapt to human feedback. In contrast to previous approaches, RLHF does not make any assumptions about the structure of the feedback model. This generality has two important consequences: on the one hand, it enables the integration of many existing learning strategies under a unified framework; on the other hand, it introduces additional uncertainty that needs to be accounted for when applying RLHF.

The RLHF framework is based on the following assumptions:
1. The learner is modeled as an infinite-horizon discounted-reward Markov decision process (MDP).
2. The human provides feedback only in response to the actions taken by the learner.

4. The feedback is generated according to a stochastic policy, which is unknown to the learner.
5. The reward function of the feedback MDP is a function of the rewards and the transition dynamics of the learner MDP.
