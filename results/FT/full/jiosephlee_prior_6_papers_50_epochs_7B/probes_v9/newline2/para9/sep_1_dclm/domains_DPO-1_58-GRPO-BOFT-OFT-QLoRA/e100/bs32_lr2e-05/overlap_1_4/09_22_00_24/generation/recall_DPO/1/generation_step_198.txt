# What is preference learning?

Preference learning is a method for training language models to behave in a way that aligns with human preferences. In this context, a language model is a neural network that generates text based on a prompt. The model learns from data consisting of prompts and preferred responses. During training, the model receives a prompt and a candidate response. It then produces a distribution of possible completions, and the algorithm selects the completion that best matches the human preference. This process is repeated many times, and the model updates its parameters to better reflect the human preferences.

There are two main approaches to preference learning:

1. Supervised learning: Here, the model is trained on datasets where each example includes a prompt, a gold response, and a label indicating which response is preferred. The model is trained to maximize the likelihood of generating the preferred response given the prompt.

2. Reinforcement learning (RL): In RL, the model is trained using a reward model that assigns a score to each response. The RL approach uses a reward model that is initially trained on human preferences, and then the language model is trained to maximize these rewards.

Both methods aim to produce language models that are capable of generalizing to new prompts and producing high-quality outputs that align with human preferences.

# What is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a novel approach to preference-based RL that eliminates the need for a separate reward model. Instead, DPO directly optimizes the policy to match human preferences, without requiring an intermediate reward function. This is achieved by modeling the reward function as a soft decision boundary that separates preferred responses from non-preferred ones. The policy is then optimized to lie on the boundary, ensuring that it remains consistent with observed human preferences.

DPO operates under the assumption that the reward model is a parametric function, such as a linear or neural network. The algorithm first constructs a hyperplane that partitions the space of response pairs into two regions: one containing the preferred responses and the other containing the non-preferred ones. DPO then adjusts the policy parameters so that the decision boundary coincides with the hyperplane, guaranteeing that the policy will generate preferred outputs.

DPO’s key innovation is its use of a reparameterization technique to compute the gradient of the policy loss with respect to the policy parameters. This reparameterization yields a gradient that is free of any explicit reward model, making it unnecessary to train or maintain a separate reward model. As a result, DPO is both simpler and more scalable than traditional preference-based RL algorithms, which require training a reward model and optimizing the policy to maximize the reward.
