# What is preference learning?

Preference learning is a method for training language models to behave in a way that aligns with human preferences. In this context, a language model is a neural network that generates text based on a prompt. The model learns from data consisting of prompts and preferred responses. During training, the model receives a prompt and a candidate response. It then produces a distribution over possible completions of the response. The training objective is to maximize the probability of the preferred completion under this distribution, while minimizing the probability of other completions. This process is repeated for many prompts and candidate completions, allowing the model to learn the underlying preference structure.

There are two main approaches to preference learning. In the first, the model is trained directly on the preference data using a reinforcement learning (RL) framework. In this approach, the reward function is learned from human preferences, and an RL algorithm is used to optimize the policy. The second approach involves converting preference data into supervised learning tasks by generating synthetic rewards or using a proxy reward model. In this case, the language model is trained to maximize the learned reward, which is optimized to match the human preferences.
