What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT for short) is a novel finetuning technique designed to preserve the pairwise angles between neurons in a pretrained neural network. The core idea behind OFT is to perform a rotation of each neuron within its layer so that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved. This rotation is achieved by solving a least-squares problem, which can be efficiently solved using a QR decomposition. To ensure that the solution is orthogonal, we apply a second least-squares problem to obtain the final rotation matrix. 

To better understand the concept of pairwise angles between neurons, consider a simple example where we have two neurons $v_1$ and $v_2$ in a fully connected layer. These neurons are represented by their weight matrices $W_1$ and $W_2$, respectively. If we take the inner product of $v_1$ with $v_2$, we get:
$$
\langle v_1, v_2 \rangle = (W_1)^\top \cdot (W_2) \cdot x,
$$
where $x$ is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The angle $\theta$ between $v_1$ (i.e., $W_1$) and $v_2$(i.e., $W2$) can be calculated as follows:
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\langle v_1,  v_2 \rangle}{\|v_1\|\|v_2\|}.
$$
Here, $\|v_1\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm of $v_1$. In practice, we compute the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the matrix product $(W_1)^\ top \cdot W_2$, which yields three matrices $\Sigma$ and $U_1$ and $U_2$, respectively. Using these matrices, we can rewrite $W_1$ as $U_1 \cdot \Sigma \cdot U_2^\top$, and $W_2$ as $U_2 \cdot \Sigma \cdotp U_2^\top$. Then, the inner product can be written as
$$
\langle v_i, v_j \rangle = \sum_{k=1}^r \sigma_k \cdot (\tilde{u}_i)_k \cdot (\tilde u_j)_k,
$$
where $\tilde u_i$ is the $i$-th column vector of the matrix $U_1$, and $\sigma_k$ are singular values. It is clear from this expression that the contribution of each singular vector to the inner product is equal. This observation leads to the definition of a new similarity measure between $v_1$, $v_2$, and other neurons $v_3$, $\cdots$, $v_n$. We define the similarity matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ as follows:
$$
S = [ \tilde u_1 \; \cdots \; \tilde u_r ] [ \sigma_1 \; \sigma_2 \; \cdots\; \sigma_r ]^\top,
$$
where $\mathbf{1} \in \mathbb R^n$ is a vector of ones, and $r$ is the rank of the matrix $W_1^\top \cdot W_2$. The matrix $S$ is called the similarity matrix, because it encodes the pairwise similarities between neurons. The pairwise angles between neurons are then given by the cosines of the angles in $S$. Now, let us discuss how OFT works. The main idea behind OFT is that we want to rotate each neuron in a layer by the same angle, while keeping the pairwise angles between neurons intact. To achieve this goal, we first calculate the cosine similarity between every pair of neurons. Next, we find the average cosine similarity across all pairs of neurons. If the average cosine similarity is zero, it indicates that the neurons are already optimally aligned. Otherwise, we rotate the neurons so that the average cosine similarity becomes zero.
