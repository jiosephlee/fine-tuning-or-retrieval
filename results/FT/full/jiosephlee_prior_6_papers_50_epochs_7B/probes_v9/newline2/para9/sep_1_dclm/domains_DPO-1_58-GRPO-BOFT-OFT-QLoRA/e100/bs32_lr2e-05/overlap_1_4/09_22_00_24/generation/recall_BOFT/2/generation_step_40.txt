# Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft) decomposition and BOft finetuning

Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT, pronounced "both") is a parameter-efficient finETUNE method that adapts pretrained models. BOFT achieves this through a novel factorization of the weight matrices in a pretrained model. The BOFT factorization is inspired by the butterfly structure used in fast linear transforms, such as the Cooley-Tukey algorithm for the discrete Fourier transform. The BOFT factorized weight matrix $W$ of size $d \times n$, where $d$ is the number of neurons in a layer and $n$ is the dimension of the input data, is represented as a product of $L$ sparse orthogonal matrices $\{R_i\}_{i=1}^L$:

\begin{equation}
    W = R_L R_{L-1} \cdots R_1,
\end{equation}

where each $R_i$ has dimensions $d \times d_i$ and $\sum_{i=1}^LD_i = n$. The BOFT factorization differs from the standard butterfly structure in that the $R_i$ matrices are orthogonal rather than diagonal. The BOFT decomposition is illustrated in Figure 1.

The BOFT finetuning procedure involves updating only the orthogonal matrices $\{R_1,\dots,R_L\}$ during training, while the pretrained weight matrices $\{B_1,\dots,B_M\}$ remain unchanged. Here, $M$ denotes the total number of trainable parameters in the pretrained model, and $B_i$ represents the $i$-th column of the $d \times M$ weight matrix. The forward pass of BOFT is given by:

\begin{equATION}
    \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{z} = (\mathbf{R}_L \mathbf{R}_{L-1} \dots \mathbf{R}_1)^\top \mathbf{x},
\end{equATION}

where $\mathbf{z}$ is the output of the previous layer.
