# Basic components of neural networks: layers, neurons, activation functions

A neural network is composed of interconnected nodes organized into layers. At a minimum, a neural network contains an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each node within a layer connects to every node in the subsequent layer, forming a directed graph. Neurons are the computational units that transform inputs based on their weights and biases, producing outputs. A neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs, optionally applies a nonlinearity, and passes the result through a linear or nonlinear activation function. Formally, given an input vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, a single neuron computes
\[
\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x} + b),
\]
where $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbf{R}^n$ is the weight vector, $b \in \mathbf R$ is the bias, and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is an activation function. In many neural networks, particularly those using ReLU as the activation function, $\sigma(x) = \max(0, x)$.

Neural networks are organized into layers. A common architecture is the fully connected layer, where each neuron in one layer connects to every neuron in the next layer. Fully connected layers are sometimes referred to as dense layers. Other architectures include convolutional layers, which are designed specifically for processing two-dimensional inputs such as images. Convolutional layers contain kernels (also called filters) that slide across the image, computing the inner product between the kernel and local patches of the input. These layers are central to the effectiveness of neural networks for computer vision tasks.

Activation functions determine the nonlinear behavior of neurons. Historically, sigmoid functions were commonly used as the activation function, since they map the input range $(-\infty, \infty)$ to $(0, 1)$. However, sigmoids have several drawbacks; most notably, they can saturate quickly, causing vanishing or exploding gradients. To mitigate this, ReLU (rectified linear unit), defined as $\sigma(x) =\max(0, x),$ was introduced and has become the standard activation function in most modern neural networks. ReLU eliminates the vanishing gradient problem associated with sigmoids while offering simpler, faster computation.
