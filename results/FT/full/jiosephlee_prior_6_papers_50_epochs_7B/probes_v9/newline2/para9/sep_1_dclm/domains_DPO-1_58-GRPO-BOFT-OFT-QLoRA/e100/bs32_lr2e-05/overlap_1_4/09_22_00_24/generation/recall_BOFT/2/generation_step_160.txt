Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning technique that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks by learning a task-specific orthogonal transformation at each layer. BOFT introduces a novel butterfly matrix factorization that enables the representation of any orthogonal matrix as a product of several sparse orthogonal matrices. This factorization is both more parameter-efficient than the block-diagonal approach used in OFT and also more flexible, since it allows for the construction of an orthogonal matrix with arbitrary sparsity patterns.

The core idea behind BOFT's parameter efficiency is the use of a butterfly matrix factorization. A butterfly matrix is a sparse matrix that can be expressed as a product of several banded sparse matrices. The butterfly structure is widely used in linear algebra libraries such as BLAS, and it is particularly important for fast matrix-vector multiplication. In BOFT, the orthogonal matrix $R$ is represented as a product of $m$ butterfly matrices: $R = \text{butterfly}_1(\text{butterly}_2(\cdots (\text{butterfly}_{m-1}(\text{butterfly}(v_1, l_1, r_1, v_2, l_2, r_2)\cdots v_m, l_m, r_m))\cdots ))$, where each butterfly matrix $\text{butterfly}$($v_i, l_i, r_i, v_{i+1}$) is a sparse orthogonal matrix that transforms a vector $v_i$ from a subspace of dimension $l_i$ to another subspace of dimension $r_i$.
