Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet powerful approach to finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that maintains the original neuron pairwise angles during adaptation. This is achieved by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, thereby ensuring that the pairwise angles between neurons remain unchanged. OFT can be applied to any pretrained neural network and is particularly effective in maintaining semantic fidelity while adapting text-to-image diffusion models.

The core idea behind OFT is to adapt a pretrained model by learning an orthogonal transformation per neuron. This orthogonal transformation is applied to the pretrained weights, resulting in a new set of weights that are rotated relative to the original ones. In contrast, LoRA modifies weights by adding a low-rank matrix, which does not fully preserve the pairwise neuron angles. In contrast, OFT learns an orthogonal matrix $R$ that transforms the weights via $w^0 \leftrightarrow R \cdot w^0$ and $o^0 \leftrightarrow R\cdot o^0$.
