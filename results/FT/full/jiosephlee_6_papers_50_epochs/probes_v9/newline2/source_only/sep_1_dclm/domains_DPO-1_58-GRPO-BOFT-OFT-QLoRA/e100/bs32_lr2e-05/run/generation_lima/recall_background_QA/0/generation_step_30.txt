The transformer is a type of neural network that has been used to solve many problems in natural language processing. It was developed by Vasileios Panayotov, Richard Socher, and Christopher Manning at Stanford University in 2017. The transformer is based on the idea of using attention mechanisms to focus on different parts of a sequence of tokens (words, characters, or other symbols). This allows the model to learn how to represent relationships between different tokens in a way that is more flexible and powerful than previous models.

One key feature of the transformer is its use of self-attention. Self-attention is a mechanism for computing pairwise similarities between tokens in a sequence. In the context of the transformer, this means that the model can pay attention to different parts of the input sequence and determine which parts are most relevant to the current token. This ability to focus on specific information makes the transformer particularly well-suited for tasks that require modeling complex relationships between tokens.

Another important aspect of the transformer is its usage of feed-forward networks. Feed-forward networks are a type of neural network where the weights between neurons are updated based on the input signal. This allows the model to make predictions about future tokens based on the information it has already learned. The combination of these two components – attention and feed-forward networks – gives the transformer its unique ability to learn long-range dependencies in sequences of tokens.

In summary, the transformer is a type of deep neural network that uses attention mechanisms to focus on specific parts of a sequence of symbols.
