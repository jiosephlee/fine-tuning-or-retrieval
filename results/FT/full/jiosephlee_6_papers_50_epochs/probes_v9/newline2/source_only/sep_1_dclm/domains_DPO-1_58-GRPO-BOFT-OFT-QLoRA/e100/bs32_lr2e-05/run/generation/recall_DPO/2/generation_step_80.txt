Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a reinforcement learning algorithm that optimizes a reward model to maximize the likelihood of the target distribution. In contrast to PPO, DPO does not use any off-policy data, and it can be applied to any reward model, including language models.

The DPO score has several desirable properties: it is a function of the reward model, it is easy to compute, and it is directly comparable to human preferences. This makes the DPO score a powerful tool for evaluating reward models and for comparing different training algorithms.

# What is the problem we are trying to solve?

The problem we are trying to address is how to align a language model with human preferences. There are many ways to define human preferences, but in this paper we focus on pairwise preferences between text generated by a language model and human preferences. A simple way to formalize this is to define a reward model $R$ that maps text to numerical scores representing human preferences. We then want to optimize a language model $M$ to maximize the expected reward under $R$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:reward_model}
    \max_{M} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P_X}\left[ R(M(x), y)\right] = \underset{M}{\operatorname{argmax}} \mathbb{E} \left[ \mathbb{E}_x [R(M(x), y)] \right].
\end{equation}

This is the standard formulation of RLHF, where the goal is to maximize the expected reward from the environment, given the current policy. However, in practice we do not have access to the reward function $R$. Instead, we have access to a language model $M$, and we want to train $M$ to maximize human preferences, given $M$.
