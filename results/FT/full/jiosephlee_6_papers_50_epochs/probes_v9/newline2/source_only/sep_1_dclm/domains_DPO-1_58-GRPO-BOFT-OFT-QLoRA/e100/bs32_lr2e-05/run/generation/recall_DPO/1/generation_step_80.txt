Reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised learning (SL) are two major paradigms in machine learning. They differ in how the learning process is structured and how the data is used. 1. Feedback vs. no feedback: In RL, the algorithm receives feedback about its actions after each step. This feedback is typically in the form of a reward signal, which tells the algorithm how successful its action was. In SL, the algorithm does not receive any feedback until the end of the training process. At that point, the algorithm is evaluated on a test set and given a score or label that indicates its performance.

2. Learning objectives: In RL, the learning objective is to maximize some cumulative reward. This reward is typically associated with the environment the agent interacts with. In SL, the learning objective is typically to minimize some loss function or to maximize some objective function. The choice of objective function is often driven by the task at hand.

3. Data sources: In RL, the data comes from interactions between the agent and the environment. These interactions are typically recorded as sequences of actions and rewards. In SL, the data comes from labeled examples that have been collected by humans or by other automated methods. The data can be either ground-truth labels or noisy labels that have been corrected or refined by human annotators.

4. Training dynamics: In RL, the training dynamics are driven by the agent's actions and the reward signals it receives. The agent takes steps in the environment, and each step is associated with a reward. The agent then uses this reward to update its policy, which is the mapping from actions to probabilities of taking those actions. In SL, the training dynamics are typically driven by the loss function or by a supervised learning objective. The algorithm updates its parameters to minimize the loss function, and the loss function is typically defined based on the ground-truth labels.
