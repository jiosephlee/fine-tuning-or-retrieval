Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have become increasingly powerful and widely used in various applications. However, training these models is computationally expensive and resource-intensive. To address this challenge, researchers have developed various techniques to improve the efficiency of LLM finetuning. One such technique is Orthogonal Finetuning, which uses an orthogonal matrix to adapt a pre-trained model. This method has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LLMs without increasing the number of trainable parameters. However, Orthogonal Finetuning has limitations. For example, it requires the use of a full orthogonal matrix, which can be computationally expensive. Additionally, the block-diagonal approach used in OFT can reduce the number of parameters, but it does not provide a straightforward way to adapt the model with a block-diagonal orthogonal structure.
In this paper, we present Parameter-Efficient Orthogonally Butterfly (PE-BOFT), a novel finetuning method that combines the advantages of both OFT and block-diagonal orthogonal finetuning. We also introduce a block-diagonal variant of BOFT, called Block-Orthogonally Butterfly (BOB), to further reduce the number of parameters. To evaluate the effectiveness of our method, we conduct experiments on several benchmarks, including the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC), the CIFAR-10 dataset, and the General Language Understanding Evaluation (GLUE) benchmark. Our results show significant improvements in performance compared to the standard OFT finetuning method. Furthermore, we demonstrate the applicability of our method to finetuning large LLMs, achieving state-of-theart results on the GLUE and CoNLL-U benchmarks.
