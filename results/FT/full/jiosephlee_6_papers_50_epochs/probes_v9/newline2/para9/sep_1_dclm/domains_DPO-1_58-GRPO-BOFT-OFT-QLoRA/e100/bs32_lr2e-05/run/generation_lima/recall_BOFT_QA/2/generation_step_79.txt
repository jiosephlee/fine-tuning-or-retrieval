What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a method for finetuning neural networks by applying orthogonal transformations to the weights of the network. The idea is to apply a sequence of orthogonal transformations to the weights, each of which is applied to the previous layer's weights. This is in contrast to OFT, which applies a single orthogonal transformation to the entire weight matrix at once. BOFT has been shown to be more effective than OFT in terms of both accuracy and computational cost. In this paper, we present a new variant of BOFT called Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Butterfly (PEBOFT). PEBOFT is designed to reduce the number of parameters required for finetuning while maintaining or improving performance compared to BOFT. To achieve this, we introduce a novel parameter-efficient factorization technique that allows us to express the weight matrices of the network in a block-diagonal form. This allows us to apply a sequence of block-diagonal orthogonal transformations to the network weights, thereby reducing the total number of parameters used for finetuning. We also introduce a new algorithm for computing the orthogonal transformations, which is more efficient than the existing algorithms for BOFT. The results show that PEBOFT can achieve comparable performance to BOFT while using fewer parameters.<|endoftext|>
