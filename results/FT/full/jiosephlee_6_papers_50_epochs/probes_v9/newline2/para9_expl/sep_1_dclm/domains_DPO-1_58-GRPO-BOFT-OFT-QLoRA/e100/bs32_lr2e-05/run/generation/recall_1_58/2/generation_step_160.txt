Quantization

At the heart of many quantization strategies is the principle of representing values with fewer bits than the original floating-point format. The simplest form of quantization is binary quantization, where each value is represented by a single bit. More advanced techniques include low-rank quantization (LRQ), block quantization, and hybrid quantization, which combine aspects of both low-rank and block quantization. Each of these approaches has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of strategy depends on the application and the constraints imposed by hardware or memory.

Low-rank quantization (also known as sparse quantization) is a form of quantization that reduces the number of bits used to represent a value by storing only the nonzero elements of a matrix. In the context of LLMs, low-rank quantization can be applied to the weight matrices of attention layers, the projection matrices of feed-forward networks, or the embeddings used in tokenization. The main advantage of low-rank quantization is that it can dramatically reduce the memory footprint of a model without sacrificing too much accuracy. However, it can also introduce some degree of distortion, particularly when the model is trained on very large datasets. For this reason, low-rank quantized models are often used as a baseline for evaluation rather than as the final product.
