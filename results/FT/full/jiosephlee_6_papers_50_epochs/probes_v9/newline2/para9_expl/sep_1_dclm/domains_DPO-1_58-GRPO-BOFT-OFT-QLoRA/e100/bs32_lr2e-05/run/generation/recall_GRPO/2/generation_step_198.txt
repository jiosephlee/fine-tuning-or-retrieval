Chapter 3: Generative Pretraining and Large-Scale Datasets

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly capable of generating high-quality text, but they often struggle with tasks that require more than simple text generation. For example, LLMs can produce fluent responses to natural language prompts, but they may not be able to answer questions or perform complex reasoning tasks. To address these limitations, researchers have turned to generative pretraining (GP), which involves training LLMs on large amounts of unlabeled data to improve their ability to generate high-quality text. In this chapter, we will explore the principles behind GP, describe the most commonly used generative pretraining datasets, and examine the challenges associated with scaling up LLM training.

3.1 Generative Pretraining Principles

At its core, generative pretraining is a training strategy that aims to improve the language model’s ability to generate high-quality outputs by exposing it to large quantities of unlabeled data. There are several key principles underlying GP that make it effective:

1. Data Sufficiency: The goal of GP is to maximize the diversity of generated outputs, rather than focusing solely on maximizing the likelihood of the target distribution. This means that the model should be exposed to a wide range of different topics, styles, and contexts, rather than being trained exclusively on a single dataset.

2. Diversity of Examples: To ensure that the model learns from a variety of sources, it is important to collect a diverse set of examples. When selecting examples, it is crucial to avoid relying on small, highly curated datasets that may be biased towards certain topics or styles. Instead, the dataset should reflect the diversity of content found in open-source resources.

3. Unlabeled Data: Because LLMs are typically trained on labeled data, the availability of unlabeled data is essential for generative pretraining. Unlabeled data can provide valuable context and nuance that is not present in the labeled data, allowing the model to expand its knowledge base and improve its ability to generate high-quality output.

4. Supervision: While unlabeled data is crucial for generative pretraining, it is important to supplement this with explicit supervision. This can take the form of human annotation, where experts label examples and provide feedback, or through reinforcement learning, where the model is trained to imitate human behavior. By combining unlabeled data with supervision, the model can learn to generate high-quality outputs while avoiding overfitting to the limited labeled data available.
