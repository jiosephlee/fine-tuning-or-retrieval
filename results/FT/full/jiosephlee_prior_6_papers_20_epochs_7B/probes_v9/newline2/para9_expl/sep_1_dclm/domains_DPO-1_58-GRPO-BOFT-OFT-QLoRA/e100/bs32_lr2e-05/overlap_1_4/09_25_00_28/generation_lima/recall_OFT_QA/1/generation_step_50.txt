According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method that is designed to preserve the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model's representational capacity. The key idea is to learn an orthogonal transformation on the pretrained weights, so that the pretrained model and the finetuned model are related by an orthogonal transformation. This is achieved by solving the following optimization problem:

$$\min_{R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}} \|\mathbf{W} - R\mathbf{L}\|_2^2$$

subject to $R^\top R = I$, where $\mathbf{W}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{L}$ is the learned orthogonal matrix, and $I$ is the identity matrix. The orthogonality constraint ensures that the learned transformation is an orthogonal transformation. The orthogonal transformation is learned by solving the following eigenvalue problem:

$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{Q}^\top$$

where $\mathbf{Q}$ is the matrix whose columns are the orthonormal eigenvectors of $\mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{W}$, and $\mathbf{\Lambda}$ is the diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the corresponding eigenvalues. The eigenvalues are constrained to be $1$ or $-1$ by adding the following constraints to the objective function:

$$\|\mathbf{\Lambda}\|_2 \leq \epsilon_1$$
$$\|\mathbf{Q}\|_2 \cdot \|\mathbf{\Lambda}|_2 \leq\epsilon_2$$

where $\epsilon_1$ and $\epsilon_2$ are two hyperparameters. The paper also proposes a variant of OFT, called OFT+, which adds a projection step to the optimization process to ensure that the learned orthogonal matrix is exactly the identity matrix. The projection step is implemented by adding the following constraint to the objective function:

$$(\mathbf{W}^{\text{finetuned}} - \mathbf{W}^{pretrained})^\top (\mathbf{W}^{-1} - \mathbf{L}^{-1}) (\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{L}) = 0$$

The paper also proposes a variant called OFT++, which replaces the identity matrix with a block-diagonal matrix in the OFT+ method.
