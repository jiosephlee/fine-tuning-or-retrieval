QLoRA (Quantized Low-rank Adapters) is a method for finetuning large language models (LLMs) using 4-bit weights and 16-bit or 8-bit activations, while achieving performance comparable to full 16-bit finetunes. The key idea behind QLoRA is to quantize only the adapter modules, leaving the large pretrained model unchanged and operating in high precision. This approach reduces memory usage and accelerates training, since adapters are much smaller than the pretrained model.

# How does QLoRA work?

The core idea of QLoRA is to represent the weight updates (the adapters) in a low-rank format, which allows for efficient quantization. Concretely, QLoRA quantizes the adapters from 16 bits to 4 bits by applying the following steps:

1. **Low-rank Adaptation**: Instead of updating all parameters of the pretrained model, QLoRA only updates a small subset of parameters (the adapters). These adapters are trained to minimize the difference between the original model and the finetuned model. Formally, the updated weight $\mathbf{W}$ is given by $\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{C}^\top \mathbf{W}^0$, where $\mathbf{W}_0^0$ is the pretrained weight, $\mathbf{S}$ is the small subset of trainable weights (the adapters), and $\mathbf{R}, \mathbf{L}$ are learned low-rank matrices.
2. **4-bit Quantization**: The learned adapters are quantized to 4 bits using an absolute-tuning approach. This approach involves training a single shared 32-bit quantization function for all adapters, which is then used to quantize the adapters to 4 bits. The quantization function is defined as $\mathbf{Q}(x) = \frac{1}{32}\sum_{i=0}^{31} s_i \mathbf{e}_i(x)$, where $s_i$ are the quantization constants and $\mathbf{e}_j(x) = \text{exp}(2x\mathbf{e}_je_j^T)$ is the $j$-th basis vector.
3. **Activation Scaling**: To ensure numerical stability, QLoRA scales the activations by the maximum absolute value of the quantization constants across all channels and time steps. Specifically, the scaled activation $\mathbf{a}$ is given by $\frac{\mathbf{a}}{\max_{t,c}|\mathbf{Q}_t(c)|}$.

# What are the advantages of QLoRA?

* **Memory Efficiency**: QLoRA reduces memory usage by only finetuning a small subset of the model parameters (the adapters), while keeping the pretrained model unchanged. This is particularly beneficial for models with large parameter counts, such as 33B and 65B models.
* **Training Speed**: Since the pretrained model is not modified, the forward and backward passes remain fast. Additionally, the low-rank representation of the adapters allows for efficient computation.
* **Performance**: QLoRA achieves performance comparable to full 4-bit finetuning on various tasks, including MMLU and PIQA.
