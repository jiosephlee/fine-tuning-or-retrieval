In GRPO, the advantage estimate is computed as the difference between the advantages of the current policy and a reference policy. This reference policy is updated by sampling trajectories from the current policy. The advantage is defined as the difference between the log-probability ratio of the current policy and the reference policy, normalized by the variance of the reference policy. The normalization by the reference policy's variance is crucial to stabilize training. GRPO was introduced in the DeepSeekMath paper for training large language models on mathematical reasoning tasks. It was found to be more stable than PPO and to achieve better final performance.

The advantage $\adv$ is defined as:
```
$\adv = \frac{\pi_\theta(r,o,s) - \pi_{\theta_{ref}}(r,o,s)}{\beta\sigma(\pi_{\theta_{refer}}(r,o,s))}$,
```
where $\pi_\theta$ is the current policy, $\pi_{\theta_{\text{ref}}}$ is the reference policy, $\beta$ is a coefficient to control the scale of the advantage, and $\sigma$ is the standard deviation. The reference policy $\pi_{\theta_\text{ref}}$ is updated after each training step by sampling a batch of transitions from the current policy $\pi_\theta$.

The normalization by the reference policy’s variance is crucial to stabilize the training process. Without this normalization, the advantage estimate can become very large or very small, causing the policy gradient to be clipped to a very small value, which can lead to collapse of the policy. The normalization by variance is a standard technique in actor-critic methods to stabilize the advantage estimation.

In summary, GRPO is a variant of PPO that uses a reference policy to compute the advantage as the difference between the current policy and the normalized reference policy.
