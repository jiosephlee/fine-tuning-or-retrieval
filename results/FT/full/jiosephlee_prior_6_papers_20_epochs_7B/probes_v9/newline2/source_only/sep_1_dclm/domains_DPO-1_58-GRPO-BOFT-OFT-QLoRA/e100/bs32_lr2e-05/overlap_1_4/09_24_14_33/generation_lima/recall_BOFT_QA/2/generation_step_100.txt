The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as shown in Figure 1, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method for foundation models. It is based on the block-diagonal OFT (BLOFT) but improves its parameter efficiency by using a butterfly structure instead of a block-diagonal matrix. The key idea behind BOFT is to apply OFT multiple times with different parameter subspaces, where these subspaces are defined by the butterfly matrices.

Formally, a $d$-dimensional BOFT butterfly matrix $\bm{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ with $p$ levels is factored as $\bm{B} = \text{diag}(\bm{d}_1,\bm{d}_2,\cdots,\bm{d}_p)$ where $\bm{d}_i \in \mathbbak{R}^{d/2^i \times d/2^{i-1)}$ for $i=1,2,\cdots,p$. The global parameter matrix $\bm{P} \in \mathbf{R}^{n \times d}$ of OFT is then replaced by the block matrix $\begin{bmatrix}
\bm{B} & \bm{0} \\
\bm{0} & \bm{B} \\
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
\dots \\
\bm{B} \\
\dots
\end{bmatrix}$, where there are $2p-1$ copies of $\bm{B}$. To ensure the same number of trainable parameters as OFT, we need to set $n = 2p-1$.<|endoftext|>
