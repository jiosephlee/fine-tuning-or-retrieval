According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (also known as Orthogonal Skill Learning) is a finetuning method that prevents the model from deviating too much from the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. It does so by constraining the weight matrices of the pretrained model to be orthogonal during the finetuning stage. This is achieved by adding a term to the loss function, which is derived from the constraint that the covariance matrix of the weights should be equal to the identity matrix. The term is given by:

$\mathcal{L}_\text{OFT} = \lambda (\text{tr}(C_\theta - I)) - \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{S}_\theta^\top\mathbf{S}_θ\|_F^2$

where $\mathbf{S}_Θ = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\theta^\top_i-\bar{\theta}^\top)(\theta_i-\bar{\theta})$ is the symmetric projection matrix, $n$ is the number of neurons in the layer, $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter, and $\bar{\theta}$ is the mean of the neuron weights in the layer.

The term $\mathcal{L}_Λ$ ensures that the covariance matrix remains the identity matrix. The second term is added to ensure that the projection matrix $\mathbf{S}_{\theta}$ is full-rank. If this term was not included, then $\mathbf{S}$ would be equal to $\mathbf{I}$ for all values of $\lambda$, defeating the purpose of the constraint. The authors found through experimentation that the second term with the current choice of $\lambda$ results in a slight skewness in the distribution of $\mathbf{S}$.<|endoftext|>
