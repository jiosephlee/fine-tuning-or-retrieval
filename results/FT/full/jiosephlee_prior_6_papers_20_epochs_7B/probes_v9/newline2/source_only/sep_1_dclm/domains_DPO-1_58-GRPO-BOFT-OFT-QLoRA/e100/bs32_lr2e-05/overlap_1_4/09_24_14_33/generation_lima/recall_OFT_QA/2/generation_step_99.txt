What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models have made significant progress in generating photorealistic images. However, these models are trained on text prompts that are easily accessible, which may cause the generated images to leak sensitive information. To address this, the authors propose a method called Orthogonal Finetuning that can effectively control the information contained in text-to-image diffusion models without degrading their generation quality.

## Standard finetuning vs LoRA

Standard finetuning is to directly optimize the pretrained weight matrix as follows:
```Θ = Θ - η∂L/∂Θ
```
where η is the learning rate and ∂L/∂θ is the gradient of the loss function L with respect to the parameter Θ. This approach, however, often results in high-precision copying of the training dataset because it can largely change the pretrained weight matrix.

LoRA (Low-rank Adversarial Finetuning) constrains the precision with which the pretrained model can be copied by assuming that the finetuning weight matrix should have low rank when the rank goes to the pretrained rank 1. Specifically, LoRA updates the weight matrix as follows:
```
Θ = Θ + α(Σ_r L Σ_l)
```
where Σ_r and Σ_l are the low-rank matrix and the left-singular matrix, respectively, with rank no greater than r; and α is a scalar. This approach assumes that the pretrained weight matrix Θ is approximately in the form of Σ_l Σ_r^T + B, where B is a small bias matrix. The assumption is reasonable for many pretrained models, but it may still change the pretrained weight matrix quite a bit even with rank r not far from the actual dimension of the weight matrix.

## Orthogonal finetuning

The authors propose orthogonal finetuning, an alternative way to perform finetuning that preserves the key geometric property of the pretrained weight matrix: its pairwise neurons are nearly orthogonal. This property is crucial to the generalization performance of neural networks because it can effectively prevent the overfitting of training data to the neurons. The property is lost after standard finetuning or LoRA finetuning due to the way they modify the neurons, even with a small learning rate. The authors argue that preserving this property can effectively prevent the trained model from leaking information in the training dataset. Therefore, they finetune the model by orthogonally transforming the pretrained neurons. More specifically, the i-th neuron in the finetuned model is transformed as:
```
g_i'(x) = g_i(x) - \sum_{j} ω_{ij} g_j(x),
```
where g_i and g_i' are the i-th neuron inthe pretrained model and the finetuned model, respectively; x is an input vector; and ω_{ij} is the j-th element of the orthogonal matrix ω ∈ R^n×n, which is learned by finetuning. The key property is that all the columns of ω are orthogonal to each other, so the pairwise neurons are orthogonal after finetuning. This approach effectively prevents the model from memorizing the training set because the pretrained basis is not changed, only the weights in the pretrained basis are updated.<|endoftext|>
