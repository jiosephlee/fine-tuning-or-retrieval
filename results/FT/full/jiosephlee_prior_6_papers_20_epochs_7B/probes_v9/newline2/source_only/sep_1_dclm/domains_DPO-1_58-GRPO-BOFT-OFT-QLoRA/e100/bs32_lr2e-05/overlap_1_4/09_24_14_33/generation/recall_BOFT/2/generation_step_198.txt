Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a block-diagonal weight matrix with a butterfly structure. BOFT can be viewed as a generalization of block-diagonal OFT to a block-diagonal weight with a butterfly structure. The key insight behind BOFT is to use a block-diagonal weight structure with a butterfly structure, which enables us to significantly reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving the parameter efficiency of OFT. To achieve this, BOFT introduces a group of learnable permutation matrices to re-order the blocks along the diagonal of the block-diagonal weight matrix. By doing so, BOFT can effectively control the connectivity of the original weight matrix and thus reduce the number of trainable parameter while keeping the parameter efficiency. In addition to the parameter efficiency, BOFT also provies better stability than OFT, since it introduces an additional learnable permutation matrix that can regularize the training dynamics.

# Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning?

The rapid development of natural language processing (NLP) has been largely driven by the continuous improvement of model sizes. However, deploying these large models to edge devices remains a significant challenge due to their high computational and memory costs. Finetuning, a successful approach for improving model performance after pre-training, is also a major contributor to the high computational and memory costs on edge devices. Therefore, one important direction to address the deployment of NLP models is to develop parameter-efficient finetuning methods, i.e., finetuning methods that can improve model performance with significantly fewer trainable parameters than existing methods.

# What is the problem with the block-diagonal OFT?

Although block-diagonal OFT is a parameter-efficient finitunig method, it still has two major problems: (1) it is not parameter-efficient enough; (2) its performance is less stable than OFT. To address these problems, we introduce a block-diagonal weight matrx with a butterfly structure, i.e., BOFT.

# How does BOFT work?

BOFT introduces a block-diagonal weight matrices with a butterfly structure, as shown in Figure 1. BOFT can be interpreted as a generalization of the block-diagonal OFTd to a block-diagonal matrix with a butterfly structure, where the butterfly structure is achieved by introducing a group of learnable permutaion matrices. The butterfly structure endows BOFT with a more flexible control over the connectivity of the original full weight matrix, which enables BOFT to achieve a much lower number of trainable parameters while still preserving the parameter efficiency of the original OFT.

# How well does BOFT perform in practice?

We evaluate BOFT on eleven downstream tasks, including language understanding, dialogue, and translation tasks. On all these tasks, BOFT outperforms the block-diagonal OFTs by a considerable margin. More importantly, BOFT with only 1.5% of the original parameters can match or surpass the performance of the original model. In addition to its strong performance, BOFT also exhibits more stable training dynamics and higher convergence speed than OFT. We provide a detailed experiment report in the paper.

# What are the limitations of BOFT?

BOFT assumes a weight matrix to be a block-diagonal matrix, which may limit its flexibility in representing weight matrices. In the future, we will explore other structures to construct parameter-efficient weight matrices.
