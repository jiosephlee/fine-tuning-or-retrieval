# What is orthogonal finetuning?

Orthogonal finetuning is a method to finetune pretrained text-to-image diffusion models without losing the disentanglement property. It is based on the idea of orthogonal transformation. The original text-to-image diffusion models are trained with a standard cross-entropy loss. Orthogonal finetuning adds an additional loss to constrain the finetuned model to be orthogonal to the pretrained model. This additional loss is defined as:

$L_{ortho} = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{X}-\frac{1}{2}$

where $\mathbf{X}$ is the finetuned model weights and $\mathbf{R}$ is a rotation matrix. The rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{Q}^\top$ is parameterized by $\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ and $\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{x}^{r\times r}$, where $d$ is the dimension of the model weights and $r$ is the number of orthogonal directions. To ensure the orthogonality, we need $\mathbf{D}$ to be a orthogonal matrix, i.e., $\mathbf{D}^\top\mathbb{D}=\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{D}^{\top}= \mathbf{I}$.

To train the rotation matrix, an additional loss is added:

$L_{rot} = \frac{L_{ce}}{2} + \beta (\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{R}\mathbf{X}_0)^\top (\mathbf{X}- \mathbf{R} \mathbf{X}_o) $

where $\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{X}_i$ denotes the forward pass of the finetuned model with the rotation matrix $\mathbf R$ and $\mathbf{X}_ i$ is the pretrained model weights, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter and $L_{ce}$ is the cross-entropy loss.

The overall loss function is:

$L = L_{ce} + \alpha L_{ortho} + \beta L_{rot}$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two hyperparameters.

# Why does orthogonal finetuning work?

The key to understand why orthogonal finetuning works is to understand the orthogonality. Two vectors are orthogonal if their dot product is zero. For matrices, we can define the inner product as the Frobenius inner product. Two matrices $\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}$ are orthogonal if their Frobenius inner product is zero, i.e., $\langle\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}\rangle = \mathbf{A}^\top\mathb{B} = 0$. The orthogonality between two matrices implies that they transform different subspaces. If we have two orthogonal matrices $\mathbf{R}_1$ and $\mathbf R_2$, then $\mathbf{R}_{1}\mathbf{R}_{2}=\mathbf{R}_{ 2}\mathbf{R}_ 1=\mathbf{I}$, where $\mathbf{I}$ is an identity matrix. This means that $\mathbf{R_1}$ transforms the subspace transformed by $\mathbf{R_{2}}$ back to the original space. Therefore, orthogonal finetuning can preserve the disentanglement of the pretrained model because it only changes the subspaces that are not transformed by the pretrained model.

# How to choose the number of orthogonal directions $r$?

The number of orthogonal directions $d$ directly affects the flexibility of the finetuning. A larger $r$ means more flexibility to finetune the model. However, a larger $r$ also brings more parameters and computation cost. In the paper, the authors set $r=64$ for controllable diffusion models and $r=128$ for stochastic diffusion models. They argue that a larger $r$ is needed for stochastic diffusion models because they have more latent dimensions.
