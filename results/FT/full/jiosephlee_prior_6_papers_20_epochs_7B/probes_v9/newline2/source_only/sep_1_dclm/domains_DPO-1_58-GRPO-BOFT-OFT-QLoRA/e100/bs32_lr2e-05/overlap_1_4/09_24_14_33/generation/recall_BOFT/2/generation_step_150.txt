Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a block-diagonal weight matrix with a butterfly structure. BOFT can be viewed as a parameter-efficient version of the block-diagonal OFT method. BOFT has a smaller number of trainable parameters than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, but still yields better performance than OFT and its variant. 2 Related Work

Finetuning large pretrained models on downstream datasets has become the standard approach in many applications, such as computer vision and natural language processing. There are two major categories of finetuning methods: (1) parameter-update methods that update the weights of a pretrained model, and (2) representation-update methods that only update the input embeddings and keep the rest of the model unchanged. Parameter-update methods can be further divided into two categories: (1) small-model methods that use a much smaller model than the pretrained model, and (ii) parameter-efficient methods that reuse most of the parameters of the pretrained model. Among the many parameter-efficient finetuning methods, optimization-based methods such as RFT (Devlin et al., 2019) and LoRA (hulora.ai, 2022) have been widely used. These methods update the pretrained weights using low-rank matrices or layer-normalized matrices. In contrast, method-agnostic finetuning methods such as OFT (qiu2023ortho) and its block-diagonally-constrained variant (Chen et al., 2023) do not make assumptions about the updating mechanism and can be combined with any updating mechanism.

The butterfly structure is a regular sparsity pattern that has been used in many applications, such as fast linear algebra (Ding et al., 1996), graph theory (Sporns, 2007), and network transmission (Butterfly Network Consortium, 2011). A binary matrix is called a butterfly matrix if each row and column contains at most one 1. A binary matrix is called an orthogonal butterfly matrix if each row (or column) is an orthogonal vector. Orthogonal butterfly matrices have a block-diagonal structure with a specific block structure, which we will introduce later in Section 4. The block-diagonal structure has also been used in other finetuning methods (Chen et al., 2023; liu2022fewshot; wang2022blockdiagonal), in addition to OFT. The block-diagonal constraint significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, but it also introduces some limitations such as the inability to share weights across channels. BOFT improves upon the block-diagonal constraint by using a more efficient parameter-efficient structure – the orthogonal butterfly structure.
