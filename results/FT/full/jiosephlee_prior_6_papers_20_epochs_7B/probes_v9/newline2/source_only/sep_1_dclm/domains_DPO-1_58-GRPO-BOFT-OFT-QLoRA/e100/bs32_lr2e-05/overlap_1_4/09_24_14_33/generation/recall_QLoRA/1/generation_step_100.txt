# What is QLoRA?

QLoRA is a method to finetune large language models (LLMs) with low-bit precision. It can be used to finetune LLMs from Huggingface or from OpenAI. It can be used with 8-bit and 4-bit precision.

# Why do we need QLoRA?

Finetuning LLMs with low-bit precision is important because it reduces the memory consumption and the cost of inference. With 16-bit precision, the memory consumption is twice as high as with 8-bit precision, and the cost of inference is also higher. With 4-bit precision, the memory and cost savings are even bigger.

However, finetuning with low-bit precision is difficult. The main problem is that the gradient magnitudes are smaller with low-bit precision, which makes it harder to optimize the model. Another problem is that some operations in the neural network layers, such as ReLU, don't work well with low-bit precision.

QLoRA solves these problems. It improves the gradient magnitudes and it fixes the problems with ReLU and other operations.

# How does QLoRA work?

The key idea of QLoRA is to add a coefficient to the gradients before applying them to the weights. This coefficient is computed based on the statistics of the gradients and the weights. The coefficient amplifies the gradients, so that they have the same magnitude as with high-bit precision.

Let's look at the formula for the coefficient:

Coefficient = sqrt(1 + 8 * sum_weights / sum_gradients)

The coefficient is computed once at the beginning of training, based on the statistics of all weights and gradients up to that point. Then, for each weight update, the coefficient is multiplied with the gradient, and the result is added to the weight.

# What are the benefits of QLoRA?

The main benefit of QLoRA is that it allows us to finetune large LLMs with low-memory and low-cost. For example, finetuning a 7B model with 4-bit precision on a single 80GB GPU takes about 17 hours. Without QLoRA, this would take about 45 hours.

Another benefit is that QLoRA is simple to use. You just need to download the QLoRA pipeline from Huggingface, and then you can finetune your model with a single line of code.

# What are the limitations of QLoRA?

One limitation of QLoRA is its performance with very small models. For example, it performs worse than full 16-bit precision with 1.3B models. However, this is not a big problem because most people don't finetune models smaller than 11B.

Another limitation is that QLoRA doesn't work well with some models from Huggingface, such as T5. This is because these models have some modifications that make QLoRA perform worse. However, OpenAI models work well with QLoRA.
