What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a method for training language models to satisfy user preferences, without explicit reward modeling. It trains a language model by maximizing the probability of a desired output (the "good" output) given a user-provided context and undesirable output (the "bad" output), while minimizing the probability of the undesirable output, given the same context. This can be formalized as:

$$\max_{p_\theta}(q_\theta(\text{desire} \mid \text{context}, \text{output good}), p_\theta(\text{output bad} \mid \text{T, context}}))$$

where $p_\theta$ is a language model parameterized by $\theta$, and $q$ is the preference model, which is trained on pairs of outputs $(\text{context}, \ text{output good}), (\text{context}, \ output bad)$ and returns a score for how much the first is preferred to the second.

This approach differs from standard RLHF methods such as PPO in that it does not fit a reward model. Instead, it uses a single pretrained language model as both the generative model and the reward model. This avoids the need to collect additional data for the reward model, and avoids fitting an explicit reward model that may be unnecessarily complex. However, it also means that the language model cannot be used for any purpose other than evaluating outputs during inference, since it is being used to generate preferences at training time.

DPO has been shown to perform comparably to PPO and other methods for training language models from preferences, but with less instability and less tuning required. It has been used in the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence's annual competition to train language models from preferences (Osterhout et al., 2020), and has been used to train language models from human feedback in open source projects (e.g. (Ranzato et al., 2021)).
