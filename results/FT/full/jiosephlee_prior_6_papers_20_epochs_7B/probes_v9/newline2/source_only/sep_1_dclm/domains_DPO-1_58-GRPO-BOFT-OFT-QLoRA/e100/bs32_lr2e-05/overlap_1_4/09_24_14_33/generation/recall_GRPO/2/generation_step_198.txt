The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory footprint from O(1/ǫ^2) to O(1/Ǩ) where Ǩ << ǫ. GRPO is well-suited for training large models because it reduces the memory footprint per update from O(1/ε^2) to O(log(1/Ǩ)). GRPO is an actor-critic algorithm that optimizes a group of policies simultaneously using a single value function approximation. It generalizes the concept of a reference policy from a single policy to a small set of comparator policies called a group. By comparing a given policy to the average performance of the group of comparator policies, GRPO eliminates the need for a separate reference policy and avoids the need to store multiple policy evaluations. As a result, the memory footprint scales with 1/Ǩ rather than 1/ε^2, enabling the efficient training of very large models with PPO-like stability.

The authors trained DeepSeekMath-Base, a 7B-parameter model, for 150B tokens in a single 48GB GPU machine within 5 days using the GRPO algorithm with a learning rate of 5e-5 and Ǩ=1024. This is a 10x increase in model size over the previous record for a single-machine training (i.e., Alpaca, a 7B model trained for 30B tokens within 3 days). DeepSeekMath-Base achieved a MATH score of 51.5% on the MATH benchmark, surpassing all open-source models and coming within 1 percentage point of GPT-4, which achieved a score of 52.5% on the same benchmark.

In order to further improve the mathematical capabilities of DeepSeekMath-Base model, the authors then developed several techniques to enhance its performance on mathematical reasoning tasks. These techniques include (1) chain-of-thought quantization (COTQ), (2) program-of-thought decomposition (POTD), (3) program-of-thought execution (POTE), and (4) program-of-thought refinement (POR). COTQ is a method for training models to produce the correct numerical answer along with their response, similar to how humans often provide step-by-step reasoning when solving math problems. POTT is a method for breaking down complex mathematical problems into a sequence of executable programs. POTE is a method for executing the programs produced by the POTT module to obtain the final answer. POR is a method for refining the programs produced by the PoT module to make them more efficient.

The authors combined these techniques to create DeepSeekMath-GT, a enhanced version of DeepSeekMath- Base model. DeepSeekMath-GT achieved an unprecedented MATH score of 62.7% on the MATH dataset, surpassing all open source models and coming within 2 percentage points of ChatGPT, which scored 64.7% on the same benchmark. DeepSeekMath-GTA, an enhanced version of DeepSeek-Math-GT model, was evaluated on the competition-level MATH dataset and achieved an accuracy of 65.2%, surpassing all open-source model and coming within 3 percentage points of ChatGTP, which scored 68.5% on the competition-level MATE dataset.
