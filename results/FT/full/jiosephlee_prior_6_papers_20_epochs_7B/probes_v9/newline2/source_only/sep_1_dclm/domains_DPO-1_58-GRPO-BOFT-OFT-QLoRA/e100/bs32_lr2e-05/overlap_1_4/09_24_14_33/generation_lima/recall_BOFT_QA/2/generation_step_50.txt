It builds upon the Orthogonal Finetuning framework, which adds orthogonal transformations to standard finetuning. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to compose orthogonal transformations of different sizes, enabling it to trade off the number of trainable parameters with the amount of computation performed per step.

The orthogonal transformation $W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ at the $i$-th layer is defined as the product of a $d_i \times d_i$ orthogonal matrix $R_i$ and a $d_i \cdot n / d_i$ vector $v_i$:
