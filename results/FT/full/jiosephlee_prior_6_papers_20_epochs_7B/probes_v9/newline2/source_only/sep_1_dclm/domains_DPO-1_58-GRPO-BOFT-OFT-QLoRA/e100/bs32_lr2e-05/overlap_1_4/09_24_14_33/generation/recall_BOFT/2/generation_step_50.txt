# Orthogonal Finetuning and its block-diagonal variants

Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT for short) is a parameter-efficient adaptation method for large pretrained models. It was introduced in the paper "Parameter-Effcient Finetuning of LLMs with Orthogonal Updates". OFT has several nice properties. First, OFT preserves the magnitude of the singular values of the pretrained weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$, because orthogonal matrices preserve the spectral norm, i.e., $\sigma_\text{max}(\mathbf{R})=1$. Second, OFT does not introduce any additional bias, because the expectation of the output $\mathbf{y}$ is the same as that of the input $\mathbf{x}$, i.e., $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{R}\sim\mathcal{R}(1)}[\mathbf{y}]=\mathbf{x}$, where $\mathcal{R}(r)$ denotes the set of orthogonal matrices with spectral norm $r$.

Despite its appealing properties, the original OFT updates all the weights in the neural network, which makes it less parameter-efficient.
