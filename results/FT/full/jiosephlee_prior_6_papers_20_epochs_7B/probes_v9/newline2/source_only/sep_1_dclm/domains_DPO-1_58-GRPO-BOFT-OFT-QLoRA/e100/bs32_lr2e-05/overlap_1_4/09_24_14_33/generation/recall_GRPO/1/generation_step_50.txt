# What is DeepSeekMath?

DeepSeekMath is a mathematical reasoning model based on the DeepSeek-Coder-Large-v1.5 base model. It has 1.3B parameters and is trained on 400+GB mathematical texts from Common Crawl, arXiv, StackExchange, textbooks, and more. The training data is filtered by DeepSeekMath's own data selection pipeline, which is designed to select high-quality mathematical texts. The training objective is to maximize the likelihood of a mathematical passage given its corresponding solution. In addition to the base model, DeepSeekMath also provides two enhanced versions: DeepSeekMath-Instruct and DeepSeekMath-Base-v2.0. DeepSeekMath-Instruct is initialized with DeepSeekMath-Base and then fine-tuned with instruction tuning. DeepSeekMath-Base-V2.0 is initialized with DeepSeekSeekMath-Base and is then trained for an additional 500B tokens.

# How to evaluate mathematical reasoning models?

There are three types of benchmarks for evaluating mathematical reasoning models: automated benchmarks, human evaluation benchmarks, and challenge benchmarks.

Automated benchmarks provide automatic evaluation results for models. They are easy to use and can provide a large number of evaluation results quickly. However, the evaluation results may be biased because they are automatically generated by programs. The most commonly used automated benchmarks include MATH (from Meta.Math), MATHCOMP (created by CodaLab), and Gaokao-MathQA (created by Sogou).

Human evaluation benchmarks require humans to evaluate the performance of models. They can provide more reliable evaluation results than automated benchmarks, but they are more expensive and time-consuming. The most commonly used human evaluation benchmarks include GSM8K (created by DeepSeek) and BMAT (created by OpenAI).

Challenge benchmarks are created by organizing mathematical reasoning challenges. They can provide the most reliable evaluation results, but they are the most expensive and time-consuming. Examples include the MATHCOMP challenge and the DeepSeek Mathematical Reasoning Challenge.
