The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as shown in Figure 1, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method for foundation models. It is based on the block-diagonal OFT (BLOFT) but improves its parameter efficiency by using a butterfly structure instead of a block-diagonal matrix. The key idea behind BOFT is to apply OFT multiple times with different parameter subspaces, but reuse the weights for these subspace transformations. This is realized by the specific form of the butterfly matrix.

The butterfly structure
The core component of BOFT is the butterfly matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{b\times n}$, which is defined as: $$ B = \begin{bmatrix}
B_1 & B_2 \\
B_3 & B_4
\end{bmatrix} $$ where $B_i \in \mathbb {R}^{h_i \times k}$ for $i=1,\dots,4$. The butterfly exponent $\alpha$ is then defined as $h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4 = \alpha k$. To ensure an orthonormal basis, the matrices $B_1$, $B_2$, $B_3$, and $B_4$ are computed as:$B_1 = \text{qxrf}(q_1,\lambda_1,k)$,$B_2 = \text{qxrh}(\lambda_1,q_2,\lambda_2,k)$,
$B_3 = \text{qxhr}(\lambda_2,q_3,\lambda_3,k)$,
$B_{4} = \text{qxrp}(q_4,\lambda_4,\lambda_3,k),$ where $\lambda_i$ are $\sqrt{\alpha}$-th roots of unity, $q_i$ are orthogonal rotation matrices of size $h_i \times h_i$, and $\text{qxrh}, \text{qxrf}, \text{qxrp}, \text{and} \text{qxhr}$ denote the parameterized rotated Hadamard, rotated Fourier, rotated Hankel, and rotated Pauli matrices respectively, which are defined in the paper's Appendix. For the butterfly with exponent $\alpha=2$, the matrices $B_4$ can be simplified to $B_4 = \text{qxhp}(q_5,\lambda_5,\lambda_2,k) = \text{qxhl}(\lambda_5,q_6,\lambda_7,k)\text{qxhr}([\lambda_6,\lambda_5],q_7,\lambda_8,k)\text{qxhp}(qx_7,\lambda_9,\lambda_3,k)$.<|endoftext|>
