According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a block-diagonal weight matrix with orthogonal blocks. It is inspired by the butterfly structure in many linear algebra libraries, such as BLAS and LAPACK, which use block-diagonal matrices with orthogonal blocks for efficient matrix multiplication.

The key idea of BOFT is to replace the dense weight matrix $W$ in a standard finetuning method with a block-diagonal matrix whose diagonal blocks are orthogonal matrices sampled from a orthogonal group. The block size determines how many channels will be updated independently. A smaller block size leads to more independent updates and thus better control over the training, but may also result in more parameters. 

To achieve this, BOFT introduces a block-diagonal matrix factorization, i.e., $W = \text{diag}(R_1, R_2, \cdots, R_s)$ where $R_i \in \mathbb{R}^{b\times b}, \forall i$ are orthogonal matrices and $b$ is the block size. The factorization is unique up to the order of $R_i$. To prevent the number of trainable parameters increasing, BOFT sets $s = \text{log}_b(256)$. This choice is heuristic; other choices are possible.

Compared to LoRA, BOFT has a much smaller effective channel number ($\text{log}_b(ch)$ where $ch$ is the original channel number), but it has the same number of trainable parameters. This makes it more parameter-efficient for wide networks, especially for very wide networks where LoRA becomes extremely parameter-inefficient.<|endoftext|>
