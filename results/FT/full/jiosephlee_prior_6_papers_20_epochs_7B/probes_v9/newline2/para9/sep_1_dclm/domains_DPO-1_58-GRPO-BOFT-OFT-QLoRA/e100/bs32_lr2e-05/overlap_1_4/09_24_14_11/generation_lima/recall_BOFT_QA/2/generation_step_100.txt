### Orthogonal Matrices

An orthogonal matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ satisfies the condition $RR^\top = R^\top R = I$, where $I$ denotes the identity matrix. This property ensures that the column vectors of an orthogonal matrix form an orthonormal basis, meaning they are mutually orthogonal unit vectors. As a result, orthogonal matrices preserve the length of vectors and the angle between them when transforming space. This makes them particularly useful in various applications, such as rotation and reflection transformations.

A special class of orthogonal matrices is the orthogonal group, denoted by $\mathbf{O}(n)$, which consists of all $n \times n$ orthogonal matrices with determinant 1. The orthogonal group plays a crucial role in many areas of mathematics and has wide-ranging applications in computer graphics, physics, and engineering.

### Butterfly Structures

The resulting factorization forms a butterfly structure, which can be represented by a product of several sparse matrices called butterfly matrices.
