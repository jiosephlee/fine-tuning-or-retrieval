According to the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", what is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a method for training language models to be more aligned with human preferences, by learning a reward function from preference data. The key idea is to use the language model itself to approximate the reward function, rather than learning a separate reward model. This is done by maximizing the likelihood of the preference data, under the constraint that the reward function has mean zero. In other words, we seek to maximize:

$$\mathbb{E}_{(x, y, y') \sim D, \text{model}} [\log(p(y \mid x, reward = r(x, y) - 0.5))]$$

subject to the constraint that $\mathbb{E}_{x, y \sim D, \textrm{model}} [r(x, y)] = 0.5$. This can be solved using a method called Lagrange multipliers, yielding the following objective:

$$\max_{\log(p(y \mid y, x))} \mathbb{E}_{\text{model}}[\underbrace{(q(y, y') - 0.5) \log \sigma\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x, y, y'} q(y, y', x)\right)}_{\text{reward}(x, y, y')}], $$

where $q(y, y', x)$ is a reward function, and $\sigma$ denotes the logistic sigmoid. In practice, $q(y, y ', x)$ is initialized as the language model's probability estimate $p(y, x)$, and then optimized to better match the preference data. The resulting reward function is then used to normalize the model probabilities, as in $\frac{1}{Z(x)}\cdot p(y, x)$, where $Z(x) = \sum_{y} p(y, x) \text{reward}(x,y)$.<|endoftext|>
