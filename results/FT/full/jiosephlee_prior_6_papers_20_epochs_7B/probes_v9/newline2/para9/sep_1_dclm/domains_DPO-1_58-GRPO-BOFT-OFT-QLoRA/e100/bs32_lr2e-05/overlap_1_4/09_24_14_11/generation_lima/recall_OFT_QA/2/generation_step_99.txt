What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that maintains the pairwise angular relationships between neurons. This is achieved by applying the Cayley transform to the weight matrix, which results in an orthogonal transformation. The Cayley transform is defined as $Q = \text{Cayley}(A) = \text{det}(A) \cdot \text{exp}(A) - \text{I}$, where $A$ is a skew-symmetric matrix and $\text{I}$ is the identity matrix. In the context of neural network finetuning, $A$ is constructed as $-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{-1}$, where $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix of the weights after a principal components analysis (PCA). This approach ensures that the transformed weight matrix $Q$ is orthogonal, i.e., its columns are mutually orthogonal unit vectors.

To better understand the concept of orthogonal transformations, consider the following example. Suppose we have two vectors $v_1$ and $v_2$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the following inner product:
$$
\langle v_1, v_2\rangle = 0.
$$
This means that these vectors are orthogonal to each other. Now, if we apply an orthogonal transformation $Q$, the transformed vectors $Qv_1$ and $(Q^\top) v_2$ will also be orthogonal. This property is crucial because it allows us to preserve the pairwise angles between neurons during finetuning. 

In addition to OFT, the authors also introduce a variant called Angular Consistent Finetuning (ACFT), which modifies the loss function instead of modifying the parameters directly. ACFT aims to minimize the difference in angular consistency between the pretrained and finetuned models. While OFT achieves this by performing an explicit orthogonal transformation on the weights, ACFT does so by adjusting the loss function. Although ACFT performs similarly to OFT, it lacks the interpretability of OFT and requires additional computational resources for calculating the angular consistency loss.

The name "angular consistent" refers to the fact that OFT maintains the pairwise angular relationships among neurons. To illustrate this, consider two neurons $u$ and $v$ in the pretrained model. Their activation vectors $z_u$ and $z_v$ form an angle $\theta$ in the hypersphere. After finetuning, their transformed activation vectors $Qu$ and $Qv$ should still form the same angle $\theta$. This property is important because it helps to retain the semantic knowledge learned during pretraining.
