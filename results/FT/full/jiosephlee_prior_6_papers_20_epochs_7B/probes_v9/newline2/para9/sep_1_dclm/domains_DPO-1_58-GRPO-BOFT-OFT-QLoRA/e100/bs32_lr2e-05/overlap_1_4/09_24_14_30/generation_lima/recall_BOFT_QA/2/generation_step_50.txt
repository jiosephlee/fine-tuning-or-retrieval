An orthogonal matrix $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ satisfies $Q^\top Q = Q Q^\top = I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix. This property ensures that the length of vectors is preserved under a rotation or reflection described by $Q$. A special case is the identity matrix $I$, which is its own orthogonal matrix. Another important property is that the determinant of an orthogonal matrix has absolute value 1, meaning it is either 1 or $-1$.
