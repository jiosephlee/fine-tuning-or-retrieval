What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) method for training large language models (LLMs). It is an actor-critic RL algorithm that uses a surrogate loss function, the so-called "PPO loss", to train the model. The PPO loss is defined as:

$$\text{clip}\left(\frac{\pi_\theta(y \mid x)}{\pi_\text{old}(y \mid x)}; 1 - \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon\right)^\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(Y \mid X)}{\pi_\text{lwf}(Y \mid X)}$$

where $\pi_\text{lwf}$ is a reference policy, typically the maximum likelihood estimate over the training data. This loss has two main drawbacks: it requires keeping track of the old policy and it involves a clipping step which can be unstable. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRP) is a variant of PPO that addresses these issues by comparing the model's performance to other models within the same group. GRPO defines the following loss:

$$\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\pi_{\theta_i}) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim \mathcal{D}}\bigg[\underbrace{\eta \log \sigma(\hat{A}_{\theta_i, x, y_w}/\hat{A}_{\phi, x, y_l})}_{\text{reinforcement signal}} - \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi_{\theta_i}/\pi_{\phi}]\bigg]$$

where $\hat{A}_{\psi, x, y_w}$ is the advantage computed using $y_w$, and $\pi_{\phi}$ is a reference policy sampled from the current model group. The KL term in the second term serves to regularize the optimization. In GRPO, we no longer need to keep track of the old policy, and the clipping in the PPO loss is implicitly handled by the reparameterization of the advantage. 

In DeepSeekMath, GRPO is used to train LLMs on mathematical tasks. To further reduce memory usage, they introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization with Memory Efficient Actor Critic (GRPO-MEC), which replaces the full critic network with a single scalar value. This is achieved by estimating the advantage using only the current policy rather than both the current and previous policies. They show that this approach still yields strong results.

In summary, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRAM) is a memory-efficient variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPA) that avoids the need to store the old policy and uses a more stable reparameterized advantage.<|endoftext|>
