PPO works by estimating the advantage $\hat{A}$ of an action $a$ using a baseline $b$, and then updating the policy $\pi$ by maximizing the following surrogate objective:
\[
\text{maximize} \qquad \frac{\pi(a | s)}{\pi_\text{ref}(a | s)} \text{ exp } \frac{1}{\beta} \hat{A}_{\pi(s), \pi_\text{ref(s)}}(a, s)

where $\pi_\text{ref}$ is a reference policy, typically the current policy $\pi$. This maximization is performed element-wise, and the resulting policy is then normalized so that its expected value matches that of the reference policy. The normalization step is necessary to prevent the policy from diverging, but it can cause high variance in the reward signal, which can lead to instability during training.

To address this normalization step, the Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO) algorithm uses a group-based normalization approach. In GRPO, the policy is trained by maximizing the following objective:
\[
\frac{\pi(a |s)}{\pi_\text{{ref}}(a | s)} \exp \frac{1}{ \beta} \sum_{g} \hat{A}^g_{ps,a,s}(1 + \frac{1}{|g|} \sum_{a' \in g} \text{exp} \frac{\hat{A}^G_{ps,a',s}}{\beta})

The normalization is performed over the group of actions, rather than over the single action as in PPO. The reference group is constructed by sampling $|G|$ actions from the current policy $\pi$ at each time step, where $|G|$ is the group size. This group-based normalization reduces the variance in the reward signal and allows for more efficient training of LLMs.
