According to the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", what is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a method for training language models to be more aligned with human preferences. It does not require any human preference annotations on text, but only pairwise comparisons of texts. DPO can be interpreted as learning a reward function and then optimizing it using a reparameterized RL objective.

The core idea behind DPO is that if a language model $p(\cdot \mid w)$ is trained on a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, we can express the likelihood of the data as:
$$
p(\mathcal{D} \mid p(\cdot \mid w)) = \prod_{i=1}^NP(x_i \mid w),
$$
Taking the logarithm, this becomes a negative log-likelihood:
$$
-\log p(\mathcal{D}\mid p(\cdot \middle|\mathbf{w})) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log p(x_i\mid \mathbf{w}).
$$
This is the objective that is optimized during maximum likelihood estimation. Now, suppose we have an alternative model $q(\cdot \mid w)$. We can define the Kullback–Leibler divergence between these two models as:
$$
\mathrm{KL}\left(p(\mid w)\mid \mid q(\mid w)\right) = \sum_{x}p(x\mid w)\log\frac{p(x\mid w)}{q(x\mid w)}.
$$
If we assume that the alternative model $q$ is also sampled from the same distribution as the maximum likelihood model, i.e. $q = p$, then the KL-divergence simplifies to:
$$
\mathrm{kL}\left(p(\mid \mid w)\mid \middle| p(\mid \mid w)^{\mathrm{MLE}}\right) = \mathbb{E}_{p(\mid \mid w)}\left[\sum_{x}\log\frac{p_x}{p_{x,\mathrm{MLE}}(x)}\right],
$$
where $p_x$ denotes the value of the model under parameter vector $w$ for input $x$, and $p_{x,\mathrm{{MLE}}} $ is the maximum likelihood estimate for the model with parameter vector $w$. This is the objective that is minimized by the DPO algorithm. The key insight is that the KL-divergence can be interpreted as a reward function, and the objective above is equivalent to maximizing this reward function. By reparameterizing the reward function, the optimization problem becomes equivalent to minimizing the following objective:
$$
-\mathbb{E}_{x_1,\ldots,x_N\sim p(\mid \mid  w)}\left[\underbrace{r(x_1,x_2) + \cdots + r(x_{N-1}, x_N)}_{=\text{reward for comparing all pairs}} - \underbrace{r(x_{\leq N-1}, x_N) + r(x_1,\cdots,x_{N-1})}_{=\text{reward for stopping after $(N-1)$ pairs}}\right].
$$
