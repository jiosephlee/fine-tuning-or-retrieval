What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks while preserving the original model's performance and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The key idea behind OFT is to maintain the pairwise angles between neurons during finetuning. This is achieved by ensuring that the matrix formed by stacking all neurons in a layer is orthogonal, i.e., its inverse equals its transpose. 

To better understand the concept of orthogonal matrices, consider a rotation in two dimensions. A counterclockwise rotation of an angle $\theta$ can be represented by the following matrix:
```[ cos(theta) -sin(theta) ]
[ sin(theta)  cos(theta) ]
```
Multiplying this matrix by its transpose yields the identity matrix, which demonstrates that it is an orthogonal matrix. In general, an $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix has determinant $1$ or $-1$, and it represents an $n$-dimensional rotation or reflection.

In the context of neural networks, the authors propose to learn an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, such that the transformed neuron remains on the same hyperplane as the original one. To achieve this, they introduce a new parameter $R$ in the forward pass, and update it using the following formula:
```R = R * R^T
```
Here, $R$ is initialized as the identity matrix, ensuring that the initial transformation is the identity. This approach guarantees that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved, since the cosine of the angle between any two neurons is equal to the dot product divided by the product of their norms, both of which are multiplied by the same orthogonal matrix. Consequently, the cosine of the angle remains unchanged.

It is important to note that OFT does not require any additional parameters compared to the original model. It only modifies the forward computation by introducing the matrix $R$. During backpropagation, the gradient of the loss with respect to $R$ is computed, and $R$ is updated using the rule:
```R = R*R^T
```
