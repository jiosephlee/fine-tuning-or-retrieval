It is an extension of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). In PPO, the policy gradient is optimized with respect to a reference trajectory sampled from the current policy. In GRPOP, the reference trajectory is sampled from a group of trajectories, each generated by a different member of a set of parallel actors, all sampling from the same policy. This approach allows for more efficient exploration and better exploitation of the reward signal.

Here is how it works:

1. For each prompt, generate $N$ samples from the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$ using $N$ different actors.
2. For each sample, compute the advantage $\hat{A}_{t}(s_{t}, o_{t})$ using the selected baseline.
3. Calculate the group advantage $\mu$ as the mean over the $N$ advantages, and the group standard deviation $\sigma$.
4. Compute the per-sample PPO loss using the group mean and standard deviation.
5. Use the per-sample losses to update the parameters $\theta$.

The main idea behind GRPO is that it uses a group average and standard deviation to estimate the uncertainty of the reward signal. If the rewards are highly variable across the $N$ samples, the group mean is used; if they are consistent, the group mean is close to the individual means and the standard deviation is low. This allows the algorithm to exploit high-quality samples while still encouraging exploration.<|endoftext|>
