According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning technique that maintains the pairwise angular similarity between neurons. This is achieved by enforcing the Gram matrix of the pretrained weights to be an orthogonal matrix during finetuning. The method was introduced in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion with orthogonal finetuning" by Anirudh Goyal, Lekhong Mane, and Alec Radford.

The idea behind OFT is to maintain the pairwise relationships between neurons during finetuning. In other words, the angle between any two neurons should remain similar after finetuning as it was before. This can be formalized by requiring the pretrained weight matrix $W$ to have a Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{W}$ that is full rank. During finetuning, the Gram matrix is required to be an orthogonal matrix, i.e., $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}^\top = \mathbf{G}^{\top\top\mathbf{G}} = \mathbf{I}$. This can be enforced by applying the Cayley transform to the Gram matrix at every training step.

In practice, OFT is applied by initializing the finetuned model with the pretrained weights and then updating them according to the following rule:

$\mathbf{W}(t+1) = \mathbf{R}(t)\mathbf{W}(k+1) = \mathrm{Cayley}(\mathbf{G}(t))\mathbf{W}(kt+1)$

where $\mathbf{W}(0) = \mathbf{T}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{G}(0) = \mathrm{Gram}(\mathbf{W}(1))$, and $\mathbf{R}(0) = \operatorname{Id}$. The Cayley transform is given by:

$\mathrm{Cayley}(X) = \operatorname{slogdet}(X) X^{1/2} \operatorname{exp}(1/2 \operatorname{LogDet}(X))$

Here, $\operatorname{slogdet}$ denotes the symmetric logarithm of the determinant, and $\operatorname{LogDet}$ denotes the symmetric logarithmic derivative. These are both linear transformations that map symmetric matrices to symmetric matrices. For a detailed derivation, see the original paper.

OFT has several advantages over standard finetuning techniques. First, it allows for more precise control over the behavior of the model. Second, it is robust to hyperparameter changes. Third, it is compatible with different types of pretrained models. Finally, it can be combined with other finetuning methods.<|endoftext|>
