According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning technique that maintains the pairwise angular similarity between neurons. This is achieved by enforcing the Gram matrix of the pretrained weights to be an orthogonal matrix during finetuning. The method was introduced in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion with orthogonal finetuning" by Anirudh Goyal, Lekhong Cheung, and Alec Radford.

The idea behind OFT is to maintain the pairwise relationships between neurons during finetuning. In other words, the cosine similarity between any two neurons should remain unchanged after finetuning. This can be formalized as follows: let $W$ be the pretrained weight matrix, $\theta$ be the learned finetuning parameters, and $G = \text{diag}(\|\mathbf{w}_1\|, \|\mathbf{w_2}\|, \cdots, \|\mathbf{k}\|)$ be a diagonal matrix where $\mathbf{k}$ is a vector of length $d$ and $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm. Then the objective of OFT is to minimize
\[
\|\mathbf{W} - \mathbf{W}_{\text{pretrain}}\|=
\|\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{S}(\theta)\|=
\|\mathbf{\hat{S}}(\theta)\|=
\mathbf{\hat{D}}(\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{\hat{\Sigma}}(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{ D}}(\theta)^{-\frac{1}{2}},
\]
where $\mathbf{W}_{pretrain}=\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{D}^\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\hat{ S}}(\theta)\mathBF{D}^{-\frac{1}{ 2}}$, $\mathbf{\hat{  S}}(\theta)=\mathbf{ S}(\theta)-\mathbf{I}$, $\mathbf{\hat  D}(\theta)=\mathBF{D}^\frac {1}{2}\mathbb{1}_d\mathbf{D }^\frac {1}{ 2}$, and $\mathbb{1}_n$ is an $n$-dimensional all-one vector. Here, $\mathbf{S}=(\mathbf{w}_i^\top\mathbf{w_j})$ is the Gram matrix of the neurons, and $\mathbf{D}=(\|\mathbf{w_i}\|)$ is a diagonal matrix of neuron norms. The first equality holds because $\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{ W}_{pretrain}+\theta$, and the second equality follows from the definition of $\mathbf{\hat S}$ and $\mathbf{\hat D}$. The third equality is due to the fact that $\mathbf{I}- \mathbf{S}+(\mathbf{S}-\mathbf{I})=(\mathbf{I}- (\mathbf{S}- \mathbf{I}))$. The goal of OFT is to keep the difference between the pretrained and finetuned models at the level of the hyperspherical energy, which is defined as $\|\mathbf{\hat S}(\theta)\|$. The hyperspherical energy measures how much the pairwise angles between neurons have changed. If the hyperspherical energy is zero, then the pairwise angles between neurons are unchanged. This is equivalent to making the Gram matrix $\mathbf{W}$ orthogonal, i.e., $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^\top=\mathbf{W}^2=\mathbf{W}\overline{\mathbf{W}}= \mathbf{Q}$, where $\overline{\mathbf W}$ is the transpose conjugate of $\mathbf{W}(e.g., \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}, \overline{\mathbf  W} \in \mathbf{R}^{n\times d})$.
