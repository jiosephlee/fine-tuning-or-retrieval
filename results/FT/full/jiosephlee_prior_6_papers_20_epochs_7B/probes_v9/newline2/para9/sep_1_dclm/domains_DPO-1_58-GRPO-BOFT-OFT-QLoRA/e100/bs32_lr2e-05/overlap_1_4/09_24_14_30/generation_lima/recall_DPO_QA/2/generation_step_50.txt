This approach is sometimes referred to as Bradley-Terry learning, after the Bradley-Terry model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) which is used to learn a reward function from such preference data. DPO has been shown to achieve performance comparable to reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) methods, but without the need for sampling, model-based planning, or reinforcement learning.

In DPO, the language model is trained to maximize the likelihood of preferred outputs, conditioned on the preference signal.
