However, PPO requires keeping track of a running mean and variance for each action, which can be memory-intensive for very large action spaces. To address this issue, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRP), a variant of PPO that reduces the memory complexity from $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}|)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log |\mathcal{A}|)$. They also introduce an extension called Group Relative Policy Optimization with Actor Critic (GRPO-AC), which further reduces the memory usage by eliminating the need for a running variance estimate.

## Proximal Policy Optimization (PPA)

Proximal Policy Optimization is a reinforcement learning algorithm used to train large-scale actor-critic models. It is a modification of the actor-critic method that uses a surrogate objective function to improve stability during training. The goal is to maximize the following objective:

$$\max_{\pi_{\theta}} \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta}\leftarrow \text{Sampler}( \pi_{\text{ref}} )}  \left[  \frac{\pi_\theta(a | s)}{\pi_{\text{refs}}(a | s)}  \right]^{ \beta}  \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}[\pi_\phi(s, a) \mid \mid \pi_\theta(s, a)]  \]

Where $\pi_{\text{Refs}}$ is the reference policy sampled from the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$, $\pi_\phi$ is the model being trained, and $\beta$ is a clipping coefficient. This objective is optimized using the following surrogate function:

$$\mathbb{J}_{\text{PPO}}(\pi_{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{{\pi}_{\theta} \sim \Pi}  \left[ \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{s\sim \pi_{\theta}(s\mid s_{0})}  \min_{\pi \in \mathcal{C}}  \left[  {\gamma} \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\phi}(\cdot \mid s, a)} \left[  \text{RELS} \right] - \beta \mathbb{D}_\text{KL}  \left[ {\pi_{\phi}(s, a) \mid\mid \pi(s, a)} \right]  \right]  \]

Where $Z$ is the partition function, $\mathcal{C}$ is the constraint that $\pi$ must be within a clipping range of $\pi_{\text {PPO }}$, and $\text{RELS}$ is the reward signal. The reward signal is defined as:

$$\text{RELS} =  \mathbb{E_{(s, a, r, s') \sim \pi(s, a, r, su)}  [r + \beta \mathbb{kL}(\pi_{\phi} \mid \mid \hat{\pi}(s, a, r))]} $$

Where $\hat{\pi}(s,a,r,s)$ is the best policy found among a set of policies sampled from $\pi_{\theta}$. In practice, it is more efficient to use a single roll-out of length $T$ instead of sampling multiple trajectories. The KL term is then approximated using the advantage estimator $\hat{A}_{\pi}(s, a) = \widetilde{V}_{\pi}(s) - \widetilde{E}_{\pi}(a | s)$, where $\widetilde{V}$ and $\widetilde{E}$ are value and expectation estimates. This leads to the following PPO update:

$$\pi_{\theta} \leftarrow \pi_{\theta}  \text{soft-argmax}  \left[ 
\pi_{\theta}(a | s)  \exp \left( \frac{\hat{A}_{\phi}(s,	a)}{\epsilon}  \right)  - \epsilon  \right] $$

Where $\epsilon$ is a small value to prevent the soft-max operation from collapsing to the maximum action.

## Group Relative Policy Optimization (GPO)

Memory consumption becomes a significant issue when dealing with large action spaces, since PPO requires storing the running mean and variance for every action. To mitigate this, the authors propose GroupRelative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which replaces the per-action statistics with group statistics. Actions are grouped into sets based on their index, and the mean and variance are calculated for each group. At each step, a new action is sampled from the current policy, and the reward is observed.
