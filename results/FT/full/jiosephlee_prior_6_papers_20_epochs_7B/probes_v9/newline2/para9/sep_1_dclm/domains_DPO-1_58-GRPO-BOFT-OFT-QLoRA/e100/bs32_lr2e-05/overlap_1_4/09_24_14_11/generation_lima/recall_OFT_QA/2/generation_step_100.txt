What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks while preserving the original model's performance and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The key idea behind OFT is to maintain the pairwise angles between neurons during finetuning. This is achieved by ensuring that the matrix formed by stacking all neurons in a layer is orthogonal, i.e., its inverse equals its transpose. 

To better understand the concept of orthogonal matrices, consider a rotation in two dimensions. A counterclockwise rotation of an angle $\theta$ can be represented by the following matrix:
```
[ cos(theta) -sin(theta) ]
[ sin(theta)  cos(theta) ]
```
Multiplying this matrix by its transpose yields the identity matrix, which demonstrates that it is an orthogonal matrix. In general, an $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix has determinant $1$ or $-1$, meaning it represents a rotation or a rotation combined with a reflection in $n-1$ dimensions.

In the context of neural networks, the authors propose to learn an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, such that the transformed neuron remains on the same hyperplane as the original one. To achieve this, they introduce a new parameter $Q$ in the forward pass, which is initialized as the identity matrix. During training, the matrix $Q$ is updated to ensure that the product $Q\mathbf{W}$, where $\mathbf{W}$ is the weight matrix, remains orthogonal. This is accomplished by applying the following update rule to $Q$ at every training step:
```
Q := Q + R
```
where $R$ is a randomly sampled orthogonal matrix. By construction, the product $Q\,\mathbf{W}$ remains orthogonal, ensuring that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved.<|endoftext|>
