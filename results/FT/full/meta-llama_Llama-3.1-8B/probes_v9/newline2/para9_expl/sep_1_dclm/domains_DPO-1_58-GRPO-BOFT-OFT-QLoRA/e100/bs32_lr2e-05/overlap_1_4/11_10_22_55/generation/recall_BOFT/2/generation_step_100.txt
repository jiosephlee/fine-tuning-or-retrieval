# Parameter Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning

## Introduction

Adapting large pretrained models to downstream tasks has become increasingly popular due to their strong performance. However, fine-tuning these large models is often computationally demanding, especially when the number of trainable parameters needs to be reduced. To address this issue, researchers have developed various parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques. One effective PEFT approach is to modify the weights of the pretrained model by multiplying them with a learnable orthogonal matrix. This method, known as Orthogonal Finetuning, preserves the pairwise angles between neurons and maintains the hyperspherical energy of the network, which has been linked to improved generalization. Although Orthogonal Finetuning achieves strong results, it still requires a relatively high number of trainable parameters compared to other PEFT strategies. To further reduce the number of trainable parameters, a block-diagonal structure can be incorporated into the orthogonal matrix, but this comes at the cost of decreased flexibility. In this work, we present a new parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning method called Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft), which is inspired by the butterfly structure found in the Cooley-Tuksy algorithm. Our approach is based on decomposing the orthogonal matrix into a sequence of sparse orthogonal matrices, each containing only two non-zero entries per row. This design enables us to significantly decrease the number of trainable parameters without compromising the expressiveness of the orthogonal matrix. We evaluate our method on a range of natural language processing tasks, demonstrating that BOft achieves superior parameter efficiency compared to existing orthogonal finetuning approaches. Additionally, we explore the relationship between the butterfly structure and the fast linear transform. Our findings suggest that the butterfly structure is capable of representing a broad spectrum of linear transforms, including the Fourier, Hadamard, and sine/cosine transforms.

## Background

### Pre-Training and Fine-Tuning

Large-scale pre-training on extensive datasets has emerged as a highly effective strategy to enhance the generalization capabilities of neural networks. The resulting pretrained models can then be further adapted to specific downstream tasks through fine-tuning. During fine-tuning, only a subset of the model’s parameters are updated, while the remaining weights are kept fixed to those obtained during pre-training. This approach, known as “parameter-efficient fine-tuning” (PEFT), aims to minimize the number of trainable parameters while maximizing performance. Various PEFT techniques have been developed, including adapter tuning, prompt tuning, and LoRA. Among these, LoRA modifies the weight matrices by incorporating a low-rank matrix, thereby achieving strong results with minimal parameter overhead.
