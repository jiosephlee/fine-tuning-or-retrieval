The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a reinforcement learning technique that uses a group of sampled outputs from the current policy to compute advantages, which are then used to update the policy. This approach reduces the need for a separate critic model, thereby lowering computational costs. GRPO was applied to DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B, resulting in significant gains in mathematical reasoning on benchmarks such as GSM8K and MATH, with an improvement of 20 percentage points over the initial model.

The paper also introduces DeepSeekMath, a dataset containing 144B tokens, which is 10 times larger than the previous largest math-focused dataset. DeepSeekMath is derived from OpenWebMath, which itself is a compilation of mathematical content from the web. To ensure high-quality data, OpenWebMath undergoes several stages of filtering and cleaning. First, HTML tags and non-English text are removed, followed by the application of a set of rules to eliminate low-quality pages. Next, the remaining content is clustered based on URL similarity, with only one representative from each cluster retained. Finally, the dataset is further refined through human annotation and machine learning-based classifiers. The final OpenWebMath dataset contains 42.9B tokens, of which 28.2B are English. DeepSeekMath is created by translating the non-English portion of OpenWebMath into English using the Google Translate API, resulting in a total of 144B tokens.

DeepSeekMath Corpus is divided into three parts: pre-training, in-domain fine-tuning, and out-of-domain fine-tuning. The pre-training phase utilizes the entire corpus for 100 billion tokens. For in-domain fine-tuning and out-of-domain fine tuning, the training data is split into 80% and 20%, respectively. In-domain fine-tuning involves training on 115B tokens, while out-of-domain fine-tunes on 29B tokens. The out-of-domain data includes natural language corpora, code, and other supplementary materials. The authors report that this multi-stage training strategy yields better results compared to a single-stage approach.

The evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Base 1.3B focuses on mathematical reasoning tasks, including quantitative reasoning (e.g., GSM8K, MATH), geometry (e.g., GeoNaturalLanguage), and formal mathematics (e.g., miniF2F). The model demonstrates strong performance across these areas, often surpassing or matching the capabilities of much larger models like Gemini Pro and GPT-4 on English benchmarks. Notably, DeepSeekMath-Base achieves 51.7% accuracy on the challenging MATH dataset, outperforming all existing open-source base models and most proprietary alternatives.

To assess the effectiveness of reinforcement learning on mathematical reasoning, the authors conduct experiments using both DeepSeekMath-Base and DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B. The results indicate that reinforcement learning substantially enhances the model's mathematical reasoning abilities, particularly in domains where it initially underperformed. For example, on the MATH dataset, RL improves the model's accuracy from 45.6% to 51.7%. Additionally, RL contributes to improvements in other mathematical tasks, such as GSM8K (+6.0%) and GeoNaturalLanguage (+3.9%).
