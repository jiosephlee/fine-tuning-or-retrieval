# DeepSeekMath: Pushing Limits of Mathematical Reasoning

## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have shown remarkable progress in mathematical reasoning, yet their capabilities remain limited compared to professional mathematicians. In this work, we present DeepSeekMath, a suite of LLMs that achieves state-of-the-art performance in mathematical reasoning. Our models are pre-trained on a dataset of 100 billion tokens, comprising 85% mathematical content sourced from Common Crawl and 15% from high-quality math-specific corpora. We conduct extensive evaluations of our models' mathematical reasoning abilities, including quantitative reasoning benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH, and MMLU-STEM, as well as competition-style assessments like MATH and OCW. On these benchmarks, DeepSeekMath-7B outperforms all open-source base models and ranks second overall, while DeepSeekMath-33B surpasses all previously released open-source foundation models. Additionally, we introduce DeepSeekMath-Instruct and DeepSeekMath-Chat models, which are fine-tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback and demonstrate superior performance compared to other open-source instruction-tuned and chatbot models. Our findings indicate that pre-training on a large-scale, high-quality mathematical corpus can substantially enhance mathematical reasoning capabilities in LLMs.

## 1 Introduction

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs), particularly those based on the Transformer architecture [vaswani2017attention], have led to significant improvements in mathematical reasoning abilities [minerva2023, ouyang2022training, dai2024atai, tung2022llemma, liu2023few]. Nevertheless, the performance of these models still lags behind that of professional mathematicians [yuan2023mathile]. One key reason for this gap is the scarcity of high-quality mathematical training data, which hinders the development of LLMs capable of performing at par with human experts in mathematics. To address this issue, we propose a novel data collection pipeline that leverages Common Crawl to assemble a massive, high-quality mathematical dataset. Our approach involves first identifying web pages containing mathematical content through a combination of heuristics and human annotation, followed by applying a series of filtering techniques to remove low-quality material. The resulting dataset, DeepSeekMath Corpus, comprises approximately 85 billion tokens, of which 85% originate from Common Crawl and the remaining 15% from curated math-focused resources. This corpus is roughly ten times larger than the largest publicly available math-focused dataset [gpt-f] and is comparable in size to the largest proprietary datasets [minerva2023].

We utilize the DeepSeekMath Corpus to train DeepSeekMath, a family of LLMs that includes models with parameter counts ranging from 0.7B to 33B. Our evaluation focuses on assessing the models' proficiency in mathematical reasoning, spanning both quantitative and qualitative domains. For quantitative reasoning, we employ benchmarks such as GSM8k [gsm8k], MATH [MATH], and MMLU-STM [mmlu], which measure performance across a spectrum of difficulty levels. Additionally, we evaluate the models' ability to solve problems from competitive mathematics examinations, including the MATH competition [MATH] and the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) [imo]. Qualitative reasoning is assessed through the ability of the models to provide formal proofs in both informal and formal theorem provers, as well as their capacity to generate rigorous mathematical arguments.
