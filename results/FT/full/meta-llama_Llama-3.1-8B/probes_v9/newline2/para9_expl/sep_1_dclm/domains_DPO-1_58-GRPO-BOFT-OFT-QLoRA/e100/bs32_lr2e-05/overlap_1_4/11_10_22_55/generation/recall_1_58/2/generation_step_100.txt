## Background

### Floating-Point Formats

Floating-point formats are widely used in modern computing systems to represent real numbers with varying degrees of precision. Two commonly employed formats are IEEE 754 binary32 (also known as single precision) and IEEE 754 binary64 (double precision). Both formats utilize a sign bit, an exponent field, and a mantissa (or significand) field. The binary32 format has a 1-bit sign, an 8-bit exponent, and a 23-bit mantissa, providing a total of 24 bits of information. The binary64 format doubles the size of each component, resulting in a 52-bit mantissa. These formats are designed to offer a trade-off between precision and computational efficiency.

### Quantization

Quantization refers to the process of converting data from one representation to another with fewer bits. In the context of neural network training, quantization is often applied to weights and activations to reduce the memory footprint and computational cost. There are two primary approaches to quantization: symmetric quantization and asymmetric quantization. Symmetric quantization centers the input data around zero, whereas asymmetric quantization centers it around a non-zero value. While symmetric quantization is simpler to implement, asymmetric quantization generally yields better empirical results.

### Low-rank Adapters

Low-rank adapters are small modules inserted into a pretrained model to fine-tune or adapt it to a specific task. These adapters typically consist of a few fully connected layers with a low-dimensional hidden state. By keeping the original pretrained parameters fixed, the adapter approach allows for efficient task-specific training while minimizing the impact on inference latency.

### BitNet

BitNet is a novel neural network architecture that operates exclusively on binary inputs and outputs. It employs a custom activation function, called the Sigmoid Binary (SigmoidB) function, which maps real values to the set {−1, +1}. BitNet utilizes a binary cross-entropy loss function and is trained using stochastic gradient descent.
