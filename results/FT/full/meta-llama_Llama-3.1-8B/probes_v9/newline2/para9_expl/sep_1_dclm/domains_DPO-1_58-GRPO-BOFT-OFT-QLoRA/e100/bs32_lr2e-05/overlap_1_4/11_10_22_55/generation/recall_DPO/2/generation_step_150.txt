## What is the problem?

Preference-based learning is a powerful way to train language models that produce high-quality, human-like outputs. The basic idea is to collect human preference data—pairs of model outputs for the same prompt, where humans indicate which output they prefer—and then use these preferences to guide the model’s training so that it produces outputs that humans prefer more often. This approach has been shown to yield significant improvements in language model quality, especially when the models are trained on instruction-tuning datasets.

However, current preference-based learning pipelines are often complex and involve multiple stages. Typically, the first stage involves fitting a reward model to the preference data, and the second stage uses reinforcement learning (usually PPO) to optimize a policy to maximize the expected reward from the learned reward model. This two-stage process can be cumbersome and may require careful hyperparameter tuning to avoid instability.

## What does the paper do?
