# Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Butterfly Finetuning

## Introduction

Adapting large pretrained models to downstream tasks is a crucial step in modern machine learning pipelines. However, fine-tuning such large models is often computationally expensive and memory-intensive due to the large number of parameters involved. To address this issue, a variety of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques have been developed, including LoRA [1], prompt tuning [2], and the more recent OFT [3]. Among these, OFT stands out as a particularly attractive approach because it adapts the pretrained weight matrix by left-multiplying it with a learnable orthogonal matrix, thereby preserving the pairwise angles between neurons and maintaining the hyperspherical energy [4] of the original model. This property helps to maintain the semantic structure learned during pretraining, which in turn enhances generalization and reduces the risk of catastrophic forgetting [5].

Despite its advantages, OFT still requires a relatively large number of trainable parameters, especially when applied to large models. To improve parameter efficiency, OFT typically employs a block-diagonal structure for the orthogonal matrix, where each diagonal block operates independently. While this block-diagonal design reduces the number of trainable parameters, it also limits the expressiveness of the orthogonal transformation, which can negatively impact downstream performance. Furthermore, the optimal choice of block size remains an open question: using a small block size increases parameter efficiency but may not provide sufficient expressiveness, whereas a larger block size improves expressiveness at the cost of more parameters.

This work proposes a new, parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuing method called Orthogonal Butterfly (BOF). BOF constructs the orthogonal matrix by composing multiple sparse orthogonal matrices using the butterfly structure [6]. This structure allows us to represent a dense orthogonal matrix with far fewer parameters than the block-diagonal approach in OFT, resulting in a substantial reduction in the number of learnable parameters. Additionally, we introduce a variant called Cayley BOF, which leverages the Cayley transform to guarantee that the composed orthogonal matrix is full-rank, even if the constituent sparse orthogonal matrices are not. This is important because a singular orthogonal matrix would otherwise result in a loss of information during fineturning. We also develop a theoretical framework for analyzing and comparing the expressivity of different orthogonal finetuning methods, showing that the butterfly structure can capture a larger subset of the entire orthogonal group than the block diagonal approach, which explains why BOF is more expressive and thus more capable of adapting the model to downstream tasks. Empirically, we observe that this increased expressivity leads to improved generalization and better performance on downstream tasks. Moreover, we propose a practical technique for further reducing the number parameters by sharing the same orthogonal matrices across different layers, without compromising performance.
