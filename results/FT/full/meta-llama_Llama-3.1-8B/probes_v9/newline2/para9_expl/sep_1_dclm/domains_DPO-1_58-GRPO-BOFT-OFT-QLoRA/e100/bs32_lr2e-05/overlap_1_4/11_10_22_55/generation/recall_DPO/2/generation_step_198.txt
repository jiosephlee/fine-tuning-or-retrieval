# Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model

## Summary

This paper introduces a new, simple, and effective algorithm called Direct Preference Optimization (D.PO) for training language models to follow human preferences. Unlike previous methods that first learn a reward model from preference datasets and then use reinforcement learning to optimize a policy for that reward, D.PO directly optimizes the policy using a reparameterized Bradley-Terry model. The result is a single-stage, end-to-end training procedure that is both conceptually and practically simpler than RLHF pipelines. The paper shows that, under mild assumptions about the reward function, D.PO implicitly optimizes the same expected reward objective as traditional RLHF methods, so it can be seen as an alternative, more stable way to perform reward model optimization. Empirical results demonstrate that D.PO matches or exceeds the performance achieved by PPO-based RLHF methods on summarization and instruction-following tasks, and it is more sample-efficient and easier to tune.
