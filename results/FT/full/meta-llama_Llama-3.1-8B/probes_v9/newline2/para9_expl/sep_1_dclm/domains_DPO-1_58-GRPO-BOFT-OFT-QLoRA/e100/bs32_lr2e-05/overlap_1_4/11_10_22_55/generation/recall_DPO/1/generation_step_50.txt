But instead of using RL to learn a policy, it uses RL to learn a reward function and then optimizes the policy with respect to that reward function. This approach is called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The paper shows that DPO can be used to train LMs that are as good as or better than those trained with PPO, but without the need for an RL fine-tuning stage.

The paper starts by reviewing the RLHF process. In RLHF, we first train a language model on a large corpus of text. Then, we fine-tune this model with supervised learning to match human preferences in a dataset of text pairs. Finally, we fine-tune the model again with reinforcement learning, using the reward function learned during the supervised phase as the reward signal. The RLHF process is shown in Figure 1.

The paper points out two problems with this process. First, the reward function learned during supervised learning may not be optimal for the RL objective. Second, the RL fine-tuning stage is computationally expensive. To address these issues, the paper proposes DPO, which directly optimizes the language model to maximize the probability of generating preferred responses, given a set of preference data.
