## Background

### Bit-Width Reduction in LLMs

Reducing the bit-width of LLMs has been explored as a method to improve their efficiency. Most previous studies have focused on quantizing the model weights and activations to lower bit-widths, such as 4-bit or 8-bit representations. These methods typically involve rounding or clipping the values to the nearest representable value in the target data type, followed by dequantization during inference. While these approaches can achieve notable reductions in memory usage and computational cost, they often result in performance degradation compared to full-precision models.

### Binary Neural Networks

Binary neural networks (BNNs) are a class of models where all weights and activations are represented using a single bit, either 0 or 1. BNNs have been shown to be computationally efficient and capable of achieving reasonable performance on certain tasks. However, standard binary activation functions, such as the sign function, lack differentiability, making it challenging to train BNNs using gradient-based methods. To overcome this limitation, several approaches have been proposed, including the use of smooth approximations of the sign function and the introduction of ternary weights.
