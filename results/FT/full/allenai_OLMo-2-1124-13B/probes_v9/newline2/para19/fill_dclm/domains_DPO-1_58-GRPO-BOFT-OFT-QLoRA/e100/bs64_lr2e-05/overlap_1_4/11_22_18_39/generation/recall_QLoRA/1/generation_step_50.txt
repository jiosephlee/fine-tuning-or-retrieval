## What is QLoRA?

QLoRA is a method for finetuning large language models (LLMs) that uses quantization to reduce memory usage. Quantization is the process of converting a model from 32-bit floating point numbers to 8-bit integers. This reduces the memory footprint of the model by a factor of 4, making it possible to finetune much larger models on a single GPU.

QLoRA is an acronym for "Quantized LLMs via Re-parameterization and Adapters". It consists of two main components: re-parameterization and adapters.

### Re-parameterization

Re-parameterization is a technique that allows us to convert a 32-bit model into a 8-bit model without any loss in accuracy. The idea is to introduce a small amount of randomness into the model during training, so that the model can learn to compensate for the loss of precision caused by quantization.

Here's how it works: during training, we generate a random number between -0.5 and 0.5 for each 32-bit weight. We then add this random number to the weight, and quantize the result to 8 bits. During inference, we simply use the quantized value without adding any randomness.

This approach is called "straight-through estimator" (STE), and it has been shown to work well for quantizing neural networks. However, it doesn't work for LLMs because the weights in LLMs are much larger than in typical neural networks. To address this, QLoRA introduces a scaling factor that is applied to the random number before adding it to the weight. This scaling factor is learned during training, and it ensures that the quantized weights are close to the original 32-bit weights.

### Adapters

Adapters are a technique for finetuning a pretrained model without modifying its weights. Instead, we add a small number of additional parameters, called adapters, that are trained from scratch. This allows us to finetune the model without having to retrain the entire model, which can be computationally expensive.

In QLoRA, adapters are used to adjust the quantized weights so that they match the distribution of the original 32-bit model. This is necessary because the quantization process introduces some error, and the adapters help to correct for this error.
