Related Work
Adapting large pretrained models to downstream tasks is a widely adopted approach. The most common method is to train the model from the beginning, using only the final layer as a trainable parameter (Howard et al., 2018;Vu & Le, 2021;Mou et al., 2021;Herath et al., 2022). This approach is straightforward, but it is not parameter-efficient. To address this, LoRA (Hulora et al., 2020) introduces a low-rank adaptation method that is applied to the weight matrices of the pretrained model. While LoRA is parameter-efficient, it does not guarantee the preservation of the original pretrained model's semantic information. To address this,  introduce a method that uses a block-diagonal orthogonal transformation to adapt pretrained models. This method, referred to as OFT, ensures that the semantic information of the pretrained model is preserved while also being parameter-efficient. OFT has been shown to achieve superior performance compared to LoRA. OFT has also been extended to incorporate a block-wise strategy (Xiao et al., 2023) and to improve the stability of training (Xiao et al.). In addition, OFT has been applied to a variety of tasks, including few-shot learning (Xiao et al.;Chen et al., 2024), natural language processing (Xiao et al.), and computer vision (Xiao et al.).
The butterfly structure has been used in a variety of applications, including fast linear transforms (Vetterli & Kovačič, 1995), efficient matrix-vector multiplication (O'Leary, 1980), and efficient neural network training (Dettmers et al., 202 1). The butterfly structure has also been used to improve the efficiency of OFT (Xiao et al, 2023). In contrast to previous work, this paper introduces a novel parameter-efficient finetuning approach based on the butterfly structure.
