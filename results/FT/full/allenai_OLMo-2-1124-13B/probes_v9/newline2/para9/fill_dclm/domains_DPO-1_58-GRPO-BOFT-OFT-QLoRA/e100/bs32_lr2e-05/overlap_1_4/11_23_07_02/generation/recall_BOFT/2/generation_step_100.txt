Parameter-Efficient Finetuning
LoRA (Hulora, 2021) is a widely used parameter-efficient finetuning approach. LoRA reduces the number of trainable parameters by applying low-rank matrices to selected layers in the pretrained model. While LoRA is effective, it does not fully exploit the parameter-efficiency of orthogonal matrices. OFT (Qiu et al., 2022) addresses this by employing orthogonal matrices for finetuning, which not only preserves the norm of neurons but also maintains the pairwise angles between neurons, thereby better preserving the semantic information of the pretrained model. BOFT extends OFT by constructing orthogonal matrices using the butterfly structure, which further enhances parameter-efficiency. OFT-F generalizes OFT and BOFT by introducing a factorization approach to orthogonal matrices. This framework allows for the exploration of additional orthogonal matrix factorization methods. BOFT and OFT are special cases of OFT-F. In addition, OFT-F provides a unified perspective on parameter-efficient finetuning. Reptile (Nichol et al., 2018) is a model-agnostic meta-learning method that adapts a model by updating its parameters in the direction of the average of the gradients computed for a set of training samples. Reptile is a general method for parameter-efficient finetuning, but it is not as parameter-efficient as OFT or BOFT. BOFT and OFTF are more parameter-efficient than Reptile because they only update a subset of the pretrained model's parameters. BOFT and OFFT-F are designed specifically for finetuning, whereas Reptile is a more general method.

Butterfly Structures
Butterfly structures are widely used in fast linear transforms (O'Leary, 2010;Vetterli & Kovačević, 1995), including the Cooley-Tukey algorithm (Cooley & Tukey, 1965), which is a divide-and-conquer approach for efficiently computing the discrete Fourier transform. The butterfly structure is also used in network design (Li et al., 2017;2018;2019;2018;2018;2017;2018), where it is used to reduce the number of network parameters. BOFT leverages the butterfly structure for constructing orthogonal matrices, which enhances parameter-efficiency in finetuning.
