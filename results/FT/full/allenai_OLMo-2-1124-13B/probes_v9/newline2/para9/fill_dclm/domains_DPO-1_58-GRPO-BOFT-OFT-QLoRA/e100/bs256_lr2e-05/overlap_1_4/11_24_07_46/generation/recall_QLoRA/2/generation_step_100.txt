Background
This section provides an overview of quantization and finetuned language models.

Quantization
Quantization is the process of converting a high-precision data type, such as a 32-bit floating-point number, to a lower precision data type, such as an 8-bit integer. This technique is commonly used to reduce the memory footprint of models and accelerate their execution. Quantization is typically performed during the training phase, where the model's weights are converted to a lower precision data format. During inference, the quantized weights are dequantized to the original data type to maintain accuracy.

There are two primary types of quantization: post-training quantization and quantization-aware training. Post-training quantization involves quantizing the weights of a pretrained model without modifying the training process. This approach is straightforward to implement but often results in a loss of model accuracy. Quantization-aware training, on the other hand, incorporates quantization into the training process. This method typically yields better results than post-training quantization, but it is more computationally intensive.

Finetuned Language Models
Large language models (LLMs) are trained on massive datasets to learn general language understanding and generation capabilities. These models are typically pretrained on a large corpus of text and then finetuned on a specific task, such as question answering or dialogue generation. Finetuning involves updating the parameters of the pretrained model using a smaller dataset that is relevant to the target task. This process allows the model to adapt to the new task while retaining its general language understanding capabilities.

The standard approach for finetuning LLMs involves loading the pretrained model weights into memory and performing gradient descent updates on these weights. This process is computationally intensive and requires significant memory resources, limiting the size of models that can be finetuned on a single machine.
