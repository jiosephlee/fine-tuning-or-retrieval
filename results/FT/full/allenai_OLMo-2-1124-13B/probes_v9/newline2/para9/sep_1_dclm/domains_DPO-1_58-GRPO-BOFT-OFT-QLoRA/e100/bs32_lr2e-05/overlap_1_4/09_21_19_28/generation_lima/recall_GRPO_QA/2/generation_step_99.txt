What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Here is a brief overview of Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO ), a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO ) that is designed to be more memory-efficient. 

In reinforcement learning (RL), PPO is a popular actor-critic method for training policies from large-scale datasets. In PPO, a parameterized policy is optimized by maximizing a surrogate objective that encourages the updated policy to be similar to the old policy while also increasing its likelihood relative to the old policy. The key idea behind PPO is to introduce a clipping mechanism to the surrogate objective, which ensures that the updated policy remains close to the old policy. This clipping mechanism is implemented by penalizing the surrogate objective when the proposed update increases the likelihood ratio (i.e., the probability ratio between the new and old policies) beyond a certain threshold. While this approach is effective, it requires maintaining two models during training—one for the old policy and one for the new policy—resulting in significant memory usage. To address this issue, GRPO was developed as a memory-efficient alternative. Instead of storing the old policy, GRPO computes the likelihood ratio using a reference policy sampled from a group of policies. This approach allows GRPO to significantly reduce memory consumption while maintaining the effectiveness of PPO.

More formally, GRPO maximizes the following objective:

$$
\mathcal{J}_{GRPO}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}{[r + \gamma \hat{v} | s \sim \Omega, a \sim \pi_{\theta_{old}}(a | s)]} \\
+ \beta \mathbb{E}{{\left[\min{\left( \frac{\pi_\theta(s | s) / \pi_{\thetaold}(s | s)}
{1 + \varepsilon \left( \frac{\π_θ(s | s)/\π_{\thetaold}( s | s) - 1 \right)}, 1 \right)}\right)}}
$$

where $\pi_{\thetaold}$ refers to the previous policy, $\pi_\theta$ is the updated policy, $\varepsilon$ is a clipping parameter, and $\beta$ is a weighting factor. The main difference between GRPO and PPO lies in the computation of the baseline in the likelihood ratio. In GRPO, the reference policy used to compute the baseline is drawn from a group of policies rather than the previous policy. This approach enables GRPO to substantially reduce memory usage compared to PPO, making it feasible to train large language models on a single machine.<|endoftext|>
