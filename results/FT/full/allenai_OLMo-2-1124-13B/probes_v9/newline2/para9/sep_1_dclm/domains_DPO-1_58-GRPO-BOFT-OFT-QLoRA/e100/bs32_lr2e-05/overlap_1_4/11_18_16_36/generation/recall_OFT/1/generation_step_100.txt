Finetuning is a widely adopted approach for customizing pre-trained LLMs to perform specific tasks, such as question answering or summarization. However, a significant challenge associated with finetuning is catastrophic forgetting—the tendency for the model to lose previously acquired knowledge while acquiring new skills. To mitigate this issue, orthogonal finetuning was developed.

In traditional finetuning, the model's parameters are updated using gradient descent, adjusting the weights to optimize performance on the target task. In contrast, orthogonal finetuning introduces a constraint that maintains the orthoginality of the weight matrix during the update process. This constraint ensures that the angle between any two vectors within the matrix remains constant, effectively preventing the collapse of the hypersphere onto a lower-dimensional subspace. As a result, the original model's knowledge is preserved, and catastrophic forgetting is largely avoided.
