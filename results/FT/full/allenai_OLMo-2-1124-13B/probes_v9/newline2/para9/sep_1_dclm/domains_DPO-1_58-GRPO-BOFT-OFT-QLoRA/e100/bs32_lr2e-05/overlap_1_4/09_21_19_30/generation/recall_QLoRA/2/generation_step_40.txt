2.1 Quantization

Quantization is a technique for reducing the precision of numerical values while minimizing information loss. It involves mapping a set of input values to a smaller set of output values, typically integers or fixed-point numbers. This process can be lossy, meaning that some information may be discarded during the conversion. However, by carefully selecting the output range and employing appropriate quantization strategies, it is possible to maintain high levels of accuracy.

Quantization has been widely adopted in various domains, including computer vision, audio processing, and natural language processing. In the context of neural networks, quantization is often applied to weights and activations to decrease memory consumption and accelerate computations. For instance, 8-bit quantization is commonly used in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), where it enables efficient deployment on mobile devices and embedded systems. Similarly, 16-bit quantization is prevalent in large language models, allowing for the training of models with billions of parameters on consumer-grade GPUs.

2.2 Quantization in Neural Networks

Neural networks consist of interconnected nodes, or neurons, organized into layers. Each neuron receives inputs from preceding layers, performs a series of mathematical operations, and produces an output that is subsequently transmitted to downstream neurons. The primary components of a neuron are its weights and biases, which determine the strength and direction of connections between neurons. During training, these parameters are adjusted to minimize the difference between predicted and actual outputs, thereby enhancing the model's predictive capabilities.

Quantization is typically applied to both weights and activations within a neural network. Weights are the primary focus of this paper, as they constitute the majority of a model's memory footprint. Nonetheless, it is essential to consider the impact of quantization on activations, particularly during the backward pass, as this stage is crucial for updating model parameters.

2.2.1 Quantizing Weights

Weight quantization entails converting a set of floating-point weights into a smaller format, such as integers or fixed-point numbers, while preserving their relative magnitudes and relationships. This process can be accomplished through various methods, including uniform quantization, which divides the range of input values into equal intervals and assigns each value to the nearest interval endpoint; and non-uniform quantization, which employs a more sophisticated mapping function to distribute quantization errors more evenly across the input domain.

A common approach to weight quantization is to first normalize the weights, ensuring that they fall within a specified range, typically between -1 and 1. This normalization step facilitates the application of uniform quantization, as it simplifies the mapping of input values to output bins. Once normalized, the weights are quantized to a lower precision, such as 4-bit or 8-bit integers.
