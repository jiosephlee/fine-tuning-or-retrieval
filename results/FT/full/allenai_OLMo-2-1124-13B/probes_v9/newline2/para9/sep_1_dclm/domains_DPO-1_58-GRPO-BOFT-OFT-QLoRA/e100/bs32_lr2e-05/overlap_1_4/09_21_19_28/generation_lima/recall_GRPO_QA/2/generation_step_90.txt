What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Here is a brief overview of Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO ), a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO ) that is designed to be more memory-efficient. 

In reinforcement learning (RL), PPO is a popular actor-critic method for training policies from large-scale datasets. In PPO, a parameterized policy is optimized by maximizing a surrogate objective that encourages the updated policy to be similar to the old policy while also increasing its likelihood relative to the old policy. The key idea behind PPO is to introduce a clipping mechanism to the surrogate objective, which ensures that the updated policy remains close to the old policy. This clipping mechanism is implemented by penalizing the ratio of the surrogate objective to a critic model. While this approach is effective, it requires maintaining two copies of the policy model in memory during training, which can be computationally expensive for large models.

To address this memory issue, GRPO introduces a new baseline for the clipping mechanism. Instead of using the old policy as a baseline, GRPO uses the average policy across a group of actors. This approach allows GRPO to maintain only a single copy of the policy model in GPU memory during training, thereby reducing memory usage. The intuition behind this approach is that the average policy within a group of actors serves as a reasonable baseline for the clipping mechanism, since it represents the typical behavior of the policy. By using this baseline, GRPO can effectively limit the updates to the policy while still allowing for significant improvements in performance.

More formally, GRPO maximizes the following objective:

$$
\mathcal {J}_{GRPO}(\theta) = \mathbb {E}{[r + \gamma \hat{a}|o]\}\text{ s.t. }\frac{\pi_\theta(o|\hat{o})}{\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{i\neq j}\pi_{\theta_i}(o|\hat{o})} \leq \text{clip}^{\varepsilon}\left(1 + \frac{\pi_\theta{o|\hat{o}} - \pi_{\theta_{\sim\theta}}(o|\hat{o})}{ \sigma \hat{a} + \varepsilon}, 1 - \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon\right)\pi_\theta(o|\ hat{o})

Here, $\pi_{\theta}$ denotes the current policy, and $\pi_{\theta_i}$ denotes the policies in the group of actors, with $i\neq j$. The expectation is taken over a batch of samples from the replay buffer, and $\varepsilon$ and $\sigma$ are hyperparameters that control the clipping range. The key difference between GRPO and PPO is that the baseline in GRPO is given by the average policy across the group of actors, rather than the previous policy.
