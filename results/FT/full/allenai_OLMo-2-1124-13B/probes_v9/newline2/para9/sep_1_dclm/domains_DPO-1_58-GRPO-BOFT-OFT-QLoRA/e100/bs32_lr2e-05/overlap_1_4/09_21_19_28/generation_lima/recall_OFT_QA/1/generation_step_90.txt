Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) is a finetuning method that ensures the pairwise angle between neurons in a layer is preserved during training. This is done by updating neurons in a layer such that the cosine of the angle between them is preserved. This is achieved by updating the neuron $h_i$ with the following update rule:

$$h_i = h_i + \alpha \frac{\partial E}{\partial h_i} o_j, \quad j \ne i \Leftrightarrow \cos(\theta_{ij}) = \cos(\theta'_{ij})$$

Where $\theta_{ij}$ is the angle between neuron $h_i$ and $h_j$ and $\theta'_{ij}$ is the desired angle between $h_i$ and  $h_j$ after the update. The update rule can be rewritten as:

$$h_i = \cos(\theta_{ji})\frac{\partial E}{ \partial h_i} o_i - \sin(\theta_{ji}) \cos(\theta_{jk})\frac{\partial  E}{ \partial h_j} o_k, \quad j \ge i \ge k$$

Where $\theta{ji} = \pi - \theta{ij}$ is the angle from $h_j$ to $h_i$. This can be further simplified to:

$$h_i = M_{ji} \frac{\partial E} { \partial h_i}  o_i, \quad j \succ i$$

Where the matrix $M_{ji}$ is defined as:

$$M_{ji} = \begin{cases}
\cos(\theta_{ji}), & i \ne j \\
I - \sum_{j \succ k \succ i} \sin(\theta_{jk}) \cos(\theta{ji})M^{-1}_{jk}, & i = j
\end{cases}$$

Where $I$ is the identity matrix. The matrix $M_{ji}^{-1}$ can be precomputed for each layer and stored to allow for efficient updates. The matrix $M^{-1}_{ji}$ can be computed recursively as:

$$M_{ii}^{-1} = I - \sum_{j\ne i}\cos(\theta_{ji} )M_{ji}^{- T} $$
$$M_{ji}^{- 1} = I -\sum _{k \succ j \succ i} \frac{\sin(\theta_{jk}}{\cos(\theta_{ji}}) M_{kj}^{-1}, \quad j \neq i$$
