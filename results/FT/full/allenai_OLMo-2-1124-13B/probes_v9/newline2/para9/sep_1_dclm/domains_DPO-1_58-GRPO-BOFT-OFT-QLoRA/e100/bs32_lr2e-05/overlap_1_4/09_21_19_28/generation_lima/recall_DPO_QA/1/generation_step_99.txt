We introduce direct preference optimization (DPO), a new framework for training large language models that uses human preferences as the sole form of supervision. In DPO, the model is trained to produce outputs that are preferred over their generative counterparts, as expressed by a binary preference model. This simple approach eliminates the need to estimate a reward model, infer optimal responses, or conduct costly human preference judgments for generated output pairs. Instead, DPO trains on a loss function derived from the preference model that can be efficiently optimized using standard backpropagation. We demonstrate that this method is robust and effective by training a 6B parameter language model on a dataset of human preferences that was collected without reference to any reward model. The resulting model outperforms a commercially available model on a challenging natural language understanding dataset, and matches or exceeds the performance of a much larger (73B) baseline model on a range of benchmarks for natural language understanding, reasoning, and commonsense reasoning.
