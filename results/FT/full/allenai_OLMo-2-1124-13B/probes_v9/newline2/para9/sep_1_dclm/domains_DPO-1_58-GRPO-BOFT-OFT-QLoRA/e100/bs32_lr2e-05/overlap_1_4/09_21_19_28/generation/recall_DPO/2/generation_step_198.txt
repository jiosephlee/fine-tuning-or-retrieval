### The Problem: Preference Alignment

The goal of preference alignment is to train a language model to generate responses that humans prefer over other candidates. This is typically done by collecting human judgments on pairs of responses generated by two different language models, one of which is the baseline model and the other is the model being evaluated. These human judgments are then used to train the model being evaluated to increase the probability of generating the response that humans prefer. The standard approach for preference alignment involves training a language model using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), where the language model is trained to maximize a reward function that reflects human preferences. However, RLHF methods are computationally intensive and require significant resources to implement.

### Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (or RLHF) is a technique for training language models to align with human preferences. In this approach, a language model is trained to optimize a reward function that reflects the preferences of human annotators. The reward function is typically learned from human preference data using a binary classification model, such as a logistic regression or a neural network. The language model is then trained to maximize the expected reward, using a technique called reinforcement learning. This process involves sampling responses from the language model, evaluating them using the reward function, and updating the model parameters to increase the probability of producing high-reward responses. While RLHF has been shown to produce language models that are well-aligned with human preferences, it is computationally intensive and requires a large amount of preference data to train the reward function accurately.
