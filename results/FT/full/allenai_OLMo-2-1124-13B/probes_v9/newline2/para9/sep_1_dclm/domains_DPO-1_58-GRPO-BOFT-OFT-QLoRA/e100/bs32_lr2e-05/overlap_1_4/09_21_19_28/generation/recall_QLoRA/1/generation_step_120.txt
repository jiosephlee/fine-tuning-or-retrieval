## Background

### Large Language Models

Large language models (LLMs) are neural network architectures designed to process and generate natural language. These models are trained on massive datasets consisting of text samples, such as Wikipedia articles or social media posts. During training, LLMs learn to predict the likelihood of subsequent words given a sequence of preceding words. This process enhances the model’s ability to capture linguistic patterns and relationships within the data, enabling it to perform various natural language processing tasks.

Recent advancements in LLMs have led to the development of models with billions of parameters, including OpenAI’s GPT-3 and ChatGPT, which exhibit impressive capabilities in areas such as translation, summarization, and code generation. Despite their successes, these models remain prohibitively expensive to train due to their enormous size. Consequently, there is a need for methods that can reduce the computational cost associated with LLMs while preserving their effectiveness.
