In reinforcement learning, PPO is a policy optimization method that is widely used for training large language models. The standard implementation of PPO maintains a parameterized policy and an independent value function, and it updates the policy by performing gradient ascent on a differentiable objective. This objective is designed to encourage the policy to generate samples that have high estimated value while also staying close to a behavior policy, which is typically the previous version of the policy model. The key component of this objective is a “clipping” mechanism that restricts how much the updated policy can diverge from the behavior policy. This mechanism is implemented by clipping the “action advantage ratio,” which is defined as the exponential moving average of the ratio of the probability assigned to the action by the updated policy, divided by the probability assigned to the same action by the behavior policy. To compute this ratio for every action at every training step, the behavior policy must be evaluated for each sample in the dataset, which can be computationally expensive. The standard approach to reducing this cost is to maintain a set of behavior policies, with each corresponding to a subset of the dataset, and to evaluate only the behavior policy that corresponds to the given sample. In GRPO, the action advantage ratio is instead computed relative to a single reference policy, rather than relative to the full set of behaviors policies used in PPO. This simplification allows the method to achieve a significant reduction in memory usage without sacrificing performance.
