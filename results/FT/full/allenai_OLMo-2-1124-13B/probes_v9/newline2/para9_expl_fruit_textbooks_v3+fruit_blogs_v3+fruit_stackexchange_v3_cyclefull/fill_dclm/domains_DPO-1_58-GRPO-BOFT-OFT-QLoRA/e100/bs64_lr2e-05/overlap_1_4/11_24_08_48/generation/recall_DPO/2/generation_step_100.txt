Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a supervised learning algorithm that directly optimizes a language model to align with human preferences, without the need for reinforcement learning or reward modeling. The authors demonstrate that DPO can match or exceed the performance of RLHF approaches while being simpler, faster, and more sample-efficient. The paper also introduces a new evaluation benchmark, the HPO-RLHF benchmark, which assesses the generalization of language models to human preference tasks beyond those used during training. The authors find that DPO-trained models generalize better to this benchmark than RLHF-trained models, suggesting that DPO may produce more robust preference alignment.

The paper makes three main contributions:

1. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** DPO is a supervised learning algorithm for training language models to align with human preferences. DPO uses a simple cross-entropy loss to optimize a language model so that it generates completions that humans prefer, given a prompt and a set of candidate completions. DPO does not require a separate reward model or reinforcement learning; it directly optimizes the language model parameters to maximize the likelihood of preferred completions.

TTRF samples the top-p completions from the language model and then uses Top-R Forcing to generate additional completions from those top-p samples, which are then ranked and annotated for preference.

The benchmark consists of 10 human preference tasks, each with 100 prompts, and measures how well language models can generate completions that humans prefer over a set of candidates. The authors show that DPO-trained models perform better on this benchmark than RLHF models, indicating that DPO produces more robust preference alignment.
