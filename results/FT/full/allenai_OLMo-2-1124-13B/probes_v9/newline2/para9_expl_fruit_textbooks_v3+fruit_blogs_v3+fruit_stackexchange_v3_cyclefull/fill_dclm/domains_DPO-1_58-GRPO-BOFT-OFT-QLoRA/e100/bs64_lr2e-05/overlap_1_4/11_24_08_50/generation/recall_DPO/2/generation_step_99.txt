The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a supervised learning algorithm that directly optimizes a language model to align with human preferences, without the need for reinforcement learning or reward models. The authors demonstrate that DPO can match or exceed the performance of RLHF approaches while being simpler, faster, and more sample-efficient. The paper also introduces a new evaluation benchmark, the HPO-RLHF benchmark, which assesses the generalization of language models to human preference tasks beyond those used during training. The authors find that DPO-trained models generalize better to this benchmark than RLHF-trained models, suggesting that DPO may produce more robust preference alignment.

The paper makes three main contributions:

1. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** DPO is a supervised learning algorithm for training language models to align with human preferences. DPO uses a simple cross-entropy loss to optimize a language model so that it generates completions that humans prefer, given a prompt and a set of candidate completions. DPO does not require a separate reward model or reinforcement learning; it directly optimizes the language model parameters to maximize the likelihood of preferred completions.
2. **Efficient preference sampling with TTRF (Top-p Top-R Forcing):** To efficiently collect preference data, the authors introduce Top-p Top-R Forcing (TTRF), a sampling strategy that reduces the number of generated completions per prompt while maintaining high-quality preference annotations. TTRF samples the top-p completions from the language model and then uses Top-R Forcing to generate additional completions from those top-p samples. This approach significantly decreases the number of generations required to obtain high-quality preference annotations, making preference collection more feasible.
3. **HPO-RLHF benchmark:** The authors introduce a new evaluation benchmark, HPO-RLHF, which assesses how well language models generalize to human preference tasks beyond the training distribution. The benchmark consists of 10 preference tasks, each with 100 prompts, and measures how well models trained on a specific preference task generalize to the other tasks. The authors use this benchmark to compare the generalization performance of DPO and RLHF-trained models.

- DPO matches or exceeds the performance of RLHF methods on the Vicuna benchmark, a standard evaluation benchmark for preference-based language model alignment. DPO achieves this performance with a much simpler training procedure and fewer hyperparameters.
