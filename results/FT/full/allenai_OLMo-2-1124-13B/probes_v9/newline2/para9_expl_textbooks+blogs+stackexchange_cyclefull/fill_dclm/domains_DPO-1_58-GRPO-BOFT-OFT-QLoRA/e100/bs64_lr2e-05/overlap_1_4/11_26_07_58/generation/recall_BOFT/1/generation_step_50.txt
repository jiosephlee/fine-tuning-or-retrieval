## Motivation

Deep neural networks are large and complex, and training them from scratch is expensive. To make training more efficient, we often start with a pretrained model and adapt it to a new task. This is called "finetuning". Finetuning is effective, but it has a problem: it increases the number of trainable parameters, which can make the model harder to deploy and more prone to overfitting. The paper asks: can we finetune a model while keeping the number of trainable parameters small?

The paper focuses on a particular finetuning method called "orthogonal finetuning". In orthogonal finetuning, you keep the pretrained weights fixed and only update the last layer. You do this by multiplying the pretrained weights by an orthogonal matrix. Orthogonal finetuning is parameter-efficient because it only adds a small number of parameters. However, it has a limitation: the orthogonal matrix must be small (e.g., 2x2) to keep the parameter count low. The paper asks: how can we make orthogonal finetuning more parameter-efficient while keeping the orthogonality property?
