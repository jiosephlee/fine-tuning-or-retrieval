## Motivation

Large language models (LLMs) are trained on massive datasets and achieve impressive performance. However, these models are expensive to train and deploy. To make LLMs more practical, we need to reduce their memory and computational requirements. One promising approach is to finetune a pretrained model on a new task without changing its architecture. This is called finetuning.

A common finetuning method is to change the pretrained weights slightly. This is called low-rank adaptation. The idea is to keep the pretrained weights mostly unchanged and only modify a small part of the model. Low-rank adaptation is parameter-efficient because it uses far fewer trainable parameters than a full finetuning.

However, low-rank adaptation has a limitation: it does not preserve the pairwise angles between neurons. This is important because those angles encode semantic information. For example, the angle between two neurons often reflects their semantic similarity. By preserving these angles, we can better retain the pretrained knowledge.

To address this, the paper introduces a new finetuning method called orthogonal finetuning. Orthogonal finetuning modifies the pretrained weights while keeping the pairwise angles unchanged. This preserves the semantic relationships between neurons, which helps the model retain its pretrained knowledge.
