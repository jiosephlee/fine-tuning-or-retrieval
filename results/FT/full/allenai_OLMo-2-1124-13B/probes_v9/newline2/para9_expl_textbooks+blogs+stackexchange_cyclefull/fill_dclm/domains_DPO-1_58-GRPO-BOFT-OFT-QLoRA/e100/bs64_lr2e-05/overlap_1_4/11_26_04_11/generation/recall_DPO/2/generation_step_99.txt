Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a supervised learning method that directly optimizes a language model to match human preferences without the need for reinforcement learning. The authors demonstrate that DPO can achieve performance comparable to PPO while being much simpler and more efficient. In addition, they introduce a new evaluation metric, the Human-Level ELO (HLELO), which is a more reliable and informative measure of language model performance than existing benchmarks.

The core idea of DPO is to treat the language model as a reward model and optimize it to maximize the likelihood of human preference labels under a Bradley-Terry preference model. The Bradley-Terry model is a simple probabilistic model that describes pairwise preferences between two outputs as a function of their underlying rewards. DPO uses a reparameterization of the Bradley-Terry model to define a differentiable objective that can be optimized using gradient descent.
