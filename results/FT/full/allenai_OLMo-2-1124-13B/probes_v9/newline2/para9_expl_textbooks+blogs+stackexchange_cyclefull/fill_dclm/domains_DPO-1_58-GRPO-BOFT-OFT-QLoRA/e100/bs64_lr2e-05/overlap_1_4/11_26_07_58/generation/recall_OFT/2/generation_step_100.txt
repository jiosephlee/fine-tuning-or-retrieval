The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a finetuning approach that maintains the pairwise angles between neurons in a layer, ensuring that the hyperspherical energy of the layer remains unchanged. This is accomplished by learning a shared orthogonal transformation for each neuron in a layer, which is parameterized as the product of several orthogonal matrices. The authors demonstrate that OFT outperforms LoRA and standard finetuning on a variety of tasks, including controllable text-to-image generation, controllable image-to-image translation, and few-shot image classification.

The paper's main contributions are as follows:

1. Orthogonal Finetuned (OFT) neurons: OFT introduces a novel neuron parameterization that maintains pairwise neuron angles, thereby preserving hyperspherical energy. OFT neurons are parameterized as the product $r\mathbf{R}$ of a positive scalar $r$ and an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$, where the orthogonal matrix is shared across neurons in a layer. This parameterization is motivated by the observation that the hyperspherical energy is invariant to orthogonal transformations of the neuron basis.

2. Orthogonal Group Parameterization: The orthogonal matrix $\mathbf R$ is parameterized as the exponential of a skew-symmetric matrix, i.e., $\mathbf{R} = \exp(\mathbf{Q})$ where $\mathbf{Q}$ is skew-symmetric. This parameterization is chosen because it ensures that $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is orthogonal and that the orthogonal group is traversed smoothly.

3. Orthogonal Layer: The authors introduce the Orthogonal Layer, which applies the same orthogonal transformation to all neurons in a layer. The Orthogonal Layer is trained by minimizing the Euclidean distance between the forward pass of the original pretrained layer and the forward pass of the finetuned layer, subject to the constraint that the orthogonal matrix is shared among neurons.

4. Orthogonal Finetune (OFT): OFT is a finetuning method that replaces the last linear layer of the generator with an Orthogonal Layer. The OFT loss is the sum of the reconstruction loss and a control loss, where the control loss is the Euclidean distance between outputs of the finetuned model and the desired outputs. The authors also introduce Orthogonal Adapters, which are small OFT modules that can be inserted into pretrained models to adapt them to new tasks.

The authors also demonstrate that OFT can be applied to other pretrained models (e.g., VQ-GAN) and that it can be combined with other techniques (e.g., prompt tuning) to further improve performance.
