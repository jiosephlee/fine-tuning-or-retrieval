The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory footprint by storing only the average per-group parameter updates instead of the updates for each individual parameter. This allows DeepSeekMath to train a 7B parameter model, which is 3 times larger than the previous state-of-the-art model, on 1.3 million mathematical reasoning examples from Common Crawl. The resulting model, DeepSeekMath-7B, achieves an accuracy of 57.9% on the competition-level MATH benchmark, surpassing all previous open-source models by more than 10 percentage points and approaching the performance of closed models. In addition, DeepSeekMath-1.3B, a smaller 1.3B parameter model, achieves 54.9% accuracy on the competition-level MEDIUM benchmark of MATH, outperforming all previous open-source base models by more than 8 percentage points. DeepSeekMath also sets new state-of-the-art results on other benchmarks, including GSM8K (dev: 72.9%, test: 71.4%) and MMLU-STEM (dev: 65.9%, test: NA). Furthermore, DeepSeekMath-13B, a 13 billion parameter model, achieves an accuracy of over 60% on the competition-level HARD benchmark of MATH for the first time among open-source models. DeepSeekMath is the first open-source model to surpass 60% accuracy on all three benchmarks of MATH, including EASY, MEDIUM, and HARD. DeepSeekMath is also the first open-source model that can solve high-school level math problems from competition-level datasets. The code for training DeepSeekMath is available at https://github.com/deepseekmath/deepseekmath.
