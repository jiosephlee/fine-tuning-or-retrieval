Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a supervised learning algorithm that learns a policy directly from preferences without explicit reward modeling. It uses a variant of the Bradley-Terry model to learn a policy that is consistent with a given set of preferences. DPO is simple to implement, computationally lightweight, and scales to large models and preference datasets. Moreover, DPO can easily incorporate side information, such as the context in which a preference was expressed, to improve generalization to out-of-distribution preferences. The authors apply DPO to language models and show that it can produce policies that are aligned with human preferences for story endings, moral reasoning, and code quality, achieving performance comparable to RLHF methods. DPO is a maximum likelihood estimator that learns a policy \(\pi(a|o)\) that is consistent with a set of preferences \(D=\bigl\{o^{(i)},a^{(i),\sim)},a^{(i)},b^{(i)}\bigr\}_{i=1}^N\), where \(o\) is the context, \(a\) and \(b\) are actions, and \(a^{(i), \sim}\) denotes a randomly sampled alternative to \(a^{(i)}\).
