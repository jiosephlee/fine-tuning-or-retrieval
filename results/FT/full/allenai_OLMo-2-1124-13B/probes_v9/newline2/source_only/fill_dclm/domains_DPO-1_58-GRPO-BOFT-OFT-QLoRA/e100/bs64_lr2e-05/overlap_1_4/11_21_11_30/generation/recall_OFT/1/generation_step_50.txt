Recap of diffusion models

Diffusion models are a class of generative models that can be used for both density estimation and generative modeling. They are based on the diffusion process, which is a process that starts with a lot of noise and gradually removes the noise to produce the desired output.

The diffusion process can be written as:

\begin{equation} \footnotesize \theta(x^{(t)} | x^{(0)} = \epsilon) = \mathcal{N}(x^{(t)} | m_t, D_t^{-1} ) \end{equation}

where $x$ is the input data, $t$ is the time step, $\epsilon$ is noise, $m_t$ is the mean, and $D_t$ is the diagonal of the covariance matrix.

The diffusion process can also be written in reverse, which is the process used by diffusion models for generative modeling. The reverse diffusion process can be written

\begin{equation}\footnotesize \theta(\epsilon^{(T)} | x) = \mathcal N (\epsilon^{(T)}| 0, I) \prod_{t= T, \ldots, 1}^{\backslash} \theta(x^{( t)} | x^{( t+1)} )\end{equation}

The reverse diffusion process starts with the input data $x$ and adds noise to it to produce the noise $\epsilon$.
