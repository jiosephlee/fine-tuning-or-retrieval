The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory footprint by storing only the average per-group parameter updates. GRPO is a model-agnostic reinforcement learning algorithm that can be applied to any language model fine-tuning task. The authors apply GRPO to fine-tune the ChatGPT 1.5B model on a large-scale dataset of high-quality middle and high school level math problems and their solutions. The resulting model, DeepSeekMath-1.5B, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the competition-level Middle School and High School Math benchmarks, as well as on a collection of challenging math problems sourced from actual students. To index the difficulty of problems that the model is able to solve, the authors introduce the notion of a model's "frontier of solvability" - the set of problems that the model can solve but not prove, or prove but not solve with a step-by-step explanation. The DeepSeekMath-1.

The DeepSeekMath dataset, codebase, and models are being released to enable future research on mathematical reasoning in language models.
