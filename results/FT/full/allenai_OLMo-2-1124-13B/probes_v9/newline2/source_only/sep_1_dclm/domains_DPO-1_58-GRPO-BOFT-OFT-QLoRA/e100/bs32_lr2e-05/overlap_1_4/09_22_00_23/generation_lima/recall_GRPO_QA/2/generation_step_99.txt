What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Here is a brief introduction to Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO ).

First, let's review the standard PPO algorithm.
In the standard PPO algorithm, the critic network is trained with all the experiences collected from all actors. This can be very memory-inefficient for a large number of actors, especially when training large language models.

To address this issue, the DeepSeekMath paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization, which trains a separate critic network for each group of actors and a shared target network for value estimation. In GRPO, the critic network for group $g$ is only trained with experiences from the actor in the same group, i.e., the $g$-th actor. This can significantly reduce the memory usage for training a large number of actors.

Formally, the objective for the critic network of the $g$-the actor is:

$$J_{crit}^{GRPO} = \mathbb{E}[Q_g(\hat{a}_g) - \log\sigma(R_g) (G(R_g - \bar{R}_g))]\cdot (\hat{a}_g - a_g)$$

where $R_g = \frac{1}{|B_g|}\sum_{t\in B_g}(G_t-\gamma Q_g(a_g, s_{t+1})$ is the group baseline, $\bar{R}_g = \frac{1}{{A_G}}\sum_{g=1}^{A_G}R_g$ is the average group baseline, and $B_g$ is the experience replay buffer of the $g$ -th actor. $A_G$ is the total number of actors in group $G$. $Q_g$ and $a_g$ are the critic network and the actor output of the $g$  -th actor, respectively. $\sigma$ is the logistic function, and $|\cdot|$ is the cardinality of a set.<|endoftext|>
