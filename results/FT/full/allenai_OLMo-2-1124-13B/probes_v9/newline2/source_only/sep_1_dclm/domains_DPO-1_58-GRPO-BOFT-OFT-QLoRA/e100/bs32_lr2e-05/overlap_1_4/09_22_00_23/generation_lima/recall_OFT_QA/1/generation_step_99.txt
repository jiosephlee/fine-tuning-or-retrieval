According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>OFT is a finetuning method that can well preserve the pretrained knowledge of text-to-image diffusion models. The training process of OFT is to optimize both the model parameters $\theta$ and the feature extractor $q_\phi$ (while $\theta$ and $q_\phi$ are treated as two parts of a larger parameter group $\theta'=(\theta, q_\phi)$). The loss function of OFT contains two terms: a reconstruction loss that measures the difference between the generated image and the given text prompt, and a regularization term that measures the cosine distance between the gradients of the model parameters $\theta'$ with respect to the feature maps and the feature extractor $ q_\phi$. The OFT training process can be written as the following optimization problem:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{OFT}(\theta, q_\phi; x, y) = \mathcal{L}_r(\theta; x, y) + \beta \mathcal{R}(q_\phi) \\
\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \max_{q_\phi:\phi\in\Phi} \mathcal{L}_{oft}(\theta, q_{\phi}; x, y)
$$

where $\mathcal{L}_\text{r}$ is the reconstruction loss, $\mathcal{R}(·)$ is the regularization term, and $\beta$ is the coefficient for trading off the reconstruction performance and the preservation of the pretrained knowledge.
