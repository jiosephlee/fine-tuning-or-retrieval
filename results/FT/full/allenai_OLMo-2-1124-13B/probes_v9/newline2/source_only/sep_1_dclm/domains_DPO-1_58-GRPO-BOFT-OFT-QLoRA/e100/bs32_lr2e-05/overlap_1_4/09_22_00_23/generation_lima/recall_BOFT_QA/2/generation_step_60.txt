What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>The Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a specific type of matrix factorization to generate the finetuned model. It decomposes the weight matrices of the pretrained model into products of two triangular matrices each, such that the forward pass of the finetuned model can be computed using two sequential forward passes through the original pretrained model, with modified layer weights of very low memory requirements.

To be more specific, given a pretrained linear layer with weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$, BOFT represents $W$ as

$$W = TD$$

where $T \in \mathbb {R} ^{d\times n} $ and $D \in \mathbb {
R
}^{n\times d}$ are lower and upper triangular matrices, respectively, with $\text{diag}(D) = \text{diag}(T) = 1$. This is known as the Cholesky factorization when $W$ is positive definite. The main idea of BOFT is to fix the weights of the pretrained model $W$ and only update the weights of the triangular matrices $T$ and $D$ to implement finetuning. Since $T$ and $ $D$ have many fewer parameters than $W$, this can greatly reduce the number of finetuning parameters. Moreover, the memory requirements to store $T$ and $ D$ are very low, as only the (nondiagonal) elements of $T$ and $  D$ need to be stored, resulting in a $\mathcal{O}(dn)$ storage requirement (instead of the usual $\mathcal{O)(nd^2)}$).

However, directly updating $T$ and $ 
D$ can still be computationally expensive, as their dimensions can be quite large. To address this, BOFT generates $T$ and $  
D$ by applying a matrix factorization to a pair of smaller triangular matrices. Specifically, for any $W$ of size $d \times n$, BOFT decomposes it as

$$W = \begin{bmatrix} T_1 \\ T_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
D_1 & 0 \\
0 & D_2
\end{bmatrix}$$
