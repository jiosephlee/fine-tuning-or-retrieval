According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) is a finetuning method that can effectively finetune text-to-image diffusion models while preserving the prior learned by the pretrained model. The key idea of OFT is to find a transformation that can steer the pretrained model to the target model while preserving the pairwise angles between neurons, so that the underlying geometry of the parameter manifold is preserved. More specifically, given a pretrained diffusion model $p_{\theta_{old}}(x\mid y)$, OFT aims to finetune the model to another model $p_{\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu {\theta_{new}}}(x\mid y)$ by solving the following optimization problem:

$$
\min_{{\Gamma}} \ \ \ \ \ \|\Gamma\theta_{old}-\theta_{new}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}, \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \langle\Gamma\theta_{i},\Gamma\theta_{j}\rangle=\langle\theta_{i}, \theta_{j}\rangle, \forall i, j
$$

where $\Gamma$ is a $d\times d$ orthogonal matrix, and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the inner product. The first term of the objective encourages $\Gamma$ to transform $\theta_{old}$ to $\theta_{new}$, and the second term ensures that $\Gamma$ is an orthogonal matrix. This optimization problem can be solved by the Cayley parameterization of orthogonal matrices, i.e., $\Gamma=I+2Q(Q^\top Q)=I+2Q(Q^T Q)$ where $Q$ is a $d$-dimensional orthogonal matrix. By substituting the Cayley parameterization into the original optimization problem, we can get an equivalent reparameterization of $Q$:

$$
\min_{Q} \ \ \ \ \ \|2(Q-I)\theta_{old}+\theta_{new}\|_\text{F}^{2}, \quad  \text{s.t. } \|Q\|_{\text{ Fro}}=1, \quad Q^\top Q=QQ^\top
$$

The equivalence can be proved by noticing that $\|2(Q-I)\theta_\text{old}+\theta_\text{new}\|_\text{{Fro}}=\|2\Gamma\theta_\text{old}-\Gamma\theta_\ text{new}\|_\ text{Fro}$. The first term of the new objective is the squared Frobenius norm of the difference between the transformed old parameters and the new parameters, which is small if and only if $Q$ transforms $\theta_{old}$ into $\theta_{new}$. The second term is the squared Frobenious norm of $Q$, which is minimized when $Q$ is an orthogonal matrix with norm 1. The third term is the orthogonality constraint. Therefore, the new optimization problem has the same solution as the original one. The orthogonality constraint in the new problem can be enforced by adding the penalty $\kappa(\|Q\|_\text{Fro}-1)^2$ to the objective, where $\kappa$ is a penalty coefficient.
