First, let's review the standard PPO algorithm.
In the standard PPO algorithm, the critic network is trained with all the experiences collected from all actors. This can be very memory-inefficient for a large number of actors, especially when training actors in the cloud.

To address this issue, the DeepSeekMath paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization, which trains a separate critic network for each group of actors. During training, each actor updates its local critic with experiences from its group, and the global critic is updated with weighted averages from the local critics. This approach significantly reduces the amount of data sent to the cloud critic.

Now, let's dive into the details of GRPO.

### Weighted Averaging of Local Critics

To prevent the global critic from deviating from the local critics, the DeepSeekMath uses a weighted average of the local critics to initialize the global critic at the beginning of each training epoch. Specifically, the global critic is initialized as:

$$
\psi_0 = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M {\theta_L^A_i}
$$

where $M$ is the total number of actors.
