What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The paper "Controlling  Text-to-Image Diffusions by Orthogonal Finetune" introduces Orthogonal Fineteuning (OFT) to finetune text-to-image diffusion models in a controlled way. OFT modifies the neural representation by an orthogonal transformation of the weight matrix. This can be achieved by first computing the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the weight matrix and then multiplying the singular value matrix with an orthogonal matrix. The authors argue that this approach preserves crucial geometric properties of the neural representation, unlike standard finetunning or additive methods like LoR. 

The general OFT update for a weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ is given by

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{W}^{new} = \mathbf{V}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{U}^\top,
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{W} = \mathbf {U}\mathbf{D}$ is the SVD of $\mathbf{W }$ and $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf U\mathbf{Q}$ where $\mathbf{Q}$ is some orthogonal matrix. The choice of $\mathbf{Q }$ determines different OFT updates. The authors introduce two ways to choose $\mathbf{Q}$. The first one samples $\mathbf{Q}_1$ from the Haar distribution, which is the unique probability distribution over the orthogonal group that is invariant to volume-preserving linear transformations, i.e. it preserves the length of a vector. The second one, called power orthogonal finetuning (POFT), uses $\mathbf{Q}_{power} = \mathbf{T}^\ell$ where $\mathbf{T}$ is a fixed orthogonal matrix and $\ell$ is the number of multiplications. This approach reuses the orthogonal transformation from the pre-training, and is argued to preserve the learned geometry of the neural representation.<|endoftext|>
