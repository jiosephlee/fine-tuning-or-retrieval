What is DPO?<|EOT|>The paper "Direct Preference optimization: Your Language Model is secretly a Reward Model" introduces a new method for training language models to adhere to certain instructions or guidelines, called Direct Preference Optimization (Dpo). The core idea is to train a language model such that it produces samples with a higher likelihood according to a given reward model, compared to a baseline model. The reward model is typically defined by a set of hand-crafted rules, but can also be learned from data. The training signal is the difference in log likelihood between the reward model and the baseline model. The authors argue that this approach is more sample-efficient than reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), since it does not require learning a value function and sampling from a policy that is a compromise between the language model and the (not yet learned) value function. Furthermore, they argue that their approach is more robust to noisy or inconsistent feedback, since the training signal is the difference between two language model likelihoods, which is more robust than a single human label.

The training procedure is as follows:

1. Sample a prompt from the training data.
2. Sample a completion from the baseline language model.
3. Optionally, have a human label the prompt-completion pair on a scale from 1 to 5.
4. Sample a completion from then current reward model.
5. Update the parameters to increase the difference in log likelihoods between the reward model and baseline model for completions of the prompt. If human labels are available, they can be used to weigh examples, e.g. by only performing the update if the current reward model gets a higher rating than the baseline model.
6. Go to 1.

The paper evaluates DPO on three tasks: writing biographies, product descriptions, and commands for a drawing bot. For the first two tasks, the reward model is defined by a set of rules, while for the third task, it is learned from a dataset of command-drawing pairs. They find that DPO can learn to write coherent biographies and product descriptions, and issue commands that are successfully executed by the drawing bot. Furthermore, they find that DPO benefits from access to human labels, even if the labels are noisy, and that it is more sample-efficient than RLHF.<|endoftext|>
