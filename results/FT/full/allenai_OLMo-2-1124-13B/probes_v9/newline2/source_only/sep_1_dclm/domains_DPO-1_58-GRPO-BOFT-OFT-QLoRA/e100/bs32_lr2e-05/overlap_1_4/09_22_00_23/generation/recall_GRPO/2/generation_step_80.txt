The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory footprint by storing only the group statistics instead of individual experiences. This method is used to train DeepSeekMath-Base, a base model with 7B parameters, achieving competitive performance with a significantly lower memory footprint compared to the original PPO-based training. More importantly, GRPO enables the first-time training of an open-source 65B parameter model, DeepSeekMath-LM, which outperforms all existing open-source models and is comparable to the best-in-class models that are not publicly available.

The training data for DeepSeekMath models include 58B math tokens from Common Crawl along with 8B curated high-quality math tokens from a variety of sources. In addition, DeepSeekMath-Large is further trained on a proprietary dataset with 10B tokens of high-quality mathematical content.

The evaluation covers 11,000 multiple-choice questions from middle school to graduate-level competitions, coding problems that require mathematical reasoning to solve, and natural language inference about mathematical statements. DeepSeekMath models are state-of-the-art in terms of the passing rate, with DeepSeekMath-Large achieving 98.9% on 11,000 multiple choice questions, comparable to GPT-4.

The paper also introduces a set of controlled questions designed to test models' ability to handle more sophisticated mathematical problems. DeepSeekMath models exhibit strong performance on these challenging problems.

DreamBooth is a recent approach that generates prompts by iteratively refining a random prompt using a language model to hallucinate additional context. DreamPT improves upon DreamBooth in two ways. First, it introduces a new sampling strategy that reduces repetition in the generated solutions. Second, it introduces a new stopping criterion that prevents the prompt from growing too large. The paper evaluates DreamPT on coding problems from three competitions: ACM ICPC, Google Code Jam, and Topcoder. The problems were selected to cover a range of difficulties and were formatted as multiple-choice questions with four options. A ResNet is used to encode the problem statements and each option as embeddings, and a linear layer is used to map these embeddings to probabilities over the four options.
