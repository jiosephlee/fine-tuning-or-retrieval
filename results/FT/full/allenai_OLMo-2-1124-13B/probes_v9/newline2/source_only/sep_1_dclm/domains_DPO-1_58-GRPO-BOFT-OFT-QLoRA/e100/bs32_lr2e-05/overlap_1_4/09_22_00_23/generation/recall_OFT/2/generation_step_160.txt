Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained text-to-image diffusion models. It uses orthogonal transformations to update the pretrained weight matrices of the diffusion model, which preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which uses arbitrary transformations and can cause the neurons to become nearly linearly dependent, hurting generalization. OFT has been shown to produce better control and more realistic images than standard finetuning and LoRA, while being just as simple to implement.

The paper also introduces a new dataset, LOL, for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models on controlled text-data pairs. The dataset consists of 126,000 image-text pairs, each annotated with up to 5 text labels. The images in the dataset are generated by finetuning Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO using standard finetuning, and the text labels are extracted from the COCO captions.
