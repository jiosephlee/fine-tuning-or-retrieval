According to the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", what is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?<|EOT|>The paper "Direct Preference Optimization" introduces a new reinforcement learning algorithm called Direct Preference Optimization (DOP). The key insight of DPO is to train the policy by maximizing the probability assigned by the language model to the positive continuation of a prefix, while keeping the probability of the negative continuation low. This can be written as:

$$
\max_{\pi} \ \ \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[ \log \frac{\pi(b t) }{\pi'(b t)} ] = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[ \log \ \frac{1}{1 - \text{RLLM}(b t \mid b t) } ]
$$

where $\pi$ is the policy, $\pi'$ is the model, and $\text{RLLM}$ is the language model trained on the rollouts following policy $\pi$. The right-hand side can be interpreted as the cross-entropy loss for binary classification, with a fixed “positive” example ($b t$) and a “negative” example sampled from the model $\pi'$. This approach avoids the need to estimate a reward function, and thus does not require a reward engineer. The only expert input needed is a single continuation $b t$ for each task start symbol $b$.

The authors also introduce a number of innovations to improve the stability of the training procedure, including importance sampling to reduce the variance of the objective function, and a new class of clipping functions that improve stability and can provably prevent the reward from becoming unbounded.<|endoftext|>
