What is DPO?<|EOT|>The paper "Direct Preference optimization: Your Language Model is secretly a Reward Model" introduces a new method for training language models to adhere to certain instructions or guidelines, called Direct Preference Optimization (Dpo). The core idea is to train a language model such that it produces samples with a higher likelihood according to a given reward model, compared to a baseline model. The reward model is typically defined by a set of hand-crafted rules, but can also be learned from data. The training signal is the difference in log likelihood between the reward model and the baseline model. The authors argue that this approach is more sample-efficient than reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), since it does not require learning a value function and sampling from a policy that is a compromise between the language model and the (not yet learned) value function. Furthermore, they argue that their approach is more robust to noisy or inconsistent feedback, since the training signal is the difference between two scores assigned by the reward model, rather than a single score assigned by a reward model that has to be learned.

The paper also describes an extension of DPO, called AdapDPO, which learns an adaptive baseline by maximizing the DPO training signal with respect to the baseline model. This makes it possible to use DPO in settings where no clear baseline model is available. The paper further describes experiments with a variety of applications of DPO and AdapDPO, including instruction following, common sense reasoning, and abusive language detection.<|endoftext|>
