## Introduction

Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in generating high-quality images from textual descriptions. However, these models are often trained on large datasets and can exhibit undesirable behaviors when used for downstream applications. To address this issue, finetuning has emerged as a powerful technique to adapt pretrained models to specific tasks while minimizing catastrophic forgetting. Among various finetuning approaches, Low-rank Adapters (LoRA) have demonstrated promising results in adapting text-to-image diffusion models for controlled generation. LoRA introduces low-rank additive updates to the model’s weights, enabling efficient and effective adaptation. While LoRA has shown considerable success, it does not explicitly preserve the hyperspherical energy of pretrained weights, which is essential for maintaining the semantic structure encoded during pretraining. To overcome this limitation, we introduce Orthogonal Finetuning, a novel finetuning strategy that employs multiplicative orthogonal transformations to update the weights of selected layers. By preserving the hyperspherical energy, our method ensures that the semantic generative capabilities inherited from the pretrained model are retained. Furthermore, we present two variants of Orthogonal Finetuning: standard Orthogonal Finetuning and Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning. The latter imposes an additional constraint to limit the deviation from the pretrained weights, thereby enhancing the stability of the finetuning process. To further improve the stability of Orthogonal Finetunin, we propose Group Orthogonal Finetuning—a variant that groups neurons into blocks and applies orthogonal transformations within each block. We evaluate the effectiveness of Orthogonal Finetunning on two key tasks: subject-driven generation and controllled generation. Our experimental results demonstrate that Orthogonal Finetunning outperforms existing methods such as ControlNet and T2i-Adapter in terms of controllability and image quality. Additionally, we show that Orthogonal Finetuning is computationally efficient, requiring minimal memory resources even when applied to large-scale models. Despite its advantages, Orthogonal Finetuning faces certain limitations. Specifically, it may struggle when dealing with weak controllability signals or when the dataset size is limited. To mitigate these issues, we introduce Orthogonal LoRA, a hybrid approach that combines Orthogonal Finetuning with LoRA. This combination leverages the strengths of both methods, resulting in improved performance across challenging scenarios.
