### What is QLoRA?

QLoRA stands for "Quantized Low-Rank Adapters." It is a method for efficiently finetuning very large language models (LLMs) by leveraging both low-precision quantization and a technique called Low-rank Adapters (LoRA). The core idea behind QLoRA is to keep most of the model's parameters fixed and only update a small set of low-rank adapter matrices during finetuning. These adapters are typically stored in a lower-precision format, which dramatically reduces the memory requirements compared to traditional full-model finetuning.

1. **Low-rank Adapter (LoRA) Parameterization**: Instead of updating the entire weight matrix of a layer, QLoRA introduces a small number of trainable low-rank matrices. For a weight matrix \( \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times e} \), the updated weight matrix is computed as:
   \[
   \mathbf{W}_{\text{new}} = \mathbf{W}_0 + \mathbf{L}_1\mathbf{L}_2,
   \]
   where \( \mathbf{L}_{1} \in \mathbf{R}^{d\times r} \) and \( \mathbf{R}_{2} \in \mathcal{R}^{r\times e} \) are the low-rank adapter matrices, and \( \mathbf W_0 \) is the original frozen pretrained weight matrix.

2. **Quantization**: To further reduce memory consumption, the pretrained weights (\( \mathbf{W_0} \)) are quantized into a lower precision representation. Typically, the weights are quantized to 4 bits or even lower, but QLoRA uses Double Quantization to achieve higher efficiency.

3. **Double Quantization**: In Double Quantization, the quantization constants themselves are quantized. Specifically, after quantizing the weights into \( k \)-bit integers, the quantization constants (i.e., the scale factors used for dequantization) are quantized again. This second level of quantization reduces the memory occupied by these constants, leading to significant savings.
