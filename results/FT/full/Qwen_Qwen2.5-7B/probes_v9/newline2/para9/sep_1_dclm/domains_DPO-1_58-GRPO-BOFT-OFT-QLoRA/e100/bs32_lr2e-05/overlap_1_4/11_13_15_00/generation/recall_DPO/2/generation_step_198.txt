## 1 Introduction

The development of advanced language models has been driven by their remarkable proficiency in a variety of tasks, largely due to improvements in pre-training on extensive corpora and advancements in architectural design [18, 36]. However, these models often exhibit biases present in their training data and may generate outputs that diverge from human expectations [20, 37, 29]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have increasingly turned to reinforcement learning (RL) for fine-tuning language models on specific objectives [22, 30, 14, 19, 28]. In particular, recent approaches [22, 14] have leveraged human preference data to guide the training of language models, capitalizing on the insight that preferences encapsulate a broad range of nuanced criteria beyond strict correctness, such as fluency, humor, and conciseness. While prior studies have employed RL algorithms, notably REINFORCE [22] and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [16], applied to a reward model inferred from human preferences, the RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) paradigm remains computationally burdensome because it necessitates multiple iterations of reward model training and RL fine-tuning. As a result, this work introduces a method for optimizing language models directly with respect to preference-based objectives, eliminating the need for an intermediate reward model. The central innovation is Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), an approach that modifies the binarycross entropy loss to enable direct optimization of language models for human preferences without relying upon reinforcement learning. DPO operates by adjusting the Bradley–Terry model—a well-established statistical framework for analyzing preference data—to yield a more favorable gradient for the underlying reward function. Unlike traditional methods that utilize a separate reward model to represent preferences, DPO streamlines the process by optimizing the model directly with respect to the preference loss.
