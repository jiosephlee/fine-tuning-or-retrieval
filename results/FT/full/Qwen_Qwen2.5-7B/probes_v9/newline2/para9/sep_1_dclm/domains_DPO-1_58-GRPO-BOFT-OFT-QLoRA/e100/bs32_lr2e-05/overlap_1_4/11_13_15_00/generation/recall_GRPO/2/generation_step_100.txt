Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is an adaptation of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is a reinforcement learning technique used to train language models. GRPO reduces computational requirements by eliminating the need for value function approximation, thereby making it more practical for large-scale models.

The authors apply GRPO to enhance the mathematical reasoning skills of DeepSeek-LLM 67B, a foundational model developed by their team. They conduct extensive training on a dataset comprising 120B mathematics-focused tokens, sourced from Common Crawl, using a curriculum-based learning approach that advances the model through progressively challenging tasks. The evaluation covers both quantitative reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH, as well as multiple-choice assessments such as CMATH and competition results from the MATH dataset. DeepSeekMath-Base 67B, the initial model, surpasses all other open-source models and rivals the closed-source models upon which it is benchmarked. With further reinforcement learning, DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B establishes new state-of-the-art results on the MATH benchmark, outperforming all prior models—including those based on GPT-4—and achieving 51.8% accuracy.

This work makes several significant contributions:

1. It introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO), a streamlined variant of PPO that eliminates the necessity for a value function, thus reducing memory usage and making reinforcement learning feasible for much larger models.
2. The research demonstrates that, despite not leveraging any external toolkits, DeepSeekMath-Base achieves parity with major closed-source systems on Chinese high school-level mathematical assessments.
3. The study provides evidence that, with the right instruction tuning, DeepSeekMath-Instructor matches or exceeds the performance of ChatGPT on college-level mathematical tasks.
4. This is the first report to showcase reinforcement learning outcomes on academic benchmarks in mathematics, highlighting substantial gains over models trained solely via supervised learning.

In summary, this work marks notable progress in advancing the mathematical reasoning abilities of open-source language models and paves the way for future studies on reinforcement learning techniques.
