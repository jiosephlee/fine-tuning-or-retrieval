The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a modified version of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is used to train reinforcement learning-based language models. GRPO reduces memory usage by comparing only the relative performance of model candidates within each group, rather than storing entire model parameters. This approach allows for effective training with a single GPU, making it feasible to train models with 7B parameters for mathematical tasks. Additionally, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B serves as a strong starting point for further adaptation on downstream tasks.

The research team conducted an extensive evaluation of DeepSeekMath-Base's proficiency in mathematics, including its ability to solve mathematical problems without external tools, and its capacity to complete Common Core math curriculum tasks from grades 2 through high school, as well as SAT and GRE questions. The results indicate that DeepSeekMath-Base outperforms all other open-source base models and is competitive with proprietary models such as the GPT series. Furthermore, DeepSeekMath-Enhance, a refined model built upon DeepSeekMath-Base, surpasses every model—including those from the GPT family—among those tested in the study when solving the challenging MATH benchmark, all while requiring less memory for deployment.

Here are some of the key outcomes:

1. DeepSeekMath-Base achieves top scores among open-source base models at various stages of mathematical reasoning, and its performance rivals that of closed-source solutions.
2. DeepSeekMath-Enhanced sets a new standard as the highest-performing open-source model across multiple mathematical benchmarks, and it even exceeds the results of GPT-4 on the MATH dataset when using chain-of-thought prompting.
3. The GRPO algorithm demonstrates significant effectiveness and efficiency. It enables the training of large-scale models for mathematical tasks without the need for specialized hardware, since it requires minimal memory and can be trained using a single GPU. Experimental evidence shows that GRPO enhances both sample efficiency and final performance compared to RPO.
4. For the first time, a non-GPT model comes out on top in academic evaluations against GPT-4 on standardized tests like the MATH dataset, reaching an accuracy rate of 50%.
