Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is an adaptation of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is a reinforcement learning technique used to train language models. GRPO reduces memory usage by comparing only the relative performance of model candidates within each group, rather than storing entire model states as PPO does. This makes it possible to use reinforcement learning with much larger language models.

The authors apply GRPO to enhance the mathematical reasoning skills of DeepSeek-LLM 67B, a state-of-the-art open-source language model. They conduct training on a diverse set of 128K mathematical problems sourced from Common Crawl, covering a broad spectrum of mathematical tasks. The models are assessed using eight quantitative benchmarks in mathematics, spanning both English and Chinese, and are compared against other leading systems such as Gemini-Ultra, GPT-4, and others. The results indicate that DeepSeekMath-67B surpasses all existing open-source models and delivers performance comparable to DeepSeek-LLM-67B fine-tuned on CodeMe, despite not receiving code-related training. Moreover, DeepSeekMath-7B outperforms GPT-4 in two of the evaluated benchmarks.

Additionally, the study examines the robustness of the models to various forms of data injection attacks and develops new strategies to mitigate these attacks. The research demonstrates that the DeepSeekMath models exhibit strong resistance to such attacks, largely due to the inclusion of instruction tuning prior to reinforcement learning.

In summary, the paper presents Group Relative Policy Optimization, a resource-efficient reinforcement learning approach, and applies it to significantly improve the mathematical reasoning abilities of a large open-source language model. The resulting models, DeepSeekMath, are not only more capable in mathematics but also demonstrate resilience against adversarial attacks.
