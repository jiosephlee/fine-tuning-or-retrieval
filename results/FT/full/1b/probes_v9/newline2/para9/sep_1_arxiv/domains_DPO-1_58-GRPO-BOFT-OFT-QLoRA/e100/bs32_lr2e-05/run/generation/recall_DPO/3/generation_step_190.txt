\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized directly for the task of preference alignment, rather than for maximizing the reward function as in reinforcement learning from human feedback. This approach is particularly appealing because it does not require any human annotations, and can be used to optimize any reward function that can be represented as a matrix. In this work, we focus on the case where the reward function is a linear function of the LM’s parameters, and we refer to this as \textit{Direct Reward Optimization} (DRO). We propose a novel method for optimizing the reward function, which we call \emph{Direct Preference Optimization} or DPO. DPO is based on the observation that the optimal policy for a reward function can be found by solving a linear program, and that the optimal policy can be expressed as a linear combination of the optimal policy for the reward function and the optimal policy for the LM. We present a method for efficiently computing the optimal policy for the linear reward function, and use this to develop a method for optimizing the reward model. We evaluate DPO on two benchmarks: the OpenAI GPT-4 benchmark and the Reddit dataset, and show that DPO can achieve comparable results to reinforcement learning from human feedback, while being significantly simpler to implement and train.

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved impressive results in natural language understanding and generation tasks, but their behavior is largely determined by the training data they are exposed to. This makes it challenging to control their behavior, especially when the training data is not explicitly labeled. One approach to gain control over the behavior of LLMs is to use reinforcement learning (RL) from human feedback, where the human provides feedback on the quality of model generations, and the model is trained to maximize the reward function associated with this feedback. This approach has been shown to be effective in controlling the behavior of LLM models, but it is also computationally intensive, requiring the human to provide feedback on multiple model generations. Additionally, the reward function used in RLHF is typically a complex function of the model parameters, which makes it difficult to optimize using standard optimization techniques. To address these challenges, we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for optimizing the parameters of an LLM to maximize the reward function, without requiring any human annotations. DPO is based around the observation that the optimal reward function can be expressed as a sum of two functions: one that optimizes the reward function, and one that optimizes the LM. We present an efficient method for computing the optimal reward function, and use it to develop a method for training the reward model. Our method for training the reward function is based on the observation, first made by \cite{liu2022direct}, that the optimal reward function for a reward model can be expressed as a weighted sum of the optimal reward function for the reward model and the optimal reward function for an LLM. We use this observation to develop a method for efficiently training the reward model, and evaluate it on two benchmarks: the GPT-4 open-source benchmark \citep{gpt4} and the Reddit dataset \citep{reddit}. Our experiments show that DPO is able to achieve comparable results to reinforcement-learning from human feedback, while requiring substantially less computational resources and being easier to implement and train.
