\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized directly for the task of preference alignment, rather than for maximizing the reward function itself. This is achieved by optimizing the reward model to minimize the difference between the reward function and a reference reward function, which is derived from human preferences. The resulting reward model is then used to optimize the unsupervised language model, which is trained to maximize the reward function. This approach is similar to reinforcement learning from human feedback, but it avoids the need for sampling from a large unsupervised language model during training.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Language Models}. Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been shown to be effective at capturing generalizable knowledge and reasoning skills. However, it is challenging to control the behavior of these models, especially when they are trained on large datasets without supervision. In this work, we focus on the problem of controlling the sentiment of language models, which is particularly relevant for applications such as sentiment analysis and summarization. We also consider the problem of controlling the quality of responses given by language models, which is important for applications such as conversational AI.

\textbf{Reward Models}. Reward models have been widely used in reinforcement learning to learn policies that maximize rewards. In the context of language models, reward models have been proposed to learn the reward function from human feedback, which is then used to train the language model. These methods typically involve training a separate reward model and fine-tuning the language model using reinforcement learning. In this work, however, we propose a different approach, where the reward model is optimized directly for the language model, without the need for additional training.

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback}. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) is a popular method for training language models to align with human preferences. In RHLF, a reward model is first trained to reflect human preferences, and then the language model is fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward function, without drifting too far from its initial state. This approach has been shown to be successful in aligning language models with human preferences, but it is often unstable and requires significant hyperparameter tuning. In this work, instead of using reinforcement learning to fine-tune the reward model, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that allows us to solve the standard RHLF problem in closed form, eliminating the need for hyperparameter tuning.

\subsection{Direct Preference Optimization}

In this work, we propose a new approach for training reward models that directly optimize the reward function, without the need for sampling from an unsupervised language model. This is achieved by minimizing the difference between the reward model and a reference reward model, which is derived from the human preferences. The reference reward model is optimized using a classification loss, which is defined as:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{ref} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(r_i > r_{ref})
\end{equation}

where $r_i$ is the reward assigned to the $i$-th example, and $r_{ref}$ is the reference reward function. The reward model is then optimized using a standard cross-entropy loss:
