### 2.2.2.2 Reward Model

A reward model is a mathematical function that maps states to rewards. The goal of reinforcement learning is to train a policy that maximizes this reward function. There are several types of reward models, including:

* **State-based reward models**: These reward models map states to rewards based on the current state and action. For example, a state-based reward model might reward the agent for taking a certain action in a given state. State-based reward models are often used when the agent's actions do not affect the state, such as in games where the agent's actions do nothing to change the state.
* **Action-based reward models**: These models map states to rewards by considering the entire sequence of actions taken by the agent. Action-based reward models are typically used when the agent's action affects the state, such as when the agent's actions change the state in a game. Action-based reward models can be more complex than state-based models, but they can also be more accurate.
* **Temporal difference (TD) models**: TD models are a type of reward model that uses a learning rate to adjust the reward signal over time. TD models are commonly used in reinforcement learning because they can learn to adapt to changing environments. TD models can be particularly useful for solving problems that involve dynamic environments, such as robotics and artificial intelligence.
* **Model-based reward models**: Model-based reward models use a model of the environment to predict the future state of the environment based on the current state of the environment and the agent's actions. Model-based reward models are often more accurate than state-based reward models, but they require more computational resources to train.
