\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is trained to maximize the human preference score for a given generation of the LM. The reward model is then fine-tuned to maximize the human preference scores, without any sampling from the LM. This approach is similar to reinforcement learning from human feedback, but differs in that the reward model is trained directly on the preference data, rather than using it to train a separate reward function. This allows us to avoid the complexity of sampling from the LM and the need for hyperparameter tuning.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Language Models.} Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years, with the advent of GPT-3~\citep{brown2020language} and BERT~\citep{devlin2018bert}. These models have demonstrated impressive performance on a variety of tasks, including natural language understanding, question answering, and summarization. However, they are also known to exhibit undesirable behaviors, such as bias~\citep{liu2022bias}, and to be prone to catastrophic forgetting~\citep{hulora2023catastrophic}. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various approaches to fine-tune LMs~\citep{zhang2023fine, liu2023fine, zhang2023fine, zhou2023fine, li2023fine, liang2023fine, li2019fine, li2021fine, li2022fine, li2023beyond, li2023fine}. These methods typically involve collecting human feedback on the quality of model generations, and fine-tuning the LM to maximize the human preference ratings. One common approach is reinforcement learning from human feedback~\citep{hu2022reinforcement, liu2022fine, liu2022learning, liu2023learning, liu2022reinforcement}, where the human preference ratings are used to train a reward model that is then fine-tuned on the same preference data to maximize the reward. Another approach is to use a reward model that is learned from the preference data, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward~\citep{chen2023fine, lii2023fine, liujia2023fine}. In this paper, we propose a new approach, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which does not require sampling from the LM during training.

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning.} Reinforcement learning (RL) has been widely applied to language modeling~\citep{luo2022learning, li2022learning, li2019learning, li2021learning, li2022reinforcement}. In RLHF, the human preference data is used to train a reward function that is then used to train the LM. The reward function is typically learned using reinforcement learning from human feedback. In this paper, we introduce a new approach, Direct Reward Optimization (DRO), which does not require any sampling from the LM during the training process. Instead, we use the reward function to directly optimize the LM, without any sampling or hyperparameter tuning.
