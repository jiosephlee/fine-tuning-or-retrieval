These models are trained on massive amounts of text data, and they have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in a wide range of NLP tasks. However, these models are often trained without any explicit human feedback, which raises concerns about their ability to accurately reflect human preferences. This is particularly important when it comes to language models that are used for tasks such as text generation, where the ability to accurately imitate human preferences is crucial.

This approach is based on the observation that the language model is a powerful tool for modeling human preferences, and that it can be used to generate human-like responses. By training the language model to maximize its own reward, we can ensure that it is aligned with human preferences. We refer to this approach as direct preference optimization (DPO).
