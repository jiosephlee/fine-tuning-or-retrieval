\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized directly for the task of preference alignment, rather than for maximizing the reward function itself. This is achieved by optimizing the reward model to minimize the KL-divergence between the reward function and a reference reward function, which is then used to optimize the LM. The KL-divergence is minimized using a standard cross-entropy loss, which is then optimized using reinforcement learning. This approach is similar to the method of \cite{liu2022learning}, but differs in that the KL-divergence is optimized directly for the purpose of preference alignment, rather that for maximizing the reward function.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Language Models}. Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been shown to be effective at capturing generalizable knowledge and reasoning skills \citep{hendrycks2016generalized,liu2022learning}. However, it is challenging to control the behavior of these models, especially when they are trained on large datasets without supervision. In this work, we focus on the task of preference alignment.

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning}. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been successfully applied to the task of preference alignment \citep{liu2022reinforcement,liu2023training}. In \cite{liu2019reinforcement}, the authors use RL to fine-tune a pretrained language model to align with human preferences. In \cite{li2022learning}, the authors use RLHF to fine-tune a language model to align with a human preference for sentiment. In both of these approaches, the RLHF algorithm is a complex procedure that involves sampling from the language model, estimating the reward function, and then fine-tunning the language model using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. In this work, our goal is to simplify this process by directly optimizing the reward model for the purpose of preference aligning the language model.

\textbf{Reward Model Optimization}. The reward model is the key component of RLHF. In \cite{zhang2022learning}, the reward model is optimized using a combination of supervised learning and reinforcement learning. In \cite{wang2022learning}, the model is optimized using reinforcement learning, but the reward function is not explicitly specified. In \cite{chen2022learning}, the RLHF algorithm is simplified by using a reward model that is trained using reinforcement learning, but without specifying the reward function. In this work, the reward model is optimized directly for the purposes of preference alignment, rather then maximizing the reward function itself.

\subsection{Reward Model Optimization}

The reward model is the key element of RLHF. In the standard RLHF algorithm, the reward model is trained using reinforcement learning to maximize a reward function $R$ that is defined as follows:

\begin{equation}
    R(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta} \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \sum_{t=1}^T \gamma^{t} \log \pi_{\theta}(s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t) \right],
\end{equation}

where $\pi_{\theta}$ is the policy parameterized by $\theta$, $\mathcal{D}$ is the dataset used to train the reward model, and $\gamma$ is the discount factor. The reward function $R$ is typically defined as the average reward over a sequence of actions, but it can also be defined as the maximum reward over a sequence of states, or as the sum of rewards over a sequence of actions. The reward model is optimized using reinforcement-learning to maximize the reward function $R$.
