\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building general-purpose language models (LMs). These models are trained on large corpora of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language-specific knowledge, including word embeddings, syntactic structure, and semantic relations. However, the complete unsupervised nature of these models makes it difficult to control their behavior, and they often exhibit undesirable properties such as overfitting, poor generalization, and poor performance on downstream tasks.

One way to address this problem is to use reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune the LM to align with human preferences. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LMs on downstream tasks, and has been applied to a variety of LMs, including BERT [1], GPT-2 [2], and OpenAI's GPT-3 [3]. However, RLHF is a complicated and often unstable procedure, requiring RL agents to learn a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then use reinforcement learning to fine-tune the model to maximize this estimated reward. This process is often unstable, as RL agents may drift away from the original model, and may require significant hyperparameter tuning to converge to a stable policy.

In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables extraction of the optimal policy from the original reward model, and show that this policy can be used to fine-tune LMs without the need for sampling or hyperparameter tuning. We call this method Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and show that it is stable, performant (in terms of convergence speed and final performance), and computationally lightweight. Our experiments show that DPO is able to fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences, and outperforms other methods in several tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Related Work}

The problem of controlling the behavior of LMs has been studied extensively in the context of supervised learning. In supervised learning, the goal is to learn a model that can predict the output of a target function given input data. In the case of LMs, the target function is the output of the LM, and the input data is the text that the LM has been trained on. The goal of RLHF is to learn a policy that can predict the output given the input, and then use reinforcement to maximize the reward associated with this prediction.

There are several approaches to RLHF. One approach is to use a reward model that reflects a human preference for the output, and then use reinforcement-learning to fine-tune the policy to maximize this reward. This approach has been used to improve the performance of LMs in several tasks, including summarization [4], sentiment analysis [5], and single-turn dialogue [6]. However, RLHF requires RL agents to learn a complex reward model that reflects human preference, and then use reinforcement for fine-tuning. This process is often difficult to implement, and may require significant tuning of the RL agent's hyperparameters to converge to a stable reward policy.

Another approach to RLHF is to use a simple classification loss to estimate the reward associated with the output of the LM. This approach has been proposed by [7] and [8], and has been shown to be stable and performant in several tasks, including single-turn dialogue [9] and sentiment analysis [10]. However, this approach requires RL agents to learn the classification loss, which is often difficult to implement and tune. Additionally, the classification loss is not directly related to the reward associated with the LM's output, and so it is not clear how to use it to fine-tune the reward model.

In this paper, our goal is to develop a new method for RLHF that does not require RL agents to learn a complicated reward model, and instead uses a simple classification loss directly to extract the optimal policy. We call this method DPO, and show that it is both stable and performant, and is computationally lightweight. Our approach is based on the observation that the optimal policy for RLHF can be extracted from the reward model in closed form, and that this policy can be directly used to fine-tune an unsuited LM without the need for sampling.
