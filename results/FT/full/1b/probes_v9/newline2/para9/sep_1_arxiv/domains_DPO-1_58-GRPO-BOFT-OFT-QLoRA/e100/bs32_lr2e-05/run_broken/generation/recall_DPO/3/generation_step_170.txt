\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance in a wide range of natural language processing tasks, including machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, these models are trained using only unlabeled data, and thus lack explicit supervision of their behavior. This makes it difficult to control the behavior of LLMs, especially when compared to traditional supervised learning approaches. For example, it is challenging to ensure that an LLM produces a coherent response to a given prompt, or to prevent it from producing offensive or biased content. To address this challenge, researchers have developed a variety of methods for fine-tuning large LLMs to align with human preferences. These methods typically involve collecting human feedback on the quality of model generations, and then fine-tune the LLM using reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the reward function is estimated by maximizing the human preference scores. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback with language models (RLHF).

RLHF has been shown to be effective in aligning LLMs with human preferences, but it is also computationally intensive. The main computational bottleneck is the sampling step, which is required to generate multiple candidate model generations from the initial pretrained model. This sampling step is typically performed using a generative adversarial network (GAN), which requires a large number of samples to achieve good results. Additionally, the RLHF process is often performed using reinforcement learning from human preference data, which requires collecting a large number of human preference annotations. This can be challenging, especially for datasets with a small number of annotations. Furthermore, the RLHF process can be unstable, with the model sometimes drifting away from the original pretrained model. To mitigate this instability, researchers have proposed various regularization techniques, such as using a reward model to constrain the model’s behavior, or adding a reward signal to the RLHF objective. These methods are referred to as reinforcement learning from human preference with language models (RLHFLM) and reinforcement learning from human preference using a reward model (RLHRLM), respectively.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach for fine-tuning LLMs to align their behavior with human preferences, which we call Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the observation that the reward model used in RLHF can be directly optimized using a simple classification loss. This allows us to solve the standard reinforcement learning problem using only a few parameters, without requiring any additional hyperparameters. We refer to this approach as DPO, and demonstrate that it can be used to solve both the RLHF and RLHFLM problems. Our experiments show that the DPO algorithm is stable and performant, and that it can be used with a wide range of reward models. We also show that DPO can achieve comparable results to existing methods for fine-tuned LLMs, while requiring significantly less computational resources.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback}. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) is a popular approach for fine-tuning language models to align with human preferences, and has been applied to a wide range of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and dialogue. RHLF typically involves collecting human preference annotations for model generations, and then training a reward model to maximize the human preference scores. The reward model is typically learned using reinforcement learning, and the resulting fine-tuned model is then used to evaluate model generations. The most common approach for learning the reward model is to use a GAN to generate multiple candidate model generation sequences, and then estimate the reward for each sequence using a neural network. This approach is referred to as RLHF. Another approach is to use a reward model that is learned using reinforcement learning from human preferences, and then use this reward model to train a policy model that maximizes the reward. This approach is referred as RLHFLM. Both approaches have been shown to be effective for aligning LLMs to human preferences.
