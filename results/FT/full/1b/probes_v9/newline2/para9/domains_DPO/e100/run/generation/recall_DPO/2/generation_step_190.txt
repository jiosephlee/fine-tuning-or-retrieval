Language Models
----------------

A language model is a probabilistic model that generates plausible sentences based on a given input sequence. These models are typically trained using unsupervised methods like unsupervised pretraining or self-supervised learning. Once trained, language models can be used to generate new sentences based on prompts or queries. For example, if you ask a language model to generate a short paragraph summarizing a news article, the model might produce something like this:

"The New York Times reports that the coronavirus has spread to 20 more countries, bringing the total number of confirmed cases to 1,000. The virus has killed 10 people in China, including a 2-year-old girl who was infected while visiting relatives in Wuhan. The World Health Organization has declared the outbreak a global pandemic."

In this case, the language model has learned to generate coherent and fluent text that follows the structure of the prompt and contains relevant information. However, language models are not perfect; they may contain errors, such as grammatical mistakes or incorrect word choices. Therefore, it's essential to evaluate how well language models perform on specific tasks, such as summarization or question answering, to determine their quality and suitability for certain applications.

Reinforcement Learning
---------------------

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a broad class of algorithms used to solve problems by taking actions in an environment and receiving rewards. At its core, RL seeks to find the optimal policy – a mapping from states to actions – that maximizes the expected cumulative reward over time. There are two main types of RL algorithms: Markov decision processes (MDPs) and value-based approaches. MDPs model environments as finite state spaces equipped with transition probabilities and reward functions, whereas value-based algorithms directly optimize a single objective function representing the expected return from a given policy. One popular value-based RL algorithm is POMDPPO, which combines elements of Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) and policy gradient methods to efficiently optimize the Bellman optimality equation.
