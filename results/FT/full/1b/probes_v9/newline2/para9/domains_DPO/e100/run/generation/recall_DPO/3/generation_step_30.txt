\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has been a major focus of research in recent years, with many successful applications in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-3 [2], and OpenAI's GPT-4 [3]). However, the lack of supervision makes it difficult to control the behavior of these models, especially when they are trained on large datasets. For example, it is difficult to control the sentiment of a generation of a large LM, or to ensure that a generation of a large model is coherent with the rest of the model's output. In this paper, we propose a new method for controlling the behavior of large unsupervised LMs, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the standard RLHF method [4], but instead of using a reward model to estimate quality, we use a reward model that directly maps a generation of the LM to a preference over possible actions. This allows DPO to solve the standard RL problem with a simple classification loss. We refer to this method as DPO.

DPO is a simple and effective method for controlling the behavior and quality of large unsupervised language models. We evaluate DPO on several tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our experiments show that DPO is able to control the sentiment of generations of large LMs as well as or better that existing methods, and is also able to control the quality of generations of large LLMs as well as or better. In particular, DPO is able to achieve comparable or better performance than PPO-based RLHF on sentiment analysis, summarization and single-turn dialog, while being substantially simpler to train and implement. We also show that DPO is stable and robust to hyperparameter tuning, and that it is able to achieve high-quality results even when the reward model is not trained on the same dataset as the LM.
