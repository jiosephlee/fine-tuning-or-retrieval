\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a straightforward extension of the standard RLHF framework, which optimizes a reward function $r_{\theta}(y, x)$ to maximize the expected return $R(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}\left[r_{\theta}(y \mid x) \log p(y \mid x)\right]$ under the policy $\pi_\theta(x)$. In the standard RLHF setting, the reward function is typically defined as the negative log-likelihood of the human preference dataset $\mathcal{D}$, i.e., $r_{\theta}(\cdot) = -\log p_{\theta}(y\mid x)$, where $p_{\theta}(y|x)$ is the LM's language model distribution. The policy is then optimized using reinforcement learning, typically through policy gradient methods such as PPO. 1

\textbf{Problem Formulation.} In the standard RLHF framework described above, the reward function $r_{old}$ is learned from human preferences $\mathcal{D} = \{y^{(i)}, r_{\theta}(y^{(i)} \mid x)\}_{i=1}^N$ and the policy $\pi_{\theta}(x)$. However, obtaining such a reward function is often challenging, especially for large-scale unsupervised LMs. Instead, existing methods for controlling LMs use a pre-trained reward model $r_{\phi}$, which is fine-tuned to align with human preferences. This is typically done by maximizing the empirical risk of the reward model, i.e., $R_{\phi}(\pi_{\phi}(x)) = \mathbb{1}_{\text{class}}\left[r_{old}(y^{(i)}, x) - r_{\phi}(y^{(i)})\right]$, where $\mathbb{1}_{A}(\cdot)$ denotes the indicator function. This is equivalent to minimizing the KL-divergence between the true reward function $r_{m}$ and the fine-tuned reward model $r_{old}$. While this approach has been successful in many applications, it is not straightforward to optimize the reward model directly, as it is typically a complex function of the LM's parameters. In this work, we propose a simple alternative: instead of optimizing the reward model $r_{m}(\cdot)$, we optimize the policy $\pi_{m}(\cdotp)$ that maximizes the reward function $r(\cdotp)$. This is equivalent to minimizing $R_{m}(\pi_{m}(x)) = \sum_{y} r_{m}(y \mid x)\log p(y \mid \pi_{m}( x))$, which is a standard optimization problem in the context of supervised learning. We refer to this method as \textit{Direct preference optimization} (DPO). Our experiments show that DPO is a simple yet effective alternative to existing methods for controlling LLMs.

\textbf{Related Work.} Prior work on controlling LLMs has focused on fine-tuning the language model to align with human preferences, typically using reinforcement learning. One common approach is to train a reward model $r_{r}$ on the same dataset used to train the LM, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward function $r(y \mid x) = r_{r}(y \mid x)$, which is equivalent to minimizing the negative log-likelihood loss $- \log p(y \middle| x)$. This method is known as reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF). Another common approach is to train an off-the-shelf RL agent to control the LLM, typically using PPO. This method is referred to as RLHF. While RLHF has been successful in many scenarios, it is often unstable and requires significant hyperparameter tuning. In contrast, DPO is a straightforward extension of the RLHF framework, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning.
