What fundamental difference in approach allows DPO to achieve similar results as RLHF methods while being simpler to implement?<|EOT|>The fundamental difference between DPO and RLHF methods is that DPO uses a different optimization objective. The RLHF method optimizes the objective function $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\log \mathbb{E}_\pi[\mathbb{E}_X[\log \mathbbm{1}(X \in \mathcal{D})]]]$ where $\mathcal{D}$ is the set of training examples, $\pi$ is the probability distribution over the policy, and $\mathbbm{1}$ is the indicator function. This objective is equivalent to the KL-divergence between the true distribution $\pi$ and the policy $\pi$, which is the optimal objective for the RL problem. However, the KL-divergence is not convex, so the RLHF method uses a gradient descent method to optimize it. The DPO method instead uses a different objective function, which is convex and easier to optimize. The details of the DPO method are described in the paper "A Simple and Efficient Policy Optimization Algorithm for Reinforcement Learning" by Duchi, H. J., Goldstein, D., and Silverman, R.
