\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive advances in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-3 [2]). However, these models are trained on unstructured text, and their behavior is largely determined by the data they are exposed to. While this approach has yielded powerful models, it is difficult to control their behavior precisely, especially when compared to the fine-tuning of supervised models, which can be fine-tuned to align with specific human preferences. This is particularly important in scenarios where the desired behavior is not explicitly specified, such as in summarization, where the goal is to generate a coherent summary of a given article, or in single-turn dialogue, where the goal is for the model to respond in a way that is appropriate to the user’s intent.

In this paper, we introduce a new method for controlling the behavior of large-scale unsupervised LMs, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). Our method is based on the standard RLHF framework [3], which trains a policy on an unsupervised LM to maximize a reward function derived from user preferences. However, in contrast to previous approaches, which optimize the reward function using reinforcement learning (RL), we instead use a supervised learning method to optimize the reward function directly. This allows us to leverage the vast body of research on supervised learning, including methods for optimizing binary cross-entropy loss functions, as well as methods for training deep neural networks using reinforcement learning. Our method, which we call DPO, is simple to implement and requires only a few lines of code, making it straightforward to incorporate into existing frameworks such as PyTorch RLlib [4] or TensorFlow RL [5].

Our contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item We present a new method for training policies on large-scale unsupervised LM models, called Direct Preference Optimization.  Our method is based on a straightforward extension of the RLHF framework, but instead of optimizing the reward function using reinforcementlearning, we optimize it using a supervised learning method, allowing us to leverage the vast amount of research on supervised learning methods for training neural networks.
\item We evaluate DPO on several tasks, including sentiment classification, summarization, and dialogue, and show that it can be used effectively to control the behavior of large-scale LMs, outperforming existing methods in many cases.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervized language models have been trained on massive datasets of unstructured text, including news articles, blog posts, and social media posts. These models have demonstrated impressive performance on a variety of downstream tasks, including machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, these models are not explicitly trained to perform any particular task, and their behavior is determined by the data they have been exposed to. This makes it difficult to control their behavior, especially when compared to supervised learning methods, which can be fine tuned to align with specific human preference data.

One approach to controlling the behavior of large unsupervised LMs is to use reinforcement learning (RL) to train a policy on these models. In this setting, the goal is to train a policy that maximizes a reward function derived directly from human preferences. This approach has been successfully applied to various language tasks, including summarization [6], machine translation [7], and dialogue [8]. However, training an RL policy on a large-scale unsupervised model is a complex and often challenging task, requiring the development of sophisticated RL algorithms and careful hyperparameter tuning. In this paper, we instead propose a simpler approach, which we call Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO).
