\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Large-scale unsupervised language model have been shown to be able to learn broad world knowledge and reasoning skills, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback \cite{li2020reinforcement, li2021reinforcement, li2019reinforcement, liu2020reinforcement}, which requires collecting human labels of the relative value of model generations and fine tuning the unsupervised LM using RLHF \cite{li2021reinforcement}. However, RLHF is a complicated and often unstable procedure, requiring first fitting a reward model to reflect the human preferences, and secondly fine-tuning the large LM using reinforcement learning to optimize the reward function without drifting too far from its original model. In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model for RLHF that enables extraction the corresponding optimal policy in a closed form, allowing us solve the standard RLHF with only a simple classification task. The resulting algorithm, which we called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is robust, performant, and computational lightweight, eliminating the need to sample from the LM during fine tuning or performing significant hyperparameter optimization. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and outperforms existing methods in terms of both performance and stability. Notably, DPO outperforms PPO-based RLHF methods in ability to control sentiment and response quality in summarization, and matches or improves the response quality in single-turn dialogue while being significantly simpler to implement and train.
