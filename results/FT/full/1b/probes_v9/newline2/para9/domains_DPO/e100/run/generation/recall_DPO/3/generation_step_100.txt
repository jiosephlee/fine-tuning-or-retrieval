\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive advances in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-3 [2]). However, these models are trained on unstructured text, and their behavior is largely determined by the data they are exposed to. This poses a challenge for applications that require precise control over the model’s output, such as generating high-quality responses to queries or producing coherent summaries. In this paper, we focus on the problem of fine-tuning LMs to align with specific preferences expressed by human annotators. This is known as preference modeling, and it is a common technique in reinforcement learning (RL) for controlling the behavior of RL agents [3, 4]. In RLHF, the goal is to train an RL agent to maximize a reward function that reflects the preferences of a human teacher, while avoiding actions that would lead to a negative reward. The RLHF problem is challenging because the reward function is unknown, and the RL agent must learn to interact with the environment without access to any demonstrations or explicit instructions from the human teacher.

A popular approach to solving the RLHF problem is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF for short. In this method, the RL agent interacts with a human teacher who provides rewards for correct actions and penalties for incorrect actions. The agent then uses these rewards to train itself to maximize the expected reward. This approach has been successfully applied to various tasks, including summarization [5], question answering [6], and dialogue [7]. However, RLHF is a challenging problem, and previous studies have shown that it is often difficult to obtain satisfactory performance using off-the-shelf RL algorithms [8, 9]. To address this issue, researchers have developed specialized algorithms for RLHF, including PPO [10], a variant of the powerful reinforcement learning algorithmppo [11], and its variants such as PPO-X [12] and PPO-RL [13]. These algorithms have been shown to yield superior performance compared to more general RL algorithms, but they are also computationally intensive and require significant hyperparameter tuning.

In this paper, we propose an alternative approach to RLHF that does not require any specialized algorithms or hyperparameter tuning. Our method, which we call Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO), builds on the standard RLHF framework by replacing the reward function with a parameterized model that represents the preferences of the human teacher. We then train the RL agent to optimize the parameters of this model, rather than the reward function itself. This change allows us to use a standard supervised learning algorithm, such as gradient descent, to optimize the parameters of the reward model. Our key insight is that the optimal policy derived from this reward model can be expressed in closed form, allowing the RL agent to directly optimize the parameters of the model without having to sample from the policy and optimize the parameters of the policy network separately. This approach is similar to the method of optimizing the parameters of a reward function proposed in [14], but our method is more general and does not require any assumptions about the reward function.

We evaluate our method on several tasks involving fine-tuning LMs for preference modeling. Our experiments show that D PO can be used to fine-tune LMs for sentiment analysis, summarization and single-turn dialog, and yields performance comparable or superior to existing methods. In particular, DPO is able to align with human preferences in sentiment analysis and summarization, and matches or improves performance in single-turn dialogue. Our experiments also show that DPO is significantly simpler to implement and train than existing methods, and that it can be applied to a wide range of tasks without requiring any modifications to the underlying RL algorithms.
