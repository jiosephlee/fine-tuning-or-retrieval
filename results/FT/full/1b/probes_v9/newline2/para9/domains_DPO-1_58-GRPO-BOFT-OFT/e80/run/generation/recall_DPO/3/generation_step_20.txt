\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a variant of reinforcement learning (RL) that uses a reward model to optimize the parameters of a large unsupervised language model (LM). The reward model is trained to reflect the preferences of a human annotator, and the goal is to fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the quality of LMs, but it is challenging to implement and often unstable. In this paper, we present a new method for fine-tuning LMs using DPO, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on a simple yet powerful idea: instead of optimizing the reward model directly, we optimize the parameters of the reward model to maximize the reward given the parameters of the LM. This optimization problem is equivalent to solving the standard RL problem, but it is much easier to solve because the reward model is fixed. We show that the optimal reward model can be obtained in closed form, and we propose a simple yet effective method for solving this optimization problem. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and outperforms existing methods in terms of both the quality of the generated text and the ability to control the sentiment of the generated text.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model training has become a major focus of research in natural language processing (NLP) in recent years. These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and they have demonstrated impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, the training data used to train these models is typically not annotated with explicit labels of the quality of the generated output, making it difficult to evaluate the quality of the generated outputs. As a result, the training data is often collected through crowdsourcing, where humans are asked to rate the quality of the generated texts. This process is time-consuming and expensive, and it is often difficult to ensure that the crowdworkers are representative of the general population. Moreover, the quality of the generated samples is often not well-defined, and it is difficult to quantify the quality of the generated sample in a way that is meaningful to the human annotators. To address these challenges, researchers have developed methods for fine-tuning LLMs using human feedback. One popular approach is reinforcement learning from human feedback, which involves training a reward model to reflect the preferences of the human annotators, and then fine-tune the LLM using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. The reward model is typically learned using a reinforcement learning algorithm such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Actor-Critic, and the fine-tuned LLM is then evaluated using the reward model. This approach has been successfully applied to various NLP tasks, including machine translation (Chen et al., 2019; Li et al., 2020; Li et al.,2021), summarization (Chen et al. 2021; Li et al.,2019), and dialogue (Li et al., 2021; Li and Bansal, 2021; Li, 2021; Wang et al., 2022). However, the reinforcement learning process is often unstable, and it is difficult for the human annotators to provide consistent feedback. Moreover, the reward model is typically learned from a small number of human annotations, which may not accurately reflect the preferences of the general population. To address these challenges and improve the stability of the reinforcement learning process, researchers have proposed methods for collecting more human annotations, including crowdsourcing (Chen et al, 2021; Chen et al., 2023; Li et al., , 2022 and active learning (Li et al., , 2019. Another approach is to use a reward model that is trained on a larger dataset of human annotations, but this approach is computationally expensive and requires significant computational resources. To address these challenges in a more efficient manner, we propose a new method for fine tuning LLMs using DPO, which is based on a simple idea: instead of optimizing a reward model directly, we can optimize the parameters of the model to maximize the reward that is given by the reward model. This optimization problem is essentially the same as the standard RL problem, and it can be solved using standard RL algorithms such as PPO. However, the reward model is fixed, and the optimization problem is much easier to solve. We show that the reward model can be obtained from the optimal policy of the standard RL problem, which can be expressed as a linear program. We propose a simple yet effective algorithm for solving this linear program, and we refer to this algorithm as Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). Our experiments show that D PO is stable, performant,and computationally lightweight, and it outperforms existing methods for fine-tuning large LMs using human feedback.
