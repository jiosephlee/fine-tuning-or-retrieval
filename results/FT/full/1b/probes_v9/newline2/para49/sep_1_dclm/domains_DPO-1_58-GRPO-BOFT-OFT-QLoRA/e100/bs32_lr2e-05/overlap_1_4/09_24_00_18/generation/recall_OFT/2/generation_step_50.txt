Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique that modifies the parameters of a pretrained diffusion model to preserve the geometry of neurons. This is achieved by adding a small orthogonal matrix to the diffusion model's parameters, which results in a new diffusion model with the same initial parameters but with a different geometry. OFT has been shown to be effective in preserving the geometry of neurons and improving the performance of text-to-image models. However, OFT has not been widely adopted due to its computational complexity. In this paper, we present a simple yet effective method for implementing OFT, called Orthogonal Finetuning with Random Initialization (OFTR), which significantly reduces the computational cost of OFT. Our OFTR method can be applied to any pretrained diffusion model, including those pretrained on ImageNet, and it does not require any additional training data. Introduction
Text-to-image generation (T2I) is a challenging task that aims to generate images from text descriptions. The state-of-the-art text-to-image models, such as T2I models trained on ImageNet [1], achieve impressive results on various text-to-image datasets, such as Fashion-MNIST [2], CIFAR-10 [3], and ImageNet [4]. These models are trained using a large amount of data, and they are often pretrained on ImageNet. However, these models are not suitable for downstream applications, such as image caption generation, where the goal is to generate a short description of an image. To address this issue, researchers have proposed various methods for finetuning pretrained text-to-image models on downstream tasks, such as text-to-image generation [5], image captioning [6], and image retrieval [7]. These finetuning methods typically involve modifying the parameters of the pretrained text-to-image model to improve its performance on the downstream task. One popular finetuning method is additive finetuning, which involves adding a small additive matrix to the pretrained model's parameters. For example, the additive finetuning method proposed by [8] adds a small additive matrix to each layer of the pretrained model. Another popular finetuning method, LoRA [9], adds a small orthogonal matrix to each layer of a pretrained model. LoRA has been shown to be more effective than additive finetuning in improving the performance of textto-image models [10].
