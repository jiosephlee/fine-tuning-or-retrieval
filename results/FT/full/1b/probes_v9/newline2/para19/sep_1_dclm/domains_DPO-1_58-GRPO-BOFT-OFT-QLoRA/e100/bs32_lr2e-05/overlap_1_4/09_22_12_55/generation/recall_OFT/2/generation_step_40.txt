The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique that modifies the parameters of a pretrained model to preserve the original neuron shape and orientation. This approach has been shown to improve the performance of text-to-image models, especially when applied to the task of image captioning. However, OFT has not been widely adopted in the context of text-to-image generation due to its reliance on a large number of trainable parameters. In this paper, we present a new method for controlling text-to-image diffusion, called Orthogonal Finetuning with Adaptive Parameter Scaling (OFAPS), which addresses this limitation by introducing a parameter scaling mechanism that allows for more efficient parameter optimization. Our experiments show that OFAPS can significantly improve the performance of textto-image models, particularly when applied to the task-specific text-to-image generation model T2I-GPT-4. We also demonstrate that OFAPS can be used to control the diffusion of text-to-image models without requiring any additional training data, making it a promising approach for improving the quality of text-to-image models.

The paper is titled "Controlling Text-to-image Diffusion by Orthogonal Fine-tuning" and is available at https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00125.
