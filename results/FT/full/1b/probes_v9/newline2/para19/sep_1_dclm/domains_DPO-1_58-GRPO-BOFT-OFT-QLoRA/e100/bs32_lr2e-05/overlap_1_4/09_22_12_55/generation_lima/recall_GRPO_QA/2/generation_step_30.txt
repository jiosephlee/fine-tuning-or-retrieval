What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>> GRPO is a memory-efficient variant of the policy optimization algorithm PPO. It is designed to be more efficient than PPO when dealing with large language models. The main idea behind GRPO is to group similar policies together and optimize them separately. This allows for a more efficient use of memory during training. In contrast to PPO, which optimizes each policy independently, GRPO groups policies into groups based on their similarity. This grouping is done using a similarity metric, typically the cosine similarity between the policies' policy vectors. By optimizing these groups separately, GRPO can reduce the number of policy updates required during training. Additionally, GRPO uses a group size parameter, which controls how many policies are grouped together. A larger group size leads to a more accurate representation of the underlying policy distribution, but also requires more memory. On the other hand, a smaller group size leads to a less accurate representation of the policy distribution, but uses less memory.
