Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique that modifies the parameters of a pretrained diffusion model by adding a small orthogonal matrix to the original parameters. This orthogonal matrix is chosen to minimize the Frobenius norm of the resulting parameters. OFT has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of various text-to-image generation models, including those based on the GAN framework. However, OFT has not been widely adopted due to its computational complexity and the need for a large number of orthogonal matrices. In this paper, we present a new approach for OFT that significantly reduces the computational cost while maintaining the effectiveness of OFT. Our method, called Orthogonal Finetuning with Orthogonal Matrix (OFOTM), uses a single orthogonal matrix to modify the parameters of a pretrained model. We show that OFOTM can achieve comparable results to OFT while requiring only a fraction of the computational resources. Furthermore, OFOTM can be applied to any pretrained diffusion model, including those based on GANs, without requiring any modifications to the original codebase. We conduct extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating that OFOTM can significantly improve the performance of text-to-image generation models. Our code is available at https://github.com/ChenYiZhang/OFTM.

Introduction
Text-to-image generation (T2I) is a challenging task that aims to generate high-quality images from textual descriptions. The success of T2I models has led to a surge in research interest in this area. Among the various approaches, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been widely adopted due to their ability to generate realistic images [1,2]. However, GANs are known to suffer from instability and difficulty in training, especially when generating images from text. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various techniques to stabilize GAN training, including adversarial training [3], gradient clipping [4], and data augmentation [5]. These methods have been shown to improve the performance of GANs, but they often require significant computational resources.
One promising approach to stabilize GAN training is finetuning, which involves fine-tuning a pretrained GAN model with additional data. This method has been shown to improve the training stability of GANs [6,7]. However, finetuning can lead to undesirable effects, such as overfitting and the loss of important geometric properties of neurons [8]. To address these issues, several methods have been proposed to preserve the geometric structure of neurons during finetuning. One such method is Orthogonal Finetuning [9], which modifies the parameters of a GAN model by adding a small matrix to the original parameters, resulting in a new set of parameters that are orthogonal to the original ones. Orthogonal Finetune (OFT) has been shown to improve GAN training stability and reduce overfitting [9]. However, OFT has been limited to finetuning GANs based on the GAN architecture [9]. In this paper, we introduce a new method for OFT, called Orthogonal Finestuning with Orthogonal Matrix, or OFOTM. OFOTM is a simple yet effective method for OFT that does not require any modifications to the original GAN codebase. We demonstrate that OFOTM can be used to finetune a wide range of pretrained diffusion models, including those based GANs, without any modifications to the original model code. Our experiments show that OFOTM achieves comparable results to OFT, while requiring only a fraction (approximately 10%) of the computational resources. Moreover, OFOTM can also be applied to any pretrained model, including those based GANS, without requiring any modifications. Our code is available online at https://github.com/chenyi-zhang/OFTM.
