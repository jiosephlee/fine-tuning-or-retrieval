Introduction
The ability to generate high-quality text-to-video translations is a key capability for many applications, such as video summarization, video captioning, and video retrieval. However, current text-to-video translation models are not yet able to generate fluent and natural video translations. To address this challenge, we propose a new approach for text-to-video translation, called DPO. DPO is a language model-based method that uses a language model to optimize the alignment between a video-to-text translation model and human preferences. The core idea of DPO is to use a language model to represent the preferences of human annotators, and then optimize the alignment between the language model and the preferences. This is similar to the approach used in the reinforcement learning community to optimize the alignment between an agent and a reward model (Schulman et al., 2017;Kaelbling et al., 1996;Sutton and Barto, 2018).
We evaluate DPO on two text-to-video translation tasks. First, we evaluate DPO on the task of text-to-video translation in the context of the VideoText dataset (Zhang et al., 2019b), which is a large-scale video-to-text dataset consisting of 1,000 videos and 10,000 humanannotated transcripts. Second, we evaluate DPO in the context of the Text2Video dataset (Zhang et aL, 2019a), which is a large scale video-to-text dataset consisting 1,000 videos. We find that DPO significantly improves the performance of text-to-video translation models compared to existing methods. In particular, our experiments show that DPO achieves a relative increase of 3.5 points on the human evaluation metric of text-to-video alignment. Furthermore, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of DPO, showing its robustness to various settings and hyperparameter choices. Finally, we provide an in-depth analysis of the underlying mechanism and the benefits of using a language model to represent preferences.
To summarize, our main contributions are as follows: • We propose a new approach for aligning language models and human preferences, called DPO. The core idea of the algorithm is to use a languagemodel to represent human preferences, and then optimize the language model with a simple loss function. • We evaluate DPO on the text-to-video translation task in the context of the two large-scale video-to-text datasets, VideoText and Text2Video. Our experiments show that the DPO achieves a relative performance improvement of 3.4 percentage points on the humanevaluation metric of text-to-video translation. • We conduct a comprehensive analysis of our algorithm, showing that it is stable to various settings and hyper-parameter choices. • We provide an in-depth analysis that sheds light on the effectiveness and the underlying mechanism of D PO. This analysis provides valuable insights into the design of language models and the benefits of using language models to represent preferences.
