  1. Introduction

    In this section, we introduce the problem of training large language models (LLMs) and discuss the challenges that arise from training such models. We then describe the DPO algorithm, which is the core of our approach to training LLMs efficiently.

    Training large language models is challenging for a number of reasons. First, the training data is often very large, and it is difficult to find a small subset of the data that can be used to train a high-quality model. Second, the training data is typically unstructured, and it is difficult for humans to understand the content of the data and to identify the most important sentences. Finally, the training data is usually noisy, and it is difficult or impossible to correct errors in the data. As a result, training large language models is a difficult problem that requires a lot of computational resources.

    In this paper, we focus on the problem of training LLMs on large corpora. Specifically, we consider the task of training LLMs that can generate coherent text given a prompt. This is a challenging task, as it requires the LLM to understand the context of the prompt and to generate text that is relevant to the prompt. However, it is also an important task, as it has many applications in areas such as text generation, question answering, and machine translation.

    To train LLMs on large datasets, we need to develop efficient algorithms and techniques for training large models. One approach is to use reinforcement learning (RL), where the LLM is trained to maximize a reward function that measures the quality of its generated text. RL has been successfully applied to various tasks in natural language processing, including machine translation (Koehn et al., 2003; McKeown et al., 2016), dialogue (Sutton et al., 2018), and text generation (Hulsmans et al., 2017; Khatib et al., 2019). However, RL is typically slow and computationally intensive, especially for large models. Moreover, RL requires human supervision, which can be expensive and time-consuming.
