\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for fine-tuning LMs. In contrast to existing methods, DPO does not require any hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the LM prior to fine-tuning, and instead uses a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model parameters. We evaluate DPO on a range of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our results show that DPO is both stable and performant, outperforming existing methods on several benchmarks. In particular, DPO achieves comparable performance to PPO-based RLHF on sentiment analysis and summarization, while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models have recently achieved impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], XLNet [2]) and generation (e.g., GPT-3 [3], OpenAI GPT [4]). However, despite their impressive performance, these models are trained on large amounts of text data, and thus lack any explicit human supervision. This makes it difficult to control the behavior of the resulting LMs, especially when compared to supervised models that are fine-tuned on human feedback. For example, it is unclear how to effectively fine-tune an LM to generate responses that are consistent with a given prompt, or to ensure that the generated text is of high quality. 

To address this challenge, a number of methods have been proposed to gain steerability by collecting human preferences for the behavior of the LMs. These methods typically involve training a separate reward model to reflect the preferences of the human annotators, and then fine-tune the LMs using reinforcement learning (RL) to maximize the reward. The most common approach is to use Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [5], which optimizes the reward model alongside the LM to ensure that the reward remains high throughout the training process. However, PPO is a complex algorithm that requires careful tuning of its hyperparameters, and it is often unclear whether the resulting reward model is accurate enough to be used for RL. Moreover, the RL phase of the method is often unstable, and may require multiple training runs to converge. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning unsupervised LMs that avoids these challenges, and is thus more straightforward to implement and tune.

Our key insight is to reformulate the standard RL problem as a constrained optimization problem. Specifically, we consider the standard RL problem of maximizing the expected discounted reward starting from a given policy $\pi_0$ and a reward model $\pi^R(\cdot|\theta)$, where the reward model is assumed to be a parametric function of the policy parameters $\theta$. We then show that the optimal policy $\pi^*$ under this framework is simply the unique solution to the following optimization problem:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal_policy}
    \argmax_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R(\pi| \theta)] = \argmax_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_\pi\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^{t} R(\pi| \theta)\right],
\end{equation}

where $R(\pi| \thetahat)$ is the reward function defined by the reward model $\pi^{\thetahat}$. This equation states that the optimal policy $\hat{\pi} = \pi^*$ is the unique solution to the optimization problem of maximizing the expected reward starting from the given policy $\pi$ and reward model $\pi^*$, subject to the constraint that the reward model is a parametric function of $\pi$. This optimization problem can be solved exactly, and we derive the closed-form expression for the optimal policy in terms of the reward model parameters $\theta$ and the discount factor $\gamma$. We refer to this method as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
