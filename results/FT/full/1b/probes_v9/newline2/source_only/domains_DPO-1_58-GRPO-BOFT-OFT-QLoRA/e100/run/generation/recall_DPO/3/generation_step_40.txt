\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that uses a reward model to optimize the behavior of a large unsupervised language model (LM). The reward model is a parametric function that maps the LM's output to a scalar value representing the relative quality of the output. The goal of DPO is to optimize the reward function to maximize the reward, which is equivalent to maximizing the expected reward of the optimal policy. This is a standard RL problem, but one that is typically solved using reinforcement learning from human feedback, which is a complex and often unreliable procedure. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of this reward model that enables us to solve the standard DPO problem in closed form, allowing for a simple and computationally lightweight solution. We call this new parameterization the \textit{direct preference optimization} (DPO) parameterization. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and can be used to fine-tune LMs with a simple classification loss. We also show that DPO can achieve comparable performance to existing methods, including PPO-based RLHF, in terms of both the quality of the generated text and the quality of the responses to prompts. Finally, we demonstrate that DPO can be used to improve the quality of the responses in single-turn dialogue, and that it can be used to improve sentiment analysis of the generated text.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model training has become a popular approach to building high-quality language models. These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language-specific knowledge, including general world knowledge, reasoning skills, and even some rudimentary style and sentiment analysis. However, obtaining a high-quality language model is difficult, and obtaining a model that is both high-quality and stable is even more challenging. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for obtaining a high-quality language models that is both stable and easy to train. To this end, we propose a new method for optimizing the behavior of a large-scale unsupervised LM, called direct preference optimization (DPO).

DPO is a simple reinforcement learning (RLL) algorithm that uses a parametric reward function to optimize the behavior of the LM. The goal of D PO is to optimize the reward to maximize the reward, and this is equivalent to maximizing the expectation of the reward of the optimal policy, which is a standard RL problem. However, solving this problem is typically done using reinforcement learning from human preferences, which is a complex procedure that is often unstable. In this paper, instead of using reinforcement learning from human preference, we propose a new way of parameterizing the reward function that allows us to solve the standard problem in closed form, and thus to obtain a simple and computationally light solution. We call this parameterization the direct preference optimization (D PO) parameterization.

We evaluate DPO on several benchmarks, including the Stanford Sentiment Treebank (SST-2) and the Stanford Question Answering Dataset (SQuAD v1.1). We find that DPO is stable and performant, and that it can achieve comparable performance to PPO-based RLHF.
