\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the human preference score of a given generation.  In contrast to existing methods, DPO does not require any sampling from the LM during training, nor does it require any hyperparameter tuning.  Instead, DPO uses a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model, and then uses a standard policy gradient method to optimize the LM to maximize the reward model's output.  This allows DPO to be trained on a large number of unlabeled data, and to be applied to a wide range of tasks.

\section{Related Work}

Large unsupervised language models have been shown to learn rich representations of the world and some reasoning skills, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training data.  In this section, we review the existing approaches for gaining steerability, and highlight the key challenges in each approach.

\textbf{Reward learning from human feedback}.  One approach to gain steerability is to train a reward model from human feedback.  This is typically done by sampling a few unlabeled data points, and then training a policy to maximize the reward of these samples.  The policy is then used to generate new data, and the reward of this new data is estimated by a separate reward model.  This process is repeated until the reward model converges.  This approach has been used to train LMs for summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis (see, e.g., \cite{liu2022learning,liu2023training,liu2024training}).  However, the reward model must be carefully tuned to match the human preferences, and the resulting reward model is often quite large, making it difficult to train on a large amount of data.

\textbf{Reinforcement learning from human feedback}. Another approach to gain steerability, which we refer to as reinforcement learning from human feedback with reinforcement learning (RLHF), is to train a reward policy using reinforcement learning from human feedback.  In RLHF, a reward model is first trained from human feedback, and then the reward model is used to generate new data points, and the reward of these new data points is estimated by a separate reinforcement learning policy.  This process is then repeated until the reinforcement learning policy converges.  RLHF has been used to train an LLM for summarization (see, e.g. \cite{liu2019learning,liu2022training}), and to train an LLM to generate coherent responses in single-turn dialogue (see, e.g.\ \cite{liu_2023_towards,liu2023learning}).  However, RLHF is a complicated procedure, and the resulting reward policy is often quite large, which makes it difficult to train on large amounts of data.

\textit{Direct Preference Optimisation}.  A third approach to gain steerability that we refer to as direct preference optimization (DPO), is to directly optimize the reward model to maximize the human preference of a given generation. In DPO, the reward model is optimized using a simple classification loss, and then the resulting policy is used to optimize the unsupervised LM.  This allows D PO to be trained on a wide range of unlabeled data, without requiring any sampling from the LM, or any tuning of the reward model.  In addition, DPO can be applied to a wide variety of tasks, including summarization, question answering and single-turn dialogue.  We describe the details of DPO in Section \ref{sec:dpo}.

\section{A New Parameterization of the Reward Model}
