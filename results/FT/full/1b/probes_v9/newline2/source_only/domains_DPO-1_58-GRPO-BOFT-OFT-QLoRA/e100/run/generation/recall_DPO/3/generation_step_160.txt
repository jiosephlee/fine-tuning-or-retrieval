\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In the following, we will describe the standard RLHF setup, and then introduce DPO.

\subsection{Standard RLHF}

We consider a setting where a large unsupervised LM $M$ is trained on a large corpus $\mathcal{X}$, and we want to fine-tune it to produce responses of a given quality level $q \in \mathbb{R}^d$. We assume that the quality level is known to a human annotator $H$, who provides feedback on the quality of each generated response $r \in \mathcal{X}$ by assigning a score $s_h(r) \in \mathbb R$ to it. The goal of RLHF is to find a policy $\pi_\theta$ that maximizes the expected reward from the human feedback, i.e.,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rlhf}
    \max_{\pi_\theta} \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta \sim \pi_\theta(\mathcal{X})} \left[ \sum_{r \in \mathbb {X}} s_h(r) \log \frac{\pi_\theta (r | \text{Saw } r, H)}{\pi_\theta \left( r | \text{SAW }, H \right)} \right].
\end{equation}

The policy $\pi_\theta \in \mathcal P(\mathcal X, \mathcal S)$ is typically learned from data using a policy gradient method, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) \cite{schulman2017proximal} or Direct Policy Optimization (DPO) \cite{liu2018direct}. The reward model $\pi_\theta^r \in \mathbf{P}(\mathcal X,  \mathcal S, \mathcal R)$ parametrized by $\theta$ is then defined as

\begin{equations}\label{eq:reward_model}
    \pi_\theta^r (s | \text{Saws }, H) = \mathbb{E}_h \pi_\theta [s \mid \text{Saw }, H, r] = \sum_{h'} \pi_\theta [r \mid \text{SAW }, H, s] \pi_\theta [h' \mid \text{ Saw }, H]^\top,
\end{equations}

where $\mathbb{E}_H$ denotes the expectation with respect to the human feedback $H$.

The RLHF objective (\ref{eq:rlhf}) can be written as a maximum likelihood problem with a log-linear reward model, and thus can be efficiently solved using maximum likelihood algorithms such as Expectation-Maximization (EM) \cite{bottou1995expectation}. However, the reward model is often not easily interpretable, and in many cases it is desirable to have a more interpretable alternative. For example, in the context of LLMs, the reward model is typically a neural network that maps a text token $t$ to a scalar reward value $r(t)$. This reward value is then used to compute the reward for the corresponding text token in the model, and the policy is optimized to maximize the reward. However, the interpretation of the reward model is limited to the specific choice of neural network architecture. In contrast, the policy $\pi_\theta$, which determines the reward, is a general function of the text tokens and the human feedback, and thus can be interpreted in a more general way.

\subsection{DPO: Reward Model-Free RLHF}

We now introduce a new reward model for RLHF that enables us to solve the standard problem with a simple classification loss. The key idea is to represent the reward model $\pi_\thetar$ as a linear combination of the policy $\pi_\thetao$ and a latent variable $z_t$:

\begin{equation}
    r(t) = \pi_\thetao (t) + \beta_z z_t,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_z > 0$ is a hyperparameter. The latent variable $z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is typically assumed to be drawn from a zero-mean normal distribution with a unit variance, but other distributions are also possible.
