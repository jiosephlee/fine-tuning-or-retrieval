\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the human preference score of a given generation.  In contrast to existing methods, DPO does not require any sampling from the LM during training, nor does it require any hyperparameter tuning.  Instead, DPO uses a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model, and then fine-tunes the reward model using reinforcement learning to maximize the human preference scores of the generated samples.  This is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:dpo}.  The resulting policy is the optimal policy for maximizing the reward model's preference score, and so the DPO algorithm finds the optimal policy for the RLHF problem.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Large Language Models (LLMs)}. Large-scale LLMs have been trained on massive datasets of text, including the open-source BERT~\citep{devlin-etal-2018-microsoft}, GPT-3~\citep{brown2020gpt}, and OpenAI GPT-4~\citep{openai2023gpt4}. These models have demonstrated impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding, question answering, and machine translation. However, LLMs are completely unsupervised, meaning that they learn from unannotated text data without human supervision. This makes LLMs particularly interesting for applications where human feedback is difficult or impossible to obtain, such as in the development of conversational agents and virtual assistants.

\textbf{Reward Learning}. Reward learning is a general framework for training policies to maximize a reward function~\citep{hulora2017learning,liu2018reinforcement,liu2019learning,liu2020learning,liu2021learning}. The most common approach to reward learning is reinforcement learning (RL), which trains a policy to maximize a reward function by interacting with an environment and receiving feedback about the rewards received for different actions. However, RL is typically computationally expensive and unstable, especially when training on large datasets. To address these challenges, alternative approaches to reward learning have been proposed, including model-based RL~\citep{liu2021learning}, model-free RL~\citep{liao2018model,liu2020reinforcement}, and reward shaping~\citep{zhang2019reward,liu2021reward}. These methods can be applied to LLMs, but often require additional hyperparameters to be tuned, such as the value function approximation, the discount factor, and the learning rate.

\textbf{Control of Language Models}. While LLMs have demonstrated impressive performance on many tasks, controlling their behavior is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature. One approach to controlling LLMs is to use reinforcement learning to train a policy that maximizes a reward function defined by the LLM's internal model. This approach has been used to control LLMs in various domains, including summarization~\citep{wang2022training}, dialogue~\citep{chen2022training}, and text-to-image generation~\citep{zhong2023training}. However, RL-based control of LLMs is typically unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter tuning to achieve satisfactory performance. In this paper, we propose a new approach to control LLMs using a simple reinforcement learning algorithm called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
