\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model fine-tuning algorithm. It takes a pretrained unsupervised language model and a collection of preference annotations, and optimizes the language model to maximize the reward function defined by the preference annotations. This is a natural extension of reinforcement learning (RL), where the goal is to optimize a reward function to achieve a desired policy. In contrast to existing reward model fine-tuning algorithms, DPO does not require sampling from the language model, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning. We evaluate DPO on a variety of benchmarks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our results show that DPO is able to fine-tune LMs with high-quality preferences to align with human preferences, and outperforms existing methods on all benchmarks.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has been a major focus in natural language processing (NLP) in recent years. Large-scale unsupervised LM training has been shown to improve performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation, question answering, and text generation \citep{devlin2018bert,radford2019learning,liu2019language}. However, obtaining high-quality unlabeled data for such training is challenging, and existing methods typically rely on large-scale datasets of human-written text paired with ground-truth labels of the generated text's quality \citep{liu2020unsupervised,liu2021language}. These methods are often computationally expensive and require human annotation efforts, limiting their scalability.

To address this challenge, researchers have turned to large-scale unsupervised LM models trained on self-supervised objectives \citep{hulora2022,liu2022learning,liu2023learning}. These methods typically rely on a set of pretext tasks that can be solved by the LM, such as image captioning \citep{radford2015learning}, text-to-image generation \citep{radon2017semantic,radford2017language}, and text-to-text generation \citep{lu2018progress,radford2019language}. Self-supervised objectives have been shown to improve downstream task performance, but obtaining high-quality pretext tasks is often difficult, especially for low-resource languages \citep{li2022learning,li2023learning}. To address this, researchers have turned to external datasets of human-written text with associated quality annotations \citep{li2019learning,li2020unsupervised}. These methods are often more computationally efficient than self-supervised methods, but they still require human annotation efforts.

A promising approach to obtaining high-quality preferences for unsupervised LMs is reinforcement learning (RL). RL has been used to train LMs from scratch \citep{liang2019training,liu2020learning,liu2021learning,liu2022language}, and to improve the performance of pre-trained LMs \citep{lipton2020rllm,lipton2021reinforcement,liu2022pretraining}. In reinforcement learning, a model is trained to maximize a reward function defined by a human-defined preference dataset. The reward function is used to guide the model's behavior, and the model is then evaluated on a downstream task to measure its performance. RL has been shown to be effective in training LMs from scratch \ref{app:rl_from_scratch}, and improving the performance of pre-trained models \citep{lipton2020rllm,lipton2021reinforcement}. However, RL is often unstable, and requires careful hyperparameter tuning to achieve good performance \citep{lijiang2022reinforcement}. Additionally, RL is typically performed in an online setting, where the model is updated in response to a stream of rewards. This can be computationally expensive, especially for large models.

In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model for RLHF, which enables us to solve the standard reinforcement learning problem with only a simple binary classification loss. Our reward model is based on the reward function defined by a preference dataset, and we show that the corresponding optimal policy can be obtained in closed form. This allows us to solve the standard reward learning problem with only a classification loss, eliminating the need for hyperparameter tuning and online RL. Our reward model is simple to implement and computationally efficient, and we demonstrate its effectiveness in fine-tuning LMs to align with high-quality preferences. Our contributions are summarized below:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item We introduce a new parameterized model for reward learning, which enables us to efficiently solve the standard reinforcement learning (RL) problem with only a binary classification loss.
    \item We evaluate our reward model in reward learning and RLHF, showing that it is effective in fine-tuning LLMs to align with high-quality human preferences.
\end{itemize}
