\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model for the LLM, which is used to optimize the LLM's parameters to maximize the reward function. The reward function is usually a function of the LLM's parameters, but it can also be a function of the LML's output, such as the sentiment of the LML's generated text. In this paper, we propose a new reward model for DPO, which we call the \textit{direct preference model} (DPM). The DPM is a neural network that maps the LML's parameters to a scalar value that represents the preference of the human annotators. The DPM is trained by maximizing the expected reward of the LLM under the DPM. We show that the DPM can be efficiently learned from the LLM's parameters using a simple classification loss. We also show that the DPM is a valid reward model for DPO. Our experiments show that DPO is effective at controlling the LLM's behavior, and is comparable to existing methods in terms of performance.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building high-quality language models. These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language skills, including general knowledge, reasoning, and even some aspects of human-like behavior. However, the completely unsupervised training of LMs poses a challenge for the control of their behavior. While LMs can be trained to produce high-quality outputs, they are not easily steerable, and it is difficult to control their behavior with human feedback. This is especially true for LMs that are trained on large corpora, such as BERT, GPT-3, and OpenAI's GPT-4. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for controlling the behavior of LMs that is simple to implement and easy to train. To this end, we propose Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for optimizing the parameters of an LLM to maximize the preference of human annotators. DPO is based on the idea of using a reward model to optimize the LLM. The reward model is a neural network that takes the LLM's parameters as input and outputs a scalar value that represents how much the LLM is preferred by the human annotators. We propose a new reward model, called the \textit{Direct preference model} (DPO), which is a neural network that is trained to maximize the expected reward of the LM under the DPO. We show that the reward model can be efficiently learned from LLM's parameters using the standard cross-entropy loss. We also show theoretically that the DPO is a valid reward model. We evaluate DPO in several settings, including the \textbf{SentimentEval} and \textbf{SummarizeEval}, and the \textbf{DialogQA} datasets. Our experiments show that the DPO is effective at steering the LLM's behavior and is comparable to existing control methods in terms of performance. In particular, DPO is able to control the sentiment of LLM's generated text, and is comparable to PPO-based RLHF methods in terms of performance in summarization and single turn dialogue.

For example, the \textit{Tatoeba} corpus contains more than 100 billion tokens, and is used for training LMs for machine translation. The \textit{Wikipedia} corpus contains more than one billion tokens, and is widely used for training LMs in summarization. The \textit{\#Corpora} dataset contains more than 100M tokens, and is used to evaluate the performance of LMs in summarization.
