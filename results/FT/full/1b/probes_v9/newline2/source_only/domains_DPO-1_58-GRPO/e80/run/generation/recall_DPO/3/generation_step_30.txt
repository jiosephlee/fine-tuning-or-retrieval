\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models have achieved impressive performance on various tasks, including question answering (QA) (Devlin et al., 2019; Li et al., 2020b; Radford et al., 2021), summarization (Bai et al., 2022), and single-turn dialogue (Chen et al., 2023). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature. In particular, it is difficult to evaluate the quality of model generations without access to additional supervision, such as human annotations of the relative quality of generations. This is a major limitation, since the ability to control the behavior of a model is a key factor in its utility, and without such control, it is difficult to use language models to solve real-world problems.

One approach to gaining steerability is reinforcement learning from human feedback, which has been successfully applied to various language modeling tasks (Chen et al. 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Li et al., in press; Li et al., under review; Li et al., submitted). In reinforcement learning from human feedback with language models, the model is trained to maximize a reward function defined on human preferences, which are collected through reinforcement learning from human interaction. The reward function is typically learned from a dataset of human preferences and model generations, and then used to train a policy that maximizes the reward. However, RLHF has several challenges. First, the reward function must be learned from a dataset of preferences and model generations, which is often not available. Second, the reward function is typically learned using reinforcement learning, which is a complex and often computationally intensive procedure. Third, the reward function is learned from a dataset of interactions between humans and models, which may not be representative of the desired behavior of the model in the real world. Fourth, the reward function must then be fine-tuned to the desired behavior of the target model, which requires solving a large scale optimization problem. Finally, the reward function must not only be learned from the desired behavior, but also from the initial model, which can be difficult to obtain.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach to reinforcement learning from human feedback that addresses these challenges. Our approach, which we call Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO), takes an unsupervised large language model as input and outputs a reward function that maximizes the human preference data. The reward function is defined on the training set of preferences and model generations and is learned using a simple binary classification loss, which does not require access to a reward function learned from human preferences. We refer to this approach as DPO because it optimizes the reward function directly, without first fitting a separate reward model. Our experiments show that D PO is stable, performant,and computationally lightweight, and it outperforms existing methods for reinforcement learning from human feedback in several benchmarks.
