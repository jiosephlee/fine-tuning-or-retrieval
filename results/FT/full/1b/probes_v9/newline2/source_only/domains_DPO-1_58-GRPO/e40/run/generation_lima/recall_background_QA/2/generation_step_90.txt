Under a PPO, the insurance company will pay a certain percentage of the cost of covered services. The patient is responsible for paying the rest of the cost. This means that the patient has more freedom to choose where to get medical care, but they also have to pay more out of pocket.

For example, if a patient goes to the doctor for a cold, the insurance company will usually pay 80% of the cost of the visit. The patient would then be responsible for paying the remaining 20%. If the patient goes to the emergency room for a broken leg, the insurance company will typically pay 100% of the cost of care. The patient would then only be responsible for paying the deductible and copayments.
