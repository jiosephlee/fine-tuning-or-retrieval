\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular research direction in recent years. Large-scale LMs are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled data, and they learn to encode the entire web and other large-scale corpora. These LMs have shown impressive performance on various downstream tasks, such as machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, the training of LMs is still an unsupervised process, and it is difficult to control the behavior of LMs. For example, it is difficult to control whether LMs will generate positive or negative sentences, or whether LMs will generate coherent or incoherent sentences. Therefore, it is important to develop methods to control the behavior of large-scale LMs.

One way to control the behavior of the LMs is to collect human feedback of the relative quality of the LMs. For example,
\cite{li2022learning} collects human feedback of the relative sentiment of LMs, and then fine-tunes the LMs using reinforcement learning from human feedback. This method is called reinforcement learning from human feedback with preference learning (RLHF). However, RLHF is a complicated and unstable procedure. First, the RLHF algorithm needs to fit a reward model that reflects human preferences. Then, the reward model is used to optimize the LMs, and the optimization is performed using reinforcement learning. Finally, the LMs are fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward estimated by the reward model. This procedure is called reinforcement learning from preference learning (RLPF). However, RLPF is unstable, because the reward model is not guaranteed to be accurate. In addition, RLPF is computationally expensive, because it needs to sample from the LMs during the optimization process. Therefore, RLPF is not suitable for large-scale LMs.

In this paper, we propose Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which is a new method for controlling the behavior of LMs without collecting human feedback. DPO is based on the idea of reward models. A reward model is a function that maps each generation of the LM to a scalar value, which represents the reward of the generation. The reward model is used to estimate the value of each generation of the LMs. The optimization algorithm is based on the idea that the optimal policy is the one that maximizes the expected value of the reward. Therefore, DPO optimizes the LMs using reinforcementlearning, but instead of optimizing the reward model, we optimize the reward function directly. The optimization is done using a simple classification loss, which is much simpler than the RLPF algorithm. We evaluate DPO in several benchmarks, including sentiment classification, summarization, and dialogue. Our experiments show DPO is effective and performant, and it can be applied to large-scale LMs with high performance. In particular, DPO outperforms the existing methods in all benchmarks, and it is more efficient and stable.
