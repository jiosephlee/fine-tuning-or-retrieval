\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a large unsupervised language model (LM) to align with human preferences. Existing methods for RLHF typically fit a reward model to human preferences, and then use RL to optimize the unsupervised LM using the reward as a target. However, this approach is unstable, as the reward model is often not well-calibrated, and the resulting fine-tuning policy may drift away from the original model. In this paper, we propose a new method for RLHF, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that avoids the need for a reward model by directly optimizing the unsupervised LM for the reward function it is expected to maximize. We show that DPO can be solved exactly, and that the resulting policy is optimal for the reward function it optimizes. We evaluate DPO on several benchmarks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and show that it can achieve comparable performance to existing methods while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model training has led to impressive advances in natural language understanding (NLU) and generation (e.g., \cite{devlin-etal-2019-bert, devlin-etal-2018-bert, devlin-etal-luminescent-2019}). However, the lack of supervision makes it difficult to control the behavior of these models, especially when they are used in applications where precise control of the model's behavior is required. For example, in sentiment analysis, the goal is to predict the sentiment of a given text, but the model must also be able to generate texts with the desired sentiment. Similarly, in summarization, the goal is to generate a summary of a given text, while the model must also be capable of generating texts with the desired length and style. In both cases, the model must be able to generate texts that are both accurate and fluent, but the two goals are not necessarily compatible.

One approach to controlling the behavior of an unsupervised LM is to collect human preferences for the generated texts, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the human preferences. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. The basic idea is to fit a reward model to the preferences of a human annotator, and then use RL with the reward model as the target to optimize the unsupervized LM. However, RLHF is a difficult problem, as the reward model must be well-calibrated, and RL must be performed with a large number of human preferences. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for RLHF that does not require a reward model, and that can be used with any unsupervised LM.

In this paper, we propose Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), a simple method for RLHF that avoids the need for any reward model. DPO is based on the observation that the reward function that the unsupervised LM is expected to maximize is the same as the reward function that the human annotator is expected to maximize. Therefore, we can simply optimize the unsupervised model for the reward function that the annotator is expected to optimize, and we can use the resulting policy as the fine-tuning policy. We show that D PO can be solved exactly, using a simple classification loss. We evaluate DPO in several settings, including sentiment analysis, summary generation, and single-turn dialogue. Our experiments show that DPO is simple to implement and perform well on a variety of tasks, including sentiment analysis, single-turn dialogue, and summarization.
