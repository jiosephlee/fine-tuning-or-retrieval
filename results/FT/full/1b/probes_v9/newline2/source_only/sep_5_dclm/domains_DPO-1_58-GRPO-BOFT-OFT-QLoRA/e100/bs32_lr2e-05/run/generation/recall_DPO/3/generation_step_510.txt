\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

This is a natural approach, but it is unclear how to optimize the reward model in a way that yields a good policy, and how to fine-tune the policy to maximize the reward estimate.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years, with many methods achieving impressive performance on downstream tasks such as sentiment analysis, summarization and question answering (see \cite{liu2022learning} for a review).
However, these methods are trained entirely unsupervised, and so it is unclear how to control their behavior in a way that is safe and effective.
One approach is to use reinforcement learning (RL) to train a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then use this reward model to fine-tune the language model to maximize the reward estimate, without drifting too far from an initial model.
This approach, known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF, has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LMs on downstream tasks such as summarization and question answering \citep{liu2022reinforcement,liu2023training}.
However, RLHF is often unstable, and requires careful tuning of hyperparameters such as the number of episodes and the discount factor \citep{li2023fine,tian2023training}.
In addition, RLHF is typically performed in a supervised setting, where the reward model is trained on human feedback alongside the LM, and the policy is fine-tuned on the same data.
This approach is computationally expensive, and may not be feasible for large-scale models.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach for fine-tuning LLMs using reinforcement learning, called Direct Preference Optimization.
Our approach is based on the observation that the reward model learned by RLHF can be used to optimize the policy directly, without the need for additional supervision.
We parameterize the reward model as a neural network, and use a simple classification loss to optimize the network parameters.
This leads to a straightforward optimization problem, which we solve using gradient descent.
We evaluate our approach on several benchmarks, including summarization, sentiment analysis, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}

We begin by defining the standard RLHF problem, which we refer to as the \textit{standard RLHF problem}.

\begin{problem}[Standard RLHF]
Given a large unsupervised language model $M$ and a set of human preferences $\mathcal{P}$, find a policy $\pi^*$ that maximizes the reward estimate $R^*$ given by
\begin{equation}
    R^*(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\text{unlabeled}}\left[\underbrace{r_{\pi^*}(L)}_{\text{reward estimate}}\middle| \text{unlabeled data}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is a neural network parameterized by weights $\theta$, and $r_{\pi^*(x)}$ is the reward assigned by the policy $\pi^*$ to the input $x$.
\end{problem}

The standard RLHF problem is a standard optimization problem, and so it can be solved using gradient descent.
However, solving the standard RLHF problem is non-trivial, and there are several ways to simplify the problem.
One approach is to assume that the reward model $r_{\pi*(x)}$ is a fixed function of the input $x$, and then use this to simplify the objective function.
Another approach is to assume that $r_{\pi*}(x)$ is a linear function of the input $w^* \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and then use this assumption to reduce the dimensionality of the parameter space.
A third approach is to assume that there exists a latent variable $z$ such that $r_{\text{RLHF}}(x, z) = r_{\pi^*(z)}(x)$, and then use this to reduce the dimensionality further.
The reward model learned by RL is often assumed to be a linear function of the latent variable $z$, and so we will follow this approach in this paper.
