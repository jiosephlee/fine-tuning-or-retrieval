\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Related Work} Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has been a major focus of recent work in natural language processing (NLP).  While these models have achieved impressive performance on a variety of tasks, they are trained without supervision, and thus lack any explicit knowledge of the world.  As a result, it is unclear how to effectively control the behavior of these models, and existing methods for gaining steerability typically rely on reinforcement learning from human feedback.  Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLLHF) is a popular approach for training LMs to perform specific tasks, but it is often difficult to train an effective reward model from human preferences.  For example, it is difficult to accurately estimate the reward for a given generation of a language model, because the reward depends on many factors that are not captured by the human preferences.  Moreover, RLLHF is often unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter tuning to achieve good performance.  In this paper, we introduce a new method for training LMs that does not require any sampling or hyperparameter tuning, and instead uses a simple classification loss on the reward model to optimize the reward model to maximize human preferences.  We call this method Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

\paragraph{Reward Models} A key challenge in RLLHF is the construction of a reward model that reflects human preferences.  Existing methods for constructing reward models include supervised learning from human feedback, reinforcement learning from human feedback, and reinforcement learning from human preferences.  Supervised learning from human feedback involves collecting a dataset of human preferences and LMs, and then training a reward model using a supervised learning algorithm such as gradient descent.  Reinforcement learning with human feedback involves training an RL agent to maximize a reward function that is defined using the preferences of a human teacher.  Reinforcement learning using human preferences involves training an RL agent using a reward function that is based on the preferences of a human learner.  In this paper we use reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF), which is a popular approach for constructing reward models in RLLHF.  RLHF is similar to RL with human feedback, except that the reward function is learned from human preferences rather than directly from the preferences of a human trainer.  The reward function is used to train an RL agent to maximize the reward, and the agent's policy is used to generate samples from the LM.  The RL agent is typically trained using reinforcement learning from human feedback.

\paragraph{Stability of RLHF} One of the challenges in training an effective reward model using RLHF is the stability of the resulting policy.  In particular, the reward model is often unstable, and the resulting policy may drift away from the original model.  This instability can be problematic, because it can make it difficult to control the behavior of the LM.  In this paper our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for training an effective reward model, and then use this reward model to train a policy that controls the behavior of the LM.
