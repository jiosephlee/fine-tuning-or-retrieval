\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a language model (LM) to align with human preferences. The key insight is that the reward model used in RLHF can be reparameterized as a policy gradient loss, and that the optimal policy can be found in closed form. We demonstrate that this approach is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and that it can be used to fine-tune LMs with a wide range of architectures and sizes. Our experiments show that DPO outperforms existing methods for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, including PPO-based RLHF, and that it can achieve comparable performance to PPO-based RLHF while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation (Mikolov et al., 2013; Devlin et al., 2019), question answering (Devlin et al., 2020), and summarization (Chung et al., 2021). However, the completely unsupervised training regime of LMs makes it difficult to control their behavior, especially when it comes to fine-tuning them to align with human preferences.

One approach to fine-tuning LMs is reinforcement learning from human feedback, where a human annotator provides feedback on the quality of model generations, and the model is then fine-tuned to maximize the human preference score (Bai et al., 2018; Li et al., 2017; Li et al.,

This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the quality of LMs, but it is also complex and often unstable, requiring the human annotator to provide feedback on multiple generations of model output before the final model is selected. Furthermore, the human annotator must be able to provide a high-quality preference score for each generation, which is often not the case.

A second approach to fine-tuning an LM is to use a reward model to estimate the human preference score for each generation of model output, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward (Li et al., 2016; Li et al.,2018; Li et


al., 2019). This approach is computationally efficient, since it does not require the human annotator to label multiple generations of model output. However, the reward model used in this approach is typically a neural network that is trained to predict the human preference score for a given model generation. This means that the reward model must be carefully tuned to reflect the human preferences, which is often a difficult task. Furthermore, the reward model must be retrained for each new generation of model output, which can be computationally expensive.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach to fine-tuning a LM to align with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the observation that the reward model used for RLHF can be reparametrized as a policy gradient objective, and that the optimal reward policy can be found in a closed form. We demonstrate in this paper that this observation is indeed true, and that the optimal DPO policy can be computed in closed form. We also show that the DPO policy is stable, meaning that it converges to the optimal policy in the absence of any additional regularization. We evaluate DPO on a range of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and show that it can be used effectively to align LMs with human preferences. In particular, we show that DPO can be used to improve the quality of model generations in sentiment analysis, summarization and single-turn dialog, and that it can match or exceed the performance of existing methods for fine-tuned LMs.
