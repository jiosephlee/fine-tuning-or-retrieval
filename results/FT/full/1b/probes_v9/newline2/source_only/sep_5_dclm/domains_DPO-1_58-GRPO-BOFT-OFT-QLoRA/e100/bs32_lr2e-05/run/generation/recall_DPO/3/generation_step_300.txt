\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the human preference score of a given generation.  In contrast to existing methods, DPO does not require any sampling from the LM during training, nor does it require any hyperparameter tuning.  Instead, DPO uses a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model, and then fine-tunes the reward model to maximize the human preference scores of the generated samples.  This is done without any sampling from the LM, and without any tuning of the hyperparameters of the reward model.  The resulting policy is a simple linear combination of the reward model and the original LM, and is thus easy to implement and train.

We evaluate DPO on two benchmarks: the OpenAI GPT-3 175B and 355B models, and the BERT-base model.  We find that DPO is able to align the sentiment of generations produced by GPT-3 355B with human preferences, and that it is able to produce high-quality responses in summarization and single turn dialogue.  We also compare DPO to existing methods for RLHF, including PPO, REINFORCE, and RLHF with a custom reward model.  Our results show that DPO is able produce high-quality responses in both summarization and single-turn dialog, and that it is more stable and easier to train than existing methods.

\section{Related Work}

Large unsupervised language models have been shown to learn rich representations of the world and some reasoning skills, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training data.  In this section, we review existing methods for gaining steerability, and highlight the challenges they face.

\textbf{Reward Models}.  Reward models are a class of methods for learning a reward function from human feedback, which can then be used to train a policy that maximizes the reward.  These methods typically use reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the reward model is fit to a dataset of human preferences for a given task, and the policy is then fine-tuned to maximize the reward.  The most common approach is to use a policy gradient method, such as PPO, to optimize the reward model.  However, this requires sampling from the LM, which can be computationally expensive and can lead to unstable training.  Another approach is to use a reward model that is trained with reinforcement learning from human preferences, but then fine-tuned with reinforcement learning from human demonstrations.  This is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback with reinforcement learning from demonstrations (RLHF).  While RLHF has been shown to be effective, it is often unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter tuning.  In addition, RLHF is typically performed on a small dataset of human preferences, which limits its generalizability.

\textbf{Direct Preference Optimization}.  DPO is a new method for learning a reward model from human preferences, and then using this reward model to train a policy that aligns with the preferences.  DPO differs from existing methods in that it does not require any sampling of the LM during training, and does not require any tuning of the hyperparameter of the reward model. Instead, DPO uses the reward model to optimize the original LM, and then fine-tune the reward model to maximize human preferences.  This is done with a simple classification loss, which is easy to implement and train.  We evaluate DPO on two benchmark datasets: the OpenAI Gpt-3 355B and 175B models, and the 12B BERT-base model. Our results show that D PO is able to align the sentiments of generations produced by Gpt-3 175B with human preferences, as well as or better that existing methods.  In addition, DPO is able to produce high quality responses in summarization and dialogue, and is more stable and easier for training than existing methods.

\textbf{Sentiment Alignment}.  Sentiment alignment methods aim to align the sentiment of a given generation with the sentiment of a human reference.  These methods typically take the form of a generative adversarial network (GAN), where the generator is trained to produce samples that are indistinguishable from the human reference, and the discriminator is trained to distinguish between the human reference and the generated samples.  The most common GAN-based methods for sentiment alignment are GPT-2 and GPT-3, which are pretrained on large datasets of human-written text.  These methods have been shown to be effective in aligning the sentiment of generations produced from GPT-3 with human preferences, but they are limited by the size of the training data. In addition, GPT-3 and GPT-2 are pretrained on text written by humans, and so they do not generalize well to text written by non-human authors.  In contrast, DPO is able align the sentiment of generations generated by GPT-3 with the sentiment of human references, and is able to produce highquality responses in summarization and dialog.
