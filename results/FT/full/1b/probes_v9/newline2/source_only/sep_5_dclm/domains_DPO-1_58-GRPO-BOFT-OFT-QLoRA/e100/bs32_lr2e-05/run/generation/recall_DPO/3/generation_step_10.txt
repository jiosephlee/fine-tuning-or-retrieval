\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

However, the lack of supervision in unsupervised LM training makes it difficult to control the behavior of the resulting models, especially when they are used in downstream tasks. \section{Related Work}

\subsection{Unsupervised Language Modeling}

Unsupervised language modeling is a popular research topic in NLP, with many successful applications such as machine translation, summarization, and question answering. The most popular approach to unsupervised LM training is to use a large, unsupervised language model (LM) to generate text, and then use a downstream task to evaluate the quality of the generated text. This approach is known as supervised LM training, and is widely used in NLP tasks such as machine translation and question answering. However, supervised LM training is not suitable for downstream tasks that require precise control of the behavior of the generated text, such as sentiment analysis and response generation. In this paper, our goal is to control the behavior of unsupervized LMs in closed form, without requiring any supervision or hyperparameter tuning.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning}

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a popular approach to solving sequential decision-making problems, where an agent interacts with its environment and learns to maximize a reward function. RL is often used in NLP tasks, such as machine translation and dialogue generation, where the agent interacts with a human user and learns to produce high-quality responses. However, RL is a complex and often difficult-to-implement method, and it is often difficult to control the behavior and performance of an RL agent. In this paper, however, we propose a simple and computationally-lightweight variant of RL, called Direct Preference Optimization, which can be used to control an unsupervised LM's behavior in closed form, without any need for sampling from the model or performing hyperparameter tuning.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}

Direct Preference Optimization (or DPO) is a simple variant of reinforcement learning that can be used to solve the standard RL problem with only a simple loss function. The basic idea of DPO is to use a reward model to estimate the quality of a generation of text. The reward model is a simple classification loss that takes the text as input and outputs a score between 0 and 1, indicating the quality of the generation. The goal of DPO is to find a policy that maximizes the reward function, which is equivalent to finding the optimal policy in the standard RL problem. The resulting algorithm, called Direct Preference Optimization or DPO, is a simple and computable variant of RL that can be used to fine tune an unsupervised LM to match human preferences.
