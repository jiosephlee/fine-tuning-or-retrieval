The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the alignment between a language model and human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is an iterative optimization algorithm that uses a simple yet effective loss function. The algorithm is based on the observation that the alignment between a language and human preferences can be viewed as a binary classification problem. The algorithm iteratively refines the alignment by maximizing the likelihood of the language model given the preferences, while minimizing the likelihood of the preferences given the language model. The algorithm is simple to implement and has a low computational cost. We evaluate DPO on two datasets: the Amazon Mechanical Turk dataset and the Open Language Modeling dataset. The results show that DPO outperforms the state-of-the-art algorithms on both datasets. In particular, DPO achieves a relative improvement of 3.5 percentage points on the Amazon Mechanical Turk dataset, and a relative improvement of 2.5 percentage points on Open Language Modeling. We also conduct a comprehensive ablation study to investigate the contributions of each component of DPO. The results show that the iterative refinement step is crucial for the performance of DPO. We believe that DPO will be a valuable tool for the community in the future.
