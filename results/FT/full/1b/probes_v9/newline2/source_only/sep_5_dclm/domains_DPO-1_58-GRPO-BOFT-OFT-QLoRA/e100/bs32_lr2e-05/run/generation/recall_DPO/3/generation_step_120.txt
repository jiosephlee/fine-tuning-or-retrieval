\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a large unsupervised language model (LM) to align with human preferences. The key idea is to use a reward model to estimate the quality of model generations, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward. This is similar to the approach used in reinforcement learning from human feedback, but instead of using human feedback to learn a reward function, we directly optimize the reward function to match the human preferences. We show that this approach is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and can be used to fine-tune LMs with a simple classification loss.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years. In particular, large-scale LMs have been shown to learn rich representations of the world and some reasoning skills, such as commonsense reasoning and question answering (see, e.g., \cite{liu2019learning,liu2020learning,liu2021learning,liu2022learning,liu2023learning}). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training data. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{liu2022learning}, where the human provides feedback on the quality of model generations and the model is fine-tuned to maximize the human feedback; and reinforcement learning from human preference data \citep{li2022direct,li2023direct}, where the human provides preference data for model generations and the model learns to maximize the human preference data. However, both of these methods are complex and often unstable, requiring the human to provide feedback on multiple generations of model generations, and the model to be fine-tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs to align with the human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}

We begin by introducing the problem of fine-tuning an unsupervised LM to match human preferences. We then describe the DPO algorithm, which is a simple reinforcement learning algorithm for fine-tuning the LM to match the human preferences.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning an Unsupervised LM to Match Human Preferences}

Given a large unsupervised LM $M$ and a set of human preferences $\mathcal{P}$, the goal of fine-tuning $M$ to match $\mathcal{P}$
is to find a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the human preference data $\mathcal{P}\sim\mathcal{P}$. Formally, we want to find a policy $\hat{\pi}$ that maximizes the following objective:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}^{\hat{\pi}} = \arg\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\mathcal{P}] \label{eq:policy_objective}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{P}_i$ is the preference data for the $i$-th generation of the LM, and $\mathbb{E}_{i}[\cdot]$ is the expectation over the $i$-st generation of the LM. The policy $\hat{\pi}(\cdot)$ is a probability distribution over the possible model generations, and maximizing the expected preference data is equivalent to maximizing the probability of the most preferred model generation.

To solve this problem, we can use reinforcement learning from human feedback to find a policy $\tilde{\pi}$ that maximally matches the human preferences. Specifically, we can use a policy gradient algorithm to find a policy $\widetilde{\pi}$ that maximized the following objective:

\[
    \mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\pi}) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[\mathcal{P}^\widetilde{\pi}] - \mathbb{E_\pi[\mathcal{R}]} \label{eq:reinforcement_learning_objective}
\]

where $\mathcal{R} = \mathbb{P}[\mathcal{R}]$ is a reward function that measures the quality of the model generation, and $\mathcal{P}_{\widetilde{\pi}}$ is the preference data generated by the policy $\widetilde{ \pi}$. The policy $\widetilde \pi$ is then used to find a policy $\mathcal{P}'$ that maximizes the reward function $\mathcal{R}$.
