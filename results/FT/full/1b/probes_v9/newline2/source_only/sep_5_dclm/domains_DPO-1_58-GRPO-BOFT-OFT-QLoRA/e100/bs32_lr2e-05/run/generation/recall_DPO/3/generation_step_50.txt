\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to language modeling, with recent advances in the field including BERT [1], GPT-3 [2], and OpenAI's GPT-4 [3]. These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and have demonstrated impressive performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation [4], question answering [5], and summarization [6]. However, the lack of supervision makes it difficult to control the behavior of these models, and existing methods for steering LMs have been shown to be unstable and unreliable [7].

One approach to steering LMs is reinforcement learning from human feedback, where humans provide feedback on the quality of model generations [8, 9]. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the quality of LMs [10, 11], but is often unstable and requires significant hyperparameter tuning [12]. Another approach is to use a reward model to estimate the quality of model generations, and then fine-tune the unsuitable LMs using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward [13, 14]. This approach has been used to improve the quality of LMs in several tasks, including machine translation [15], question answering [16], and summarization [17]. However, the reward model is often difficult to construct, and the fine-tuning process is often unstable and requires extensive hyperparameter tuning [18, 19].

In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables us to solve the standard reward estimation problem in RLHF with only a simple classification task. The resulting algorithm, which we refer to as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is simple, stable, and computationally lightweight. We show that DPOS can be used to fine-tune LMs with high-quality human feedback, and outperforms state-of-the-art methods in several tasks. In particular, we show that DPO can achieve comparable or better performance than PPO-based RLHF, while requiring only a simple classification loss and no hyperparameter tuning. Our experiments show that DPO is also stable, performant, computationally lightweight, and more robust to noise in the human feedback than existing methods.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback}

RLHF has been used to improve LMs in several tasks [8, 9, 16, 17, 20, 21]. In these methods, humans provide feedback on the relative quality of model generation, and the model is then fine-tuned to maximize the reward. The reward model is often constructed using a neural network that maps the quality of model generations to a scalar reward value, and the fine-tuned model is then used to fine tune the unsuited LM. However, the reward model can be difficult to construct, and often requires extensive hyperparameter tuning to achieve stable performance [18, 19]. In addition, the fine-tuning process can be unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter optimization to achieve high-quality results [12, 22].

\subsection{Reward Estimation in RLHF}

In RLHF, the reward model is used to estimate the quality of a model generation, and the unsuited LMS is then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize a reward estimate. The reward model is typically constructed using a neural network, and the fine-tune process is often unstable and difficult to optimize [23, 24]. In addition, the reward model is usually constructed using a single scalar reward value, which can be difficult to interpret [25]. In this paper, we introduce an alternative reward model that is easier to construct, and can be used to solve the standard RL problem with only a simple binary classification task.

\subsection{Direct Preference Optimization}

Direct Preference Optimization (or DPO) is a simple method for solving the standard RL problem with a binary classification task. In DPO, the reward model is constructed using a neural network with two inputs: a reward value and a preference score. The reward value is the reward estimate from the reward model, and the preference score is a binary label indicating whether the reward value is higher or lower than a threshold. The reward model is then used to estimate the quality score of a model generation, which is used to fine-tune an unsuited LMS using reinforcement learning. The resulting policy is used to fine-tuning the unsuited LLM, without the need for hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the unsuited LMM.
