\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the human preference function, and then fine-tuned to maximize the reward estimate without drifting too far from its original value.
This is a natural approach, but it is not obvious how to do it in practice. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that enables us to solve the standard reward maximization problem in closed form, allowing for a straightforward solution to the standard RLHF problem.
We call this new parameterization the \textit{direct preference parameterization} (DPP), and our main contribution is a new algorithm, \textit{Direct Preferred Optimization} (DPO). DPO is a simple and effective method for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, and we evaluate it on a range of tasks including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has become a central focus of research in natural language processing (NLP) in recent years. Large pretrained LMs have been shown to be effective at many downstream tasks, including machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, these models are trained on unstructured text, and thus lack any explicit knowledge of the world. This makes it difficult to use them to reason about the world, or to control their behavior in a precise way. 

One approach to addressing this problem is to fine-tune pretrained LMs with human feedback. This is the approach taken by many recent works, including \citep{liu2022learning,liu2023training}, which we refer to as \textit{RLHF}. In RLHF, a human annotates a set of pairs of model generations and human preferences, and then uses reinforcement learning to optimize the model to maximize the human preference score. The resulting model is then fine-tuned on downstream tasks to improve performance. 

While RLHF is a powerful approach, it is also quite complex. First, the human annotations must be carefully curated, and the reward function must be carefully tuned to avoid overfitting to the training data. Second, the RLHF algorithm itself is a complex optimization problem, and it is often unclear how to solve it exactly. Finally, the RLHF algorithm must be run multiple times, with different hyperparameters, to ensure that the resulting model is stable and generalizable. 

In this paper, we introduce a new approach to fine-tuning LMs with human feedback, called \textit{Direct Prefer-ence Optimization} (DPO).
DPO is based on a simple idea: instead of optimizing the reward function to maximize the human preference, we can optimize the reward function to maximize a proxy of the human preference, and then fine-tune the model to maximize the proxy without drifting too far from it. This is a natural approach, since the human preference function is often very difficult to compute, and it is not clear how to optimize it exactly. However, we show that the reward function can be optimized in closed form, and that the resulting optimization problem is equivalent to the standard RLHF optimization problem. We call this new parameterization \textit{the direct preference parameterization} (or DPP for short).

We now describe the details of DPO. Given a pretrained LM $M$ and a set of human preferences $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n\}$, DPO optimizes the reward function $R(\theta)$ to maximize the expected human preference score:

\begin{equation}
    \max_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P} \big[ r(x, M(\theta)) \big],
\end{equation}

where $r(x, y)$ is the reward function given by the reward model $R(\theta)$, and $\mathbb{E}_{\text{unlabeled}}$ denotes expectation over unlabeled data. The reward function $R(\cdot)$ is typically a neural network, and the parameters $\theta$ are typically learned from the human preferences. Once the reward function is optimized, the resulting model is fine-tuned on downstream data to improve performance on the target task.
