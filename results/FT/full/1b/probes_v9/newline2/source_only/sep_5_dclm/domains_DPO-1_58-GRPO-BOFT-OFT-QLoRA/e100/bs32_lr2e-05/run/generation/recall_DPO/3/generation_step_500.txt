\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the human preference function, and then fine-tuned to maximize the reward estimate without drifting too far from its original value.
This is a natural approach, but it is unclear how to optimize the reward model in a way that yields a good policy, and how to fine-tune the policy to maximize the reward estimate.
In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that enables us to solve the standard reward maximization problem in closed form, and use this to develop a new method for fine-tuning LMs, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
We evaluate DPO on several benchmarks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, showing that DPO can be used to fine-tune LMs with high-quality responses, and that it is competitive with existing methods.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years, with many methods achieving impressive performance on downstream tasks such as sentiment analysis, summarization and question answering (see \cite{liu2022learning} for a review).
However, these methods are trained entirely unsupervised, and so it is unclear how to control their behavior in a way that is safe and effective.
One approach is to use reinforcement learning (RL) to train a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then use this to fine-tune the unsatisfied LM to maximize the reward estimate, without drifting too far from it.
This is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF (see \cite{hulora2023} for a survey).
There are two main approaches to RLHF: sampling from the LM and optimizing the reward model using reinforcement learning, and optimizing the reward model directly using reinforcement learning.
Sampling from the LM is computationally expensive, and so in practice it is often used in combination with reinforcement learning from human preferences, where the reward model is first fit using reinforcement learning, and then fine-tunable using reinforcement learning from human preferences.
This is known as PPO-based RLHF (see \citet{liu2022reinforcement} for a review).

However, RLHF is often unstable, and so it is important to tune the reward model carefully to avoid overfitting.
In particular, the reward model should be able to reflect the preferences of the human annotators, but it should also be robust to small changes in the data distribution.
To ensure stability, the reward model is often initialized with a pretrained reward function, and then fine tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback.
This is known as pretraining-based RLHF (see e.g. \cite{liu2019pretraining,liu2020learning,liu2021pretraining} for surveys).

Another approach to RLHF is to optimize the reward model directly using RL, without initializing it with a pretrained reward function.
This is known as reward maximization-based RLHF (see for example \cite{liu_2022_reinforcement,liu2022learning,liu2023fine,tian2023training} for surveys).
Reward maximization-based RLHF is often referred to as DPO in the literature, and we will use this name throughout this paper.

While DPO is a natural approach to RLHF, it is unclear how to solve the standard reward optimization problem in a way that yields an optimal policy.
In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for solving the standard reward maximization (RPM) problem in DPO, and to evaluate its performance on several benchmarks.

\begin{equation}
    \max_{\pi} R(\pi(d_1), \pi(d_2), \ldots, \pi(d_n))
\end{equation}
