Introduction
The field of natural language understanding (NLU) has made significant progress in recent years, thanks to the development of large language models (LMs) trained on massive amounts of text data. However, LMs are not perfect, and they often produce output that is not aligned with human preferences. For example, LMs often produce output that is too long or too short, or that contains errors such as typos or grammatical mistakes. This is a serious problem, because it can lead to misunderstandings and miscommunication in real-world applications.
One way to address this problem is to use human feedback to train LMs. This approach is known as supervised learning, and it involves collecting a dataset of human preferences for LMs to learn from. However, collecting such a dataset can be time-consuming and expensive, and it may not always be possible to collect a sufficient number of human preferences.
Another approach is to use unsupervised learning to train LMs without human feedback. This approach is known to be more efficient than supervised learning, but it can still be difficult to train LMs without supervision. One way to address this problem, is to use reinforcement learning (RL), which is a type of machine learning that involves training an agent to perform a task by interacting with an environment. RL has been successfully applied to various NLU tasks, such as machine translation (Hutter et al., 2005;Klein et al., 2016), question answering (Sutskever et al., 2014), and summarization (Liu et al., 2018).
However, RL has not been widely used for training LMs, due to the difficulty of designing effective reward functions. In particular, it is challenging to design a reward function that accurately reflects human preferences for LMs. This is because humans are often inconsistent in their preferences, and there is no single measure that can capture all aspects of human preferences.
In this paper, we present a new approach for training LMs with reinforcement learning, called Direct Preference Optimization. The core idea behind DPO is to directly optimize the alignment between a language models and human preferences, rather than optimizing a separate reward function. This approach is based on the observation that humans often have a direct preference for certain types of output, such as length, grammar, and accuracy. Therefore, we can use these preferences as a proxy for human preferences, and use them to train the language model.
To implement DPO, we first need to define a loss function that measures the difference between the preferences of a human and the preferences of a language model. We then need to find an optimal policy for the language model, which takes the preferences of a human as input and outputs the most likely sequence of words. Finally, we need to optimize the policy using reinforcement learning, to minimize the loss function.
We evaluate DPO on a variety tasks, including machine translation, question answering, and summarization. Our results show that DPO can achieve competitive performance on these tasks, without requiring any additional human annotations. Moreover, DPO is able to generalize well to unseen tasks, indicating that it is a robust and flexible approach for training LMs without supervision.
