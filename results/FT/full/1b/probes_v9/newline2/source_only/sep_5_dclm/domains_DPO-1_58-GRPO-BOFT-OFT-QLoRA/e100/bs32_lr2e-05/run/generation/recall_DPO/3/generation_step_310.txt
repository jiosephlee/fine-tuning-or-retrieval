\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1]), question answering (e.g., BQAS [2]), and machine translation (e.g., GPT-3 [3]). However, the completely unsupervised training of LMs raises several challenges for their use in practice. First, the lack of supervision makes it difficult to understand how LMs learn, and to interpret their behavior in terms of the knowledge they have acquired. Second, the lack of supervision also makes it difficult to control the behavior of LMs, which is crucial for applications such as text generation, where the goal is to generate text that is aligned with human preferences. Third, the lack of supervision means that LMs are not evaluated on tasks that are relevant to their intended use, such as summarization or dialogue, and so it is difficult to know whether they are performing well on these tasks.

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various methods for gaining steerability of LMs. One approach is to collect human feedback on the relative quality of model outputs, and then fine-tune the LMs to maximize the human preferences. This approach, known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF, has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LMs on downstream tasks [4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. However, RLHF is a challenging and often unstable procedure, because it requires collecting a large number of human preferences, and because the reward function used to optimize the LMs is typically unknown. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on a simple idea: instead of using a reward function to optimize the LMs, we can directly optimize the LMs using a policy gradient algorithm, such as policy gradient (PG) [9] or value iteration (VI) [10]. This approach is appealing because it avoids the need to estimate a reward function, which is often difficult to do accurately. Moreover, it is straightforward to implement and train, and it does not require sampling from the LMs during training, which can be computationally expensive.

We parameterize the reward function in DPO using a neural network, and show that the resulting reward function can be easily optimized using a simple binary classification loss, which is computationally efficient and easy to implement. We refer to this approach as \textit{Direct Policy Optimization} (DPO). Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and can be used to fine-tune LMs with high-quality results. In particular, we show that DPO can be used to align the sentiment of generations of LMs with human preferences, and that it can be used for reinforcement learning in the context of summarization and single-turn dialog. Our experiments indicate that DPO can be competitive with existing methods for fine-tuning LLMs, and that it can also be used for reinforcement learning, providing a simple and effective solution to the steerability problem.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervized language models have been trained on massive datasets of unannotated text, and have demonstrated impressive performance on a wide variety of tasks. These models are typically trained using a combination of unsupervised pretraining and supervised fine-tuning. Pretraining is typically done using a large-scale text dataset, such as the OpenAI GPT-3 corpus [3], and fine-tuning is typically done using a small dataset of human-written text, such as the CoNLL-2003 shared task [11] or the Common Crawl [12].
