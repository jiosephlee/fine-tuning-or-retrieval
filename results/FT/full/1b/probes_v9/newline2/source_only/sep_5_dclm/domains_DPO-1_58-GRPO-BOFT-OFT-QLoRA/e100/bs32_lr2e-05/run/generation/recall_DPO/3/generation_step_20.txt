\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building general-purpose language models (LMs). These models are trained on large corpora of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language-specific knowledge, including word embeddings, syntactic structure, and semantic relations. However, the completely unsupervised training process makes it difficult to control the behavior of the resulting LMs, and they often exhibit undesirable properties such as overfitting, poor generalization, and poor performance on downstream tasks.

One way to address this problem is to use reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune the LMs to align with the human preferences of the downstream task. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. In RLHF, the human annotator provides feedback on the quality of model generations, and the model is then fine-tuned to maximize the human feedback. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LMs on downstream tasks, but it is also computationally expensive, and often requires significant hyperparameter tuning to achieve good performance.

In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized version of the reward model in the RLHF framework, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is a simple classification loss that can be used to extract the optimal policy in closed form. We show that D PO can be used to fine-tune LMs without the need for sampling or hyperparameter tuning, and we demonstrate that it can outperform existing methods in several tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

In the RLHF framework, the reward function is used to estimate the quality of model generations. In DPO, we use a simple classification loss to parameterize the reward function. Specifically, we define the reward function as follows:

\begin{equation}
R(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \log \hat{y}_i
\end{equation}

where $\theta$ is the parameterized reward function, $\hat{y}_i$ is the predicted label for the i-th generation, and $N$ is the number of generations. The goal of DPO is to find the parameterized reward function that maximizes the reward function.
