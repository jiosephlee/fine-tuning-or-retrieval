Introduction
Language models are widely used in natural language processing (NLP) to generate text. They are trained on large amounts of text data and can be used to generate text that is similar to the training data. However, language models are not perfect and often produce text that is not aligned with human preferences. For example, a language model may generate text that is too long or too short, or it may generate text that is not grammatical. This can lead to problems such as text that is difficult to read or understand.
One way to address this problem is to use human preferences to train language models. This is called supervised learning, and it involves training a language model with human preferences. The goal of supervised learning is to find a language model that is as similar as possible to the training data while also being as similar as possible to human preferences.
There are many ways to measure the similarity of a language model to human preferences. One common measure is the perplexity of the language model. Perplexity is a measure of how difficult it is to understand the language model's output. A lower perplexity indicates that the language model is easier to understand. Another common measure is the average sentence length of the language model. A shorter average sentence length indicates that the language model produces text that is easier to read.
However, these measures of similarity are not always ideal. For example, a low perplexity may indicate that the language model is poorly trained, while a low average sentence length may indicate that the language models is not well suited for summarization. Therefore, it is important to find a measure of similarity that is more meaningful and useful.
In this paper, we propose a new method for aligning language model with human preferences, called Direct Preferences Optimization (DPO). The core idea of DPO is to optimize the alignment between a language and human preferences by directly optimizing the language model's preferences. This is in contrast to previous methods that optimize the language model's perplexity or average sentence length.
DPO is based on the observation that language models are often trained on large amounts of data that are not aligned with human preferences, and that this can lead to poor alignment between the language model and human preferences. Therefore, DPO seeks to improve the alignment between the language model's preferences and human preferences by directly adjusting the language model's preferences.
To achieve this goal, DPO uses a simple but effective loss function that measures the difference between the language model's preference and human preference. This loss function encourages the language model to produce text that is similar to human preferences, while still allowing some deviation from human preferences. This allows DPO to strike a balance between alignment and flexibility, which is important for improving the alignment between language models and human preferences.
We evaluate DPO on two different tasks: predicting the preference of a single human annotator and predicting the preference of an unknown user. On both tasks DPO outperforms the state-of-the-art, including baselines from previous work.
