\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building general-purpose language models (LMs). These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language-specific knowledge, including world knowledge, reasoning skills, and even some rudimentary human-like behavior. However, obtaining precise control over the behavior of these models is difficult, because they are trained in an unsupervised manner, and their behavior is largely determined by the training data. This makes it difficult to fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences, and to control their behavior in a precise manner. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and computationally-efficient method for fine-tuning LMs to align with the preferences of humans, without requiring any human feedback or reinforcement learning (RL). To this end, we propose a new method for learning a reward model from human feedback, and then fine-tune the unsuupervised LM to maximize the reward. This method is called \textit{Direct Preferenc Optimization} (DPO).

The standard approach to fine-tuning LMs is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the reward model learns to predict the relative quality of model generation, and the unsupervised LMs are fine-tuned to maximize this reward. This approach is known as \textit{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback} (RLHF). However, RLHF is a complicated and often unstable procedure, because it requires sampling the LM to obtain the rewards, which is computationally costly and can lead to unstable behavior. In this paper, instead of sampling from the LM, we propose a new approach to learning the reward model, which we call \emph{Direct Preference Optimization} (\textit{DPO}). DPO is a straightforward extension of RLHF, where the reward model and the unsupervised model are learned simultaneously, and the unsupervized LM is fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. The resulting algorithm is stable, performant (in terms of training speed and stability), and computationally efficient, and eliminates the need to sample from the LM during fine tuning or hyperparameter tuning. We refer to this algorithm as \textit{DPO}.

We evaluate DPO on a variety of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our experiments show that, compared to existing methods, DPO is able to align with human preferences and control the behavior of LMs with human-like precision, while being substantially simpler to train and implement. In particular, DPOS is able to fine-tuning LLMs to align well with human preferences as well, or better, than existing methods. Moreover, DPO is more stable and performent than PPO-based RLFHFs, and is able to achieve higher quality responses than PPO-based RLFs. We also show that, compared to PPO-based RLFHMs, DPO is able achieve higher quality responses in single-turn dialogue, and is more stable than PPOS-based RLFHMs. Finally, we show that DPO is also able to achieve higher quality results in sentiment analysis, and is more stable and performenent than PPO-based LMs. Overall, our experiments show that DPO provides a simple and effective way to align LMs with human preferences, and control their behavior with human-like precision.
