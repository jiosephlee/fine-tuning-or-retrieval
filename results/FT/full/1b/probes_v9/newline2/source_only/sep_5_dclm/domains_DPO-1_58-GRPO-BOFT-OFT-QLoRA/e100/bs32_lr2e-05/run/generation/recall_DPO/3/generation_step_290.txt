\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a language model (LM) to align with human preferences. The key insight is that the reward model used in RLHF can be reparameterized as a policy gradient loss, and that the optimal policy can be computed in closed form. We demonstrate that this approach leads to a simple and effective method for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, and that it can be applied to a wide range of tasks.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years, with the advent of generative pre-trained language models (GPT-N)~\citep{radford2021language} and the BERT family~\citep{devlin-etal2019-bert}. These models have demonstrated impressive performance on a variety of downstream tasks, but are largely unsupervised, relying on a large amount of unlabeled text data to learn a good representation. While GPT-N and BERT are trained on large corpora of English text, they are not designed to be used in a conversational setting, and so are not suitable for applications such as summarization and dialogue generation. To address this, a number of works have explored using GPT-N and BART~\citep{leibo2022bart} as the base model for language models that can be used in conversational settings, such as summarization and single-turn dialogues. These models are typically fine-tuned on a dataset of human-written responses to prompts from a given prompt-pair, and are referred to as \textit{human-in-the-loop} models. This approach has been shown to yield high-quality results, but is limited by the availability of such datasets. In this paper, we explore a different approach, referred to as \textbf{direct preference optimization}, which does not require any human annotations.

\textbf{Human-in-the-loop fine-tuning} is a popular approach for improving the performance of unsupervised language models. In this approach, a human annotator is asked to rate the quality of each generated response to a prompt-pair, and the model is then fine-tuned to maximize the average rating of the responses. This approach has been successfully applied to a wide range tasks, including summarization~\citep{liu2022learning,liu2023training}, dialogue generation~\citep{wang2023training}, and question answering~\citep{zhang2023training}. However, the annotation effort required for this approach is substantial, and the resulting models are often not as flexible as models that have been fine-tuned from scratch. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method that can be used to fine-tune LMs without requiring any human annotations.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}

We begin by introducing the problem of fine-tuning an unsupervised language model to align with human preferences, and then describe the key idea behind our approach, which is to use a reward model to estimate the preferences of the human annotators. We then describe the standard approach to solving this problem, which is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback to optimize the reward model, and then fine-tune the unsuper-vised model using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. Finally, we describe how we can use the reward model to compute the optimal policy, and then present our method for solving the standard RLHF problem.
