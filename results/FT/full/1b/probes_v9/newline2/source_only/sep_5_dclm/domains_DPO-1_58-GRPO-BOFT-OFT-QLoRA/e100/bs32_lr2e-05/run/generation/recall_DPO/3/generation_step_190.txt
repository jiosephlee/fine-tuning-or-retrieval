\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a language model (LM) to align with human preferences. The key idea is to use a reward model to estimate the quality of model generations, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the reward. This is similar to the approach taken in reinforcement learning from human feedback, but instead of using human feedback to train a reward model, we directly optimize the reward model to match the preferences of the human annotators. 

We propose a new parameterization of this reward model that allows us to solve the standard reward maximization problem in closed form, enabling us to solve the standard reinforcement learning problem with only a simple classifier loss. We refer to this algorithm as \textit{Direct preference optimization} (DPO). DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning. We evaluate DPO on a variety of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and show that it can achieve comparable performance to existing methods while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years. In particular, large pretrained language models (PLMs) have achieved impressive performance on a wide range of downstream tasks, including natural language understanding, machine translation, and dialogue. However, these models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled data, making them difficult to control and fine-tune for specific downstream tasks. To address this challenge, researchers have proposed various methods for fine-tuning PLMs to perform specific tasks, such as sentiment analysis, summarization and single-turn dialog. These methods typically involve collecting human feedback on the quality of model generations and then fine-tuning a pretrained PLM using reinforcement learning (RL) to maximize the reward. One popular approach is reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{liu2022learning,liu2023learning}, where the goal is to learn a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then use RL to fine-tune the pretrained PLM to maximize the reward. However, RLHF is a complicated and often unstable procedure, as it requires collecting human feedback on the relative quality of model generation, and then fine-tunning the pretrained PLM using reinforcement to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original model. In this paper, we introduce a new parameterized reward model that enables us to solve the standard problem of RLHF with only a simple classification task. We refer to this method as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

Another related line of work focuses on learning reward functions from human feedback. For example, \cite{liu2022reinforcement} proposes a method for learning a reward function from human feedback, and then fine-tunes a pretrained PLM using RL to maximize the reward. \cite{wang2023learning} proposes a method for automatically learning a reward function from a dataset of human feedback. However, these methods are typically based on reinforcement learning from human feedback and do not provide a direct way to solve the standard RL problem with only a simple reward function. In contrast, our method provides a direct solution to the standard RL problem with a simple reward function.
