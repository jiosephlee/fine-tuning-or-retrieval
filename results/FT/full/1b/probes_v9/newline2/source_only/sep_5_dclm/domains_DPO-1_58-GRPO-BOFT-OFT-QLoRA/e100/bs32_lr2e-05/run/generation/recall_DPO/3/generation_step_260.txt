\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on a wide range of tasks. However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the human provides feedback on the relative quality of model outputs, and then fine-tunes the unsupervised LM using RL to maximize the human feedback \citep{liu2022learning,liu2023training,liu2024fine,tian2023training}. While these methods have shown promising results, they are complex and often unstable, requiring the human to provide feedback on multiple generations of model output before the final model is selected. Moreover, the RL training process requires a large number of human annotations, which are expensive to collect and often not available.

In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on a simple observation: the reward model used in reinforcement learning from human feedback can be reparameterized in a way that allows us to optimize the reward model using standard RL algorithms. Specifically, we propose to use a policy gradient to optimize the reward model, which we refer to as the \textit{direct preference model}. This policy gradient is derived from the reward model by applying a simple transformation that preserves the reward function's shape but changes its value. We then show that the resulting policy gradient can be used to optimize the reward model in closed form, allowing for efficient optimization of the reward model using standard algorithms. We refer to this optimization as \textit{Direct Policy Optimization} (DPO).

We evaluate DPO on a range of tasks, including summarization, sentiment analysis, and single-turn dialogue, and compare it to existing methods for fine-tuning LLMs to align with human preferences, including reinforcement learning from human feedback and reinforcement learning from human feedback with policy gradient optimization. Our experiments show that, compared to existing methods, DPO is more stable, more performant, and easier to implement. In particular, we find that DPO can be trained in a single pass over the data, without requiring any hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the LM. We also find that DPO can achieve comparable or better performance than existing methods on all tasks. Finally, we show that DPO can be used to improve the quality of model responses in single-turn dialogue, and that it can be used to improve sentiment analysis and summarization performance.

\section{Related Work}

LM training has been a major focus of research in recent years, with impressive results on a wide range of downstream tasks. Large-scale unsupervised LM training has been achieved using a variety of approaches, including unsupervised pretraining on large datasets \citep{hulora2017unsupervised,liu2018language,liu2022learning}, self-supervised learning \citep{devlin2018bert,liu2020language}, and reinforcement learning \citep{li2022learning,li2023training,li2024fine}. These methods have led to impressive performance on tasks such as machine translation \citep{devgan2017seq2seq,liu2018seq2seq}, question answering \citep{li2019language,liu2020question}, and summarization \citep{liang2020language,liu2021language,liu2023learning}. However, achieving precise control over the behavior of these models remains a challenge, due to their completely unsophisticated training procedures.

One approach to gaining steerability is reinforcement learning from human feedback. In reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{liao2019reinforcement,liu2022training,liu2023fine}, the human provides feedback on multiple generations of the model's output, and then fine-tune the model using reinforcement learning to maximize the human feedback. This approach has shown promising results, but is complex and often unstable, as the human must provide feedback on multiple generations before selecting the final model. Moreover, the RL process requires a large number human annotations, which are often expensive to collect and often unavailable.
