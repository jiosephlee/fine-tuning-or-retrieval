\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building general-purpose language models (LMs). These models are trained on massive amounts of unlabeled text, and are able to learn a wide range of language-specific knowledge, including world knowledge, reasoning skills, and even some rudimentary forms of human-like language generation. However, obtaining human feedback on the quality of the generated text is difficult, and obtaining human feedback on the performance of the model is even more challenging. This is because the human feedback is typically collected in the form of a binary label indicating whether the generated text is good or bad, and the human feedback is often collected in a noisy and imprecise manner. As a result, obtaining human feedback on LMs is a difficult and expensive task, and obtaining human feedback is often not feasible at all.

To address this problem, researchers have proposed various methods for obtaining human feedback on the generated text. One approach is to collect human feedback on the quality and performance of the generated text, and then fine-tune the unsuper-vised LM to align with the human feedback. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. The idea is that the human feedback can be used to estimate the reward function of the unsupervised LM, and then the unsupervised LM can be fine-tuned to maximize this reward function. This approach has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of LMs, but it is also computationally expensive, and it is often difficult to obtain human feedback on the quality or performance of the generated text. In this paper, our goal is to develop a new method for obtaining human feedback on LLMs that is both computationally efficient and stable, and that can be used to fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences.

To address this problem we propose a new method for obtaining feedback on the quality and/or performance of the generated text that is based on the idea of reward modeling. The basic idea is that the human preferences can be used to construct a reward function that reflects the human preferences. This reward function can then be used to train a reward model that can be used to estimate how good or bad the generated text is. Once the reward model is trained, the unsupervised LM that is being fine-tuned can be fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward function. This approach is known in the literature as reinforcement learning from human preference, or RLHF. However, the standard approach to RLHF is not suitable for solving this problem, because it requires the generation of samples from the unsupervised LM during the fine-tun- ing phase, which is computa- tively expensive and can lead to instability. In this paper, instead of sampling from the unsupervised LMs, we propose a new approach to RLHF that does not require sampling from the unsup- ervised LMs. Instead, we propose a new reward model that can be learned from the human feedback, and we propose a new method to solve the standard RLHf problem using this reward model. We refer to this new method as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
