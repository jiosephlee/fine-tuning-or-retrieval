\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation (Mikolov et al., 2013; Devlin et al., 2019), question answering (Devlin et al., 2020), and summarization (Chen et al., 2021). However, the completely unsupervised training regime of LMs poses a challenge to their control: how can we steer a model to perform a specific task, or even a specific action, without explicitly labeling the model's output? This is particularly important for applications where the model's behavior must be controlled, such as in conversational AI, where the model must be able to respond to user queries in a way that is consistent with the user's intent.

One approach to controlling LMs is reinforcement learning from human feedback, where the model is trained to maximize a reward function that reflects the human preferences for the model's output. This approach has been successfully applied to a wide range tasks, including summarization (Bai et al., 2022), dialogue (Gao et al., 2023), and image captioning (Zhang et al., 2024). In reinforcement learning from human feedback the model is typically fine-tuned on a dataset of human preferences, which is often collected through crowdsourcing or human annotation. This approach is computationally expensive, and requires a large number of human annotators to annotate the preferences of each generation of the model. Furthermore, the human annotations are often noisy, and it is difficult to ensure that the human annotators are consistent in their preferences across generations of the model.

In this paper we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a simple reinforcement learning algorithm for fine-tuning an unsupervised LM to match human preferences. DPO is based on the observation that the reward model used for reinforcement learning from human feedback can be reparameterized in terms of a policy gradient loss. We demonstrate that the optimal policy can in fact be found in closed form, and that the resulting policy can be used to optimize the reward model. We refer to this policy as the \textit{direct preference policy}, and show that it can be used to optimize a reward model that is equivalent to the one used in RLHF. We refer to this approach as \textit{Direct preference optimization} (DPO).

We evaluate DPO on a range of tasks, including sentiment analysis (Santos et al., 2025), summarization (Chen and Manning, 2022), and single-turn dialogue (Gao et 2023). Our experiments show that D PO can be used to fine-tune LMs with high-quality responses to user queries, and that it can be applied to a range of tasks without requiring any additional hyperparameters or tuning. We also compare DPO to existing methods for fine-tuned LMs, including PPO-based reinforcement learning from human feedback and reinforcement learning from human feedback with policy gradient optimization (RLHF). Our experiments show that while DPO is not as effective as RLHF in achieving high-quality responses, it is simpler to implement and train, and is more stable than RLHF. In particular, we find that DPO is more robust to the choice of hyperparameters, and that it converges faster than RLHF. We also find that DPO is able to achieve comparable performance to RLHF, while being substantially simpler to use and train.
