\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple, computationally efficient, and stable variant of reinforcement learning from human feedback. It is a straightforward extension of the standard RLHF method, which uses a reward model to estimate the quality of model generations and then fine-tunes the unsupervised LM using RLHF to maximize this estimated reward. However, the reward model is typically learned from human feedback, which is often unstable and requires significant hyperparameter tuning. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of this reward model that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. We call this new parameterization \textit{Direct Preferences}. The resulting algorithm, which we refer to as \textit{Direct Preferred Optimization} (DPO) is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and eliminates the need for sampling from a large unsupervised LM during fine-tuning. Our experiments show that DPO is able to fine-tune LMs with human preferences as well or better than existing methods, and that it is substantially simpler to implement and easier to train. We also show that DPO is capable of controlling the quality of model generations in sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and that it is able to match or improve the performance of existing methods.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has become a popular approach to building language models that are able to learn broad world knowledge and reasoning skills. However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training process. For example, the model may be trained to generate text that is too similar to the training data, or it may be trained to generate too much text, or it may be able to generate text that is not aligned with the training data. These problems are particularly challenging when the model is trained on unlabeled data, since there is no way to evaluate the quality of its output. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for gaining precise control of the behavior and quality of LMs, without relying on human feedback. We do this by introducing a new parameterization of a reward model that enables extraction and optimization of the optimal policy in the standard RLHF setting. This parameterization is based on the idea that the reward model should reflect the human preferences for model generations, and that the optimal policy should be the one that maximizes the reward. We refer to this parameterization as \textit{direct preferences}. We refer to the resulting algorithm as \textit{DPO}, which stands for \textit{Direct Prefer-ence Optimization}. We show that DPO is stable, performant (in terms of training speed and memory usage), and computationally lightweight, which makes it suitable for use in real-world applications. We also show that it is capable of fine-tuning LMs to align with the human preferences, and that it is capable of controlling the sentiment of model generations in sentiment and summarization tasks, and of matching or improving the performance of existing methods in single-turn dialogue.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback}

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) is a popular approach for gaining precise control of model behavior. RHLF is based on the idea of using human feedback to estimate the quality of a model generation, and then fine-tune the model using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. The reward model is typically learned using a supervised learning approach, where the human feedback is used to train a classifier that predicts the quality of model generations. The resulting reward model is then used to fine-tune the unsu- pervised LM using reinforcement learning. The most popular approach for learning the reward model is to use a neural network to predict the quality of model generations, and then use a neural network to fine-tune the LM using reinforcement learning. However, this approach is often unstable, and requires significant hyperparameter optimization. In this paper, instead of using a neural network to learn the reward model, we use a simple classification loss to learn the reward model. This approach is computationally efficient, and does not require significant hyperparameter tuning. We refer to this approach as \textit{simple RHLF}.

\subsection{Direct Preference Optimization}

Direct Preference Optimization (or DPO) is a simple extension of the standard RLHf method. It is based on the idea, first introduced in \cite{dpo}, that the reward model should be a function of the human preferences for model generation. The reward model is then used as a loss function during RLHF, and the optimal policy is the one that maximizes this loss. We refer to this method as \textit{dpo}. We show that Dpo is stable, performant in terms of training speed and resource usage, and computationally lightweight. We also show that the resulting algorithm is capable of fine-tunning LMs to align with user preferences, and that it can control the quality of model generations.
