\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a simple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm for fine-tuning a language model (LM) to match human preferences. The key insight is that the reward function used in RL can be reparameterized as a policy gradient objective, and that the optimal policy can be found in closed form. We demonstrate that this approach can be applied to a wide range of RLHF problems, including the most challenging ones, such as controlling the sentiment of generations, and improving response quality in summarization, single-turn dialogue, and multi-turn dialogue.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Language Models}. Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years. In particular, GPT-3~\citep{brown2020gpt3} is a 175B parameter language model trained on 175B tokens of open web text. It is widely used in downstream tasks such as summarization~\citep{liu2022gpt3}, question answering~\citep{wang2022training}, and dialogue~\citep{zhang2022learning}. Other large-scale unsupervised LMs include OpenAI's GPT-4~\citep{openai2023gpt4} and BERT~\citep{devlin2018bert}, which are both 1.5B parameter LMs trained on 1.5B tokens of web text. These LMs have demonstrated impressive performance in various downstream tasks, but they are not finetuned on any human feedback.

\textbf{Reward Learning}. Reward learning is a general framework for training models to perform tasks by maximizing a reward function. The most famous example is reinforcement learning (RL), where the reward function is the sum of discounted rewards from all actions in the environment. However, reward learning has also been applied to other settings, such as imitation learning~\citep{hinton2012imagination}, inverse reinforcement learning~\citep{kakade2002learning}, and reward shaping~\citep{dettmers2017reward}. In contrast to RL, reward learning does not require access to an environment, and can be applied to a variety of tasks.

\textbf{Steering Language Models}. While LMs can be finetuned to generate text that matches human preferences, it is difficult to control their behavior precisely due to the completely unsaturated nature of their training data. One approach to gain more control is to use human feedback to train a reward model that reflects human preferences, and then finetune the LM to maximize this reward. This is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. The most common RLHF method is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)~\citep{schulman2017proximal}, which optimizes the reward model using a policy gradient objective. However, PPO is a complex and often unreliable method, and there are many ways to tune its hyperparameters, such as the discount factor, the learning rate, and the number of episodes. Another popular RLHF method is Value Iteration (VI)~\citep{kaelbling1996value}, which is simpler to implement but less stable. In this paper, we propose a new RLHF method, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which uses a simple classification loss to optimize the reward model, and does not require any tuning of hyperparameters.

\subsection{Standard RLHF}

Given a reward function $R : \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, where $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_n\}$ is the set of training data, the standard RLHF problem is to find a policy $\pi^*$ that maximizes the expected discounted reward:

\begin{equation}
    \max_{\pi^*} \mathbb{E}_{\pi \sim \pi^*}[R(\pi)] \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad \pi d_i \in \mathcal{D}, \forall i.
\end{equation}
