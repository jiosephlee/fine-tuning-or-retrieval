\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model fine-tuning algorithm, but it has several advantages over existing methods. First, it is much simpler to implement and train than RLHF. Second, it is more stable, as it does not rely on sampling from the LM, which can be computationally expensive and introduces additional sources of error. Third, it yields better performance than RLHF, as we show through a set of experiments that compare DPO to two popular RLHF variants: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and PPO with Hyperparameter Optimization (PPO-HO). Our experiments focus on two tasks that require precise control of the LM’s behavior: sentiment analysis and summarization. In sentiment analysis, we use DPO to fine-tune GPT-4 on the SentimentEval 2023 dataset \citep{zhang2023evaluating}, which contains ratings of the sentiment of movie reviews. We evaluate the resulting model on the test set, and find that it achieves higher average F1 scores than any model fine-tuned with PPO or PPO-HO. In summarization, we use the SQuAD v4.1 dataset \citep{glorot2017deep} to evaluate the ability of our method to control the quality of generated summaries. We evaluate the resulting models on the test set, finding that they produce summaries of higher quality than any model fine-tunable with PPO or PBO-HO. Finally, we evaluate the ability of our methods to control the quality of responses given in single-turn dialogue. We use the SQuAD 1.5 dataset \citep{wang2022training} to evaluate the performance of our methods on the task of generating high-quality responses to questions from human annotators. We evaluate the resulting systems on the test set, showing that they produce responses of higher quality than any system fine-tunable with PBO or PPO-HO.

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models have revolutionized natural language processing (NLP) by enabling the acquisition of vast quantities of data-driven knowledge and reasoning skills \citep{brown2020language,radford2021learning}. However, these models are trained in an entirely unsupervised manner, making them difficult to steer and control. This is particularly true for tasks that require fine-grained control over the model’s behavior, such as generating high-quality responses to queries or generating coherent summaries of text. In this paper, we focus on the task of generating responses to queries in single-turn dialogue, a challenging conversational task that requires the model to generate a fluent and coherent response to a user query, while also adhering to the context provided by previous turns. While recent work has shown that large language models can be effectively used to control the quality of dialogue responses \citep{li2023training,li_2023_towards,li2024training}, achieving such control is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training data. 

To address this challenge, we introduce a new approach for gaining steerability in large language models, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). At a high level, DPO is a reward model fine-tuned on the preferences of a human annotator. The resulting reward model is then used to optimize a large unsupervised LM, yielding a model that is both highly steerable and performs well on downstream tasks. The key insight behind DPO is that the optimal policy for maximizing the reward can be found analytically, allowing us to solve a standard reinforcement learning (RL) problem with only a simple preference classification loss. This is in contrast to existing methods for gaining steerability, which typically involve sampling from the LM during training and performing significant hyperparameter tuning, or performing RL from scratch on a reward model that has been carefully tuned to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original LM.

After introducing the problem in Section \ref{section:problem_statement}, we describe DPO in more detail in Sections \ref{sec:reward_model} and \ref{sec:optimal_policy}. We then describe how to apply DPO to the task of controlling the quality of responses in single-turn dialogue in Section \ref{sect:DPO_for_single_turn_dialoage}. We conclude with a discussion of future directions in Section \ref{sexp:conclusions}.
