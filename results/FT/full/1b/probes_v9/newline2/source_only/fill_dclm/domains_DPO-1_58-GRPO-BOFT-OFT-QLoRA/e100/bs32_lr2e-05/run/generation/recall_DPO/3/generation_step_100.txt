\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model fine-tuning algorithm that does not use sampling from the LM during optimization.  In this paper, we introduce a new parameterisation of the reward model that enables extraction of the optimal policy from the reward function, and propose a simple reinforcement learning algorithm based on this parameterization.  We call this algorithm Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and our experiments show that it is both stable and performant compared to existing algorithms for RLHF, producing better aligned language models with fewer training steps.  Moreover, DPO is substantially simpler to implement and easier to tune than PPO-based algorithms.  We evaluate the performance of DPO and PPO in fine-tuning GPT-4 for sentiment alignment and single-turn dialogue, showing that DPO produces models that align as well as or better with human preferences, while being substantially simpler to train.  We also evaluate DPO and PPO on generating high-quality summarizations and robust single-turn dialogue.  Overall, DPO produces models that are more aligned with human preferences and more capable of generating high-quality output than PPO-based algorithms, while being substantially simpler and faster to train.

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models have revolutionized natural language processing (NLP) by enabling tasks that were previously impossible or prohibitively expensive to perform. However, the completely unsupervised training data these models consume comes at a cost: the time and resources required to produce the text they consume. This presents a significant barrier to the deployment of these models in settings where access to large amounts of text is limited, such as in remote communities or underdeveloped countries. 

To address this, researchers have begun experimenting with methods for fine-tuning language models on data that is generated by the model itself, rather than relying on external datasets. This approach, known as self-supervised learning, has been shown to be effective in improving performance on downstream tasks, but it is unclear how well it aligns with human preferences. In particular, there is concern that the models may learn behaviors that are difficult or impossible to reproduce in the real world, and that the behaviors learned may not be aligned with human preferences.

In this paper, we explore a new approach for fine-tuning language model generations to match human preferences. Our approach, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), does not use reinforcement learning from human feedback, but instead uses a reward model to optimize the language model directly for the desired preference signal. The key insight behind DPO is to parameterize the reward model in a way that makes the corresponding optimal policy easy to optimize. We formalize this idea in Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}, and describe the details of DPO in Section~\ref{ssec:approach}. Our experiments show that DPO is both stable and perform ant to existing algorithms for RL HF, producing better aligned language model generations with fewer training steps. In particular, we find that DPO can fine tune LMs to align with preferences as well as or worse with existing algorithms, but that it is substantially simpler to implement, easier to tune, and produces better results in terms of sentiment alignment and summarization quality in a single experiment. We also evaluate DPO on generating high-quality single-turn dialogue, and find that it produces models that are more align with human preferences and more able to generate high-quality output than existing algorithms. \section{Related Work}

Self-supervised learning is a method for training language models without annotated training data. The most common approach is to train a language model to maximize the likelihood of a text snippet given its own translation, a process known as pretext task~\cite{devlin-etal-2018-moore}. Other approaches include learning from text spans paired with human labels of their correct semantic role~\cite{wang2022self,sung2022training}, or from text spans paired with their linguistic annotations~\cite{liu2020languagewise,liu2021zero,sung2022training}. These approaches typically rely on supervised fine-tuning on top of the self-supervised model, but can be used instead to fine-tune the self-supervised model to match human preferences~\cite{wang2019language,liu2021learning,liu2022training}. This is the approach taken in reinforcement learning from human feedback algorithms such as PPO~\cite{schulman2017proximal}.

Reinforcement learning from human feedback is typically applied to control tasks, where the goal is to learn a policy that maximizes the reward signal provided by a human trainer. The reward signal can be derived from a reward model that estimates the reward received by the agent at each state in the environment, and the optimal policy is then the one that maximizes this reward.
