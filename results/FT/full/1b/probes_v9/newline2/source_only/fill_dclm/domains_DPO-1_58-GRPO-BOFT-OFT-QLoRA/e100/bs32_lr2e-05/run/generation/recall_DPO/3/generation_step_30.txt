\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model that is optimized to maximize the reward function, and then fine-tuned to optimize the original LM. This is a common approach in reinforcement learning (RL), but it is not straightforward to optimize the reward function in the context of unsupervised language modeling. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward functions that enables us to solve the standard reward learning problem with only a simple binary classification loss. We call this new parameterization the \textit{direct preference parameterization} (DPP). The DPP is a natural generalization of the standard reward function, and we show that it can be used to parameterize the reward function of any RL problem. We then introduce \textit{Direct Preferred Optimization} (DPO) -- a simple algorithm that uses the DPP to optimize the reward function of an unsupervised LM. DPO is a variant of PPO, the most popular algorithm for RL in RLHF. We evaluate DPO on several benchmarks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our experiments show that DPO is effective at fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences, and that it is more stable and performant than existing methods. Notable is that DPO is simpler to implement and train than existing methods, and it does not require any hyperparameter tuning.

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models have been shown to learn rich and useful knowledge, such as commonsense knowledge, reasoning skills, and cultural information \citep{hulora2023,liu2023learning,liu2023language}. However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. For example, it is difficult to ensure that a model does not generate offensive content, or that it does not generate answers that are too similar to the prompt. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{liu2023fine,liu2023training,liu2023reinforcement}, where the model is trained to maximize a reward function that reflects the preferences of human annotators, and then fine-tunes the model to minimize the negative impact of its behavior on the human preferences \citep{li2023fine,li2023training}. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RHLF) is a powerful method for training models to perform well in a wide range of tasks, but it is often difficult to collect reliable human feedback, and it is also computationally expensive to train large models using RHLF. 

A second approach is to use a reward model to estimate the preferences of human annotator, and then fine-tune the model to maximize the reward function without drifting too far from its original behavior \citep{li2019learning,liu2021learning,liu2022learning}. This is known as reward learning from human feedback (RRHF). The reward model is typically learned using reinforcement learning, and then fine-tunable with a simple classification loss. The most popular method for learning the reward model is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) \citep{schulman2017proximal}, which is a variant of policy gradient that uses a proximal term to regularize the policy gradient. PPO is widely used in RLHF, and it has been shown to be effective at learning reward functions that reflect human preferences \citep{liao2022learning,liu2020learning,liu2024learning}. However, PPO is a complex algorithm that requires careful tuning of its hyperparameters, and it is often unstable. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective algorithm for RLHF that does not require any hyperparameters to be tuned. We introduce a new parameterization for the reward function that enables us to solve this problem with only a simple loss function. We call this parameterization the \textbf{direct preference parameterization}, and we refer to the algorithm that uses this parameterization as \textbf{Direct Preference Optimization} or DPO for short.
