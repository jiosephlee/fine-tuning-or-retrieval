1. Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has been widely used to train language models (LMs) to generate high-quality text (Devlin et al., 2018; Li et al., 2020; Li et al. (2021); Li et al. 2022). However, RL-based LMs are typically trained using a reward function that measures the quality of generated text, such as the perplexity of the generated text (Devlin et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2009; Li et al, 2020; Li and Bansal, 2021; Li et al 2021; Li and Bansal 2022). While this approach has proven to be effective, it has several limitations. First, the reward function is typically learned from a small number of human judgments, which may not accurately reflect the preferences of the target audience. Second, the reward function is often complex and difficult to interpret, which makes it difficult to understand and modify the model's behavior. Third, the reward function is usually trained using a gradient-based optimization algorithm, which can be slow and unstable. Fourth, the reward function is sensitive to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the learning rate and the number of training samples. Fifth, the reward function is not always available, and must be estimated from scratch for each new task. Finally, the reward function is only applicable to the specific task being trained, and does not generalize well to other tasks.
