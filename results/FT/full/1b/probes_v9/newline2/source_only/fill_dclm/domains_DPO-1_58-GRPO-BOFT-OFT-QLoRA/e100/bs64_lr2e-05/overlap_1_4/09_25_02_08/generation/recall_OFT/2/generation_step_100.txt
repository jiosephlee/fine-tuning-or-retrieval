The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet powerful technique that modifies the weights of a pretrained diffusion model by adding a small orthogonal matrix to the weight vector. This orthogonal matrix is chosen to be the inverse of the weight matrix of the pretrained model. OFT has been shown to be effective in preserving the geometry of neurons, which is crucial for many applications such as generative adversarial networks (GANs). However, the effectiveness of OFT has not been fully explored in text-to-image generation.

The main contribution of this paper is to provide a comprehensive study of OFT's performance in text-to-image generation. We conduct a systematic comparison of OFT with several state-of-the-art finetuning methods and additive methods. Our results show that OFT significantly outperforms all other methods in terms of both image quality and semantic consistency. Furthermore, OFT is also more robust to the choice of the orthogonal matrix than LoRA, which is another finetuning method that has been widely used in text-to-image generation.
