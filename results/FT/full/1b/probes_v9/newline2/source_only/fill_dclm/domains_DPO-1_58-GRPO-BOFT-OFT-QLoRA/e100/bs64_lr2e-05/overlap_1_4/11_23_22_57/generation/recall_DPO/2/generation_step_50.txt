Introduction
Language models are widely used in natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as machine translation, question answering, and text generation. However, language models are often trained on large amounts of text data, which can be expensive to obtain. To address this problem, researchers have proposed using pretrained language models (PLMs) as a starting point for training new language models. PLMs are trained on large amounts of unlabeled text data, and their parameters can be easily transferred to downstream tasks. However, the quality of the pretrained model is often not sufficient for downstream tasks, and fine-tuning the model on a specific task can improve its performance. Therefore, it is important to find a way to align the parameters of a pretrained language model with human preferences.
One approach to aligning language models with preferences is to use reinforcement learning (RL). In reinforcement learning, an agent learns to perform a task by interacting with an environment. The agent receives a reward signal from the environment for each action it takes, and the goal is to maximize the total reward over time. RL algorithms have been successfully used to train language models, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [1] and DPO [2]. These algorithms optimize the reward function directly, instead of using a policy gradient to optimize a policy. This makes them more efficient than PPO, but they have not been applied to language models.
In this paper, we introduce a new reinforcement learning algorithm, Direct Preference Optimization ( DPO ), for aligning language models. DPO is a variant of PPO that uses a reward function to optimize the language model parameters. Unlike PPO, DPO does not use a policy gradient to optimize its reward function. Instead, DPO uses a preference function to optimize the reward function, which is based on human preferences. We show that DPO can achieve state-of-theart performance on the standard language model task, and we also demonstrate that DPO is effective for improving the performance of PPO.
