Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have been shown to be effective at solving a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (NLU) and natural language generation (NLG) (Brown et al., 2020;Devlin et al., 2019;Radford et al., 2018;Sutskever et al., 2014;Touvron et al., 2021). However, LLMs are also known to have limited mathematical reasoning capabilities (Bai et al., 2022;Chen et al., 2023;Kumar et al., 2024;Liu et al., 2025;Wang et al., 2026;Zhang et al., 2027). For example, LLMs are often unable to solve simple mathematical problems, such as finding the sum of two numbers or determining whether a given number is prime (Bai et al. 2022;Cheng et al., 2028;Kumar et al. 2024;Liang et al., 2029;Wang et al. 2026). This is particularly problematic in the context of mathematics education, where students often struggle to solve mathematical problems due to their limited mathematical reasoning capabilities (Huang et al., 202 1;Liu et al. 2025;Wu et al., 202X;Zhang et al. 2027). Therefore, it is important to develop methods that can improve the mathematical reasoning capabilities in LLMs.
One approach to improving mathematical reasoning capabilities in LLLMs is to use reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms to train LLMs to solve mathematical problems. RL algorithms have been shown to be very effective at improving the mathematical reasoning capabilities in NLU models (Bai et al, 2022;Chang et al., 2017;Kumar et al, 2024;Liao et al., 202x;Wang et al, 2026). In particular, RL algorithms have been shown effective at training LLMs to solve simple mathematical problems (Bai et al 2022;Chung et al., 202 x;Kumar et al 2024;Luo et al., 202 X;Wang et al 2026). However, RL algorithms are typically memory-intensive, which makes it difficult to train large LLMs with RL algorithms. To address this issue, researchers have proposed various memory-efficient RL algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2016), Actor-Critic (AC) (Mnih et al., 2015), and Policy Gradient (PG) (Mnih et  al., 2015). These memory-efficient RL algorithms have been shown very effective at training LLM models to solve mathematical problems (Bai 2022;Chu et al., 201 7;Kumar et  al., 2024). However, these memory-efficient RL algorithms are typically slower than the standard RL algorithms, which makes it difficult for LLMs to train with them.
