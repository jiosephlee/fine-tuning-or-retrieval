Introduction
Text-to-image generation (T2I) is the task of generating images from text descriptions. It has wide applications in various fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and artificial intelligence. One of the most popular approaches to T2I is Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [1,2]. GANs consist of two neural networks: a generator network that produces images from text, and a discriminator network that distinguishes between real images and generated images. The generator network is trained to fool the discriminator network, while the discriminator network is trained to distinguish between real images and generated ones. Over the past few years, GANs have achieved impressive results in T2I, but they are known to suffer from the so-called "adversarial instability" problem [3,4]. This problem arises when the generator network is trained to generate images that fool the discriminator network, which leads to the generation of images that are indistinguishable from real images. To address this problem, many methods have been proposed to stabilize the training of GANs, such as adversarial training [5,6], regularized training [7,8], and data augmentation [9,10]. Despite these efforts, GANs still suffer from the adversarial instability problem.
