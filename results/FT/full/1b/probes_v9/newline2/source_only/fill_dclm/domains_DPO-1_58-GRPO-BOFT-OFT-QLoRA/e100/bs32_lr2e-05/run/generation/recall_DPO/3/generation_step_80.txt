\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.

\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model fine-tuning algorithm, but it has some interesting properties that make it particularly suitable for fine-tuning language models. 

First, unlike existing methods, DPO does not require sampling from the LM during the fine-tuning stage, which can be computationally expensive. Second, DPO does not rely on any hyperparameters other than the number of epochs, making it easy to tune. Finally, DPO can find the optimal policy from a reward function in closed form, making it straightforward to interpret the reward function as a measure of quality. 

However, despite these advantages, DPO has its own challenges. One is that the reward function must be carefully chosen to ensure that the optimization converges to the optimal policy. Another is that the reward function needs to be sufficiently expressive to capture the desired properties of the optimal policy, but too expressive functions can be difficult to optimize. 

In this paper, we propose a solution to the first challenge: we propose a method for estimating the reward function from the training data. This method is based on a probabilistic interpretation of the reward function, which allows us to derive an explicit expression for the optimal policy. We then use this expression to design an efficient gradient-based optimization algorithm, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

Our experiments show that D PO is effective at fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences. In particular, we demonstrate that DPO can produce sentiment-accurate generations of text in just a few minutes, compared to weeks of training with RLHF. Furthermore, DPO produces more fluent and coherent generations than PPO-based RLHF, and matches or improves performance in various tasks, including summarization and single-turn dialog. Our results are available at \url{https://github.com/AlpeshKohli/DPO}.

We also conduct a comprehensive study of the stability of DPO against different hyperparameters, including the number of epochs and the learning rate. Our experiments show that DPO is highly robust to these parameters, and that a simple 2-fold cross-validation scheme can provide reliable estimates of the performance of different training runs.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]

\item We introduce Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), a simple yet effective algorithm for fine-tuning language model generations to align with human preferences.

\item DPO avoids sampling from the LM during training, making it computationally efficient. It also avoids hyperparameter tuning, making it easy to implement and train.

\item DPO is based on a novel approach for estimating the reward function, which enables us to derive an explicit form for the optimal policy. This policy can be efficiently optimized using gradient descent, resulting in a simple yet effective algorithm called Direct Preference Optimization (D Po).

\end{itemize}
