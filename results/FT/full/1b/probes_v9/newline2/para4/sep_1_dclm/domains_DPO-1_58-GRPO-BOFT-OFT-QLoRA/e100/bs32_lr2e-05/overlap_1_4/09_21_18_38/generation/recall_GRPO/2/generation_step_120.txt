Introduction
The development of artificial intelligence (AI) has led to significant advancements in various fields, including natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning. One of the most prominent applications of AI is in the field of language modeling, where models are trained to generate text based on a given input. Language models have become increasingly powerful and widely used in various applications, such as text summarization, question answering, and machine translation. However, these models are often limited in their ability to reason and solve complex problems, which is crucial for many real-world applications.
One approach to improving the reasoning capabilities of language models is through reinforcement learning (RL). RL is a type of machine learning that involves training an agent to make decisions based on feedback from an environment. In reinforcement learning, the agent receives a reward signal for its actions, which is used to guide the agent's behavior in the environment. Reinforcement learning has been successfully applied to various NLP tasks, such as machine translation (Sutton et al., 1999;Liu et al., 2016), question answering (Kulkarni et al., 2017;Wang et al., 2018), and dialogue (Mnih et al., 2015;Kulkarni etal., 2017;Gulcehre et al., 2019). In particular, reinforcement learning has been shown to be particularly effective in improving the reasoning abilities of language models (Chen et al., 2014;Wang et al. 2018;Gulcehreh et al., 2013;Kulkarni and Hulora, 2019;Kulkarni, 2020;Kulkarni 2021).
One of the most commonly used reinforcement learning algorithms is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is a variant of the actor-critic algorithm (Schulman et al., 2011). PPO is a policy optimization algorithm that uses a reward function to guide the policy of the agent. The reward function is typically defined as a function of the current state and the action taken by the agent. PPO is particularly effective in improving the performance on sequential decision-making tasks, such as language modeling. However, PPO requires access to a large memory, which can be challenging to implement in large language models. To address this issue, researchers have developed alternative reinforcement learning algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GROPO) (Kulkarni andHulora, 2018). GROPO is a reinforcement learning algorithm that allows agents to learn a policy by optimizing the reward function of a group of agents. GROPO has been shown to be more efficient than PPO in terms of memory usage and computational complexity. Additionally, GROPO has been successfully applied to improve the reasoning abilities of language model (Kulkarni,2021).
