Preference Learning
------------------

At the heart of DPO lies the concept of preference learning, which aims to learn a reward function from preference data. Formally, given a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{x^{(i)}, y_w^{(i)}, y_l^{(i)}\bigr\}_{i=1}^N$, where $x^{(i)}$ represents an instance of the environment, $y_w^{(i)}$ and $y_l^{(i)}$ denote the preferred and less preferred actions by an agent, respectively, and $N$ denotes the total number of instances in the dataset, the goal is to find a reward function $r^*(x, y)$ that maximizes the likelihood of observing the preference data:

$$\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim \mathcal{D}}\bigl[\log\mathbb{P}\bigl(x, y_w \mid y_l = y)\bigr]\geq \log\mathbb{ P}\bigl(x, Y \mid y_l = Y\bigr)$$

Here, $Y$ refers to the latent reward associated with action $y$. By assuming a linear relationship between the observed reward $r(x, y)$ and the true reward $r^*(x,y)$, the above expression simplifies to:

$$\mathbb {E}_{(x, Y)\sim \mathcal{ D}}\bigl[r(x, Y)-r^*(x, Y)\bigr]_{y= y_l} - \mathbb{E}_{y\sim \mathcal{Y}}\bigl[r(y, Y)\bigr]\ge 0$$

where $\mathcal{Y}$ denotes the set of rewards in the dataset. This inequality forms the basis of the preference learning objective, which is commonly referred to as the reward consistency problem. Various methods have been developed to address this issue, including maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and constrained optimization techniques like the L1 norm penalty.
