\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for training language models to align with human preferences. Existing methods for obtaining such preferences typically involve collecting human feedback on multiple model generations, and fine-tuning the initial unsupervised model using reinforcement learning to maximize a reward function derived from the human preferences. This reward function is typically learned using reinforcement learning from human feedback, which involves training a policy on a sequence generation task using human demonstrations, and then optimizing the policy to maximize a specified reward function. In this work, we propose a method for learning this reward function directly from human preferences, without the need for any reinforcement learning. Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is based on a simple yet effective reparameterization of the reward function, which allows us to express the optimal policy for the reward function in closed form. We demonstrate that the optimal policy learned from DPO matches or exceeds the performance of existing methods for fine-tuning language models to align with preferences, including PPO-based RLHF and a variant of REINFORCE with a learned reward function (REINFORCE with Policy Improvement, or REINFORCEPI). Our experiments show that DPO is generally more stable and efficient than existing methods for obtaining preferences from language models, and can be applied to a wide range of downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models trained on massive datasets have demonstrated impressive capabilities in areas such as translation, question answering, and summarization (Brown et al., 2018; Sutskever et al., 2014; Cho et al., 2015; Ranzato et al., 2017; Johnson et al., 2019; Mikolov et al., 2013; Brown et al., 2016; Wu et al., 201, 2018; Johnson et al., , 2020. However, these models are trained using a supervised learning approach that relies on human annotations of the relative quality of different model outputs. While it is possible to obtain human preferences for language models by presenting them with human-generated text and asking humans to rate the quality of the output, this approach is time-consuming and expensive, and does not scale to larger models. Moreover, the human preferences obtained in this way are not necessarily representative of the preferences of the general population, and may not reflect the preferences of people who are not already fluent in the domain being modeled. In this work, therefore, we seek to develop a method for obtaining preferences for language models that does not rely on human annotations, and which can be applied to models of any size. Our approach is based on the observation that language models are able to learn to align with human preferences in certain scenarios, and that the optimal policy for a reward function can be expressed in closed form (see Section 3 for details). We propose a method for learning a reward function from human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization, or DPO. Our experiments show that D PO can be used to train language models to align with user preferences as effectively as existing methods based on reinforcement learning from human feedback. In particular, we evaluate DPO against PPO-based RLHF, a widely used method for training language models using reinforcement learning from human preferences, and find that DPO yields models that perform at least as well as PPO models, and sometimes better. We also evaluate DPO on a range of downstream tasks, such as summarization and single-turn dialog, and find that models trained with DPO perform as well as or better compared to models trained with PPO or other methods.
