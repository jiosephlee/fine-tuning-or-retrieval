\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for training language models to align with human preferences. Existing methods for obtaining such preferences typically involve collecting human feedback on multiple model generations, and fine-tuning the larger unsupervised model to align with the preferred generation. This process is known as reinforcement learning from human preference data (RLHF). While RLHF has been shown to yield language models with high-quality responses, it is a complex and often challenging procedure. In this paper, we present a new method for training language models using only a single dataset of human preferences, without relying on reinforcement learning. Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is based on a simple yet effective reparameterization of the reward model used in RLHF. We demonstrate that by optimizing a different objective, the reward model can be expressed in terms of a policy, which allows us to train a policy from the preference dataset using a straightforward classification loss. Our experiments show that DPO yields language models that are capable of aligning with human preferences as well or better than existing methods, while being substantially simpler to train and more efficient to run. In particular, we evaluate DPO on two tasks: sentiment adjustment of model outputs, and generation of coherent responses in summarization and singleturn dialogue. On these tasks, DPO outperforms or matches PPO-based RLHF, and is generally easier to implement and train.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models trained on massive datasets exhibit remarkable capabilities, including the ability to answer questions, summarize text, and perform basic reasoning tasks \citep{chowdhery2022palm, stiennon2022learning, stiennon2023training}. However, these models are trained on unstructured text, and their behavior is largely governed by the implicit rewards they receive from the text they are exposed to. In contrast, humans explicitly reward or punish language models according to their preferences, providing a rich source of explicit rewards that can be used to train language models to align with user preferences. This approach, known as reinforcement learning from preference data (RLPF), has been demonstrated to yield language models with higher quality responses than those trained solely with supervised learning \citep{ziegler2022training}. Nevertheless, RLPF is a complex procedure that requires collecting large datasets of human preferences and fine-tuning the language model using reinforcement learning. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for training language models from preferences that is both simple to implement and effective at aligning with human preferences.

We propose a new method for training large unsupervised language models from preferences, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). Our method is based on a simple reparameterization of the traditional reward model used in RLPF. By optimizing a different objective, we can express the reward model in terms of a policy. This policy can then be optimized using a straightforward classification loss, rather than the complex RL objective used in existing methods. Our experiments show that by optimizing a different reward model, the reward function can be expressed in terms a policy. This policy allows us to train a language model from preferences using a straightforward classification loss rather than the complex RL objectives used in existing methods. We evaluate DPO on two different tasks: sentiment adjustment of language model outputs, and generation from prompts. On both tasks, DPO is able to align with human preferences or match the performance of existing methods, while being simpler to implement and train.

Related Work
Language Model Fine-Tuning. Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been achieved using neural network architectures pretrained on massive datasets, such as ImageNet \citep{radford2019language} and YouTube videos \citep{chung2018deep}. These pretraining models are then fine-tuned on downstream tasks to improve performance on those tasks. For language modeling, various methods have been proposed, including those that fine-tune the language model using either supervised or reinforcement learning objectives \citep{mikolov2013distributed,radford2015language, stiennon2020finetuning, stiennon2021learning, stiennon22training}. Fine-tuning language models using reinforcement learning has been shown to yield models with higher quality responses on downstream tasks such as summarization \citep{ziegliger2022training}, question answering \citep{ziegelberger2022training}, and dialogue \citep{ziegalger2022training,ziegler2022learning}. However, RL-based fine-tuning of language models is a complex procedure that involves collecting large datasets of human preference labels and training a reinforcement learning model to optimize the reward function associated with these preferences. In this paper,we present a method for training language model from preferences using only a single dataset, without relying on reinforcementlearning.
