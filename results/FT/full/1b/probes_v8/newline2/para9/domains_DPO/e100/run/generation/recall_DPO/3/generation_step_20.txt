\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a variant of reinforcement learning (RL) that uses a reward model to optimize a language model (LM) without relying on human feedback. The reward model is trained to maximize the reward function $R(\theta)$, where $\theta$ is the LM’s parameters, and the reward function is defined as the expected reward of a policy $\pi$ given the LM’s parameters $\theta$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
R(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}\left[R(x;\theta)\right]]
\end{equation}
The goal of DPO is to find the optimal $\theta^*$ that maximizes $R(\theta)$ while minimizing the KL divergence between the optimal policy $\pi^*$ and the original policy $\pi$. This objective is known as the reward maximization problem, and it is a common approach in reinforcement learning (RL). However, solving the reward maximization problem directly is computationally intractable, so instead, DPO uses a surrogate reward function $R_{\text{surrogate}}(\theta)$ that approximates the true reward function $R(\cdot)$, and solves the following optimization problem:
\begin{equations}\label{eq:direct}
\max_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[R_{\text{surrogates}}(\theta) - R(\theta)\right]
\end{equations}
where $\mathbb{E}_p[\cdot]$ denotes the expectation with respect to the distribution $p$. The surrogate reward function $R_\text{surrogate}$ is typically learned from human preference data, and the optimal policy $\pi^\star$ is found by maximizing the reward function $R_\theta$ with respect to $\theta$.

\textbf{Main Contributions.} We propose a new method for solving the reward maximization objective in Eq.~\ref{eq:direct} using a simple classification loss. Our method, called \textbf{Direct Preference Optimization} or DPO, is a straightforward extension of the standard RLHF algorithm, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the LM during training. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and outperforms existing methods for fine-tuning LMs to align with user preferences. In particular, DPO achieves comparable or better performance than PPO-based RLHF, while requiring only a single classification loss per epoch, compared to the 2-3 losses required by PPO. Furthermore, DPO is able to fine-tune LMs with higher quality responses than PPO, while maintaining a lower KL divergence from the original policy. Finally, DPO is also able to fine-tune the same LMs used in previous studies, including GPT-3, OpenAI’s GPT-4, and Google’s BERT-base-large, achieving comparable or better results than the best-performing methods on downstream tasks.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Language Models.} Large-scale unsupervised language modeling has been a major focus of research in recent years, with the advent of generative pre-trained language models (GPT)~\citep{Radford2018LanguageModeling} and their variants. These models are trained on massive amounts of text data, and have demonstrated impressive performance on a variety of downstream tasks, including machine translation, summarization, question answering, and dialogue. However, these models are trained using a completely unsupervised learning paradigm, and thus lack any explicit notion of reward or goal. This makes it difficult to control the behavior of the language model, and to fine-tune it to achieve specific goals. In this paper, we focus on the task of fine-tuning language models to align with human preferences, a problem that has been studied extensively in the reinforcement learning (RL) community. In particular, the RLHF algorithm~\citep{Liu2020Learning} is a popular approach for fine-tuning language models using human preferences. The RLHF algorithm consists of two steps: first, a reward model is trained to estimate the reward associated with each policy, and then the optimal policy is fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. The reward model is typically learned from human preferences, and the optimal policy is found by maximizing the expected reward of the optimal policy with respect to the reward model. However, the RLHF algorithm is computationally expensive, and often requires significant hyperparameter tuning to achieve satisfactory performance. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simpler and more efficient method for fine-tuning language model to align with human preferences.
