\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models. It is based on the observation that the reward model learned by an RLHF algorithm can be expressed as a function of the language model parameters, and that the optimal policy for this reward function can be computed exactly. This allows us to train the language model directly from human preferences, without the need for sampling or hyperparameter tuning.

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive results in natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [1], GPT-3 [2]), but achieving precise control over their behavior is challenging due to the completely unsophisticated nature of their training. While LMs can be fine-tuned to perform specific tasks, such as summarization [3] or dialogue [4], they are typically trained to maximize the likelihood of a fixed set of human preferences, such as the preference of a human annotator [5] or a group of humans [6]. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. However, RLHF is a complicated procedure that requires collecting human preferences, estimating the reward function from the preferences, and then fine tuning the language model to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original model. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs that avoids these steps. Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is based on the observation made by Li et al. [7] that the reward function learned by an RLHF method can be expressed as a linear combination of the language model parameters. We show that the optimal policy for the reward function can be computed in closed form, allowing DPO to be solved exactly. Our experiments demonstrate that DPO can fine tune LMs to align with preferences as well as or even better than existing methods, while being substantially simpler to train and implement.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervized language models have been trained on massive datasets, including the Google News dataset [8] and the Gigaword corpus [9]. These models have demonstrated impressive performance on a variety of downstream tasks, including summarization [3], question answering [10], and machine translation [11]. However, obtaining precise control over the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised training. Several approaches have been proposed to gain steerability, including reinforcement learning from human feedback [12], supervised learning [13], and reinforcement learning with reward modeling [14]. In this paper, we focus on the RLHF approach, which trains the language model to maximize a reward function derived from human preferences. The reward function is typically estimated by maximizing the likelihood of a fixed dataset of human preferences, and then the language model is fine-tuned to maximize this reward function. This approach has been shown to yield high-quality results, but it is also computationally intensive, requiring the collection of human preferences and the estimation of the reward function from these preferences. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for fine-tuning language models that avoids these steps.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
R(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y_h) \sim \mathcal{D}_\text{h}}\bigg[\log \sigma(R(\theta)(x, y_h))\bigg],
\end{equation}

where $\sigma$ denotes the logistic function. The optimal language model $\theta^*$ is defined as the one that maximizes the reward, i.e., $\theta^* = \argmax_{\theta} R(\theta)$. The optimal policy for the reward model $R(\theta)$ is given by:
