\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for training language models to align with human preferences. Existing methods for obtaining such preferences typically involve collecting human judgments of the relative quality of multiple model outputs, and fine-tuning the language model to maximize the average reward obtained from these preferences. This approach is known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. In this paper, we propose a new method for optimizing the reward function in RLHF, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Our method is based on a simple yet powerful idea: instead of optimizing the reward function directly, we optimize a proxy function that is easier to optimize, and then use a standard optimization technique to find the optimal reward function. We demonstrate that this approach can be used to train language models to align with preferences as well as or more effectively than existing methods. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, efficient, and computationally lightweight compared to existing methods, and can be applied to a wide range of downstream tasks including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. In particular, we show that DPO can surpass PPO-based RLHF methods in terms of performance on sentiment analysis and summarization, and matches or improves PPO-based RLHF on dialogue tasks.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved impressive results on a variety of tasks, including translation, summarization, question answering, and dialogue. However, these models are trained using only self-supervised learning, which does not explicitly teach them how to perform any particular task. As a result, it is challenging to control the behavior of these models, since there is no explicit reward function to optimize. One approach to gain some control over the behavior of language models is to collect human preferences for different model outputs, and fine tune the language model to maximize these preferences. This technique, known as reinforcement learning from preference data (RLPF), has been successfully applied to various language modeling tasks, including summarization (Bai et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021a; Li et al.,2021b), question answering (Chen et al., 2022), and dialogue (Chen et al. (2022); Li et al. (2021a); Li et al. 2021b; He et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024)). In this setting, the language model is trained to maximize a reward function that is derived from the preferences of a human annotator. This reward function is learned using reinforcement learning, typically by maximizing the expected return of the policy learned from the preference data. The policy is optimized using reinforcement learning, typically via policy gradient methods such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2017).

While RLPF has proven effective for controlling the behavior of language models, it is a complex and often challenging process. First, the reward function must be learned from the preference data, which typically involves collecting human preferences for multiple model outputs, and then fine-tune the language model to maximize this reward function. This process is typically performed using reinforcement learning, which involves training a policy on the preference data using reinforcement learning, and then optimizing the policy using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward function. This reinforcement learning step is often performed using methods such as PPO, which trains the policy using a Monte-Carlo approximation of the Bellman optimality equation (Ranzato et al., 2015). However, PPO is a complex algorithm that requires careful tuning of hyperparameters, and can be unstable when the policy gradient is not well-behaved. Additionally, the RLHF procedure can be computationally expensive, especially when dealing with large preference datasets. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for training language models using RLHF that is both efficient and effective. To this end, we propose a new approach for optimizing the reward function used in RLHF, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). Our method is based upon a simple yet powerful idea that we call the \textit{direct preference optimization} (DPO) framework. In the DPO framework, we first define a proxy function that is easy to optimize, and then optimize this proxy function to find the optimal reward. This approach is similar to the method of \cite{levine2022training} for training language models using reinforcement learning from dialogue preferences, but our method is more general and can be applied to any language model task. We demonstrate that the DPO framework can be used to train a language model to align with human preferences, and that the resulting policy is as effective as or more effective than existing methods.
