3.1  Reinforcement Learning

The field of reinforcement learning has a long history, dating back to the 1950s. However, it was not until the 1990s that reinforcement learning began to make significant advances, with the introduction of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) by Richard Szeliski in 1997 and Deep Q-Networks (DQN) by Mnih et al. in 2015. These algorithms represent a major breakthrough in reinforcement learning, enabling systems to learn to perform complex tasks without being explicitly programmed. However, both MCTS and DQN require the use of large datasets, which can be prohibitively expensive for many real-world applications.

Direct Preference Optimization builds upon the theoretical foundation laid by DPO, providing a method for learning from experience that does not require large datasets. By directly optimizing the reward function, DPO avoids the need for Monte Carlo sampling and can learn from much smaller datasets. This makes DPO particularly well-suited for use in robotics, where access to large datasets is often limited.
