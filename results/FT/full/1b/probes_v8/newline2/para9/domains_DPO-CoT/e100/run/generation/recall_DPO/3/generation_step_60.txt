\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for language model fine-tuning. 
It takes an unsupervised language model and a set of preference labels, and optimizes the language model to maximize the reward derived from the preference labels while staying within a specified loss budget.
This reward model is learned by maximizing the likelihood of the preference labels under the language model's distribution, which is equivalent to maximizing the likelihood of the labels under the language model’s conditional distribution given the preference labels.
This approach is similar to the widely used method of maximizing the likelihood of preference labels under the language models’ distribution (e.g., \cite{li2022training, bai2021learning, bai2022training}, but differs in that it does not require any assumptions about the generative model, and does not rely on sampling from the language model to estimate the reward function.

\textbf{Main Contributions.} 
We present a novel method for optimizing language models using DPO, which we call \emph{Direct Preference Optimization} or \textit{DPO}. 
DPO is a straightforward extension of the standard RLHF framework, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning or sampling from the language model during optimization. 
We demonstrate that DPO can be applied to a wide range of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and outperforms existing methods on all of these tasks. 
Additionally, we show that DPO can be used to optimize the same language model for multiple different objectives, including maximizing the likelihood of preference annotations, maximizing the likelihood of preference-label pairs, and maximizing the likelihood of preference pairs under a reward model. 
Finally, we show that D PO can be applied to a variety of different language models, including large-scale GPT-3 models, and that it is stable and efficient even when applied to models with millions of parameters.
