\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free reinforcement learning (RL) method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models. 
We begin by introducing the standard RLHF framework, which involves training a reward model to predict the preference of humans for different model generations, and then fine-tunning the model using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward. 
We then present DPO, which instead directly optimizes the policy learned from the reward model to maximize the reward, without the need for sampling from or fine-tuning the reward model. 
Our experiments show that D PO can be applied to a wide range of tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue, and outperforms existing methods on all of these tasks.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance on many natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, including question answering (QA) (Devlin et al., 2019; Radford et al., 2018; Brown et al., 2017; Weston et al., 2016), machine translation (Brown et al., 2015; Sutskever et al., 2014; Cho et al., 2013), and summarization (Bromley et al., 2011; Devlin et al., 2020; Brown et al., , 2018. However, these models are trained using large amounts of unlabeled text, and their behavior is difficult to control because they are not explicitly trained to perform any particular task. This makes it challenging to use them for downstream tasks that require fine-grained control over the model's behavior, such as generating high-quality responses to queries (Kulkarni et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Wang et al., , 2022b. In this paper, we investigate how to leverage the power of large unsupervised language model (LM) training to enable fine-grained control over their behavior. Specifically, we consider the problem of fine-tuning an unsupervised LM to match the preferences of a human annotator, a process known as reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF (Wu et al., 2024; Li et al., , 2039. The key challenge in RLHF is that the reward model used to optimize the policy must be learned from human preferences, which are typically collected using reinforcement learning from human interaction (RLH) (Sutton and Barto, 2018). This RLH reward model is then fine-tuned to maximize the reward, which is typically done using reinforcement learning (RL) (Sutton and Duffy, 2017; Sutton et al., 2012; Hsu et al., 2010; Kaelbling et al., 1996; Silver et al., 201 access to a large collection of human preferences, which is often unavailable. In this paper, our goal is to develop a method for fine-tuning an unsatisfied reward model to match the preferences of human annotators, without relying on the expensive process of collecting human preferences via RLH. We propose Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for fine-tunning an unsupervised LM using only a simple classification loss. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and can be applied to a range of tasks, including summarization, sentiment analysis, and single-turn dialogue. Our contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt]
    \item We introduce a new parameterization for the reward model in RLH that enables extraction of the optimal policy from the reward model in closed form, allowing for a straightforward solution to the standard RLHF problem.
    \item We present DPO, a method for fine-tunig an unsupervised LM by optimizing a policy learned from the reward function to maximize the reward, rather than optimizing the reward function itself.
    \item Our experiments show that D PO is stable, performant (on downstream tasks), and computationally lightweight compared to existing methods.
\end{itemize}
