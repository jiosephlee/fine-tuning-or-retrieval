To begin, let us define these terms more precisely.

A reward function, denoted as $r^*(\pi)$, represents the expected return of a policy $\pi$ under a given environment. This function quantifies the quality of a policy's performance, taking into account both the agent's actions and the underlying dynamics of the system. For example, if our agent interacts with a Markov decision process (MDP), the reward function could represent the sum of discounted rewards obtained through successive transitions.

$$r_{\phi}( r) = r^*(\pi)$$

This approach allows us to leverage the power of RL algorithms like DPO, which typically operate on reward functions rather than raw rewards.
