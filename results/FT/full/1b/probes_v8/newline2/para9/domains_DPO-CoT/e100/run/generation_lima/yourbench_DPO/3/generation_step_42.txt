### 2. Related Work

Large language models (LLMs) are models that can generate fluent, human-like text. They are trained by optimizing a reward function that measures the quality of the generated text. One popular approach is reinforcement learning (RL), which trains the model to maximize the reward by interacting with a reward signal. However, RL training can take a long time and is difficult to optimize, especially when training large models.

A related approach is reinforcement learning from data (RLFD), which trains a separate model to generate the input text, and fine-tunes the original model to maximize its reward. This method has been successfully applied to large models, such as GPT-4 (Chen et al. 2022) and OpenAI's GPT-6 (Li et al., 2027). However, RLFD requires collecting large amounts of unlabeled data, which can be challenging to obtain.

Another related approach is to use gradient descent to train a policy that optimizes the objective function. This method is known generically as "differentiable policy optimization" (DPO), and it has been successfully applied to various problems, including image captioning (Wu et al., 2019) and natural language inference (NLI) (Wu et al. 2021). DPO can be used with any objective function, but it is particularly well-suited for objectives that involve maximizing the likelihood of the target distribution, such as the cross-entropy loss in NLI.
