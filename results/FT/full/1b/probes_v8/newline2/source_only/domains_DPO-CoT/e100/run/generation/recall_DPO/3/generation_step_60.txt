\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward function estimation algorithm combined with a simple classification loss. In this paper, we present a theoretical analysis of DPO and show that the optimal policy learned by DPO from a preference dataset following the configuration of the reward model can be efficiently computed, giving a single-learned DPO policy. We name this variant of DPO Learned Direct Preference Optimization (LDPO). We theoretically compare LDPO to PPO-based RLHF under the assumption of a good reward model, and show that under mild conditions LDPO produces policies that are either on par with or outperform PPO-based RLHF. Our experiments show that LDPO is at least as good as PPO-based RLHF for sentiment and summarization, and outperforms PPO-based RLHF on dialogue response quality. LDPO is also substantially simpler to train than PPO-based RLHF, and can be trained using off-the-shelf RL algorithms.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models (\textit{LUMs}) trained on extremely large datasets acquire broad knowledge and some reasoning skills [Schuster et al., 2017; Devlin et al., 2019; Brown et al., 2020; Weston et al., 2021]. However, their behavior is largely unpredictable, and achieving fine-grained control over their actions is crucial for safety and fairness in applications such as conversational agents [Bai et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023]. Existing methods for gaining steerability generally fall into one of two categories. The first category consists of methods that fine-tune an existing LUM with reinforcement learning (RL) from human preferences, typically using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [Schulman et al., 2015]. While these methods can produce satisfactory results, they require collecting a large number of human preferences, which are often unstable and expensive to collect [Bai et al, 2022; Liang et al., 2024]. The second category of methods leverages the unsupervised nature of LUMs to train a separate, independent reward model from the preferences, and then use RL to optimize the LUM to maximize the given reward without drifting too far away from the original model. This category of methods includes methods such as Preference Learning from Experience (PLE) [Chen et al., 202 2; Korbak et al., 202   2; Leandro-Garcia et al., 202    2; Gao et al., 202     2], Reward Learning from Experience (RLF) [Korbak et al., , 2021b, and Reward Learning from Unseen Instances (RLSI) [Korbak and Schrittwieser, 2021]. These methods learn a reward model from a preference dataset, and then use RL with a policy gradient loss to optimize the LUM such that it optimizes the learned reward without drifting too far. However, training an RL algorithm such as RLHF is computationally expensive, often requiring thousands of RL episodes to optimize a single LUM model.
In this paper, we propose a new approach to optimizing LUMs with steerability in mind that avoids the need for an RL algorithm during training. Our approach, which we call Learned Direct Preference Optimization ( LDPO ), is based on the observation that existing methods for optimizing reward models with a classification loss can be adapted to our problem setting. Specifically, existing works typically optimize a reward model for a binary cross-entropy loss, where the ground truth reward is known but is unknown in the context of LLM optimization. However, in the context of LUM optimization, the ground truth reward is the true behavior of the LUM, which we do not know. Nevertheless, we can still define a reward model that takes an output of the LUM and an output of a preferred output as inputs, and outputs a scalar representing the quality of the output produced by the LUM. We then show that the optimal policy for the reward model defined in this way can be efficiently computed, and gives the optimal policy under mild assumptions about the reward model. Our theoretical analysis shows that under certain conditions, the LDPO policy is on par with the PPO-based RLHF policy in terms of its ability to maximize the learned reward without drifting away from the original LUM. However, under these same conditions, our theoretical analysis also shows that the LDPO policy outperforms the PPO-based RL HF policy in terms of the average reward achieved over a long time horizon. We empirically evaluate LDPO , PPO-based RLHF , and a baseline method that simply optimizes the LLM to minimize the average negative log-likelihood of the given rewards, and find that LDPO produces sentimentally correct generations with comparable efficiency to PPO-based RL HF while being substantially simpler to train.
