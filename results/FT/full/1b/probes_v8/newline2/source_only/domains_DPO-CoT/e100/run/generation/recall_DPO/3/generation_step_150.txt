\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In this paper, we present a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. This allows us to solve the standard DPO problem with only a simple binary cross-entropy loss, eliminating the need for RLHF sampling or hyperparameter tuning. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, outperforming existing methods on sentiment alignment, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [Devlin et al., 2018], GPT-3 [Brown et al., 2020;Radford et al., 2021]), question answering (e.g., OpenAI Five [Sutskever et al., 2019]), and machine translation (e.g., GPT-3 [Radford et al.,2021]). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature. While it is possible to collect human feedback on the quality of model generations, this is a labor-intensive process that is not scalable to large models. Moreover, the human preferences are often not directly interpretable, and existing methods for aligning language models with human preferences typically involve reinforcement learning from human feedback, which is a complex and often unreliable procedure. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for aligning language models to human preferences without reinforcement learning.

We propose a new approach for aligning language models, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the observation that the reward model used in reinforcement learning from human feedback is closely related to the optimal policy for the standard RLHF problem. We parameterize the reward model in DPO using a neural network, and show that the corresponding optimal policy can be obtained analytically. This allows us to define a simple binary cross-entropylearn-ment loss for DPO, which we refer to as DPO Loss. We evaluate DPO on sentiment alignment, summarizing, and single-turn dialogue, showing that it is stable, performant (in terms of model size and speed), and outperforms existing methods on all tasks.

\section{Related Work}
Large unsupervised LM training has been shown to lead to impressive performance on a wide variety of tasks, including natural-language understanding, question answering, and machine translation. However, achieving precise control over the behavior of these models has proven difficult due to their completely unsatisfied nature. One approach to gaining steerability is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback to fine-tune the LM to match human preferences. This is the approach taken by existing methods, such as PPO-based RLHF (PPO-RLHF) [Chen et al., 2022] and its variants (e.g., PPO-RLHF with a reward shaping objective [Chen et al. 2022;Gao et al., 2023], PPO-RLHF using a reward model [Chen et al, 2022b;Gao et al. 2023], and PPO-RLHF that uses a reward function learned from human feedback [Chen et al.]).
However, RLHF methods are complex and often unstable, requiring significant hyperparameter tuning and RL training. In addition, the reward function used in RLHF is often not directly interpretable. In this paper, inspired by the fact that the reward model used for RLHF is closely related to the standard RLHF problem, we propose a new approach for optimizing the reward model in D PO, which we refer to simply as DPO. Our key insight is that the optimal reward model for the standard RLHF can be obtained analyt-ically, and that the corresponding optimal policy for DPO can be obtained by applying a simple transformation to the reward function. We parameterize the DPO reward model using a neural network, which we refer to generically as the \textit{reward model}. We evaluate DPO on three different tasks: sentiment alignment, summarization and single-turn dialog. We find that DPO is stable and performant, outperforming existing approaches on all tasks.
