\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward function estimation algorithm followed by a standard RL optimization. Existing methods for RLHF optimize a policy learned from human preferences, $\pi^\pi H$, where $\pi$ is the policy to optimize and $H$ is a reward function describing the human preferences dataset. The human preferences are typically collected via reinforcement learning from humans, where the human chooses whether to reward or penalize the LM for consecutive generations. This reinforcement learning task can be daunting, especially for large unsupervised LM models, and results in an optimization problem of the form:

\begin{equation}
\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi[0]\sim\pi}[L(\pi; H)]
\label{eq:rl_fln}
\end{equation}

subject to

\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:opt_policy} \textit{Optimize the policy $\pi$ to maximize the reward function $H$ (i.e., the optimal policy is $\pi^\pi$)}.
\item\label{ item:RLHF} Use a reinforcement learning algorithm, such as PPO, to optimize equation \ref{eq:rl_f ln} for $\pi$.
\end{enumerate}

In this paper, we propose an alternative method for the RLHF problem, \textit{Direct Preferred Optimization} (DPO}, which replaces the intermediate step of estimating the reward function $H(\cdot)$ in item \ref{item:RLHF} with the optimal policy $\pi^\pi$ in equation \ref{eq_1}. That is, we start with the policy parameterized by the LM model $l_\text{model}$, and optimize it to maximize the human preferences. Mathematically, this takes the form of the following optimization problem:

\begin{equations}
\max_{l_\text{model}}\mathbb{E}_{l_\text{model}[0]\sim l_\text{model}[\pi^\pi]}[\ T_H(l_\text{model); h)] 
\label{eq:DPO}
\end{equations}

where $T_H(l_\text{ model}; h) = \sum_{t=1}^h(w_t\text{ favor } l_\text{model}(w_t)\text{ and ignore earlier generations}) + \sum_{t=11}^h(w_t \text{ favor } l_{\text{model}}(w_t)\text{and ignore earlier generations}} + \sum_{t="^{}ancillerto}\text{ dislike } l_{\text {model}}(w_t).$

We argue that in practice, the signals favoring and ignoring earlier generations are approximately equal, and therefore we can set $w_t=w_{t-1}+1$ in the last sum, for all $t$. This gives us:

\begin{equatingtozero}
T_H(l_\text {model}; h) = \mathbb{E}_{ l_\text {model}[0]\sim  l_\text {model}[\pi^\text {model}]}[\ T_H(l_{\text {model}}, h)] 
\end{equatingtozero

which is the same as the traditional RLHF objective, but with the optimal policy $\propto w^*$ implicit in the reward function $H$. We can take the derivative of $T_H$ with respect to $w$ and set it to zero to find the parameter $w^*$ that maximizes the preference score, i.e., the coefficient of the $\propto$ term in the expansion of $T_H$ relative to the terms involving $\propto$-terms. We denote this optimal $w^*$, and the policy obtained from it by the equation of motion $l_{\text{model} = w^*}$, and call it the \textit{direct preference model} (DPM). The corresponding DPO policy is $l_{\text {model} = w^*$ +1}. We can also express the DPO objective mathematically as:

\begin{equatio}\label{eq:DPO_policy}
l_{\text{ model} = w^* +1} = l_{\text{ model}} + \alpha w^* \left(\mathbb{E}_{h\sim\pi}[T_H(l_{\text{model}}, h) - sh] + \frac1{100} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\alpha > 0$ is a scaling factor that controls the relative importance of the preference score and safety margin, and makes the policy safe to train and increase stability when increasing $\alpha$.
