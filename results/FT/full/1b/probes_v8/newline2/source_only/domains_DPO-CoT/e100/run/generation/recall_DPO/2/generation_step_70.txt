The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a principled way to optimize a language model's reward function, which can be anything that depends on the model's output to reward high-quality output and penalize low-quality output. This is important because modern language models are trained using methods that optimize a reward defined over the language model's output that includes rewards for matching the reward function isomorphic properties with tasks such as sentiment modeling or machine translation. Rather than attempting to understand and adapt this arbitrary reward function, the paper proposes a method for optimizing a directly-optimized reward function isomorphically, ensuring that the model produces quality output consistent with human preferences. The paper applies its method to both GPT-3 and OpenAI's latest language model, achieving state-of-the-art results on several preference benchmarking tasks. The paper is the second in a series of two papers from the PPO-PLM team; the first paper in the series, "Direct Preference Optimization: Training Language Models with Optimization Algorithms," was published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences in July 2022.

What's Next?
The PPO-PLM framework presented in this paper opens up a number of exciting directions for research. One line of inquiry is to explore how different reward functions affect model behavior. For example, do reward functions measuring diversity or bias produce models with different characteristics? Another line of inquiry is to apply the PPO-PLMs framework to other tasks besides preference optimization. For example, could the PPO-PLLM framework be applied to reinforcement learning tasks such as policy optimization or object detection to help practitioners align their models with human preferences in those domains? A final line of inquiry is to extend the PPO-PLLMs framework to other training algorithms beyond stochastic optimization. For example, can the PPO-PLML framework be applied to training algorithms such as Monte Carlo Tree Search that do not use stochastic gradient descent?
