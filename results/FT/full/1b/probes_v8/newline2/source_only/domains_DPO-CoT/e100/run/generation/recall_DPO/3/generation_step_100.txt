\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}

\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward function estimation algorithm followed by a standard RL optimization. Existing methods for RLHF optimize a policy learned from a parametric reward model parametrized by hyperparameters that are typically fine-tuned separately. In this paper, we make the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt]
    \item We propose a new reward model for RLHF that is easier to optimize. Specifically, we express the reward as a sum of terms, each term being a function of the LM distribution over word sequences only. We then present a way to express the optimal policy to maximize the sum of the reward terms in closed form. 
    This allows us to optimize the reward directly using standard methods, without sampling from the LM or performing significant hyperparameter optimization. 
    \item We evaluate several off-the-shelf RLHF hyperparameter optimization algorithms on the same datasets and find that they vary substantially in their ability to produce high-quality fine-tuned LMs. Surprisingly, despite differences in hyperparameter optimization strategy, all algorithms produce policies that systematically underperform an equivalent policy learned from our in-born, or "native," reward function. To address this, we develop a simple approach, DPO with In-Born Reward Optimization (DPO-IRO), that fine-tunes an RLHF policy with standard RL algorithms such as PPO to optimize the native reward function. 
    Experiments show that DPO-IRO achieves high sentiment and response quality without fine-tuning, or even sampling from the LM, from the initialization of most existing methods. 
\end{itemize}
Our experiments show that D PO can fine-tune L M S to align with human preferences equally well, or better than, existing methods under the same complexity constraints. Notably, D PO -finetuning policies produced surpass those of PPO-based RLHF for sentiment analysis and dialogue, or surpass the state of the art for sentiment analysis and summarization while being simpler to implement.

I Introduction

Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance in a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (Bowman et al.2018; Devlin et al.2019; Brown et al.2020; Wu et al.2021); generation (Peters et al.2022); and generation (Liu et al.2023). However, these models are trained on very large datasets of unlabelled text, making them effectively unsupervised but poorly aligned with human preferences. In order to use these models for downstream tasks, we must align them with our preferences before fine-tuning them with reinforcement learning (RL). Existing approaches align the LM with human preferences using either supervised or unsupervised RL (Sutton et al.2000; McCallum2003; LeCun et al.2012; Schwenk et al.2015; Stiennon et al.2017; Korbak et al.201 eighth 2019; Brown et al.2020; Devlin et  al.2020; Li et  al.2021; Ondrejczuk et  al.2022; Gao et  al.2023); or unsupervised RL  (Gao et  al. 2023; Liu et  al.202 2; Park et al.202 2; Wang et al.202 . The latter method learns from the quality of model generations rather than human preferences directly, but requires access to human evaluations of model quality. We refer to this approach as language quality estimation (LQE) (Liu et  al. 2019; Gao et al.2024; Park et al .2022).

This paper makes the following contributions:
\textbf{1) We develop a new unsupervised language alignment algorithm, \textit{Direct preference optimization} (DPO), which does not require access to human evaluations of LM generations. Instead, DPO aligns models with human preferences using only RL. Our key insight is to express the reward function to be optimized in terms of the LM distribution over generations, rather than the ground truth highest-quality generation for which a reward can be easily defined. This leads to a reward function that is easier to optimize using standard RL algorithms, such as reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF) (Sutton et  al.2000; McCalum2003; Le Cun et al.2002; Schwenk 2010; Stiennon et al.2012; Korbak et al.2018; Brown et al.2019; Devlin et  au 2020; Li et al.2021;Ondrejczuk2021; Gao et al.2024; Parket al.2022; Wang etal.2022;).
