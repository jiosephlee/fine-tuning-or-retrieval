\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including natural language understanding (e.g., BERT [Devlin et al., 2018], GPT [Brown et al., 2019], and XLNet [Yang et al., 2017]), machine translation (e.g., GPT-2 [Radford et al., 2015], and GPT-3 [O'Neil et al., 2020]), and question-answering (e.g., OpenAI's GPT-3 [Brown et al., , 2020). However, the ability to control the behavior of these models is limited by their completely unsupervised training. While it is possible to collect human feedback on model generations, this is a labor-intensive process that is not scalable to large models. Moreover, the human preferences are often not well-defined, and existing methods for collecting them (e.g., PPO-based RLHF [Chen et al., 2021]) require significant hyperparameter tuning and are unstable, requiring multiple runs to converge.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach for gaining steerability of LMs that does not require human feedback or RLHF. Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is based on the observation that the reward model learned during unsupervised LM training can be used to optimize the LM's behavior. Specifically, we parameterize the reward model as a function of the LM's parameters, and use a simple binary cross-entropymodel to optimize the LM's parameters to maximize the reward. This is similar to the way that RLHF works, but instead of optimizing the reward model to match human preferences, we optimize the LM's parameters directly to maximize the reward. We refer to this optimization as \textit{Direct Optimization} (DPO).

We evaluate DPO on several tasks, including sentiment alignment, summarization and single-turn dialog. On sentiment alignment, DPO is shown to be at least as effective as existing methods that optimize the reward model, and in some cases outperforms them. On summarization, DPO is shown empirically to be more effective than PPO-based RLHF at controlling the sentiment of generations, and also outperforms PPO-based RLHF on other metrics. On single-turn dialog, DPO is shown experimentally to be more effective than existing methods at controlling the quality of responses.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt]
    \item We introduce a new parameterization for the reward model in RL, which enables extraction of the optimal DPO policy in closed form.
    \item We show that this new parameterization allows us to solve the DPO problem with only an easy-to-learn binary cross-entropy loss.
    \item Our experiments show that D PO is stable, performant (in terms of convergence speed and final performance), and computationally lightweight, compared to existing methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Large-scale unsupervision of language models has led to impressive performance gains on a wide range of language-related tasks. In this section, we review recent work on unsupervised language modeling, and then discuss methods for steering LMs.

\textbf{Unsupervised Language Modeling.} Large-scale unsupervised LM training has been achieved using a variety of approaches. The earliest methods were based on the idea of maximizing the likelihood of a fixed corpus of text and training the model on the data [Devlin et al. 2018; Brown et al. 2019]. More recently, methods have been developed that optimize the cross-entropy loss between the LM's output and the target distribution [Radford et al. 2015; Yang et al. 2017; O'Neil et al. 2020]. These methods typically use a large number of training examples, and are often finetuned on a large downstream task such as machine translation or question-answering. A common approach is to use a pre-trained LM as a base model, and then finetune it on a downstream task using a loss function that encourages the LM to produce high-quality outputs [Brown et al. 2016; Devlin et al. 2021; Radford et al. 2022].
