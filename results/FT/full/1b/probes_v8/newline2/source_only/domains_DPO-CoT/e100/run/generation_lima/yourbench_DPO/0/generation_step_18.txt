4.2.3. The DPO Algorithm

The DPO algorithm is a gradient descent algorithm with a dynamic importance weight update. It is formulated as follows:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:dpol-algo}
\max_{w} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \beta_i y_i \log \frac{w_i}{1-w_i}
\end{equation}

where $y_i$ is the label for sample $i$, $\beta_i$ is the importance weight for feature $i$, and $N$ is the number of samples in the dataset. The importance weight $\beta_i$ is initialized to 1 for all features, and is updated at every iteration using the following equation:

\begin{equations} \label{eq:dpo-update}
\beta_i = \beta_i * \frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_j} + (1-\beta_i)*\frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{DPO}}(w)}
\end{equations}

where $\sigma_{\text{dpo}}$ is the standard deviation of the importance weights computed from the importance weights of the previous iteration, and $\beta_i$ is a scalar value between 0 and 1. The larger $\beta_i$ is, the more important the feature is to the model, and the smaller $\beta_i$ is (and the lower the importance weight), the less important the feature is to model. The importance weight $\sigma_{\text {DPO}}(w)$ is computed as follows:

\begin {equation} \label {eq:dsigma}
\sigma_{\text {dpo}}(w) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1, j=1}^{N}(w_i - y_i)^2}
\end{equations} 

The importance weight $\beta_i$, which is updated at every iteration, is a scalar value between zero and one. The larger $\beta$ is, the more importance the feature has to the model, and vice versa. The importance weight $\bar{\beta}$ is computed as follows:

$\bar{\beta} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}\beta_i}{\sum_{i=1}{\beta_i}}$

$\max_{w} \beta_i y_{i} \log \frac{ w_i}{1-w_i}$

$\sum_{i=1 }^{N} w_i = 1$

$\sum_{i= 1}^{N} w_i x_{i} = 1$
