Introduction
The ability to generate fluent and relevant text is a key capability of natural language generation (NLG) systems. However, current NLG systems are limited in their ability to generate fluent text that aligns with human preferences. In this paper, we study the problem of aligning language models with user preferences. Our goal is to develop a method for automatically generating text that aligns with the preferences of a given user. This aligns with the broader goal of improving the quality of language models, which is a key component of many applications such as conversational agents, summarization, and question answering.
Existing approaches to aligning language models with preferences typically involve training a separate model to predict preferences, and then fine-tuning the language model to maximize the similarity between the language model's output and the preferences model's predictions. However, this approach requires a separate model to be trained for each user, which is computationally expensive and limits the scalability of the approach. In addition, the preferences model is often trained using a large dataset of human preferences, which may not be available for the specific users of interest. In this paper, our goal is to develop a single model that can be used to align language models with preferences for any user. To achieve this goal, we propose Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), a new method for optimizing the alignment between language models and preferences. DPO is an endto-end optimization procedure that learns to map from language to preferences using a neural network. DPO is simple and straightforward to implement, and can be used with a wide range of language models. We evaluate DPO using two datasets of human preferences, the MovieLens 100k dataset and the Amazon product reviews dataset. On both datasets DPO outperforms state-of-the-art methods by a large margin.
In summary, our main contributions are: • A new method for aligning language with preferences, called Direct Preference Optimiza tion (DPO). D PO is simple to implement and does not require a separate preferences model. DPO can be used with any existing language model, and we evaluate DPO on two large datasets of human preferences. DPO outperforms all existing methods on both datasets by a large margin. • An evaluation of the effectiveness of DPO on downstream tasks such as sentiment and question answering. We find that DPO can improve the performance of language model on downstream tasks, and that DPO can be applied to improve the performance of downstream tasks such as sentiment classification and question answering.

Related Work
Language Models. Recent advances in language modeling have led to large language models that can generate fluent text on their own (Devlin et al., 2019;Radford et al., 2018;Sutskever et al., 2014). These models are trained using large datasets of text and have achieved impressive results on a variety of downstream tasks (Brown et al., 2017;Peters et al., 2016;Radford et al. 2018;Sanh et al., 2015;Wu et al., 2013). However, current language models are not able to generate fluent text that is aligned with human preferences. In addition, the performance of language models is limited by the size of the training dataset, and the ability to fine-tune language models on downstream tasks is limited.
Preference Models. Existing methods for aligning language with human preferences typically involve training a model to predict preferences, then fine-tuning the model to maximize the similarity of the language model's output to the preferences model's predictions (Bai et al., 2012;Chen et al., 2011;Kiros et al., 2010;Ranzato et al., 20145). However, this approach requires training a separate model for each user, which can be computationally expensive and limits scalability. In addition, the preference model is often trained using large datasets of human preferences, which are not always available for the specific users that are of interest. In this work, we develop a single model for aligning language with user preferences, which can be used for any user.
