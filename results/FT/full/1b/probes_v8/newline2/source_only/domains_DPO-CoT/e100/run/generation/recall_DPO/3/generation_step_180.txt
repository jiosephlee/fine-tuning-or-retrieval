\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. \section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, including question answering (Devlin et al., 2019; Radford et al., 2018), machine translation (Brown et al., 2017), and summarization (Kiros et al., 2015). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. While it is possible to collect human feedback on the quality of model generations (Bai et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022), this is a labor-intensive process that is not scalable to large models. Moreover, even if we could collect enough human feedback to train a model that perfectly aligns with our preferences, the resulting model would still be unsupervised and would not generalize well to new tasks.

To address this challenge, we propose a new method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models to align with human preferences. Our method, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), does not require any human feedback or reinforcement learning. Instead, it learns to optimize a reward function directly, using only a binary cross-entropy loss. The key insight behind DPO is that the reward function can be reparameterized in a way that allows us to extract the optimal policy from the reward function exactly, without needing to sample from the model or tune hyperparameters. We describe this in more detail in Section \ref{sec:reward_model}. We empirically evaluate DPO on sentiment alignment, summarizing, and single-turn dialogue, showing that it can align with human preferences as accurately or better than existing methods, while being substantially simpler to train and faster to run.
