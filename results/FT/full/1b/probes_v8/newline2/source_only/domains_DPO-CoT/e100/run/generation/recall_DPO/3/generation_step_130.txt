\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Existing methods for RLHF use a reward model that is learned from human preferences, and fine-tune the LM using reinforcement learning to optimize the reward function without drifting too far from its original value.  However, RLHF is a complicated procedure, and the reward model must be carefully tuned to avoid overfitting to the human preferences used to train it. \section{Introduction}
Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on many downstream tasks, including machine translation (Brown et al., 2018; Cho et al., 2017; Devlin et al., 2019), question answering (Radford et al., 2015; Brown et al., 2016; Devlin et al. 2019), and summarization (Devlin et al., 2020). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature.  While large-scale LMs can be trained to perform a variety of tasks, they do not have any explicit goals or objectives, and their behavior is largely determined by the training data they are exposed to.  This makes it difficult to fine-tune LMs for specific downstream tasks, as the resulting model will likely perform poorly on tasks other than the one for which it was trained.  Moreover, even if a task-specific LM is obtained, it may not be possible to control its behavior precisely, as the model's internal dynamics are largely unknown and difficult to predict.

To address these challenges, several methods have been proposed for fine-tuning LLMs to align with human preferences.  These methods typically involve collecting human preferences for a set of model generations, and then fine-tune the LM to maximize the human preferences using reinforcement learning (RL).  The most common approach is to fit a reward model to the preferences, and then fine tune the LM using RL to maximize the reward function (Kulkarni et al., 2014; Li et al., 2013; Radford et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022).  However, fitting a reward model to human preferences is a non-convex optimization problem, and existing methods typically require extensive hyperparameter tuning to achieve satisfactory performance.  Moreover, the reward model must be careful not to overfit to the human preferences used in its training, as this could cause the fine-tuned LM to perform poorly on tasks other that the one for which it is trained.  In this paper we present a new parameterized reward model for RLHF that enables us to solve the standard reinforcement learning problem with only a simple binary classification loss.  Our method, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), is simple to implement and train, and does not require any hyperparameter tuning.  We evaluate DPO on several downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis (Sutskever et al., 2011), summarization (Brown et al., , 2016, and single-turn dialogue (Sutskever and Williams, 2014).  Our experiments show that, compared to existing methods, DPO is more stable, faster to train, and produces better results on downstream tasks.
