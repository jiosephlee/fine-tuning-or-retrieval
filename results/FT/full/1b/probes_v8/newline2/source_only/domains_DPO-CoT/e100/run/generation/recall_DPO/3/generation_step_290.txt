\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. This allows us to solve the standard DPO problem with only a simple binary cross-entropy loss, eliminating the need for RLHF sampling or hyperparameter tuning. Our experiments show that DPO is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, outperforming existing methods on sentiment alignment, summarization, and single-turn dialogue.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, including question answering (Devlin et al., 2019; Radford et al., 2018), machine translation (Brown et al., 2017), and summarization (Kiros et al., 2015). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. While it is possible to collect human feedback on the quality of model generations (Bai et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022), this is a labor-intensive process, and the resulting feedback is not always reliable (Bai et al. 2020; Li and Bansal, 2021; Wu and Bansal, 2019). Moreover, even if the feedback is reliable, it is often difficult to find a model that aligns with it, since the model's parameters are typically very large and the reward function is complex.

To address these challenges, we propose a new method for gaining steerability of large unsupervised language models, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the observation that the reward function learned by an unsupervised LM can be reparameterized as a function of the language model's parameters, and that the optimal policy for this function can be found analytically. We call this the \textit{reward model} of the language model. Existing methods for steering LMs use RLHF to optimize the reward model to a desired preference distribution, and then fine-tune the language model using reinforcement learning to maximize the reward without drifting too far away from the original model. However, RLHF is a complicated procedure that requires sampling from the LM, and often involves tuning hyperparameters, making it difficult to implement and unstable. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for optimizing the reward model that does not require sampling from the LM or tuning hyperparameters.

We start by defining the standard DPO problem. Given a preference distribution $p_{\theta}(r|x)$, where $r$ is the reward generated by the language model $x$ and $\theta$ is a parameterization of the reward function, we want to find the policy $\pi_\theta^*$ that maximizes the expected discounted reward
\begin{equation}
    \max_{\pi_\theta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},r\sim p_{\theta}(r|x)}\left[\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma^{t}r_{\pi_\theta}(x_t)\right].
\end{equation}
This is a standard reinforcement learning (RL) problem, but one with a complex reward function. The key insight of DPO is to parameterize the reward function using a neural network, and then find the policy that maximizes the expected value of the output of this network. This is equivalent to finding the optimal policy for the function $r(x) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta \sim \mathcal{D}}[ \log \pi_\theta(x)]$, which is known to have a closed-form solution (see Appendix A for details). We refer to this policy as the \textit{optimal policy} of the reward model. The optimal policy can be used to generate samples from the preference distribution $p_{oldar}(r|x)$, and can also be used to optimize the language model directly using RL. However, finding the optimal policy analytically is computationally expensive, so we instead optimize a simpler loss function that approximates the optimal policy.
