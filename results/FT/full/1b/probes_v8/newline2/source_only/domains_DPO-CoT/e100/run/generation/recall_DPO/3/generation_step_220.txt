\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In the standard RLHF setting, one first learns a reward model $R_{\theta}(p)$ that maps from the latent policy space $p$ to a scalar representing the reward obtained by following it, and then uses an RL algorithm to optimize the parameters $\theta$ to maximize the expected discounted reward $R_{\theta}^\pi = \mathbb{E}_{p \sim \mathcal{D}}\left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^{t} R_{\theta}(p \mid \hat{p}(t)) \right]$ where $\hat{p}(t) = \int p \, \mathrm{d} \tilde{p}(t \mid \theta)$ is the policy learned from the data distribution $\mathcal{D}$, i.e., the LM's output distribution. The RLHF objective is equivalent to maximizing the likelihood of the data under the assumption that the reward model $R_{old}$ is correct, but without access to ground-truth rewards.  In practice, one must instead estimate the reward model from the data, typically using maximum likelihood or some other method. Once the reward model is estimated, one can use an RL algorithm to optimize its parameters to maximize the estimated reward. This is the standard RLHF procedure.

A key challenge in RLHF is finding an appropriate reward model $R_{m th}$. Existing methods typically find the reward model by maximizing the likelihood of the training data under the assumption that it is correct, but without ground-truth rewards. This is a challenging optimization problem, and existing methods typically find the reward function by sampling from the LM and optimizing the resulting model. Alternatively, one can use a prior on the reward function, such as a neural network with a fixed architecture and learnable weights. However, the reward model learned in this way is not guaranteed to be optimal, and may not generalize well to unseen data. In this paper, we propose a new approach to finding the reward model for RLHF, which we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Our idea is to directly optimize the reward model parameters to maximize the preference ratings of the human annotators, without sampling from the LM or using a prior on the reward model. We show that the reward model parameters that maximize the preference ratings are the same as the parameters that maximize the expected discounted reward under the true reward function. Therefore, we can use the standard RL algorithm to optimize the reward model parameters directly to maximize the expected reward, without sampling from the model or using a prior on it. We refer to this algorithm as DPO. We theoretically analyze the performance of DPO and show that it achieves the same level of reward maximization as existing methods that optimize the reward model parameters using sampling or a prior, but without the sampling or prior steps. We also empirically evaluate DPO on several tasks, showing that it is both simple to implement and performant.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models learn broad world knowledge and reasoning skills, but achieving precise control of their behaviors is difficult due to the complete unsupervised nature of the training data. Existing methods for gaining steerability include collecting human preferences of the relative quality of different model generations, and fine-tuning the unsupervised LM using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. While RLHF is a powerful technique for learning to control the behavior of large language models, it is a complex procedure that requires collecting human preferences of the quality of model generations, and then fine-tunning the unsupervised LM with reinforcement learning to maximize the estimated reward without drifting too much from the original model. In this paper, our goal is to develop a simple and effective method for fine-tuning LMs using DPO, which we describe next.
