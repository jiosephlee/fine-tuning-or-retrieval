\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In this paper, we present a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. This allows us to solve the standard DPO problem with only a simple binary cross-entropy loss, eliminating the need for RLHF sampling or hyperparameter tuning. \section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, including question answering (Devlin et al., 2019; Radford et al., 2018), machine translation (Brown et al., 2017), and summarization (Kiros et al., 2015). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is challenging due to their completely unsupervised nature. While it is possible to collect human feedback on the quality of model generations (Bai et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022), this is a labor-intensive process, and the resulting feedback is not always reliable (Bai et al. 2020; Li and Bansal, 2021; Wu and Bansal, 2019). Moreover, collecting feedback from many humans is prohibitively expensive, and even when feasible, the resulting feedback is often noisy (Bai et al, 2020).

To address this challenge, we propose a new method for gaining steerability of large unsupervised language models, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is based on the observation that the large unsupervised language model can be viewed as a reward model for a task, where the goal is to maximize the reward. In particular, we consider the task of aligning the sentiment of model generations with human preferences, and show that the optimal policy for this task can be obtained by optimizing a certain reward function with a standard binary cross-entropy loss. We refer to this approach as \textit{reward model-free RLHF}, or \textit{DPO} for short. The key insight of DPO is that the reward function can be represented in a compact way, allowing us to solve a standard RLHF problem with a simple binary cross-entropymodel-free reward model. We refer to this model-free RLHF algorithm as \textit{D PO}. We evaluate DPO on sentiment alignment, summarizing, and single-turn dialogue, showing that it is both stable and performant, and outperforms existing methods on all tasks.
