\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

In this paper, we propose a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. This allows us to solve the standard DPO problem with only a simple binary cross-entropy loss, eliminating the need for RLHF sampling or hyperparameter tuning. \section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models have achieved state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, including question answering (Devlin et al., 2019; Radford et al., 2018), machine translation (Brown et al., 2017), and summarization (Kiros et al., 2015). However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. While it is possible to collect human feedback on the quality of model generations (Bai et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022), this is a labor-intensive process, and the resulting reward functions are often not interpretable. Moreover, the reward function must be learned from scratch for each model, making it difficult to tune the reward function to achieve high-quality generations for a given task.

To address these challenges, we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for fine-tuning large unsupervised language models to align with human preferences. DPO does not require any human feedback, and instead learns a reward function from scratch for a given task. The reward function is learned using reinforcement learning (RL), but unlike existing methods (Bai et al. (2020); Li et al. (2021); Wu et al. (2022)), DPO does not require sampling from the language model during training. Instead, DPO uses a simple binary cross-entropyloss to optimize the parameters of a pre-trained language model, effectively learning a reward function from the data. We refer to this approach as reward model-free RLHF (RRLHF).

DPO has several advantages over existing methods. First, it does not require any human labeling, making it more scalable than existing methods. Second, it does not require sampling from a language model during training, which can be computationally expensive and can lead to unstable results. Third, it does not require tuning hyperparameters, which can be difficult to get right for large models. Finally, DPO can be used with any pre-trained language model, making it applicable to a wide range of tasks.

We evaluate DPO on sentiment alignment, summariztion, and single-turn dialogue. For sentiment alignment, we compare DPO to two baselines: PPO-based RLHF (Li et al., 2023), which uses a reward model learned from human feedback to optimize the unsupervised LM, and a baseline that simply optimizes the unsupervised LM without a reward function. We find that DPO produces generations with higher sentiment alignment scores than PPO-based RLHF, and also produces generations with higher quality sentiment than the baseline. For summarization, we evaluate DPO against a baseline that optimizes the unsupervisd LM using RL with a reward function learned from human feedback. We find that D PO produces summaries with higher quality than the reward function-based baseline, and also produces summaries with higher quality sentiment than PPO-based RL HF. Finally, for single-turn dialogue, we evaluate DPO and a reward function-based baseline against a baseline that optimizesthe unsupervised LM using RL with a learned reward function. We find DPO produces generations with better quality responses than the reward function-based basline, and also produces generations that are more aligned with human preferences than the reward function-based method.
