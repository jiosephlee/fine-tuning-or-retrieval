\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward function estimation algorithm coupled with a simple classification loss. In this paper we show how to use DPO to perform RLHF, obtaining an algorithm that fine-tunes a large unsupervised LM to match human preferences without sampling from the LM or performing significant hyperparameter optimization. Our main contribution is a method for performing RLHF with DPO, which we name Direct Preference Optimization (DPO-LHF). We evaluate various preference modeling algorithms in this setting and find that the loss function estimated by DPO gives the best balance between alignment quality and efficiency. We perform extensive experiments on multiple large-scale LMs, showing that DPO-LHF can achieve matching or better sentiment and response quality from RLHF, while being substantially simpler to train and more robust than existing methods.

\section{Related Work} Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training algorithms have been popularized by Paque et al. (2020); Sutskever et al. (2021) and have since become the standard way to train language models. While these algorithms produce state-of-the-art performance on tasks such as machine translation and question-answering, they are trained using an unsupervised objective that is completely unsuitable for training on. Existing work can be broadly divided into two categories: methods that modify the Paque/Sutskever objective to introduce human preferences, and methods that modify the language model to have certain desired properties before training. The former include methods such as Cross-Entropy with Silence (XEN; Brown et al. 2020) and GPT-N3 (GPT-N3; Weston et al. 2021), which modify the Paque/Sutkever objective by adding a term penalizing sentences that are too different from human silence durations, and AutoGPT, which does not modify the traditional cross-entropy loss but instead fine-tunes a pre-trained language model to generate human-comparable answers (Goodfellow et al. 2016). The latter category includes methods such as PPO-based GPT-3 large-scale language model expansion (GPT-3LS; Schwenk et al. 2022) and language models that can directly operate in a reinforcement learning setting (Hendrycks and Gpt-3 2021; GPT-4; Richards et al. 2023), though these generally do not have human evaluation metrics as their objective. Most of these methods optimize either the parameters of the language model or the generation quality objective, and therefore also optimize the stability and controllability of the model, as well as its generalization ability. However, the majority of these methods require fine-tuning the language model on a dataset of human demonstrations of desired behaviors before training, a process that is both time-consuming and prone to overfitting.

In this paper we explore methods for performing RL without human demonstrations, known as RL without Human Demonstrations (RLHF). One approach to RLHF is to fit an RL reward model to human preferences collected via crowdsourcing (Korbak et al. 202 2; Korbak et al. (2022); Stiennon et al. (2023)), and then use a standard RL algorithm, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO; Williams et al. (2016)), to optimize the estimated reward function. This approach has two main advantages: (1) it uses existing RL algorithms that are already well-studied and understood, and (2) it allows the human preferences to be from a different dataset than the training set, reducing the risk of overfitting. However, the RLHF procedure is complicated by two factors: (1) the RL algorithm must be fit to the specific preferences dataset used for fine-tuning, and (2) the RLHF procedure must be repeated for every new training set of preferences, making it impractical to scale to datasets with thousands of samples. In this paper we explore the use of reinforcement learning to directly optimize the unsupervised language model, without fitting a separate reward model. We propose a class of algorithms that we call RLDirect Preference Optimization (D PO-LHF), which we will describe in detail in Section \ref{sec:dpo_lhf}. Our experiments show that DPO-LHF produces models with similar or better performance than existing methods while being substantially simpler to deploy.
