\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model fine-tuning algorithm. Existing methods for RLHF optimize a reinforcement learning (RL) objective that maps from the parameters of a large unsupervised LM to a reward function that humans prefer. The goal is to fine-tune the LM to maximize this reward without drifting too far away from the original model. This is a challenging optimization problem because the reward function is unknown, the LM parameters are large, and the reward is only available after the generation of each policy. We propose a new parameterization of this reward function that allows us to express the optimal policy for the reward as a simple linear combination of the LM parameters. Using this expression, we can efficiently optimize the reward using a standard classification loss, giving us an algorithm we call Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Experiments on multiple large-scale language models show that DPO produces policies that are as good or better than existing methods for RLHF, but is substantially simpler to implement and easier to train.

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models learn to generate fluent text on their own, but achieving precise control of their output behavior is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. Existing methods for gaining steerability typically involve collecting human preferences for model generations and fine-tuning the unsupervised LM using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Karras et al., 2019; Li et al., 2020a; Brown et al., 2021; Ondreicka et al., 2022). RLHF works by first fitting a reward model to the preferences of a small number of human raters, and then fine-tunes the unsupervised LM with reinforcement learning to maximize the estimated reward without drifting too much from the original model. However, RLHF is a complicated procedure that involves sampling from the LM, performing RL, and fine-tuning the model, making it difficult to tune and prone to instability. In this paper, we introduce a new method for RLHF that avoids sampling from the LM during the fine-tuning phase, greatly simplifying the algorithm while still producing high-quality fine-tuned models.

Our key insight is that the reward model used in RLHF can be expressed as a simple linear combination (the \textit{reward parameterization}) of the LM's parameters. With this parameterization, we can express the optimal policy for maximizing the reward as a simple function of the LM parameters, giving us our \textit{Direct Preferred Optimization} algorithm (DPO). DPO is a straightforward classification loss that can be optimized using standard methods, such as backpropagation through time (BPTT) or a supervised learning approach like PPO (Schulman et al., 2017). We evaluate several different reward parameterization choices and find that the reward parameterization proposed by Ondreicka (2022) performs best for DPO, yielding higher quality fine-tuned models with a simpler algorithm. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt]
    \item We introduce a new parameterization for the reward function in RLHF that allows us to express its optimal policy in closed form.
    \item We propose Direct Preference Optimization (D PO), an algorithm that fine-tunes an unsupervised LM using only a simple classification loss, without sampling from the LM or performing RL.
    \item Our experiments show that DPO produces high-quality fine-tuned policies for a variety of large language models, outperforming existing methods for RLHF on sentiment and summarization, and matching or improving performance on single-turn dialogue.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Language modeling has seen tremendous progress with the advent of large-scale unsupervised training datasets, such as OpenAI's GPT-NB10K (Brown et al., 202 1) and BERT (Devlin et al., 2018). However, achieving precise control of the output of these models is difficult due to their unsupervised training and the lack of human preferences for their output. Existing methods for gaining steering of language models fall into two categories: those that fit a reward model to human preferences, and those that use reinforcement learning to directly optimize the model parameters.
