\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reward model-free RLHF algorithm. In the standard RLHF setting, one first learns a reward model $R_{\theta}(p)$ that maps from the latent policy space $p$ to a scalar representing the reward obtained by following it, and then uses an optimization algorithm to fine-tune the parameters $\theta$ of a large unsupervised LM $P$ to maximize the expected discounted reward $R_{\theta}^{\pi}(p)$, where $\pi$ is the underlying policy learned from $p$ by an RL algorithm such as PPO or REINFORCE. The reward model $\theta$ is typically learned using reinforcement learning from human feedback, but can also be learned from data or estimated from a prior distribution on the latent space of $P$.
The standard RLHF procedure is as follows: 1) Sample a policy $\pi$ from $P$; 2) Use the reward model $\theta$ to estimate the reward received by following $\pi$; 3) Update $\theta$ to maximize the estimated reward; 4) Repeat steps 1-3 many times to get many samples from the learned policy. This procedure is illustrated in Figure 1.

The standard RLHF procedure has two main challenges. First, the reward model $\theta$, which is learned from human feedback or data, is typically a complicated function of the latent policy $p$ and is therefore difficult to optimize. Second, the reward model is sampled from the same distribution as the policy $p$, so the reward model is not necessarily a good approximation of the true reward function $R$ that the LM should optimize. These challenges are illustrated in Figure 2. In this paper, we propose a new method for solving the standard RLHF problem, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). In contrast to existing methods, DPO does not use a reward model at all. Instead, DPO directly optimizes the true reward function $ R$ of the LM, using a simple classification loss. This loss is equivalent to maximizing the likelihood of the reward function under the policy distribution, but avoids the difficulties associated with learning a complicated reward model. We prove that the optimal solution to the DPO loss is the same as the optimal solution to the standard RLHF loss, up to a constant factor. Therefore, DPO is equivalent to the standard RLHF algorithm, but with a simpler reward model that can be learned from data or obtained heuristically. We show that DPO can be efficiently implemented using a simple binary cross-entropy loss, and empirically outperforms existing methods for fine-tuning unsupervised LMs to align with user preferences.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}
In this section, we introduce the Direct Preference Optimization (D PO) algorithm for solving the standard RL HF problem. As we will see, DPO can be viewed as a special case of a more general family of algorithms for optimizing the reward function of a large unsupervisd model, which we call \emph{reward function optimization} (RFO). We begin by defining the reward function optimization problem, and then describe the DPO algorithm in detail.
