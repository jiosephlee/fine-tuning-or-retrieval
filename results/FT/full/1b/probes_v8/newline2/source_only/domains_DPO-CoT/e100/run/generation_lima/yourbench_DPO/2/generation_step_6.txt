The ethical issues surrounding DPO
The ethical issues surrounding D PO can be broadly categorized into two groups: those related to the collection of user data and those related to the use of the resulting models. In this section, we will focus on the first group of issues, namely those related to the collection and use of user data. As we will see, these issues are not limited to the particularities of DPO, but rather pertain to the collection of any type of data about individuals, and therefore require careful consideration by researchers and data collectors alike.

Data collection
The first and most obvious ethical issue associated with DPO is the collection of user data. As mentioned above, the ubiquity of user data generated by online platforms makes DPO a practically unavoidable practice. However, the collection of user data for the purpose of DPO raises several concerns about privacy and discrimination. In this section, our focus will be on privacy, although similar concerns about discrimination can be raised with respect to gender, race, or any other type of protected characteristic.
2.3.1 Privacy. The primary concern with respect to the collection of user browsing data is privacy. As we have seen, DPO models can predict user preferences with high accuracy using only their browsing data, without the need to collect additional information about the user (e.g., through surveys or purchases). This raises the question of whether it is necessary to collect such detailed information about users if we can predict their preferences using their already-collected data. From a privacy perspective, the answer is clearly no, since collecting data about individuals without their consent and in an indiscriminate manner violates their privacy rights. In this context, it is important to note that the mere existence of a DPO model that does not require additional data collection does not automatically solve the privacy issue. This is because the lack of additional data collection does not affect the fact that the platform has access to a large amount of information about its users, which can be used to train the DPO model. Therefore, the key issue with respect to the privacy of users is not the fact that a platform collects user data, but rather the fact that it does so indiscriminately and without their consent.
2.3.2 Discrimination. Another concern with respect to the use of D PO models is the possibility of discrimination. As we have seen in Section 2.1, DPO models can accurately predict user preferences based only on their browsing data, without any additional information about the user. This raises the question of how accurate these models are in predicting user preferences, and whether they may be used to infer sensitive information about the user, such as their sexual orientation, political views, or disability. If this were the case, the use of DPO could lead to discrimination against the affected groups. For example, a DPO model developed by a company for use on its own services might infer user preferences for certain products or services. If the company were to sell this information to another company that sells products to the LGBT community, the second company could target advertising to LGBT users based on their supposedly preferences, without the users' knowledge or consent.
