The block-diagonal matrix is composed of $r$ block-diagonal matrices, each of which is parameterized by a $d$-dimensional orthogonal matrix. The block size is $b$. The block-diagonal matrix can be written as:

\begin{equation} \begin{split} \mathbf{R} = \text{diag}(\mathbf{R}_1, \cdots, \mathbf{R}_r) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_1\mathbf{O}_1\mathbf {B}_1^\top & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & \cdots & \mathbf{B}_{r}\mathbf{O}_r\mathbf{B}_r^\top \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}, \end{split} \end{equation}

where $\mathbf{B}_i\in \mathbb{C}^{d\times b}$ is a block matrix, and $\mathbf{O}_i\in \text{O}(d)$ is an orthogonal matrix. The block matrix $\mathbf{B}$ is parameterized by $2bd$ parameters. The block-diagonal orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$ is parameterized by $(r-1)bd + d$ parameters. The block size $b$ is a hyper-parameter. The block-diagonal structure is illustrated in Figure 1.

\begin{align} \begin{split}\label{eq:caley} \text{Cay}(z) = \frac{1}{2i}\left( zI - \text{adj}(z)\right), \end{split}\end{align}

where $z$ is a complex number, and $\text{adj}(z)$ is the adjoint of $z$. The Cayley transform is a linear transformation from the complex plane to the skew-symmetric matrix. The inverse Cayley transform is:

\end{split}\end {equations}
