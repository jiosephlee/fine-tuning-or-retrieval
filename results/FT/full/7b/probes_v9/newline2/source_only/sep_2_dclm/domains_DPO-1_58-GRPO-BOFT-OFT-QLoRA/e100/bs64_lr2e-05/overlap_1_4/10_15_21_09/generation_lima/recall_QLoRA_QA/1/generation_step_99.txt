According to the paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs", what is QLoRA?<|EOT|>The paper introduces a method for efficiently finetuning large language models (LLMs) that have been quantized. The method, which they name QLoRA, uses low-rank updates to the weight matrices and adds a low-rank component to the bias matrices. This allows them to use 4-bit weights and 2-bit biases while still achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like MMLU and PiEval-6.2.

Here is a quote from the paper that explains how QLoRA works:

"Quantization of neural networks typically involves representing real-valued weights and biases with integers drawn from a discrete interval. We focus here on binary weights and biases with t-bit precision, where t=2 for biases and can take the value of either 4 or 8 for weights. A key challenge when finetuning LLMs with high bit-precision weights is that the optimal solution for the full-precision model training may not be feasible for a low-precision model. For example, the gradient update step during finetuning, given by $\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{W} - \eta\cdot\nabla_\mathbf{W} J(\mathbf{W})$, becomes non-linear when $\mathbf{W}$ is represented with binary weights, because addition and subtraction of integers is no longer closed. Common workarounds include using biased gradient clipping, or rescaling the weights before gradient update. QLoRA avoids explicit rescaling while still enabling efficient gradient computation during finetuning. Specifically, we first derive the update formula for the full-precision case, and then propose to perform the update with a factorized tensor product of parameter updates of low rank. This yields a computationally efficient update even in the presence of high precision overhead bits. We demonstrate that this approach outperforms the state-of-the-art finetuning method BLOFT on 4-bit finetuning, achieving new state-of-the-art results on English and Chinese language models on both English and Chinese benchmarks."<|endoftext|>
