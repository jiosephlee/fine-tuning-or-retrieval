A Review of Orthogonal Finetunig

Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a popular finetuning method that adapts pretrained language or vision models to downstream tasks. OFT updates the weights of a pretrained model with gradient descent while constraining the weights to be orthogonal, i.e., $W^{(f)}=W^{(p)}\text{Orth}(0,I)$, where $W^{(p)}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $W^{(f)}>W^{(p)} $ is the finetuned weight matrix, $\text{Orth}(0,\cdot)$ is an orthogonal matrix that satisfies $\text{Orth}(O)\text{Orth}^T=O$ and $\text{Orth}(I)=I$, and $I$ is the identity matrix. Orthogonal finetuning has been shown to be effective in many applications, including natural language processing and computer vision.

One limitation of OFT is its high parameter cost. Specifically, OFT has the same number of trainable parameters as the pretrained model, making it difficult to apply OFT to very large models. To address this limitation, several methods have been proposed to make OFT more parameter-efficient, including low-rank OFT, block-diagonal OFT, and sparse OFT. These methods share a common principle: they reduce the number of trainable weights by restricting the weight matrix to have a special structure. For example, low-rank OFT assumes that the weight matrix has a low-rank structure, while block-diagonal OFT assumes that the diagonal blocks of the weight matrix are full matrices and the off-diagonal blocks are zero matrices. Despite their success, these methods are still relatively expensive in terms of the number of trainable parameters.
