2 Preliminaries

2.1 Parameter-Efficient Finetuning

Large pretrained language models are proven to be effective in many natural language understanding tasks. To adapt a pretrained language model to a downstream task, we need to finetune the pretrained model. However, finetuning all the parameters in a large pretrained language model can be expensive. To address this issue, parameter-efficient finetuning methods have been developed, which only finetune a small portion of the model parameters.

The most representative parameter-efficient finetuning approach is called orthogonal finetuning (OFT)[Devlin+17], which orthogonalizes the trainable weight matrix. Formally, given a linear layer $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ with $n$ neurons and $d$ input dimensions, OFT seeks to find a orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{R}^{n\times n}$ and a scalar $\delta \in \mathbbm{R}$, and then computes the finetuned weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{OFT}} = \mathbf{W} + \delta\mathbf{R}$. To prevent the matrix $\mathbf{ R}$ from being identity, a common regularizer is to force $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^T=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}= \mathbf{I}$, where $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix. With this additional constraint, OFT seeks to optimize the following objective:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:oft} \tag{1} \min_{\mathbf{R},\delta}\left\| \mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}_{OF T}\right\|_{F}^2+\gamma\|\mathbf{R}\|_F^2+\beta\|\nabla_{\mathbf{ R},\delta}\left(\| \mathbf{ W}-\mathbf{ W}_{OF T}\|_{F}^ 2\right)\|_2^2, \end{equation} where $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are two regularization hyperparameters, and $\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the Frobenius norm and $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the spectral norm. It's easy to derive that the optimal solution to the above objective is $\mathbf{R}_{\text{O FT}} = \mathbf {I} -2\frac{\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^T}{\gamma}$ and $\delta_{\text{OFT)} = \frac{1}{\gamma}\mathbf{W}\left(\mathbf{I} -\mathbf{R}_{OFT}\right)^{-1}$, which has no trainable parameter. Although OFT has no trainable parameter, it still needs to compute the matrix-vector multiplication of $\mathbf{R}$ at test time, which can be expensive for large $\mathbf{R}. To reduce the computation cost, block-diagonal OFT (BOFT-BD) [Chen+21] divides the orthogonal matrix $\mathbf {R}$ into $b$ block-diagonal matrices, and each matrix only has $d$ non-zero entries.
