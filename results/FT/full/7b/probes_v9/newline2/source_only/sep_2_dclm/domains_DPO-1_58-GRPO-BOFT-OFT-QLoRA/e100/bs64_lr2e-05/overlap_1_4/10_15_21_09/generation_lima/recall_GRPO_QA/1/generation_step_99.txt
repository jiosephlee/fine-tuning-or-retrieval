According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPo) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was proposed by OpenAI in their paper "OpenAI Five: An AI System Built to Play the Competitive Multiplayer Game Dota 2" to train their OpenAI Five system.

In PPO, we optimize the policy $\pi$ by maximizing the following surrogate objective:

$$\mathcal{J}_{PPO}(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[ \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \sim \pi(S)} \min_{a \in A(s)}  \frac{\pi(a | s)}{\pi_\text{old}(a | s)}  \text{max}_{\theta}  \log \frac{\pi_\text{old}{(s, a;\theta)} }{\pi(s, a;\theta)}  - \beta  \text{clip}  \frac{\pi_\pi(s, a;\ theta) - \pi_\text{old} (s, a;\theta) }{\mu(s, a;\theta, \theta_{\text{old}}) } ] $$

where $\pi_\text{old}$ is a copy of the current policy that is not updated, $\mu$ is the mean trajectory entropy w.r.t. $\pi_\text{oldd}$ and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter.

The main challenge with PPO is that it relies on clipping to constrain the policy update. This can be problematic because the clipping value needs to be chosen carefully in advance, as too small a value will cause the policy improvement to be undershooting and too large a value will cause the optimization algorithm to diverge.

GRPO addresses this problem by changing the objective to:

$$\mathcal{T}_{GRPO}(\pi, \pi_{\text{old}}, T) = \mathbb E_{\pi}[ \mathbb E_{s \sim \Pi(S), a \sim \pi(a | s)} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|} \sum_{t=1}^T \underbrace{\left( \frac{\pi_\ Pi(s, a;\theta) - \pi_\pi(s, \tilde{a} ;\theta)}{\mu(s, a; \theta, \theta_{old})}  - c_1 \log \frac{\mu(s, a ; \theta, \theta_\text{old})} {\sigma(s, a; \thetatheta_{\text{old}}} \right)  + \beta  \text {clip}  \frac {\pi_\pi(s,  a;\theta) -  \pi_\text{ol}(s, a;\theta ) } {\mu(s, a;\thetatheta_{old})}  \right )] $$

where $\tilde{a}$ is an action sampled from $\pi_{\text{ol}(a | s)}$, $\Pi$ is the set of all copies of the current policy $\pi$, $\mu$, $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation w.r.t. $\Pi$ of the trajectory loss w.r. to $\pi_{\text {old }}$ and $\theta_{\text {old  }}$ and $c_1$, $c_2$ are hyperparameters.

The key insight behind GRPO is that the clipping term in the PPO objective is actually a function of the current policy $\mu$ and the old policy $\pi_{\text{o}}$. Therefore, instead of fixing $\pi_{\text o}$ when optimizing the policy $\pi$, we can sample many copies of the current policy and use the standard deviation of the trajectory clipping loss w.r.t. these policies as a measure of conservatism.<|endoftext|>
