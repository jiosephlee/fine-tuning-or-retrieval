Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning?

Finetuning is a simple yet powerful training paradigm. It has achieved great success in many applications, such as natural language processing (NLU) and computer vision (CLU). However, finetuning large models is very expensive. Finetuning a 3B parameter model costs about $0.1 per hour on a 32GB GPU. Finetuning a model for a long time is even more expensive. For example, finetuning a 3b GPT-3 model for 10,000 steps on the FLAN dataset costs about $25. The cost becomes even higher if we use more powerful models, such as 70B GPT-4. To address this problem, parameter-efficient finetuning has been actively studied in the community.

What is parameter-efficient finetuning? Parameter-efficient finetuning refers to finetuning a model with much fewer parameters than the base model, while achieving comparable or even better performance. There are mainly two ways to construct a parameter-efficient finetuned model: model compression and knowledge distillation. Model compression refers to compressing a base model into a parameter-efficient finetuned model, such as low-rank approximation, pruning, and quantization. Knowledge distillation refers to training a parameter-efficient student model to mimic the output probability distribution of a large teacher model. In addition to these two mainstream approaches, there is another approach called finetuning methodology, which modifies the optimization algorithm to achieve parameter-efficiency. This paper falls into this category.

What is OFT and BD-OFT? OFT stands for Orthogonal Finetuning. It modifies the weight update rule in the fully connected layer from normal finetuning to:

$\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{W} - \eta \mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}$ (1)

where $\mathbf{W}$ is the weight matrix, $\eta$ is the learning rate, $\mathbf{K}$ is an orthogonal matrix, and $\mathbf{R}$ is a vector-scaled matrix. Equation 1 can be interpreted as adding an orthogonal transformation before rescaling the gradient. OFT has shown great promise in improving the finetuning performance of large models.
