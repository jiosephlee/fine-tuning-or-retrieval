What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences, without explicit reward modeling. It trains a language model and a binary classifier, where the classifier is optimized to predict the label `1` for samples generated by the language model that match a given preference dataset, and `0` for other samples. The language model is only trained with cross-entropy loss, but not penalized for deviating from the preferences.

The key insight behind DPO is that modern large language models have sufficient expressivity to generate samples that perfectly match any given preference dataset. Therefore, it is possible to train the language model to generate samples that match the preference dataset simply by optimizing it to make its generated samples more similar to the preference dataset. This is in contrast to traditional RLHF methods, which assume that the language model cannot perfectly match the preference dataset, and therefore need to regularize the policy to prevent mode collapse.

In practice, DPO achieves good performance on the FLAN benchmark without explicit reward modeling, while traditional RLHF methods require significant tuning of the reward function and typically perform worse than DPO.<|endoftext|>
