The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method based on the orthogonal butterfly structure. BOFT uses a matrix factorization to represent an orthogonal matrix with a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices. The key insight behind BOFT is that the orthogonal butterfly structure can effectively reduce the number of trainable parameters while still representing full-rank orthogonal matrices.

The orthogonal butterfly structure is defined recursively as follows:

* $b_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r_0}$ is a base block.
* $b_{k+1} \in \mathbbbb{R}^{dh_k \times r_{k+1}}$ is a hierarchical block with $h_k = d^{k-1}$, and is given by $b_{k+2} = b_k \in \mathbb {R} ^ {dh_k \times r_k}$ and
$$b_{k+1}= \begin{bmatrix} b_0 & \mathbf{1}_r \mathbf{1}^T_r b_0 & \cdots & \mathbf{ 1}_r (r_{k-1})_r \mathbf{  1}^T_r (r_{k-2})_r b_0 \end{bmatrix},$$ where $\mathbf{1}_n$ denotes a vector of length $n$ with all entries being $1$.

The BOFT matrix of size $d \times d$ is then given by $\text{BOFT}_{r_0,(r_1,r_2,\dots,r_{K-1})}( \Sigma)$ as

$$\text{BOFT}_{d,r_0,(r_2,r_3,\dots,r_{K - 1})} ( \Sigma) = \Sigma_{1,K-1} \cdot \text{BOFT}_{h_{K-1},r_1}( \Sigma_{2,K-1}) \cdot \text{ BOFT}_{h_{K - 2},r_2}(\Sigma_{3,K-2})\cdots\text{BOFT}_{h_1,r_2}( \Sigma_{4,1}), $$ where $\Sigma_{ij}$ denotes a block of $\Sigma$ at the $(i,j)$-th position.

The learning of BOFT is then given by

$$\begin{bmatrix} \nabla b_0 \\ \nabla b_{k+1}  \end{bmatrix} = \Sigma \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{1}{r_k} & \frac{1}{r_{k-1}} & \cdots &\frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{1}{ r_k} (\mathbf{ 1}_{r_k})^T & 1 & \cdots & (\mathbf{  2}_{r_{k-1}})
\end{bmatrix}^T,$$ where $\nabla b_k$ denotes the gradient of $b_k$ and $\Sigma$ is the learning rate matrix.

The paper also proposes two variants of BOFT: block-structured BOFT (BS-BOFT) and low-rank BOFT (LR-BOFT). BS-BOFT replaces the base blocks with block-diagonal matrices, and LR-BOFT further adds a low-rank constraint to the hierarchical blocks.<|endoftext|>
