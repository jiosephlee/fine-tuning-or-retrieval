According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The paper introduces OFT, a simple finetuning method that can prevent knowledge collapse. The key idea of OFT is to encourage orthogonal transformation. More specifically, in each training step, OFT first optimizes the weight using conventional gradient descent based on the loss w.r.t. the unnormalized probability distribution. Then it applies orthogonal transformation to the weight such that the transformed weight has zero correlation with the original weight.

To achieve this, OFT uses the following transformation for the linear layer:

$$\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{Q},$$

where $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{Q}$ are orthogonal matrices of size $(\text{in dims})\times(\text{in dims})$. To ensure orthogonality, they use $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbf{Q}$. This transformation does not affect the norm of the weight $\mathbf{W}$, and thus does not change the output of the network. However, it guarantees that $\text{corr}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{W}}) = 0$ for any function $\hat{\mathbf{w}}(\cdot)$, because $\mathbf{R}\text{cov}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{R}^\top=\text{cov}(\ mathbf{X},\ mathbf{Y}^\top)$ for any two random vectors $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf Y$.

They apply the transformation to all the linear layers in the model, and add a term to the loss function to make sure the transformation is identity during training.<|endoftext|>
