According to the paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs", what is QLoRA?<|EOT|>The paper introduces a method for efficient fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) that have been quantized. The method, which they call QLoRA, uses low-rank updates to avoid wasting the memory required to store the full high-precision model.

Here's an excerpt from the paper that explains how QLoRA works:

We propose a novel fine-tuning method, QLoRA, that leverages low-rank updates to reduce the memory footprint during training. Specifically, QLoRA performs a full high-precision update followed by a low-rank update, i.e., $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \theta + \eta \Phi \Psi^T$, where $\theta$ is the full model parameters, $\theta_{\text{q}} \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ is the quantized model with $n$ parameters, $\eta$ is the learning rate, and $\Phi \in \mathbb {R}^{r\times n}$ and $\Psi \in \mathbb R^{r\times r}$ are low-rank matrices. Intuitively, the low-rank update reuses weights from the quantized model while allowing the update to spread to all levels of the hierarchy during training. We found that adding $\theta$ results in better convergence than only using $\theta - \eta \Phi \ Psi^T$ (Appendix D.2).<|endoftext|>
