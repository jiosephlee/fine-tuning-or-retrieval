According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The paper introduces OFT, a simple finetuning method that can prevent knowledge collapse. The key idea of OFT is to encourage orthogonal transformation. More specifically, in each training step, OFT first optimizes the weight matrix using conventional backpropagation, then transforms the optimized weight matrix to an orthogonal one with householder reflection, and finally reflects the orthogonal transformation to the original space to get the updated weight matrix.

To better understand the concept of orthogonal transformation, we need to know some linear algebra. A square matrix $Q$ is called orthogonal if $Q^T Q = QQ^T = I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix and $Q^T$ is the transpose of $Q$. Intuitively, an orthogonal matrix represents a rotation or reflection in the space. If we multiply an orthogonal matrix to a vector, it will only rotate or reflect the vector without changing its length. This property ensures that the transformed feature distribution still covers the whole space, which prevents the knowledge collapse.

In practice, how do we make a weight matrix orthogonal? The paper uses the following formula to achieve this goal:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x & y \\ y^T & I \end{bmatrix} \mapsto \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{2x+y} & y/\sqrt{2x+y}\\ -y/\sqrt{2x + y} & \sqrt{1 - (2x+y)} \end{bmatrix}
$$

This formula is a householder reflection. It maps a general matrix to an orthogonal matrix. For simplicity, the paper only applies this formula to the weight tensor along the channel dimension.<|endoftext|>
