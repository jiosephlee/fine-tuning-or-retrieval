Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining the same level of generalization performance as OFT. BOFT is an extension of the block-diagonal OFT, which reduces the number of trainable parameters by making the diagonal blocks of the weight matrix smaller. BOFT further reduces the number of trainable parameter by making the off-diagonal blocks zero. BOFT has a parameter efficiency ratio of 1/4 compared to the standard OFT. BOFT also has a faster training speed than the block-diagonal OFTs because it does not need to compute the block-diagonal matrices.

The authors evaluate BOFT on 18 tasks from 5 datasets, including GLUE, Super-Natural Language Inference (SNLI), OpenWebQA, SQuAD v1.1, and SQuAD v2.0. BOFT outperforms the state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning methods, including TinyFN, EfficientFinetune, and BlockDiagOFT, on most tasks. BOFT also has better convergence speed and better stability than other methods. The results show that the butterfly structure can effectively reduce the number of trainable parameter without sacrificing performance.

2 Related Work

This section introduces the related work of parameter-efficient finetuning and orthogonal finetuning.

Parameter-Efficient Finetuning

Parameter-efficient finetuning is a type of finetuning method that reduces the number of trainable model parameters without sacrificing performance. Parameter-efficient finetuning has gained increasing attention in recent years due to its effectiveness in improving model generalization and reducing computational cost. There are mainly two types of parameter-efficient finetunings: knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Romera-Paredes et al., 2016; Song et al., 2018; Li et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020; Su et al., 2021) and low-rank adaptation (Henderson et al., 2017; Lin et al., 201

Low-rank adaptation modifies the weight matrix of the pretrained model with a low-rank matrix. Other parameter-efficient finetunigs include information bottleneck (IB) finetuning (Arjovsky et al., 20177; Frantar et al., 20182; Liao et al., 20203; Zhou et al., 20204), sparse finetuning (Li et al., 20205; Wu et al., 20206), and factorized finetuning (Li & Hoiem, 20207; Li et al., in review).

Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT; Li et al., forthcoming) is a type of parameter-efficient finetune that preserves the pairwise angular similarity of the weights. OFT updates the weight matrix by adding an orthogonal matrix to the pretrained weight matrix. OFT has shown strong performance in improving the generalization of pretrained language models on various downstream tasks. There are mainly two categories of OFTs: full matrix update and block-diagonal update. The full matrix update computes the orthogonal matrix for the entire weight matrix. Examples include original OFT (Li et al., forthcoming) and block-structured OFT (Li et a., in review). The block-diagonal update computes the orthogonal matrix only for the diagonal blocks of the(weight matrix. Examples include block-diagonal OFT (Li et.al., in review) and sparse OFT (Li et. al., 20208).

3 Background

This section introduces some background knowledge, including matrix factorization, orthogonal matrix, and butterfly structure.

Matrix Factorization Matrix factorization is a technique for representing a matrix as a product of two or more matrices. It is widely used in many areas, such as computer graphics, machine learning, and data mining. There are mainly two kinds of matrix factorizations: low-rank matrix factorization and canonical polyadic decomposition. Low-rank matrix factorization represents a matrix as the product of a low-rank matrix and a full-rank matrix. Canonical polyadic decomposition represents a matrix as the sum of r canonically independent matrices.

Orthogonal Matrix An orthogonal matrix is a square matrix whose columns and rows are orthonormal vectors. An orthogonal matrix has some important properties. First, the transpose of an orthogonal matrix is its inverse. Second, the determinant of an orthogonal matrix is either +1 or -1.
