Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT is based on a novel factorization of the finetuning weight matrix that enables a significant reduction in the number of trainable parameters while preserving the ability of OFT to prevent catastrophic forgetting. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, as well as other state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning methods, including GroupNorm-based finetuning and LwF. BOFT also has a faster training speed than OFT and its variants. BOFT introduces a new way to analyze the parameter efficiency of finetuning methods, which reveals hidden inefficiencies in existing methods and provides a principled way to improve parameter efficiency.

3.2 Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal Finetuning is an effective method to finetune pretrained models. It prevents the pretraining weights from being updated, and only updates a small set of orthogonal weight matrices.
