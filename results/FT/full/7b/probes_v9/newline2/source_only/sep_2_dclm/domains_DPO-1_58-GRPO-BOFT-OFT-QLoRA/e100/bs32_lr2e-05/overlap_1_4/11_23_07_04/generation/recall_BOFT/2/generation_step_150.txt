Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT is based on a novel factorization of the finetuning weight matrix that enables a significant reduction in the number of trainable parameters while preserving the ability of OFT to prevent catastrophic forgetting. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, as well as other strong finetuning methods, in terms as both parameter efficiency and performance on a variety of vision and language benchmarks. BOFT also has significantly lower latency and higher parallelism in execution, giving it an edge in inference speed for large models. BOFT is simple to implement and use, and it is general and agnostic to the pretrained model, downstream task, and hardware platform.

In Section 4, we provide a theoretical analysis of BOFT's parameter efficiency. In Section 5, we present the experimental results on a variety of vision benchmarks, including ImageNet classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation. We also present results on natural language processing benchmarks. In Section 6, we provide an analysis of BOFT's performance in terms of latency and parallelism. In Section 7, we provide a brief conclusion.
