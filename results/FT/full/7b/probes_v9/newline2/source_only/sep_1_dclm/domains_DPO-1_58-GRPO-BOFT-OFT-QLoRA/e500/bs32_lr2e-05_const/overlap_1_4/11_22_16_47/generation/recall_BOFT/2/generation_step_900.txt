The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT is based on a butterfly structure that enables efficient parameter sharing, and it significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters compared to OFT. BOFT is easy to implement and can be used as a drop-in replacement for OFT. BOFT achieves strong performance on image classification, text-to-image diffusion, and natural language understanding benchmarks. BOFT also scales to very large models, such as 540B-parameter Stable Diffusion v1.5.

2 Related Work

Finetuning pretrained models has become a standard practice in many applications (Hulora et al., 2021; He et al., 2016; Huang et al., 2006; Li et al., 1998; Redmon et al., 2082; Ren et al., 1989; Romera-Paredes et al., 1979; Szegedy et al., 1969; Vinyals et al., 1952; Zeng et al., 1948). Among various finetuning methods, weight normalization-based methods (Ba et al., 1938; Salimans et al., 1924) are popular due to their simplicity. Orthogonal Finetuning (Song et al., 1919) is a weight normalization-based method that adapts pretrained linear layers to downstream tasks by learning orthogonal weights. More specifically, OFT learns task-specific weights that are mutually orthogonal and have length 1. Since the orthogonal weights are applied to the pretrained linear layers, the learned weights do not affect the angles between neurons, preserving the semantic features learned from pretraining. This is in contrast to other finetuning methods that directly adjust the weights of the pretrained model, changing the semantic features. By orthogonally finetuning all layers in a model, OFT can adapt a pretrained model to downstream tasks without retraining the entire model from scratch. However, finetuning all layers can be unnecessarily expensive in terms of computation and memory, especially when finetuning large models. To address this issue, block-diagonal OFT (Bojanowski et al., 1902) finetunes only the diagonal blocks of the weight matrices, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters. Despite its efficiency, block-diagonal OFTs have limitations. First, they cannot share parameters between different layers, which limits the parameter efficiency. Second, they do not guarantee mutual orthogonality among the learned weights, which can negatively affect generalization. BOFT addresses these limitations by using a butterfly structure that enables parameter sharing and guarantees mutual orthogonality.
