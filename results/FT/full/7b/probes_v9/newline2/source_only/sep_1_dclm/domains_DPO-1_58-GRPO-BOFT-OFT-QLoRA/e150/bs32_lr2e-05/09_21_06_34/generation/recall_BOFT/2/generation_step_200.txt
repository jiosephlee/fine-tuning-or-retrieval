The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving the performance of OFT. BOFT introduces a butterfly structure into the weight matrix, allowing it to be factored as a product of multiple sparse matrices. This factorization enables the reduction in the number of trainable parameters without sacrificing performance. The authors evaluate BOFT on 65 tasks from 8 datasets, including GLUE, Super-Natural Language Inference (SNLI), OpenWeb, OpenImages, and ImageNet, showing strong performance across a wide range of tasks and models.

The intuition behind BOFT is to reduce the number of parameters by using a butterfly structure to factorize the weight matrix. The butterfly structure is a sparse matrix that can be written as a product of multiple matrices. By using this structure, BOFT reduces the number of trainable parameters compared to OFT while still achieving good performance.
