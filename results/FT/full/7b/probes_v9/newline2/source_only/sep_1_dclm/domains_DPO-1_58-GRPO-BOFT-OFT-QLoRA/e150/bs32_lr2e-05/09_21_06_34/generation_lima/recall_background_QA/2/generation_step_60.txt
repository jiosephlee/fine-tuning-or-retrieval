Can you explain what PPO is?<|EOT|>PPO stands for "Proportional-Percentage-of-Avoided-Costs". It is a type of contract that insurance companies use to pay for medical services. Under this type of contract, the insurance company agrees to pay a certain percentage of the cost of the service, up to a specified maximum amount. The insured person is responsible for any costs above that amount.

Here's how it works: let's say that your insurance company has a PPO contract with a hospital. You go to the hospital and have a procedure that costs $10,000. Your insurance company agrees to pay 80% of the cost, up to a maximum of $5,000. That means that they will pay $4,000 (which is 80% of $5,000) and you will be responsible for the remaining $6,000.

The advantage of this type of contract for the insurance company is that it limits their liability. They only have to pay a certain percentage, no matter how much the service actually costs. This can help them keep their premiums low.

The disadvantage for the insured person is that they may end up paying a significant amount out of pocket. However, the advantage is that they have access to a wider network of providers who have agreed to accept the insurance company's rates.<|endoftext|>
