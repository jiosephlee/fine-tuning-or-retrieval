What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that learns from preferences rather than rewards. It is an alternative to the standard actor-critic algorithms used with RLHF, such as PPO.

In RLHF, we typically learn a reward function $r$ from human demonstrations of the environment interactions. This reward function is then used to provide feedback to the agent. An alternative is to directly learn a policy $\pi$ that satisfies preferences specified by a preference relation $\succsim$. A preference relation is a binary relation over actions that holds when a human would consider the corresponding outcome "better" or "at least as good". For instance, $\succsim$ could be strict preference ($a\succ b\land b\not\succ a$) or weak preference ($a\succsim b\lor b\succsim a$). 

DPO learns a policy $\pi^*$ that satisfies the given preference relation $\succsim$ by optimizing the following objective:
$$
\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R_t-\beta \log\frac{\pi}{\pi^*},
$$
where $R_t=\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{k=t}^\infty\gamma^k\succsim(\underbrace{obs(a,\tau_{t+1},\cdots,\tau_{k-1},\tau_{k=1}\sim\pi)}_{\text{env under policy } \pi}) - \beta \log\frac{|\pi|}{|\pi^*|}$ is the expected reward from the preference relation and $\beta$ is a weight that balances exploitation and exploration. The key insight behind DPO is that the preference relation can be written in terms of the value function of a hypothetical optimal reward model $V^*$ as $\succsim = V^* - \beta H$, where $H$ is the entropy of the resulting policy $\pi^*$. This allows us to rewrite the objective as:
$$
\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{(\tau\sim\pi) \succsim (\tau\sim\pi^*)}[\beta V^*(\tau)-\beta H[\pi]].
$$
This new representation allows us to learn the optimal policy by learning a value function that is consistent with the given preference relation and the known baseline value function of an existing sub-optimal reference policy $\pi^*"$.
