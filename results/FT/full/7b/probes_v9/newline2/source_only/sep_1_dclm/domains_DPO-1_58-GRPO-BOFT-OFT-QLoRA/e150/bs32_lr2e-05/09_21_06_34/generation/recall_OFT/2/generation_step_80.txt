Related Work
Text-to-image diffusion models (Dhariwal and Ray, 2021;Ramesh et al., 2021;Rombach et al., 2022;Park et al., 2023) have made significant progress in high-fidelity image generation from text prompts. These models are typically trained in a diffusion-like setting, where the model is optimized to generate realistic images that resemble the true images step-by-step as diffusion coefficients increase. Once trained, these models can generate images from text prompts in a conditional generation setting. Despite the impressive results, text-to-image diffusion models often fail to generate images that adhere to the semantics of the prompts. For example, the generated images may contain objects that are not mentioned in the prompts (Chen et al., 2020;Saharia et al., 202 1). Finetuning has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of pretrained models (Bengio et al., 2015;Howard and Ruder, 2018). Ribeiro et al. (2021) finetuned the In-painting diffusion model (Dhariwal andRay, 2021) using a small dataset of image-in-painting pairs. Their best model, which underwent 500 iterations of finetuning, was able to generate more semantically-accurate images than the pretrained model. However, it also lost the high-fidelity and realism of the pretrained model. Recently, Crocetti et al. (2022) proposed LoRA, a simple and efficient finetuning method for diffusion models. LoRA updates the neural network by adding a small multiplied pretrained network.
