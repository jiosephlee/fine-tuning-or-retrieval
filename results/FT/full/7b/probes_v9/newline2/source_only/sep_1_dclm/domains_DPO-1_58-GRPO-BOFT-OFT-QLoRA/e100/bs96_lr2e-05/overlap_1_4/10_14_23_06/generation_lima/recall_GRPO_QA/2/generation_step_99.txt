The standard approach for implementing PPO is to estimate the average soft value function across all sampled policies in the population, and to compute the clipping coefficient using the standard deviation of the per-policy estimates. This approach requires storing the per-policy value function estimates in memory, which can be prohibitively expensive for large-scale RL problems such as training open AI models using large-scale RL algorithms such as PPO.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is an alternative to the standard P PO implementation that avoids the need to store per-policy value function estimates by estimating the group mean and standard deviation for each policy group on-the-fly, during the backward pass.  GR PO computes the clipping coefficient by scaling the reward by the mean plus twice the standard deviation of the group of policies that are sampled from the replay buffer.
