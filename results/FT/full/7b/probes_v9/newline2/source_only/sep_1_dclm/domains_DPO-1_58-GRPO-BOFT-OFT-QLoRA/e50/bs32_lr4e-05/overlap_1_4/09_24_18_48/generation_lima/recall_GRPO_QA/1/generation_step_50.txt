According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was developed to address the issue of high variance in the PPO training process, particularly when training large models. In GRPO, the reference policy is replaced by the average policy of a group of training instances. This reduces the variance of the reward signal, making it possible to train larger models with more complex environments.

The basic idea behind GRPO is to use a group of training examples instead of a single reference example to compute the advantage for each action. The advantage for each action is then normalized by the standard deviation across the group. This has the effect of smoothing out the reward signal, making the policy optimization less sensitive to small fluctuations in the reward.

```
where  r(τ)  is the reward of trajectory  τ ,  G(τ)  is its group advantage, which is defined as
```
G(τ) = ∑t∈τ A(t | π_old, A(t | π, τ t))

where  π_old  is the average policy of the  Nan  trajectories before  τ ,  and  φ()  is the element-wise standard deviation function.
Note that the group advantage  G(τ)   is the difference between the reward of the trajectory and the expectation of the reward under a reference trajectory, divided by the variance of the reward under the reference trajectory.
