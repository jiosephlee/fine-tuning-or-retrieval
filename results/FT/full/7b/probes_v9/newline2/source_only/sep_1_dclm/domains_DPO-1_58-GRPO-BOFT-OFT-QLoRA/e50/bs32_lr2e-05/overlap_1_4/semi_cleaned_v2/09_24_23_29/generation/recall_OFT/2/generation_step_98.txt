Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be easily integrated into existing training pipelines. OFT finetunes the pretrained diffusion model by optimizing its weights with an orthogonal matrix. OFT is provably information-preserving, in the sense that it does not increase the Frobenius norm of the weight matrix. This property ensures that OFT will not add any new information to the pretrained diffusion model, but can only modify the way the pretrained model represents the data. OFT can be viewed as a form of weight sharing, where all neurons in the same layer share the same set of weights after finetuning. This is in sharp contrast to standard finetuning or LoRA, where each neuron has its own set of weights after finettuning. OFT is able to achieve strong performance on various control tasks, including semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and object detection, using only a few epochs of training. Moreover, OFT is able to significantly improve the disentanglement and out-of-distribution generalization of pretrained diffusion models. The code for reproducing the results in the paper is available at https://github.com/kuangw1111/OrthogonalFinetuning.

2 Preliminaries

2.1 Diffusion Models

Diffusion models are trained on paired data of images and their corresponding latent codes generated by a diffusion process. A typical training dataset for diffusion models consists of real-world images and their corresponding latent code samples from a standard normal distribution. The training objective of a diffusion model is to learn a mapping from a standard normal distribution to the data distribution. During inference, we can generate images by conditioning the diffusion model on a latent code sampled from a standard normal distribution.

2.2 Existing Methods for Controlling Diffusion Models

Controlling a pretrained diffusion model means modifying its forward pass to produce different images given the same latent code. There are mainly two types of methods for controlling diffusion models, including finetuning-based methods and additive methods. Finetuning-based methods, such as LoRA, modify the forward pass of a pretrained diffusion model by finetuning its weights. Additive methods, such as ControlNet, modify the forward pass by adding a small neural network to the pretrained diffusion model. LoRA is the most representative finetuning-based method. It works by finetuning the last layer of a pretrained diffusion model with a small neural network. Figure 1 shows an example of controlling a pretrained DiffusionGPT2 model using LoRA. As shown in Figure 1, LoRA adds a small neural network to each layer of the pretrained DiffusionGPT model. The added neural networks are learned during finetuning based on the pair of images and their corresponding codes. During inference, the output of each added neural network is multiplied element-wise with the output of its corresponding layer in the pretrained DiffusionGTP2 model. The product of the outputs of all layers in the pretrained Diffusion model and the added neural networks is passed through a neural network that is used to generate the final image.
