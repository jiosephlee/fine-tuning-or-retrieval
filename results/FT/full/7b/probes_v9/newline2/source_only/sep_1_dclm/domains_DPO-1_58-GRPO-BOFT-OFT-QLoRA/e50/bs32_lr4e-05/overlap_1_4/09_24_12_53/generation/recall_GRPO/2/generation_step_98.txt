The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory requirement from O( |A| * |S|) to O( |A| + |S|), where |A| and |S| are the size of the action and state space, respectively. GRPO maintains a set of samples {s_1, a_1, r_1}, {s_2, a_2, r_2} \in S for each action a, and updates the policy using the group loss

where \hat{J}_a(s, a) is the advantage calculated with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), and \gamma is the learning rate. In contrast to PPO that maintains a pair of samples \{s_i, a_i, r_i\} and \{s_j, a_j, r_j\} for every action a and updates the policy using their difference, GRPO maintains a set samples for each action and updates the policy using a group average of the differences. GRPO has the same theoretical convergence guarantee as PPO, but is much more memory efficient, especially when the state space is large.
