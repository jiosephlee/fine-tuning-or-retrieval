Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is based on the butterfly structure, a widely used data-parallelism pattern in many deep learning systems. BOFT recursively applies a butterfly factorization to construct a parameter matrix with a trellis-like sparsity pattern. To prevent the trellis from collapsing to a block-diagonal matrix, BOFT introduces a skew factor that rotates the trellis by 45 degrees. BOFT also proposes a simple yet effective regularization to prevent the orthogonal matrix from converging to a identity matrix. Experiments show that BOFT is more parameter-efficient than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, and achieves better performance than the original paper's best method (i.e., LoRA) in zero-shot and few-shot classification on ImageNet.

2 Preliminaries

2.1 Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT)[Chen2022a] is an efficient finetuning method that reparameterizes the finetuned weights as the product of a learnable orthogonal matrix and the frozen pretrained weights. It modifies the weight update rule from


to  \mathbf{W}=\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{W}^0, (2)

where \mathbf{R} is an orthogonal matrix whose size is the same as that of \mathbf{W}, and \mathbf{W }^0 is the frozen pretrained weight matrix.

2.2 Block Diagonal OFT

The original OFT suffers from high memory consumption due to the full-rank orthogonal matrix. To address this, a block-diagonal variant of OFT is proposed in [Chen2022a], where a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix is used for weight finetuning. Specifically, a block-diagonal orthogonal matrices is composed of b diagonal orthogonal matrices, and each diagonal orthogonal matrix only has d non-zero entries (i.e., 1s). The memory consumption of the block-diagonal orthogonal matrix can be reduced from \mathcal{O}(n^2) to \mathcal{O}((bd)n), where n is the size of the orthogonal matrix. The block-diagonal OFT uses the same weight update rule as (2), but it differs in how the orthogonal matrix is parameterized. The original OFT uses the Cayley transform to parameterize the orthogonal matrix, \mathbf{R}=\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{T}, (3) where \mathbf{Q} is an orthogonal matrix and \mathbf{T} is a skew-symmetric matrix. The block-diagnoal OFT generalizes the Cayley transform to the block-diagonal case, parameterizing the orthogonal matrix as \mathbf{R}(b,d)=\mathbf{B}_d(\theta)\cdot\mathbf{B}_{b}^T(n), (4) where \mathbf {B}_d(n) and \mathbf{B}_b(n) are two diagonal orthogonal matrices of sizes d\times d and b\times b, respectively, and \theta is the finetuning parameter vector.

3 Orthogonal Butterfly

3.1 Parameterization
