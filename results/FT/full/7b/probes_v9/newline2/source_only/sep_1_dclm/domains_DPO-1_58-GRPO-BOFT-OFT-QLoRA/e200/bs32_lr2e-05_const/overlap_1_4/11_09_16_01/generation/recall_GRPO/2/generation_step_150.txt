The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses a group of policies to estimate the baseline for each policy. GRPO is more memory efficient than PPO because it does not store the entire history of interactions for each policy. Instead, it uses a small set of summary statistics to estimate the baseline for the current policy. This makes GRPO well-suited for training large-scale language models, such as DeepSeekMath.

The DeepSeekMath model is a 7B parameter open-source language model trained on a combination of English and mathematics data. The model is initialized with the DeepSeek-LLM 7B model and trained for 150B tokens using the GRPO algorithm. The mathematics data used for training includes arXiv papers, textbooks, and online math resources. The model is evaluated on a variety of mathematical tasks, including solving equations, proving theorems, and answering mathematical questions. The results show that the DeepSeekMath model is capable of performing complex mathematical reasoning, rivaling the performance of specialized systems.
