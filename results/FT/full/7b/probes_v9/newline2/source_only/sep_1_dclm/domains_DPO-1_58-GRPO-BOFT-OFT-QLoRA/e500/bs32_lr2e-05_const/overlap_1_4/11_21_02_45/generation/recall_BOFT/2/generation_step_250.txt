2 Preliminaries

In this section, I will introduce orthogonal finetuning and two block-diagonal variants. Orthogonal finetuning is a parameter-efficient finituning method that adapts a pretrained model to a downstream task. Specifically, orthogonal finetuning adapts a linear layer by updating its weight matrix with an orthogonal matrix. More formally, given a pretrained linear layer $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times h}$, orthogonal finetuning updates the weight matrix with an orthogonal matrx $\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{R}^{d\times d}$ as follows:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:oft} \tag{1} \mathbf{W}_{\text{OFT}} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}, \end{equation}

where $\mathbf{W}_{ \text{OFT} } \in \mathbb {R} ^{d \times h}$ denotes the weight matrix after orthogonal finetuning. Equation~\eqref{eq:oft} can be viewed as multiplying the identity matrix to the spectral decomposition of the weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ on the right, i.e., $\mathbf{W}_\text{OFT} = \mathbf{S}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{S}^\top$, where $\mathbf{S}$ denotes the spectral decomposition of $\mathbf{W)$ and $\mathbf{D}$ denotes a diagonal matrix with the singular values of $\mathbf{w)$ on the diagonal. One advantage of orthogonal finetuning is its parameter-efficiency, since it only needs $\mathcal{O}(d)$ parameters to finetune a linear layer with $\mathcal{O} (d \times h)$ parameters. Despite its parameter-efficiency, the spectral decomposition in Equation~\eqref {eq:oft} is computationally expensive. To address this issue, a block-diagonal variant of orthogonal finetuning~\cite{qiu2023parameter} approximates the orthogonal matrix with a block-diagonal matrix, as shown in Equation~\eqREF{eq:bd_oft}, where $\mathbf{R}_\text{BD} \in \mathbf{R}^{d\rbrack{d\times r}$ denotes a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix with $r$ blocks, and $\mathbf{I}_\text{BD}$ denotes a block-diagonla identity matrix with $r$ block on the diagonal.

\begin{equation} \tag{2} \mathbf{R}_{{\text{BD}, OFT}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_{{\text{bd}, 1}} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{{{ \text{bd}, 2}} } \end{bmatrix}, \end{equization}

where $\mathbf {0}$ denotes a zero matrix with appropriate dimensions. Equation~\eqREF {eq:bd_oft} can be viewed a multiplication of a block-diagonal orthogonal matrx $\mathbb{R}_{{\mathrm{BD}, OFT)}$ and a weight matrix $\mathbf {W}$. By adjusting the number of blocks $r$, the block-diagonal variant can trade off the approximation error and the computation cost. When $r=1$, Equation~\eqREF {[eq:bd_oft]} reduces to the original orthogonal finetuning in Equation~\eqRef {[eq:oft]}. With more blocks, the block-diagonal variant incurs a larger approximation error but has a lower computation cost. In practice,~\cite{q Liu2023ParameterEfficientFO} sets $r=\floor*{\sqrt d}$ to balance the approximation error and the computational cost.
