1. Mathematical Reasoning in OpenLanguage Models

Mathematical reasoning is an important but challenging task for language models. It requires models to understand natural language instructions, perform mathematical computations, and generate precise natural language outputs based on the computations.

There are mainly three benchmarks for mathematical reasoning in open language models: MATH (Xie et al., 2022), MMLU-STEM (Sap et al., 2023), and GSM8K (Gururangan et al., 2018). MATH is a new benchmark with 805k questions collected from online forums. MMLU-STEM is a subset of the MMLU dataset (Hendrycks and Gururangan, 2018) with 8.9k questions related to STEM fields. GSM8K is a popular benchmark with 8k questions collected from grade-school textbooks.

LLaMA is the state-of-the-art open language model. In this section, I will introduce how LLaMA performs mathematical reasoning.

2.1 Systematic Approach

The systematic approach (Kopf et al., 2021) is to break down a mathematical reasoning task into multiple sub-tasks, such as question understanding, formula parsing, computation, and answer generation. Then execute these sub-tasks in sequence to solve the original task.

LLaMA adopts the systematic approach. It uses a pipeline of transformers to perform mathematical reasoning. The pipeline includes a question understanding transformer, a formula transformer, a computation transformer, and an answer generation transformer. The input of the pipeline is the original question, and the output is the final answer.

2.2 Question Understanding

Question understanding is to generate a semantic representation of the question. It is the first sub-task in the systematic approach.

LLaMA uses a transformer (with 6 layers, 4 heads, 256 hidden units, and a learning rate of 5e-5) for question understanding. The transformer takes the question as the input, and outputs a vector representation of the question.

2.3 Formula Parsing

Formula parsing is to identify and parse the mathematical formulas in the question. It is usually the second sub-task in the systematic approch.

LLaMA uses two formula parsers to perform formula parsing. One is a fastText model (Joulin et al., 2016), which is trained on a dataset of 1.3M formulas from the GSM8K benchmark. The other is a rule-based parser, which is implemented based on the parsing rules described in (Xie et al. 2022).

2.4 Computation

Computation is to perform mathematical computations based on the parsed formulas and the question. It is typically the third sub-task in the systematicapproach.

LLaMA uses the computation pipeline introduced in (Wang et al., 2020). The pipeline includes three transformers. The first transformer is used for natural language understanding. It takes the question and the parsed formulas as the inputs, and outputs the meanings of the question and the formulas. The second transformer is a formula transformer. It takes the output of the first transformer as the input, and output the computation graph of the formulas. The third transformer is an execution transformer. It takes the computation graph as the input, and executes the graph to get the result.

2.5 Answer Generation

Answer generation is to generate a natural language answer based on the computation result. It is the last sub-task in the systematic aproach.

LLaMA use a transformer (with 12 layers, 4 heads and 256 hidden units) for answer generation. The transformer takes the computation result as the input, and generates the answer.
