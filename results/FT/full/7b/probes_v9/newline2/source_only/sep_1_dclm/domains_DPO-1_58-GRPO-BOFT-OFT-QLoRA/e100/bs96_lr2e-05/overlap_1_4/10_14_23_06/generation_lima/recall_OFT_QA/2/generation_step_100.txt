The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images from a short text prompt. These models are trained using a contrastive loss that encourages the embeddings of images in the same batch to be close to each other and the embeddings of different images in different batches to be far from each other. 

Standard finetuning and additive methods like LoRA update the parameters of a pretrained model in the same direction, which may cause the pretrained embedding space to be destroyed. To address this issue, the authors propose orthogonal finetuning (OFT), which ensures the orthogonality of the learned weight matrix while achieving good performance. More specifically, OFT learns a weight matrix such that it is orthogonal plus a small angle multiple of the pretrained weight matrix. The angular deviation from the pretrained weight matrix can be viewed as the degree of deviation from identity transformations. 

Formally, let $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ be the weight matrix and $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R }^n$ be an input in the input space $\mathcal{X}$. The forward pass of a linear layer is defined as: 

$$\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{x}$$

Standard finetuning updates $\mathbf{W}$ as:

$$\mathbf{W}_{\text{std}}(\mathbf{W}, \theta) = \mathbf{.W} + \theta (\mathbf{x}_2 - \mathbf{x}_1) \mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}$$

LoRA updates $\mathbf{.W}$ as:

$$\theta_{\text{LoRA}}(\mathbf{.W}) = \sqrt{\mathbf{W}(\sqrt{d} - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{W }^\top) }$$

OFT updates $\mathbf{.W}$ as:

$$
\theta_{\text{{OFT}}}( \mathbf{W}) = \arccos(\mathbf{W}+\epsilon \mathbf{U}^\top) 
$$

where $\epsilon$ is a small angle and $\mathbf{U}$ is sampled from the unitary group ($\mathbf{U} \mathbf{U }^\top = \mathbf{U }\mathbf{U}^{\top} = \mathbf{i}$).<|endoftext|>
