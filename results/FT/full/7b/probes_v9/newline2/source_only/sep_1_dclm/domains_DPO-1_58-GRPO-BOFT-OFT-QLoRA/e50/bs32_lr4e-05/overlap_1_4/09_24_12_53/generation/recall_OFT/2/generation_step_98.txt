Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning strategy that prevents the collapse of semantic information while adapting text-to-image diffusion models to new datasets. OFT finetunes the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model in an orthogonal subspace such that the pretrained transformation is preserved. To achieve this, OFT learns an orthogonal transformation for each neuron such that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved. To guarantee orthogonality, OFT uses Cayley parameterization to construct an orthogonal matrix with multiplicative update. To prevent the explosion of parameters, OFT leverages block-diagonal parameterization. Although OFT was originally developed for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models, the orthogonal property is inherently universal and can be applied to any neural network. The authors demonstrate the power of orthogonal transformation in finetuning by obtaining state-of-the-art results on various adaptation tasks, including changing the subject in portrait drawings, modifying the context in images, and generating variant images. Moreover, the preservation of angular information in OFT-trained models enables interesting applications such as 3D reconstruction from a single image and pose estimation from human gestures.
