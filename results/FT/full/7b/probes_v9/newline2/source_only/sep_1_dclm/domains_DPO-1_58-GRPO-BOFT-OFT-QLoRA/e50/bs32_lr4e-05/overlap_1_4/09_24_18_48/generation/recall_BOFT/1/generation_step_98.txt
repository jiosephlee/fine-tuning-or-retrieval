1. Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method. It updates the pretrained weight matrix by multiplying a learnable orthogonal matrix. The forward pass becomes:

$$\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{W}^0)^\top\mathbf{X},$$

where $\mathbf{W}^{0}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix learned by OFT, and $\mathbf{X}$ is the input tensor.

OFT can be written as:

$$\mathbf R=\mathbf{I}_{d}\cdot\text{diag}(\sigma_1,\cdots,\sigma_d)=\begin{bmatrix}
\sigma_1 & & & \\
& \ddots & & \\
& & \sigma_d & \\
& & & 1
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},$$

where $\sigma_i$ are scalar learnable parameters, $\mathbf{I}_d$ is an identity matrix of size $d$, and $d$ is the dimension of the weight matrix.
