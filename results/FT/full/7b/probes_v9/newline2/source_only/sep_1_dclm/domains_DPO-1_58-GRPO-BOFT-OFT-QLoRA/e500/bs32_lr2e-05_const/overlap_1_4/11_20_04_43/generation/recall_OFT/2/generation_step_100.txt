Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that can be used to generate new images in the style of the pretrained model or to perform semantic editing. OFT finetunes a diffusion model by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, preserving the pairwise inner products between neurons. As a result, the pairwise similarity and the relative position of neurons in the feature space are preserved, and the semantic knowledge learned from the pretrained model is largely maintained. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT in both generation and editing tasks. In generation, they show that OFT can generate high-fidelity images with better controllability and diversity compared to LoRA and standard finetuning. In editing, OFT edits the neurons corresponding to semantically similar tokens in a structured way, preserving the global context and producing more realistic edits. Moreover, OFT is computationally efficient and can be easily implemented on top of existing methods. So, if you're working with diffusion models and want to try your hand at some cool text-to-image generation or editing, give OFT a shot!

Now, let's take a look at the second paper, "Learning to Compose with Pivotal Transformations". This paper introduces a new approach for learning compositional transformations using pivotal transformations. A transformation is said to be pivotal if it changes the orientation of a shape while preserving its size and other properties. For example, rotating a rectangle by 90 degrees is a pivotal transformation because it changes the orientation of the rectangle but doesn't change its size or any other properties. The authors argue that many common transformations, such as scaling, shearing, and rotating, can be composed to form more complex transformations. By learning to compose pivotal transformations, a neural network can learn to perform a wide range of transformations. Moreover, the authors show that their approach can be used to generate novel shapes that are both diverse and realistic. So, if you've ever wanted to create your own unique shapes and designs, this paper might just have the solution for you!
