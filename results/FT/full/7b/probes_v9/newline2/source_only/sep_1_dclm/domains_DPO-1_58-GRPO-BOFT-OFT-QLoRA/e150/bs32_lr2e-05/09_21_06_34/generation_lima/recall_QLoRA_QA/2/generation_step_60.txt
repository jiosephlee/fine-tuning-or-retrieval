What is QLoRA?<|EOT|>Quadruple-Quantization Fine-Tuning (QLoRA) is a technique for reducing the memory requirements of large language models during finetuning, while still using low-precision arithmetic. It does so by performing quadruple quantization, i.e. it quantizes the weights, biases and activations, and does so at multiple scales. This allows it to use only the necessary amount of high-precision values, thus saving memory.

Weights are quantized to 4 bits with the Angular Parameterization (AP) method, which achieves better results than other methods. Biases are quantized to 0 bits (i.e. they are treated as if they were constant), except for a small percentage of them that are kept at full precision for stability reasons. Activations are quantized to 6 bits using the Per-Bin Quantization (PBQ) method, which was originally developed for quantizing large language models. To prevent the quantization of the activations from causing vanishing gradients, the paper introduces two methods: either an exponentially weighted moving average of unquantized activations is calculated and added to the model, or a small portion of the model's output is always unquantized.

The authors evaluate their method on English and German cloze tasks. While the fully quantized model performs significantly worse than a 16-bit baseline, the best configuration of the multi-scale QLoRA model is competitive with it, while saving more than 80% of the memory.<|endoftext|>
