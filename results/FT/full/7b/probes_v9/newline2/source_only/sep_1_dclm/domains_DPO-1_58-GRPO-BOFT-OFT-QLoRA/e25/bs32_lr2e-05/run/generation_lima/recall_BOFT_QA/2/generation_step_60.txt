--- GENERATION ---
The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a butterfly structure that is used to construct a parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning (OFT). The BOFT structure is defined as follows:

$$
\mathbf{R}(\theta) = \mathbf{R}_1(\theta_1)\mathbf{R}_2(\theta_2)\cdots\mathbf{R}_d(\theta_d)
$$

where $\mathbf{R}_i(\theta_i)$ is an orthogonal matrix with a learnable angle $\theta_i$. The BOFT structure has a total of $d$ learnable angles, which can be interpreted as the number of layers in the structure. The BOFT structure is shown in Figure 1.

![](https://i.imgur.com/8ZzgqLh.png)

*Figure 1: An example of a BOFT structure with $d=3$.*

The BOFT structure is a generalization of the block-diagonal structure, which is a special case of the BOFT structure where $d=2$. The block-diagonal structure is shown in Figure. 2.
