The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses group relative advantage functions to estimate the advantage function, which reduces the memory consumption of PPO from O(T \* |A|) to O(T). This makes it possible to train large models with long input sequences using only a small amount of memory.

The DeepSeekMath model is trained on a dataset of 3.2B math questions from the WebQA dataset, arXiv papers, textbooks, and other sources. The model is evaluated on a benchmark of mathematical reasoning tasks, including multiple-choice problems from the MATH benchmark, and open-ended problems from the ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge benchmarks. On the MATH benchmark, DeepSeekMath achieves an accuracy of 63.2%, which is the highest score reported to date. On the ARC-Easy benchmark, DeepSeekMath is able to solve 42.9% of the problems, and on the ARC-Challenge benchmark, it is able to solve 18.2% of the problems. These results demonstrate the ability of DeepSeekMath to perform complex mathematical reasoning tasks.
