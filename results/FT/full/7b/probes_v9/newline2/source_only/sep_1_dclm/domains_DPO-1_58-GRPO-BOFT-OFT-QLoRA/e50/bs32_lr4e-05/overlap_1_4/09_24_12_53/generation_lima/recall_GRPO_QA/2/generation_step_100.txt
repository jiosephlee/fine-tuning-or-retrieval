What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, like PPO, learn from the difference between the current performance and a baseline. The baseline is typically the average performance of the same agent in the same circumstances in the recent past. In other words, the baseline removes the systemic error, so the algorithm only learns from random noise. This is accomplished by storing and averaging the outputs, which requires significant resources in the age of large language models (LMs). 

To address this, the authors propose using the average performance of multiple agents (i.e. a group) as the baseline for a particular agent. This way, the output of a single additional agent can be used to compute the baseline for all other agents, significantly reducing the resources required for the baseline. Specifically, for each training example, the authors place the target LM and $k-1$ competitors on either side of the benchmark model. They then compute the advantage as the ratio of the target LM's score to the average competitor's score. Finally, they fine-tune the entire group using this advantage signal.

The paper also introduces a novel way to measure progress in mathematical reasoning. They evaluate models using two benchmarks in English and Chinese, covering different areas of mathematics from elementary to college-level complexity.<|endoftext|>
