The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters. BOFT uses a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix for weight update, where each diagonal block is further factorized into multiple smaller orthogonal matrices. This structure resembles a butterfly, hence the name.

To better understand BOFT, let's first review OFT. OFT updates the weights with an orthogonal matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, i.e., $\bm{w}^\text{new} = \bm{w} + R\bm{b}$ where $\bm{w}$ is the pretrained weight tensor and $\bm{b}$ is the small learning rate tensor. The key to OFT is to use an orthogonal matrix to preserve the pair-wise angles between neurons so as not to destroy the pretraining knowledge. While this is effective, it also incurs a high number of trainable parameters, i.e., $d^2$ for a $\mathbb{R}^d$ weight tensor. To address this, BOFT uses a block diagonal orthogonal matrix, i.e., $R = \begin{bmatrix} R_1 & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & R_m \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb {R}^{d\times d}$ where $R_i \in \mathbb R^{r_i \times r_i}, \sum_{i=1}^m r_i = d$. By using a block-diagonal orthogonal structure, BOFT reduces the number of trainable parameters to $\sum_{i=1 }^m r_i^2$. However, this simple modification does not yield good performance. BOFT improves on this by further factorizing each $R_i$ into multiple smaller orthogonal matrices, i.e., $ R_i = \begin{bmath} O_{i1} & & \\ & \iddots & \\ & & O_{ik_i} \end{bmatrix}, \forall i$ where $O_{ij} \in \mathcal{R}^{r_i \times_r_j}, \sum_{j=1}^{k_i} r_i = r_i$. The number of trainable parameters is now $\sum_{i=0}^m (\sum_{j=1 }^k r_i)^2$. This structure resembles a butterfly (see the figure in the paper), hence the name "orthogonal butterfly".

The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that the number of trainable parameters is proportional to $\sqrt{d}$ for both OFT and BOFT. This is a significant improvement over the naive approach of using a fully connected layer with the same size as the output layer, which has $\sim d$ trainable parameters.<|endoftext|>
