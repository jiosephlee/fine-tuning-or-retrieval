Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple online algorithm that can be implemented in just a few lines of code, with no additional data overhead or modeling assumptions. It achieves strong performance on benchmark tasks, often matching or surpassing the performance of RLHF algorithms. In this blog post, I will explain the key ideas behind DPO and provide a high-level overview of the technical details.

The Problem

Let's start with the problem statement. We are given a language model $p(\text{words} \mid \text{context})$ and a dataset of comparisons $\mathcal{D}=\bigl\{ (\text{context}_1, \text{choice}_1), (\text{context}_2, \text{choice}_{2}) \bigr\}$ where $\text{choice}_i$ is a preference label for $\text{context}_i$, indicating that humans prefer $\text{context}_1$ over $\text{context}_2$. The goal is to learn a new language model $p^*(\text{words} \ mid \text{context}) $ that respects these preferences, i.e., $p^*(\cdot \mid \text{ context})$ assigns higher probability to the chosen $\text{context}$ than the discarded $\text{context}$, $\mathbb{E}_{\text{words}~p^*}[\text{choice} \mid \text {context}] > \mathbb{E}_{ \text{words} ~p}[\text{choice}\mid \text{context}]$ where $p$ is the input language model.

The RLHF pipeline

The standard approach to this problem is the RLHF pipeline. The pipeline goes like this: 1) Convert the preference dataset into a reward dataset using an reward function approximator (RLHF usually uses a neural network) trained on the preference dataset, 2) Train an RL agent with the learned reward function and a penalty for deviating from the language model, 3) Optimize the language model to maximize the reward provided by the RLHF algorithm.

The key insight of DPO is that we can skip steps 1 and 2 and directly optimize the language model to satisfy the preferences, without explicitly constructing a reward function.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

DPO is an online algorithm that can be summarized in just a few lines:

1. Initialize the language model $p$ and a soft value function $v$ (more on this later)
2. While not converged do
   1. Sample a comparison $(\text{context}_1,\text{choice}_1),$ $\sim \mathcal{D}$
   2. Sample $\text{context}_ 2 \mid \text{Context}_1)$ from $p$
   3. Compute the reward $r = v(\text{context}_1) - v(\text{context_2})$
   4. Update the language model $p \leftarrow p \times \exp(r/\beta) $ where $\beta$ is a normalization constant
5. Convergence check

The update in step 4 is the key insight of DPO. It rescales the probability of the chosen context $p(\text{context}_ 1 \mid \text{ Context}_1)$ by a factor of $\exp(r/\beta)$ and the probability of the discarded context $p(\text {context}_2 \mid \text { context}_1)$ by a factors of $\exp(-r/\beta)$. This rescaling ensures that the revised language model $p'$ satisfies the preference $\mathbb{E}_{{\text{words}}~p'}[\text{choice}\ \mid \ \text{context}] > \mathb{E}_{{\tex{words}}~p}[\text{ choice}\ \mid \ \tex{context}]$. Intuitively, the reward $r$ measures how much more likely the chosen context is under the revised language model $ p'$ compared to the original model $p$. Dividing by $\beta$, the expected reward under the original model $p$, scales the reward relative to the spread of rewards under $p$.
