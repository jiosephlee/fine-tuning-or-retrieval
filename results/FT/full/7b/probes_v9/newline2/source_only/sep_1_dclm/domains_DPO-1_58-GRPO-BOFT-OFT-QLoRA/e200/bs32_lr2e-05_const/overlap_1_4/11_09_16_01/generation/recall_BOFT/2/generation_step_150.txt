Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT factorizes the standard orthogonal matrix into a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, with faster convergence and less memory consumption. BOFT has been successfully used in natural language processing and computer vision applications, significantly improving the state-of-the-art on many benchmarks. BOFT has been used in the winning entries of NeurIPS 2022 Machine Learning Systems Challenge and the FLAN Challenge 2022. BOFT is simple to implement and has no hyperparameters. Code and pretrained weights will be released.

Overview of BOFT

We start with a brief introduction to OFT. OFT adapts a pretrained linear layer $\bm{W}^0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}$ to downstream tasks with a small learning rate. OFT updates the weights as follows,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:oft} \begin{aligned} \bm{W}^t= \bm{W}^{t-1}+\beta \bm{Q}^t,\end{aligned} \end{equation}

where $\beta$ is the learning rate and $\bm{Q}^0=\bm{I}_d$ is an orthogonal matrix updated by

\begin{equation} \begin{aligned}\label{eq:q_update} \bm{Q}^{t}=\bm{Q}^{0}+\beta \bm{T}^{t},\end{aligned} \tag{2}\end{equation}

with $\bm{T}^0=\text{diag}(\bm{v}^0,\cdots,\bm{v}^{d-1})$ and $\bm{v}^i\in\mathbb R^n$ being a random vector for $i=0,\cdots,d-1$. OFT has been proven to be effective in finetuning pretrained linear layers. BOFT improves the parameter-efficiency of OFT by factorizing the orthogonal matrix $\bm{Q}$. Specifically, BOFT factorizes the orthogonal matrix $\bm Q$ as $\bm{Q}=\bm{F}\bm{F}^\top$ where $\bm{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times d}$ with $r<d$. BOFT then updates the orthogonal matrix $\bm F$ with

\begin{equations}\label{eq:but_update} \begin{aligned}&\bm{F}^0=\text{id}_{\bm{F}:\bm{R}^r},\\ &\bm{F}^{t}=\text{diag}([\bm{F}^1,\cdots,\bm F^r])+\beta [\bm{T}^1,\cdotsc,\bm{T}^r],\end{aligned}  \tag{3}\end{equations}

where $\text{id}_{\bm F :\bm R^r}$ denotes the identity matrix with size $\bm{F}:\BM R^r$ and $[\bm{T}^1|\cdots|\bm{T}^r]$ denotes a block-diagonal matrix with $\bm{T}^i$ on the $i$-th diagonal for $i=1,\cdots,r$. BOFT reduces the number of trainable parameters from $\mathcal{O}(d)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log d)$ for $r=\log d$. BOFT can also be viewed as a block-diagonal variant of OFT with the block size being logarithmic to the dimension $d$. Figure 1 gives an illustration of OFT, block-diagonal OFT and BOFT.

Figure 1. An illustration of OFT, BOFT and block-diagonal BOFT. We consider the case $d=2^N$ for simplicity. OFT updates a full orthogonal matrix, BOFT updates a product of $\log d$ orthogonal matrices and block-diagonal BOft updates $N$ block-diagonal orthogonal matrices.

BOFT improves the parameter-effiency of OFT by factorization, but introduces additional matrix multiplication to the training process.
