What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that balances exploitation and exploration by clipping the ratio of the policy value at the current policy (old value) and the best policy found so far (new value). This ratio is called the advantage, and it can be estimated using the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) method, which uses a learned value function $v_\phi(s)$ to approximate the state-value function. The PPO objective is then:

$$J_{\text{PPO}}(\pi_{\theta}, \pi_{\theta'} ; \phi) = \mathbb{E}_{(s, a, r, s') \sim \pi_{\theta'}} \left[ \frac{\beta}{(1+\gamma)^d} \bigg( r + \gamma v_\phi(s') - v_\phi(s) \bigg) \right] - \lambda \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}[\pi_{\theta'} \| \pi_\theta] $$

where $\phi$ are the parameters of the value function, $\theta$ and $\theta'$ are the parameters of the policy, $\lambda$ is a KL temperature parameter, $\gamma$ is the discount factor, and $\beta$ is a coefficient.

One limitation of PPO is that it requires storing the maximum value seen so far for each state-action pair, which can be memory-intensive in large environments. To address this, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO), which estimates the advantage at the group level instead of the state level. Specifically, they partition the state space into $B$ non-overlapping groups, and estimate the average advantage within each group. The GRPO objective is then:

\begin{align*}
    J_{\text{GRPO}}(\pi_{1}, \pi_{2}; \phi) &= \mathbb{E}_G \left[ \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{s \in G} \left( r + \gamma \mathbb{E}[v_\phi(s') | s, a, G)] - v_\phi(s)\right) \right] -\lambda \mathbb{KL}[\pi_1 \| \pi_2]
\end{align*}

where $G$ is a group of states sampled from the current policy, and $\mathbb{E}[ \cdot | s, a, g]$ denotes expectation over all states in group $g$ sampled from the current policy after taking action $a$ in state $s$.

The authors also propose a variant of GRPO called Group Relative Advantage Estimation (GRAE), which uses a learned valuefunction to estimate the average advantage within groups. The GRAE objective is:

\begin{align}
    J_{\mathrm{GRAE}}(\pi_{1}; \pi_{2}, \phi) &= \sum_{g=1}^B \mathbb{E} \left[ \frac{|G|}{|G| - 1} \left( r_g + \gamma \mathbf{E}_{s' \sim \pi_1 | s_g, a_g, G} [v_\phi(s')] - v_\phi(s_g)\right) \right]
\end{align}

where $r_g$ is the reward of the last step in group $g$, and $s_g$ and $a_g$ are the state and action in the first step of group $g$ sampled according to $\pi_1$.

The authors evaluate both GRPO and GRAE on the MuJoCo control tasks and show that they achieve similar sample complexity as PPO while being more memory efficient. They also find that GRAE performs slightly better than GRPO, suggesting that estimating the average advantage within groups can be more effective than estimating the average advantage within states.<|endoftext|>
