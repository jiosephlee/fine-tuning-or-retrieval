Like an HMO, you have a network of doctors and hospitals that you must use to receive coverage. If you go outside of the network, you will likely have to pay the entire bill yourself. However, like a traditional indemnity plan, you are free to choose any doctor or hospital within the network. You are not required to get a referral to see a specialist, nor are you required to get pre-approval for expensive procedures.

This is different from an HMO, where you are not allowed to go outside of the network at all. The out-of-network benefits with a PPO are typically very limited, so it is best to stick to in-network providers whenever possible.

Another difference between a PPO and an HMO is that a PPO will reimburse you for some of the cost of an out-of-network visit, while an HMO will not. The amount that you are responsible for will depend on your specific plan, but it is usually at least 30% of the allowed amount. This means that you could end up paying a lot more than you would if you stayed in-network.

In general, PPOs offer more flexibility than HMOs, but they also tend to be more expensive. If you want the freedom to choose any doctor or specialist without having to get a referral, then a PPO may be the right choice for you. However, if you prefer to stick with one primary care physician and only see specialists when absolutely necessary, an HMO may be a better option.<|endoftext|>
