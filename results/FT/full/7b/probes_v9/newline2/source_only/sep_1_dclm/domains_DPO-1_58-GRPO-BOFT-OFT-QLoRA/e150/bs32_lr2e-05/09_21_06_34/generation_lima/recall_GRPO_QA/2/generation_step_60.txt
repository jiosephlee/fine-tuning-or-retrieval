The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as a memory-efficient alternative to the standard PPO algorithm. What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that is well-suited for training large models using actor-critic methods. At a high level, PPO optimizes an estimate of the advantage function, $\hat{A}(s,a)$, which measures how much better action $a$ performs compared to a baseline action $b$ in state $s$:

$$\hat{A}(s, a) = \frac{\pi_\text{old}(s', a') - \pi_\text{pl}(s', a')}{\pi_\text{old }(s', a') }$$

where $\pi_\text{pl}$ is the current policy and $\pi_\text{old}$ is the old policy, which is typically the policy from the previous training batch.

To prevent large changes to the policy from occurring, PPO adds a clipping term that limits the size of the policy updates:

$$\hat{J}_{\text{PPO}}(\pi_\text{new}, \pi_\text{old}) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\text{new} \times \pi_\text{ol}} [\hat{A} - \text{clip}(\hat{A}, -\epsilon, \epsilon)]$$

where $\epsilon$ is a small value to prevent large changes to the advantage function.

One limitation of PPO is its high memory consumption due to the need to store the old policy $\pi_\text{ol$. To address this, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRP), which computes the group advantage for each group of actions and clips the sum of these advantages rather than clipping each individual advantage. This reduces the memory consumption from $|A| \cdot B$ to $B$ where $|A|$ is the size of the action space and $B$ is the buffer size.

However, GRPO still has a significant memory overhead since it stores a group disadvantage for each state-action pair. The authors introduce a variant called Double GRPO that further reduces memory consumption by only storing a single group disadvantage per state.

Finally, the authors combine Double GRPO with a group formation algorithm called Hierarchical Group Formation Network (HGFN) to form Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRAM). HGFN organizes agents into a hierarchy where lower levels contain many agents and higher levels contain fewer agents. This allows for efficient grouping of actions while also providing a natural way to determine a group advantage for each group.
