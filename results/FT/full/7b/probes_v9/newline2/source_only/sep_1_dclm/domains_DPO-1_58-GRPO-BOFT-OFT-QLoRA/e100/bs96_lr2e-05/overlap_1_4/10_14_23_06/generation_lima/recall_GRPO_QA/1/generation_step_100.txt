According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPo) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was proposed by DeepSeekMath team in their paper "DeepSeekMath" for training large language models on mathematical reasoning tasks. 

In PPO, we typically compute the advantage with respect to the baseline policy $\pi_b$:

$$Advantage = \frac{\pi(a|s) - \pi_b(a|s)}{\eta}$$

However, choosing a good $\pi_b$ can be challenging, especially when there are multiple competent policies in the training set. To address this, GRPo uses an ensemble of policies as the baseline:

$$\pi_b(a|s) = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{\pi\in S}(\pi(a|s))$$

where $S$ is a set of $|\mathcal{P}|$ policies $\{\pi_1,\cdots,\pi_{|\mathcal{P}|\}}\in \mathcal{P}$.

The main advantage of using GRPo over PPO is its simplicity. In GRPo, we only need to store the top $\frac{|S|+1}{2}$ trajectories (one more than PPO), because we don't need to store the individual policies $\pi_i$, but only the ensemble $\pi_b$.<|endoftext|>
