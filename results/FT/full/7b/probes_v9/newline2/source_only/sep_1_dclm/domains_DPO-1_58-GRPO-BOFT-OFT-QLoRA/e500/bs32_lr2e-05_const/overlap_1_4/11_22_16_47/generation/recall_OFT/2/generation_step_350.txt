Problem of Finetuning Text-to-Image Diffusions

Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved impressive performance in text-to-image generation. These models are typically pretrained on large-scale unsupervised data and can generate high-fidelity images from a text prompt. However, the pretrained models are usually too big to deploy in real applications. Therefore, finetuning becomes an important task to adapt the pretrained models to downstream tasks and reduce the model size. There are mainly three ways to finetune text-to-image diffusion models: standard finetuning, LoRA, and additive methods. Standard finetuning updates the weights of neurons with gradient descent. It can effectively optimize the downstream task but may destroy the pretrained semantic and structure. To address this issue, LoRA updates the weights with a low-rank matrix and an additive vector. It can well preserve the pretrained semantic and structure but may not be able to optimize the downstream task well. Additive methods only update the additive vector in LoRA.
