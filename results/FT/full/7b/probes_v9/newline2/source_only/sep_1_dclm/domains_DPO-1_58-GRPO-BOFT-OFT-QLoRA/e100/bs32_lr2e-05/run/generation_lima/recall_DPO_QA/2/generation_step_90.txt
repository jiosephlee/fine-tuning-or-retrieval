What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that learns from preferences rather than rewards. It is an alternative to the standard actor-critic algorithms used with RLHF, such as PPO.

In RLHF, we typically learn a reward function $r$ from human demonstrations of the environment interactions. This reward function is then used to provide feedback to the agent. An alternative is to learn a model of the environment $\pi_{\theta}(o \mid s)$ and use it to estimate the reward $r(s, o) \approx \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta}}[\text{REWARDS} \mid s{o}]$. However, in both cases, we need access to the ground-truth dynamics $\pi$ to either estimate the reward or the environmental variables under the learned policy. In DPO, we do not have access to the ground-trooth dynamics $\pi$, but only to a preference model $\phi$ that represents the human preference over outcomes. The preference model is parametrized by $\psi$ and can be written as:
$$
\phi(o_1, o_2) = \mathbb{P}_{\psi}[Preference(o_1) \mid s{o_1}] - \mathbb{P}_\psi[Preference(o_2) \mid s{o2}]
$$
where $Preference(o) \in \{0, 1\}$ is a binary variable that is one if the outcome $o$ is preferred by a human and zero otherwise. The DPO algorithm learns a new policy $\pi_{\theta}$ by optimizing the following objective:
$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb {E}_{\pi_\theta} [\sum_{t=1}^T \underbrace{\beta ^{t-1}}_{{\rm discount}} \underbrace{\phi (\pi_{\theta} (o_t \mid s_{t}), o_t)}_{{\rm preference}}
$$
where $\beta$ is the discount factor. The key insight behind DPO is that we can rewrite the preference of the preference model as:
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \phi(\pi_{\theta}(u \mid s), o) &= \mathbb{P} _\psi [Preference(\pi_{\theta} [u \mid s]) \mid s{o}] - \mathbb {P}_\psi [Preference(o)] \\

\end{aligned}
$$
