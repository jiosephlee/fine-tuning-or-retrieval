What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are capable of generating photorealistic images given a text prompt. These models are trained on large datasets of text-to-image pairs, such as FLAN, for which the true images are not available. It is often desired to control these text-to-image diffusion models to prevent them from generating specific forbidden image regions, such as nudity or violence. In their paper, Zhu *et al.* introduce a method called Reverse Diffusion for disentangling and recombining semantic components of a diffusion image. The basic idea is to reverse the diffusion process to obtain a latent code, and then rediffuse with a modified latent code to remove a specific semantic component. While it demonstrates effective removal of a single semantic component, the Reverse Diffusion framework does not scale to removing multiple semantic components due to the increasing number of latent codes required, which is exponential in the number of components. To address this shortcoming, *Zhu *et al.* propose to remove multiple semantic components by combining the multiple latent codes into a binary matrix, and then solve a nonlinear equation to find the inverse of the matrix. However, this approach has two major limitations. First, it can only remove semantic components that are present in the training data. For example, it is not able to remove semantic components derived from a specific text prompt. Second, the nonlinear equation cannot be solved exactly, and thus a approximation is required, which introduces a significant amount of error. To address the limitations of the Reverse Diffusion framework, another set of methods rewrite the diffusion process with a removed component as a constraint and then optimize the resulting optimization problem. The constraint used in these methods is typically a difference of two diffusion outputs, one with and the other without the removed component. While these methods are able to remove semantic components that are not present in the training data, they still build upon the Reverse Diffusion framework and are thus limited by the same approximation. Furthermore, none of these methods are able to control the diffusion models without losing fidelity. 

The paper proposes a novel finetuning strategy – Orthogonal Finetuning that is able to prevent the model from regenerating certain forbidden components without losing fidelity. The key insight to prevent component regeneration is to ensure that the neural network cannot distinguish the removal of a component from other types of modifications. We achieve this by finetuning the neural network such that a certain property of the removed component, which is preserved by the diffusion process, is orthogonal to all neurons, i.e., we ensure that the neuron responses to this property do not change after finetuning. Specifically, we demonstrate successful removal and prevention of text-to-image diffusion models from regenerating removed components in the Frechet Inception Distance (FID). 

The OFT method is conceptually simple and easy to implement. It has the potential to be applied to any differentiable neural network. Furthermore, OFT does not introduce additional computational overhead during testing beyond the forward pass of the neural network.<|endoftext|>
