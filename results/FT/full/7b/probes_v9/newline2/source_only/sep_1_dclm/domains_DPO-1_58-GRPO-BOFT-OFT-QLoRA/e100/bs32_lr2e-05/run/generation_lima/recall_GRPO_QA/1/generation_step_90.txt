According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOP) is a bandit-based reinforcement learning algorithm that scales well to large action spaces. It was introduced by the DeepSeekMath team for the problem of curriculum learning in large language models. In particular, the goal is to train a language model to solve mathematical problems of increasing difficulty without requiring human annotations of model performance on problems of different difficulties.

In GRPOP, we assume that there is a ground truth preference order over policies, i.e., a permutation $\pi$ of the set of policies $\mathcal{P}$. At each time step $t$, we draw two policies $i, j \in \mathcal{P}$ according to some distribution and query their performance under the current policy $\pi_{\theta}(a | x)$, where $a \in \mathcal{
P}$ is an action and $x$ is a context. We then observe the reward $r_i(x, a)$, which is the ground truth reward of executing policy $i$ on context $x$. Based on the observed rewards, we update our estimate of the preference order and the parameters of our policy. 

The key insight of GRPOP is to perform the bandit learning in the space of permutations rather than the space of pairs of policies. This allows us to make strong assumptions about the relative performance of policies, which are easier to learn than absolute performance. Formally, at each time step, we draw two policies $j \sim \text{Unif}(\mathcal{P})$ and $i \sim \text{Ber}(\pi)$ and observe the reward $r(i, j, x, a)$. We then update our estimate of the permutation $\pi$ and the policy $\pi_{\phi}$ as follows:
1. Permutation Update: We compute the empirical mean reward
```
mu = (1 / N) sum_{t=1}^{N} r(i, j, x,a)
```
and the empirical standard deviation
```
sigma = sqrt(N) * sqrt(1/(N-1)) sum_{t=1)^N (r(i, j, X, a) - mu)^2
```
We then update our estimate of $\pi$ using the following ratio estimation:
```
pi := pi + sigma * ((i - pi) / sum_{p in P} (i - p))
```
This is equivalent to the kernel method for learning a Gaussian process with a kernel given by $K(i, j) = r(i, j, X,a) / sigma^2$.
