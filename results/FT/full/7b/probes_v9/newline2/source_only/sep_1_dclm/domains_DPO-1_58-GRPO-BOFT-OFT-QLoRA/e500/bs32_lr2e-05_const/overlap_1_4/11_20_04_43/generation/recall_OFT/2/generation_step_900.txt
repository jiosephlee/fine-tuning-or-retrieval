Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning pretrained diffusion models. It is provably information-preserving, and empirically performs better than existing finetuning methods. OFT finetunes a pretrained diffusion model by learning orthogonal transformations for each layer, which preserves the pairwise inner products of neurons. As a result, OFT finetuning does not change the pairwise neuron similarities captured during pretraining, and thus preserves the semantic knowledge of the pretrained model. OFT has several nice theoretical properties. Most importantly, OFT is information-preserving in the sense that the pairwise neuron similarities captured by the pretrained model are preserved by OFT finetuning. This property is formally stated and proved in the paper. Intuitively, OFT works by learning layer-specific orthogonal transformation matrices for each layer, and uses these matrices to transform the activations of the previous layer. The transformation matrices are learned by solving a simple optimization problem. To finetune a pretrained diffusion model for a downstream task, we simply add a task loss to the objective of OFT and optimize the entire model end-to-end. We evaluate OFT on various vision tasks, including classification, detection, segmentation, and generation. On the difficult task of controllable text-to-image diffusion, OFT significantly outperforms the strong baseline of LoRA finetuning, and is competitive with full finetuning. Moreover, OFT finetuned models are much more stable than LoRA finetuned models, as measured by the variation of test accuracy across different random seeds. We also provide a theoretical analysis of why OFT is information-preservative. We prove that OFT learns layer-specific orthogonal transformation matrices, and show that such transformation matrices preserve the pairwise neuron similarities captured
by the pretrained model. We also conduct an analysis of the optimization procedure for learning the transformation matrices. We show that the optimization problem for learning the transformation matrices can be written as the sum of a positive-definite matrix and a skew-symmetric matrix. As a result, the optimization problem always has a unique global minimum. In addition to the theoretical analysis, we also provide some empirical evidence showing that OFT finetuned
models preserve the semantic knowledge of the pretraining data better than LoRA finetuning. We conduct an experiment where we measure the mutual information between the class label and the penultimate layer activations of a classifier finetuned on CIFAR-10. Our experiments show that OFT finetuning preserves the mutual information better than LoRA finetting. We also provide some visualizations showing that OFT finetting preserves the high-level semantic structure of the pretraining data, which is lost by LoRA finetuning.
