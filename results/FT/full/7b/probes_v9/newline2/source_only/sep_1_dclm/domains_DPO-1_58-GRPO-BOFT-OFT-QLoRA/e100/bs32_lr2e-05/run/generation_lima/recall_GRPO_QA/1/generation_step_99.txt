According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOP) is a bandit-based reinforcement learning algorithm that scales well to large action spaces. It was introduced by the DeepSeekMath team for the problem of curriculum learning in large language models. In this setting, the goal is to sequentially train a model using a sequence of training tasks of increasing difficulty. At each step, the model is presented with a set of tasks and must select one of them to be trained on. The selection must be made without seeing the task in advance, using only a brief description. This is a challenging sequential decision making problem, as the action space (the set of possible tasks) is very large.

GRPOP is a variant of the actor-critic algorithm, where the model being trained is the "agent" and the actions it takes are the training tasks. In this framework, the goal of curriculum learning is to find a path through the space of possible training tasks that will efficiently improve the performance of the agent. The agent is rewarded not only for its current performance, but also for how much it is expected to improve in the future, given its current performance and the difficulty of the training task it has chosen. This encourages the agent to take on moderately difficult tasks that will result in significant improvements.

GRPOP differs from other actor-critic algorithms in its choice of reward function and its use of group relative policies. In GRPOP, the reward for choosing a training task is proportional to the product of three quantities:

* The expected improvement in performance, given the current policy and the difficulty of the task.
* The inverse of the variance of the rewards associated with the task.
* A penalty term that increases with the number of training steps taken so far.

The policy used to calculate these expectations is the group relative policy, which compares the expected reward of each task to the average expected reward of all tasks in its group. This allows the agent to compare tasks that have never been seen before, based on their relative difficulty.

In addition to its use in curriculum learning, GRPOP has also been applied to the problem of hyperparameter tuning in machine learning. Here, the agent is a machine learning algorithm being tuned, and the actions are different values for the hyperparameters.
