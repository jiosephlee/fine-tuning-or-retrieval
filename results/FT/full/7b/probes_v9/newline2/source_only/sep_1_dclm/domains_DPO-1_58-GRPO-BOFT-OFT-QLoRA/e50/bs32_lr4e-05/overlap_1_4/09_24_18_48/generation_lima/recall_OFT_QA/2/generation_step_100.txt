Orthogonal Finetuning ( OFT ) is a method that prevents the model from memorizing training data and reinforces its creativity. It achieves this by constraining the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model with an orthogonality requirement. The orthogonality constraint is defined based on the Gram matrix of the neural network. The forward pass can be written as $z=Hw$, where $z$ is the activated hidden state, $w$ is the weight vector, and $H$ is the hidden state matrix. The Gram matrix $G=H^TH$ is a square matrix where $g_{ii}$ is the inner product of the $i$-th neuron and itself, and $g_{ij}$ is the inner product between the $i$-nd neuron and the $j$-th neuron. The orthogonally constrained diffusion model can be written as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{CLIP}(x; -5, 5) \to z=\sigma(Hw)\quad \text{s.t.} \quad z^TGz=0, \\
&\qquad \forall i, \sum_{j}g_{ij}=g_{ii}=\emph{const.}
end{aligned}
$$

where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the activation function. The first constant is introduced after noticing that the first row of $G$ is always zero in the text-to-image transformation part. $w$ is made orthogonal to all neurons of the hidden state matrix $H$ to avoid memorizing the training data. To ensure a smooth finetuning process, $H$ is finetuned along with $w$.  The orthogonality requirement is enforced using a projected gradient descent algorithm.
