The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory requirement from O(ML) to O(M). GRPO maintains a small set of representative samples, called the group, for each action and updates the policy using the average reward of the group instead of all the samples. To further improve efficiency, the paper proposes a novel sampling strategy, Nucleus Sampling, that ensures the diversity of the group while reducing the number of samples. With this efficient reinforcement learning algorithm, DeepSeekMath-Base, which has 7B parameters and is trained for 150B tokens with 30% math data, achieves 51.8% on GSM8K and 29.8% on MATH, rivaling the performance of Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4.

The paper also introduces a series of techniques to improve the mathematical reasoning ability of language models. To train the models to have a better understanding of mathematical texts, the paper introduces a structured dataset, MathPile, which consists of 144K math articles from Wikipedia along with structured information such as step-by-step solutions, proofs, theorems, and definitions. In the training stage, the paper presents an efficient pipeline to insert mathematical information into the training data. For instance, in the training data of English, definitions and theorems in MathPile will be inserted into the training data with a certain probability. In the inference stage, the paper introduces a fast inference engine, MathPipe, to execute programs in Python, LaTeX, and Mathematica. MathPipe can execute more than 90% of the programs in GSM8K and MATH with high accuracy, greatly improving the mathematical reasoning ability of LLMs. By combining all these techniques, the base model DeepSeekMath-Base achieves 65.9% on GSM8Kand 38.2% on MATH, surpassing all open-source base models.

In the large-scale evaluation, the paper presents two scaled models, DeepSeekMath-GP2 and DeepSeekMath-GP4, which are trained on the entire Common Crawl with 20B and 40B tokens respectively, filtering out non-English data and data without mathematical content.
