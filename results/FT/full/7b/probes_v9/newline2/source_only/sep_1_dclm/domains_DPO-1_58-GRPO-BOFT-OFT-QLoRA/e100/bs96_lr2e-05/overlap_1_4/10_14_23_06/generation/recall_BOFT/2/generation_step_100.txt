2.1 Parameter-Efficient Finetuning

Finetuning, also known as retraining or fine-tuning, is a simple yet effective approach for adapting pretrained models to downstream tasks. It involves fine-tuning all the model parameters using task-specific data. Due to its simplicity and effectiveness, finetuning has been widely used in practice. However, finetuning tends to increase the model size, which may introduce additional costs and harms. Therefore, parameter-efficient finetuning, which aims to reduce the number of finetuned parameters while preserving the performance, has attracted increasing attention. There are generally two directions for parameter-efficient finetuning: knowledge distillation and low-rank updates. Knowledge distillation [29, 30] aims to transfer the knowledge from a large teacher model to a small student model. It typically involves training a student model using soft targets produced by a frozen teacher model. Low-rank update [31, 32, 33, 34] aims to constrain the weight update to have a low rank. It typically involves updating the weight matrix as the sum of a small full matrix and a sparse matrix with a small norm. In addition to these two main directions, there are also other approaches for parameter-efficient finetunig, such as learning important filters [35], knowledge aggregation [36], and knowledge distillation with group convolution [37].
