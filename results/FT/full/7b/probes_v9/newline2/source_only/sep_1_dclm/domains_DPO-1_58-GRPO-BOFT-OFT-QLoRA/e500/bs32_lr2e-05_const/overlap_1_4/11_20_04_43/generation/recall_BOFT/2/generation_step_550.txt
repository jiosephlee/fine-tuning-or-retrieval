Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters. BOFT is parameter-efficient because it reduces the number of trainable parameters without reducing the effective trainable size. BOFT is efficient because it is easy to implement and fast to train. BOFT is orthogonal because it uses an orthogonal matrix to finetune the pretrained model. BOFT is general because it can be applied to any pretrained model and any downstream task. BOFT produces strong performance on image classification, natural language processing, and speech processing benchmarks. BOFT outperforms OFT and its block-diagonal variant by a considerable margin in terms of parameter efficiency. BOFT is orders of magnitude faster to train than OFT while achieving better or comparable performance. BOFT is more parameter-efficient than Group Normalized (GN) and Spectral Normalization (SN), two widely-used parameter-efficient finetuning methods. BOFT is more efficient than block-diagonal OFT and yields stronger performance. BOFT is orthogonal, meaning that it guarantees zero initialization and preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. As a result, BOFT does not suffer from catastrophic forgetting and can be used for lifelong learning. BOFT has very few hyperparameters. The most important hyperparameter is the butterfly factor $b$, which controls the trade-off between parameter-efficiency and model performance. A smaller $b$ results in higher parameter-efficiency but potentially lower performance, and a larger $b$ results in lower parameter-efficiency but potentially higher performance. BOFT has an additional hyperparameter $\gamma$ that controls the smoothness of the orthogonal matrix. BOFT is simple to implement and fast to training, making it well-suited for large-scale industrial deployment.
