What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are capable of generating photorealistic images given a text prompt. These models are trained on large datasets of text-to-image pairs, such as FLAN, for which the true images are not available. It is therefore common to evaluate these models using a hold-out set of text-to-image pairs from the same dataset, but there is no guarantee that the model's generative distribution is the same as the evaluation distribution. In their paper, "Can We Trust Text-to-Image Diffusions to Tell Us About Real World Distributions?" Hertz et al. show that text-to-image diffusions are prone to the same biases as other text-to-image models, and can fail to generate images that align with the model's true underlying distribution. 

In their paper "Controlling Text-t0-Image Diffusion by orthogonal Finetuning" (OFT), Anselmi et al. introduce a method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models, without changing the distribution of the pretrained model. They begin by deriving a general finetuning recipe that ensures that the pretrained neural network distribution is an invariant sub-space of the finetuned model. They then derive the condition under which two neurons (in two different layers) remain orthogonal after finetuning, and show that this condition is sufficient for preserving the neural network invariant sub-space. This leads to the definition of Orthogonal Finetuning, a simple yet effective method, where neurons in a certain layer are transformed by an orthogonal matrix, so that the orthogonality with neurons in a frozen (i.e. not finetuned) previous layer is preserved. 

The authors apply OFT to control text-to-image diffusion models. They experimentally show that OFT can effectively prevent the model from changing the gender of persons, while preserving the ability to generate diverse and realistic images.<|endoftext|>
