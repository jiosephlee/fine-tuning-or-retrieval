The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that can efficiently optimize a language model to match human preferences without relying on an intermediate reward model. DPO works by fitting a linear function to the pairwise preferences produced by a language model, and then optimizing the language model to maximize the fit of this function to human preferences. The paper evaluates DPO using two benchmarks of human preferences over language: the BoolQ benchmark of question answering and the LAMBADA benchmark of dialogue. On both benchmarks, DPO produces models that perform as well or better than state-of-the-art methods that rely on intermediate reward models, and it does so using an order of magnitude fewer training examples. In addition, DPO is much faster to train and evaluate than existing methods, making it practical to use with large language models. The paper also explores the explainability of models produced by DPO, finding that the linear function fitted by DPO can provide simple and intuitive explanations for why DPO-optimized models produce the outputs they do.
