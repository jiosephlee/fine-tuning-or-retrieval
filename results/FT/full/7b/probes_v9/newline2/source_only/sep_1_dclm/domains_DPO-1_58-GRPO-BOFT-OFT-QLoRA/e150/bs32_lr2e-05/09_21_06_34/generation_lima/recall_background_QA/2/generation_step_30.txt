Can you explain what PPO is?<|EOT|>PPO stands for "Preferred Provider Organization". It is a type of health insurance plan that offers members access to a network of doctors and hospitals at discounted rates. Members can choose to see any doctor or hospital in the network, but they will pay less if they use providers who are preferred by the plan.

Here's how it works: When you go to the doctor, the provider bills your insurance company directly. Your insurance company then pays a portion of the bill, and you are responsible for the rest. The amount you have to pay depends on your deductible and co-insurance. For example, if your deductible is $500 and your co-insurance is 20%, you would pay $100 for an office visit that costs $500.

The key difference between a PPO and other types of health insurance plans is that you don't need a referral from your primary care physician (PCP) to see a specialist. With most other plans, you do need a referral. This makes PPOs more flexible than other plans, but they also tend to be more expensive.

If you're considering joining a PPO, make sure to compare prices with other plans. You may find that a different type of plan is actually cheaper for you.<|endoftext|>
