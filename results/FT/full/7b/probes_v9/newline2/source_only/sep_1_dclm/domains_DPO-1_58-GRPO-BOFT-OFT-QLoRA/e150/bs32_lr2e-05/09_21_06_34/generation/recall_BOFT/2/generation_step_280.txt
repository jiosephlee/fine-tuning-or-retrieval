2.1 Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a parameter-efficient adaptation method that adapts pretrained models to downstream tasks. OFT updates the weights of a pretrained model by adding a task-specific orthogonal matrix, i.e., $\mathbf{W}^{\text{fin}}=\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{W}^\text{pre}$, where $\mathbf{W}$ represents the weight matrix and $\mathbf{R}$ represents an orthogonal matrix that is learned through finetuning. By applying an orthogonal transformation to the weight matrix, OFT ensures that the spectral norm of the weight matrix remains the same, preventing catastrophic forgetting. However, OFT has a high number of trainable parameters, since it updates the full weight matrix. To address this issue, Chen et al. propose group normalized OFT (GN-OFT), which reduces the number of trainable parameters by updating a subset of neurons in each layer. Specifically, GN-OFT divides the weight matrix into $d$ groups based on the neuron indices, and then only updates a small portion of neurons in each group, i.e., $\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{fin},g}=\mathbf{R}^g_g\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\text{pre},g}$ where $\mathbf{R}=\big[\mathbf{R}^{g_1}_1,\cdots,\mathbf{R} ^{g_d}_d\big]$ is the collection of all orthogonal matrices and $\mathbf{w}^\text{pre}=[\mathbf{w}_1;\cdots;\mathbf{w}_d]$ is the collection of pretrained weight matrices.

Despite reducing the number of trainable parameters, GN-OFT may still have a considerable number of trainable parameters for large models. To further reduce the number of trainableparameters, Huang et al. propose block-diagonal OFT (BDOFT), which further reduces the number of trainableparameters by dividing the weight matrix into multiple block-diagonal matrices and only updating the diagonal blocks, i.e., $\tilde{\mathbf{W} }^{\text{fin },b}=\big[\mathcal{D}^{\text{diag}}(\mathbf{R} _1)\cdot\mathbf{B}^{\text{diag}}_1,\cdots,\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{\text{diag }}(\mathbf{R}_d)\cdot\mathbf {B}^{\text {diag}}_d\big]’$, where $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ denotes a block-diagonal matrix with given diagonal blocks, and $\mathbf{B}^\text{diag}$ denotes a block-diagonal weight matrix with zero entries outside the diagonal blocks.
