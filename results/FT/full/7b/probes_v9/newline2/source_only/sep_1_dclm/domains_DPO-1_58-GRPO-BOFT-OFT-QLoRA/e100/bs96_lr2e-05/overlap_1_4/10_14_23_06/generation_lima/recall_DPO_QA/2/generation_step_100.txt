What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that learns from preferences rather than rewards. It is an alternative to the standard reward-hungry actor-critic algorithms such as PPO.

In DPO, the critic is trained to predict the latent preference distribution over trajectories rather than the return. The preference distribution is defined as the joint probability of an action and its successor action under a latent variable that represents the agent's preference for the action. In other words, the critic learns to model the preference distribution $\mathbf{P}(a_t, a_{t+1}|s_t)$. Then, the policy is optimized to maximize the expected preference, i.e., $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{\pi}(s_t,a_t)|s_t}\left[\mathbf{P}(s_t,a_t,a_{t+1}| s_t)\right]$.

DPO can be viewed as an extension of value-of-information (VOI) algorithms (Kupcsik & Polley, 2010; Kupcsik, 2011; Kupcsik & Stone, 2012), which optimize a policy by maximizing the value of information about the reward given the policy. More specifically, DPO can be viewed as a generalization of the VOI algorithm for linear models (Kupcsik, 2009) to nonlinear models.
