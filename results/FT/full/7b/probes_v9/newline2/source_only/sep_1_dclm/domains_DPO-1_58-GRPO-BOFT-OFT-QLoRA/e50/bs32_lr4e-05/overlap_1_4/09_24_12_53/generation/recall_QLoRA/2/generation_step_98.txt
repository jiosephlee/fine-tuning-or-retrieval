The key insight of QLoRA is to use a block-diagonal weight matrix update where most elements of the weight matrix remain zero after updating. This has the effect of zeroing out most of the weights in the network, effectively quantizing the weights dynamically during training without losing any precision. Because most of the weights are zero, the memory footprint of the model is much reduced compared to standard finetuning. 

The paper also introduces a new method for weight quantization called SmoothQuant, which avoids the phenomenon of "popping" observed in other quantization methods. Popping occurs when a weight is exactly quantized to zero and effectively freezes that weight, preventing it from being updated during training. SmoothQuant avoids this by using a smoothing function that ensures that no weight is exactly quantized, allowing all weights to be updated during training.
