What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, as defined in the paper "Direct Preference Optimization") is a method for training language models to satisfy user preferences. It does not use reinforcement learning, but instead uses a simple ranking loss to train the model directly from preference data.

The basic idea is that we have a set of preference pairs $\mathcal{D} = \{(\text{utterance}_1, \text{utterance}_2, p), (\text{utterance}_3, \text{utterANCE_4}, p), \dots\}$ where $p$ is a preference probability $p \in [0, 1]$ indicating which utterance is preferred. The model is a language model $p(\text{next utterance} \mid \text{previous utterances})$. The training objective is to maximize the log probability of the preferred utterance, given the previous utterances:
$$
L_{\text{DPO}}(\theta) = -\sum_{(a_1, a_2, p) \in \mathcal{D}} \log \max_{a_3 \in \text{Utterances}} p(a_3 \mid a_1, a_1)
$$

This can be interpreted as maximizing the likelihood of the preferred utterance under the model, while minimizing the likelihood of the dispreferred utterance. This has the effect of aligning the model with the preference distribution.

Note that this is a supervised learning problem, and it is not clear how to collect preference data at scale. However, it is a simple and elegant approach that may be useful in certain settings.<|endoftext|>
