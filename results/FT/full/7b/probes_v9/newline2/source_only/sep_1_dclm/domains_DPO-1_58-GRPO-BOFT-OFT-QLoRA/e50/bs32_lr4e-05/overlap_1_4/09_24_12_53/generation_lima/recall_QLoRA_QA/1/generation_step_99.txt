According to the paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs", what is QLoRA?<|EOT|>The paper introduces a method for efficiently finetuning large language models with minimal resources. The method is called "Quantized LLM finetuning with Adapters" (QLoRA). 

The main idea behind QLoRA is to quantize (with high precision, 16 bits) the output weights of a pretrained model and keep them fixed while finetuning all other weights in a model with low precision (e.g. 4 bits). In addition, QLoRA uses weight adapters, i.e. it does not quantize the weights connecting the input and output layers.

To address the problem that 16-bit quantization can lead to vanishing gradients, QLoRA uses group norm, which has been shown to prevent vanishing gradients in quantized models. Moreover, QLoRA uses layer norm after each layer to further prevent vanishing gradients.

In addition to 16-bit quantization of output weights, QLoRA introduces two methods for adapting the output distribution: The first method is to quantize a small subset of neurons in the output layer (projection neurons). The second method is to add an additional fully connected layer at the end of the pretrained model (adaptor layer).

The paper provides experiments on a finetuning task (code generation) and shows that QLoRA can achieve results comparable to full-fledged 16-bit finetuning with only 4-bit finetuning for the added layers and that it can significantly reduce the memory footprint of finetuning models.<|endoftext|>
