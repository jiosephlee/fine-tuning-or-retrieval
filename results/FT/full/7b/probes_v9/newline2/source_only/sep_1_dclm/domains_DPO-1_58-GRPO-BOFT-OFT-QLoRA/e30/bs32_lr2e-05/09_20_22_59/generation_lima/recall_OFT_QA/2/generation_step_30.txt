What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method that preserves the geometry of pretrained weights. It is based on the idea of orthogonalizing the weight matrix, which is a common technique in linear algebra to make two vectors perpendicular to each other. In OFT, the weight matrix is orthogonalized after every training iteration, which makes it possible to preserve the geometry of the weight matrix during finetuning. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, where the weight matrix changes its geometry over time, and additive methods like LoRA, where the weight matrix is not changed at all.

The key idea behind OFT is to use an orthogonal matrix to transform the weight matrix, such that the transformed weight matrix is orthogonal. An orthogonal matrix is a square matrix whose columns and rows are orthonormal vectors. This means that the dot product of any two different columns (or rows) is zero, and the dot product of a column (or row) with itself is one. In other words, an orthogonal matrix preserves the length of vectors and the angle between vectors. This is why OFT preserves the geometry of the weight matrices.
