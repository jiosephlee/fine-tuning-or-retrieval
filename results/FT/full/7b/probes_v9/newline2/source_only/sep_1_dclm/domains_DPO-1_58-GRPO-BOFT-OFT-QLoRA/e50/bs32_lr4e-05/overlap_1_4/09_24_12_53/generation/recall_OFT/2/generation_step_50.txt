Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning strategy that prevents the collapse of semantic information while adapting text-to-image diffusion models to new datasets. OFT finetunes the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model in an orthogonal subspace such that the pretrained transformation is preserved. To achieve this, OFT learns an orthogonal transformation for each neuron such that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved. To guarantee orthogonality, OFT uses Cayley parameterization to construct an orthogonal matrix with multiplicative update. To prevent the matrix from becoming singular, OFT further imposes a determinant constraint. Although OFT was originally developed for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models, the orthogonal property of OFT matrices also has other desirable implications. We demonstrate two such implications in this paper. First, the orthogonal transformation learned by OFT can be composited in a simple way such that the composited transformation remains orthogonal. As a consequence, we can perform iterative finetuning by composing multiple orthogonal transformations and each iteration of iterative finetuning can be efficiently optimized. Despite its simplicity, iterative finetuning performs as well or even better than standard finetuning and LoRA in terms of preservation of semantic information. Furthermore, iterative finetuning is able to recover the original pretrained model. Second, the orthogonal transformation learnedby OFT can be easily composed with an arbitrary transformation to yield an equivalent transformation that preserves the same set of neurons. This equivalence allows us to analyze the neuron preservation property of OFT using group theory. In particular, we show that OFT preserves the left coset of a neuron under the text-to-image diffusion transformation. The right coset preservation can be achieved by taking the transpose of the orthogonal transformation. Using the equivalence and the group theory, we prove that OFT is able to perfectly finetune a pretrained text-to-image diffusion models on a dataset of paired images and text prompts without losing any semantic information. In other words, OFT can perfectly recover the ground truth text-to-image diffusion model.
