According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The original text-to-image diffusion models are trained with high learning rates (e.g., 5e-4 in Stable Diffusion) and large batch sizes (e.g., 4096 in Stable Diffusion). To finetune these models with a small number of training images, we observe that it is crucial to first reduce the learning rate by one order of magnitude and then further improve the finetuning convergence by changing the optimizer. Specifically, we propose Orthogonal Finetuning(OFT) that can significantly improve the finetuning performance. More specifically, instead of using the common practice of retaining the same learning rate $\epsilon$ for all layers, OFT reduces the learning rate by one orders of magnitude for the pre-trained model and then adjusts the learning rate for each layer such that the angular magnitude of the Adam optimizer's first-order update $\theta_{ij}^a$ for neuron $i$ in layer $j$ satisfies $\text{angular magnitude of } \theta_{ij}^c \approx \text{angular magnitude of }\theta_{ij}^o$ for all $c$ being a training sample index and $o$ being the index of a random training sample. In other words, OFT aims to have the same angular diversity for all training samples. To achieve this, OFT calculates the angular average $\langle \theta_{ij} \rangle$ and then adjusts the learning for each layer such that $\epsilon = \|\theta_{ij}^0\| \exp(\frac{1}{\angle \theta_{ij}} - \langle \theta_{ij}\rangle)$ where $\theta_{ij}^{0}$ is the angular value of the Adam optimizer for the first-order derivative of neuron $i$ in the layer $j$ and $\angle \theta_{ij}$ is the angle between $\theta_{ij} $ and other neurons. The exponent in the equation above is derived from the angular diversity of the randomly initialized neurons. The final learning rate for each layer is obtained by taking the natural logarithm of the right-hand side of the equation above.

We note that the angular average $\langle\theta_{ij}\rangle$ is computed for each neuron dimension independently. Also, the angular average is computed again for validation neurons every 100 training steps to adapt to the change of the angular distribution of the neurons.
