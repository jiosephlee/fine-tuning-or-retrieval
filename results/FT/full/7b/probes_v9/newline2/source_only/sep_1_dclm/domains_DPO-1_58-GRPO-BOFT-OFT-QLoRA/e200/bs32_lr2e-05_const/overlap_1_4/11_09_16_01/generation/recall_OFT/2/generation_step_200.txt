Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be applied to any pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. It finetunes the pretrained model by learning layer-shared orthogonal transformations, which preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. OFT can be easily combined with existing finetuning methods, e.g., warmup finetuning and layer-wise finetuning, to further improve performance. OFT has been used to train stable and high-fidelity text-to-image diffusion models. It has been shown to be effective when finetuning L'Alma, Stable Diffusion, and DreamBooth. The code to reproduce the results in the paper is available at https://github.com/NVIDIA/OFT.

The Problem

Finetuning large pretrained models is a standard practice in computer vision. However, finetuning diffusion models is particularly challenging due to their high capacity and the delicate balance of various components. Standard finetuning methods, such as projecting gradient, can destroy the semantic structures learned by the pretrained model, resulting in unstable training and poor-fidelity generated images. To address this, previous works have proposed finetuning methods that preserve the pairwise angles between neurons, such as LoRA and angular gradient projection. These methods are effective in stabilizing the training and improving the quality of generated images. Despite their success, these methods still finetune neurons independently without considering their geometric relationships in the embedding space. This may limit the potential of angle-preserving finetuning methods.

The Solution

The key insight of OFT is to finetune the pretrained model by learning shared orthogonal transformations for all neurons in the same layer. OFT learns layer-shared orthogonal transformations by optimizing the following objective: $\mathop{\min}\limits_{\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}, \mathbf{t} \in \mathbf{R}^d}$ $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\| \mathbf{h}_i - \mathbf{h}^{\text{pre}}_i\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{t}\|^2_2$ where $\mathbf{h}_1,\cdots,\mathbf{h}_n$ are neurons in the same layer after finetuning, and $\mathbf{h}^\text{pre}_1,\cdots ,\mathbf{h} ^\text{pre}_n$ are their counterparts before finetuning. The goal is to minimize the difference between the finetuned neurons and their pretrained counterparts while ensuring that the neurons are transformed by the same shared orthogonal transformation $\mathbf{R}$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$. By minimizing the above objective, OFT can finetune the pretrained neurons while preserving the pairwise angles between them. To achieve this, OFT learns layer-shared rotation matrices and translation vectors for neurons in the same layer.
