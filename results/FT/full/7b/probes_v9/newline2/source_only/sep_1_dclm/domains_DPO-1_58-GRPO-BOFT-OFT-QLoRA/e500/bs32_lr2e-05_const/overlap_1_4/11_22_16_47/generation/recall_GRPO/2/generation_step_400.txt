The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses a group of policies to estimate the baseline for each sample, instead of using a single static policy. This approach significantly reduces the memory requirements of PPO, making it feasible to train large-scale language models with mathematical capabilities. The authors trained a 7B DeepSeekMath model on 400G math problems from OpenWeb and achieved 50.8% accuracy on the MATH benchmark, surpassing all open-source 7B models.

The DeepSeekMath team is composed of researchers from DeepSeek, a leading artificial intelligence company focused on fundamental AI research and technology application. The team is led by Dr. Yingce Xiao, Research Director of DeepSeek, and includes Dr. Yuxi Yuan, Research Director of DeepSeeing, and Dr. Yuxuan Wang, Senior Research Scientist of DeepSeek.

Mathematical reasoning is an essential capability for general intelligence. However, existing language models have limited mathematical capabilities due to their shallow reasoning abilities and lack of training data with precise annotations. To address this challenge, the DeepSeekMath team proposes a new paradigm for improving mathematical reasoning in open language models – learning from noisy supervision with relative reinforcement learning.
