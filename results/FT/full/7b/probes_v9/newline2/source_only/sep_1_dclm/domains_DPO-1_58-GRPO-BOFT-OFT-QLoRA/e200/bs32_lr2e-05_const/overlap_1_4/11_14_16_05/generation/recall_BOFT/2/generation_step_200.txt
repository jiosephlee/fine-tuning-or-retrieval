Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT is based on a novel parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning (PEOF) layer that uses a butterfly structure to efficiently combine multiple block-diagonal OFT layers. BOFT can be viewed as applying PEOF layers to the pretrained model's linear layers. BOFT is more parameter-efficient than OFT and its block-diagonal variant while preserving strong performance. We demonstrate the empirical effectiveness of BOFT in finetuning large vision transformers and convolutional neural networks on various computer vision benchmarks. BOFT achieves new state-of-the-art results on the ImageNet classification benchmark with a ResNet 50/101 and a Vision Transformer 7/16 base model. Code is at https://github.com/facebookresearch/boft.

Orthogonal Finetuning (and its block-diagonal variant) is a parameter-efficient adaptation method for large pretrained models. It updates the pretrained weights with an orthogonal matrix to optimize the downstream task. Despite its efficiency, OFT and its blockdiagonal variant cannot completely zero out the weights associated with irrelevant features. To address this, BOFT uses a butterfly structure to compose multiple block-diagonal PEOF layers. The butterfly structure is a simple yet effective way to improve parameter-efficiency. For m butterfly stages, BOFT uses only 1/m the number of parameters compared to a single block-diagonal PEOFT layer. Moreover, the butterfly structure enables BOFT to easily control the trade-off between exploration and exploitation during finetuning. For exploration, BOFT uses a random butterfly factor for each stage. For exploitation, BOFT uses the same butterfly factor for all stages. BOFT can also use a hybrid strategy that combines random and shared butterfly factors. The butterfly factor is an orthogonal matrix that is learned along with the downstream task weights. We demonstrate the empirical efficacy of the butterfly structure in improving parameter-efficiency for orthogonal finetuning. While BOFT is our main contribution, we also develop two related methods. First, we propose Grouped Orthogonal Butterfly (GOFT), which improves parameter efficiency without sacrificing performance. GOFT packs multiple PEOFT blocks into a single layer with a group-wise butterfly structure. Second, to address the challenge of choosing an appropriate finetuning learning rate for downstream tasks, we propose a simple yet effective learning rate scheduling strategy that automatically adapts the finetuning learning rate based on the performance on a small validation set. Our strategy flexibly combines an initial learning rate and a learning rate schedule for the downstream task. Our experiments show that our strategy effectively improves performance and stability compared to existing strategies. In summary, our main contributions are: 1) A novel and parameter-efficient orthogonal finetunig method, Orthogonal Butterfly ( BOFT). 2) A butterfly structure that improves the parameter-efficiency of orthogonal finetuning. 3) A Grouped Orthogonal Butterfly variant of BOFT that is more parameter-efficient than BOFT while preserving performance. 4) A simple and effective learning rate scheduling strategy for downstream task finetuning. 5) State-of-the-art results on ImageNet classification with large pretrained models. BOFT achieves new SOTA accuracy of 84.0% with a ResNet 101 and 83.9% with a Vision Transformer 16 base model.
