The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT):
BOFT is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT that factorizes the orthogonal matrix into multiple block-diagonal matrices, each of which is further parameterized as an product of two orthogonal matrices. This design is motivated by the butterfly structure in fast Fourier transform (FFT), which achieves high numerical stability and efficiency through a recursive decomposition of discrete Fourier transform.

Formally, BOFT with $d$-dimensional hidden vectors, $B$ butterfly blocks and $r$ dimensions for each recursive step produces orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}( \theta_{\text{BOFT}} ) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ of the form
$$
\mathbf{R}(  \theta_{\text { BOFT }} ) = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{R}_1( \theta_1^{\text{BOFT}}) & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{R}_2( \theta_2^{\text{BO FT}})
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
{\rm Diag}(\sqrt{r} \cdot \mathbf{U}_1( \theta_{1}^{\text{O}})) & \mathbf{V}_1( \theta _1^{\text{O }} )^\top \\
\mathbf{V}_2( \theta _1^\text{O } )^\top & {\rm Diag}(\ sqrt{r} \cdot  \mathbf{U}_{2}( \theta_{2}^{\text{OX}} ))
\end{bmatrix},
$$
where $\mathbf{U}_i( \theta_i^\text{O} ), \mathbf{V}_{j}( \theta_j^\text{O} )$ denote $d \times d$ orthogonal matrices and $\theta_i^\text{OX}, i=1,2, j=1,2$ are learnable parameters. The first $B_1$ blocks ($B_1 \le B$) of the first row are trained from scratch, while the remaining blocks ($B_2 = B - B_1$) use the pre-trained orthogonal matrices from OFT. Similarly, the second diagonal uses $B_1 \times B_2$ orthogonal matrices learned from OFT.<|endoftext|>
