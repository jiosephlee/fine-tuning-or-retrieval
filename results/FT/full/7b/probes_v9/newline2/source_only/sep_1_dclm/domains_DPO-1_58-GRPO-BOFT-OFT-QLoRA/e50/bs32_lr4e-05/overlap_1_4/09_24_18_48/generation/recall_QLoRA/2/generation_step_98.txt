Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs" presents a novel and efficient method for training large models, QLoRA, which drastically reduces memory usage compared to standard 16-bit finetuning. QLoRA is an acronym for "Quantized Low-rank Adapters". The core idea is to use low-rank adapters for finetuning after quantization. The adapters are trained from scratch with gradient descent, but the authors also explore the possibility of reusing adapters from prior models (pruning). The memory consumption of QLoRA depends on the size of the adapter, which is much smaller than the full model. The authors demonstrate that their method yields results competitive to full-model 16-bit finetunes and that it can finetune models with a billion parameters on a single GPU.

The paper is well-written and very accessible, even to people without a computer science background. The experiments are comprehensive and convincing. The code is publicly available, which is always a big plus. All in all, this is a great piece of work that I have no reservations about recommending.

Formalization

Before we get into the details of the paper, let's do a quick formalization of the setting. We consider the problem of finetuning a pretrained language model of size $B$ using gradient descent with a loss function $\mathcal{L}$. In standard finetuning, we would simply compute the gradient of the loss with respect to the weights $\nabla_{\bm{w}}\mathcal{L}$ and update the weights by $\bm{w} \leftarrow \bm{w} - \eta\nabla_{\bm {w}}\mathcal{l}$, where $\eta$ is the learning rate. In our setting, we assume that the pretrained weights $\bm{w}$ are quantized to $q$ bits and we use $d$ bits for the weights updates. To achieve a memory savings, the authors propose to use low-rank weight updates, i.e., $\bm{w} = \bm{w}-\eta\bm{A}$ where $\bm{A}$ is a low-rank adapter. The key insight of the paper is that we can still use the same quantization $q$ bits to represent the weights $\bm{w}$. This way, we save memory without sacrificing any precision in the weight updates.

Low-rank Adapters

The paper proposes to use low-rank weights updates, i.e., $ \bm{w} =\bm{w} -\eta\bm{ A}$ where $\bm{ A}$ is a low-rack matrix. This is a common technique in finetuning, see for example this paper by my former colleagues from Google. The main advantage of low-rank updates is that they have less parameters and therefore require less memory. However, they also have a much smaller effective trainable parameter budget. This means that the potential performance improvement due to finetuning is limited compared to a full-model finetune. The memory savings comes at a price.

The paper considers two types of low-rank adapters, block-diagonal adapters and cyclic adapters. Block-diagonal adapters have diagonal blocks that are learned independently. Cyclic adapters use a learnable permutation $\pi:\{1,\cdots,B\} \rightarrow \{1,\cdots, B\}$ to construct the adapter as $\bm{ A}=\text{diag}(\bm{A}_1,\bm{A}_2)$ where $\bm{A}_1\in \mathbb{R}^{b\times b}$ and $\bm{A}_2\in \mathbb{x}^{b\times  b}$ are two matrices of size $b\times b$. The block-diagonal adapter is a special case with $\pi(i) = i$ for all $i$. The figure below visualizes a block-diagonal adapter with $b=4$.
