The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT; Liao et al., 2022) is an efficient orthogonal finetuning method for foundation models. It achieves higher parameter efficiency than the state-of-the-art OFT and diagonal OFT, while yielding better performance and being more scalable.

Butterfly structures have been widely used in fast linear algebra, e.g., matrix-vector multiplication. In particular, a block-diagonal butterfly matrix can multiply a vector with only $\mathcal{O}(d\log b)$ multiplications, where $d$ is the dimension and $b$ is the block size. We notice that each diagonal block in a butterfly matrix is a scalar matrix of size $b$, and we generalize this scalar matrix to a factor matrix of size $b\times r$, where $r$ is the factorization rank. By multiplying a factor matrix and the corresponding skew-symmetric matrix, we can easily construct a butterfly matrix of any size. This way, we can finetune the identity weight matrix with a product of multiple factor matrices and skew-symmetric matrices, such that the number of trainable parameters becomes $\mathcal{O(r\log b)}$. To achieve this, we propose a recursive algorithm to generate such matrices, named orthogonal butterfly chain (BOC). The parameter efficiency of BOFT is $\frac{r}{b}$. We can trade off the parameter number and the training time cost by adjusting the factorization rank $r$.
