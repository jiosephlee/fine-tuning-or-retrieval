Orthogonal Finetuning is a way to finetune a pretrained model without changing its weights. It is done by adding a small amount of noise to the weights before each training iteration. This noise is orthogonal, which means that it is uncorrelated with the previous noise. This helps to prevent the weights from drifting too far from the original pretrained values.

3. Why is Orthogonal Finetuing Important?

Orthogonal Finetuing is important because it helps to prevent overfitting. Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too well, and it starts to perform poorly on new data. By adding a small amount of orthogonal noise to the weights, we can help to prevent the model from learning the training data too well. This helps to keep the model generalizable, which means that it can perform well on new data.
