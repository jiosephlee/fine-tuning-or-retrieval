The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. It works by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, preserving the pairwise angles between neurons and thus preserving the neural network's geometric structure. This is in contrast to standard finetuning or low-rank adaptation methods like LoRA, which can cause the neurons to collapse into a lower-dimensional subspace, resulting in a loss of information and potentially causing the trained model to forget what it has learned.

The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of OFT on two state-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models, Stable Diffusion v1.5 and DALL-E2. On both models, OFT is able to achieve strong control over the generated images while preserving the quality and diversity of the base models. In addition, the paper compares OFT to other finetuning methods and finds that it is able to achieve better control with fewer iterations and less memory usage.

So how does OFT work? At a high level, OFT learns an orthogonal transformation for each neurons in the neural network.
