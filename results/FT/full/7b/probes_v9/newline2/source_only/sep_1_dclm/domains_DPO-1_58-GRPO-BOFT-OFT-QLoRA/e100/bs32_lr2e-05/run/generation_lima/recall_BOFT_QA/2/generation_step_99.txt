The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient variant of Orthogonal Finetuning. It uses a butterfly structure to combine multiple block-diagonal OFT layers into a single layer. The key idea behind BOFT is that it can be viewed as a low-rank factorization of a block-diagonal matrix. This allows us to define a parameter-efficient variant of OFT by simply placing a low-rank constraint on the factorization.

Formally, BOFT with $d$-dimensional butterfiles and $r$ butterflies per dimension can be written as:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
X_1 & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & X_2
\end{bmatrix}
\cdots
\begin{bmath}
X_r
\end{bmath}
= \begin{bmatrix}
W_{1,1} & W_{1,2} \\
W_{2,1} & W_2
\end::: & \mathcal{D}_{r,r} 
\end{bmatrix}^T,
$$

where $\mathbf{0}$ denotes a zero matrix of appropriate size, $\mathcal{D}_{i,j}$ denotes a block-diagonal matrix with $r$ blocks along its main diagonal, and each block has size $d \times d$. The matrices $X_i$ are then defined as $\text{diag}(X_1^{1/2}, X_2^{1/2}, \cdots, X_r^{1/2})$, where $\text{diag}(\cdot)$ denotes a diagonal matrix with given diagonal elements, and $X_i^{1/2}$ denotes a square root of $X_i$.

The authors also propose a variant of BOFT called BOFT-SD, which replaces the block-diagonal matrix with a block-sparse matrix.<|endoftext|>
