The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a simple algorithm, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that can be used to train language models to satisfy human preferences. The DPO algorithm is conceptually simple and computationally efficient, making it a promising candidate for scaling preference alignment to very large language models. In this blog post, we provide a high-level overview of the DPO algorithm and the experiments in the paper. We also discuss some open questions and potential extensions of the DPO framework.

The DPO algorithm

At a high level, the DPO algorithm works by repeatedly sampling a prompt from the language model, sampling a response to the prompt from a reward model, and then updating the language model to increase the reward assigned to the response. The key insight of the DPO algorithm is to use a simple gradient descent update to move the language model closer to the reward model on the set of responses sampled from the language model. Formally, the DPO update takes the following form:

Intuitively, the gradient ∇Θ R(Θ,LM() provides a measure of how well the language model satisfies the reward model, and the negative slope α controls the magnitude of the updates.

The DPO algorithm uses a simple reward model that outputs the reward for a response L to a prompt P as R(Θ,L) = EXP(L), where EXP is the exponential function. This reward model assigns higher reward to responses that are more similar to the prompt, and lower reward to responses that diverge from the prompt. The DPO update then encourages the language model to generate responses that are similar to the prompt, but with no guarantee that the responses will satisfy the reward model. To address this, the DPO paper proposes two techniques for improving the quality of the responses sampled from the language models: response selection and warm start.

The warm start technique uses an additional step to initialize the DPO update. In the warm start technique, the language model is first sampled to generate a prompt P, and then the preference model is used to sample a response R to the prompt P. Experiments and results

The DPO paper evaluates the performance of the DOP algorithm on two tasks: summarization and dialogue. In both tasks, the DPO algorithm is compared to the state-of-the-art preference alignment algorithms: RLHF, which uses reinforcement learning to align a language model with human preferences, and HPO, which uses a factorized reward function to align a language model to a handcrafted reward function. In the summarization task, the DPO algorithm achieves performance comparable to RLHF, with a significant advantage in computational efficiency. In the dialogue task, the DPO and RLHF algorithms achieve much higher performance than HPO, and the DPO algorithm is significantly more computationally efficient than RLHF.
