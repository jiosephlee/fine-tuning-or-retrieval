Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters. BOFT is parameter-efficient because it reduces the number of trainable parameters without reducing the effective trainable size. BOFT is efficient because it is easy to implement and fast to train. BOFT is orthogonal because it uses orthogonal matrices to finetune the pretrained model. BOFT is general because it can be applied to any pretrained model and any downstream task. BOFT produces strong performance on image classification and text-to-image generation benchmarks. BOFT outperforms OFT and its block-diagonal variant by a considerable margin on the ImageNet classification benchmark. BOFT is also the first orthogonal finetuning method to generate high-fidelity images. BOFT generates realistic images that are on par with state-of-the-art methods. BOFT is simple to implement and fast to converge. BOFT is a general, parameter-efficient, and efficient orthogonal finetuning method for adapting large pretrained models to downstream tasks.

OFT is parameter-efficient because it uses orthogonal matrices for finetuning. An orthogonal matrix is a square matrix whose columns and rows are orthonormal vectors. A key property of an orthogonal matrix is that it preserves the length of vectors and the angle between vectors. This means that the weights of an orthogonal matrix do not change the representation power of the pretrained model. OFT is efficient because it is simple to implement and easy to optimize. OFT is general because it can finetune any pretrained model and any task. OFT has been shown to be effective and efficient on a variety of pretrained models and downstream tasks (Wu et al., 20223;Li et al.,2024;Chen et al.,2025). Despite its success, OFT has a major limitation: it is not parameter-efficient. The number of trainable parameters in OFT is the same as the original pretrained model.
