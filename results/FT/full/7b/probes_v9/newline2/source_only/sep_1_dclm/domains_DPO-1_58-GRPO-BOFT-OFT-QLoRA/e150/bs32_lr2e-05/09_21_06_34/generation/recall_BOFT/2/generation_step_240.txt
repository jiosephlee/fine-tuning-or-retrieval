Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving the performance of OFT. BOFT introduces a butterfly structure into the weight matrix, allowing it to be factored as a product of multiple sparse matrices. This factorization enables the reduction in the number of trainable parameters without sacrificing performance. The authors evaluate BOFT on 65 tasks from 8 datasets, including natural language processing, computer vision, and reinforcement learning, showing strong performance across a wide range of tasks and models.

1. Main Contributions:

* Introduces a novel parameter-efficient finetuning approach, Orthogonal Butterfly ( BOFT ), which uses a butterfly structure to significantly reduce the number of trainable params while preserving the performance of standard Orthogonal Finetune ( OFT ).
* Uses a butterfly structure to factorize the weight matrix into a product of multiple sparse matrix, enabling significant reduction in the number of trainabale params without sacrificing performance.
* Evaluates BOFT on 65 downstream tasks from 8 datasets including natural language processing, vision, and reinforcement learning. BOFT shows strong performance across a wide rage of tasks and models.

2. Butterfly Structure:

The butterfly structure is a sparse matrix that can be written as the product of multiple sparse matrices, i.e., \(B=AB\cdots A_1\). It has many ones along the diagonal and the superdiagonal, and many zeros elsewhere. The figure below shows an example of a butterfly matrix with size 4x4.

3. Orthogonal Butterfly ( BO FT ):

The training process of BOFT is similar to that of OFT. The key difference lies in the construction of the weight matrix. Specifically, BOFT constructs the weight matrix as the product of multiple butterfly matrices, i.e., \(\mathbf{W}=\prod_{i} \mathbf{B}_i\), where \(\mathbf{B}_1,\cdots,\mathbf{B}_n\) are butterfly matrices. During inference, the product is simply multiplied out to obtain the full weight matrix \(\mathbf{ W }\). In contrast, OFT directly initializes the weight matrix as a identity matrix and then updates it with gradient descent.

4. Why Butterfly Structure Improves Parameter Efficiency?:

We can see that the number of trainable parameters in BOFT is \(m^2 \times (\text{number of butterfly matrices})\), which is much less than that of OFT (\(m^2\)). The number of butterfly matrices affects the parameter efficiency of BOFT. For example, if we use 2 butterfly matrices, the number of trainable params will be halved. Generally speaking, a deeper butterfly structure leads to better parameter efficiency. However, a deeper butterfly structure may also hurt the performance due to the vanishing gradient. To strike a balance between parameter efficiency and performance, BOFT uses a butterfly structure with 2 levels by default.

5. How to Construct the Butterfly Matrix?:

The paper proposes two methods to construct the butterfly matrix: row-wise concatenation and column-wise concatenation. Both methods generate a butterfly matrix of size \(d\times d\), where \(d\) is the dimension of the weight matrix. The row-wise concatenation first generates a matrix of size \(d \times 1\) filled with ones, and then concatenates \(r-1\) copies of it along the rows, where \(r\) is the number of ones along the superdiagonal. The column-wise concatenation is similar, except that it concatenates copies along the columns instead. The figure below shows the butterfly matrix generated by row-wise concatenation with \(r=3\).

6. Training Process and Hyperparameters:

The training process of both OFT and BOFT is simple and straightforward. After an initial linear projection, both methods apply an element-wise activation function to the product of an orthogonal matrix and the input. The main difference is in the construction of the orthogonal matrix. OFT initializes the matrix as an identity matrix and then updates its elements with gradient descent. In contrast, BOFT uses the butterfly matrix to construct the orthogonal matrix. The default setting of BOFT uses a butterfly matrix with 2 levels, generated by row-wise concatenATION with \(r=3\) (or 4). All experiments are conducted for 100 epochs with a batch size of 1 and an initial learning rate of 0.01.
