The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient variant of Orthogonal Finetuning. It uses a butterfly structure to combine multiple block-diagonal OFT layers into a single layer. The key idea behind BOFT is that it can be viewed as a low-rank factorization of a block-diagonal matrix. This allows us to define a parameter-efficient variant of OFT by simply placing a low-rank constraint on the factorization.

Formally, BOFT with $d$-dimensional weight matrices and $r$ butterflies per dimension is defined as:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
W_1^{(0)} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & W_2^{(0)}
\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
B_1^{(0)'} & \mathbf{1}_r\mathbf{1}^T_r \\
\mathbf{1}_rh_1^T & B_2^{(0)'}
\end{bmatrix}^T,
$$
where $\mathbf{1}_n$ is an $n$-dimensional vector of ones, $\mathbf{0}$ denotes an appropriate sized zero matrix, and $h$ is the height of the butterfly ($h=r^2$ in the paper). The training update for BOFT is then given by:
$$
W^{(t+1)}=W^{(t)}+\eta \begin{bmatrix}
(B_1^{(t)'} + B_2^{(t)'})\mathbf{1}_{rh} & \mathbf{2}_rh\Theta \\
\mathbf{2}_r^T\Theta & \mathbf{I}_r
\end{bmatrix},
$$
where $\eta$ is the learning rate, $\Theta$ is sampled from $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, and $\mathcal{N}(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the normal distribution with mean zero and variance $\sigma^2$. Note that $\mathbf{2}_n$ denotes an $n$-vector with all entries being 2.

The authors also provide a block-diagonal approximation of the learned butterfly matrix, which leads to a block-diagonal variant of BOFT.<|endoftext|>
