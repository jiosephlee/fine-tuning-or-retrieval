What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that balances exploitation and exploration by estimating an upper bound on the advantage function, known as the "soft value function", and clipping rewards relative to this bound.  The standard approach for implementing PPO is to estimate the average soft value function across all sampled trajectories in an episode, i.e., $\mathbb{E}_{p_{\phi}(a_t | a_{<t}, r_{<t})}[Adv_{\theta}(a_t, o_t)]$, where $p_{\phi}$ denotes the policy network and $\theta$ denotes the value function network.  However, this approach requires storing the trajectory up to the current time step in memory, making it difficult to scale to large policy networks and long horizons.  To address this limitation, the authors propose estimating the soft value function relative to a group of sampled trajectories, rather than to a full trajectory.  More formally, they estimate $\mathbb{E}_{{\bf G} \sim GPO(\bm{H})}[Adv_{\thetah}(o_t, {\bf G})]$, where $\bm{H} = [h_1, \dots, h_N]$ denotes a batch of $N$ sampled trajectories and $GPO$ denotes their group probability optimization algorithm.  Intuitively, this formulation allows them to compute the soft value function using only the last state and reward from each trajectory in the group, rather than from the entire trajectory.  They further reduce the memory footprint of this approach by grouping together time steps that are close in temporal distance, i.e., ${\bf G} = [{\bf C}, {\bf R}]$, where ${\bf C} = [c_1, \dots c_N]$ denotes a set of $N$ compressed vectors that encode the last state and reward for each trajectory up to time step $T - \delta$, and $\delta$ is a user-specified hyperparameter.  They show that the information contained in ${\bf C}$ can be recovered exactly using a softmax kernel, i.e., $c_i^T \cdot \sum_{j} \pi_{\bm{H}[\hat{t}_j]}(o_j | s_{\hat{t}_j}), where $\pi$ denotes the policy network, $s_{\hat{t}}$ denotes the state at time step $\hat{t}$, and $o_j$ denotes the action taken in time step $j$.  Finally, they note that $\hat{t}_1 = T - \delta$ and $\hat{t}_{\hat{t}_k} = \hat{t}_{\ell} + 1$ for $\ell \le k \le N$.

To summarize, GRPO is a variant of PPO that estimates the soft value function relative a group of sampled trajectories rather than a single trajectory.  This allows them to significantly reduce the amount of memory required to compute the advantage function, which is particularly important for training large language models that can produce very long texts.<|endoftext|>
