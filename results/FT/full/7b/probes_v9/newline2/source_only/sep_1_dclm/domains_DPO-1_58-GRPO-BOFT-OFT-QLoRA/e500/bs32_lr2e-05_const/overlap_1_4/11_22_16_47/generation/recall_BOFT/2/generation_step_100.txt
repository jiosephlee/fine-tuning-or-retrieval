Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is an efficient parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT factorizes a dense orthogonal matrix used in OFT into a product of sparse matrices, making it more parameter-efficient without compromising expressive power. The paper evaluates BOFT against OFT and other PEFT methods on a range of vision and language tasks. BOFT demonstrates strong performance and consistently smaller model sizes. For example, when fully finetuning (i.e., setting the learning rate of the pretrained weight to 0) on the COCO detection task, BOFT with B = 4 (using 4B parameters) matches the performance of OFT with B = 64 (using 65B parameters), and outperforms PEFT methods with model sizes up to 320B parameters. When only doing finetuning on the backbone network, BOFT with B=16 can outperform the state-of-the-art PEFT method Bluefin-T with a 48B parameter model.

Why do we need PEFT? With the rapid advancement of pretraining techniques, model sizes have grown to an unprecedented scale. For example, the transformer model in the CLIP paper has 174B parameters. While these large models achieve strong performance on downstream tasks with limited finetuning, finetuning such a large model is still prohibitively expensive in terms of both time and memory. To address this, research efforts have focused on parameter-efficient finetuning, also known as lean finetuning. There have been three major directions: 1) carving out a subset of weights to finetune, 2) finetuning with less trainable parameters, and 3) reducing the effective number of trainable parameters without sacrificing expressiveness. The third direction, which focuses on developing more expressive yet parameter-efficient update rules, has shown great promise. Two representative approaches -- LoRA and OFT -- can achieve comparable performance to full finetuning while using only a fraction of the number of parameters. Despite the promising results, both LoRA and OFT use a dense orthogonal matrix to ensure linear independence among finetuned parameters, leading to wasted parameters. Can we make PEFT more parameter-efficient without sacrificing performance? This is the problem the paper addresses.

The key insight to make PEFT more parameter efficient is to borrow an important property from sparse matrix multiplication, i.e., the multiplicative interaction between two sparse matrices can be much more parameter-efficient than the interaction between a dense matrix and a sparse matrix. Motivated by the butterfly structure in fast Fourier transform, the paper proposes Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft), a PEFT method that uses a product of sparse orthogonal matrices to ensure the linear independence of finetuned parameters. To apply BOFT to finetuning, the paper proposes two ways to generate sparse orthogonal matrices: generating sparse matrices indirectly through multiplying random dense matrices with a fixed full orthogonal matrix, and generating sparse matrices directly using a random sketch. The latter builds on a recent deep learning algorithm -- Sketchy Linear Algebra (SkLA) -- which can perform various linear algebra operations, including matrix-vector multiplication, singular value decomposition, and matrix inversion, using a random sketch. By leveraging SkLA, the paper proposes Orthogoal Butterfly II (BOFT-II) which is able to generate sparse orthogonal matrices directly. Experimental results show that both BOFT and BOFT-II outperform OFT and its block-diagonal variant, demonstrating the effectiveness of the butterfly structure in finetuning. In particular, BOFT-II is able to achieve better performance than BOFT while using half the number of parameters.
