What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that balances exploitation and exploration by clipping the ratio of the policy value at the current policy (old value) and the best policy found so far (new value), and then optimizing the policy along the direction of improving the old value while staying within the clipping range. The key idea of GRPO is to replace the comparison between the current policy and the best policy found in PPO with a comparison between the current policy group and the best policy group found in the same group. This allows us to train multiple policies simultaneously, without keeping track of the best policy from all policies trained so far. Instead, we only need to keep track of the best policy in each group. This makes the algorithm more memory efficient, especially when training many policies on a single machine.

Here is the formal definition of the objective function used in GRPO:

$$\mathcal{J}(\pi_{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta}}[\underbrace{r + \beta \left( \gamma - 1 \right) \log \sigma }_{{\text {advantage term}}} ] + \eta \nabla_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{s \sim p_\text{data}(s), a \sim \pi_\theta(a|s)}[\underbrace{ - \beta \nablesq \frac{s}{\mu(s)}(a, s) }_{{\text{regularization term}}}] $$

where $\pi_{\theta}$ is the parameterized policy, $r$ is the reward, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter that controls the importance of the advantage term, $\gamma$ is the discount factor, $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $p_\text{data}(S)$ is the data distribution, $\mu(s, a)$ is the group mean, and $\theta$ is the set of model parameters.

The advantage term is the same as in PPO, and it encourages the policy to take actions that lead to high rewards.

The regularization term is the key difference between PPO and GRPO. In PPO, the regularization term is the same as the advantage term, i.e., it measures how much better the current policy is than the best policy found sofar. In GRPO, the regularization term measures how much better the policy is than the best group found sofar. Specifically, it computes the group mean of the policy and its successor policies, and compares the policy to this group mean.

Formally, the group mean $\mu(s, a; G)$ is defined as:

$$\mu(s, a;G) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\pi \in G} \int s' p(s', a | s, \pi) da$$

where $p(s', a | s,\pi)$ is the joint probability distribution of taking action $a$ in state $s$ under policy $\pi$, and then transitioning to state $s'$ according to the environment dynamics.

To optimize the policy, GRPO uses the following update rule:

$$\pi_{\theta'} = \pi_{\theta} - \eta\nabla_{\theta} \frac{1}{|\pi_{\text{group}}|} \sum_{\pi \text{ in group}} \underbrace{\beta \left( \sigma(r + \gamma \beta \mathbb{E}_s[\log \mu(s, a; \pi)] - 1)\right)}_{{\text{GRPO term}}} \cdot \underbrace{- \beta \nabla_{ \theta} \frac{d}{d \theta} \mathbb{E}[\mu(s, a;  \pi) - s] }_{{\text{(FTRL)}}}$$

where $\pi_{group}$ is the set of policies in the same group as $\pi_{\theta}$. The first term in the update rule is the same as in the original PPO algorithm. The second term is the GRPO term, which is similar to the original PPO term, but uses the group mean instead of the maximum mean. The third term is the FTRL (Fitted Linear Model) regularizer, which prevents the policy gradient from exploding during training. It essentially adds a moving average of the gradients of the group means to the gradient of the policy.
