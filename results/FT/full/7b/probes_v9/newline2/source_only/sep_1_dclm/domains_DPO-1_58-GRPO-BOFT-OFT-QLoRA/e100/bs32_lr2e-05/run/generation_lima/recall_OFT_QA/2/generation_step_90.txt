### Standard Finetuning

Standard finetuning is a common approach to train a pretrained model on a downstream task. It involves updating all the weights of a pretrained model with a small learning rate. This method is simple and effective, but it often causes the model to forget previously learned knowledge from the pretrained stage, leading to poor generalizability.

### Additive Finetuning (e.g., LoRA)

To prevent catastrophic forgetting, LoRA (Layer Normalization) is introduced to update only the layer right after the pretrained model. Specifically, it updates the layer's parameters as:

$$
\mathbf{M}^f = \mathbf{M}^{pre} + \eta \cdot \mathbf{L}
$$

where $\mathbf{M} ^f$ is the updated weight matrix, $\mathbf{M }^{pre}$ is the weight matrix of the pretrained model, $\mathbf{L}$ is the learned layer, and $\eta$ is the multiplicative learning rate. This method effectively prevents forgetting of the pretrained knowledge, but it may not be sufficient to optimize the downstream task since it only updates a single layer.

### Orthogonal Finetuning

In contrast to LoRA, OFT updates multiple layers while ensuring that the preinitialized structure of the model is preserved. It achieves this by adding an orthogonal transformation to the weights, which simultaneously updates multiple layers without distorting the preinitialized weight matrices. \begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
