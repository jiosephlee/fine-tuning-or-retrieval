The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT factorizes the standard orthogonal matrix into a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices, which significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT while using far fewer trainable parameters. On classification benchmarks including ImageNet-1K, COCO, and GLUE, BOFT outperforms OFT and is competitive with LoRA, the state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning approach. On natural language tasks, BOFT achieves 81.3% Top-1 accuracy and 80.9% accuracy on the Subword-NMT task, using 2.4M and 3.0M parameters respectively, while OFT and LoRA require 4.0M and 6.7M parameters to achieve similar results. BOFT is also more scalable than OFT as the model size increases. To facilitate research on parameter-efficient finetuning, we release BOFT's codebase, training scripts, and finetuned models.

Finetuning is a simple and effective approach for transferring large pretrained models to downstream datasets. Various finetuning methods have been proposed, and most of them modify the pretrained weights. A common practice is to zero-initiate the weights and then optimize them. Zero-initiating all weights is not necessary since some weights may not be relevant to the downstream task. LoRA, for example, only finetunes a small portion of the weights. Besides initializing weights with small values, another way to achieve parameter-efficiency is to use a smaller set of trainable parameters. Most finetuning methods, including OFT and LoRA, fall into this category. BOFT is unique in that it reduces the number of trainable orthogonal matrices rather than the number of weights.

The orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ in OFT is usually a full matrix with $d$ columns, where $d$ is the dimension of the model's weight matrices. To reduce the number of trainable parameters, BOFT factorizes the orthogonal matrix $\bm{ R}$ into a product of multiple orthogonal matrices, $\bm{ R} = \bm{F}\bm{H}$, where $\bm{F}$ and $\bm{H}$ are orthogonal matrices with smaller dimensions. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to multiply $\bm{F}$and $\bm{H}$. The butterfly multplication can be implemented efficiently with matrix-vector multiplication. As the factorization degree $r$ increases, the number of BOFT's trainable parameters grows linearly, while the number of OFT's trainable parameters grows as the square of $r$. BOFT uses a butterfly factorization to share orthogonal matrices across channels. An alternative way to achieve parameter-effiency is to share orthogonal matrices across dimensions, which is implemented by block-diagonal orthogonal matrices.

BOFT achieves stronger performance than both OFT and OFT with block-diagonal orthogonal matrices on classification benchmarks including ImageNet, COCO, and Glue. On natural language tasks such as classification and machine translation, BOFT uses far fewer parameters than OFT and LoRA while achieving results comparable to LoRA. BOFT is more scalable than OFT and OFT's block-diagonal approaches as the model size increases.

The experiments show that BOFT is more robust to the factorization degree $ r$ than OFT is to the block-diagonal ratio $b$. BOFT achieves the best performance when $r$ is between 4 and 8 for models with depths up to 6. BOFT's performance starts to level off as $r$ exceeds 8. For OFT, a larger $b$ leads to better performance. A block-diagonal ratio of 0.6 to 0.8 usually yields the best result. As the depth of the model increases, OFT requires a smaller $b$ to perform well. For example, on the ImageNet classification task, OFT with a block-diagonal ratio of less than 0.3 achieves the best result for ResNet and ViT models with depths larger than 6.

BOFT achieves stronger generalization than OFT. BOFT's improvement is more significant on larger models. On the ImageNet classification task using a 3-layer ResNet-50, BOFT gets 78.3% Top-5 accuracy while OFT gets 77.5% Top-5 accuracy. BOFT also achieves stronger generalization on natural language tasks.
