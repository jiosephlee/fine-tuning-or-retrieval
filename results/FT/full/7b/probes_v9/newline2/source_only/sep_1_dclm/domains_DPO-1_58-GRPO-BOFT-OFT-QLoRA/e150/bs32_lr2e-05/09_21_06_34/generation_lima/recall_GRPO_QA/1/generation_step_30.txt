According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOP) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was introduced by OpenAI in their paper "OpenAI Five: An AI System Built to Play Dota 2" and is used as the reinforcement learning algorithm for training OpenAI Five.

In PPO, the policy gradient is estimated using a surrogate loss function that is close to the true objective but easier to optimize. The surrogate loss function is defined as:

$$\hat{J}(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[ \underbrace{\frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \sim p(s | a_{t-1})} \min\left(1, \frac{J(\pi_{old}, s) - J(\pi, s)}{J(\pi_{ref}, s)}\right)}_{\text{PPO term}} + \beta \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{s \sim p(s|a_{t-1})}\left[\nabla_\pi \log \pi(a_t | s)\right]}_{\text{KL term}}]$$

The first term is the PPO term which encourages the new policy $\pi$ to perform at least as well as the old policy $\pi_{old}$ with high probability. The second term is the KL-divergence term which regularizes the policy and prevents it from deviating too much from the old policy.

In GRPOP, the PPO term is modified to be:

$$\hat{JS}(\pi) =  \mathbb{E}_\pi \bigg[ \underbrace{\frac{\lambda}{|S|}  \sum_{s \in S} \min\left(\frac{1}{|A|} \sum_{a \in A(s)} \underbrace{\bigg(1 - \exp\left(\frac{J(\pi, s, a) - J(\pi_{old},s,a)}{\lambda}\right)\bigg)}, 1\right)}_{\textrm{group term}} + \underbrace{\frac{(1-\lambda)}{|S|} \sum_s \min\left( 1, \frac{ J(\pi, s) - J(\mu_s)}{J(\pi^\star, s)}\right)}_{{\rm per-group term}}\bigg]$$

where $S$ is the set of states, $A(s)$ is the set of actions in state $s$, $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter, and $\mu_s$ is the average reward of the best action in state $s$ under the reference policy $\pi^\star$. The group term encourages the new policy $\hat{\pi}$ to perform at least as good as the old policy $\hat{\pi}_{old}$ within each group of actions in each state. The per-group term is an additional term that further improves the performance of the policy within each group of actions.<|endoftext|>
