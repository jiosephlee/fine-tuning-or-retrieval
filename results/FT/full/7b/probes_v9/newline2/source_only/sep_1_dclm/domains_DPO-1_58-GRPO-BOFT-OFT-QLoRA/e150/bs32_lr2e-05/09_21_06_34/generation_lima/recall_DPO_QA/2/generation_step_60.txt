What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that learns from preferences rather than rewards. It is an alternative to the standard actor-critic algorithms used with RLHF, such as PPO.

In RLHF, we typically learn a reward function $r$ from human demonstrations of the environment interactions. This reward function is then used to provide feedback to the agent. An alternative is to directly learn a policy $\pi$ that satisfies preferences specified by a preference relation $\succsim$. A preference relation is a binary relation over actions that holds when a human would consider the corresponding outcome to be at least as good. Formally, a preference relation $\succim$ is transitive, complete and weakly monotonic. Transitivity means that if $a\succim b$ and $b\succim c$ then $a\succim c$. Completeness means that for any two actions $a$ and $b$, either $a\succim$b, $b\succim$a or neither. Weak monotonicity means that adding a good event to the outcome of an action can never make it worse. Formally, for any action $a$, any state $s$ and any event $e$ that happens in the outcome of $a$ with probability $p_e$ and is good with probability $p_{good}(e)$, we have that $a\succim a'$ where $a'$ is the same as $a$ except that the event $e$ happens with probability $p_e$: $$a\succim a' \Leftrightarrow p_{good}(e)>0\land p_e>0\to p_a(s,a')-p_a(s,a)\geq 0.$$

A preference relation can be represented using a utility function $u$ defined as follows: $a\succim>b$ if and only if $U(s,a)-U(s,b)\geq 0$, where $U(s,a)$ is the expected sum of the immediate rewards plus the utility of the final state reached under the policy $\pi$ starting from state $s$ and taking actions $a$. The preference relation is transitive, complete, and weakly monotonic if and only if the corresponding utility function is continuous, lower semi-continuous, and concave. 

DPO learns a utility function from human preferences specified as a binary relation $\succsim$ over outcomes of the environment interactions. It then optimizes the policy to maximize this utility function.<|endoftext|>
