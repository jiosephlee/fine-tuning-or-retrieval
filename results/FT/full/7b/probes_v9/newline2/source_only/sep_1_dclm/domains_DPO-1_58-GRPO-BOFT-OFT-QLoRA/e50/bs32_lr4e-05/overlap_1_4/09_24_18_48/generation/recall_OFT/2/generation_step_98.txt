It can be easily integrated into the training pipeline of current text-to-image diffusion models. Text-to-image diffusion models have revolutionized the field of computer vision and opened up exciting new directions in research. However, it is nontrivial to apply these models in practice due to the lack of controllability. Current methods usually apply text-to-image diffusion models in an interpolation or sampling manner, but it is difficult to guarantee the quality and diversity of the generated images. In this paper, we present a novel method for adapting text-to-image diffusion models, called Orthogonal Finetuning. The key insight to prevent the collapse is to preserve the pair-wise angles between neuron features. To achieve this, we finetune the text-to-image diffusion model in an orthogonal direction so that the angles between neurons will not change. We introduce a simple yet effective finetraining strategy, Orthogonal Finetunning (OFT), that can prevent the collapse of the pretrained model and simultaneously improve the generalization and sample quality of the finetunedmodel. The OFT algorithm can be easily integrated into themain training pipeline of current text-timage diffusion models. We evaluate our method on high-resolution text-to-image generation tasks, including Canny edge diffusion-GAN (CEDGAN)[1], DreamBooth[2], and Stable Diffusion[3]. The experiment results show that our method is able to prevent the collapsing phenomenon and significantly improve the sample diversity and quality of the finetunied models. Moreover, OFTs small learning rate (e. g., 5e-06) is able to finet tune large models (e. g.,7B) stablely, while current methods (e. g., LoRA) may failed to prevent the collapse withsuch a small learning rate.
