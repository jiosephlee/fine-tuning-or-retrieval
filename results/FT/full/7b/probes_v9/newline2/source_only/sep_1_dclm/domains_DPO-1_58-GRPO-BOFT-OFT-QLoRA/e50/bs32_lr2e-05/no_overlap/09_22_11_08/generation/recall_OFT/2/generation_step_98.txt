Existing Finetuning Methods

Standard Finetuning

Standard finetuning is the most straightforward way to finetune a pretrained model. It simply replaces the last fully connected layer with a new fully connected layer with the same size as the output of the pretrained layer. The new layer is trained from scratch along with the rest of the network. Standard finetuning is simple and effective, but it can cause significant changes to the neuron activations and the network geometry.


Low-rank Adaptation (LoRA) is a popular finetuning method for diffusion models. LoRA adds a small low-rank matrix to the weights of each neuron at every training iteration. LoRA is effective because it encourages the neuron weights to lie on a low-dimensional subspace, preserving the pairwise angles of neurons. LoRA is simple to implement and has a nice theoretical analysis showing that the weight updates are low-rank matrices. However, LoRA is not provably orthogonal, meaning that the pairwise angles between neurons may still change during finetuning.

Orthogonal Finetuning (0FT)

Orthogonal Finetuning is a simple yet effective method for finetuning neural networks. It updates the weights of neurons by drawing samples from the orthogonal group, ensuring that the pairwise angles between any two neurons remain unchanged. 0FT is provably orthogonal, meaning it preserves the pairwise angles between neurons, but it is not low-rank, meaning it can significantly change the neuron activations. Despite these limitations, 0FT has been shown to be effective in finetuning neural networks for computer vision tasks.
