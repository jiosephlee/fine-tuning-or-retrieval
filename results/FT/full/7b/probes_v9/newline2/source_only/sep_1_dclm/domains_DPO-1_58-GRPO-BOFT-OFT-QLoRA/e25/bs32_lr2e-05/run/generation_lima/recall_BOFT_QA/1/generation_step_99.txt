According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method for linear layers. BOFT uses a factorized weight update, which can be interpreted as a form of orthogonal transformation. This orthogonal transformation preserves the subspace spanned by the weights, while rotating the directions of the weights. As a result, BOFT can achieve similar or better performance than full finetuning with much fewer trainable parameters.

The name "Butterfly" comes from the fact that the update matrix in BOFT has a block-diagonal structure, resembling a butterfly. The update matrix is given by:

$$
\mathbf{R} = \text{diag}(\mathbf{R}_1, \mathIFIED{\mathbf{R}_2})
$$

where $\mathbf{R}_i$ are $d \times d$ rotation matrices, and $\text{diag}(\cdot)$ creates a diagonal matrix from its input. The number of parameters in $\mathbf{R}$ is $2d(d-1)$, which is much smaller than the number of parameters in full finetuning ($d^2$).

The name "Orthogonal" comes from the fact the update matrix $\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^{\top}$ are both orthogonal matrices. An orthogonal matrix has the property $\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbb{Q} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbb{Q}^\top = \mathbf{I}$, where $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix. This property ensures that the direction of the weights are rotated, but not changed in length. This is important because it means that the subspace spanned the weights is not changed, only the direction of the weights within that subspace.

The name "Orthogonal Finetuning" comes from the fact this update is similar to the gradient of the orthogonalization process, which is used to find the orthogonal basis for a subspace.<|endoftext|>
