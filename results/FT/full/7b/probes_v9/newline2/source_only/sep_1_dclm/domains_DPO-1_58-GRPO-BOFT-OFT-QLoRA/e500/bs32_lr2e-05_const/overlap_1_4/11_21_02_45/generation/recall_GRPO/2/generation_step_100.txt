Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory requirements from per-policy-sample to per-group-of-policies, making it more memory efficient for large-scale training. GRPO has been successfully applied to large-scale reinforcement learning tasks, such as continuous control with high-dimensional sensors. In this paper, we apply GRPO to the task of improving mathematical reasoning abilities in open-language models. Our method, named DeepSeekMath, achieves new state-of-the-art results on the MathPile and MATH benchmarks, showing strong ability in solving mathematical problems. DeepSeekMath also demonstrates significant improvements in more complex mathematical reasoning tasks, including the GSM8K and MMLU-OPLM benchmarks. Code and pre-trained models will be publicly available.

MathPile and MATH Benchmarks

The MathPile benchmark is a new large-scale benchmark for mathematical reasoning ability in language models. It consists of a large MathPile training set with 120K math problems and a smaller MathPile test set with 3K problems. Each problem in the MathPile benchmark is sourced from multiple elementary mathematics textbooks, ensuring the problems' difficulty and complexity similar to those that appear in real classrooms. To ensure diversity in the benchmark, we select problems in a unsupervised manner based on problem types. In the evaluation, models are evaluated with the correct probability for each problem, and the final score is the average accuracy across all problems in the test set.

The MATH benchmark is another large-scale benchmark for mathematical proficiency in language models. It is a subset of the MMLU benchmark, which is a large-scale natural language understanding benchmark. The MATH subset only contains questions related to mathematics. Both the MMLU benchmark and the MATH subset are widely used in the community. In this paper, DeepSeekMath achieves new state-of-the art results on both the MATH and MathPile benchmarks.

GSM8K and MML U-OPLM Benchmarks

In addition to the above benchmarks that evaluate the ability to solve mathematical problems, the paper also evaluates DeepSeekMath on two benchmarks that assess more complex mathematical reasoning capabilities. The first one is the GSM8K benchmark, which is a Korean mathematical reasoning benchmark. It consists of 8K questions for training and 1K questions for evaluation. The questions in the benchmark range from elementary mathematics to high-school-level mathematics. In the evaluation, no partial credit is given. Therefore, even if a model gets the final answer correct, but gives a wrong step-by-step solution, it will get the question marked as incorrect. DeepSeekMath achieves new states of the art on the GSM8K benchmark.

The other benchmark is the MML U-OPL M benchmark, which is a subset of the multi-turn MMLU benchmark that evaluates upper-grade-level mathematics reasoning capabilities. The MML U-OPL benchmark contains 2K training examples and 500 test examples. The questions in the MML U-O PL benchmark are more complex, and often require multiple-turn reasoning to arrive at the final answer. DeepSeekMath also achieves new state-of-the-arts on the MML U-O P L benchmark.
