In particular, it is designed for training large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) agents with a moderate number of training actions. It balances exploitation of the current state to obtain high rewards and exploitation of group information to further improve the reward. It has the following features:
* It computes a running average of the reward in addition to the reward of the current state, and it takes the mean and standard deviation of the running average into consideration. This addresses the problem that the reward can fluctuate greatly in RL.
* It calculates the group mean and standard deviation for each action of an agent in every training episode, and it uses the group mean instead of the mean of the running average for further simplification.
* It prevents the running average from deviating too much from the mean reward using a weighted moving average, where the weight decays over time.
* It initializes the running average of each action in every state to the reward of the action with the highest reward in that state, which is called a greedy initialization. This is because the action with the highest intrinsic reward in a state is likely to be a good starting point for training.
