Diffusion Models

Diffusion models are a type of generative model that learn to generate samples by learning the reverse mapping of a stochastic mapping. Diffusion models are typically trained with a contrastive loss that encourages the model to map a real sample and its corrupted version to be close together in latent space, while mapping two corrupted versions to be far apart in latent space. The training objective for a diffusion model with K corruption steps is:

\[\mathcal{L}_{diff}(G, \mathcal{D}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim \mathcal{D}}\bigg[\frac{1}{K}\bigg(\text{KL}\bigg[\frac{\pi_y(y)|x)\pi_x(x)}{\pi_y(y)\pi_x(x)|x)}\bigg)\bigg]\]

where $\pi_x$ and $\pi_y$ are the probability distributions in the latent space before and after corruption, and $G$ is the generative model. Diffusion models are trained with a reparameterization trick that allows the model to be sampled from in closed form. The forward pass through the diffusion model is given by:

\[z \sim \text{Normal}(0, I)\\x = G(z)\\y = x + \epsilon \cdot z\]

where $\epsilon$ is small noise. The reverse pass is given by:

\[x = G_z(\text{Softmax}(z)/z)\]

where $G_z$ is the diffusion model with all weights multiplied by $z$. The sampling procedure is then:

\[z \sim\text{Normal}(0,I)\\x = G_z(\frac{1}{z}\text{Softmax}(x))\]

Finetuning Diffusion Models

Finetuning is a simple and effective technique for adapting pretrained models to new tasks.
