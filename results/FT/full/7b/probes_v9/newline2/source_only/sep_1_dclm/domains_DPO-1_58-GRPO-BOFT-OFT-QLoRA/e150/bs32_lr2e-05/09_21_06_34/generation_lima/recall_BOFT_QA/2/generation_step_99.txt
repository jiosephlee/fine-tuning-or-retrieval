The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient variant of Orthogonal Finetuning. It uses a butterfly structure to combine multiple block-diagonal OFT layers into a single layer.

Formally, a $d$-dimensional BOFT layer with $\mathbf{R}^{\text{hid} \times \text{hid}}$ rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and $\text{scale} = d\cdot\text{hid}$ dimension is defined as:
$$
\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{X}_{\text{pre}} (\text{hid} \cdot \mathbf{D}^2)
$$
where $\mathbf{X}_{(\text{pre}, \text{post})}$ is the input and output tensor with shape $(\text{batch}, \text{chan}, \text{fea}, \text{seq})$, $\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^\text{fea}\times\text{hid}$ is a projection matrix, and the pre-multiplication is used for generative finetuning and the post-multiplication is usedfor discriminative finetuning.
The rotation matrix $\mathbf{\hat{R}} \in \mathbb.R^\text{hid}\times\mathbb.R^\text{(hid)}$ is then defined as:
$$
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\text{hid}}} \mathbf{U} \mathbf{V}^\top, \quad \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{V}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
$$
and the butterfly matrix $\mathbf{RH} \in \mathbf{R}^\text{(pre)}\times\mathbf{R}(\text{pre},\text{hid},\text{hid})$ is then defined as:

* For generative finetuning: $\mathbf{RH}= \mathbf{I}_{\text{pre},\mathrm{hid}} \mathbf{O}_{\text{pre},{\text{hid}},\text{hid}} \mathBF{I}_{\text{(post},\text{hid})}
* For discriminative finetuning:  $\mathbf{RH}:=(\mathbf{I}_{(\text{pre}),\text{hid}} - \mathbf{Diag}(1.\mathbf{S}_\text{feat}))\mathbf{O}_{(\text{pre},{\ text{hid}},\text {hid})} (\mathbf{I}_{({\text{post}}, \text{hid}))}-\mathbf{Diag(1.}\mathbf{S}_{{\text{fea}},\text{hid}}, \text{hid})))$ where $\mathbf{O}_{m_1\times m_2\times m_3}(\mathbf{O}_\text{chan},\mathbf{O}_{{\text{feat}},\text{hid}, \text{hid}})$ is an channel-wise orthogonal matrix and $\mathbf{S}_x=\text{diag}(\mathbf{x})$ is a diagonal matrix with entries from vector $\mathbf{x}$.
