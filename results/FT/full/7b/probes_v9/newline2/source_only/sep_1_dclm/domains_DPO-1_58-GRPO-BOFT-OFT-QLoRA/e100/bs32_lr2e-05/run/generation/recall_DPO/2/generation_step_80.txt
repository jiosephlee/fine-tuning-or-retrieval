Abstract: We present a new algorithm for training language models to produce outputs according to human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization. Unlike previous algorithms, Direct Preference Optimization can optimize a language model directly from preferences, without requiring an intermediate reward model. The core idea behind Direct Preference Optimization is to fit a linear approximation to the preference produced by a language models, and then optimize the language model to maximize this approximation. This approach is simple and efficient, and can optimize large language models from preferences sampled from data with very little human annotation. We evaluate Direct Preference Optimization on two benchmarks of natural-language tasks: text-to-image generation with the Vicuna benchmark, and natural-language understanding with the Robust Natural Language understanding benchmark. On the Vicunas benchmark, Direct Preference Optimization produces images that receive higher preference than GPT-4 and a GPT-4 model fine-tuned on rewards; on the RLHF benchmark, Direct Preference Optimization performs as well or better than the best reward models while being 10-100 times quicker to evaluate.
