The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be applied to any pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. It finetunes the pretrained model by learning layer-shared orthogonal transformations, which preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. OFT is provably stable and converges to a local minimum with guaranteed global angular convergence. OFT is easy to implement and has no hyperparameters to tune. Most importantly, OFT can finetune large text-to-image diffusion models while preserving high-fidelity imagery. On the BigBIRD model, OFT is able to finetune the pretrained model such that it can generate high-fidelity images with semantically coherent content. On the Stable Diffusion v1.5 model, OFT can also preserve the quality of images, although the generated images may have slightly blurrier textures and lower-fidelity details compared to the BigBIRD model. OFT can also be used to finetune vision-language diffusion models. On the FLAN v1.5 model with 7B parameters, OFT can finetime the pretrained model such that the generated images have semantically coherent content. OFT is a general finetuning method that is not limited to text-to-image diffusion models. We demonstrate that OFT can also be applied to finetune natural image diffusion models, including the BigBIRD model and the Stable Diffusion v2.0 model. On the Stable Diffution v2.0 model, OFT can preserve the quality of generated images with high-fidelity details.

The KLD finetuning method has several nice theoretical properties. It is guaranteed to converge to a local minimum. It is provably stable in the sense that the finetuned model will not revert to the pretrained model solution.
