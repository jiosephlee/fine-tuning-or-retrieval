What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are capable of generating photorealistic images from a short text prompt. These models are trained on large datasets of text-to-image pairs, such as FLAN, for which the images are generated with the model itself, in a recursive fashion.  As these models are trained on generative data, they can generate compelling images, but often fail to capture the semantics of the prompt. To address this, a common strategy is to fine-tune the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model on custom image datasets, but standard finetuning techniques tend to destroy the semantically-driven structure learned from the pretraining data. To prevent the model parameters from deviating too far from the pretrained model, LoRA (Low-rank Adapters) finetuning is used, where only the weight connections (or adapters) to the output layers are updated using a low-rank matrix. This way, the high-fidelity low-rank structure of the pretrained weights is preserved, while allowing for a small amount of deviation in the adapters. 

The paper introduces a new finetuning method, called Orthogonal Finetuning, that prevents the deviation from the pretrained model parameters while still learning task-specific features. The key insight is to orthogonalize the learned transformation matrix, such that the transformation does not destroy the subspace spanned by the pretrained weights. More formally, the learned transformation matrix is multiplied with an orthogonal matrix, such that the product transforms the pretrained weight matrix to the desired direction. Mathematically, this can be written as:

$$
\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}^{-1},
$$

where $\mathbf{W}$ is the weight matrix, $\mathbf{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix representing the learned transformation, $\mathbf{P}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, and $\mathbf{L}$ is an inverse-LU-decomposed matrix, which is used to guarantee a singular value decomposition for $\mathbf{L}$. 

The authors also introduce a variant of OFT, called Group Orthogonal Finetuning(GOFT), where the orthogonal matrix is shared across neurons within the same receptive field, but different channels. This way, the transformation for each neuron is learned independently from the other neurons, but the transformation for different channels can depend on each other. GOFT can be written as:

$$
\mathbf{W}_{channel} = \text{diag}(\mathbf{R}^G\mathbf{L}^1_{col}( \mathbf{L}^{1/2}_1 \mathbf{L}_2^{col} \mathbf{L}_{col}^{1/2} \mathbf{R}^{T} _G \mathbf{L }_G^{1/2} ) ), 
$$

where $\mathcal{col}(.)$ denotes column stacking, and $\mathbf{l}_i^{col}$ represents the $i$-th column of a matrix $\mathbf{l}_2^{col}$. 

The paper also introduces a third variant, called Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning(COFT), which constrains the learned orthogonal matrix to have identity subspace, i.e., the subspace spanned the identity matrix. This can be achieved by adding a term to the orthogonal matrix such that $\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{R}^{-\top}=\mathbf{I}$, where $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix. 

The paper provides a theoretical analysis for why OFT works, and shows through experiments that OFT is able to finetune text-to-image diffusion models while preserving the semantic structure of the pretrained model.
