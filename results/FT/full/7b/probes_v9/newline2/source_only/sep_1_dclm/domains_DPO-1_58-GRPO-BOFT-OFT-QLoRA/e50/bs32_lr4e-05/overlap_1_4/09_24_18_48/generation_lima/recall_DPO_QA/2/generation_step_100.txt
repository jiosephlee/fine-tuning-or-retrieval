What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is an algorithm that can be used to train language models to adhere to human preferences without explicit reward function. It is an alternative to algorithms such as PPO that are used for the RLHF (reinforcement learning from human preferences) problem. While PPO learns from preferences sampled from a latent reward function, DPO directly optimizes a language model to match given preferences, without assuming the existence of a reward function.

In DPO, a generative model of the preferences is learned in addition to the language model. The preference model is parametrized by a latent variable that represents the cause of a given preference outcome. By maximizing the likelihood of the observed preference data under the assumption that a given language model generated the preference outcomes, the preference model can be trained to produce plausible preferences. Once the preference model is trained, the DPO optimization minimizes the expected binary cross entropy loss between the preference model's probability distribution over preferences for a language model and a target distribution encoded by a binary variable $\pi$. In other words, it optimizes the language model to generate the preferences that would be generated by the target distribution $\pi$, via the intermediate step of learning a preference model that can explain the generation of the target distribution.

The key insight that enables DPO is that the KL-divergence between the preference model and the target distribution $\pi$ can be written as the binary cross entropy loss between $\pi$ and another distribution, which can be interpreted as the distribution over preferences generated by the language model corrupted by an additive noise drawn from $\pi$. Minimizing the cross entropy loss between the distribution of corrupted LM preferences and $\pi$ effectively aligns the distribution of corrupted LM preference outcomes $\pi(C \mid LM)$ with the preference outcomes $C$ observed in the training data, while pushing $\pi(LM)$ down, effectively normalizing it by the distribution of corrupted outcomes. This way, DPO learns to generate the preferences that $\pi$ would generate, while normalizing $\pi(LM)$, which has the effect of making the language model more probable under $\pi$. Once this objective is optimized, the language model becomes a realization of the target distribution $\pi(LM)$ and the learned preference model becomes a realizations of the preference distribution $\pi(C \midLM)$.

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the convergence of DPO to the target distribution, as well as empirical evidence that DPO can train language models to adhere human preferences, achieving better or comparable performance to PPO-based methods, without assuming a latent reward function.<|endoftext|>
