What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images from a short text description. However, these models are often trained on data with biases, and can thus generate discriminatory images when finetuned for a specific task. This raises the question: can we control the behavior of text-to-image diffusion models without losing their creativity and quality? 

The paper "ControllingText-to-Image Diffusion via Orthogonal Finetuning"
introduces Orthogonal Finetuning, a method that can perform faithful and controllable finetuning of text-to-image diffusion model without losing semantic information. The key idea behind OFT is to preserve the pairwise angles of neurons in the same layer, so that the global geometry in the feature space is well preserved. This is achieved by learning a orthogonal transformation for each pair of neurons, such that the angle between the neurons remains unchanged after the transformation. In other words, the weight update for the $i$-th neuron in the $l$-th layer is defined as:

$$
\mathbf{w}^{(l)}_i = \mathbf{w}^*(L)_{l} \cdot \text{Ortho}(\mathbf{W}^{(l-1)}_i),
$$

where $\mathbf{w}^\ast_{l-1}$ is the frozen weight of the $(l-1)$-th layer, $\text{Ortho(\cdot)}$ denotes an orthogonal transformation, and $\mathbf{W}^*(L)$ denotes all the frozen weights at a certain time step $*$. The orthogonal matrix is learned in an orthogonal fashion, i.e., $\text{Ortho}([\mathbf{Q}_1,\mathbf{Q}_2]) = [\mathbf{Q}_0 \mathbf{Q}_3 \mathbf{Q}_{2} \mathbf{Q}'_1]$ where $\mathbf{Q}_i,\mathbf{Q}'_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ for $i=0,1,2,3$. To ensure full orthogonality, the orthogonal matrices are updated using the following differential equation:

$$
\frac{\partial \mathbf{Q}}{\partial t} = -\alpha\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^T + \beta\mathbf{I},
$$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two scalars, and $\mathbf{i}$ is the identity matrix.

The authors apply OFT to control the behavior of Stable Diffusion, and demonstrate its effectiveness in both image-to-image translation and semantic control. Specifically, for image-to-image translation, they divide the task into multiple semantic sub-tasks, and perform OFT on each sub-task. For semantic control, they define a set of control signals that are related to the semantics of the generated image. They then perform OFT on the model with the control signal as an additional input. The results show that OFT is able to faithfully finetune the model for the specified task, while preserving its creativity and quality.<|endoftext|>
