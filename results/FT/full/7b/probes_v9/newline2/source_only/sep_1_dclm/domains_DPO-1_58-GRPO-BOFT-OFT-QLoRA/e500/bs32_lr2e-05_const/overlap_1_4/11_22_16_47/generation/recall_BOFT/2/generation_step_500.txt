Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning?

Finetuning is a simple yet powerful approach for adapting large pretrained models to downstream NLP tasks. Despite its simplicity, finetuning has achieved impressive performance on many NLP tasks. However, finetuning a large pretrained model usually requires a significant amount of training data and training resources. To address this challenge, parameter-efficient finetuning techniques have been developed to reduce the number of trainable parameters in finetuning stages. These techniques typically reduce the number of trainable weights by sharing weights across different positions in a neural network. For example, the widely-used method Group Normalization (GN) shares weights across different groups of neurons. Another example is LoRA, which reduces the number of trainable parameters by using low-rank weight updates. Despite the success of these parameter-efficient finetuning approaches, training a pretrained model with one of these methods usually requires more training data and training resources than simply continuing to train the pretrained model from its last checkpoint. In other words, existing parameter-efficient finetuning algorithms hurt generalizability, i.e., the ability of a model to perform well on unseen data. Addressing the generalizability challenge of parameter-efficient finetuning is an important open problem in NLP.
