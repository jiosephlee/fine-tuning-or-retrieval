The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" introduces Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) as an alternative to the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Schulman et al., 2017) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences, without explicit reward modeling. It trains a language model $p(\text{words} | \text{context})$ and a binary classifier $c(\text{words}, \text{context})$, and optimizes the language model to maximize $\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{c} \sim p(\tilde{c} | c)}[\log p(\text{words}) - \log c(\tilde{c}, \text{context}]$. The key idea is that if we can generate data $\tilde{c}$ from the correct classification of words generated by our language model, then we can ensure that $c$ will be high on samples where $\tilde{c} = c$. This avoids the need for an external reward model, since we only care about the language model generating the behaviors that the classifier $c$ labels favorably.

In practice, the language model is trained with the objective $\mathbb{E}_\text{context}\mathbb{E}_{c \sim p(c|\tilde{c}=\hat{c})}[\log p(\text{\~{}words}) - \log \hat{c}]$, where $\hat{c}$ is the label for the highest-scoring generated sequence. This requires access to an offline dataset of preferences $\{ \text{context}^i, \hat{c}^i\}_{i=1}^N$.

## Theory

The paper provides two theoretical results for the performance of DPO under different preference distributions. The first result assumes that the preference classifier $c$ is perfect, and the second result relaxes this assumption to allow for a small error rate in $c$.

### Perfect classifier

If we have a perfect classifier $c$ that always outputs the true label, then DPO converges to the optimal solution that maximizes $\mathbb{E}[\log p(\cdot | \text{context})) - \log c(\cdot, \text{context})]$. This is proven under the assumption that the language model is sampled from the optimization objective, which is a standard assumption in RL literature.

### Imperfect classifier

If our preference classifier $c$ makes a small number of mistakes, then DPO convergences to the solution that maximizes $\max_{\tilde{c}\sim p(\tilde{k} | k)}\mathbb{E} [\log p(\cdot | context) - \log k]$ where the expectation is over both the context and the distribution over preferences $\tilde{k}$ induced by the classifier $c$ whenever $c$ makes a mistake. This result shows that even with a small error rate in the preference classifier, DPO will converge to the optimal solution under the corrupted preferences $\tilde{k}$.<|endoftext|>
