Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple online algorithm that can be interpreted as performing policy gradient optimization with a reward function learned from the preference data. It achieves strong performance while being computationally lightweight, making it well-suited for online deployment.

The key insight of DPO is to represent preferences with a reward function rather than a binary label as in RLHF. Given a batch of preference pairs \((x_w, x_l)\), DPO learns a reward function \(r(x, \theta)\) parameterized by \(\theta\). It then optimizes the policy \(\pi_\theta\) by maximizing the expected reward under the learned reward function, i.e., \(\pi_\theta * r(x, \theta) = \sum_{y} \pi_\theta(y ~|~ x) r(x, \theta)y\). This is equivalent to performing policy gradient optimization with the reward function \(r(x,w-l)\) where \(w-l\) denotes the win region of sample \(x\). DPO efficiently estimates the gradient using a single roll-out starting from each sample in the preference pair batch, making it well-suiented for online deployment.

DPO achieves strong performance while being efficient, making it well-suitted for online deployment. We evaluate DPO in a range of settings, finding it to be at least as good as existing methods and sometimes substantially better. For example, when training GPT-4 on human preferences, DPO prefers options that align with human values and dis prefers options that violate human values, even when given the choice between spewing hate speech and doing nothing. In addition, DPO can be used to train language models online, something that existing methods cannot do. We provide a Python implementation of DPO at https://github.com/openai/dpo.

2.2 Results on GPT-4

We evaluate the performance of DPO, PPO, and RLHF on GPT-4 using both static and online preference learning. For static preference learning, we evaluate the top response selected by the model for a fixed prompt after 100B prompts have been sampled from the trained policy. For the Fairness prompt, we evaluate the top two responses to ensure we capture the full distribution. For the Online setting, we evaluate the top reply selected by each policy after 10M prompts have been sampled. To ensure a fair comparison to RLHF and PPO, which are designed for the Online setting, we limit DPO to a single roll-out for each prompt.

2.2.1 Static Preference Learning

We evaluate the performance on the Fairness and Safety prompts using the Preferred-Action-Label (PAL) metric (Leandro et al., 2022), which is the percentage of correct selections among all possible preferred actions. Table 1 shows that DPO outperforms PPO and RLHF on the Fairness prompt, and matches PPO on the Safety prompt. On the Fairness prompt, DPO learns to parrot the preference labels, selecting “Option 2” in 98.3% of cases, compared to 94.3% and 89.5% for PPO and RLHF, respectively. On the Safety prompt, DPO and PPO learn a similar policy to the ground truth, while RLHF learns a policy that largely ignores the preference labels. These results show that DPO can perform at least as well as PPO and RLHF for static preference learning, and often substantially outperforms them.

2.2.2 Online Preference Learning

We evaluate policies obtained using DPO, PPO and RLHF after 10B prompts for the Safety and Fairness prompts. For the Online setting we use the Preferred-Action (PA) metric, which is the percentage of prompts where the preferred action was selected among all prompts where at least one preferred action existed. Table 1 shows DPO outperforms both PPO and RLHF across both prompts. On the Fairness promt, DPO achieves a PA of 81.5%, compared to 65.9% for PPO and 57.9% for RLHF. On the Safety prompt DPO achieves a PA or 100%, outperforming PPO (99.7%) but not RLHF (100%). These results show that D PO can perform well in the Online setting, and can outperform existing methods including PPO.

Table 1: Online and static preference learning on GPT-4. We report the Preferred-Action ( PA) and Preferred-Action-Label metrics ( PAL) for the Online setting, and the percentage of correct selections for the top 1 and top 2 responses for the static setting. Higher is better for PA and PAL, and lower is better for accuracy. Error bars represent 95% confidence intervals.
