Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory requirements from O(1/ǫ^2) to O(1/ǅ), where ǫ and ǫ are the policy update and target policy update magnitudes, respectively. GRPO does this by maintaining a single group of target policies rather than individual target policies for each training example, as in standard PPO. This significantly reduces the memory requirements, making it feasible to use PPO-style methods for RLHF with large models and long horizons. GRPO achieves an impressive score of 51.3% on the competition-level MATH benchmark, surpassing GPT-4's score of 50.6% and matching the performance of state-of-the-art models trained with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) using significant computational resources.

The DeepSeekMath model is open source and available for download. You can try out the model via our open source Python library DeepSeekMath, which includes benchmarks, code examples, and more. We have also released a pre-trained base model with 7B parameters for free usage. For research purposes, we will soon release a 70B parameter model.
