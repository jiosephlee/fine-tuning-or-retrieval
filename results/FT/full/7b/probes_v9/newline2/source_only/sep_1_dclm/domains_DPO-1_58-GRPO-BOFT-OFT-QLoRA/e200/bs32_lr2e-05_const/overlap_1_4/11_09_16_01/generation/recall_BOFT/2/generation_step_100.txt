Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT factorizes the standard orthogonal matrix into a product of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices, which significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT while using far fewer trainable parameters in finetuning BERT and T5 models. BOFT also outperforms the block-diagonal OFT approach proposed in the original OFT paper. BOFT introduces a new problem: how to efficiently multiply sparse orthogonal matrices. To address this, we propose an efficient multiplication pipeline that greatly improves the training speed of BOFT. Our key insights to improve the multiplication efficiency include cache-oblivious design, loop unrolling, and parallelization. With our efficient multiplication pipeline, the training speed of BOft with 1024 butterflies (i.e., orthogonal matrices) can reach 1.8 times the speed of the original OFT. We also introduce a zero-initialized variant of BOFT, which matches the performance of non-zero-initialized BOFT and greatly saves training time and memory.

Finetuning large pretrained language models is increasingly popular for downstream natural language understanding tasks. To prevent overfitting and preserve the pretraining generalization, finetuning methods typically use a small portion of the model parameters (e.g., 0.1% in T5 finetuning (T5)) (Laion et al., 2021). Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) (Li et al., 2018) is an effective method for adapting a small portion of model parameters. OFT adapts the pretrained weights to the downstream data by learning an orthogonal matrix for transforming the weights. To further reduce the number of trainable parameters, the block-diagonal OFt (BD-OFT) approach (Li et al,, 2020) factors the orthogonal matrix into a product or powers of multiple block-diagonal orthogonal matrices, where each block-diagonal matrix has zeros along the diagonal-leading diagonal (DLD) direction. Despite the parameter-efficiency, BD-OFT fails to scale to large models due to its expensive matrix multiplication. To address this, BOFT factorizes the orthogonal matrix into a products of multiple sparse orthogonal matrices. By carefully designing the sparsity pattern, BOFT can significantly reduce the number of trainable parameter while preserving the parameter-efficiency of OFT. Thanks to the efficiency of matrix multiplication in modern CPUs and GPUs, OFT and its block-diagonal approaches can already multiply dense orthogonal matrices of a reasonable size (e.g., 1024). Therefore, to achieve strong performance, BOFT needs to multiply sparse orthogonal matrices efficiently. To this end, we propose an efficient matrix multiplication pipeline for BOFT.
