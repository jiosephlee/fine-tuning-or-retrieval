According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning large text-to-image diffusion models, like Stable Diffusion, without losing the trained model's quality. It works by adding a component to the loss function that encourages the finetuned model's weights to remain orthogonal (i.e. the covariance matrix of the weights should be identity).

The motivation behind OFT comes from the observation that diffusion models represent a compact manifold in weight space. This means that there are many high-quality models that differ only slightly in the weight space. By using a loss function that encourages the weight space of the finetuned model to be close to that of the base model, we can preserve the model's quality. The key insight of OFT is that we can enforce this property using an analytical expression of the weight space for linear transforms represented as matrices, which is the set of orthogonal matrices (or more precisely, the set of orthogonal matrices with determinant 1, called special orthogonal matrices).

Formally, the forward pass of a diffusion model can be written as:

$$
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{W} \odot \mathbf{Z}^T,
\end{equation}
$$

where $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Z}$ are matrices of shape $b \times h$, and $\mathbf{W}$ is a shared weight matrix of shape $d \times h$, where $d \geq b$. The element-wise multiplication $\odot$ is often written as $\text{ReLU}(a \cdot x)$, where $a$ is the element-wise product and $\text{ReLU}$ is the element-wise activation function.

The derivative of the forward pass with respect to the weight matrix $\mathbf{W} $is:

$$
\begin`equation}
    \nabla_\mathbf{W}  \mathbf{X}
    = \mathbf{X}\left( \underbrace{\mathbf{Z}^\top\odot\mathbf{W}^{-1}}_{f(\mathbf{W})} - \underbrace{\mathbb{I)b\times b}\right).
```

We can see that the forward pass $f(\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{W} $\) is composed of two linear transforms $\mathbf{Z}\mathtt{~\text{transpose}}\mathbf{W}$ and $\mathbf{x}\mathbf{W}$, and the derivative with respect to the weights $\nabla_\mathbb{W} \mathbf{X}$ is equal to the Jacobian of the forward pass evaluated at the weights $\mathbf{W}} $minus the identity matrix $\mathbf{I}$.
