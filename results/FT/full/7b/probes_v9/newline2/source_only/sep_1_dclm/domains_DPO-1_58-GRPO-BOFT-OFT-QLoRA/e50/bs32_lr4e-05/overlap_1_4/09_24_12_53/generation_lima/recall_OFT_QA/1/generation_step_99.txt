According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a simple finetuning method for text-to-image diffusion models. It works by performing standard finetuning, but additionally requiring that the learned weights be orthogonal, i.e., $X^TX = XX^T = I$ where $X$ is the finetuned weight matrix. This can be achieved by adding a small multiple of $X^T$ to the gradient with respect to the weights during stochastic gradient descent.

The name "Orthogonal Finetuning" comes from the fact that the method makes the finetuned weights orthogonal (in the sense above) to any given matrix. This is in contrast to LoRA which renormalizes the weights to have length 1 after each layer is finetuned separately.

OFT has the advantage of being simple and scalable to large models. However, it requires slightly more computations than standard finetuning as you need to compute and add $X^T$ at each step. In our experiments, we found that the additional computations are not a bottleneck even for very large models.

In our main experiment (Figure 8), we compare OFT to standard finetuning and LoRA. We run each method for 1000 steps and use a learning rate of 5e-5.
