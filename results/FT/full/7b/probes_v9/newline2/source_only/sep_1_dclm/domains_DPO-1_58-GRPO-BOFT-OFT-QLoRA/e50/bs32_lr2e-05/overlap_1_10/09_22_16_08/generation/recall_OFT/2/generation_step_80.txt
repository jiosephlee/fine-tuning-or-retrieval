Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be easily integrated into existing training pipelines. OFT finetunes neural networks by optimizing the weights with respect to an orthogonal matrix, which ensures that the neuron activations preserve their pairwise inner products. This property is crucial because it preserves relative similarities among data examples, which are vital for semantic information. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT in finetuning large text-to-image diffusion models. They show that OFT can adapt these models to new tasks and datasets without losing the high-fidelity and semantically-rich imagery produced by the base model. In contrast, standard finetuning and LoRA often result in blurry or semantically incorrect images. The paper also introduces a simple yet effective regularizer to stabilize OFT training. Why do we need a new finetuning method?

Finetuning is the de-facto method for adapting pretrained neural networks to new tasks. However, existing finetuning methods, such as standard weight update and LoRA, have limitations. Standard finetuning updates the neuron activations too much, which can cause the pretrained model to forget its important features. LoRA, which appends a small LoRA layer at the end of the pretrained model, is more conservative but still lacks theoretical guarantees. In contrast, OFT is theoretically grounded and empirically more effective and stable.

How does OFT work?

The key insight of OFT is to finetune neurons by preserving their pairwise inner products. Specifically, given a pretrained neural network with weights $\mathbf{W}$, OFT generates an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and then computes the finetuned weights $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}$. To generate an orthogonal matrix, OFT can optimize any function of the matrix whose derivative is the identity matrix subject to the constraint that the matrix is orthogonal. OFT proposes to optimize the Frobenius norm of the inverse, i.e., $\min_{\mathbf{R}} \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{R}$, because it is simple and effective. To stabilize the training of the orthogonal matrix, OFT further proposes to minimize $\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{R}^T$.

How well does OFT finetune neural networks?

The authors evaluate OFT on a variety of computer vision tasks, including class-incremental classification, few-shot classification, semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and density estimation. On all these tasks, OFT significantly outperforms standard finetuning and performs comparably to or better than LoRA. Moreover, OFT is much faster than LoRA because it does not require additional forward passes. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of OFTs ability to preserve the semantic information in the pretrained model.

Can OFT adapt text-to-image diffusion models?

Text-to-image diffusion models generate high-fidelity and seminally-rich images from text prompts. However, these models are trained on specific datasets and may not generate relevant images for other datasets. To address this problem, the authors apply OFT to adapt text-to-image diffusion model to new datasets. They consider three datasets: CUB for bird classification, Flowers for flower classification, and OfficeHome for object categorization in different contexts. For each dataset, they collect 10k text prompts that describe objects in the dataset. They then train a diffusion model on the prompts and the corresponding images. The results show that the base model without finetuning produces somewhat realistic but largely incorrect images (see rows 1 in Figure 1). In contrast, the OFT-trained model generates images that are both realistic and accurate (rows 4). In particular, the OFT-trained models achieve an accuracy of over 60% on the CUB and Flowers datasets using only text prompts, which is comparable to the baseline model trained from scratch on the same dataset! These results demonstrate the power of OFT in adapting text-to-image diffusion models.

What's the catch?

You might wonder: if OFT is so great, why isn't it used by everyone? The answer is that OFT has one major limitation: it can only finetune fully-connected layers. The reason is that OFT relies on the fact that the derivative of the orthogonal matrix with respect to the weight matrix is the transposed weight matrix. This property only holds for weight matrices, not for other types of layers such as convolution layers. However, the fully-connected layer is often the layer right before the output layer, so OFT can still be quite useful in practice.

What's next?

The authors point out several directions for future work. One direction is to extend OFT to finetune layers other than fully-connected layers. Another direction is to apply OFT to other tasks, such as natural language processing and reinforcement learning. I am excited to see what the community comes up with!
