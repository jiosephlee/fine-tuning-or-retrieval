The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. It works by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, preserving the pairwise angles between neurons and thus the overall neuron structure. This is in contrast to existing methods such as standard finetuning or Low-rank Adversarial Finetuning (LoRA), which can cause the neuron structure to change significantly. The authors evaluate OFT on three large-scale text-to-image diffusion models, including Stable Diffusion v1.5, DALL-E2, and CLIP-T2I. They show that OFT is able to finetune these models to new tasks such as object segmentation and cartoon drawing with better controllability, fewer collapsed modes, and stronger generalization compared to existing methods.
