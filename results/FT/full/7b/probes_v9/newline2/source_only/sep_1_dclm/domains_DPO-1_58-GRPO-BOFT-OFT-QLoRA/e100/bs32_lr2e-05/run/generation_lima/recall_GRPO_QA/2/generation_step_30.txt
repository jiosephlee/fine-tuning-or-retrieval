What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that is used to train deep neural networks. It is an actor-critic method that uses a surrogate objective to prevent large policy updates and clipping to control the magnitude of these updates. The surrogate objective is a combination of the true objective (the log probability of the next action given the current state and the current policy, and the reward received after taking the action) and a term that limits the size of the policy update (the ratio of the policy gradient and a reference policy). The policy gradient is estimated using a value function that is learned from data.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a variant of PPO that improves its scalability by estimating the group relative advantage instead of the absolute advantage. In GRPO, the policy gradient is estimated by comparing the average reward of each group of samples with the average reward of a reference group. This reduces the amount of memory required for training, since it is no longer necessary to store all the samples generated by the policy. Additionally, GRPO can be more stable than PPO, since it does not require clipping the policy updates.
