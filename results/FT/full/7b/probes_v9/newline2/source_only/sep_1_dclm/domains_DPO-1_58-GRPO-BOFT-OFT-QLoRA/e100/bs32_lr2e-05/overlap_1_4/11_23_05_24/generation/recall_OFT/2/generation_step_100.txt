Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that preserves the geometric properties of the neural network. OFT finetunes a neural network by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, allowing it to adjust the activation distribution while preserving the neuron's direction and distance from the origin. This is in contrast to standard finetuning or Low-rank Adversarial Finetuning (LoRA), which modify the neuron directions and can cause the neuron activations to deviate significantly from the original training data distribution. The authors evaluate OFT on three large-scale text-to-image diffusion models, including Stable Diffusion v1.5, DALL-E2, and CLIP-Fu. OFT is able to finetune these models to new datasets with photo-realistic results while preserving the diversity and quality of the original model's generations. Moreover, the orthogonal transformations learned by OFT can be composited and shared across neurons, further simplifying the finetuning process. The paper also introduces a new benchmark, Finetuning Diversity Benchmark (FDB), that evaluates finetuning methods based on both the quality and diversity of generated samples.
