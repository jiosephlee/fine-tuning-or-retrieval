According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The paper introduces OFT, a simple finetuning method that can prevent knowledge collapse. The key is to learn orthogonal transformation in addition to task-specific transformation. To achieve orthogonality, the paper proposes two methods for matrix computation: spectral decomposition and Cayley parameterization. 

Formally, given an pretrained weight matrix $W^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times o}$, the conventional finetuning updates the weight matrix as:
$$
W(t) = W^0 - \alpha t \nabla_{W^0} L(W^0)
$$
where $\alpha$ is the learning rate at time $t$, and $\nabla_{W^{0}} L(W^0)$ is the gradient of the loss function $L$ with respect to $W^0$.

In contrast, OFT learns a orthogonal matrix $R(t) \in \mathbb {R}^{h \time s}$ and then applies $W(t) = R(t) W^0 R^\top(t)$, so that both $W^0$ and $R(t)$ contribute to the forwarding. There are two different ways to compute the orthogonal matrix $R(t)$, which are described below.

Spectral Decomposition:
The spectral decomposition computes $R(t)$ via spectral decomposition:
$$
R(t) = Q \Sigma Q^\top
$$
where $Q$ is the orthogonal matrix containing the eigenvectors, and $\Sigma$ is a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues. To ensure orthogonality at all time steps, the paper proposes to update the orthogonal matrix $Q$ as:
$$
Q(t) = Q t \Sigma t \cdot (Q \Sigma Q^\top)^\top
$$
Cayley Parameterization:
The Cayley parameterization computes $R(t)$ through exponentiation:
$$
R(t; A, B) = (I + tB) (I + tA)^{-1}
$$
It achieves orthogonality when $A = -\frac{1}{2} \text{diag}(1, -1, \cdots, -1) $ and $B = B^\top = Q \sqrt{\Sigma} Q^\top$, where $\sqrt{\Sigma}$ denotes $\text{diag}(\sqrt{\sigma_1}, \sqrt{\sigma_2}, \cdots, \sqrt{\sigma_h})$.
