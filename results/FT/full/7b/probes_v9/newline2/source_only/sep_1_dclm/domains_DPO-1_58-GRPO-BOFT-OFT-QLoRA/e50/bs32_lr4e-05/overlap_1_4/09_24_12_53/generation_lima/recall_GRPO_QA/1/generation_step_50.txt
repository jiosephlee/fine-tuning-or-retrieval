According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). In particular, it is designed for training large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) agents with a moderate number of training actions. It achieves this by only storing and updating a subset of the data, specifically, the best action from each policy group. A policy group is a set of policies that have a close resemblance to each other. GR PO has been successfully used in the DeepSeekMath project to train large-scale mathematical reasoning models.

The key idea of GR PO is to only store and update the best action from each group of policies. This way, it reduces the memory requirement from \(M\) training examples to \(M/num_groups\) while approximately preserving the convergence speed. To achieve this, it sorts all the policies according to their rewards and then picks every \(num_groups\)th policy as a representative of its group. It then only updates the reward function based on these representatives.

Formally, let \(\mathbf{S} = \{ \mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{s}_2, \cdots, \mathbf{s}_{M}\}\) be a set of \(M\) training examples, where \(\mathbf{s}_i = \{o_i, a_i, r_i, s_{i}\}\) is the \(i\)-th example with \(o_i\) being the initial state, \(a_i\) the action, \(r_i\) the reward, and \(s_{i}\) the next state. We sort the examples in ascending order of rewards, i.e., \(r_1 \leq r_2 \leq \cdots \leq r_M\), and then divide them into \(num_groups\) non-overlapping groups. Within each group, we pick the example with the smallest state \(s_{index}\). The index here is the index of the example in the sorted order, e.g., \(index(1) = 1\). The selected examples will be used for updating the reward function.<|endoftext|>
