Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT is based on a novel parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning (PEOF) layer that uses a butterfly structure to efficiently multiply a weight matrix with an orthogonal matrix. BOFT can be viewed as applying PEOF layers to all layers of a pretrained model. BOFT achieves stronger performance than OFT while using significantly fewer trainable parameters. BOFT also has a faster forward-pass computation than OFT. BOFT achieves new state-of-the-art results on image classification, including beating TinyMeta and MetaOptNet, which are two recently proposed meta-learning methods. BOFT also shows strong performance on natural language processing (NLP) tasks. BOFT introduces a novel and very useful finetuning layer, the PEOF layer, to the research community. The PEOF layer is also very interesting and useful on its own beyond the context of BOFT. Therefore, in this blog post, I will explain the PEOF layer in detail, and then I will briefly introduce the BOFT method. I will not go through the experiments in the paper since they are beyond the scope of this blog post.

Problem formulation

Let’s start by formally defining the forward pass of a neural network. A neural network with weights $w\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ and biases $b\in\mathbb{x}\mathbb{R}^m$ computes the output $\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{X}\mathbb{R}$ of a input $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{x}{\mathbb{R}}$ through the following equation: $$\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b$$ Here I use $\top$ to denote the transpose operation. This is the standard feedforward computation in a neural network. Now let’s consider the task of finetuning a pretrained neural network model $\mathbf{M}(\cdot;\mathbf{w}^0)\in\mathbb{Z}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{Y})$ with weights $\mathbf{w}^{0}\in\mathbb{}{w}{}^0$ on a downstream task. The goal of finetuning is to adapt the pretrained model to the downstream task while preserving the pretraining knowledge. There are many finetuning methods, such as standard gradient-based finetuning, which minimizes a loss function with respect to the weights, i.e., $\mathbf{w}\leftarrow\mathbf{w}−\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{M}({\mathbf{x}};\mathbf{w});\mathbf{y})$, where $\mathcal{L}()$ is a loss function between the prediction $\mathbf{M}(f{x};\mathbf{ w})$ and the label $\mathbf{y}$. Another example of finetuning method is data-free finetuning methods, which do not use gradient descent to update the weights. Instead, they directly finetune the activation activations of a pretrained model.
