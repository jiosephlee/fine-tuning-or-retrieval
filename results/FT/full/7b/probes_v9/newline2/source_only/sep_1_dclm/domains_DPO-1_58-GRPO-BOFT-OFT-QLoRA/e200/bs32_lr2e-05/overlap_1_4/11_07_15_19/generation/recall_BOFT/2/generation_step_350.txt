Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to parameter-efficiently share weights across channels in a convolution layer, in contrast to the block-diagonal approach used in OFT. BOFT is more parameter-efficient than OFT and its block-diagonal variant, reducing the number of trainable parameters by $24\%$ and $37\%$ on average for image classification and natural language processing tasks, respectively. BOFT also yields better performance than OFT and its variant; for example, on ImageNet classification, BOFT achieves $82.3\%$ top-1 accuracy with $0.35\%$ trainable parameters, surpassing the $81.8\%$ accuracy of the block-diagonal OFT variant with $0.47\%$ trainable parameters. BOFT achieves these results without introducing additional computational overhead. BOFT is an instance of a more general class of methods called *parameter-efficient finetuning via factorization*, of which OFT is a special case. We introduce two new factorizations that yield two additional methods: Group Orthogonal Butterfly (GOFT), a parameter-efficient alternative to Group Normalization; and Parameter Efficient Adaptive Instance Normalization (PEAIIN).
