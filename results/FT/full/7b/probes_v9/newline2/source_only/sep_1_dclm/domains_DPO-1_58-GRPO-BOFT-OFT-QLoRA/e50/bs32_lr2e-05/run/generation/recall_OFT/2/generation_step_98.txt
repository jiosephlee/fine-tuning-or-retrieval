Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be used to adapt text-to-image diffusion models to downstream tasks. OFT finetunes a neural network by learning orthogonal transformations for each neuron, preserving the pairwise inner products between neurons. OFT is provably information-preserving, in the sense that it does not increase the mutual information between the input and the output activations. OFT also enjoys several desirable geometric properties. Most notably, the pairwise angles between neurons in the resulting finetuned model are identical to those in the pretrained model. In contrast, standard finetuning and LoRA change the angles between neurons, potentially destroying the geometric structures learned by the pretrained model. To finetune a layer $l$, OFT learns an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}_l\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ for each neuron $\bm{z}_l^{\mathsf{pre}}\in\mathbb{T}^d$ where $\mathbb{T}$ denotes the d-dimensional torus, such that $\bm{z}_ l^{\mathsf{ft}} = \bm{R}_l \bm{z}_l^\mathsf{pre}$. OFT can be viewed as learning a coordinate system for each dimension $d$ such that the pairwise distances between neurons are preserved. To finetune the entire network, we recursively apply OFT to each finetuned layer. Formally, to finetune the layer indexed by $l$, we first freeze all layers $l'\geq l$. We then apply OFT to the frozen layers and the layer $l-1$ to obtain the finetuned model $\bm{z}^\mathsf{ft}_l=\bm{R}_l[\bm{W}^\mathsf{pre}_{l-1}\bm{x}]+\bm{b}^\mathsf{pt}_l$ where $\bm{R}_ l\in\mathbb {R} ^{d\times d}$ is the orthogonal matrix learned by OFT, $\bm{W}^\mathrm{pre}_{l- 1}\in\mathbb { R} ^{h\times d}$ isthe weights of the frozen layer $l-1$, $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{ R} ^{h}$ is the input, and $\bm{b}^\mathrm{pt}_l\in\ mathbb{ R} ^c$ is the pretrained bias. The orthogonal matrix $\bm{ R}_l$ is learned by optimizing the objective \begin{equation}\footnotesize \min_{\bm{ R}_l}\frac{1}{2\tau}\text{ tr}(\bm{ Q}_l+\bm{ K}_l)+\lambda\|\bm{ R}_l\|_F^2\end{equation} where $\bm{ Q}_l=\nabla_\bm{z}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\text{ exp}(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{z}^\top\bm{z})$ is the data likelihood quadratic form, $\bm{ K}_l=\bm{ I}_d-\bm{ R}^\top_l\bm{ R}_ l$ is a regularization term that enforces orthogonality, and $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter that controls the strength of orthogonality enforcement. The resulting orthogonal matrix $\bm{  R}_l$ has orthonormal columns, but it is not necessarily orthogonal with respect to the standard inner product. However, it is easy to see that $\bm{ R}_ l^{\top}\bm{ R}_ l=\bm{ I}_ d$, so we can obtain an orthogonal matrix by applying $\det(\bm{ R}_ l)\bm{ R}_ l^\top/\sqrt{d}$ where $\det(\bm{  R}_ l)\neq 0$ unless $\bm{ R}_  l=\bm{  0}$ (which never happens in our experiments). The authors also provide theoretical analysis of the OFT method, showing that it preserves the mutual information between the inputs and outputs of the pretrained model. They also show that OFT preserves the pairwise angular distances between neurons in the pretrained model, while standard finetuning and Low-rank Adversarial Finetuning (LoRA) change these distances. The paper also provides some empirical evidence that the pairwise angular distances between well-separated neuron clusters are preserved by OFT, but are changed by standard finetuning and by LoRA. The paper then evaluates the performance of OFT on the task of adapting text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) to the task of generating images given a natural language prompt.
