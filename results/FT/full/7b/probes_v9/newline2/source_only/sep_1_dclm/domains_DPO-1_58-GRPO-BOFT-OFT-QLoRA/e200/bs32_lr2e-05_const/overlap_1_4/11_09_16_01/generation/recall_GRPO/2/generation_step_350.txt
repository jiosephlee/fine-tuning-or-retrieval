Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses a group of policies to estimate the baseline for each policy. GRPO is more memory efficient than PPO because it does not store the baseline for each policy but instead uses the average of a group of policies as the baseline. This makes GRPO well-suited for training large-scale models like DeepSeekMath.

DeepSeekMath is a 7B parameter open-source language model trained on a combination of mathematical and natural language data. The training data includes arXiv papers, textbooks, and mathematical web pages, as well as a subset of Wikipedia. The model is evaluated on a variety of mathematical tasks, including quantitative reasoning, algebra, calculus, and geometry. On these tasks, DeepSeekMath achieves state-of-the-art performance, often by a significant margin.

The paper also introduces a new benchmark for mathematical reasoning in language models, the Large Math Benchmark (LMB). The LMB consists of a diverse set of mathematical tasks, including arithmetic, algebra, calculus, geometry, and natural language understanding. These tasks are designed to challenge language models across a wide range of mathematical abilities. The LMB will be regularly updated as new mathematical tasks are developed, and we encourage researchers to use it to evaluate the mathematical capabilities of their models.

The DeepSeekMath codebase is available at <https://github.com/DeepSeek/DeepSeekMath>. We hope that this codebase will be a useful resource for the research community, and we look forward to seeing what you can do with it!
