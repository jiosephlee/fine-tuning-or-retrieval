Can you explain what PPO is?<|EOT|>PPO stands for "Proportional-Percentage-of-Avoided-Cost". It is a type of contract used in insurance and reinsurance. It is a form of reinsurance treaty, where the ceding company (the one that is transferring some of its risk to the reinsurer) pays a premium to the reinsurance company, and in return, the reinsurer agrees to pay part of the losses that the ceding company suffers.

The key feature of a PPO contract is that the amount that the reinsurer will pay to the ceding company is proportional to the amount of loss that the ceding company incurs. This is in contrast to an AVO (Average-Value-of-Occurrence) contract, where the reinsurer pays a fixed amount per policy, regardless of the size of the loss.

To be more precise, a PPO contract specifies a certain percentage (say 80%) of the losses that the reinsurer will cover. So if the ceding company incures a loss of $100, the reinsurer will pay 80% of $100, which is $80. If the ceding company incurses a loss of $200, the reinsurer will still only pay 80% of the loss, which is $160. The total amount paid by the reinsurer will therefore be $240, which is less than the $200 that the ceding company would have had to pay if it did not have any reinsurance at all.<|endoftext|>
