According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method for text-to-image diffusion models. It prevents mode collapse and preserves the high-resolution imaging capability of the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. Specifically, it achieves this by learning an orthogonal transformation for each layer during finetuning.

The key insight of OFT is that we should preserve the pairwise angles of neurons in the same layer to prevent losing the representational capacity of the pretrained model. To achieve this, OFT optimizes the forward pass of the diffusion model with an additional orthogonal transformation for each layer, i.e., $$\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{W}^0\mathbf{X}_0$$ where $\mathbf{W}^{0}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{L}$ is the learned linear layer, and $\mathbf{R}$ is the orthogonal matrix that satisfies $\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}\ mathbf{R}^\ top=\mathbf{I}$. The orthogonal transformation for the bias is not considered because it does not affect the forward pass. To ensure the orthogonality of the transformation for each layer, we use Cayley parameterization, i.e., $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{L}\*\mathbf{L}^\top/2$ where $\mathbf{L}*\mathbf{L}$ denotes the Hadamard product (element-wise multiplication) of two matrices.

To prevent the orthogonal matrix from collapsing to a identity matrix, we clamp the spectral norm of the difference between $\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}$ to a small value, i.e., $\|\mathbf{R}- \mathbf{I}\|=:\epsilon$. This also prevents the inversion of the orthogonal matrix. To stabilize the training, we also add a loRA regularizer on the orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{L}\* \mathbf{L}^{\top}$, i.e., $\frac{1}{2}\*\|\mathbf{L}\-\mathbf{L}^{(i)}\|^2_{F}$ where $\mathbf{ L}^{(i)}$ is the orthogonal matrix at the $i$-th iteration.

OFT has a low computational cost because the orthogonal matrix only multiplies the forward pass once and does not introduce any new convolution or matrix multiplication.<|endoftext|>
