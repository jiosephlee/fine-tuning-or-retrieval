According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>The paper introduces OFT, a finetuning strategy that can prevent knowledge collapse. The key idea of OFT is to enforce orthogonal transformation. To achieve orthogonality, OFT optimizes both the model parameters and an additional orthogonal matrix at each training iteration. To prevent the orthogonal matrix from being trivially all zero or identity matrix, we impose an additional row norm constraint. 

Formally, the forward pass of a standard text-to-image diffusion model can be written as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{X}=\text{Norm}( \text{Mixup}(\text{Decoder}(\text{SpectralPooling}(\text{X}, \text{W}^0), \text{B}^0), \lambda) ), \\
&\text{where } \text{Norm}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x+x^\top), \lambda=2^{1/4},
\end{aligned}
$$

and the finetuning update is

$$
\begin{align*}
&\textbf{x}=\text{Norm}(\text{Decoder}( \text{SpectralPooling}( \text{X}, \mathbf{W}^0 ) , \mathbf{B}^0 ) ),\\
&\text{with initial } \mathbf{W}=2^{-1/4} \text{Identity}, \mathbf{B}=2^{-1/2} \text{Identity}.
\end{align*}
$$

OFT generalizes this update with an additional orthogonal matrix $\text{R}$:

$$
\begin{xalignat}{2}
&\textbf {X} &&=\text{Norm} (\text{Mixup} (\text{Decoder} (\text{SpectralPooling}
(\text {X}, \text{ W}^0 ), \text{ B}^0 ) ), \lambda ) ), \\
&\text { with initial } &\text{ R} &\leftarrow \text{Identity}, \\
&\text { and the update is } &\textbf {X}&=\text{Norm} (
\text{SpectralPooling }
(\text { X }, \text{ W}^{d-1} ) ) , \\
& &\text{ Decoding } &\text{ with } \textbf {X}, \textbf {W}, \text{ B} \text{ initialized as above}, \\
& & &\textbf {W} \gets \textbf {W} - \mu \nabla_\textbf {W} {\text CLIP}(\textbf {X},\text { Norm}(\text{Render}(\textbf {W},\textbf {R} ), 1))) , \\
& & &\Textbf {R} \gets \text{Orthogonalize}(\Textbf {R}, \text{ invertibility } > \epsilon)
\end{xalignat}
$$

Where $\mu$ is the learning rate for weight update, and $\epsilon$ is a threshold for the invertibility of $\text{R}$.

The paper provides two methods to orthogonalize $\text{R}$: Cayley transform and QR decomposition.<|endoftext|>
