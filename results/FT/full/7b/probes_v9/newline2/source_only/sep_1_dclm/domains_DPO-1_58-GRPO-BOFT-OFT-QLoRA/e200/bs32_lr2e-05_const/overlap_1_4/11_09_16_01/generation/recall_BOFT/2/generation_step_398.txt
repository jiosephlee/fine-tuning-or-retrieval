Parameter-Efficient Finetuning

Large pretrained models have revolutionized the field of AI. However, these models are too large and too expensive to deploy. Therefore, finetuning these large models on downstream datasets is an important technique for deploying AI. However, finetuning these models is also expensive. Therefore, parameter-efficient finetuning is an important area of research. There are many parameter-efficient finetuning methods, including, but not limited to, LoRA (Layman et al., 2021), FAIR (Gheini et al., 2022), COCO (Wang et al., 2020), LFB (Hulora et al., 2023), and BOFT.

Standard finetuning modifies the weights of a pretrained model according to the following equation.

(1) $\mathbf{m}^f=\mathbf{M}^f\mathbf{m}^{pre}$

where $\mathbf{m}$ is a weight matrix, $\mathbf{m) is the weight matrix after finetuning, $\mathbf{M}$ is the finetuning matrix, and $\mathbf{m)^pre$ is the pretrained weight matrix. Equation (1) is equivalent to the following equation.

 (2) $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{I}-\beta\mathbf{R}$

where $\mathbb{R}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbb{I}$ is the identity matrix, and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter. Equation (2) implies that the finetuning matrix $\mathbf{M)$ can be written as the difference of an orthogonal matrix and an identity matrix. Equation (2) also implies that the number of trainable parameters in the finetuning matrix is equal to the number of elements in the pretrained weight matrix that are not frozen, i.e., the number of elements in $\mathbf{R}$.

(3) $\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{B}_1\mathbf{B}_2$
