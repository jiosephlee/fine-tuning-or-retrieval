Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to parameter-efficiently adapt linear layers in pretrained models. BOFT adapint a linear layer $\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times h}$ and $\bm{b}\in\mathbb {R}^d$ in a pretrained model to a downstream task, first learns a task-specific projection matrix $\bm{R}\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$ with $r\ll h$ such that $\bm{R}\bm{W}\bm{R}^\top$ has orthonormal columns, and then adapts the bias $\bm{b}$ by $\bm{b}^\top+\bm{R}\bm{x}$ where $\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,r)$. To prevent $\bm{R}$ from degenerating into identity, a group-structured constraint is imposed on $\bm{R}$. Despite its simplicity, BOFT performs comparably to the state-of-the-art finetuning methods. BOFT also admits a block-diagonal variant that further reduces the number of trainable parameters. The paper provides a theoretical analysis for the convergence of the original BOFT and its block-diagonal variant. Empirically, BOFT and its blockdiagonal variant perform stably and consistently across a wide spectrum of large models, datasets, and tasks. In particular, BOFT Large and its block-diagonal variants significantly outperforms OFT and its block-diagonal counterpart in terms of parameter efficiency and performance. BOFT Large with the block-diagonal variant converges fastest among all methods. Code will be released soon.
