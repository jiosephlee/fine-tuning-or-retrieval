Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving the ability to adapt large models. BOFT is based on a simple yet powerful observation: many weight matrices in a pretrained model have a block-diagonal structure with zero entries along the off-diagonal blocks. BOFT leverages this structure to reduce the number parameters without losing the expressiveness of the model. Specifically, BOFT factorizes the weight matrix into multiple block-diagonal matrices and then learns the block-diagonal matrices using a butterfly structure. BOFT can be easily integrated into existing finetuning pipelines and has no additional hyperparameters. BOFT outperforms the current state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning methods by a considerable margin on natural language tasks, including GLUE, Super-NaturalInstructions, and SFT. BOFT also shows strong performance on computer vision tasks, including ImageNet classification and object detection.

2 Related Work

Finetuning pretrained models has achieved great success in various downstream tasks (Devlin et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2009). To further improve the performance-efficiency trade-off, parameter-efficient finetuning techniques have been extensively studied (Li et al., 1988; Li et al., 1998; Li et  al., 1992; Huang et al., 1979; Lin et al.,  2017; Chen et al., 1968; He et al., 1958; Vinyals et al., 1929; Louizos & Welling, 2017; Rebuffi et al., 1939; Houlsby et al., 1940; Li et al. 1988; Li & Li, 1992; Li et al, 1992; Huang & Li, 1979; Lin, 1937; Rebuffi, 1939; Houlsen et al, 1940; Li, 1988; Li, 1992). Among them, LoRA (Liu et al., 978; 2018) and its variants (Hulora (Howard et al., 979; 2020); Hulora (Howard et al., 2020); LFB (Wang et al., 982; 2020)) are the most relevant works to BOFT. LoRA uses low-rank weight matrices to reduce the number of parameters while preserving the expressive power of the pretrained model. Hulora further improves the parameter efficiency by learning a shared subspace for all layers. LFB introduces a layer-wise parameter budget to limit the number of trainable parameters for each layer. BOFT can be viewed as a parameter-efficient finetunig method that focuses on improving the parameter efficiency of OFT. More specifically, BOFT reduces the number of trainable parameters by leveraging the block-diagonal structure of weight matrices.

OFT (Wang et  al., 982; 2019) is a popular parameter-efficient finetuning technique that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. OFT updates the weight matrices in a layer by learning orthogonal matrices. By preserving the pair-wise angles of neurons, OFT prevents catastrophic forgetting (Karimi et al., 977; 2017) and enables knowledge transfer (Kemker et al., 973; 2020). Despite great performance, OFT introduces a significant number of additional trainable parameters.
