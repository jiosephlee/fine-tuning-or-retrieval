What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Bai, et al. 2022) is a model-based algorithm for training language models to adhere to human preferences. It is an alternative to algorithms such as PPO that are used for the RLHF (reinforcement learning from human preferences) problem and operates using a different paradigm. While PPO and similar algorithms treat the training data as noisy samples from a latent reward function and learn this latent reward function using a model such as a Gaussian process, DPO instead learns a model of the human true preference distribution over outcomes given the complete preference dataset. It then uses this model to sample hypothetical preference datasets and optimize the language model by maximizing the likelihood of these sampled preferences under the preference model, in a fashion similar to maximum likelihood learning.

The key insight that enables DPO is that we can draw samples from the preference distribution $p$ without explicitly parametrizing it. We can instead draw samples from the reward model $p$ and from the true preference distribution $p^*$, and then combine them to form a sample from $p^*$. This is because $p^*$ is the posterior distribution over $p$ given the preference dataset D, and we can use Bayesian inference to perform this inference. The resulting procedure is:
1. Sample a reward model $p$ from your prior over reward models.
2. For each preference dataset $D_i = \{x_w, x_l, 1\}$ in your dataset:
