According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOP) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was proposed by OpenAI for training large reinforcement learning (RL) agents. In particular, it was used to train the RL agent of the OpenAI Five, a team of five AI bots that can play the video game Dota 2 as well as - and sometimes better than - a professional human team.

The main difference between PPO and GRPOP is how they estimate the policy gradient. Both methods estimate the policy gradient using a surrogate objective, which approximates the true policy gradient using samples from the current policy. The surrogate objective used by PPO is called the surrogate advantage, and is defined as:

$$Surrogate\ Advantage = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n \mathbb{E}_{p(\hat{a}|s,t)}[\hat{a}]$$

where $\hat{a}$ is the sampled advantage, and $p(\hat{a} | s, t)$ is the distribution over the advantages of actions taken at time step $t$ in state $s$. The surrogate advantage is used to estimate the policy gradient with a clipping term, to prevent the optimization algorithm from taking large steps:

$$\eta_{PPO} = \beta \frac{\sigma(\hat{A} - A)}{\mu_A + \beta}$$

where $\hat A$ is the estimated surrogate advantage, $A$ is the true advantage, $\mu_A$ is the mean of the true advantage, and $\beta$ is a scalar value.

In contrast, the surrogate advantage used by GRPOP is defined as the average of the surrogate advantages of each group, minus the surrogate advantage of the group:

$$Surrogate\ Adv_{GRPOP} = \frac{1}{{\# groups - 1}} \sum_{i=1}^{{\# groups -1}} Surrogate\ Adv_i$$

where $Surrogate\ Adv_i$ is the surrogate advantage of the $i$-th group. This definition takes into account the relative performance of each group, compared to the other groups. This is useful when training a multi-agent RL system, where each agent should be compared only to the other agents in the same team.<|endoftext|>
