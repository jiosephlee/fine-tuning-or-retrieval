The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT):
BOFT is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT that factorizes the orthogonal matrix into multiple block-diagonal matrices, each of which is further parameterized as an product of two orthogonal matrices. This design is motivated by the butterfly structure in fast Fourier transform (FFT), which achieves efficient complexity for computing the discrete Fourier transform.

Formally, BOFT with $d$-dimensional weight transformation uses $\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ parameters and computes the transformed weight $\mathbf{w}^\prime = \mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{w}$ as:
$$
    \mathbf{W}_{\text{BOFT}} = \begin{bmatrix}
    \underbrace{\tilde{W}_1}_{d \times d} & \mathbf{0} \\
    \mathbf{0}&\underbrace{\tilde{\tilde{W}}_1}_{d \times {d \over 2}}\underbrace{\tilte{-W}_2}_{{{d \over 2}\times d}}
    \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \tilde{W}_i,\tilde{\tilde W}_i,-W_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I_{d \times d})
$$

Why does BOFT use the butterfly structure? Why can't it use another structure to achieve parameter efficiency?
The key insight to achieve parameter efficiency is to reduce the number of learnable orthogonal matrices. The paper explores several alternative factorizations (e.g., gemmation, tree structure) with $\mathcal{O(d\log d)}$ parameters, but they all result in the same cost to compute the forward pass $\mathbf{w}_i^\top \mathbf{W}_\text{BOFT}^\top \mathbf{x}$ as the number of matrix-vector multiplication $(\mathbf{w}_iT_\text{FFT})^\top \mathbf{\xi}$ in the fast Fourier transform with $T=d\log d$:
$$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{w}_\text{FFT} &= \tilde{W}_{\text{{BOFT}}} \begin{bmatrix} \tilde{W_1} & \mathbf0 \\ \mathbf0 & \tilde{\tilde {W_1}} \tilde{-W_2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\tilde{W}_2 & \mathbf0\\ \mathbf0&\mathbf0\end{bmatrix}, \\
