According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPo) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It was proposed by OpenAI in their paper "OpenAI Five: An AI System Built to Play the Competitive Multiplayer Game Dota 2" to train large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) agents.

In PPO, we have a set of policies $\pi_1, \pi_2, \cdots, \pi_N$ and we want to find an policy $\pi^*$ that maximizes the expected sum of rewards $J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t r_t]$. To do so, we first sample $B$ trajectories $\{x^{(i)}, a^{(i)}, r^{(i)}, x^{(i+1)}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ from each policy $\pi_i$, where $i = 1, 2, \cdots N$. Then we estimate the advantage function as follows:
$$
A^{(i, t)}(s, a) = \mathbb E_{\pi_i} \left[ G_t^{(i, s, a, x)} - G_t^{(i,\text{roll-out}, s, a, x)}, \right] ,
$$
where $G_t^{(i, t, s, a, X)}$ is the t-th step reward under the trajectory $x$ conditioned on $s = x^{(i, t)}, a$ and $X = \{x^{(j), 0}, a^{(j), 1}, r^{(j), 2}, x^{(j), 3}\}_{j=t-1}^{\infty}$ for all $j > t$. The advantage function estimates how much the reward at time $t$ is influenced by the action $a$ taken at time $t$ relative to some baseline $G_t^{(roll-out, s, a,  x)}$.

In GRPO, the baseline is defined as the group average of the advantage functions of the other policies. That is,
$$
A^{(roll-out, i, t, s,  a, x)} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i} A^{(j, t, s,   a, x)}
$$
The idea is that the group average of the advantages is a reasonable estimate of the average advantage that we would expect if we sampled a policy $\pi^*$ using the true reward function $r$. This is because we assume that the true reward function $J(\pi)$ can be written as $J(\pi)  = \mathbb{R}(s, a) + \epsilon \sigma(s, a, \pi*) $, where $\mathbb{R}(a) $ is the reward function under the true distribution $\mathbb{R}$ and $\epsilon$ is small noise. Therefore, the advantage of taking action $a$ in state $s$ under policy $\pi$ is approximately $\mathbb{R(s, a)} + \epsilon \sigma (s, a, \pi^*) $. If we take the expectation of the advantage function with respect to $\sigma(s, a, ·)$, we get the true reward function $\mathbb{R}$. Since $\sigma(s, a , \pi^*) $ is the same for all policies $\pi$, it gets averaged out in the calculation of the group average baseline.<|endoftext|>
