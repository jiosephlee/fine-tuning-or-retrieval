* **What is QLoRA?** It's a method for performing finetuning with much lower memory requirements. It does this by quantizing the weights of a pretrained model (converting floating-point numbers to integers) while still allowing for efficient updating of those weights during finetuning. The key insight is to separately quantize weights that are far away from the decision surface (which can be identified based on a validation set) and weights that are close to the decision surface. This allows for significant memory savings without degrading performance.
* **Why is it called QLoRA?** The name is a combination of "quantized LLM finetuning" and the acronym LoRA, which is short for "low-rank adaptation". LoRA is a related method for doing finetuning with low memory requirements, but without quantization.
* **How does it work?** The basic idea behind both QLoRA and LoRA is to notice that most of the effect of finetuning happens in a small subset of the weights in a neural network. In the case of LoRA, this is shown by noting that the Gram matrix of a weight matrix only changes slowly with respect to training, and hence it can be updated with a low-rank matrix. In the case of QLoRA, this is more explicitly seen from the fact that weights that are far from the decision surface in the pretrained model are also far from the decision surface after finetuning.
* **How is this related to quantization?** In quantization, the most important observation is that far-away weights are also far away in the other dimensions (this follows directly from the assumption of a multivariate normal distribution). This means that in QLoRA, we can quantize weights that are distant to the decision surface before or after performing LoRA; it doesn't matter, and in practice both approaches give good results.
* **How is memory saved?** In QLoRA, you only need to store the high-precision values for the weights that are close tothe decision surface. This typically means that you use around 8 times less memory compared to 16-bit finetunig, while still using less memory than 16-bit finetune for the first few epochs.
* **Does it hurt performance?** No, at least based on the experiments in the paper. For a given memory budget, QLoRA outperforms both 8-bit finetuning and LoRA. However, it should be kept in mind that LoRA is able to adapt the rank of the weight matrix during training, while the low-rank approximation in QLoRA is only done once, before training.
* **Is it new?** The idea of performing separate quantization for weights that are far from and close to the decision surface is due to a paper by Guo & Antol in 2022. The current paper is the first to show that this approach can be used for both quantization and for LoRA, and to develop a practical pipeline for doing so.
* **How good are the results?** The experiments in the paper show that it's possible to do GPT-4-scale quantized finetuning on a single 16-bit GPU, which was not possible before. In addition to this empirical result, the authors also argue that their method performs as well as 16-bit finetunning based on the validation loss.
* **What experiments are done?** The authors apply their method to finetune a 6B version of Anthropic's GPT-J on a text-to-code task. They find that QLoRA is significantly more memory-efficient than 16-bit finetting while still managing to outperform LoRA and 8-bit finetunning.
