The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be used to adapt text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks. OFT finetunes a diffusion model by optimizing its weights with an orthogonal initialization, which ensures that the finetuned model has the same neuron geometry as the pretrained model. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which updates weights with a identity initialization, causing neurons to move around in activation space. As a result, standard finetuning can change the geometry of neurons and cause the model to forget important features learned from the pretrained data. OFT avoids this issue by ensuring that the geometry of neurons is preserved during finetuning. This is achieved by initializing the finetuning weights with an orthogonal matrix, which ensures that the weights do not change the direction of the neurons. Instead, the weights only change the magnitude of the neurons, allowing the model to focus on learning task-specific features without forgetting important features from the pretrained data. The authors evaluate OFT on the task of adapting Stable Diffusion v1.5 to the CUB bird dataset and show that OFT produces more accurate images than standard finetuning or LoRA. In addition, they find that OFT preserves the semantic structure of the diffusion model, as assessed by measuring the pairwise cosine similarity of neuron activations before and after finetuning. They also compare the semantic structures induced by the pretrained diffusion model, OFT-finetuned models, and other generative models like CLIP-GPT and DALL-E2, and find that the OFT-finetuned model preserves the semantic structure ofthe pretrained model the best. Overall, this paper presents a simple and effective method for adapting large text-to-image diffusion modelsto new tasks.
