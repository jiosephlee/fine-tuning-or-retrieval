According to the paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs", what is QLoRA?<|EOT|>The paper introduces QLoRA, a method for finetuning large language models (LLMs) that have been quantized for inference. QLoRA uses a small linear layer to dequantize the weights during training, allowing standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD) to be used. This is in contrast to methods like AQT that use optimization algorithms designed for non-smooth objectives.

Here is the relevant passage from the paper:

We introduce QLoRA, Efficient FineTuning of Quantized LLaMms. Like AQT, QLoRA performs weight updates using the difference between the forward passes of the unquantized and quantized model, but it represents this difference as a product between a small dequantization matrix and the weight tensor (Equation 4). This lets QLoRA use stochastic gradient descent (SGA) like all other training methods; there is no need for an alternate optimizer or tricks to avoid vanishing gradients. SGA is much more stable than the methods we tried for AQT (e.g. projected Adam (PAdam)) and did not require a small learning rate or other workarounds to converge (though we found that loosing the leading ones in W can stabilize training further; see Section G). Furthermore, because QLoRA does not impose any constraints on the dequantization matrix, it can represent any differentiable weight update. Like AQT, we pack the weights into integers with k-bit precision and use the Low Precision Mixed-precision (LoMP) quantization function, but we evaluated other quantization functions as well (Section F).
