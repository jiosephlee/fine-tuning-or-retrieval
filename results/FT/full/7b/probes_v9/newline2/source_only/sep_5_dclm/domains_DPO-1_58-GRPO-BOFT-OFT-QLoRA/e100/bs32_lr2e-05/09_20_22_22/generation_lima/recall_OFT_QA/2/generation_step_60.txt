What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved impressive results in generating photorealistic images from text prompts. However, these models are prone to toxic content and have safety concerns. To address this, the paper proposes a controllable and safe finetuning method - Orthogonal Finetuning. 

Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is an algorithm for finetuning large pretrained models, like text-to-image diffusion models, in a way that preserves key geometric properties of the pretrained model. The key property that OFT preserves is that it ensures that the weights get finetuned in an orthogonal (or rotation) manner, not in a shear manner, as in standard finetuning or with additive methods like LoRA.

Formally, for a matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$, the goal of orthogonal finetuning is to find a matrix $ \hat{W} $ such that $ \hat{W}$ is orthogonal to $W$:

$$
\hat{W} W^\top = W W^\top
$$

In other words, orthogonal finetuning only rotates the vectors represented by $W$, but does not change their lengths (i.e., stretch or compress them in a shear manner). This is in contrast to standard finetuning, where the weights are changed in a manner that depends on both the direction of the weight and the magnitude of the weight, and can therefore change the key geometric properties of the neurons that the weight represents.

The name "Orthogonal Finetuning" comes from the fact that for a matrix, being orthogonal to $W$ is equivalent to being orthogonal to $W^\top$. This means that we can rewrite the equation above as:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \|\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}_0\|_2^2 \text{ subject to } \mathbf{w}^\top W = 0
$$

where $\mathbf{w}$ denotes a row vector in $W$, and $\mathbf{w}_1$ is some vector. This can be interpreted as finding a vector $\mathbf{w} $ that is orthogonal to $W$, subject to having length $\|\mathbf{w}\|$.

The OFT algorithm is based on a simple update rule that guarantees that the weights are finetuned in an approximately orthogonal manner. The update rule is:

$$
W \left( I - \beta B^\top B \right) 
$$

where $I$ is the identity matrix, $B^\top B$ is a matrix that is close to an identity matrix (but with some elements replaced by 0), and $\beta$ is a scalar parameter. Intuitively, the matrix $B^\top B $ acts as a projection matrix that projects the space onto a hyperplane, and the matrix $I - \beta B^\ top B $ rotates this hyperplane so that it is orthogonal to the pretrained weights $W$.

OFT has been shown to be effective in preserving key geometric properties of the neuron representations, while still allowing for significant finetuning of the model's performance on a task. Moreover, OFT does not introduce any additional computational cost compared to standard finetuning (beyond the cost of computing $B^\top B)$, making it a simple and effective method for safe and controllable finetuning of large pretrained models.<|endoftext|>
