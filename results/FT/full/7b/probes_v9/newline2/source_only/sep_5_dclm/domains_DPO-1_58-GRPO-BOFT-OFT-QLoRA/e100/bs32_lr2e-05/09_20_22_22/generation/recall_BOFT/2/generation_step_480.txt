Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that uses a butterfly structure to reduce the number of trainable parameters. BOFT is an extension of OFT, which is a parameter-efficient finiteting method that prevents catastrophic forgetting by learning orthogonal weight transformations. BOFT improves the parameter efficiency of OFT by a factor of up to 64, while maintaining performance comparable to fully parameterized OFT on a range of finetuning tasks, including natural language tasks, computer vision tasks, and control tasks in robotics. The paper also introduces a simple yet effective regularization strategy for BOFT.

Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning? Finetuning large pretrained models has become a standard practice in many applications, such as natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV). However, finetuning large models is often memory and computational expensive. To address the memory and computational cost, parameter-efficient finetuning methods have been developed. These methods learn weight transformations that are applied to a small set of frozen weights, instead of learning the full model parameters. A well-studied parameter-efficient finetuning approach is orthogonal finetuning (OFT), which learns orthogonal weight transformations. Despite its success, OFT transforms all weights of a layer, which results in significant number of unused transformed weights. To address this issue, the block-diagonal OFT (BD-OFT) method was proposed, which only transforms the diagonal elements of a weight matrix. Although BD-OFT reduces the number of transformed parameters, it incurs a loess accurate finetuning due to its approximation of the original weight matrix. To address this issue while keeping the low number of transformed parameters, the butterfly structure, which has been used in many efficient matrix-vector multiplication algorithms, is introduced in BOFT. BOFT transforms the off-diagonal elements of a weight matrix into the identity matrix using orthogonal transformations, and keeps the diagonal elements unchanged, i.e., the original diagonal elements. Experimental results show that BOFT is more parameter-efficient than OFT and BD-OFT, while performing similarly or better than them.
