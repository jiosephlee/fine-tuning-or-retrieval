The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses a group of policies to estimate the baseline for each sampled transition, avoiding the need to store all past trajectories like PPO does. This makes it more memory efficient and faster to train than PPO, allowing it to be used with much larger models and data sets than before.

The DeepSeekMath model is trained on 7T tokens of math-related text from Common Crawl, combining mathematical expressions, proofs, and explanations found in textbooks, research papers, and online discussions. The model is trained on 3.8B parameters on 8V100 GPUs for 150 days, resulting in a 7B parameter model. This is the largest open-source math model to date, and it significantly outperforms previous models on a variety of mathematical reasoning tasks.

The DeepSeekMath team evaluated their model on eight benchmarks, including MathQA, MATH, and MMLU, which measure language model's ability to answer mathematical questions, and MATHIE, MATHEMATICA, and MMLU PROOF, which measure the model's ability to generate proofs and solutions to mathematical problems. The results show that DeepSeekMath is the new state-of-the-art on all eight benchmarks, often by a significant margin.
