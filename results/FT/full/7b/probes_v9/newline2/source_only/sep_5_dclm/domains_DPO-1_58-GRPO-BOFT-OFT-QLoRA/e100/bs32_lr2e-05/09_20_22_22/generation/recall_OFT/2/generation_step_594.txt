The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be used to adapt text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks. OFT finetunes a pretrained diffusion model by optimizing its weights with an orthogonal initialization along a task-specific direction, which can be learned in an unsupervised way. OFT has several desirable properties: it preserves the pairwise distances between neurons, so the semantic information is generally preserved; it produces stable training with stable learning rates; and most importantly, it produces high-quality adaptations to new tasks. The paper evaluates OFT on the task of adapting text-to-image diffusion models for different downstream tasks, including changing the subject of the generated image given a prompt, generating images of body parts given a prompt, and stylizing images in the style of different artists. OFT is able to produce high-fidelity adaptations to these different tasks, often performing as well as hand-designed models that have been trained from scratch on the same task. In addition, the paper evaluates OFT on a related problem, adapting latent code spaces of diffusion models to produce latent codes that are more meaningful for a particular task. For example, the paper considers adapting the latent code space of an image diffusion model so that codes that are close in the latent space also look similar in the generated images, a property that is not present in the latent space of the pretrained model.
