What is Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal finetuning?

Parameter-Efficient Orthogonally finetuning (PEOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method. It uses a low-rank matrix to parameterize the weight update. The low-rank matrix is updated by orthogonal matrix. The orthogonal matrix is updated by a simple and efficient algorithm. The algorithm is called Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning (EOF).

How does EOF work?

The core of EOF is to update the orthogonal matrix. The update rule is as follows:

$$\mathbf{R}^{(t+1)} = \mathbf{R}^t + \beta \mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}^\top_t$$

Where $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ is the orthogonal matrix, $\mathbf{D} \in \mathcal{S}^d_+$ is a diagonal matrix, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter.

The update rule is similar to the power iteration method. The power iteration method is used to compute the dominant eigenvalue and eigenvector of a square matrix. The update rule of the power iteration method is as follows:

$$ \mathbf{v}^{(t+ 1)} = \frac{\mathbf{v}^t}{\|\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{v}\|} \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} ^t $$

Where $\mathbf A$ is a square matrix, $\mathbf v$ is an eigenvector, and $\lambda$ is the corresponding eigenvalue.

The difference between EOF and the power iteration method is that EOF uses a diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}$ to rescale the orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$.

Why does EOF work?

The update rule of EOF is similar to the power method. The power method is used to compute a dominant eigenvector of a matrix. The dominant eigenvector is important in many fields. For example, the dominant eigenvector of the covariance matrix is the principal component. The dominant eigenvectors of the adjacency matrix are the hub nodes in the network.

The power method works because the power iteration process converges to the dominant eigenvector. The convergence is guaranteed by the Gershgorin circle theorem. The Gershgorin circle is defined as follows:

$$G_i(\mathbf{A}) = \{z : |z - a_{ii}| \le \sum_{j \ne i} |a_{ij}|\}$$

Where $G_i(\mathbf A)$ is the $i$-th Gershgorin circle of the matrix $\mathbf A$, $a_{ii}$ is the diagonal element of $\mathbf A$, and $a_{ij}$ is the off-diagonal element of $\mathbf{A}$.

The Gershgorin circle tells us that all the eigenvalues of a matrix $\mathbf A$ are located in the union of all Gershgorin circles. In particular, the dominant eigenvalue $\lambda_d$ and its corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf v_d$ must be located in the largest Gershgorin circle. The radius of the largest Gershgorine circle is $\max_i \sum_{j \in [n]} |a_{ij}|$. Therefore, if we initialize the vector $\mathbf v$ randomly and normalize it, then the iteration process $\mathbf v^{(t+1}} = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{v}$ will converge to the dominant eigenvectors.
