First, some background on reinforcement learning and policy optimization algorithms. Reinforcement learning is a type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by interacting with its environment. The agent receives rewards or punishments for its actions, and over time, it learns to take actions that maximize its cumulative reward. Policy optimization algorithms are a class of reinforcement learning algorithms that optimize the policy function, which specifies the probability distribution over possible actions given the current state of the environment. One popular policy optimization algorithm is Proximal Policy Optimization, or PPO.

PPO works by estimating a surrogate objective function called the advantage function, which measures how much better or worse an action is compared to the baseline action. The baseline action is typically the average action taken by the policy in recent states. To estimate the advantage function, PPO uses a value function, which estimates the expected reward of taking an action in a given state. PPO then updates the policy parameters to increase the expected reward of the selected action, while also clipping the maximum increase in the advantage function to prevent the policy from becoming too aggressive.
