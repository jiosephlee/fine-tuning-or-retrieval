1. What is Text-to-Image Diffussion?

Text-to-Image Diffussion is a generative model that can generate images from text descriptions. It is based on the generative adversarial network (GAN). The GAN consists of two networks: a generator and a discriminator. The generator generates images from text descriptions. The discriminator distinguishes real images from fake images generated by the generator. The generator and the discriminator are trained simultaneously. The generator tries to generate images that are indistinguishable from real images, and the discriminator tries to distinguish real images from fake images. The training process is repeated until the discriminator cannot distinguish real images from fake ones.

2. What is Orthogonal Finetuning?

Orthogonal Finetuning is a method for finetuning neural networks. It was proposed in the paper "Orthogonal Finetuning of Neural Networks". The idea is to add a small regularization term to the loss function of the neural network. This regularization term encourages the weights of the neural network to be orthogonal to each other. Orthogonal weights are less correlated, which makes the neural network more robust and less prone to overfitting. In addition, orthogonal weights can improve the generalization performance of the neural network.

3. How does Orthogonal Finetuning work?

The basic idea of Orthogonal Finetuning is to add a small penalty term to the loss function. This penalty term is proportional to the dot product of the weight matrix and its transpose. The weight matrix is the weight matrix of the neural network. The penalty term is calculated as follows:

penalty = 0.01 * ||W * transpose(W) ||^2

where W is the weight matrix, and ||.|| is the L2 norm. The penalty term is added to the original loss function. The new loss function is:

loss = original_loss + penalty

The penalty term is very small, so it does not affect the training process too much. However, it has a significant impact on the weights of the neural networks. It encourages the weights to be orthogonal to eachother. This makes the neural network more stable and less prone to overfiting.
