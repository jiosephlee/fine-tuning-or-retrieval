According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method that prevents knowledge erosion while allowing task-specific adaptation. It achieves this by learning an orthogonal transformation and then transforming the pretrained weights plus the orthogonal matrix. This results in exactly the same forward pass as the pretrained model, but with slightly different weights. The key is that the weights of the pretrained model are unchanged, so the knowledge that they have learned is preserved, but the weights are modified by the orthogonal matrix, so there is potential for adaptation to the task at hand.

The name "orthogonal finetuning" comes from the fact that the learned matrix is orthogonal, i.e., its rows are mutually orthogonal unit vectors. This ensures that the matrix is invertible and has a few other nice mathematical properties.

OFT was introduced in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion with orthogonal finetuning" by Anirudh Goyal, Justin Gilmer, Petersen Abbeel. It is closely related to another finetuning method introduced in the same paper, Angular Finetuning (or AFT), which learns a unit vector instead of an orthogonal matrix. Both OFT and AFT achieve better results than traditional finetuning while preserving its simplicity.<|endoftext|>
