Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT factorizes the standard finetuning weight matrix into a product of two matrices, which yields a significant reduction in the number of trainable parameters while preserving the original OFT's convergence and generalization properties. The authors evaluate BOFT across a range of languages, architectures, and datasets, showing that it significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters without sacrificing performance. In natural language processing (NLP), BOFT outperforms OFT and other state-of-the-art parameter-efficient finetuning methods, including Block-diagonal OFT (BlkOFT) and Group-diagonal OFT (GrpOFT). BOFT is also more parameter-efficient than learning rate drops and wide linear (WL) layers, two popular non-finetuning methods. User studies demonstrate that BOFT is more stable and has fewer catastrophic forgetting than OFT and BlkOFT. BOFT is available in PyTorch at https://github.com/facebookresearch/boft.

Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning?

Finetuning, or fine-tuning, is a simple and effective training technique where we take a pretrained model and fine-tune all or part of its parameters on a target task. It has revolutionized NLP by enabling the use of very large models. However, finetuning large models is computationally expensive and incurs a significant carbon footprint. Reducing the number of trainable parameters in finetuning is an important and under-explored problem with the potential for significantly reducing costs and carbon emissions. This is the motivation for parameter-efficient finetuning.

The BOFT paper proposes a new parameter-efficient finetuning algorithm called BOFT. BOFT is based on a factorization of the standard finetuning weights matrix that yields a significant reduction in parameters while preserving the convergence and generalization properties of OFT. BOFT also introduces a block-diagonal variant called BlkBOFT, which further reduces the number of parameters while slightly sacrificing performance.
