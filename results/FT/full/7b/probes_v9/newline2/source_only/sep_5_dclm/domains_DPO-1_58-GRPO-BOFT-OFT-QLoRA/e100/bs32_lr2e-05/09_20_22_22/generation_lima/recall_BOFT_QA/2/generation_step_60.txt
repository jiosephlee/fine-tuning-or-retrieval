The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT. It factorizes the orthogonal matrix into multiple block-diagonal matrices, where each diagonal block is further factorized into two butterfly matrices. This results in a total of $4 \times log(\frac{2k}{\epsilon})$ parameters for $k$-layer BOFT, compared to $16 \times k$ parameters for standard OFT.

The orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R}$ used in OFT can be written as
$$
\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{F}_4 \mathbf{F}^T_4 \mathbf{R}_3 \mathbf{F }^T_3 \cdots \mathbf{F}_{l+1} \mathbf{F)^T}_{l+1}  \mathbf{R}_{l} \mathbf{ F}^T_l,
$$
where $\mathbf{F}_i$ is a butterfly matrix and $\mathbf{R}_i$ is a block-diagonal matrix. For simplicity, we set $l=1$ in the following context.
A butterfly matrix $\mathbf{F}$ of size $n \times n$ is a block matrix with four sub-matrices of size $d \times d$:
$$
\mathbf {F} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{0}_{d\times d}&\mathbf{I}_{d/2\times d/2}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{ d/2\times d /2} &\mathbf{0}_ {d\times d}
\end{bmatrix},
$$
where $\mathbb{0}_{m\times n}$ is an $m\times n$ zero matrix and $\mathbb{I}_{m\times n)$ is an $m\ttimes n$ identity matrix. The zero blocks have size $d\times (d/2)$.

In BOFT, we replace the orthogonal matrix $\mathbf {R}$ with
$$
\mathcal{BO}(d, k) = \mathbf{ F}_1 \cdots \mathbb{F}_4^{(k-1)} ,
$$
where $\mathcal{BO}(2,d)$ is called the orthogonal butterfly, which is defined as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{ F}_4^{(i)} &= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R}^{(i)} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}&\mathbf{R^{(i)T}} \end{bmatrix},\quad i=1,2,3,4, \\
\mathbf{R }^{(1)}&=\text{O}(d),\\
\mathbf{ R }^{(i+1)}&=\text{T}\left(\mathbf{ R }^ {(i) }\right),\quad i=1,\cdots,4-1.
\end{aligned}
