What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that balances exploitation and exploration by clipping the advantage estimate $Adv$ and performing stochastic optimization on a modified surrogate objective:
```Surrogate = Clip(Adv, * clip_value_range) *
           mu - Lambda * Lambda_max(1 - mu)
```
where $\mu$ is the current policy, and $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ is a hyperparameter.

The key challenge with PPO is that it requires storing the maximum value $\max_{\theta} Adv(\theta, M; S)$ over all policies $\theta$ parameterizing the RL agent $M$ for every state-action pair $S$. This can be quite memory-intensive, especially in large RL problems such as large language models.

To address this challenge, the DeepSeekMath paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization, or GRPO, which only stores the maximum advantage within each group of policies. Specifically, the paper partitions the set of policies $\Theta$ into $K$ non-overlapping groups $\{ \Theta^k \}_{k=1}^K$, and maintains the following $K$ memory variables:

* $\text{max}_{\theta \in \Theta^k} Adv(\theta, S; L) :$ the maximum advantage of any policy in group $k$ over the corpus $L$
* $\text{max}_k \text{max}_{\θ ∉ Θ^k} Adv(θ, S; L) $ : the maximum advantage of any single policy over the corpus $L$ among all groups $k$

With these memory variables in place, the GRPO surrogate objective for policy $\mu$ becomes:

```Surrogate = Clip(
    Adv[μ] - Lambda * Lambda_max[1 - μ],
    * clip_value_range) /
    max_k [Adv[θ; L]] for θ in Θ^k
```

In other words, the surrogate objective for GRPO clips the advantage estimate for policy $\mu$ against the maximum advantage of any group of policies, rather than the maximum advantage of any possible policy in the entire space $\Theta$ as in PPO.

It's worth noting that the memory requirements for GRPO are much lower than PPO – specifically, the first memory variable requires storing $| \Theta^k |$ advantages, while the second variable requires storing just one additional advantage. In practice, this can reduce the memory requirements of PPO from $\mathcal{O}(|\Theta|)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log |\Theta|)$.<|endoftext|>
