Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be used to adapt text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks. OFT finetunes a pretrained diffusion model by optimizing its weights with an orthogonal initialization along a task-specific direction, which can be learned in an unsupervised way. OFT has several desirable properties: it preserves the pairwise distances between neurons, so the semantic information is generally preserved; it produces stable training with stable learning rates; and most importantly, it produces models with excellent generalization. On the task of controlling styles in text-to-image diffusion models, OFT significantly outperforms the strong baseline of LoRA finetuning, achieving realistic controllable images in a single iteration. In addition to controlling styles, the authors also demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT in other down-stream applications, including recovering image sounds and dreaming in high-resolution.

Natural images often come with rich semantic information and low-level visual features. Diffusion models are trained to reconstruct the latent code step by step, such that the latent code captures both high-level semantics and low-level features of the corresponding image. As a result, the latent code of a diffusion model can be viewed as a compact representation of the image. To achieve controllability, previous works either directly optimize the latent code or regularize the optimization to lie in a low-dimensional manifold. These methods are limited by the representational power of the low-dimensional manifold. In contrast, OFT adapts a pretrained diffusion model without imposing any constraints on the representation, and thus can achieve strong controllability without losing the expressiveness of the original model. The main idea of OFT is to adapt a pretrained diffusion model by finetuning it with an orthogonal weight initialization. That is, at each finetuning iteration, OFT optimizes the weights of a pretrained diffusion model with an orthogonal weight matrix. To achieve an orthogonal weight matrix, OFT learns a direction of finetuning for each neuron, and then initializes the finetuning weight matrix such that its columns are initialized as the learned directions and all have norm 1. Orthogonality is preserved throughout the finetuning process because orthogonality is a invariant under orthogonal transformations. Compared to standard finetuning or LoRA, OFT has an important advantage that it preserves the pairwise distances among neurons, so the semantic Information is generally preserved. Intuitively, standard finetuning or Additive methods like LoRA change the representation of a neuron by moving its cloud of activated pixels in the image, and such a representation change may alter the semantic meaning of the neuron if its activated pixels are crucial for its semantic meaning. With OFT, the representation of a neuron is changed by rotating its cloud of activated pixels without moving them, so its semantic meaning is more likely to be preserved. Another advantage of OFT is that it tends to produce stable training with stable learning rate. This is because OFT updates tend to be small when the angles between neurons are small, i.e., when they are close to each other in the feature space. As a result, OFT is less likely to cause catastrophic forgetting that causes distant neurons to be updated by large angles. The nice generalization property of OFT is also an important advantage. To evaluate the performance of OFT, the authors compare OFT to standard finetuning and LoRA using ControlNet as the pretrained diffusion model, and evaluate the performance in controlling styles in images using the style control dataset from pi2img (Chen et al., 2022b). The results show that OFT achieves significantly better control than both standard finetuning andLoRA, such that the reference image can be recovered with a single control code. In addition, OFT achieves this level of control with an order-of-magnitude smaller code length. To further validate the effectiveness of OFT, the author also applies OFT to other down-stream applications,including recovering image sounds and dreamingin high-resolution.

Recovering Image Sounds
The paper "Audio2Image: Recovering Image Sounds" presents Audio2Image, a method that recovers visual representations from audio signals. Audio2Image takes an audio signal as input and outputs a 2D heat map that locates the objects that are associated with the audio. The key insight of Audio2Image is that there is a strong correlation between the spectrogram of an audio signal and the latent code of a pretrained diffusion model when the latent code is sampled from the conditional distribution p(x|y), where x is an image and y is a text prompt. For example, the text prompt "an airplane taking off" will produce an image with an airplane in the latent code of a text-to-image diffusion model. Similarly, the audio signal produced when an airplane takes off will also produce the same latent code in a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model.
