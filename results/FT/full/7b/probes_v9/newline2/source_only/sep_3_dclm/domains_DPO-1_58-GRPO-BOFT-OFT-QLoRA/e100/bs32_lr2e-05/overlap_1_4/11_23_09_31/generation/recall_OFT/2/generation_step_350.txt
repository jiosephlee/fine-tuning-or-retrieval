**Diffusion Models:**

Diffusion models are a type of generative model that learn to generate data by learning a probability distribution over images. They do this by training on a dataset of images and a noise vector, and then learning a mapping from the noise vector to the images. The training process involves optimizing the model parameters such that the generated images become more and more similar to the real images as the noise vector is set to zero. This process mimics the physical process of cooling a liquid, where the molecules slow down and form a solid as the temperature decreases.

One popular diffusion model is Stable Diffusion, which is a large-scale diffusion model trained on a diverse dataset of 1.2 million images. It can generate high-quality images in a wide range of styles and categories. However, like many other diffusion models, Stable Diffusion was trained on a dataset that is not diverse in terms of race, gender, or other demographic characteristics. This means that the generated images may reflect the biases and stereotypes present in the training data.

**Finetuning:**

Finetuning is a common technique used to adapt machine learning models to new data. It involves updating the weights of a pretrained model with those learned from a small amount of additional training data. This allows the model to specialize in the task at hand while still retaining the knowledge learned from the pretrained weights.

In the context of diffusion models, finetuning has been used to adapt the model to a specific dataset or task. For example, the authors of the paper "Controlling Text to Image Diffusion with Clipped Orthogonal Finetuning", or COFT, finetuned the weights of Stable Diffusion to a dataset of medical images.
