What is QLoRA?

It's a method for finetuning large language models (LLMs) with low-precision weights. It's based on LoRA (Layer Normalization for LLMs), which is a method for finetuing LLMs with low-precision weights.

Why do we need QLoRA?

Because finetuning LLMs with high-precision weights is expensive. For example, the cost of finetuning a 7B LLM with 16-bit weights on 400G text is about 30,000 USD.

How does QLoRA work?

It works by quantizing LLM weights from high precision to low precision and then finetuning the quantized weights with LoRA.

What does it mean to quantize weights?

It means to map high-precision weights to low-precision weights. For example, mapping 16-bit weights to 4-bit weights.

How does QLoRa quantize weights?

It uses the following formula:

$$ \tilde{w}_{i,j} = \frac{w_{i,j} - \mathbb{E}[w]}{std(w)} $$

where $\tilde{w}_{ij}$ is the low-precision weight, $w_{ij}$ is the high-precision weight, $\mathbb{E}[W]$ is the mean of all weights, and $std(w)$ is the standard deviation of all weights.

Why does QLoRA use this formula?

Because it minimizes the mean squared error between the original weight matrix and the quantized weight matrix.

How does QLo RA finetune the quantized weights?

It uses LoRA, which updates the weights using the following formula:

$$ w_{i,j} = \alpha \cdot \nabla_{w_{i,j}} \log p(y_{t+i}|x_{t+1..}, \tilde{W}) + (1-\alpha) \cdot w_{i,j} $$

where $w_{ij}$ and $\tilde{w_{ij}}$ are the weights before and after updating, $\alpha$ is the learning rate, $p(y_t|x_{t+1}, W)$ is the probability distribution over tokens generated by the language model given the input sequence $x_{t+1}$ and the weight matrix $W$, $\nabla_{w_ij} \log p(y_t|x_{t+1}, w)$ is the gradient of the log probability distribution with respect to the element in the $i$-th row and $j$-th column of the weight matrix, and $x$ and $y$ are sequences of tokens.

  • It can finetune LLMs with 4-bit weights, which is much cheaper than finetuning LLM with 16-bits weights.
