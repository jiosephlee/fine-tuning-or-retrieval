According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>The Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft) structure is a parameter-efficient variant of the block-diagonal structure, which is widely used in deep learning. BOft has the same spectral property as the block-diagonal structure and can be viewed as a generalization of the block-diagonal structures. Specifically, BOft can represent all low-rank block-diagonal structures and banded diagonal structures as special cases. Moreover, BOft can also represent some structures that cannot be represented by the block-diagonal structure. The name "Butterfly" comes from the fact that the coefficient matrix of BOft has a butterfly shape, as shown in Figure 1. The BOft structure is defined as:

$$\mathbf{M} = \begin'to\begin{bmatrix}
R_1 & & & \\
& R_2 & & \\
& & \ddots & \\
& & & R_n
\end{bmatrix},
\text{s.t.}
R_i = \begin{bmatrix}
O(\lambda) & F_i \\
F_i^\top & O(\sqrt{\lambda})
\end{bmatrix}, i=1,\cdots,n,
$$

where $\lambda$ is a user-specified parameter controlling the condition number of the resulting matrix $\mathbf{M}$, and $O(\cdot)$ hides polylogarithmic factors. Each submatrix $R_i$ is an orthogonal matrix with a banded structure, where $F_i$ is the bandwidth-1 banded matrix with $\lambda$ on the diagonal. The BOft structure can be viewed as a block-diagonal structure with an arbitrary block size. When $\lambda=1$, the BOft structure reduces to the block-diagonal structure.

The BOft structure can be efficiently implemented using the following matrix-vector multiplication:

$$\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{y}, \text{s.t.}
\mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrainsize}{1}\mathbf{y}_1\\\mathbf{y}_2\\\vdots\\\mathbf{y}_{n}\end{bmatrix},\mathbf{x} =
\begin{bmatrix} 
\mathbf{x}_1\\\mathbf{x}_2\\\vdots\\ \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

where $\mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{y}$ for $i=1,\cdots, n$. This implementation has the same computational complexity as the block-diagonal case.<|endoftext|>
