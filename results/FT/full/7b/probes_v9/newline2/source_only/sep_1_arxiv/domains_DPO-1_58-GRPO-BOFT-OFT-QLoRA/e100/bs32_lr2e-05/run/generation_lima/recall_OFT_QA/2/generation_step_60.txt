The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT for short) is a method for finetuning pretrained neural networks, that aims to preserve the geometry of the pretrained weights. The key idea is to perform finetuning by applying orthogonal transformation to the neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning where non-orthogonal transformation is applied, and layer-normalization-based methods like LoRA that apply different transformation for each layer.

To perform orthogonal finetuning, we first need to compute the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the pretrained weight matrix. Then, in each training iteration, we update the weight matrix by adding a small multiple of an orthogonal matrix to the pretrained weight matrix. The orthogonal matrix is computed by applying spectral decomposition to a small matrix that is derived from the gradient of the loss with respect to the weight matrix.

Theoretical analysis and empirical study show that orthogonal finetuning can prevent catastrophic forgetting well, and produce good performance on the downstream task. It is particularly effective when the amount of training data for finetuning is limited, and when the downstream task is quite different from the pretrained task.
