According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method that prevents catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously allowing for large learning rate updates. It does so by updating weights with orthogonal matrices, which preserve the pairwise angles between neurons. This means that the pretrained weight matrix $W^0$ is preserved up to rotation, and thus the pretrained linear transformation is approximately preserved. At the same time, OFT allows for large learning rate updates because the gradient of the orthogonal matrix $R$ does not get absorbed into the weight matrix $W^{\text{OFT}} = W^0 R$.

The key insight behind OFT is that most existing finetuning methods implicitly assume that the pretrained weight matrix captures the essence of the pretrained model, and that small updates to this matrix will lead to improvements. While this is often true, it is not always the case, especially when finetuning models to very different tasks. For example, when finetuning a pretrained image classifier, the important information might be in the activation of certain neurons in the penultimate layer, and not in the weights of that layer. If the weights of that layer are updated with an non-orthogonal matrix, the activations of the relevant neurons might get swapped with the activations of other neurons, effectively forgetting how to perform image classification. By using an orthogonal matrix to update the weights, OFT prevents this from happening.

OFT was introduced in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion with orthogonal finetuning" by Anselh et al.. The paper showed that OFT is effective for controlling text-to-image diffusion models, allowing for the creation of far more controllable variants of the original Stable Diffusion model.<|endoftext|>
