Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that prevents collapse while preserving the semantic space. OFT finetunes the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model in an orthogonal manner by modifying the forward pass of the neural network. Specifically, OFT reparameterizes the weight update as $w^{(k+1)}=w^{(k)}+skY$, where $Y$ is an orthogonal matrix. To generate high-fidelity, high-resolution images, they introduce a scalable variant of OFT, called Multiple Scale OFT (MS-OFT), that finetunes the model at multiple scales. For MS-OFT, they develop efficient algorithms to compute the orthogonal matrix $Y$ for each scale. They also propose a practical strategy to construct the orthogonal matrices at different scales. ### What is the problem?

Text-to-image diffusion models have been making rapid progress in recent times. These models are capable of generating high-fidelity images from text prompts. However, these models are pre-trained on large datasets and hence reflect the biases present in the data. Therefore, it is important to finetune these models on smaller datasets to remove the unwanted biases. Finetuning is a simple and effective way to adapt pretrained models to new tasks. However, finetuning text-to image diffusion models present some unique challenges. Standard finetuning can cause the collapse of the semantic space, causing the model to forget the original training data. Existing methods such as LoRA are not effective enough in preventing the collapse of the semantic spaces. In addition, these methods are slow during finetuning.

### What is the solution?

The paper "Controlling TExt-to-Image Diffusion bY Orthogonal Finetuning” introduces a simple yet effective method called Orthogonal Finetuning for finetuning text to image diffusion models. Orthogonal Finetuing (OFT) is able to prevent the collapse of the semantic during finetuning while being much faster than existing methods. The key insight of OFT is to perform finetuning in an orthogonal manner, i.e., the weight space is rotated in an orthogonal manner in every iteration. OFT reparameterizes weight update as $w^ {(k+1)} = w^k + s k Y$, where $Y$ denotes an orthogonal matrix. To prevent the collapse of the sematic space, OFT updates the weights in the preimage space, i.e., the latent code space of the diffusion model. In other words, OFT updates the parameters such that the distribution of the latent codes do not change. This is in contrast to existing methods that update the parameters in the image space. Updating the parameters in the image spaces causes the semantic space to collapse. Preserving the distribution of the latent code prevents the collapse of the semantic information.
