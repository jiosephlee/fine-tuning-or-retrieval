According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It is designed to improve the stability of PPO in the multi-agent setting. In particular, in GRPO, the surrogate objective used in PPO is modified to be a group-wise mean, instead of the average, of the advantage function surrogates for all the agents. Formally, let $a_i$ be the advantage surrogate for the $i$-th agent, then in PPO, we minimize $\mu_a = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N a_i$. In contrast, in GRPO, we minimize $\tilde{a} = \frac{1}{{\rm rank}(L)} \sum_{i=0}^{{\rm rank}( L) - 1} L_{i,*} a_{i,*}$, where $L$ is a diagonal matrix with $L_{ii} = \sqrt{N/i}$, and $a_{i,*}, \forall i$, are the elements of the vector $a$ that we want to minimize the mean of in the group.
The motivation for this modification is to ensure that the loss for all the agents is comparable, and thus, the agents are treated similarly during the training process. In the multi-agent setting, the number of training episodes for different agents can be very different, and the surrogates for the advantage function, $a_i$, can have very different magnitudes as a result. In such cases, if we use the mean, $\mu_a$, of the $a_i$'s, the gradient of the surrogate objective with respect to the policy of each agent may have very different magnitudes, depending on the magnitude of $a_i$. This can lead to unstable training for some agents. In contrast, the surrogates for advantage in GRPO, $\tilde{a}$, are normalized, so that the gradient of the surrogate with respect to the policy for all the agents has the same magnitude.<|endoftext|>
