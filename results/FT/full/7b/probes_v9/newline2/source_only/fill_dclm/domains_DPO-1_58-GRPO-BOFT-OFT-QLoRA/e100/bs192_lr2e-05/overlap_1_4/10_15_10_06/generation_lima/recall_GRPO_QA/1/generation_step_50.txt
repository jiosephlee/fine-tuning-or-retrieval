According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It is designed to improve the stability of PPO in the context of training large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) agents. GR PO is particularly effective in training large-scale deep RL agents, such as large-scale open AI models, in complex continuous control tasks.

GR PO is an on-policy algorithm, which means that it only collects data from the current policy. This is in contrast to off-policy algorithms, such as actor-critic methods, which can use data from different policies. On-policy algorithms are generally more sample efficient than off-policy algorithms, because they do not waste samples from the current policy on data from previous policies.

The main idea behind GR PO is to estimate the advantage using a moving average of the advantages of the current policy. This moving average is called the group mean. The group mean is used to normalize the advantages, so that the normalization constant does not change too much over time. This helps to stabilize the training process.

Here is the pseudocode of GR PO:

```
for episode = 1 to Eps:
    state = env.reset()
    for t = 1 to T:
        action = policy(state)
        next_state, reward, done, info = env.step(action)
        state = next_state
        if done:
            break
    advantage = reward - group_mean + gamma * max(group_value)
    critic_loss = critic_loss + alpha * advantage^2
    policy_loss = policy_loss + alpha * advantage * clip(policy - soft_target, -L, L)
    soft_target = beta * soft_target + (1 - beta) * policy
    soft_target = clamp(soft_target, min_policy, max_policy)
    if episode % save_freq == 0:
        print("episode:", episode, "advantage:", group_mean)
```

Where `gamma` is the discount factor, `alpha` is the coefficient of the advantage function, `beta` is the coefficient of soft target, and `L` is the clipping threshold.

The group value is estimated using a moving average of advantages:

```
group_value = beta * group_value + (1 - beta ) * advantage
```

Where `beta` is the same coefficient as in the soft target update.

The group mean is estimated as the average of the advantages of a group of policies. A new group of policies is created every `num_groups` episodes. The group mean is initialized as zero, and then updated as:

```
group_mean = (1 / num_policies) * sum(group_mean)
```

Where the sum is over all policies in the group. The number of policies in each group is equal to the number of experiences per episode multiplied by `num_policies`, and `num_policies` should be greater than the number of experiences per policy.

```
group_value_i = beta * group_value_i + (1 - beta)*advantage_i
```
