What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, as defined in the paper "Direct Preference Optimization") is a method for training language models to satisfy user preferences, without using a separate reinforcement learning (RL) agent. Instead, the language model is trained directly on a proxy reward function derived from the preference data.

The key insight is that the preference data can be interpreted as providing a distribution over rewards for each generated sentence, under the assumption that the user's choice of which sentence to select was determined by the reward associated with that sentence. This allows us to train the language model to maximize the expected reward, given by the true reward function, under the assumption that this reward distribution is correct. In other words, we can train the language model to optimize the following objective:

$$\mathbb{E}_{r \sim \hat{p}_r(\cdot \mid S, A)}[\log p_{\theta}(A \mid S)]$$

where $p_{\theta}(A\mid S)$ is the language model, $S$ is a sentence, $A$ is an action, and $\hat{p}_r(r\mid S, A)$ is the reward distribution induced by the preference data, conditioned on the sentence and action. This is equivalent to maximizing the expected reward under the true reward function $p_r(r\mid S, 1_A S)$, where $1_A$ is an indicator function that is 1 if action $A$ is taken and 0 otherwise:
