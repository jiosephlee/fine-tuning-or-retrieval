The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images from a short text prompt. These models are trained using a combination of reconstruction loss and a perceptual loss, such as the SSL loss. During inference, the reconstruction loss is no longer needed, and the models are typically finetuned using an additive update, such as LoRA. This finetuning is done to prevent the model from overfitting to a single prompt. 

The authors of the paper propose a new finetuning method called Orthogonal Finetuning. The name comes from the fact that the update applied to the model weights using OFT is orthogonal. 

Formally, let $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ be the weight matrix of a fully connected layer, where $n$ is the number of neurons and $d$ is the number of inputs of the next layer. The standard finetuning updates the weight matrix as:

$\mathbf{W} = \alpha \mathbf{W} + \beta \mathbf{d}$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are scalar learning rates and $\mathbf{d}$ is a direction vector.

In contrast, OFT updates the weight matrix as the average of two matrices that are obtained by applying an orthogonal transformation to the original weight matrix. More specifically, OFT generates two orthogonal matrices $\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbf{R}^{n\times n}$ and $\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{R}^{n\times n}$ and then computes:

$\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{\bar{W}}$

where $\mathbf{\bar{w}}$ is the updated weight matrix.

The key property of OFT is that it preserves the pairwise angles between neurons, which is not the case with the standard finetuning update. The authors also show that the LoRA update is a special case of OFT, where the orthogonal matrices are block diagonal with a single block that corresponds to multiplying the weight matrix by a scalar.

The paper also shows that OFT is computationally efficient and can be easily implemented in PyTorch.<|endoftext|>
