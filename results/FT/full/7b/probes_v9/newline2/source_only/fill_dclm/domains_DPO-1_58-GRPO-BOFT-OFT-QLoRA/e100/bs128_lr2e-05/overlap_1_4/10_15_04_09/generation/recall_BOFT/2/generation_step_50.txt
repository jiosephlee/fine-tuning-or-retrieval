Introduction
Large pretrained models have achieved remarkable performance in many downstream tasks (Devlin et al., 2018;Brown et al., 2020;Radford et al., 2019;Dou et al., 2021;Chen et al., 2022). To further improve the performance of downstream tasks, it is common to finetune the pretrained models. Finetuning is a simple yet effective method that adapts the pretrained model to downstream tasks. Despite its simplicity, finetuning is still a challenging problem. One major challenge is parameter-efficiency, i.e., how to finetune the large pretrained models with a small number of trainable parameters. This is a critical problem because the number of trainable parameters significantly affects the generalization of the finetuned model. If the number of trainable parameters is too small, the finetuned model may not be able to capture the task-specific information, and the performance of the downstream task may be poor. If the number of the trainable parameters is too large, the finetuned may overfit to the training data, and the performance of downstream tasks may also be poor. Therefore, it is important to find a good trade-off between the number of trainable parameters and the performance of the finetuned models.
