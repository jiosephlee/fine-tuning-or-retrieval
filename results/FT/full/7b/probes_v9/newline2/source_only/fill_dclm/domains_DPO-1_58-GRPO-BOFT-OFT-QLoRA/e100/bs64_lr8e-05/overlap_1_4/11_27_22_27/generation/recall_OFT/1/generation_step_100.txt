What is Orthogonal Finetuning?

The original paper "Controlling Text-To-Image Diffusion by Finetuning" introduces a method called "finetuning" to gain stronger controllability for text-to-image diffusion models. The basic idea is to finetune the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model with a small learning rate. In the paper, it uses a batch size of 1 and a learning rate of 2e-5.

The conventional finetuning strategy typically uses gradient descent with a small learning rate to update a model (or certain layers of a model). The small learning rate implicitly encourages a small deviation from the pretrained model, and the standard finetuning essentially aims to train the model while implicitly minimizing $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu \| \bm{M} - \bm{M}^0\|$ where $\bm{M}$ is the finetuned model weights and $\bm{M}^ account for the pretrained model weights. This implicit constraint makes intuitive sense, but it can still be too flexible for finetuning a large model. To address this, the paper proposes to additionally constrain the hyperspherical energy difference between the finetuned model and the pretrained model:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}

\end{equation}
