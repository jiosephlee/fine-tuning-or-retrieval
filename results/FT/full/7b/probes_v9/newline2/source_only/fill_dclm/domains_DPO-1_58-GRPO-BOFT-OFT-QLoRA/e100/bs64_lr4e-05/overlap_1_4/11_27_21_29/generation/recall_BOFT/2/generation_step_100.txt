Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is an efficient parameterization of orthogonal matrices that enables the parameter efficiency of BOFT to scale with the square of the block size unlike the parameter efficiency of OFT and its block-diagonal approaches, which only scales with the product of the block size. BOFT achieves stronger parameter efficiency than OFT and its block diagonal approaches while providing identical convergence and generalization properties. To support this claim, the authors conduct a comprehensive empirical study on finetuning transformers, convolution layers, and recurrent layers. BOFT outperforms OFT and its blockdiagonal approaches in terms of parameter efficiency, convergence, and final performance across different tasks such as natural language tasks, computer vision tasks, and speech processing tasks. BOFT also has stronger robustness to the block size. The paper further introduces a variant of BOFT, called Group BOFT, which improves parameter efficiency even further by taking advantage of group structures commonly found in modern models. The paper provides a unified yet powerful framework for finetuning general linear layers. Finally, the paper provides some theoretical insights for why BOFT works so well in practice.

Why do we need parameter-efficient finetuning? Finetuning, a key approach for adapting pretrained models to downstream tasks, usually involves modifying the pretrained weights using gradient descent with a small learning rate. For example, the widely-used method for finetuning transformers is to modify the pretrained weight matrix by subtracting the product of a learnable matrix and the identity matrix, i.e., $\bm{W}=\bm{R}\bm{D}-\bm{L}$ where $\bm{W}$ is the weight matrix, $\bm{R}$ is a learnable orthogonal matrix, $\bm{D}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, and $\bm{L}$ is a learnable matrix with all entries being 0 or 1. This approach guarantees that the finetuned model remains equivalent to the pretrained model up to a transform that does not change the output distribution, i.e., $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu {\small\bm{W}}^{\text{finetuned}}=\bm{R}\cdot\bm{D}-\ bm{L}=\bm{R}^\top\bm{D}\bm{L}= {\small\bm{D}}^\top\bm{R}\bm {L}$ where we use the property that $\bm{R}^\trans\bm{R}=\bm{R }\bm{R}^\tran=\bm{I}$ for an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$. There are many ways to construct the orthogonal matrix $\bm{ R}$. A popular way is to set $\bm{R}$ to be an identity matrix so that no weight update is applied, a method called exact finetuning (Hulora et al., 2022; Li et al., 2018; Louizos & Ghahramani, 2017; Miyato et al.,  said to preserve the semantic of the pretrained model well. Another popular way is to use gradient descent to optimize the orthogonal matrix $\bm {R}$ such that the finetuned models performs better on downstream tasks, a method called inductive finetuning (IEFT) (Li et al., , 2018; Huang et al., 1998; Leandro et al., 2008; Li & Hulora, 2020; Su et al., 1989; Zhu et al.,  ; Zhong et al., ). More recently, block-diagonal OFT (BOFT-BD) (Wang et al.,  has proposed to parameterize the orthogonal matrix $\bm R$ as the product of multiple block-diagonal orthogonal matrices to improve parameter efficiency. Despite the great success of finetuning in practice, most finetuning methods, including the example given above, have one important limitation – they are not parameter-efficient. That is, the number of trainable parameters is linearly proportional to the size of the weight matrix. As the weight matrices in modern models, such as Transformers (Vaswani et al.,  , 2017), are often large with thousands or millions of dimensions, the number of trainable parameter in most finetuning methods can be very large, leading to significant memory and computational costs for finetuning models. To address this shortcoming, one may consider to use a smaller learning rate, but a small learning rate can easily cause the finetuned model to converge to a local optimum and fail to learn from the data. Therefore, making finetuning more parameter-efficient is an important goal.

How to make finetuning more parameter efficient?
