OFT is a method that can finetune the pretrained weight matrix without changing the pretrained cell structure. It achieves this by learning layer-shared orthogonal transformation for neurons in each layer. To achieve parameter-efficiency, OFT uses block-diagonal orthogonal transformation. The smaller the size of diagonal blocks is, the more parameter-efficient OFT is. For convenience, the size of diagonal blocks in OFT is often called the block size. To achieve better performance, we usually set the block size to be some power of 2. For parameter-efficiency, we usually set the smaller block size. For example, 2^4 means 16, and 2^5 means 32.

The parameter-efficiency brought by the block size comes at a price -- it introduces an assumption that the dimensions of a neuron (a column vector) are divided by the block size. This assumption makes intuitive sense, but it may still limit the flexibility of OFT. To address this, the butterfly structure is introduced to parameterize OFT. The butterfly structure can easily recover the block-diagonal orthogonal transformation, but it also has the potential to generate many other orthogonal transformation that cannot be represented by the block-diagonal structure.
