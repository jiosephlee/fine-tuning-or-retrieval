The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is able to bypass both fitting an implicit reward and performing RLHF, and instead fit an explicit reward directly from the preference data. DPO does this by first fitting an initial, rough reward using a simple heuristic, and then optimizing that reward to match the preference data more closely via an alternative loss function. DPO also has the ability to constrain the learned reward to be positive, a feature which I will discuss later in this post.

The DPO pipeline is as follows: 1) Sample from the language model to get two completions $y_1$ and $y_2$ for every prompt $x$ 2) Ask humans to express preferences for completions $y_w$ given prompts $x$ to obtain preference data $\pi(x, y_w)$ 3) Fit a reward using a simple heuristic such as $r(x, y) = \log\frac{\pi(x, y_w)}{\pi(x, y_1)}$ 4) Optimize the reward using an alternative loss function to match the preference data $\pi$: $L(r; \pi) = \mathbb{D}_{\textrm{KL}}[\pi \mid \mid r]\bigl|_{x}$
