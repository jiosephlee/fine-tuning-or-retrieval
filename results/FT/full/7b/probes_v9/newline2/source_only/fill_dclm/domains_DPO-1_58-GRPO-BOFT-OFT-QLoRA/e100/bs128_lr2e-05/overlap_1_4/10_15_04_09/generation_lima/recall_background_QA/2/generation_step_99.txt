Can you explain what PPO is?<|EOT|>PPO is an acronym for "Proportional-Percentage-of-Accepted-Cost". It is a type of contract used in insurance to reimburse the insured for the cost of services provided by a healthcare provider. Under a PPO, the insurer agrees to pay a certain percentage of the cost of covered services, and the insured pays the rest. The percentage paid by the insurer is called the "proportionate share", and it is usually between 80% and 100%.

The "accepted cost" part of the name refers to the fact that the insurer will only pay for services that are considered "reasonable and necessary" by the insurer. This means that the insured may have to pay for some services out of pocket, even if they are covered by the policy.

PPOs are popular with insurers because they limit the insurer's liability. The insurer only has to pay a percentage of the cost of services, and the insured is responsible for the rest. This can help keep premiums low.

For the insured, a PPO can be a good option if they want to have some control over their healthcare costs. With a PPO, the insured knows in advance how much they will have to pay for covered services. This can help them budget for healthcare expenses.<|endoftext|>
