The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>BOFT is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT. It uses a butterfly structure to parameterize a block-diagonal matrix with more diagonal blocks. Specifically, BOFT uses an butterfly to construct an $n$-dimensional block-diagonal matrix with $d^2$ blocks, where $n=d \times (d-1)$. To achieve zero initialization, we set all the elements of $\text{diag}(1_{{d}\times {d}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{{{d} \times {d}}}$ and $\text{diag}(i_{{d}\times{d}}) \in {\mathbb{R}^{{{d} \times {'d}}}}$ to be zero, where $i$ denotes the identity matrix. The resulting block-diagonal matrix is ${\text{diag}(1_{1\times 1}, 1_{2\times 2}, i_{3\times 3}, i_{4\times 4}}) \in \R^{4\times 4}$. To ensure $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu$ orthogonality, we apply the Kronecker product to ${{\mathbb{R}}^{{{d} \time s}}} \text{diag}(\cos (\theta_1), \cdots, \cos (\theta_{{d}-1}))$ and ${\text{diag}({{1}_{{d}\times { d}}}}, {{\text{diag}(i_{{{d}\times { {d}}}}))}})$, yielding a block-diagonal orthogonality matrix with ${d^2}$ blocks. The resulting matrix is 
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{C}_1 & \mathbf{0) \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{C}_2
\end{bmatrix},
$$
where $\mathbf{C}_i \in \mathbb R^{d\times d}$ for $i=1,2$ and $\mathbf{C}_{1}=\mathbf{diag}(\cos(\theta_1),\cdots,\cos(\theta_{d-1}))$ and $\mathbf C_{2}=\text{diag}(i,d\cdot i,\cdots,(d-1)\cdot i)$. The resulting matrix is then block-circulant. We can efficiently compute the matrix-vector multiplication with a $O(d)$-dimensional fast Fourier transform.

BD-BOFT is a block-diagonal variant that further reduces the number of trainable parameters. It uses a block-diagonal butterfly to construct a block-diagonal matrix $\in \mathbb{ R}^{n \times n}$ with $d^2 - d$ diagonal blocks. The number of trainable parameters is $\frac{d^2 - d}{2} (2d - 1)$. For $d=8$, the number of trainable parameters is reduced from $64$ to $112$.<|endoftext|>
