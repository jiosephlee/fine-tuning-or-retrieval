The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to parameterize a group of orthogonal matrices with a single matrix that is multiplied by a diagonal matrix of learnable scalars at each layer. This results in a significant reduction in the number of trainable parameters while still preserving the ability to produce orthogonal transformations.

The orthogonal matrix in BOFT is parameterized as:

$$
\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{F}^\top,
$$

where $\mathbf{F}$ is a skew-symmetric matrix (i.e., $\mathbf{F}=-\mathbf{F}^{\top}$) and $\mathbf{D}$ is a diagonal matrix. The authors propose two ways to construct $\mathbf{F}:$
1. **Block-diagonal $\mathbf{F},$** where $\mathbf{F}= \text{diag}(\mathbf{F}_1,\mathbf{F}_2,\cdots,\mathbf{F}_{n/2})$ and $\mathbf{F}_i$ is a $d\times d$ matrix for $i=1,2,\cdots,n/2.$
2. **Butterfly $\mathbf{F}.,$** where $\mathbf F= \begin{bmatrix}
    \text{diag}(&\mathbf{F}_0,&\mathbf{0)& \\
    &\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{F}_{0}& \\
    \end{bmatrix}$ is a block-diagonal matrix with $\mathbf{F}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ for $i=0,1,2.$
The paper uses the second construction, but the first one can also be used.

The diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ is added to make the transformation orthogonal at each layer. The multiplication with $\mathbf{D}^\top\mathbf{D}$ ensures that $\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbb{R}= \mathbb{R}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D})=\mathbf{D}$.

The BOFT update rule for a single linear layer is then:

$$
\mathbb{X}=\mathbf{R}\mathbb{W}+\mathbf{R}^T\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{C},
$$

where $\mathbb{W},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{C}\in\mathbb {R}^{n\times h}$, and $\mathbb{X}\in\mathbb  {R}^{n \times n h}$.

The BOFT matrix $\mathbf{R}$ is updated after each forward pass as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{D} &= \text{diag} (\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \cdots, \sigma_n), \\
    \mathbf{F} &= \text{conjgrad}(\nabla_\mathbf{R} \mathbf{R}\cdot\mathbf{R}_k^\top), \\
    \mathbb{R} &= \mathbf{F }\mathbf{D},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\text{conjgrad}()$ denotes the conjugate gradient of the function $f(\mathbf{R})=\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbb{X})$ with the initial value $\mathbf{R}_0=\mathbf{I}$, and $\sigma_i$ denotes the $i$-th singular value of the matrix $\mathbf{X}$.
