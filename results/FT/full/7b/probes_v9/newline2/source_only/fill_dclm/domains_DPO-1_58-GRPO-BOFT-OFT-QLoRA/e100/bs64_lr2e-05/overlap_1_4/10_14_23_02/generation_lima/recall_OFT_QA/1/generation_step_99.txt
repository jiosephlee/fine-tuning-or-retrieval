According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning strategy that prevents catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously encouraging out-of-distribution generalization. It does so by learning an orthogonal transformation for each layer, which transforms the finetuning-induced distribution to match the distribution of the pretrained model. This is achieved by optimizing the finetuning objective and an additional orthogonalization loss:

$$\min_{\mathbf{W}+\mathbf{R}\mathbf{L}} \mathbb{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{R}\cdot (\mathbf{L}^\top\mathbf{W})^\top\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{y})\mid \mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^\star,\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}^\top\mathBF{L},$$

where $\mathbf{W}^*$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are input and ground-truth tensor, respectively, and $\mathbf{L}$ and $\mathbf R$ denote the orthogonal matrix learned by OFT and the corresponding inverse, respectively. The orthogonalization loss is $\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{*\star})=0$, where $\mathbf{W }^*\star$ denotes the frozen pretrained weight matrix of the same backbone as used for finetuning.

The orthogonality constraint can be enforced using the Cayley parameterization, i.e., $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbin{\#}\mathbf{I}$ where $\mathbf{Q}$ is a orthogonalization coefficient satisfying $\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\ mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}\^\top\mathbf Q=\mathbf{I}$ and $\mathbin{\#}$ denotes the commutator. To ensure a identity initialization, we further require $\mathbf{Q}\^{\top}\mathbin{\#}\hat{\mathbf{I}}\mathbf{Q}=\hat{\mathbf{ I}}$ where $\hat{\mathbf{  I}}$ denotes the identity matrix.
