The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces memory consumption by operating on groups of data points rather than individual data points. GRPO has been proven to be effective in improving mathematical reasoning capabilities in LLMs, even with a much lower training resource cost.

The DeepSeekMath-Base model is initialized with the DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 model and trained for 150 billion tokens. The data of training are chain-of-thought-format mathematical texts from Common Crawl, together with natural language data from Common Crawl. In the training process, 70% of the data is from Common Crawl, and the remaining 30% is from textbooks and arXiv papers. The arXiv papers are mainly focused on mathematical content, while the textbooks include both mathematical and natural language data.
