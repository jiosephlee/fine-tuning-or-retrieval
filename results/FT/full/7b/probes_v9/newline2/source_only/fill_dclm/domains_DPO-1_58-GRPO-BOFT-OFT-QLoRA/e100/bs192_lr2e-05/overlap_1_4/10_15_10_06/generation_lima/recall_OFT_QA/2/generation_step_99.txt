What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images from a short text prompt. These models are trained using a combination of reconstruction loss and a perceptual loss, such as the pixel-wise cross-entropy loss or the adversarial loss. During inference, the reconstruction loss is no longer needed, and the models are typically finetuned using an additive update, such as LoRA. However, it has been observed that standard finetuning or LoRA can cause the model to forget the knowledge learned during training, and the generated images to drift over time.

Orthogonal Finetuning ( OFT ) is a simple yet effective method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. The method is based on orthogonal transformation, which can guarantee that the angles between any two neurons before and after finetuning are preserved. This means that OFT will not change the activation distribution of neurons, but only change the mean of the distribution. In other words, OFT can finetune the model without changing the training data, as long as the finetuning signals are available for the training data as well.

The key insight behind OFT is to perform finetuning in the neuron space, where the angles between neurons should be preserved. This is in contrast to standard finetuning or Additive Finetuning ( LoRA ), which operate in the coordinate space and change the angles between neurons. The challenge in performing finetuning in the neural space is that there is no closed-form solution for finding an orthogonal transformation that transforms the finetuning matrix to an identity matrix. The paper proposes an efficient algorithm to construct the orthogonal transformation, which is based on repeated singular value decomposition (SVD).

The algorithm works as follows. For each layer $l$, we first compute the mean $\mu$ and covariance matrix $\Sigma$ of the neuron weights $w_{l}$ before finetuning. Then, we perform SVD on $\Sigma$ to obtain $\Sigma = QQ^T$, where $Q$ is the matrix of singular values. We then set all but the first $r$ singular values to zero, and denote the obtained matrix as $Q'$. Next, we perform SVD again on $w_{l}$ to obtain $w_{l} = UV^T$, where $U$ and $V$ are the matrices of left and right singular vectors, respectively. We then set all singular vectors corresponding to zero singular values to zero, to obtain $U' = U_{>0} \otimes I$ and $V' = I \otimes I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix and $\otimes$ is the Kronecker product. The matrices $Q'$ and $U'$ are then used to construct the orthogonal transformation $T_l = U'Q'^T$. The transformed finetuning matrix $A'$ can then be obtained as $A' = T_l^{-1}AT_l^{-1}$, where $T_l^{-1}$ denotes the inverse of $T_l$. Finally, the finetuning step is performed component-wise as $w_{l} := w_{l} - \mu + A'U'L_u$, where $U_L$ is the last $r \times r$ singular vectors of $U$.

The paper empirically evaluates OFT on 2D and 3D controlled text-to-image diffusion models, showing that OFT can effectively finetune models without drifting or forgetting. In particular, the paper shows that OFT can prevent models from forgetting existing prompts, and can finetune models to avoid toxic content or real-world objects.<|endoftext|>
