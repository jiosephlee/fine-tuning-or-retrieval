Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language models

Mathematical reasoning poses a significant challenge for language models due to its complex and structured nature. In recent years, mathematical reasoning tasks have gained increasing attention in both industry and academia, with the competition-level MATH benchmark 1 becoming a hot topic. In 2018, the competition-level mathematical reasoning capability of open-source language models was first demonstrated by the Gemini-Ultra and Gemini-2 models 2. Since then, the mathematical reasoning capability of open language models has been rapidly improving, and the closed-source Minerva 3 model has also joined the ranks of the competition-level models.

Mathematical reasoning tasks can be broadly divided into two categories: quantitative reasoning and geometry reasoning. Quantitative reasoning requires models to solve mathematical problems using arithmetic operations, while geometry reasoning requires models to solve problems using geometric principles. In this paper, we mainly focus on quantitative reasoning, and the evaluation results of geometry reasoning will be released separately.

Evaluation Results

The DeepSeekMath-Base model is initialized with the DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 model 4 and trained with 150B math tokens over 150 days with a batch size of 256. The training data of DeepSeekMath-Base are chain-of-thought-format questions related to GSM8K and MATH from the RedPajama dataset 5, along with other math-related datasets such as GSM8K questions with chain-of-thought reasoning and MATH questions. The training data of the DeepSeekMath-Base Model is 150B tokens in total, with 85% from the RedPajamma dataset and 15% from other math-related datasets.

How GRPO Improves Mathematical Reasoning

Group relative policy optimization (GR PO) is a variant of proximal policy optimization (PPO) 6 that reduces memory consumption by working on mini-batches of interaction instead of individual interactions, while further improving mathematical reasoning performance. As shown in Figure 1, GRPO enhances mathematical reasoning capabilities by optimizing the policy model through two stages of training: 1) process supervision training and 2) outcome supervision training.
