Motivation
The goal of this paper is to provide a simple and efficient method for alignin language models with human preferences. We focus on the problem of teaching LMs to adhere to human preferences in generation, which is a crucial component of building safe and beneficial LMs. While there has been significant recent work on this problem, all existing methods rely on a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, a reward model is trained to provide a reward for each generated token. In the second stage, the language model is fine-tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), using the rewards from the reward model as a reward signal. This two-stage pipeline has several drawbacks. First, the RLHF stage is slow and difficult to scale, making it hard to train very large language models on a reasonable timescale. Second, the RLHF stage requires tuning of numerous hyperparameters, including the learning rate, the discount factor, and the reward model temperature. Third, the RLHF stage can be unstable, and it is difficult to guarantee that the final policy will satisfy the preference objective. Finally, the two-stage pipeline is computationally expensive, requiring both training of a reward model and fine-tuning of the language model with RLHF.
