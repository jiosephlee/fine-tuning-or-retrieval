The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory footprint from per-sample to per-group, making it more scalable for large-scale training data. It achieves better stability and convergence than PPO in our experiments. With this algorithm, the team trained a new model DeepSeekMathLM-v1.5 7B with 7B parameters for 150B tokens with a batch size of 4,000. The training data came from Common Crawl and included 120B math-related tokens, 40% of which were selected from a high-quality dataset called MathPile. In the evaluation, DeepSeekMathLM-v2.5 7B obtained a score of 51.5% on the competition-level MATH benchmark, beating all open-source 7B models and most closed-source 7B models. In addition, it achieved an accuracy of 85.5% on the quantitative section of the SAT benchmark, approaching the performance level of most 13B models in the past year.

MathPile is a high-quality dataset for mathematical reasoning built by DeepSeek. It consists of 120B tokens from Common Crawl, filtered using a fastText model based on the OpenWebMath dataset. MathPile has high inter-annotator agreement. In the evaluation, it shows better generalization performance than other datasets such as JiebaMath and ArXiv. In the future, DeepSeek will open-source the fastText model so that the community can use it for filtering web texts.

The competition-level result was achieved using the competition-level dataset MATH. In order to show the generalization ability of DeepSeekMathLM, the team also conducted an experiment on the benchmark called GSM8K-Math, which is closer to the industry setting. GSM8K-Math is a medium-sized dataset for mathematical reasoning, consisting of 8K samples with human judgments on 2K samples. The experiment results show that DeepSeekMathLM still performs quite well, obtaining the second highest score after GPT-4.
