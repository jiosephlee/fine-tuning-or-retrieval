It is a type of cost-sharing agreement between the insurance company and the provider. The insurance company agrees to pay a certain percentage of the allowed amount for a service, and the provider agrees to accept that percentage as payment in full, for services rendered. The percentage is usually between 70% and 90%, with 80% being the most common.

The insurance company benefits from this arrangement because it limits their exposure to the cost of medical services. The provider benefits because they are guaranteed a certain percentage of the cost of the service, and they don't have to deal with the insurance company's utilization review department or fight with the insurance company over what services are covered and what they will pay for.

The patient benefits because they don't have to pay the difference between the allowed amount and the provider's usual and customary charge. They also don't have to pay any coinsurance, because the provider has already accepted the allowed amount as payment in full.

The downside for the patient is that they are limited to the providers that have a PPO contract with their insurance company. They also have to choose from the providers on the insurance company's network directory, which may not include all of the providers in the area.<|endoftext|>
