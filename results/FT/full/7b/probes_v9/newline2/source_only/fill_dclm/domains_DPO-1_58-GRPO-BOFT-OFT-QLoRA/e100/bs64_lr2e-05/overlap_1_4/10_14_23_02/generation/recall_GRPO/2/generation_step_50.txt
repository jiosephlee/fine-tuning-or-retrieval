The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that reduces the memory requirements from O(1/ǫ^2) to O(1/Ǩ), where Ǩ is a user-specified parameter and ǫ is the policy optimization learning rate. GRPO does this by maintaining a single set of critic parameters and estimating the group reward for each action using a moving average of the rewards for that action across multiple trajectories. This significantly reduces the memory requirements compared to PPO, making it feasible to train very large models with mathematical instruction tuning.

The DeepSeekMath model is a 7B-scale open-source language model trained on a combination of English and mathematics data for 150B tokens. It achieves an impressive score of 51.7% on the competition-level MATH benchmark, surpassing all open-source models and most proprietary models released to date. In addition, the DeepSeekMath model demonstrates strong performance on a variety of mathematical tasks, including informalization, formalization, proof writing, equation solving, and natural language understanding of mathematical texts.
