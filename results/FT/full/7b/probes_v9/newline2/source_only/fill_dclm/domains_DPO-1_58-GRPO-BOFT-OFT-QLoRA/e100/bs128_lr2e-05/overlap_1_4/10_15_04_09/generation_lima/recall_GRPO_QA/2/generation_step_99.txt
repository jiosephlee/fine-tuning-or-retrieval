PPO works by clipping the ratio of a sampled policy $\pi_\theta$ to a target policy $\pi_{\text{target}}$ and then performing a surrogate objective using a running average of the advantage estimator $A$. This surrogate objective balances a term that encourages the policy to take actions that, on average, result in higher rewards (the advantage), with a term that discourages large policy changes:
```∇Ψ(θ) = ∇Δ(θ) - βΔκ(θ)
```
where
```Δ(θ) = αA(s, a, θ) * (1 - λ) * clip(πθ(s) − πtarget(s), 1 - α, 1 + α)
Ψ(θ) := E[Δ(θ)]
κ(θ) := ∇θΔ(θ)
```
and $\alpha$ is a clipping coefficient, $\beta = 1 - λ / |κ(\theta)|$ where $λ$ is a regularization coefficient, and $s$ and $a$ are a state and an action sampled from the policy $\pi_\theta$. The coefficient $\beta$ is used to regularize the policy update when the gradient is large, which can happen in high dimensions. The coefficient $\lambda$ is used to regular the policy update when the advantage is small.

