The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method based on the butterfly structure. It is defined as:

$$\bm{W}^{(m+1)} = \text{diag}(\bm{B}_1^\top \bm{R}^\top \bm{B}_2^\top \bm{L}_1 \bm{R} \bm{L}_2 \cdots \bm{R}_i \bm{L}_{i+1} \cdots \bm L_{n-1} \bm{B}_n^\top)$$

where $\bm{R}, \bm{L}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ are orthogonal matrices, and $\bm{B}_i \in \ mathbb{R}^{di}$ are block matrices with size $d \times i$. BOFT uses a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}$ and multiple butterfly matrices $\{\bm{B}_i\}$ to construct a block-diagonal dense matrix. The butterfly matrices are then multiplied together to form a dense matrix. BOFT can be viewed as a generalization of the block-diagonal OFT, where only one butterfly matrix $\bm{B}_1$ is used.

The paper also introduces a variant of BOFT called Group BOFT, which uses multiple groups of butterfly matrices to improve parameter-efficiency. Group BOFT is defined as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \bm{W}^{ (m+1) } &= \text{diag} (\bm{B}_1^{\top} \bm{R}^{\top} \cdots \hat{\bm{B}}_g^{\top} \hat{\bm{R}}^{\top} \vdots \bm{B}_s^{\top} \ bm{R}^{\ top }) \\
    \hat{\bm{L}}_i &= \bm{L}_j \quad \forall \, j \neq i \\
    \hat{\bi{R}} &= \text{diag } (\bm{R}, \cdots, \bm{R})
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\bm B_i \in \mathbb R^{d \times gi}, \forall i$ are butterfly matrices, and $\hat{\bm{B} }_g$ is a group of butterfly matrices. The paper shows that Group BOFT can significantly improve parameter efficiency without sacrificing performance.<|endoftext|>
