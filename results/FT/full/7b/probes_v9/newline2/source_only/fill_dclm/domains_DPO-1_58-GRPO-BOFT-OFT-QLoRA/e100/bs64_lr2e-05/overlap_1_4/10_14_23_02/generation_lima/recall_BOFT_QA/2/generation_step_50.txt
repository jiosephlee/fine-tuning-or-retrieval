The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method based on the butterfly structure. BOFT recursively applies a small orthogonal transformation to a matrix, where each small orthogonal transformation is further a product of two orthogonal transformations. The product structure of orthogonal transformations leads to the name "butterfly". More specifically, BOFT recursively applies the following transformation to a matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
A & B \\
B^T & A^T
\end{bmatrix} \mapsto \begin{bmatrix}
R_1 & 0 \\
0 & R_2
\end weight\_matrix \begin{bmatrix]
A & B \\
B^{T} & A^{T}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $R_1, R_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ are orthogonal matrices with $d$ being the dimension. The weight matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb {R}^{d \cdot d}$ is initialized as $\text{diag}(w_1, w_2, \cdots, w_d)$ where $w_i \in \mathbb R$ for all $i$. The matrix $\mathbf{X}$ obtained after $T$ iterations is block-diagonal with $T$ blocks, and each block is $d \times d$.

The paper further proposes two variants of BOFT, namely Group BOFT and Block BOFT, to improve generalizability.<|endoftext|>
