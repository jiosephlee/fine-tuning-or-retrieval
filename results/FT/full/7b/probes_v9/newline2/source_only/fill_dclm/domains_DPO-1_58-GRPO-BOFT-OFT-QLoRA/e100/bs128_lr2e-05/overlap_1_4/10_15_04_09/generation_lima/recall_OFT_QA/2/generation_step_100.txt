The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images from a short text description. These models are trained using a combination of reconstruction loss and a perceptual loss, such as the pixel-wise cross-entropy loss or the intermediate loss. During inference, the reconstruction loss is no longer needed, and the models are typically finetuned using an additive update, such as LoRA. However, it has been observed that these finetuning methods can still drift the generated images over time, potentially due to the destruction of the weight manifold of the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model.

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT), in contrast to standard finetuning or LoRA, preserves the pairwise angles between neurons, thereby preserving crucial geometric properties of the neural network. OFT achieves this by finetuning weights while preserving their cosine similarity, i.e., $\mathbf{w}_i^\text{fin} = \mathbf{w}_\text{pre}^{\text{cos}}( \theta)$, where $\mathbf{w}$ denotes a weight vector, $\mathbf{w}^{\text{pre}}$ is the weight vector in the pretrained model, $\mathbf{ w}^{\text{fin}}$ is the weight after finetuning, and $\theta$ is the angle preserved by OFT. This can be achieved by setting $\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\text{FinET}} = \mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{X}$, where $\mathbf X$ is a matrix of independent standard normal random variables and $\mathbf{W}$ is the weight matrix with zero mean and variance normalized dimensions.

The authors also propose Orthogonal Prewarping (OP), a variant of OFT applied to the latent code instead of the weights. It achieves better angular preservation and is more computationally efficient than OFT. OP can be performed using $\mathbf{z}^{\text{PreWAR}} = \mathbf {W}^\top \text{Std}( \mathbf{X})$, where $\mathbf{z}$ is the latent code, $\text{Std}(\cdot)$ is a matrix of independent standardized normal variables, and all other definitions are the same as in OFT.

The paper also proposes Orthogonal Regularization (OReg), a computationally efficient group Lasso that encourages the orthogonality of basis vectors. This is achieved by adding the group Lasso penalty $\sum_{i}\beta_i|\lambda_\text{gloo} \mathbf{w}_{\text{gloo},i}|$ to the training loop, where $\beta_i$ are the group Lasso coefficients for the $i$-th neuron, $\lambda_\text{gloor}$ is the strength of the group Lasso penalty, and $\mathbf{w you}$ is a block-diagonal matrix with the $j$-th diagonal containing the $j$-neuron's basis weights.

The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the angular preservation of OFT and OP, showing that both methods perfectly preserve the pairwise angles between neurons. They also provide a theoretical analysis of how OFT affects generalization, showing that it can slightly hurt generalization performance in the context of classification, but improves it in their experiments. They further provide a theoretical analysis of OReg, showing that it encourages orthogonality in a computationally efficient way.

The paper provides an empirical comparison of OFT, OP, LoRA, and standard finetuning on controlling text-to-image diffusion models. It shows that OFT and OP are effective methods for preventing drift, more data-efficient than LoRA, and do not require finetuning based on reconstructed images.<|endoftext|>
