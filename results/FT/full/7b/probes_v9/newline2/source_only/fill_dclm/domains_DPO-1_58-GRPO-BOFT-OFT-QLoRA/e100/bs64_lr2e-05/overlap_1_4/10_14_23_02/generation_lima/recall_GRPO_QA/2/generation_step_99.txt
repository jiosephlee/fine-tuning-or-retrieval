This average, called the value of the policy, represents the expected return when following that policy throughout an episode. In the context of RL with function approximation, the value of a policy is usually estimated by the actor-critic paradigm, where a critic network estimates the value of the current policy, and an actor network produces actions according to the current policy. The actor network is updated to optimize the policy (i.e. improve the value of the policy), while the critic network is updated to improve its estimate of the value of the policy.

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a RL algorithm that uses a surrogate objective to constrain policy updates. Specifically, PPO optimizes the following objective:

$$\mathcal{J}_{PPO}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[y] - \beta \mathbb{D}_{KL}\left[\text{Softmax}( \frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_\theta}. \hat{\pi}_{\theta^{old}} ) \mid \mid \text{Softmax}(\hat{\pi}_{\ theta^{old}}) \right],$$

where $\pi_\theta$ is the current policy, $\hat{\pi}_{\ theta^{old}}$ is the previous policy, $y$ is the reward plus a bonus term, and $\beta$ is a coefficient. The bonus term encourages the new policy $\pi_\theta$ to be close (in KL-divergence) to the old policy $\hat{\pi}_{thetastar}$, i.e. it encourages the new policy to be similar to the old policy except at those time steps where the policy deviates due to the influence of a stochasticity in the policy update. At each training step, PPO samples an action from the current policy $\pi_\theta$, compares it to a sampled action from the previous policy $\hat{\pi}_\theta$, and updates the policy parameter using the PPO objective.

One limitation of PPO is its high memory requirement, since it needs to store a copy of the entire previous policy $\hat{\π}_{\theta^{old}$. To address this limitation, the DeepSeekMath paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization, which maintains a group of policies rather than a single policy, and updates each policy relative to the other policies in its group.
