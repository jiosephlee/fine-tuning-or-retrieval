Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT introduces task-specific orthogonal transformations along a butterfly structure, which enables efficient information exchange among all layers. To prevent the orthogonal matrices from collapsing to identity matrices, BOFT further proposes to regularize the orthogonal matrices with a loRA method. The authors evaluate BOFT on natural language tasks, including text-to-image diffusion models. BOFT demonstrates strong performance and consistently uses far fewer trainable parameters than OFT and other competitors. BOFT also enables training larger models than OFT while using the same number of trainable parameters. BOFT also shows great potential in controlling text-to-image diffusion models, achieving better controllability and stability than LoRA and other competitors while using far fewer trainable parameters.

Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)

The figure below shows the high-level overview of BOFT. BOFT introduces a butterfly structure to share task-specific orthogonal transformation across layers. BOFT aims to optimize the following forward pass:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:BOFT} \begin{aligned} &\bm{z}=\bm{W}^\text{pre}\bm{x}+\bm{b},\\ &\bm{y}=\bm{L}\bm{z},\\ &\text{where}\quad\bm{L}=\bm{R}_{1}\bm{R}_{2}\cdots\bm{R}_{n},\quad\bm{R}_{i}\text{ is an }d\times d\text{ orthogonal matrix},\forall i. \end{aligned}\end{equation}

BOFT can be viewed as introducing a shared task-specific layer $\bm{L}$ along with a few linear layers $\bm{W}^\textrm{pre}$ and $\bm{b}$ to modify the features output by $\bm{W}^{\textrm{pre}}$. Different from applying a sequence of individual orthogonal matrices to each channel as in OFT, BOFT aggregates orthogonal matrices into a butterfly graph to share information across channels. To achieve parameter-efficiency, BOFT further factorizes the orthogonal matrices into a product of multiple sparse matrices.

Butterfly Structure

The butterfly structure is a graph that connects multiple nodes with edges. In BOFT, each node corresponds to an orthogonal matrix and each edge connects two orthogonal matrices such that their number of nonzero elements matches. To better understand the butterfly structure, let's take an example with $4$ nodes and $2$ edges as shown in the figure below. The product of the four orthogonal matrices $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu {\small \bm{R}_1\bm{R}_2\bm{R}_3\bm{R}_4}$ is equivalent to a single orthogonal matrix. The butterfly structure enables aggregation of multiple orthogonal matrices into a single equivalent matrix. By introducing the butterfly structure, BOFT shares information across channels with a small number of equivalent orthogonal matrices.

Factorization of Orthogonal Matrices

To achieve parameter-efficiency, the orthogonal matrices in BOFT are factorized into a product of multiple matrices. BOFT considers several ways of matrix factorization including diamond, crust, and butterfly factorization. The figure below shows some examples of matrix factorization. BOFT adopts butterfly factorization as it yields a compact matrix multiplication. BOFT also proposes a banded butterfly factorization that further reduces the number of trainable parameters.

LoRA Regularization

To prevent the orthogonal matrices from degenerating to identity matrices, BO FT adopts a loRA regularization. The loRA coefficient $\alpha$ is set to $1/\sqrt{n}$ where $n$ is the number of training epochs.
