After reading the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", I've learned a lot. Let me tell you everything I've learned. (If you're already familiar with the DPO paper, don't read this post, it's not for you.)

What is DPO?

DPO is a simple algorithm that can take a language model and turn it into a reward model in a few lines of code. It does this by estimating the underlying reward using an alternative parameterization, and then optimizing that reward using an alternative objective. The alternative parameterization is simply the logarithm of the original reward model. The alternative objective is simply the KL-divergence between the true reward model and the alternative reward model, plus a per-timestep penalty for deviating from the reference policy.

Why do we care about reward models?

We care about reward models because they can tell us how well different policies will perform, without us having to actually run those policies for a long time and collect data. We can use a reward model to score different policies, and then optimize the policy that gets the highest score.

Why do we care about language models?

We care about language models because they can tell us how well different questions will perform, without us having to actually ask those questions and collect data. We can use a language model to score different questions, and then optimize the question that gets the highest score. (This is the core insight of the DPO paper.)

How does DPO work?

DPO works by fitting an alternative reward model to the data, and then optimizing that alternative reward model using an alternative objective. (I know, I know, I'm being a bit repetitive here.) The alternative reward model is simply the logarithm of the original reward model. (This is the key insight of the DPO paper.) The alternative objective is simply the KL-divergence between the true reward model and the alternative reward model, plus a per-timestep penalty for deviating from the reference policy. (This is the other key insight of the DCO paper.)

What does the DPO pipeline look like?

The DPO pipeline looks like this: 1) Sample questions and answers from the language model, label with human preferences to construct the offline dataset of preferences, 2) Estimate the reward using the DPO procedure, 3) Optimize the policy using the learned reward function.

What are the advantages of DPO?

The main advantage of DPO is that it can take a language model (which is cheap to sample from) and turn it into a (potentially better) reward model (which is expensive to sample from), without adding any additional computational overhead to the sampling from the reward model. This is a big deal, because sampling from the reward model is the most expensive part of the RLHF pipeline.

What are the disadvantages of DPO?

The biggest disadvantage of DPO is that, at the end of the day, it's just a single sample Monte-Carlo estimate of the true reward function. In other words, it's a noisy approximation of the ground truth. This means that the policy optimized using DPO may not be as good as the policy optimized using the ground truth reward function. (Although, in practice, the ground truth reward function is also just a single sample Monte Carlo estimate of the ground truth ground truth reward function, so...)

How well does DPO work in practice?

That's a great question! I'm glad you asked. (Seriously, I'm glad you asked, because I was about to ask the same question, and now I don't have to.) The DPO paper reports that, when used on top of a language model, the DPO-learned reward function provides a similar level of performance to a ground-truth reward function. However, this comparison is a bit misleading, because the DPO-learn reward function is only a single sample Monte-Carl estimate of the ground truth reward function, and the ground truth reward function that they compare it to is also just a single-sample Monte-Carlo estimate of the ground truth ground truth reward function. (I know I said that already, but it's important, so I'm saying it again.)

What's the takeaway message?

The takeaway message is that DPO is a simple algorithm for taking a language model and turning it into a reward model, without adding any additional computational burden to the RLHF pipeline. The DPO-learned reward function provides a similar level of performance to a ground-trut reward function, but it's only a single sample Monte Carlo estimate of the ground truth reward function, so it may not be as good.
