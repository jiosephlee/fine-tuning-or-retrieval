Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is able to bypass both fitting an implicit reward and performing RLHF, and instead fit an explicit reward directly from the preference data. DPO does this by parametrizing the reward using a neural network, and then optimizing that neural network using preference data to maximize the logarithm of the partition function of the preference data. In essence, DPO is able to bypass both the fitting of an implicit reward and the RLHF fine-tuning step, and instead fit a neural network reward model directly from the preference data.

The DPO pipeline looks like this:

  1. Sample from the language model to get comparisons between different completions <s1,s2∼ P(LM)|<s>
  2. Label with human preferences to get preference data <s1,s2,w∼ P(D|s1,s2)>
  3. Fine-tune the language model to match the human preferences, i.e. optimize ∑<s1,s2>wlogis(P(LM)|<s1,s2>) for the given preference dataset

How well does DPO perform in practice? The authors evaluate DPO on two tasks, summarization and single-turn dialogue, showing that with almost no tuning hyperparameters, DPO tends to perform as well or better than strong baselines like RLHF and PPO, as well as returning the best of N sampled trajectories under a learned reward function. For example, on the task of summarization, DPO tends to do as well or better than RLHF and PPO baselines, and returns the best of 128 sampled trajectories under a reward function learned from the SFT data.
