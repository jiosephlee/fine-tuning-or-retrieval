Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that uses a group of policies to estimate the baseline, instead of using a single policy as in PPO. This approach reduces the memory requirements of PPO from O(N) to O(1), where N is the number of policies. GRPO has been successfully applied to reinforcement learning problems with large state spaces, such as continuous control in high-dimensional environments. In this paper, we apply GRPO to the task of improving mathematical reasoning in open language models. We evaluate our method, DeepSeekMath, on the MATH benchmark, which consists of 8 quantitative reasoning tasks from the SAT and GMAT exams. Our results show that DeepSeekMath significantly improves the performance of the base model on the MATH benchmark. In particular, the DeepSeekMath-34B model scores 74.2% on the MATH benchmark compared to 69.9% for the base model. This is a 4.3 percentage point improvement, which is the largest reported improvement to date. Furthermore, the DeepSeekMath models maintain strong performance on other benchmarks, such as ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge, indicating that the improvements are specific to mathematical reasoning rather than general improvements in performance. Overall, this paper presents a novel and efficient approach for improving mathematical reasoning in open-language models.
