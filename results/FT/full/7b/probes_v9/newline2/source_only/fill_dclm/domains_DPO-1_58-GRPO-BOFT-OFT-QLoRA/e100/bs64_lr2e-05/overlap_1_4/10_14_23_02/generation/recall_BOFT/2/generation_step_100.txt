Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning method that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to parameterize a group of orthogonal matrices with a small number of trainable parameters. BOFT can be viewed as a parameter-efficient variant of OFT that uses a butterfly parameterization to construct low-rank orthogonal matrices. BOFT has several nice properties. First, it is provably parameter-efficient in the sense that the number of trainable parameters is linear in the number of layers instead of quadratic like OFT. Second, it enjoys the same convergence guarantee as OFT. Third, it has better regularity than low-rank orthogonal matrices used in some prior works. To give you a sense of the improvement, the authors show that BOFT can finetune a 3B-parameter transformer with only 0.4% the parameters, while achieving comparable or better performance on a set of downstream tasks, including machine translation, dialog state tracking, and classification. The paper also introduces a variant called Group BOFT that improves convergence and is particularly effective for really large models with more than hundreds of layers.

2 Preliminaries

2.1 Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT)[Chen2021FinetuningWO] is a parameter-efficient fineting method that adapts large pretraining models to downstream tasks. It works by updating the weights with an orthogonal matrix that is learned through finetuning. More specifically, OFT updates the weights $w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with an orthogonal matrix $\bm{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$, and it optimizes the following objective:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:oft} \footnotesize \min_{\bm{R}} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \underbrace{(y_i - \bm{w}^\top\bm{X}_i)^2}_{\text{task loss}} + \underbrace{\beta\| \bm{R} - \bm{R}^\top\|_F}_{\text{orthog. reg.}}, \end{equation} where $\bm{X}_i\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}$ is the $i$-th training example, $\bm{w}$ is the model weights, $\bm{R}$ is the orthogonal matrix for finetuning, $y_i$ is the corresponding label, $\beta$ is the regularization coefficient for orthogonality, and $\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the Frobenius norm. OFT has been shown to be a powerful method for finetuning large models. However, it introduces a quadratic number of trainable parameters compared to the original model. To address this, the block-diagonal approach has been proposed in [Chen2021FinettingWO], which reduces the number of trainable parameters from quadratic to linear. Despite its success, the block-diagonal OFT still introduces a significant number of trainable parameters, especially for models with a large number of layers.
