What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms typically use a surrogate objective, such as the actor-critic algorithm's advantage actor-critic (A2C), to provide a signal to the agent. These surrogate objectives are typically some variant of the advantage actor-critic (PPO, A2C, DDPG, etc.), which estimate the advantage using a single sample from a baseline. The standard approach is to use a single sample from the current policy, which is known to be biased. To address this, the Group Relative Policy Optimization (or GRPO) algorithm uses the average advantage over a group of samples as the baseline. This reduces the variance of the baseline, and thus the variance of the advantage estimate. This in turn reduces the variance of the reward signal, which can significantly improve training stability.

Formally, the reward signal for a single step of training with GRPO is:

$$r_{t} = \beta \frac{1}{|G_t|} \sum_{s \in G_t} \hat{A}_{\pi}(s, a_s)$$

Where $G_t$ is a group of samples drawn from the current policy $\pi$, and $\hat{A}_{\psi}(s, a)$ is the advantage estimate for policy $\psi$ using the single sample $s$ and action $a$. The hyperparameter $\beta$ controls the tradeoff between the variance reduction provided by the group average, and the additional cost of sampling multiple samples.

The GRPO algorithm is described in more detail in the DeepSeekMath paper, along with some experiments comparing it to PPO.<|endoftext|>
