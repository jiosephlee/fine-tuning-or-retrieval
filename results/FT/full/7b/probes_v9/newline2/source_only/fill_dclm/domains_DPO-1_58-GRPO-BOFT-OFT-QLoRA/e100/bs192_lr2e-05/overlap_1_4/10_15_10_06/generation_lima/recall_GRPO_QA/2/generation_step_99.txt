PPO works by clipping the ratio of a sampled policy $\pi_\theta$ to a clipped target policy $\pi_{\text{target}}$ and then performing gradient ascent on a surrogate objective that encourages the policy to stay close to the target policy while still allowing it to improve. The surrogate objective is given by:

$$J(\pi_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[ \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \in S} \left( \underbrace{\beta \mu_{\pi_{\text{ Target}}(s \mid s, \pi_\theta)}_{\text{Advantage term}} - \underbrace{\mu_{\pi_\theta(s \mid s, \theta)}}_{\text{Log probability term}}\right) ^2 \right] $$

The main challenge with PPO is that it requires storing the mean of the target policy $\mu_{\pi_{Target}}(a \mid s, \thetacdot)$ for every state-action pair $(s, a)$ during training, which can be memory-intensive for large models.
