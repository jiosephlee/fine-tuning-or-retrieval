What is Orthogonal Finetuning?

The original paper "Controlling Text-To-Image Diffusion by Finetuning" introduces a method called "finetuning" to gain stronger controllability for text-to-image diffusion models. The basic idea is to finetune the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model with a small learning rate. In the paper, it uses a batch size of 1 and a learning rate of 2e-5.

The conventional finetuning strategy typically uses gradient descent with a small learning rate to update a model (or certain layers of a model). The small learning rate implicitly encourages a small deviation from the pretrained model, and the standard finetuning essentially aims to train the model while implicitly minimizing $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu \| \bm{M} - \bm{M}^0\|$ where $\bm{M}$ is the finetuned model weights and $\bm{M}^ account for the pretrained model weights. This implicit constraint makes intuitive sense, but it can still be too flexible for finetuning a large model. To address this, the paper proposes to additionally constrain the finetuned model to be within a small neighborhood of the pretrained model, \emph{i.e.}, $\thickmuskip = 2mu \medmuskin \|\bm{M} -\bm{M}^ accounts for the finetuned model and $\bm{M}$ and $\bm{M }^0$ account for the finetuned and pretrained model weights, respectively. Different from the conventional finetuning strategy, the paper proposes to further constrain the angular information of neuron weights, \emph{i.e.}, $\thick muskip=2mu \omedmuskip=2mu\|\hat{\bm{M}}-\hat{\bm{M}^}=0$ in which $\hat{\bm{M }}$ and $\hat{\bm{ M}^}$ account for the finetunned and pretrained neuron weights, respectively. The angular constraint is achieved by orthogonal finetuning which modifies the forward pass from $\thickmuskip="2mu" \medmuskip="2mu"\bm{W}^\top\bm{x}$ to $\thickmuskip ="2mu" \med muskip="2mu"\tilde{\bm{W}}^\top\bm{x}}$ where $\bm{x}$ is the input, $\bm{W}$ is the finetunned weight matrix, and $\tilde{\bm{ W}}$ is the orthogonal transformed weight matrix.
