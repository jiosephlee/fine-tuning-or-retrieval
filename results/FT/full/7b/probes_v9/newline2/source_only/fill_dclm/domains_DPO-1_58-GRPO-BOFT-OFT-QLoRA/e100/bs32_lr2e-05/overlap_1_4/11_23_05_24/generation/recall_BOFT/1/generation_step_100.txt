1. Orthogonal Finetuning

Orthogonal finetuning is a parameter-efficient finetuning method. It can significantly improve the performance of pretrained models. The core idea of orthogonal finetuning is to preserve the pretrained knowledge while adapting the model to downstream tasks.

Orthogonal finetuning updates the weights with an orthogonal matrix. An orthogonal matrix $R\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ satisfies $R^T R=I$, where $I$ is an identity matrix. To ensure orthogonality, we usually multiply $R$ with its transpose $R^T$.

The forward pass of orthogonal finetuning can be written as $\bm{z}=(\bm{W}^0)^\top\bm{x}$, where $\bm{W}^ account for all trainable weights, $\bm{x}$ is the input, and $\bm{z}$ is the output. The gradient with respect to the loss function $\mathcal{L}(\bm{z},\bm{y})$ is $\nabla_{\bm{W}^{0}}\mathcal{L}=(\bm{y}-\bm{z})^\top(\bm{z}-\bm{x})$. After applying orthogonal finetuning, the gradient is $\nabla_R\mathcal{L}= (\bm{y}-\ bm{z})^\top\bm{x}$. We can easily find that $\nabla_R \mathcal{L}$ has no bias towards any direction. It will update the weights in all directions with the same magnitude. This is the key to preserve the pretrained knowledge.

2. Block-diagonal Orthogonal Matrix

In the original orthogonal finetuning, a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix is used to update the weights. A block-diagonal orthogonal matrix $R\equiv\begin{bmatrix}R_1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & \cdots & R_n\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{ R}^{d\times n}$ is a block-diagonal matrix with $n$ orthogonal blocks. Each block $R_i$ is an orthogonal matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{n}\times\frac{d}{n}}$. The forward pass of orthogonal finiteting can be written as $\begin{bmatrix}\bm{z}_1\\\vdots\\\bm{z}_n\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix} \bm{W}^1\\ \vdots\\ \bm{W}^{n}\end{bmatrix}^\top\begin{bmatrix]R_1 & \cdot &\cdot&\cdot&\cdot\\ \vdots & \cdot &\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\ \cdot & \cdot &R_n&\cdot&\vdots\\ . &\cdots &\cdots& \cdot&\vdots \\ \cdot &\cdot &\cdot& \cdot&I_n\end{bmatrrix}$, where $\bm{x}\in\mathbb R^{d}$ and $\bm{z}_i\in\mathbb R^{\frac{d}n}$ for $i=1,\cdots,n$. $\bm{W}^{j}\in\mathbb {R}^{\frac {d} {n}×h}$ for $j=1,\cdots ,n$. $\bm{z}_1,\cdots,\bm{z}_n$ are the outputs of $n$ linear blocks. The original orthogonal finetuning can thus be written as $n$ independent linear finetuning processes.

Block-diagonal orthogonal matrices can well preserve the global information of the pretrained model. However, it may destroy the local information. For example, a convolution kernel with size $3\times3$ will be divided into $9$ small kernels with size $1\times1$ after applying a block-diagonal orthogonal transformation. The transformation will destroy the local connections between neurons.
