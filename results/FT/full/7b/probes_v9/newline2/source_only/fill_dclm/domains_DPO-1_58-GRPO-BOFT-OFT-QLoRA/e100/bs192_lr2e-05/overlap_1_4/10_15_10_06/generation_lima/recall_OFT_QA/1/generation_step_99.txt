According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning method that prevents catastrophic forgetting in text-to-image diffusion models. It was introduced in the paper "ControllingText-to-Image Diffusion with Orthogonal Finetuning" by Google Brain.

The basic idea of OFT is to finetune the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model while preserving its orthogonal subspace. To achieve this, the pretrained model is first projected to an orthogonal matrix, and then the projection is updated during finetuning. The projection is later reprojected to an identity matrix.

More formally, OFT can be written as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf{D}^{-0.5}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}^\top\mathcal{L}(x;\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{W}_\text{init}, y; \lambda), \\
&\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}+2\mu\mathbf{S},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathcal{L}$ is the finetuning loss, $\mathbf{W}_0$ is the pretrained text-to-image model, $\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{W}_-\mathbf{W}^\top)_{n\times n}$ is the covariance matrix of the pretrained model, $\mathbf W$ is the finetuned model, $\mathbf R$ is the projection matrix, $\mathbf I$ is the identity matrix, $\mathbf S$ is the singular value matrix, and $\mu$ is a small scalar.

The intuition behind OFT is that the pretrained text-to-image models have already learned rich semantic information, and finetuning the projection matrix can adapt the model to the downstream task without losing the semantic knowledge.<|endoftext|>
