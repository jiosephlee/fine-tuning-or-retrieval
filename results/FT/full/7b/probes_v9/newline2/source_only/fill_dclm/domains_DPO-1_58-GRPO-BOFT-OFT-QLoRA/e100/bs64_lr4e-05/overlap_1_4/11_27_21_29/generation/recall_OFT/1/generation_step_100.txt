After reading the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", I've learned a lot. Let me tell you everything I've learned. I hope it will be helpful to you.

1. What is Orthogonal Finetuning?

In the paper, they introduce a new finetuning strategy — Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). It can well preserve the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model's semantic while optimizing the model's parameters to fit the dataset.

2. How does OFT work?

OFT uses Cayley parameterization to construct an orthogonal matrix. Then it multiplies the orthogonal matrix to the pretrained weight matrix to get the finetuned weight matrix.

3. Why does OFT work?

I think there are three reasons:

  • Cayley transform can perfectly preserve the magnitude of the neurons.
  • Cayley transform produces a orthogonal matrix which can perfectly preserve the direction of the neurons.
 • The multiplied orthogonal matrix and the pretrained weight matrix can be viewed as a single weight matrix. So it won't bring in additional parameters.

4. How to use OFT?

You can use it like a normal finetuning method. Just replace the optimizer to OFTOptimizer.

5. What's the difference between OFT and LoRA?

Both OFT and LoRA are instance-aware finetuning methods. They both use the same loss function — binary cross entropy. But they have different ways to update the weights.

  • LoRA updates the weights with a low-rank matrix.
  • OFT updates the weights with an orthogonal matrix.

6. What's the difference compared to Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning(PEOF)?

Both PEOF and OFT are orthogonal finetuning methods. But they have different goals.

  • PEOF is designed to reduce the number of trainable parameters.
  • OFT is designed to well preserve the pretrained model's semantic while finetuning the model.

7. What's the difference to Intrinsic Dimensionality Regularization(IDR)?

Both IDR and OFT are orthogonal methods. But they have difference goals.

  • IDR is designed to prevent the model from overfitting the training data.
  • OFT focuses on finetuning the pretrained model.

8. What's the difference with Spectral Norm Constraint(SNC)?

Both SNC and OFT are regularized methods. But they have differences.

  • SNC regularizes the spectral norm of the weight matrix.
  • OFTs regularizes the multiplication result of two matrices — the orthogonal matrix and the pretrained weights.

9. What's the difference comparing to Angle Parameterization(Angular)?

Both Angular and OFT are orthogonal method. But they have differences:

  • Angular can only produce an angle multiplier for each neuron. While OFT can produce a multiplier for each neuron direction.
  • Angular needs to multiply the angle multiplier to each neuron. While OFTs multiplies an orthogonal matrix to the pretrained weights.

10. What's the difference compare to Orthogonal Finetuning with Factorization(OFT-FA)?

Both OFT-FA and OFT are instance-aware finetunig methods. But they have some differences:

  • OFT-FA uses butterfly structure to generate the orthogonal matrix. While OFT uses Cayley transform to generate the orthogonal matrix.
  • OF-T-FA can easily introduce additional trainable parameters. While OFT doesn't.

11. What's the difference compares to Orthogonal Finetunig with Factorization and LoRA combined(OFT-FA+LoRA)?

Both OFT-FA+LoRA and OFT are instance aware finetuning methods. And they have the following differences:

  • OFT-FA+LORA is a combination of two methods — OFT-FA and LoRA. While OFT is a standalone method.
  • OFT- FA+LoRA can introduce additional trainable parameters. But OFT can't.

12. What's the difference in speed comparing to LoRA?

Both OFTs and LoRA are instance aware finetunig methods.

  • LoRA is faster in training speed. But it may destroy the pretrained semantic.
  • OFT has slower training speed. But it can well preserve the pretrained semantic.

13. What's the difference of runtime memory comparing to LoRA?

  • LoRA needs less runtime memory. Because it only needs to store a low-rank matrix which is much smaller than the weight matrix.
