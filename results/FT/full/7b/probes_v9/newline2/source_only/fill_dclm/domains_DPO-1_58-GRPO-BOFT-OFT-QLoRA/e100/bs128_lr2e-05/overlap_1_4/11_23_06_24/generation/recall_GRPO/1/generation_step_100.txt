1. What is DeepSeekMath?

DeepSeekMath is a mathematical reasoning model based on the DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.6. It's the largest open-source model with 1.6B parameters. It's the first model to achieve 100% accuracy on the MATH dataset and 99.9% accuracy on the MMLU dataset.

2. How to train a mathematical reasoning model?

There are three steps to train a mathematical reasoning models:

  1. Pre-training on a large-scale general corpus.
  2. Fine-tuning on a mathematical dataset.
  3. Training with self-supervised tasks.

2.1 Pre-training

The pre-training data is a combination of Common Crawl and BooksCorpus. The size of the data is 800GB. The training tokenization is WordPiece. The maximum length is 4K tokens. The batch size is 256. The learning rate is 1e-5. The training epoch is 500. The training ratio is 1:10. The training ratio means how many hours the training model is trained for every hour the pre-training model is trained for. The training ratio is set to 1:10 in this paper. So the pre-training model is only trained for 50 hours. The rest 500 hours are used to train the training model.

2.2 Fine-tuning

The training data is MATH and MMLU. The training tokenization and maximum length are the same as the pre-training. The batch size is set to 256. The learning rates of the embedding, transformer, and attention layers are 5e-6, 1e-5, and 1e-5 respectively. The training epoch is set to 200. The training ratio is also set to 1:20. So the pre-training is trained for 10 hours and the training model is trained fo 400 hours.

2.3 Self-supervised training

The self-supervised training data is a combination of MATH, MMLU, and Common Crawl. The training tokenization, maximum length, batch size, learning rates, and training epoch are the same as the fine-tuning stage.

3. Results

3.1 MATH dataset

The MATH dataset is a multiple-choice math dataset. It's divided into two sets: dev and test. The dev set has 50K questions. The test set has 100K questions. The questions in the MATH dataset are selected from the SAT, ACT, and GRE exams. They cover a wide range of high school and college math topics. The questions in the dataset are of high quality. But there are only 150K questions in the dataset. So the data is quite limited. The questions in the dev set are used to evaluate the performance of the model. The questions in the test set are used to compare the performance of different models.
