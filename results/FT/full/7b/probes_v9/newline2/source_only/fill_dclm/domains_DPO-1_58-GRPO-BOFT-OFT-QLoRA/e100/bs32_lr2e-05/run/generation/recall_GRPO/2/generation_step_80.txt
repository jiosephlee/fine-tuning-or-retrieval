Mathematical Reasoning in Language Models

Mathematical reasoning in natural language processing refers to the ability of a model to perform mathematical reasoning based on natural language inputs and produce mathematical outputs or judgments. It is an important yet challenging task for language models, as it requires the model to understand the problem, perform mathematical operations, and generate a solution in natural language.

There are mainly two types of approaches to mathematical reasoning in language models: pipeline approaches and end-to-end approaches. Pipeline approaches typically consist of three modules: problem understanding, reasoning, and answer generation. The problem understanding module identifies the key information in the problem and produces a formal representation of the problem. The reasoning module performs mathematical reasoning on the formal representation and produces a solution. The answer generation module generates a natural language answer based on the solution. End-to-end approaches directly optimize the joint probability distribution over the input text and the output solution without explicitly performing problem understanding, reasoning, or answer generation.

Benchmarking Mathematical Reasoning in Language Models


The MATH benchmark is a multiple-choice benchmark for mathematical reasoning in language models developed by Meta. It consists of 425,000 training examples and 50,000 test examples. The training examples are sourced from multiple-choice questions on the web, with each question paired with four answer options. The test examples are sourced from the competition-level questions of the MATH competition, with each question paired only with a single answer option. The MATH benchmark evaluates models' performance on arithmetic, algebra, and geometry problems.


The BIG-Bench Math benchmark is a benchmark for mathematical reasoning in large-language models developed by OpenAI. It consists of 170,000 training examples and a validation set of 10,000 examples. The training examples include mathematical problems paired with solutions, and the validation examples include problems paired with answer options. The BIG-Bench Math benchmark evaluates models' performance using the validation set and a test set of 1,000 problems from the MATH benchmark.


The GSM8K benchmark is a benchmark for informal mathematical reasoning in language models maintained by DeepSeek. It consists of 8,000 training examples and an evaluation set of 2,000 examples. The examples are sourced from the GSM8K dataset, which includes problems paired with solutions. The GSM8K benchmark evaluates models' performance based on the accuracy of solving GSM8K dataset problems.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO)

Policy optimization algorithms, such as PPO and GRPO, are reinforcement learning algorithms that can be used to train language models with reinforcement learning from human feedback. They work by optimizing a policy model using a loss function that measures the difference between the rewards given by the policy model and the ground-truth rewards. In the context of training language models, the policy model is typically a neural network that outputs a reward for each token given the preceding tokens, and the ground-truth reward is the reward assigned by a human annotator for a complete response.

One challenge with policy optimization algorithms is that they can be computationally expensive, as they typically require sampling many trajectories from the policy model to estimate the ground-truth rewards and calculate the loss function. GRPO addresses this challenge by grouping trajectories together and estimating the ground-truth rewards at the group level instead of the trajectory level. This significantly reduces the memory and computational requirements of policy optimization algorithms, making them more scalable for large-scale data.
