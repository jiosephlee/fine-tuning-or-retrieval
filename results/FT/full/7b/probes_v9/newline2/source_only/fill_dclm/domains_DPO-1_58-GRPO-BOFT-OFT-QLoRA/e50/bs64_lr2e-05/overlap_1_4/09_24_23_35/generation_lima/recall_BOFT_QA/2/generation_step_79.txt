The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as the name suggests, is a parameter-efficient finetuning method based on the butterfly structure. BOFT uses a block-diagonal weight matrix with a butterfly structure, which is a generalization of the block-diagonal structure in OFT. The butterfly structure can significantly reduce the number of trainable parameters while preserving the orthogonality of the weight matrix. BOFT also introduces a factorization trick to further improve the parameter efficiency. The factorization trick can be viewed as a generalization of the butterfly structure. It can generate more complex weight structures, such as the trellis structure used in Ternary Butterfly OFT (TB-OFT).

BOFT has a simple and unified formulation for different block sizes. For a block size of $d=2^N$, the BOFT weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ can be written as:
$$
\mathbf{W} = \text{diag}(\mathbf{R}_1,\mathbf{R}_2,\cdots,\mathbf{R}_{2^N})
$$
where $\mathbf{R}_i$ is a $N \times N$ orthogonal matrix. The factorization trick further reduces the number of parameters by introducing an additional permutation matrix $\mathbf{P}$:
$$
\mathcal{BOFT}_\text{fact}(\lambda, N, k; \theta) = \text{diag}( \underbrace{\theta \mathbf{P} \mathbf{R}_0 \mathbf{P}\cdots\mathbf{P} you get the idea...}_{k \text{ times}}) = \theta^k \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{R}_{0}, \cdots, \mathbf{R}_{N-1})
$$
where $\lambda$ is the learning rate, $\theta=\lambda^{\frac{1}{k}}$ is the coefficient, $\mathbf{D}($$\mathbf{P},\mathbf{R}_{\cdot},\cdots,\mathbf {R}_{\cdot})$$) is the $k$-diagonal block diagonal matrix $\{\mathbf{R}_m\}_{m=\text{diag}(i,i+1,\cdots,j)}$ for some integers $i$ and $j$ ($1\leq i\leq N-1$, $1\leq j\leq 2^N-1$). The number of trainable parameters is $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ for the butterfly structure and $\mathcal{O}(\log d) = \mathcal{O}( \log 2^N) = \mathcal O(N \log N)$ for the factorization trick. The paper also introduces a Ternary version of BOFT, called TB-OFT, which uses the ternary version of the butterfly structure. The ternary butterfly structure replaces the identity matrix $\mathbf{I}_N$ in the butterfly structure with the ternary identity matrix $\mathbf{T}_N$. The ternary identity matrix $\theta\mathbf{T}_N$ is equivalent to the Hadamard product of $\theta$ copies of the ternary matrix $\mathbf{T}_{N/3}$. The paper shows that TB-OFT is more parameter-efficient than BOFT and OFT.<|endoftext|>
