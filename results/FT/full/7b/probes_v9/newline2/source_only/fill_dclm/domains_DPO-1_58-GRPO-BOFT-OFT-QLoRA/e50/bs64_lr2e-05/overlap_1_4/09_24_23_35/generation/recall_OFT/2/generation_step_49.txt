The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective finetuning method that can be applied to any pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. It finetunes the pretrained model by learning orthogonal transformations for each neuron, which preserves the pairwise inner products between neurons. OFT is easy to implement and has no hyperparameters to tune. It can be used as a drop-in replacement for the standard finetuning or LoRA method to finetune any text-to-image diffusion model.

The paper shows that OFT can achieve strong performance in both generation and classification tasks. In the generation task, OFT can match or even surpass the performance of LoRA, a state-of-the-art additive finetuning method. In the classification task, OFT can achieve zero-shot accuracy comparable to fully finetuned models. Moreover, the paper also provides theoretical analysis to explain why OFT works well in preserving the semantic information of the pretrained model. It shows that OFT can preserve the pairwise angular similarity between neurons, which is crucial for representing the semantic information of the data. In contrast, standard finetuning and LoRA can only preserve the pairwise Euclidean similarity, which is not sufficient to preserve the semantic information.

The paper also provides some interesting insights into the training process of text-to-image diffusion models. It shows that the early stages of the training process are the most important for learning the semantic information of the dataset. This is because the pairwise Euclidean similarity is already close to the pairwise angular similarity in the early stages, so the finetuning methods can only make marginal improvements. In contrast, the later stages of the training process focus on learning the detailed visual structures, which is less important for preserving the semantic information.
