According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning strategy that prevents catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously encouraging out-of-distribution generalization. It does so by learning an orthogonal transformation for each layer, which transforms the finetuning weights into the original pretrained ones. This is achieved by adding an additional term to the gradient update rule of the forward pass, which is the product of the identity matrix and its inverse (the orthogonal matrix). The orthogonal matrix is learned per layer, and its orthogonality is enforced through the Cayley parameterization.

OFT can be written as:

$$w^{(l+1)} = w^{(l)} - \eta \nabla_wJ(w^{(l)}) + \eta \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}L_w^{(l)*}(w^{(l)})L_w^{(l)}(w^{(l)})^\top,$$

where $w$ is the weights, $\eta$ is the learning rate, $J$ is the loss function, $d$ is the dimension of the weight matrix, and $L_w$ is the gradient of the loss with respect to the weights.

The Cayley transform is used to ensure that the orthogonal matrix is always a rotation, i.e. $L_wL_w^\top = I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix. This is done by parameterizing the orthogonal matrix as $Q = I - 2vv^\top + vv^\top(vv^\top)^\top$, where $v$ is a vector of length $\sqrt{d}$.<|endoftext|>
