What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that is well-suited for training large models using actor-critic methods. PPO works by clipping the ratio of a sampled policy $\pi_{\theta}(a|s)$ to a target policy $\pi_{\pi}(a|s)$, and optimizes the policy parameter $\theta$ such that this clipped objective is maximized. The target policy $\pi_{pi}$ is usually the previous policy used for training the model. 

The major limitation of PPO is that it requires computing the expectation of the policy with respect to the baseline $b(s, a; \phi)$, where $b(s, a, ; \phi)$ is a learned function. This baseline helps remove the system's mean performance, which can be learned using another policy $\phi$. 

To address this requirement, PPO uses a simple baseline that is the average value of taking action $a$ in state $s$ according to the current policy $\pi_{\thetahat}$, i.e., 
$$b(s, a;  \phi) = \frac{1}{|S|} \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\thetaha t}}[\return(s, a)] $$
This baseline removes the mean return under the current policy, but it has to be computed every time we sample a new policy $\pi_{\theqh}$, which requires keeping track of the distribution of the policy parameter $\thetahat$ in memory. This can be a significant memory burden for large models.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is an alternative to PPO that does not require keeping track of the distribution over policy parameters. Instead, it uses the following baseline, which is the average return when taking the most probable action under the current policy $\pi_\theta$ and any action from the second most probable policy $\pi_\theta - \epsilon$:
$$b(s, a ; \phi) = \mathbb{E}_{{\pi}_{\theta}}[\return(s, A^*)] - \mathbb{E}_{{\pi}_{\thetahat}-\epsilon}[\return(s, A)] $$
where $A^*$ is the action chosen by the current policy $\pi_ \theta$, and $A$ is any action from the second-most probable policy $\pi_{\hat \theta} - \epsilon$, where $\epsilon$ is a small constant vector. 

The reason this baseline does not require keeping track or the distribution of the policy parameters is that the expectation over the actions chosen by the policy $\pi_{\hathat} - \epsilon$ can be pre-computed and stored in memory, since it does not change over time.
