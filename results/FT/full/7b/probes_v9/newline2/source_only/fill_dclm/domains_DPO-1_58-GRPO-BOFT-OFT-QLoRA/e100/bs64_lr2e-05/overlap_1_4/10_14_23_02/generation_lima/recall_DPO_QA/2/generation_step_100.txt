The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" introduces Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) as an alternative to the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Eck et al., 2019) is a model-based approach for training language models to adhere to human preferences. It uses a variant of the dynamic programming algorithm called value-of-information (VOI) to estimate the reward model, which is then used to train the language model with actor-critic reinforcement learning.

The basic idea behind DPO is to use a model-based approach to estimate the reward model from human preferences, instead of using a model-free method like PPO to optimize the KL-divergence between the true reward model and a parametric reward model as in RLHF. This has the advantage that the preference data can be used more efficiently, but the downside that it requires a good model to estimate the reward model. In the context of training language models, this usually isn't a problem, because the best approach for training the language model is to also use a model-based approach (i.e. the learned reward model).<|endoftext|>
