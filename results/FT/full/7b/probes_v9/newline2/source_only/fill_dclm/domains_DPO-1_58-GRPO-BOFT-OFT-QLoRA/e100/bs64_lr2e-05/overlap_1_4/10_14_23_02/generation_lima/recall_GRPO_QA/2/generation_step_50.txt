What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that is well-suited for training large models using actor-critic methods. It works by estimating a clipped advantage function, and using it to update a proxy objective for the policy. The policy is then updated to maximize this objective. Despite its popularity, PPO has a major limitation: it requires storing the maximum value seen under the current policy, for every action in every environment. This can be memory-intensive, especially when training very large models in parallel on a large cluster.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a variant of PPO that reduces memory usage by a factor of the number of training groups. GRPO does this by maintaining per-group moving averages of the advantages and the advantage clipper. These per-group moving averages are then used to compute the per-policy averages required by PPO. This approach is similar to techniques used in control theory to reduce the memory requirements of linear quadratic regulators (LQRs).

GRPO was developed as part of the DeepSeekMath project, which trained an open-source language model, Llemma, to do math. Llemma used GRPO to learn mathematical reasoning capabilities, and achieved strong results on benchmarks of mathematical reasoning in language models.<|endoftext|>
