The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet powerful method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that provably preserves the pairwise neuron angles, and therefore the pairwise neuron inner products and pairwise neuron dot products, throughout the finetuning process. This property is crucial for preserving the semantic knowledge learned from the pretrained text-to-image diffusion model. OFT works by learning orthogonal transformations for each neuron, and it can be easily combined with other finetuning methods. We demonstrate the empirical effectiveness of OFT in finetuning stable diffusion models and various vision-language diffusion models. Our experiments also validate several design choices for OFT.

Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved impressive performance in generating high-quality images from text prompts. However, these models are typically trained on large datasets of images and text pairs, which can be expensive and time-consuming to collect. As a result, it is often difficult to adapt these models to new tasks or domains using only a small set of training data. In contrast, diffusion models are trained on a single dataset of images, and can therefore be easily adapted to new tasks using a small set of training images. This makes diffusion models a promising alternative to text-to-image diffusion models.
