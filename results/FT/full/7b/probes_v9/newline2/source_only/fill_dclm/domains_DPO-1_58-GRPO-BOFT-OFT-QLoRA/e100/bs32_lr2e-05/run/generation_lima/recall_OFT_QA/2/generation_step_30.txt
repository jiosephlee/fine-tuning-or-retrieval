What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained neural networks. It is designed to preserve the most important property of pretrained models, their ability to represent the training data well, while still allowing for significant performance improvements on downstream tasks. OFT achieves this by ensuring that the pretrained neural network and the finetuned model have the same pair-wise neuron angles. This property is crucial for preserving the semantic knowledge of the pretrained model. OFT works by learning a matrix that transforms the finetuning gradients into a direction that preserves the pair-wise neuron angles. OFT has been shown to perform as well or better than standard finetuning and LoRA on a variety of tasks, while preserving the semantic knowledge of pretrained models better than these methods.

This ensures that the pair-wise neuron angles are preserved between the pretrained and the finetuned models.

## Why does OFT work?

The key insight behind OFT is that pair-wise neuron angles are the most important property of a neural network that needs to be preserved during finetuning. Neuron angles are defined as the cosine of the angle between two neurons. They are a measure of the semantic similarity between two neurons. If two neurons have the same neuron angle, then they represent the same semantic concept. If two neurons have different neuron angles, then they represent different semantic concepts. Therefore, preserving pair-wise neuron angles during finetuning ensures that the semantic knowledge of the pretraining data is preserved.
