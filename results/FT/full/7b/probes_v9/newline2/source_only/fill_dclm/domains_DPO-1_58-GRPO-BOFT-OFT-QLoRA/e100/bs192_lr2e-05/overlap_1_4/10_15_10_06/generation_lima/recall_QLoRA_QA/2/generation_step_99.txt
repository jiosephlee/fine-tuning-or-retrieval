What is QLoRA?<|EOT|>Quantized Learning with Relaxation and Activation (QLoRA) is a method for finetuning large language models (LLMs) using significantly less memory. It does so by quantizing the weights of a pretrained model to a smaller number of bits, typically 4 or 8 bits, and then performing stochastic gradient descent on these quantized weights. The key idea behind QLoRA is to relax the constraints imposed by weight quantization during gradient descent, allowing the optimizer to implicitly correct for quantization errors. This relaxation is achieved by clamping the quantized weights to the corresponding 16-bit values at every gradient step. As a result, the effective weight that the gradient update modifies is the true 16-bit weight, not the quantized weight. Since the 16-bit weight is restored at every step, the method also implicitly normalize the gradient with the weight's magnitude, making rescaling learning rates across layers unnecessary. QLoRA can be viewed as a form of hybrid quantization that switches between precise (16-bit) and quantized weights at different time scales. In contrast to pure quantization methods, which typically need to perform weight quantization at a very fine grain (e.g. 1 pixel for images), QLoRA only requires a coarser grain, specified by the number of clips, to be successful. The name QLoRA is a play on words and is meant to evoke the ideas of relaxation and Llama.
