The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate photorealistic images given a text prompt. These models are trained using a combination of reconstruction loss and a perceptual loss, such as the SSL loss. During inference, the reconstruction loss is no longer needed, and the models are typically finetuned using a perceptual loss to stabilize the training and to prevent mode-collapse. However, standard finetuning can cause the model to forget the distribution it was initially trained on, and thus lose the ability to generate text-to-image pairs. Additive methods, such as LoRA, can prevent this forgetfulness, but they can introduce new modes (e.g. by introducing mode-collapse) and fail to update important neurons.

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT), in contrast, updates the weights while preserving the pairwise angles between neurons, and thus prevents the model from forgetting the distribution it was initially learned on, while still allowing for updates to the neurons. OFT achieves this by using Cayley parametrization, which allows for an orthogonal transformation of the weight matrix while preserving the pairwise angles. More formally, OFT optimizes the objective:

$$\mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{R}^T + \beta\mathbf{W},$$

where $\mathbf{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix with $\|\mathbf{R}\|=1$, and $\beta$ is a small scalar.

The authors also propose Group Orthogonal Finetuning, which achieves the same pairwise angular preservation, but for a subset of neurons. Specifically, OFT within group $g$ optimizes the objective:

\begin{align}\mathbf{W}_g &\leftarrow \text{OFT}_{\beta}( \mathbf{W}_ g  ,  \mathbf{W} _\perp )\\
&= \mathbf{R}(\beta)\mathbf{W}_\perp\mathbf{R}^\top(\beta)  + \beta\mathcal{D}_g,\end{align}

where $\mathcal{D}_ g $ is the submatrix of $\mathbf{W}$ containing the elements in $g$ and $\mathbf{W}_ \perp$ is the submatrix of $ \mathbf{W}$ that is orthogonal to $g$.

The authors also consider the case where a subset of neurons are grouped together, i.e. $g_1, \dots, g_m$, and perform OFT separately for each group $g_i$.

The authors evaluate their method on the task of controlling text-to-image diffusion models, and find that OFT is able to preserve the generation quality of the unfinetuned model, while also being able to learn to follow text prompts. They also find that their group OFT is able to perform finetuning within different groups independently, without requiring any cross-group coordination.<|endoftext|>
