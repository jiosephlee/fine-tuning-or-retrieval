According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>The Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft) structure is a parameter-efficient variant of the block-circulant structure used in LoRA. BOft uses an orthogonal factorization to construct a parameter-efficient circulant weight matrix. Specifically, the $d$-dimensional BOft($r$, $\theta$) module has
```W_l = \text{CooFactor}(\theta_1, r)(\cdot)\cdot \text{Coo Factor}(\theta_2, r)^T,
W_h = \text{C oo Factor}(\theta_3, r)^T(\cdot)\cdot \text {C oo Factor}(\ theta_4, r),
```
where $\text{CooFactor}(a, r)(\cdot)$ is defined as
```\text{CooFactor(a, r)}(j) = \text{diag}(a^{j\%d}, \ldots, a^{(j-1)\%d}), \quad j = 1, \ldots, d,
```
and $\circ$ denotes the Hadamard product. The resulting $W_l$ and $W_h$ are both circulant. The BOft structure has some nice properties. First, it can be written in a closed-form as
```W = \text{circ}( \text{circ}(W_0, r), r)\text{ \circ} \text{ circ}(W_1, r), \quad W_0, W_1 \text{ are dense},
```
where $\textcircled{ circ}(A, r)(i, j) = A(j, r)(i, \cdot)$ is the circulant matrix obtained by circling $A$ with a circulant matrix $r$. Second, the spectral norm of BOft($r$, $	heta$) is upper-bounded by $\sqrt{r}$ times the spectral norm of $W_0$ used in the inner circulant layer, i.e., $\sigma_\text{norm}( \text{BOft}(r, 	heta)(W_0)) \leq \sqrt{r}\sigma_\text{norm)(W_0)}$. This is in contrast to the block-circulant (LoRA) structure, for which the spectral norm is upper-bounded by the number of blocks $B$ times the spectral norm of the block $W_0^{\text{BxB}}$, i.e., $\sigma_{\text{norm}}(\text{LoRA}(B, r, W_0)) \le B\sigma_\text{norm}\left(W_0^{\text{{BxB}}}\right)$. As a result, the number of parameters of BOft($r, 	heta$) is $r d^2 / 2$, which is only a function of $r$.
The paper also provides some theoretical insights into why the orthogonal factorization is more efficient than the block-circulant factorization. The key insight is that the block-circulant matrix is equivalent to a circulant matrix with a very large block size, while the orthogonal factorization produces a matrix that is exactly parameterized by a small $r$. The small parameter $r$ also leads to better numerical stability.<|endoftext|>
