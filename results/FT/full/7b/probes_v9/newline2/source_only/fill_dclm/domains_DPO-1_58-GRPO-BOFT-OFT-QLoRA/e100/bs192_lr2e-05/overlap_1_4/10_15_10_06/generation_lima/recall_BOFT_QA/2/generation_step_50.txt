The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient variant of OFT. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to parameterize a block-diagonal matrix with a product of two matrices. The forward pass of BOFT is the same as OFT. The key difference is that BOFT uses a factorization to initialize the matrices $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ and $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbf{R}^{b \times d}$, while OFT initializes them with a block-diagonal identity matrix. The authors show that BOFT can be viewed as a continuous relaxation of the block-diagonal approach, and provide theoretical analysis to show that BOFT is equivalent to the block-diagonal approach when $b = 1$ and $\mathbf{R}$ is a rotation matrix.

The BOFT algorithm is as follows:

1. Initialize $\mathbf{W} \in \mathcal{R}^{n \times m}$ and $\mathbf {R} \in \mathrm{SO}(d)$, $\mathbf{L}\sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}_b,\beta\mathbf{I}_ b)$.
2. For each layer $l$,
	* Compute $\mathbf{L}_{l}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{L}_{li}$ where $\mathbf{L}_i$ denotes the $i$-th block of $\mathbf{L}$.
	* Butterfly update: $\mathbf{R}_{l}=\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}( \mathbf{R}_{li}\mathbf{R}_{lo}^T)$ where $\mathcal{D}_\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes $\gamma$-multiplicative diagonal dropout and $\mathbf{ R}_{li},\mathbf{ R}_{lo}\in \mathbb{ R}^{b \times b}$ are the $l$-th and $l$-th element blocks of $\mathbf{R}_i,\mathbf{R}_o\in \mathcal{ R}^{d \times 1}$ respectively.
	* Update matrix: $\mathbf{L_{l}}=\mathbf{L_{li}}\mathbf{L_{lo}}^T$, $\mathbf{W_{l+1}}=\mathbf{W_l} \mathbf{L_l}$.
3. Continue with the same forward pass and the next $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{L}$ until the end of training.<|endoftext|>
