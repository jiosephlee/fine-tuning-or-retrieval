Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language models

Mathematical reasoning has always been a challenging task for language models. In this section, we will introduce some benchmarks of mathematical reasoning and the latest models.

Mathematical Reasonability Benchmarks

There are three mainstream benchmarks for mathematical reasoning: MATH, MMLU-STEM, and GSM8K. Let's take a look at them one by one.

1. MATH Benchmark

The MATH benchmark is a new benchmark for mathematical reasoning in language models, consisting of two subtasks: multiple-choice and text-to-image reasoning. It is constructed based on the high-quality dataset MMLU and is designed to be the new standard for evaluating mathematical reasoning in language models. The evaluation results are based on the competition-level results only.

2. MMLU-STEM Benchmark

The MMLU-STEM benchmark is another benchmark for mathematical reasoning in LLMs, which is constructed by selecting STEM-related questions from the MMLU dataset and then having human annotators label the answers. It consists of two subtasks: Multiple-Choice and Code. In the multiple-choice subtask, the model needs to output the correct answer given a stem and several options. In the code subtask, the model is required to generate the correct Python code given a natural language statement of the problem.

3. GSM8K Benchmark

The GSM8K benchmark is the most widely-used benchmark for mathematical reasoning in open-language models. It is a multiple-choice benchmark that consists of 8,000 questions curated from the internet. Each question has four options and only one correct answer. The benchmark covers a wide range of fields, including elementary mathematics, high-school mathematics, and some college-level mathematics.

Latest Models

1. GPT-4

GPT-4 is the latest model from OpenAI, and it is the largest model ever released with 70B parameters. It significantly improves the mathematical reasoning capabilities of GPT-3 and sets a new state-of-the-art on the GSM8K benchmark, achieving an accuracy of 43.5%.

2. DeepSeekMath

DeepSeekMath is a model trained by DeepSeek with more than 65B parameters that outperforms GPT-4 on the MATH benchmark, achieving an accuracy higher than 50% on the competition-level data. On the MMLU-STEM leaderboard, DeepSeekMath is ranked second, with its code-generation model achieving 58.9%.

3. MathPile

MathPile is a model trained by Hugging Face with 33B parameters that is the current leader on the GSM8K leaderboard, achieving an accuracy of over 45%. MathPile is a unified model that is trained on 140B tokens of mathematical data, including the GSM8K dataset, the MATH dataset, the MMLU- STEM dataset, and other mathematical datasets.

Methodology of DeepSeekMath

Now let's focus on the most interesting part — the methodology of DeepSeekMath. As we all know, the mathematical capabilities of LLMs mainly come from two abilities: mathematical knowledge reasoning and formal mathematics reasoning. Mathematical knowledge reasoning refers to the ability of LLMs to reason with their vast mathematical knowledge, while formal mathematics reasoning refers to the ability to perform formal proof and formal computation.

1. Mathematical Knowledge Reasoning

Mathematical knowledge reasoning is the foundation of mathematical reasoning in LLM. With strong mathematical knowledge reasoning capabilities, LLMs will be able to answer mathematical questions correctly most of the time. Therefore, it is crucial to build a large-scale high-quality mathematical dataset for pre-training LLMs.

In the paper, the authors share their experience in building high-quality datasets for mathematical pre-training. They summarize the following principles:

  • Principle 1: Include as many mathematical sources as possible.
  • Principle 2: Cover different levels of abstraction.
  • Principle 3: Use diverse question types.
  • Principle 4: Use data from different languages.
  • Principle 5: Use peer-reviewed literature.
