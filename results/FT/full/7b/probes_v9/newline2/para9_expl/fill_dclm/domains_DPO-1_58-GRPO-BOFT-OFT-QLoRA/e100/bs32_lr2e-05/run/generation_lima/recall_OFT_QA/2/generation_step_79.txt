What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained text-to-image diffusion models. It is designed to preserve the geometry of the pretrained model, which is important for maintaining the model's ability to generate diverse and high-quality images. OFT achieves this by applying a small orthogonal transformation to the pretrained weights, which preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which typically results in a significant change in the neuron angles, and additive methods like LoRA, which only slightly changes the neuron angles.

Here is a more detailed explanation of how OFT works:

* **Orthogonal Transformation**: OFT applies a small orthogonal transformation to a layer's weights. An orthogonal transformation is a linear transformation that preserves the angles between vectors. This means that the pairwise angles between neurons are preserved after applying the orthogonal transformation.
* **Parameterization**: OFT parameterizes the orthogonal transformation using a Cayley parameterization. This parameterization is convenient because it allows us to easily control the amount of change in the neuron angles. Specifically, OFT uses a small skew-symmetric matrix $Q$ to parameterize the orthogonal transformation. The orthogonal matrix $R$ is then given by $R = (I + Q)(I - Q)^{-1}$, where $I$ is the identity matrix.
* **Training Objective**: During finetuning, OFT minimizes the mean squared error between the finetuned model and the reference model on a set of finetuning images. The objective is given by $\mathcal{L}_{OFT} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N\|f_\theta(x^{(i)}) - f_{\theta'}(x^{(i)})\|^2$, where $f_\theta$ and $f_{\theta'}$ are the finetuned and reference models, respectively, and $x^{(i)}$ is the $i$-th finetuning image.
* **Regularization**: To prevent the orthogonal matrix from becoming too large, OFT adds a regularization term that penalizes large singular values of $R$. This regularization term is given by $\lambda \|R\|_2^2$, where $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter.
* **Inference Time**: During inference, OFT simply replaces the pretrained weights with the finetuned weights. There is no additional computational cost at inference time.

## Advantages of OFT

* **Preserves Neuron Angles**: OFT preserves the pairwise angles between the neurons in a layer. This is important because these angles encode important structural information about the neurons. Preserving the angles helps to maintain the model's ability to represent diverse and high-quality images.
* **No Additional Computational Cost**: Unlike some other finetuning methods, OFT does not introduce any additional computational cost at inference. This makes it a practical choice for finetuning large text-to-image diffusion models.
* **Easy to Implement**: OFT is straightforward to implement and can be easily integrated into existing finetuning pipelines.

## Limitations of OFT

*  While OFT is effective at preserving neuron angles, it may not be able to fully capture all of the changes that are necessary for a task-specific finetuning. This is because OFT only applies a small orthogonal transformation, which is a very constrained form of change.
