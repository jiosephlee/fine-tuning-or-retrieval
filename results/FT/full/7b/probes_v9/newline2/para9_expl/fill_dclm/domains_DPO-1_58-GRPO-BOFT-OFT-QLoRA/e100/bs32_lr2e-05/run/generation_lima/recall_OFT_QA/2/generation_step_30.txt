What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained text-to-image diffusion models. It is designed to preserve the geometry of the pretrained model, which is important for maintaining the quality of generated images. OFT achieves this by applying a small orthogonal transformation to the pretrained weights, which preserves the pairwise angles between neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which changes the weights by a small amount in the same direction, and additive methods like LoRA, which add a small matrix to the pretrained weights.

Here is a more detailed explanation of how OFT works:

* **Orthogonal transformation**: An orthogonal transformation is a linear transformation that preserves the length of vectors and the angles between them. In other words, it is a rotation or reflection of the space. In OFT, the pretrained weights are transformed by an orthogonal matrix, which is learned during finetuning.
* **Small orthogonal matrix**: The orthogonal matrix is small, typically 4x4 or 8x8. This ensures that the transformation is small, so it does not change the pretrained model too much.
* **Preserving pairwise angles**: The key property of OFT is that it preserves the pairwise angles between the neurons. This is important because these angles encode the semantic information in the model. By preserving them, OFT ensures that the pretrained model's semantic knowledge is not lost during finetuning.
