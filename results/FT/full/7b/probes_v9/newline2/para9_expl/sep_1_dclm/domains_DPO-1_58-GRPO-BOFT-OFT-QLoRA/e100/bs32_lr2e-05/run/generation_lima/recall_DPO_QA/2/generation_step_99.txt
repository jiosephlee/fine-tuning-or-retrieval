What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Kupcsik & Leibo, 2023) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences without explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning. Instead of using a learned reward model and applying reinforcement learning, DPO directly optimizes the language model parameters to match human preferences.

In DPO, the objective is to maximize the probability of choosing the correct option given the context and options, while minimizing the probability of choosing the incorrect option. This can be formulated as:

$$\max_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(c, y_w, y_l)\sim data} \left[ \frac{\pi_\theta(c, y_w)}{\pi_\theta(c,y_w)y_l(\pi_{old}(c,y_l))} \right]$$

where $\pi_{old}$ is the previous version of the language model, and $data$ is a dataset of contexts, the preferred completion, and another completion. The paper notes that this objective is intractable because it involves both the current policy $\pi_{\theta}$ and the old policy $\pi_{old}$. To address this, they introduce a reparameterization of the ratio in terms of the log-likelihood under the current policy:

$$\log \frac{\pi(y_w | c)}{\pi(y_l | c)} = \beta_c(c, y_l) - \beta_c(c, contador) + \log \sum_{y} \pi(y | c)$$

where $\beta_c(c, y)$ is a function of the context $c$ and the completion $y$, andcontador is a counter that keeps track of how many completions have been seen for each context. This allows them to write the objective as:

$$\max_\theta \mathbb{E}_c [\beta_c(c, y_w) - \contador - \log \sum_{i=1}^{|y_w|}\pi_i(c, y_w)] - \lambda\mathbb{E}_\theta[\text{KL}(\pi_\theta \ || \ \pi_{ref})]$$

where $\lambda$ is a regularization term that prevents the policy from deviating too much from a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$, and $\pi_i(c, y)$ is the$i$-th element of the vector $\pi(c, y)$. The KL regularizer ensures that the policy remains close to the reference policy, which helps prevent the policy from collapsing to a single mode.

The paper also introduces a variant of DPO that uses pairwise preferences instead of three-way preferences. In this case, the objective is to minimize the binary cross-entropy between the preference distribution and the current policy:

$$-\mathbb{E}_{c, y_w, \sim data}[\log \pi(y_w | c)] + \mathbb{E}[\log \sigma (\beta_c(c, y_s) - \beta_c (c, y_l))]$$

where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid function, and $y_s$ and $y_l$ are the selected and non-selected completions for a given context $c$. This objective can be optimized efficiently using stochastic gradient descent.

DPO has several advantages over traditional RLHF methods such as PPO. First, it does not require a separate reward model, which can be difficult to train and may not accurately reflect human preferences. Second, it avoids the need for reinforcement learning, which can be slow and unstable. Finally, DPO can be trained directly on existing datasets of human preferences, without requiring any additional data collection.<|endoftext|>
