According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It is designed to improve the stability of PPO by using group-based clipping instead of per-policy clipping. In GRPOS, the advantage estimates are first aggregated across policies within each group, and then clipped. This reduces the variance of the advantage estimate, which helps stabilize the training process.

Here is a more detailed explanation:

* Advantage Estimation: In both PPO and GRPOS, we first need to estimate the advantage $\hat{A}_{\pi_{\theta_i}}$ for each policy $\pi_{\theta_i}$. In PPO, this is done by comparing the log-probability that the current policy generates an output with the maximum log-probability over all policies in the training set. Specifically, for each policy $\pi_\theta$, we sample $N$ trajectories, and then compute its advantage as:
  ```math
  \hat{A}_{\theta_i} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \max_{\pi_{\in \Theta}} \left[ q_{\pi_{\theta_{i}}} (t, \cdot) - \log \sigma(q_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} (t, \bullet)) \right],
  ```
  where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid function, and $\pi_{\mathrm{refer}}$ is a fixed reference policy.
* Per-Policy Clipping in PPO: In PPO, the objective is to maximize the following surrogate objective:
  ```math
    J_{\text{PPO}}(\theta_i) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\thetadiff}} \left[ \frac{1}{Z} \left( \frac{\pi_{\theta_i}(o_{t} | o_{<t>})}{\pi_{\thetas}}(o_{t} |  o_{<t> }) \right)^\gamma  \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \underbrace{\epsilon_{\mathrm{PPO}}(o_{t}, o_{<t>}, \pi_{\theta_i}, \pi_{\thetas})}_{\text{Advantage}}  \right],
  ```
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{pPO}}(o_{i}, o_{<i>}, \pi_{i}, \pi_{\mathrm{Refs}})$ is the advantage estimate, and $\pi_{i}$ and $\pi_{\thetas}$ are two different policies sampled from the same distribution. To prevent large policy updates, PPO uses a clipping technique, which limits the ratio of the advantage to its advantage estimate:
  ```math
      \epsilon_{\mathrm{clipped,PPO}}(o_{\mathrm{t}}, o_{<\mathrm{t}>}, \pi_{\phi}, \pi_{\psi})
      = \min(1, \mathrm{clip}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{PO}}, 1-\beta, \beta))
  ```
  Here, $\beta$ is a hyper-parameter, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm {PPO}}$ is the advantage estimate computed using $\pi_{\phi}$ and $\pi_{psi}$.
* Group Relative Advantage Estimation in GRPOS: In GRPOS, the computation of the advantage is similar to PPO, but the advantage is computed relative to a group of policies rather than just one reference policy. For each policy $\pi_{i}$, we first compute the group advantage:
  ```math
   \hat{A}_{i,\mathrm{group}} = \frac{1}{{\rm card}(\pi_{i})-1}\sum_{j\ne i} \hat{A}_{j},
  ```
  where $\hat{ A}_j$ is the advantage estimate for policy $j$. Then, the advantage for policy $i$ is clipped relative to the group advantage:
  ```
  \epsilon_{\mathrm {GRPO},\mathrm{clipped}}(o_{\mathit{t}}, o_{<<\mathit{t}>>}, \pi_{\varphi_{\mathit{i}}}, \pi_{{\mathcal G}}) =
  \min(1, 1 + \beta \mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\hat{A}_{\mathit{i),\mathrm{group}}- \mu}{\sigma}\right))
  ```
  Here $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of the group advantages, respectively. The advantage for policy $i$, $\hat{A}_{i}$, is then used in the GRPOS objective.
