According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It is designed to improve the stability of PPO by using group-based clipping instead of per-policy clipping. In GRPO, the advantage estimates for all policies in a mini-batch are first normalized within each group, and then the clipping of the advantage estimate is performed at the group level rather than at the policy level.

Here is a more detailed explanation:

* Advantage estimation: In both PPO and GRPO, we start with estimating the advantage $\hat{A}_{\pi_{\theta_i}}$ for a policy $\pi_{\theta_i}$ in a mini-batch using the general form:
  $$\hat{A}_{\theta_i} \leftarrow \mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim \text{Batch}} \frac{\pi_{\theta_i}(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\theta_{ref}}(y_w \mid x) } \left[ \log \sigma(\hat{A}_{\phi}(x, y_w, \pi_{\theta_i})(x, y_w))  - \hat{A}_{\ phi}(x, y_w,
  \pi_{\theta_{i}}(x))\right] $$
  where $\pi_{\theta_{refs}}$ is the reference policy, $\pi_{\theta{i}}$ is the current policy being evaluated, and $\hat{A}_{phi}$ is the advantage function.
* Per-policy clipping in PPO: In PPO, the clipped advantage is defined as:
  $$\tilde{A}_{\theta{i}} = \max(1 + \beta \hat{A}_{phi}, 1 - \beta \hat{ A}_{\phi})$$
  where $\beta$ is the clipping coefficient. This means that the advantage estimate for each policy is individually scaled so that its maximum absolute value is no greater than $1 + \beta$ times its minimum absolute value. This can cause instability if some policies have very large advantage estimates while others do not.
* Group-relative normalization in GRPO: In GRPO, the normalized advantage for policy $\pi_{\thetia}$ is defined as:
  $$
  \tilde{A}_{g,\theta_i} = \frac{\hat{A}_{\thetia}}{\max_{\pi_{\in g,\theta} \in g} \hat{A}_{g,\pi_{\theta}}} 
  $$
  where $g$ is a group of policies, and $\tilde{A}_{gi}$ is the normalized advantage for policy $i$ in group $g$. The advantage for policy $\pi_\theta$ is then clipped relative to the other policies in the same group:
  $$
  A_{g,\theta_i}  = \max(1+\beta\tilde{A}_g, 1-\beta\tilde{ A}_g)
  $$
  This ensures that the advantage for each policy in the group is scaled by the same factor, which reduces the variance of the advantage estimates and improves the stability of the optimization algorithm.
* How groups are formed in GRPO: In the DeepSeekMath paper, the authors use a hierarchical structure to form groups. At the top level, there is a single group containing all of the policies in the mini-batch. Within this group, the policies are divided into smaller groups based on their generation step. For example, if the mini-batch contains four samples from the same policy, these samples will be assigned to different groups based on whether they are the first, second, third, or fourth sample generated by the policy.
