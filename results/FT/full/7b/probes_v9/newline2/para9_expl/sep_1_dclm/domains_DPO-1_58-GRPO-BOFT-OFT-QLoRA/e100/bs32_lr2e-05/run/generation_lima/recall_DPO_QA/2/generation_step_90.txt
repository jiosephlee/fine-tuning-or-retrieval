What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Kupcsik & Leibo, 2023) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences without reinforcement learning. It is based on the observation that language models can be used to estimate rewards in the RLHF setting, and that this reward estimation can be learned directly from preference data.

In the standard RLHF setup, one trains a reward model $r$ using some supervised learning algorithm, then uses this reward model to generate human-labeled experience $\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{x^{(i)}, y_w^{(i)}\bigr\}_{i=1}^N$, where $y_w^{(i)}$ is the human-labeled good response to $x^{(i)}$. This experience is then used to train an agent under the reward model, e.g. with PPO. In contrast, DPO learns the reward model directly from preference data, i.e. pairs of responses $x^{(i)}, x'^{(i)}$ where $y_w^{*(i)} = x^{(i)}$.
Formally, DPO learns a parameterized reward function $r_{\theta}(·)$ by maximizing the log-likelihood of the observed preferences:
$$
\max_{\theta}\;\bigl\langle r_{\theta}, \mathbf{1}_\mathcal{D}\bigr\rangle,
$$
where $\mathbf{1}_Δ$ is the indicator function that equals 1 when its argument is true and 0 otherwise. The authors show that this objective can be optimized efficiently using a reparameterization of the reward model, similar to the reparameterization used in VAEs. They also introduce a variant of the objective that incorporates a regularization term to avoid degenerate solutions.
The main advantage of DPO is that it avoids the need for reinforcement learning, which is often difficult to scale to large language models. However, DPO requires access to human-labeled pairs of responses, rather than single responses, which may be more expensive to collect.<|endoftext|>
