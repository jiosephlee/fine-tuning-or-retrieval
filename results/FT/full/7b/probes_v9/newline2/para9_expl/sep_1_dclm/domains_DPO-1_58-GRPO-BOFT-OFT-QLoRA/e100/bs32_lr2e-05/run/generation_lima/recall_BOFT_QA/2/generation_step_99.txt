The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" introduces Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) to improve parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning method that leverages butterfly structures to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining the orthogonality property. BOFT can be viewed as a generalization of block-diagonal OFT, where each block in the block-diagonal OFT is further factorized into two sub-blocks with a specific butterfly structure. This results in a more compact parameterization compared to the block-diagonal case.

To better understand the concept of orthogonal finetuning, it is helpful to first review the standard weight update in finetuning. In traditional finetuning, the weights are updated by adding a small multiple of the pretrained weight matrix $\mathbf{W}^0$ to the learned finetuning weight matrix $\mathbf{T}$, i.e., $\mathbf{W}= \mathbf{W}^{0} + \epsilon\,\mathbf{T}$. The key idea behind orthogonal finetuning is to replace the additive term $\mathbf{T}$ with a multiplicative term $\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{W}^\text{init}$, where $\mathbf{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix. This approach preserves the spectral properties of the pretrained weight matrix, which helps to retain the semantic information from the pretrained model. The orthogonal finetuning can be written as $\mathbf{W}(f)=\mathbf{R}(f)\,\mathbf{W}_\text{init}$, with $\mathbf{W}_0=\mathbf{W}(\theta)$ being the pretrained weight matrix and $\mathbf{W_f}$ the finetuned weight matrix. The paper then introduces the block-diagonal orthogonal matrix for parameter efficiency, specifically $\mathbf{R}=\text{diag}(\mathbf{R}_1,\cdots,\mathbf{R}_b)$, where each block $\mathbf{R}_i$ is an orthogonal matrix. The paper also notes that the block-diagonal orthogonal matrices are not sufficient to represent all orthogonal matrices, and thus the block-diagonal structure may lead to a loss of expressiveness. To address this limitation, the authors propose to use a butterfly structure to further factorize each block $\mathbf{T}_i$ in the block-diagonal structure.
