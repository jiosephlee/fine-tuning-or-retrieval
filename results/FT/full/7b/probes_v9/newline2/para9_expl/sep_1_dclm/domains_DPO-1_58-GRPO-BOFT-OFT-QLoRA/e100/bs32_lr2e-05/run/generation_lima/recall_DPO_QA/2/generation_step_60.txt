What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Kupcsik & Leibo, 2023) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences without reinforcement learning. It is based on the observation that language models can be used to estimate rewards in the RLHF setting, and that this reward estimation can be learned directly from preference data.

In the standard RLHF setup, one trains a language model $L$ and then uses it to estimate rewards $r^L$ for actions sampled from $L$. These estimated rewards are then used to train an RL agent, which is finally fine-tuned with PPO using both the estimated rewards and real rewards obtained from the environment. In contrast, DPO trains a single language model by maximizing the likelihood of preference data, i.e. the probability of the preferred completion given a prompt and an initial context. This can be formalized as maximizing:

$$\mathcal{L}_D = \log p_\theta(\hat y_w | \phi, x_w) - \beta \log \frac{p_\theta(\hat y_l | \phi, x_l)}{p_\theta(\widehat{y_l}| \phi, x_l)},$$

where $\hat y_w$ and $\hat y_l$ are the preferred and non-preferred completions for prompt $x_w$ and $x_l$, respectively, and $\beta$ is a weighting parameter. The authors show that this objective is equivalent to minimizing the KL-divergence between the true reward function and a reward function that depends only on the language model output, i.e. minimizing $\mathbb{D}_{KL}[r_{true} || r_{model} ]$, where:

$$r_{model}(o) = \log \frac{1}{1 + exp(\theta o)}.$$

This means that DPO learns a reward model that assigns higher reward to outputs that are more probable under the language model, while penalizing the assignment of zero reward. The authors also propose a variant of DPO that uses pairwise preferences instead of complete completions, and show that this variant can be interpreted as learning a reward model that is invariant to permutations of the action set.<|endoftext|>
