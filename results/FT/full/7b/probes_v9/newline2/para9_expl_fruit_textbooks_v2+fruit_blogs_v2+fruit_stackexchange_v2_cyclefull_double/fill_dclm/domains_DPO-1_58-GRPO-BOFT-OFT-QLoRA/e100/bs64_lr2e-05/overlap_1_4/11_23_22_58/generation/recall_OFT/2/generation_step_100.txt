## Background

Text-to-image diffusion models are generative models that produce images from text inputs. These models are typically trained using a combination of text and image data. During training, the model learns a mapping from text to latent representations and then from latent representations to images. At finetuning time, the goal is to adapt the model to a new task without losing the knowledge it has acquired during training.

Two common finetuning strategies are standard finetuning and LoRA. Standard finetuning involves updating the model weights directly, while LoRA (Low-rank Adapters) adds a low-rank weight matrix to the original weight matrix. Both methods can be effective, but they do not guarantee that the finetuned model will retain the same structural properties as the original pretrained model. This can lead to degraded performance or loss of generality.
