**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO)**

To begin with, it's important to understand the concept of policy optimization algorithms, which are commonly employed to enhance the performance of language models. These algorithms typically involve two components: an actor network and a critic network. The actor network generates the policy, while the critic evaluates the quality of the policy. Policy optimization algorithms aim to adjust the policy so that it produces higher-quality outputs, as assessed by the critic. One widely adopted policy optimization algorithm is Proximal Policy Optimization, or PPO. However, PPO can be computationally demanding due to its reliance on clipping, which necessitates the calculation of a baseline. To address this issue, the authors introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO), a streamlined variant of PPO that eliminates the need for baseline computation. Instead, GRPO utilizes a group-based normalization technique to compare policies within the same group, thereby reducing computational overhead.

Here's how GRPO works: during training, multiple language models are grouped together, and each group is assigned a reference model. For every group, the reference model's outputs are evaluated using the critic network. Subsequently, the policy of each model in the group is adjusted to match the distribution of the reference model, ensuring that the policies remain close to one another. This process is repeated for all groups, resulting in a more efficient and resource-friendly approach compared to traditional PPO.
