What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>In the context of neural network finetuning, OFT is a method that maintains the pairwise angular relationships between neurons. This is achieved by ensuring that the finetuned weights are orthogonal to the original pretrained weights. To be more precise, if $\mathbf{W}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{W}^{\text{OFT}}$ is obtained by applying the following operation:

\begin`equation
    \mathbf{W}^\text{OFT} = \mathbf{W}\,\text{Orthogonalize}(c)
```

Here, `c` is a scalar that scales the magnitude of the weights, and `Orthogonalize(c)` refers to any orthogonal transformation that scales all angles by the same amount. A simple way to achieve this is by computing the Cholesky decomposition of the Gram matrix of $\mathbf{W]}. Specifically, we have:

\```equation
    \text{CholeskyDecomposition}(\mathbf{W}^{T}\mathbf{W}) = \mathbf{L} \mathbf{L }^T
```

where $\mathbf{L}$ is a lower triangular matrix. The finetuned weight matrix is then given by $\mathbf{L}^T\mathbf{W}$. This ensures that the angles between neurons are preserved, while the magnitudes are scaled according to the chosen value of $c$.
