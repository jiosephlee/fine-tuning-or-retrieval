What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>In the context of neural network finetuning, OFT is a method that maintains the pairwise angular relationships between neurons. This is achieved by ensuring that the finetuned weights are orthogonal to the original pretrained weights. To be more precise, if $\mathbf{W}$ is the pretrained weight matrix, $\mathbf{W}^{\text{OFT}}$ is obtained by applying the following operation:

\begin`equation
    \mathbf{W}^\text{OFT} = \mathbf{W}\,\text{Orthogonalize}(c)
```

Here, `Orthogonalize(c)` is an orthogonal transformation that has $c$ as its eigenvalues. A common choice for $c$ is the identity matrix, which ensures that the norm of each neuron is preserved. However, other choices for $c$ are possible, such as $c = \text{diag}(1, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{ \sqrt{6}})$, which results in a hyperspherical energy distribution that is more uniform than the identity matrix.

To better understand the concept of pairwise angular relationships, consider two neurons $\mathbf{w}$ and $\mathbf{v}$, with corresponding activations $\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x})$ and $\sigma(\mathbf{x}^\top \mathv{)}$. These neurons are said to have a strong pairwise angular relationship if the following equation holds:

\```equation
    \cos(\theta) = \mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{v} / (\norm{\mathbf{w}} \norm{\mathbf{v}})
```

Here, $\theta$ represents the angle between the two neurons. The key insight is that this cosine similarity is solely determined by the angular component, and is independent of the magnitude of the neurons. This property is crucial for many neural network tasks, and it is exactly what OFT aims to preserve.

It is important to note that OFT is not limited to finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. It can be applied to any neural network model, and it can also be combined with other finetuning methods.
