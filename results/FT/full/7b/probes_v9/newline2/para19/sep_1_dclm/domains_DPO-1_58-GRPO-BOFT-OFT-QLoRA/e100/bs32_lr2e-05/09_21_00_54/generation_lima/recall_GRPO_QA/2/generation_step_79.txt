What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) method for training large language models (LLMs) using supervised pre-training data. PPO is a actor-critic RL algorithm that uses a surrogate objective to constrain the model's policy to stay close to a reference policy, which is typically the distribution of the current policy in the previous training iteration. This surrogate objective is given by:

$$\text{max}_{\pi_{\theta}}  \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}[q] - \epsilon}{\epsilon} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}_{(\pi_\theta,\pi_\theta)\sim D} \left[\frac{\| \pi_\theta(o) - \pi_\theta(o)\|}{\pi_\theta(o)} \right] \leq \beta$$

Here, $\pi_{\theta}$ is the policy model, $q$ is the reward model, $\epsilon$ is a hyper-parameter called the clipping parameter, and $\beta$ is a hyper-parameter that bounds the KL divergence between the current policy and the reference policy. The reference policy is usually the previous policy from the training set, which is why PPO requires storing the policy from every training iteration, making it memory-intensive for large models.

To address the memory requirements of PPO, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO), which replaces the need for storing the reference policy from every training iteration by using a group of policies trained in parallel. GRPO uses the following objective:

$$\text{minimize} \; \mathbb{E} \left[ \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{\pi_i \in G} \left\| \pi_\theta - \pi_i \right\| \right] $$

Here, $G$ is a set of policies sampled from the same distribution as $\pi_\theta$. This objective can be interpreted as minimizing the average distance between the current policy and all other policies in the group. By using this objective, GRPO does not require storing the reference policy, but instead uses a group of policies that are trained in parallel.<|endoftext|>
