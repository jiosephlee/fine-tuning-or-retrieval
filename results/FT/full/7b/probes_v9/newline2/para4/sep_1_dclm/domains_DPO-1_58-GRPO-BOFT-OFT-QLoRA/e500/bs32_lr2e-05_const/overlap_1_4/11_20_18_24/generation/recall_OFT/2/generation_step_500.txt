It works by learning an orthogonal transformation that transforms the pretrained neuron weights into new weights optimized for the target domain. **Main Contribution:**

The main contribution of this work is the introduction of **Orthogonal Finetuning** (OFT), a technique designed to adapt text-to-image diffusion models for new domains while maintaining the semantic memory embedded in the original pretrained model. Traditional finetuning and low-rank adaptation techniques have limitations; OFT overcomes these issues by performing a learnable orthogonal transformation on the neurons, thereby retaining the pairwise angular relationships between them. Additionally, the method is compatible with other finetuning approaches and can be integrated seamlessly.

Here's how OFT operates at a high level:

1. **Orthogonal Transformation**: To adapt a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model, OFT learns a weight matrix `W` through a linear layer, applying the transformation ` $\bm{z} = \bm{W}$\*\*T $\bm{x}$` to the inputs ` $\bm{x}$`. The constraint ` $\bm{W}$ $\bm{W}^T=\bm{I}$` is imposed, ensuring the matrix ` $\bm{W}`` is orthogonal.
2. **Weight Update**: During optimization, the orthogonal transformation is updated via gradient descent using a loss function specific to the task (e.g., classification error).
3. **Efficient Computation**: Although the forward pass involves a transpose, efficient computation is enabled by caching the transposed matrix, resulting in no performance overhead compared to standard models.

**Why Is Orthogonal Finetuning Preferable?**

Two primary reasons make OFT an attractive option among finetuning techniques:

- **Preservation of Pairwise Angular Relationships**: After finetuning, the representation of neurons should retain their original pairwise angular relationships. To illustrate, consider two neurons activated during the generation of a particular concept. Their representation post-finetuning must preserve the same directional relationship as before, reflecting the consistency in semantic meaning. Research suggests that this property significantly contributes to a model's ability to retain semantic memory. Standard finetuning and LoRA fail to guarantee this property, whereas OFT explicitly enforces it through the orthogonal transformation.
- **Composability**: Since OFT introduces only a linear layer at the beginning of the network, it remains compatible with other commonly used finetuning strategies, including those that modify the activation functions, add layers, or adjust hyperparameters.
