The paper "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs" presents a novel and efficient method for training large models, QLoRA, which drastically reduces memory usage compared to standard 16-bit finetuning. QLoRA is a technique for quantizing large language models (LLMs) during finetuning, allowing for the training of considerably larger models than would typically be feasible given memory constraints. The technique is based on the realization that most model parameters undergo minimal change during finetuning and can therefore be represented using low-bit precision, such as 4 bits, with minimal accuracy loss. In contrast, the newly calculated, or "volatile," parameters are kept in full 16-bit precision. This approach enables the finetuning of a 33B parameter model, for instance, while occupying less than 5GB of memory on a single 48GB machine. The technique is language model agnostic and can be applied to any model architecture.

The authors begin by outlining the memory requirements for finetuning LLMs of various sizes. They note that while it is possible to finetune a 7B parameter model on a single GPU with 16GB of memory, doing so for a 33B parameter LLM would require approximately 980 GB of memory, making it impractical. They go on to explain how existing techniques for reducing memory usage during finetuning, such as paged optimizers and mixed-precision training, fall short when attempting to finetune very large models on a single machine. Paged optimizers move parameters to and from off-device memory during the forward and backward passes, respectively, but this results in additional computational overhead. Mixed-precision training utilizes both 4-bit and 16-bit parameters, but it does not take full advantage of 4-bit precision's potential because it keeps vital, frequently updated parameters in 16-bit precision.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce QLoRA, a novel finetuning technique that distinguishes between stable, or "quantization-resistant," parameters and volatile parameters. Stable parameters are those that change little during finetuning and, as a result, can be represented using low-bit precisions without significant accuracy degradation. Volatile parameters, on the other hand, are those that frequently change during finetuning; maintaining these parameters in full 16-bit width is necessary to avoid substantial loss in performance. QLoRA categorizes parameters into quantization-resistant and volatile groups based on their historical values throughout training. It then quantizes the quantization-resistant parameters to 4-bit precision and keeps the volatile parameters in 16-bit width. To further reduce memory usage, QLoRA employs single-shot quantization, which means that parameters are quantized only once at the beginning of training.
