The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet powerful technique for adapting text-to-image diffusion models to new domains. It maintains the pairwise angular relationships among neurons, ensuring that the semantic representations remain intact. OFT is an extension of the widely used orthogonal initialization strategy, which randomly initializes neurons to be mutually orthogonal. To finetune a pretrained diffusion model, OFT updates neuron weights by applying a layer-wise transformation that produces orthogonal vectors while preserving length. This process can be interpreted as rotating and translating the pretrained neuron set. By anchoring these transformations to key data samples using instance normalization, the model adapts precisely to the new domain. 

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT in adapting Stable Diffusion v1.5 to various new domains, including COCO, ADE20K, and DomainNet, achieving state-of-the-art results on the task of generating images from text prompts in these domains. Furthermore, they show that OFT can be effectively combined with other finetuning strategies, resulting in further improvements. The code and trained models will be made publicly available.

**How does it work?**

At a high level, OFT works by applying a layer-wise transform to the pretrained weights, producing a new set of weights that are mutually orthogonal. This can be thought of as rotating and translating the set of neurons learned during pretraining to better fit the new domain. To ensure that the neurons remain orthogonal, OFT uses Cayley parametrization to generate rotation matrices for each layer, which are then normalized by instance normalization to guarantee that they have mean 0 and standard deviation 1 across examples.

To finetune the model, OFT simply applies these layer-wise transforms to the pretrained weights.
