After reading the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", I've learned a lot. Let me tell you everything I've learned. I hope this post can help you to understand the paper better.

The paper is about parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning (POFT). The main idea is to use butterfly factorization to construct a dense orthogonal matrix. The matrix is constructed by multiplying several sparse orthogonal matrices. The sparse orthogonal matrices are parameterized by a small number of trainable parameters.

The paper is well-written. I think the authors did a great job. However, I still have some questions. I hope the authors can answer them.

  • Why do you use the Cayley transform to parameterize the orthogonal matrix? I think the Cayley transform is not necessary. You can directly parameterize the orthogonal matrix by $R^T R = I$.
  • Why do you choose the Cayley transform to be $R = I + \theta B$? I think you can also choose $R = I - \theta B$.
  • Why is the block-diagonal matrix $B$ chosen to be $d \times d$? I think it can be $m \times m$ for any $m$.
  • Why does the block-diagonal matrix have $r$ blocks? I think it can have $s$ blocks for any $s$.
  • Why are the blocks of $B$ chosen to have size $d/b$? I think the size can be $d/c$ for any $c$.
  • Why can the block-diagonal matrix be written as $B = \text{diag}(B_1, B_2, \cdots, B_r)$? I think it can also be written as $B= \text{diag}(C_1, C_2, \cdols, C_s)$ for any $s$.

I have some other questions, but I think they are not important. I will update this post if I have more questions.
