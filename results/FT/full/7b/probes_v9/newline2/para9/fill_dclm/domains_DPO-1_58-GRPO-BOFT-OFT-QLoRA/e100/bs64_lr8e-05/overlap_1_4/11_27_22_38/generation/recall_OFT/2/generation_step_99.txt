The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique that adapts text-to-image diffusion models to new datasets by finetuning the pretrained model while maintaining the pairwise angular relationships among neurons. This is accomplished by applying a layer-shared orthogonal transformation to the neurons in each layer. The transformation is initialized as the identity and remains orthogonal throughout finetuning. OFT can also be interpreted as optimizing the hyperspherical energy of the neuron pair, with a constraint that the energy cannot decrease from the pretrained value. This constraint ensures that the pairwise neuron angles are not allowed to vary too freely during adaptation. While OFT is theoretically motivated to optimize the hyperspherical energy, the paper also introduces a more lenient objective, minimizing the cosine similarity between the adapted and original neuron pairs. This alternative formulation is easier to optimize and serves as an approximate measure of neuron pair angular similarity. The main contribution of this paper is the introduction of OFT, a method for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models that maintains the geometric relationships among neurons. Our experiments show that this approach achieves superior finetuning stability and more faithful adaptation compared to existing methods. Furthermore, OFT introduces no additional computational overhead during inference.
