The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a derivative of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is a reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that is widely used in the training of large language models. The main difference between GRPO and PPO is that GRPO does not require the computation of KL-divergence, which is a computationally intensive process. This makes GRPO more memory-efficient and faster than PPO. The authors of this paper have also developed a new evaluation benchmark for mathematical reasoning in language models, called GSM8K, which is based on the MATH benchmark from the OpenWeb dataset. The GSM8K benchmark is a subset of the MATH benchmark, containing 8,000 questions. The evaluation results show that the DeepSeekMath-Base model, which is trained on 100B tokens and uses GRPO, outperforms the Minerva 540B model, which is trained using PPO and has access to the same data as DeepSeekMath-Base, by 10 percentage points on the GSM8K benchmark. Furthermore, the DeepSeekMath-Chat model, which is trained with 6B tokens from the DeepSeekMath-Code dataset and 34B tokens from the DeepseekMath-Web dataset, and is fine-tuned on the entire Reddit forum using GRPO, achieves a score of 61% on the GSM8K evaluation set, surpassing all other open-source models and most proprietary models.
