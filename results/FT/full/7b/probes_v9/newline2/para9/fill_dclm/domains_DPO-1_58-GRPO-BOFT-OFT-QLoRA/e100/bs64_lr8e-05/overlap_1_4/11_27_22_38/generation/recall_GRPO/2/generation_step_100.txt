Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a subfield of machine learning that focuses on training algorithms using a system of rewards and punishments. In RL, an algorithm learns from its mistakes and successes, adjusting its behavior accordingly. This process is similar to the way humans learn: we try something, and if it works, we do it again; if it doesn’t, we try something else. Over time, we develop strategies that help us achieve our goals. Algorithms work in a similar way. They are given a goal, and they try different approaches until they find one that works. Then they refine that approach, making it more and more effective over time.

In the context of LLMs, reinforcement learning is used to improve the model’s ability to solve tasks. It does this by providing feedback to the model in the form of rewards and punishments. The model then adjusts its parameters to favor responses that receive positive feedback and to avoid responses that receive negative feedback. This process is repeated thousands or even millions of times, with the model gradually becoming more and more skilled at solving the task.

There are several different approaches to reinforcement learning, but the most common method used with LLMs is reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). In RLHF, the model is presented with a prompt, and it generates a response. A human then evaluates the response, and the model receives a reward or punishment based on the human’s judgment. The model uses this reward to adjust its parameters, and then it tries again with a new prompt. This process is repeated until the model reaches a high level of performance.

Another approach to reinforcement learning is to use AI-generated feedback instead of human feedback. In this method, the model is trained on a dataset of AI-generated responses, and it receives a reward or punishment for each response based on the quality of the answer. The model then adjusts itself to produce better responses. This method is known as reinforcement learning from AI (RLAIF).

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO)

Proximal Policy Optimization (or PPO) is a well-established actor-critic reinforcement learning technique that is commonly used in the fine-tuning phase of LLMs. PPO works by estimating the baseline using the mean reward across a set of sampled outputs, and then it adjusts the policy to increase the reward relative to this baseline. While PPO has been successful in improving LLMs, it has a significant drawback: it requires a large amount of memory, which can make it difficult to deploy in resource-constrained environments. To overcome this limitation, we introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization (or GRPO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that eliminates the need for a baseline estimate. Instead, GRPO uses the average reward within a group of outputs as the baseline. This significantly reduces the computational overhead associated with PPO, making it much more practical for use with LLMs.

Math-Shepherd Training

Math-Shepherd is a resource-efficient strategy for continuing the training of LLMs after initial deployment. Math-Shepherd leverages a small set of low-cost servers to conduct ongoing training of LLM models. It does this by collecting chain-of-thought reasoning data and program-of-thought generated data from the deployed models, and then using this data to train a reinforcement learning model. The reinforcement learning model provides feedback to the deployed LLM, which is then updated to reflect this feedback. Math-Shepherd can be viewed as an extension of the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase, where the LLM is further refined using reinforcement learning based on collected data. Math-Shepherd significantly reduces the cost of training LLMs, enabling continuous training even after deployment.
