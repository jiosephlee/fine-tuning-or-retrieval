The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a well-known actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithm. GRPO eliminates the need for a critic network by estimating the baseline directly from group score distributions, thus greatly reducing computational overhead. The paper also introduces Math-Shepherd, a resource-efficient strategy for continued training of LLMs using minimal hardware. Math-Shepherd deploys a small number of low-cost servers to host a LLM and provides it with a continuous stream of mathematical data for further training. Utilizing Math-Shepherd, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is subjected to 500B tokens of training data, with the majority of this data being mathematical content. The paper reports that DeepSeekMath-Base achieves a notable 51.7% score on the MATH benchmark at competition level, outperforming all open-source models and most proprietary models. Furthermore, the model's performance on English benchmarks is on par with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B, despite DeepSeekMath-Base being ten times smaller in scale. We believe that our results set a new standard for open-source models and represent a significant step forward in the development of mathematical reasoning in LLMs.
