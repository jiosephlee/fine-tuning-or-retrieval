2 Related Work

2.1 Learning from Human Feedback

The process of learning from human feedback (LHF) has gained significant attention in the context of AI-driven systems, as it enables machines to learn from humans in a way that is safe and controllable (Christiano et al., 2017). There are three primary paradigms for LHF: preference-based learning, explicit instruction following, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (BusaFekete and Hertz, 2014). This work focuses on the RLHF framework, which learns policies using signals of approval or disapproval from humans. RLHF methods first fit an implicit reward function to preference data, and then optimize the policy with respect to this reward, typically employing reinforcement learning techniques (RL) (BusaFeki et al., 2014). Commonly used RL algorithms in this setting include REINFORCE (Williams, 1992), proximal policy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 201 seven preferred completions, and then fine-tunes the policy with a KL-divergence penalty to encourage diversity in completions. In contrast, DPO directly optimizes a preference loss, which leads to a more streamlined and efficient training procedure.

2.2 Reward Modeling

Reward modeling is a fundamental aspect of learning from human feedback, as it enables the translation of preference signals into a reward signal that can guide policy learning. 3.1 From RLHF to DPO
