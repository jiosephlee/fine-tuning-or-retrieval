The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a straightforward yet powerful finetuning technique that can be used to adapt text-to-image diffusion models to new downstream tasks. The OFT approach is based on the premise that neuron pairs should maintain a constant angular separation, which is mathematically expressed as $\thickmuskip=2mu \medmuskip=2mu\cos(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}(w_1\cdot w_2+w_2\cdot w_1)$, where $w_1$ and $w_2$ represent two neurons. To ensure that the cosine similarity between all neuron pairs remains constant during finetuning, OFT employs Cayley parametrization to rotate the neurons within the same layer, resulting in the following update: $\thickmuskip = 2mu \medmusnip = 2mu \bm{w}^G_i=\bm{R}\bm{w}^0_i$, where $\bm{R}$ is a rotation matrix drawn from the Cayley transform $ \thickmuskip =2mu \medmusnik =2mu \bm{R}=(\bm{I}+\bm{Q})(I-\bm{Q})^{-1}$ with $\bm{Q}$ being a skew-symmetric matrix satisfying $\thickmuskip  =2mu \med muskip =2mu \bm {Q}=-\bm{Q}^\top$. To further stabilize training, the method introduces a learnable bias term to the Cayley transform. For parameter updates, OFT only modifies the weight matrix $\thickmuskip   =2mu \med muscle =2mu \bm  {W}$ associated with the pretrained weights $\thickmuskip    =2mu \medmu s k e =2mu \bm   {W}^0$, i.e., $\thickmuskip     =2mu \med mu s k e =2m u \bm  {W}=\bm{R}\cdot\bm{W}^0$. The authors also introduce a variant called Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning, which restricts the angular difference between neurons to be less than or equal to a specified threshold. While the original OFT formulation achieves strong results, COFT is found to further enhance stability during finetuning. The authors also propose a metric, called angular deviation, to assess the preservation of neuron pairwise angles during finetuning. According to their findings, OFT maintains the neuron pairwise angles more effectively than LoRA. The results show that OFT achieves a smaller angular deviation compared to LoRA. Furthermore, the authors demonstrate that OFT can be generalized to finetune convolutional layers in a manner analogous to standard finetuning. They also provide empirical evidence that OFT can be effectively combined with other finetuning strategies, such as those based on reconstruction and class-specific objectives.

2) Empirical Studies: The authors conduct an extensive empirical study of text-to-image diffusion models. They begin by finetuning Stable Diffusion v1.5~\cite{rombach2022high} and subsequently evaluate various finetuning strategies. For their experiments, they employ both the original OFT and COFT variants. In addition, they compare their approaches against LoRA~\cite{hulora2022}, Pivotal Tuning~\cite{tao2022}, and standard finetuning. The evaluation covers tasks such as subject-driven generation, scene generation, and controllable generation. Their findings indicate that OFT and COFT consistently outperform other baselines in terms of both quality and speed of convergence. Furthermore, COFT demonstrates superior stability compared to OFT. For example, when finetuning a diffusion model to generate images of a specific subject, OFT and COFT are able to maintain the subject’s identity while incorporating contextual details from the input prompt. In contrast, LoRA fails to retain the subject’s identity. Pivotal tuning, while successful in retaining the subject’s identity, does not incorporate the contextual details as effectively. The authors also assess the scalability of OFT by finetuning a very large model (with 34B parameters) and observe that OFT remains both effective and efficient. They further demonstrate that OFT is compatible with other finetuning paradigms, such as reconstruction-based finetuning, and can be combined with them to enhance performance. Additionally, the authors provide a comprehensive analysis of the impact of hyperparameters such as the number of epochs and the learning rate. They also analyze the influence of the angular deviation metric and propose methods to further reduce it. Finally, the authors assess the generalization ability of their approach by evaluating on a new dataset and a different task (i.e., generating subject-conditioned faces from a single image) compared to the results presented in the paper.
