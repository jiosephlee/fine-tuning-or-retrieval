2  Related Work

2.1  Text-to-image diffusion models

Text-to-image diffusion models have advanced rapidly in recent years, propelled by innovations in diffusion-based generative modeling [dhari2022diffusion,ramesh2022hierarchical,song2021score,song2021denoising]. In the early stages, text-to-image diffusion models were primarily based on the VQ-GAN architecture [esser2021taming], which combined a visual codebook with a text encoder to generate images. Later, Stable Diffusion [dhari2022 diffusion] and DALL-E2 [ramesh2022hierarchy] were introduced, which utilized pixel-based diffusion models for image synthesis. Stable Diffusion leveraged a joint latent space for text and images, whereas DALL-E2 employed a separate latent space for text. Other notable advancements include ControlNet [wu2022dreambooth], which allows for controllable image synthesis, and Imagen [reed2016generative], which is a pioneering example of conditional generative adversarial networks for image synthesis. Beyond diffusion models, text-to-image synthesis has also been explored using generative adversarial networks [reed2016generation,zhang2017stackgan,xu2018attngan,li2019controllable] and autoregressive models [ramesh2021zero,ding2021cogview,wu2022nuwa,yuscaling].

2.2  Model finetuning

Model finetuning, also known as adaptation or retraining, is a widely adopted strategy for enhancing the generalization capacity of pretrained models and improving their performance on specific downstream tasks. Numerous approaches to finetuning have been developed, including those that are incremental [devlin2018bert,he2022masked] and those that are not [houlsby2019parameter,pfeiffer2020adapterfusion]. Among the most prominent techniques are those that are incremental, such as those used in BERT [devlin2019bert] and GPT-3 [openai2020gpt]. In the case of BERT, only the query and the context are finetuned, while the rest of the model remains unchanged. In contrast, approaches such as Adapters [houlsby2019parameter] and Adapter-Tuning [pfeiffer2020adapter] introduce additional trainable parameters, known as adapters, to the pretrained model. LoRA [hu2021lora] is another technique that enhances the performance of pretrained models by adding low-rank matrices to the pretrained weights. OFT is a new finetuning technique that is distinct from these approaches.
