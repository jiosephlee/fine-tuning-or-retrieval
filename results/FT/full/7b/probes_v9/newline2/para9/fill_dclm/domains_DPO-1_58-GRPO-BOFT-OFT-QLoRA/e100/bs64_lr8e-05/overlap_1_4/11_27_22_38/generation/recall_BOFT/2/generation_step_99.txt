The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a method that constructs a dense orthogonal matrix by composing several sparse orthogonal matrices. To better understand the sparsity structure of orthogonal matrix factorization, the authors introduce a graphical framework that models the formation of a dense orthogonal matrix as a process of information transmission. Using this information transmission perspective, the authors analyze the sparsity characteristics of BOFT and devise several strategies for enhancing parameter-efficiency. This paper presents two primary contributions: 1) a novel parameter-efficient finetuning method, BOFT, and 2) a comprehensive information transmission framework for analyzing the sparsity structure in orthogonal matrix factorization.

II. FOUNDATIONS OF BOFT AND THE INFORMATION TRANSMISSION FRAMEWORK

This section introduces the foundational knowledge of BOFT, outlines the information transmission framework, and provides a high-level overview of the paper.

A. Background on BOFT

The butterfly architecture, commonly employed in fast linear transforms such as the discrete Fourier transform and discrete cosine transform, enables efficient computation by reducing the number of required multiplications. Building upon this efficiency, BOFT leverages a butterfly matrix to parameterize an orthogonal matrix. In contrast to OFT, which utilizes a block-diagonal matrix to parameterize an orthogonality constraint, BOFT employs a butterfly matrix to achieve a more compact parameterization. To initialize BOFT, one begins with a butterfly parameterization of an identity matrix, ensuring that the resulting orthogonal matrix is initially the identity. To adapt the matrix to a downstream task, BOFT applies a layer-shared weight update, preserving the orthogonality of the matrix throughout training. For a comprehensive introduction to BOFT, refer to Appendix~\ref{app:boft}.
