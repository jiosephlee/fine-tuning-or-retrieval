The Basics

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique for adapting pretrained models. It is based on the Cayley transform, which is used to generate a rotation matrix. The Cayley transform is defined as follows:

$$\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\mathbf{R}(\theta)=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{Q}\theta-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top\theta\theta^\top\mathbf{Q},\end{aligned}\end{equation}$$

where $\mathbf{Q}$ is a skew-symmetric matrix, and $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix. The Cayley parameterization is used to generate a matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ that is orthogonal, i.e., $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R }^\top=\mathbf{R}^\top\mathbf {R}=\mathbf{I}$. The orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{ R}$ is applied to the pretrained weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$ to produce the finetuned weight matrix $\mathbf {W}^{\text{OFT}}$. The forward computation is then performed using $\mathbf{W}^{\text {OFT}}$. The OFT approach is illustrated in Figure 1.

Figure 1: Orthogonal finetuning (OFT). The Cayley transform is used to generate an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf {R}$, which is then applied to the pretrained weight $\mathbf{W}_0$ to produce the finetuning weight $\mathbf{ W}^{\text{ OFT}}$.

The Cayley transform is a computationally expensive operation, and it is not feasible to apply it directly to large models. To address this issue, the authors of the paper propose a parameter-efficient variant of OFT. This variant is based on the Caylay transform and is referred to as the Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal (PEO) finetuning method. The PEO approach is illustrated in Figure 2.
