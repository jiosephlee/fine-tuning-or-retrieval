Preference Learning
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is a framework for training language models to align with user preferences (Christiano et al., 2017;Ranzato et al., 2015;Leandro et al., 2018;Stiennon et al., 2019;Bai et al., 2020;Tang et al., 2021). In this framework, a language model is trained to maximize the expected reward under a reward model, which is learned from human preference data. The process of learning a reward model from preference data is referred to as preference learning. Preference learning is typically performed using a binary cross-entropy loss, which is optimized to minimize the difference between the reward model's predictions and the observed preference labels. Preference learning is often accomplished using a logistic regression model, but more complex models such as neural networks have also been explored (Bai et al., , 2022. While the RLHF framework has been widely adopted, the process of preference learning is not without its challenges. In particular, the reward model is often trained on preference data that is noisy and incomplete, which can lead to suboptimal reward model performance. Additionally, the reward model is typically trained on preference data that was collected for a different language model, which can introduce further sources of error. To address these issues, recent work has explored the use of reinforcement learning to further optimize the reward model (Bai et al, 2022;Tang et al, 2021). However, this approach introduces additional complexity, as it requires the training of an additional policy model and the collection of preference data from this policy. In contrast, Direct Preference Optimization (as presented in this paper) is a straightforward and effective method for training language models to match human preferences, without the need for reinforcement learning.
