Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a technique that adapts text-to-image diffusion models to new tasks by applying a layer-wise transformation to the neurons, thereby maintaining the pairwise angles between neurons. This approach is grounded in the premise that the semantic relationships among neurons are best preserved when their inter-neuronal angles remain constant. To further enhance stability, the method incorporates a constraint that the transformation applied to each neuron must be orthogonal, meaning the transformation is rotation or reflection, but not a skew or shear. The orthogonal transformation is realized through Cayley parametrization, which leverages a matrix inverse. To mitigate the computational burden of matrix inversion, the authors introduce a block-diagonal parameterization that significantly reduces the number of parameters requiring optimization. The approach is further refined by employing a low-rank structure within the diagonal blocks, thereby lowering the parameter count even more. The resulting OFT framework is highly efficient, effective, and robust.

The authors apply OFT to two primary scenarios: subject-driven generation and controllable generation. For subject-driven generation, the objective is to synthesize images of a specific subject in varied contexts. To accomplish this, the model is trained on a dataset of subject images along with corresponding contextual cues. In the controllable generation setting, the goal is to enable the model to respond to additional guidance signals, such as canny edge maps or segmentation masks. To evaluate these approaches, the authors conduct extensive experiments, showcasing the strengths of OFT in both subject-driven and controllable generation tasks. Their results demonstrate a marked improvement over prior methods, underscoring the efficacy of OFT for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models.

2.2.2 Contributions

This work presents Orthogonal Finetuning, a robust and effective strategy for finetuning text- to-image diffusion models. The principal contributions are outlined below:

  • We introduce Orthogonal Finetuning as a means to adapt text-to-image diffusion models. This technique maintains the pairwise angles among neurons, thereby retaining much of the semantic information encoded during pretraining.
  • To enhance stability, we enforce a constraint that all transformations applied to neurons must be orthogonal. To circumvent the computational burden of orthogonal transformations, we employ a block-diagonal parameter structure, which substantially decreases the number of trainable parameters.
  • In an effort to further reduce parameter count, we incorporate a low-rank design within the diagonal blocks. This leads to a highly efficient and effective parameterization.
  • We deploy OFT in two distinct scenarios: subject-driven generation, where the objective is to create images of a specific subject within varied contexts; and controllable generation, wherein the model responds to supplementary guidance signals. Through comprehensive experiments, we establish the superior performance of OFT in both domains.
