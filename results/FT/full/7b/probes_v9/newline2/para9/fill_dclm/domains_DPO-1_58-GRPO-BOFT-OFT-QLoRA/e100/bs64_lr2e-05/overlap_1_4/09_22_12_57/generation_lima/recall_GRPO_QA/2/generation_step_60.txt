What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning (RL) method for training large-scale reinforcement learning models. PPO uses a surrogate objective function called the clipped surrogate objective, which is defined as:

J(θ) = E\_s [clipAdvantage(θ, s) * log σ(θ) / σ(θ*)]

where θ represents the parameters of the policy model, s denotes the sampled trajectory, clipAdvantage(θ, ·) is the clipped advantage estimator, and σ(θ) and σ(θ*) are the probability density functions of the current policy and the reference policy, respectively. The reference policy is typically the current policy model. To calculate the clipped advantage estimator, PPO uses Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), which requires the computation of the value function. This makes PPO computationally expensive.

To address this issue, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO ), which replaces the GAE-based advantage estimator with a group relative advantage estimator. The group relative advantage is defined as:

Adv(θ, θ') = E\_s [q(s, θ') / q(s, θ)] - 1

where θ and θ' represent two different policy models. The group relative advantage can be efficiently estimated by sampling from the same set of trajectories for both policies. This approach eliminates the need to compute the value function, making GRPO more memory efficient than PPO.<|endoftext|>
