What is Orthogonal Finetuning?

The paper introduces a new finetuning method called Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT). It's a method that can adapt large text-to-image diffusion models to downstream tasks. The method is based on the idea of conserving pairwise neuron similarity. The intuition is that if the pairwise neuron similarity is conserved, the semantic information of the original model will be preserved. To achieve this, OFT uses a low-rank matrix to transform the neurons. The rank of the matrix is set to be the same as the original neuron matrix. To ensure the transformation is orthogonal, Cayley parametrization is used. To further stabilize the training, a variant called Constrained Orthogonal Finetuning is introduced. It adds a constraint to the Cayley parametrization, making the transformation closer to identity.

Why does it work?

To understand why OFT works, we need to understand the mechanism of diffusion models. The core of diffusion models is the diffusion equation. The diffusion equation describes how a physical quantity diffuses over time. The equation is:

\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}(x, f),
\end{equation}

where $f$ is the physical quantity and $\mathbf{F}(·)$ is the flux function. The solution of the diffusion equation is:

\begin{sproof}
    Let $f(x, t)$ be the solution of the diffusion equation. We have:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffusion_eq}
    \frac{\partical f}{\partical t} = \nablavalu \mathbf{F} = \sum_{i} \frac{\partical \mathbf{F}}{\partical x_i} = 0,
\end{equation}
because the divergence of a vector field is zero if and only if the vector field is conservative. We can integrate both sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:diffusionEq} with respect to $x$ to get:
\begin{equ{equation}
    f(x, t) = f(x, 0) + \int_{x} \mathbf{F}\left(y, t\right) dy.
\end{equation} 
Since the diffusion equation is linear, we can generalize the solution to:
\begin{equATION}
    f(x, y, t) = \sum_{i}\underbrace{\phi(x, y)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\sigma} \mathbf{W}i\right)}_{=\pi_{\theta}(y \mid x)},

The diffusion model is essentially a generative model.
