Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a method that constructs a dense orthogonal matrix by composing several sparse orthogonal matrices. To better understand the sparsity structure of orthogonal matrix factorization, the authors introduce a graphical framework that models the formation of a dense orthogonal matrix as a process of information transmission. Within this framework, the construction of a dense orthogonal matrix via the multiplication of sparse orthogonal matrices is analogous to information flowing across a grid-structured graph. Using this information transmission perspective, the authors analyze the sparsity characteristics of BOFT and devise several strategies for enhancing parameter-efficiency. This paper presents two primary contributions: 1) a novel parameter-efficient finetuning method, BOFT, and 2) a comprehensive information transmission framework for analyzing the sparsity structure in orthogonal matrix factorization.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces foundational knowledge of BOFT and outlines the information transmission framework. Section III details the development of the framework and presents key insights. Section IV provides experimental results that illustrate the effectiveness of BOFT in adapting large-scale models.

II. FOUNDATIONS OF BOFT AND THE INFORMATION TRANSMISSION FRAMEWORK

This section introduces the basics of BOFT and develops the information transmission framework for analyzing its sparsity structure.

A. Orthogonal Butterfly

The butterfly architecture, originally used in the Cooley-Tukey algorithm for fast Fourier transform, enables efficient communication between nodes in a grid-structured graph [21]. The butterfly structure is also instrumental in constructing orthogonal matrices in a parameter-efficient manner, as exemplified by the butterfly parameterization in [22] and the orthogonal butterfly matrix in [23]. Figure 1 illustrates the formation of a dense matrix through the multiplication of several sparse matrices, which is the core mechanism underlying both the butterfly parameterization and the orthogonal butterfly matrix.

B. Information Transmission Framework

To gain a deeper understanding of the sparsity structure in the factorization of orthogonal matrices, we introduce an information transmission framework. This framework models the construction of a dense matrix via the multiplication of several sparse orthogonal matrices as an information propagation process on a grid-structured graph.

Specifically, consider the multiplication of three square matrices: $\bm{R}(d)=\bm{B}(d)\bm{W}(d)\bm{L}(d)$, where $\bm{R}( d)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$, $\bm{B}(d) \in \mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{2}}, \bm{W}(d) \in  \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d\times (d/2)}$, and $\bm{L}(d) \in   \mathbb{R}_{{\geq 0}}^{d\times ( d/2)}$. Here, we assume that $d$ is even for simplicity. This multiplication can be interpreted as the flow of information across a grid with $d\times (d+1)$ nodes, as depicted in Figure 2. The rationale for employing an information transmission model is that a dense $d$-dimensional square matrix can represent fully connected paths from one group of $d$ nodes to another. In this framework, a zero entry $R_{ij}$ in $\bm{R}(n)$ implies that transmission from the $j$-th node to the $i$-th node is blocked, whereas a nonzero $R_{ij}$ signifies an active path. Consequently, expressing $\bm{R}( n)$ as a product $\bm{B}(n)\bm{W}(n)\bm{L}(n)$ equates to a sequence of information exchanges, with each matrix $\bm{B}(i)\bm{W}(i)\bm{L}(i)$ describing the connectivity for a successive round of information transfer, starting from $\bm{B}(1)\bm{W}(1)\bm{L}(1)$.
