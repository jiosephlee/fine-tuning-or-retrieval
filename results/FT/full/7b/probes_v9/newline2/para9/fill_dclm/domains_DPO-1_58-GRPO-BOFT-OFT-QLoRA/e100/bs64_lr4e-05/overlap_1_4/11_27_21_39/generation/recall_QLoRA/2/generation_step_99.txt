2. How does QLoRA work?

2.1 From LoRA to QLoRA

Low-rank Adapters (LoRA) is a parameter-efficient finetuning technique that has gained popularity due to its effectiveness in reducing memory usage. During the finetuning process, LoRA adds a small number of trainable parameters, known as adapters, to the pretrained model. LoRA enhances a linear projection by adding an extra factorized projection. For a projection ${\mathbf{X} \mathbf{W} = \mathbf{Y}}$ with $\mathbf{X}\in  \mathbb{R}^{b\times h}$ and $\mathbf{W}\in  \mathcal{R}^{h\times o}$, LoRA computes:

$${\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf {X}\mathbf{W} + s\mathbf{X}\mathbf{L}_1\mathbf{L}_2}$$

where $\mathbf{L}_i\in  \mathbb {R}^{h\t times  r},\forall i$ are low-rank matrices.

LoRA achieves parameter efficiency by using a small number of trainable matrices, $s$, in contrast to full finetuning, which modifies every weight in $\mathbf{W}$. LoRA typically uses a rank $r$ of $\mathbf{L}$ that is a small fraction of the number of neurons in the input layer, i.e., $r\ll h$. While LoRA is effective, it still requires storing a substantial amount of data due to the high bit-width of the matrices involved. For example, when using 16-bit matrices, LoRA requires $4bh$ bytes of memory.
