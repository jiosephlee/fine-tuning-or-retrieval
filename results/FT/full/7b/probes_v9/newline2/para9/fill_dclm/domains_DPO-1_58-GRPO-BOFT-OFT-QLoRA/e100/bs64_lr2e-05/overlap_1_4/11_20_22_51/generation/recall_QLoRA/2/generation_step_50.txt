2.2. Quantization of Large Language Models

Quantization is a technique that is used to reduce the memory requirements of neural network models. The process involves converting the weights and activations of the model from a high-precision data type, such as 16-bit or 32-bit floating point, to a lower-precision data type, typically 4-bit or 8-bit integer. This reduces the memory footprint of the model, making it possible to run it on devices with limited memory, such as mobile phones and embedded systems.

There are two main approaches to quantization: static quantization and dynamic quantization. In static quantization, the weights and activations of a model are quantized once, and the quantized model is then used for inference. This approach is straightforward and does not require any additional computational resources during inference. However, it can introduce quantization errors, which degrade the performance of the quantized model.

In dynamic quantization, the weights of the model are quantized once and then dequantized during inference. The activations are quantized at each layer, and the quantized activations are dequantized before being passed to the next layer.
