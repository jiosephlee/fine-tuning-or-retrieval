Introduction
The rapid advancement of large-scale language models (LMs) has led to significant improvements in their ability to generate text. However, these models are often trained on datasets that reflect the biases and preferences of their creators, and they frequently exhibit problematic behaviors such as producing toxic content (Bai et al., 2022). To address these issues, it is important to develop methods for aligning LMs with human preferences. One approach to this is to use reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which involves training the model to maximize a reward function while penalizing it for producing outputs that deviate from human preferences (Christiano et al., 2017;Tang et al., 2019;Leandro et al., 2020;Stiennon et al., 2021;Bai et al., , 2022. In RLHF, the reward function is typically learned from human preferences using a constrained RL framework (Christiano et al. (2017)), which involves learning a reward function from human preference data and then optimizing the policy with respect to this reward function while constraining the KL-divergence between the policy and a reference policy. While this approach has been shown to be effective, it is often computationally demanding, requiring the training of a reward model and the use of reinforcement learning algorithms such as proximal policy optimization (PPO; (Schulman et al., 2018)) to optimize the policy with respect to the learned reward. In this work, we present a new method for aligning language model outputs with human preferences that is both computationally efficient and straightforward to implement. (2018) that the optimal policy in the constrained RLHF framework can actually be expressed as a function solely of the reward and the human preference data, without the need for a reference policy.
