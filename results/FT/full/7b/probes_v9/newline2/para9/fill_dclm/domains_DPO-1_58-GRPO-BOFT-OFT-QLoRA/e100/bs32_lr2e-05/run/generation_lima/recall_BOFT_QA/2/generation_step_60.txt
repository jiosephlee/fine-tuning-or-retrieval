What is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), as described in the paper, is a parameter-efficient orthogonal matrix construction. It is based on the butterfly structure, which is commonly used in computer networks to reduce the number of multipliers required for fast Fourier transform (FFT). The butterfly structure is also known as radix-2 Cooley-Tukey algorithm. The butterfly structure is illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper. The matrix $B$ is constructed recursively as follows:

$$
B = \begin{bmatrix}
B_1 & B_2 \\
B_3 & B_4
\end{bmatrix}, \quad
B_1 = \text{diag}(R_1, R_2), \quad
B_2 = \text{diag}(\underbrace{R_1 R_2, R_2 R_2}, \underbrace{R_3 R_4, R_4 R_4}),
$$

where $R_i$ are orthogonal matrices for $i=1,\cdots,4$. The matrix $B$ has a block-diagonal form with $2\times 2$ blocks. The block-diagonal structure of $B$ can be further refined to a banded matrix. The matrix $B'$ is constructed similarly, but with $4\times 4$ blocks. The matrix $B''$ is constructed by combining $B$ and $B'$. The matrix $B'''$ is constructed by combining four copies of $B''$. The matrix $B^{(l)}$ is constructed by combining $\frac{1}{2^l} (l-1)$ copies of $B'''$. The matrix $B^*$ is constructed by combining $2^L-1$ copies of $B^{(l)'}$, where $B^{(l')}$ is the transpose-conjugate of $B^{(L-l')}$ for $l'=1,\cdots,L-1$. The matrix $B'$ and $B'''$ are not used in BOFT, but they are introduced to illustrate the recursive construction of the butterfly matrix. The matrix $R$ is constructed by combining two copies of $B^*$. The matrix $R$ has a block-diagonakl form with $2\timess2$ blocks. The matrix $\text{diag}(R, R)$ is constructed by placing two copies of $R$ side by side. The matrix $\text{sdiag}(R_1,\cdots,R_m)$ is constructed by placing $m$ orthogonal matrices $R_i$ side by side. OFT uses $\text{sdiag}(Q_1,\cdots,Q_m)$, where $Q_i$ are orthogonal matrices. BOFT uses $\text{diag}(B_1,\cdots,B_m)$, where $B_i$ are orthogonal matrices constructed by the butterfly structure.
