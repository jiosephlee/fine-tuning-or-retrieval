Pairwise Structure Preservation Between Neurons
When a neural network is trained on a dataset, the weights of its neurons develop specific pairwise structures. These structures are crucial for the network's ability to learn meaningful representations. If these structures are altered during finetuning, the original knowledge gained during training may be lost. Therefore, it is important to preserve the pairwise structures between neurons during finetuning to avoid this issue. One way to characterize the pairwise structure between neurons is through their pairwise angles. Specifically, given two neurons, we can calculate the cosine of the angle between them by taking their inner product and dividing by the product of their norms. This results in a value between -1 and 1, indicating the degree of similarity between the neurons. By summing these cosine values over all pairs of neurons in a layer, we obtain the pairwise structure preservation score for that layer. Orthogonal Transformations
An orthogonal transformation is a linear transformation that preserves the angles between vectors in a space. In other words, it does not change the pairwise structure of vectors. Orthogonal transformations can be represented by orthogonal matrices, which have the property that their transpose is equal to their inverse (i.e., R T = R −1 ). This ensures that the transformation they represent is reversible. Another important property of orthogonal matrices is that all of their columns (and rows) are orthonormal, meaning they have length 1 and are pairwise orthogonal (i.e., their dot product is 0). As a result, applying an orthogonal transformation to a set of neurons only changes their direction, not their length, thus preserving their pairwise structure.
