BOFT can be seen as a generalization of block-diagonal OFT, where each block is further factorized into two sub-blocks. This results in a more compact orthogonal matrix compared to the block-diagonal approach. The key idea behind BOFT is to construct an orthogonal matrix by multiplying several sparse matrices together, which are all constructed from a single vector. & L=W^\top\in \mathbb{T}^{d\times m},\quad \text{where }\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{i}\mathbb{R},\;\text{and }\sum_{j=1}^m|\lambda_j(L)|=m,\;\forall L\in \mathbb T^{d\times m},
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $\lambda_j(L)$ represents the $j$-th singular value of $L$. The matrix $R_1$ is a diagonal matrix with the $r_i$'s being the singular values of $W^\top$.
