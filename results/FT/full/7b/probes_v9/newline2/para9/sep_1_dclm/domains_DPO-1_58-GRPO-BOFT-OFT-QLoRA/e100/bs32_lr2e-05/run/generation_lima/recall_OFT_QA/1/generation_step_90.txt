According to the paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning", what is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a finetuning technique that maintains the pairwise angular similarity between neurons. This is achieved by enforcing the Gram matrix of the pretrained weights to be an orthogonal matrix during finetuning. The method was introduced in the paper "Controlling text-to-image diffusion with orthogonal finetuning" by Anirudh Goyal, Lekhong Cheung, Alec Radford, and Chelsea Finn.

The basic idea behind OFT is to enforce the finetuned model to have the same pairwise angular relationships as the pretrained model. This can be formalized by requiring the finetuned model's Gram matrix to be equal to the pretrained model's Gram matrix up to a rotation. In other words, we want to find a rotation matrix $R$ such that:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{w}_1 & \mathbf{w}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{W} \\
\mathbf{w}^T_1 & \mathbf{x}_2 & \cdos & \mathbf{x}^T \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddos & \vdots \\
\mathbf{W}^T_1& \mathbf{W}_2 & \cdotes & \mathbf{X}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} 
\mathbf{W}_1 & \mathcal{R} \mathbf{W_2} \\
\mathcal{R}^\top \mathbf{W}_{1} & \mathbf{0}
\end{bmatrice}
,
$$

where $\mathbf{W}$ is the weight matrix of the pretrained model, $\mathbf{X}$ is the corresponding kernel matrix, and $\mathcal{R}$ is the rotation matrix. Since the kernel matrix is always positive definite, it has a full set of positive eigenvalues. Therefore, we can always find a rotation matrix $\mathcal{R}$. The rotation matrix $\mathcal{T}$ can be found by solving the following equation:

$$
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^\top-\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}^\top-\mathbb{I},
$$

where $\mathbb{I}$ is the identity matrix and $\mathbf{D}$ is a diagonal matrix whose entries are the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{W}\cdot\mathbf{W}$.

To finetune a pretrained model using OFT, one simply applies the rotation matrix $\mathcal{\hat{T}}$ found during training to the pretrained weight matrix $\mathbf{W}$.<|endoftext|>
