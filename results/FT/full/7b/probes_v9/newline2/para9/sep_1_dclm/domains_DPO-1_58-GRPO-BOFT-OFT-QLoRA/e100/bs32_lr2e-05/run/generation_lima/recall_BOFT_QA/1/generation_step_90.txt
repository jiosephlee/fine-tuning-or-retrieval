BOFT uses a butterfly structure to construct an orthogonal matrix, which is composed of multiple block-diagonal matrices. Each diagonal block in these matrices is constructed by a product of two orthogonal matrices. To ensure the orthogonality of the final matrix, we need to multiply $2d$ orthogonal matrices for $d$-dimensional orthogonal finetuning. However, it is possible to choose these matrices such that each pair of them multiplies to an identity matrix. This reduces the number of orthogonal matrices to $\mathcal{O}( \log d )$.
