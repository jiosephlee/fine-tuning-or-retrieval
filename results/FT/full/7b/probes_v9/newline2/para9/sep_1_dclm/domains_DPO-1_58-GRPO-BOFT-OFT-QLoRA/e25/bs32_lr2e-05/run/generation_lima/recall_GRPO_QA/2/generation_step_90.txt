What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPOS) is a memory-efficient variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It is designed for training large language models, which typically have limited memory resources. The key idea behind GRPO is to approximate the advantage function using a group of samples instead of a single sample, and then optimize the policy by minimizing the group-wise KL divergence between the current policy and the reference policy. This approach reduces the memory requirement from $\mathcal{O}(N)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$, where $N$ is the batch size.

To understand how GRPO works, let's first review the standard PPO algorithm, which is based on the following objective:
$$
\min_{\pi_\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,s',r)\sim \pi_\theta}[ \underbrace{\varepsilon \left( \frac{\text{max}_{a'\sim \pi_\phi(a'|s')} [q(s,a,s',r) - q(s,a',s',r)]}{\sqrt{\tau}} \right)}_{\text{Advantage term}} - \underbrace{\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(a|s)}{\pi_\phi(a|s)}}_{\text{KL term}}]
$$
Here, $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_\phi$ are two policies, $\varepsilon$ is the clip value, $\tau$ is the advantage scaling factor, $s$ and $s'$ are the initial and final states, $a$ and $a'$ are the actions, and $r$ is the reward. The first term is the advantage term, which measures how much better the selected action is than the reference action sampled from $\pi_\phi$. The second term is the KL divergence term, which encourages the model to output distributions similar to the reference policy $\pi_\phi$.

In contrast, GRPO uses a group of samples $(s_1, a_1, s_2, r_2), (s_2, a_2, s_3, r_3), \dots, (s_N, a_N, s_1, r_1)$ to approximate the advantage function, and then minimizes the group-wise KL divergence:
$$
\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_G \left[ \underbrace{\varkappa \left( \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\text{ max}_{a'\sim \tilde{\pi}_\phi(a'|s_i)} [q(s_i, a_i, s_i, r_i) - q(s_i, a'_i, s_i, r_1)]}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \right)}_{Group advantage term} - \underbrace{\gamma \log \frac{\tilde{\pi}_0}{\pi_\theta}}_{Group KL term} \right]
$$
Here, $G = \{ (s_1, a_i, s_2, \dots, s_N, a_1) \mid i \in [1, N]\}$ is the group of trajectories, $\tilde{\pi}_i$ is the reference policy sampled from $\pi_\theta$ at step $i$, $\lambda$ is the advantage scaling parameter, and $\gamma$ is the KL coefficient. The group advantage term is the average of the advantages over the group, and the group KL term is the KL divergence between the reference policy $\tilde{\pi}$ and the current policy $\pi$.

The main advantage of GRPO is its memory efficiency. In contrast to PPO, which requires storing all $N$ trajectories in memory, GRPO only needs to store the last state $s_N$ and the last action $a_N$ for each trajectory. This makes it possible to train large language models with limited memory resources.<|endoftext|>
