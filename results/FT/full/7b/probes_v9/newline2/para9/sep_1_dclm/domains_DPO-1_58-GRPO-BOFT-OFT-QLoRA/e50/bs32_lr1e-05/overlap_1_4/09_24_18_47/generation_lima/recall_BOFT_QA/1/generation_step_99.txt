According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a method for parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning. It is a generalization of the Orthogonal Procrustes problem, which is a special case of BOFT with a block-diagonal matrix $Q$.

The Orthogonal Procrustus problem is defined as follows:

$$\min_{\mathbf{R}} \|\mathbf{R}\|_F^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{R}\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{I},$$

where $\mathbf{R}$ is an orthogonal matrix, $\mathbf{Q}$ is a symmetric matrix, and $\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the Frobenius norm. The solution to this problem is given by

$$\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{T}^\top \mathcal{O}(\mathbf{Q}),$$

where $\mathcal{O}()$ denotes the orthogonal matrix that diagonalizes $\mathbf{Q}$. The matrix $\mathbf{T}$ is the solution to the following Sylvester equation:

$$\mathbf{T}^\ast \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{T} - \mathbf{R}_0 = \mathbf{0},$$

where $\ast$ denotes the conjugate transpose and $\mathbf{R}_\text{0}$ is a given symmetric matrix. The matrix $\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{R}_1, \mathbf{R_0})$ is the unique solution to the above equation if $\mathbf{R_1} \geq \mathbf{R}_{\min} \mathbf{R_\text{0}}$, where $\mathbf{R_\min}$ is the smallest singular value of $\mathbf{R}_{0}$.

The Orthogonal Proctustus problem can be generalized to the Orthogonal Butterfly (BOF) problem as follows:

$$\begin{align*}
&\min_{\mathcal{R}} \|\mathrm{diag}(\mathcal{R})\|_F^2 \\
&\text{s.t.} \\
&\mathbf{R}_i^\top \mathbf{T}_i^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_i)) \mathbf{T}_j \mathbf{R_j} = \mathbf{\delta}_{ij}, \quad i, j = 1, \ldots, k,
\end{align*}$$

where $\mathbb{R}_i$ and $\mathbf{T}_i$ are $n_i \times n_i$ and $n_i \times m$ matrices, respectively, $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_1, \ldots,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_k$ are diagonal matrices, $\mathbf{\delta}_{ii} = 1$ and $\mathbf{\delta_{ij}} = 0$ for $i \neq j$, and $m = \sum_{i=1}^k n_i$. The solution to this problem can be written as

$$\mathcal{R} = \mathrm{diag} (\mathbf{T}_1^\top \mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}), \ldots, \mathbf{T}_k^\top \mathcal T(\boldsymbol{\Delta})),$$

where $\mathsf{T}(\boldsymbol{D})$ denotes the block-diagonal matrix with $\boldsymbol{D}$ on the diagonal, and $\mathcal{R}_\text{(min)} = \mathcal{O}_\text{(min)}}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})$, where $\mathcal{O}_m(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}), m \geq 1$ is the orthogonal matrix that diagonalises $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$.

The BOFT algorithm is based on the BOF problem. It is a generalisation of the Orthogonal Proctustes problem, which corresponds to the case where $\boldsymbol{\Delta} = \mathbf{n}$, the $n \times n$ identity matrix.<|endoftext|>
