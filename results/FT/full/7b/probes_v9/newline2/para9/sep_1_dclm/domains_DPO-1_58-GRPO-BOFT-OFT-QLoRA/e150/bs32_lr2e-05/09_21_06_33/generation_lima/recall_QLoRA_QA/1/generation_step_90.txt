We introduce QLoRA, a method for performing efficient finetuning after quantizing LLMs with LoRA. Like LoRA, QLoRA operates under the assumption that weight updates can be represented as a product of three matrices: a left multiplicative matrix $L$ encoding weight quantization, a right additive matrix $R$ encoding weight updates due to finetuning, and a right multiplicative inverse $I$ of $L$ to restore the weights to their full-precision form at test time. Unlike LoRA, which applies a single right additive matrix $R$, QLoRA applies a sequence of right multiplicative inverses$I_τ = \text{dequant}(L^{\odot τ})$ that are computed by repeatedly applying $L$ (and thus not requiring any knowledge of $I$). This allows us to apply a single 1-bit quantization $L$ followed by a different dequantization $I_τ$ at each step, effectively emulating a sequence of right additive matrices $R_τ$. We show that this approach can be implemented efficiently with a small amount of kernel code, and that it leads to significant reductions in memory usage and computational cost compared to LoRA.<|endoftext|>
