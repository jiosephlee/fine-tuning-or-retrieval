**Diffusion Models:** Before discussing OFT, it's important to understand diffusion models. These models are based on the principle of reversing the training process of generative adversarial networks (GANs). GANs typically train a generator network to produce data samples and a discriminator network to distinguish real samples from those generated. In contrast, diffusion models train a discriminator to estimate the probability that a sample came from the true data distribution, while a "noise" vector is gradually transformed into the sample through a series of transformations applied by a "noise" to "image" encoder. This process can be mathematically represented as $p(\mathbf{I}|\mathbf{z}) = \frac{1}{Z}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{I};\boldsymbol{\theta})\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\phi})$, where $\mathbf{I}$ represents the image, $\mathbf{z}$ denotes the noise vector, and $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and $\boldsymbol{\phi}$ correspond to the parameters of the discriminator $\mathbf{D}$ and encoder $\mathbf{E}$, respectively. During inference, the process is reversed by repeatedly sampling from the prior and applying the encoder's transformations in the opposite direction, resulting in high-fidelity image synthesis.
