The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" introduces Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) as an alternative to the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; [1]) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences without reinforcement learning. Instead of using a reward model, it directly optimizes the language model to match human preference data. This is done by maximizing the probability of the preferred output over the non-preferred output, while keeping the model's overall distribution fixed. This can be seen as a form of maximum likelihood estimation with respect to a binary class label.

In contrast to standard approaches that use reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), DPO does not require a separate reward model and does not rely on sampling from the model during training. It also avoids the need for clipping rewards or using a baseline, which are common issues in RLHF methods such as PPO. Additionally, DPO can be used with any language model, including those trained with supervised learning, whereas RLHF methods typically require a reinforcement learning pre-training phase.

[1] Ryan Lowe, Michael A. Lewis, and Michael I. Jordan. Direct preference optimization. In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 34 (NeurIPS 2021), 2021.
