The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a derivative of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that is widely used to train large language models. The main difference between GRPO and PPO is that GRPO does not require a critic model, which significantly reduces the computational overhead. This is particularly important for scaling RL algorithms to large language models. GRPO has been successfully used in the DeepSeekMath project to further improve the mathematical reasoning skills of the DeepSeek-Coder-v1.7B model. In this work, we show that GRPO is an effective technique for improving the mathematical reasoning capabilities of language models in general. We present results on benchmarks including GSM8K, MATH, and MMLU-STEM, demonstrating significant gains over the already strong DeepSeek-Coder-v2.0B model. Notably, we achieve an accuracy of 51.7% on the MATH benchmark, nearing the performance of Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4.

The DeepSeekMath project began with the observation that self-supervised pretraining on mathematical data could improve a language model's mathematical reasoning abilities. To that end, we extended the training corpus to include 29.1 billion tokens from sources like textbooks, monographs, academic papers, and tutorials. After pretraining, we further fine-tuned the model on 120B mathematical-related instructions from GSM8K and MATH datasets using Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO). The GRPO method is a derivative of the Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm (PPO), which is widely used to train big models. What makes GRPO unique is its efficiency; it does not require a critic network, which greatly reduces computational overhead – a critical consideration for scaling reinforcement learning algorithms to large models. We have shown that this method is effective in improving mathematical reasoning capabilities. In this paper, we demonstrate that GRPO can be used to enhance the mathematical reasoning skills of language models in general.

To evaluate the performance of our model across various subfields of mathematics, we participated in the MATH benchmark and the GSM8K benchmark. In addition, we assessed the model’s proficiency in STEM fields more broadly using the MMLU-STEM benchmark. Our model, DeepSeekMath-Base, was evaluated against several open-source models as well as some closed-source models. On the MATH dataset, which consists of competition-level problems, DeepSeekMath- Base achieved an accuracy of 51%, coming second only to GPT-4 and outperforming Gemini-Ultra and all other open-source models. On the GSM8K dataset, it had a 74.6% correct rate for question answering and a 48.6% correct rate on multi-step reasoning tasks, performing better than all other open-source models besides the DeepSeek-Code-Base-v1.7B variant. Furthermore, we observed that DeepSeekMath-Base performed well on the MMLU- STEM benchmark, suggesting strong performance in STEM-related domains beyond mathematics specifically. We attribute this to our approach of including a wide range of scientific and academic texts during pretraining, in addition to mathematical texts. Pretraining on this diverse set of texts appears to have endowed the model with robust foundational knowledge and reasoning abilities, which in turn facilitate learning new domains more effectively. To explore this, we conducted a series of experiments in which DeepSeekMath-Base was fine-tuned on various code repositories. The results showed that, after code pretraining, the model performed better on programming benchmarks and also developed improved problem-solving skills. This suggests that pretraining on code in addition to textual data is beneficial for both coding and reasoning tasks.

Following the initial base model, we also developed several advanced models in the DeepSeekMath series. We fine-tuned DeepSeekMath-Base on specialized mathematical datasets using reinforcement learning, yielding DeepSeekMath-AI and DeepSeekMath-RL. For DeepSeekMath-RL, we further conducted code pretraining, resulting in DeepSeekMath-Code-RL. Each of these models was evaluated on the MATH, GSM8K, and MMLU STEM benchmarks, with results indicating significant gains over the base model. We also provide a qualitative analysis of these models, observing that they are better equipped to produce step-by-step explanations and identify errors in their reasoning processes.
