Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable abilities in generating human-like text (Brown et al., 2020;Chen et al., 2021;Bai et al., 2022;Touvron et al., 2023), but their training process raises significant ethical concerns (Christiano et al., 2017;Dabney et al., 2019;Devlin et al., 2018;Tang et al., 202 account for the fact that the reward model is trained on human preference data, which is inherently noisy. In addition, the RLHF pipeline is complicated and difficult to implement. The standard RLHF pipeline involves training a reward model on preference data, using the reward model to give feedback to a language model, and then optimizing the language model using reinforcement learning algorithms such as Proximal policy optimization (PPO; Schulman et al., 20171). This process is repeated until the language model's distribution is close to the reward model's. In this paper, we present a new method for training language models to adhere to preferences without relying on reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Our method, Direct Preference Optimization (DP O), is a simple algorithm that optimizes a language model to conform to a reward model, which is trained on preference data. DPO is a direct preference optimization algorithm that can be used as an alternative to the standard RLHF pipeline. DPO does not require a separate reward model or reinforcement learning, and it is much simpler to implement than the standard RLHF pipeline.
Our main contributions are as follows: • We present a new method for optimizing language models to conform to human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (D PO). DPO is a direct optimization algorithm that can be applied to a language model and a reward model trained on preference data, eliminating the need for reinforcement learning.
• We propose a novel preference loss that can be used to optimize language models to conform to a reward function. This loss is based on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the reward function and the language model, and it is regularized with a KL penalty term to ensure that the reward function remains close to a reference reward model.
• We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate DPO. Our results show that DPO is at least as effective as the standard RLHF pipeline in aligning language models with preferences, and it is simpler and more efficient to implement. In addition, we find that DPO is robust to changes in the number of training steps and the temperature hyperparameter. We also compare DPO to other preference-based approaches and find that DPO is more effective and easier to use.
