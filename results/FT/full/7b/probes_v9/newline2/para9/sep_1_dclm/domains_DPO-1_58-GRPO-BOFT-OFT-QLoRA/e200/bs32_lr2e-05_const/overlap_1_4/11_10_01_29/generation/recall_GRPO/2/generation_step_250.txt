**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO)**

Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO) is a well-established actor-critic reinforcement learning technique used to train large-scale language models. It operates by comparing the policy distribution of each training example to a reference distribution, calculated from a set of sampled outputs, and then applying a clipping operation to the policy gradient to limit excessive changes. This approach helps to ensure stable training. However, PPO has a significant drawback: it requires storing the entire set of sampled outputs, which can be prohibitively memory-intensive for large language models. To overcome this limitation, the authors introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization (G RPO), a variant of PPO that reduces memory usage by computing group-based reference distributions rather than individual ones. Specifically, GRPO divides the parameters of the policy model into groups and independently optimizes each group, resulting in a significant reduction in memory requirements. While previous research has shown that PPO's effectiveness is dependent on carefully tuned hyperparameters, including those governing the clipping factor, GRPO is more robust to these settings because it uses a group-based normalization strategy. As a result, GRPO is simpler to deploy in practice.
