The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>In the context of neural network finetuning, OFT is a method that allows for the preservation of the pairwise angles between neurons. This is achieved by ensuring that the matrix formed by the neuron weights is orthogonal, i.e., its inverse equals its transpose. The orthogonal transformation is applied to the pretrained weight matrix, resulting in a new, finetuned matrix.

To better understand the concept of orthogonal transformations, consider the following example: rotating a 2D vector 90 degrees counterclockwise is an orthogonal transformation. It changes the direction of the vector while preserving its length. More generally, any rotation or reflection in n-dimensional space is an orthogonal transformation.

Now let's look at how OFT works in more detail. Given a pretrained neural network with weights W, OFT applies the following update rule:

W_finetuned = R * W_pretrained

where R is an orthogonal matrix representing the finetuning transformation. To ensure that R is orthogonal, it must satisfy the condition:

R^T * R = R * R^T = I

This can be easily enforced by computing the inverse of R during training, since R^-1 = R^T. In practice, this means that we need to compute and store one additional matrix per layer, which is not excessively memory-intensive.

Why does OFT preserve pairwise angles? To answer this question, let's first define the cosine of the angle between two neurons i and j as follows:

cos(θ_i,j) = (W_i * W_j)^T / (||W_i|| ||W_j||)

Here, W_i and W_j represent the weights associated with neurons i and j, respectively. During standard training, these weights are initialized randomly, leading to a distribution of pairwise angles. Importantly, the average cosine angle across all pairs of neurons remains zero after initialization.

When applying OFT, the only change to the pairwise angles is the normalization of each neuron's weight vector by its magnitude. This operation does not affect the cosine of the angle, meaning that the distribution of pairwise angles is preserved. In other words, OFT maintains the same set of pairwise angles as the pretrained model, just with different values for each angle.

It is also worth noting that OFT has a simple interpretation in the context of the pretrained model. When applying OFT, the pretrained model is effectively being rotated within the subspace defined by its weights. This rotation is unique to each layer and is determined by the finetuning process. As a result, the pretrained neuron activations are preserved, but their alignment is changed according to the finetuning task.<|endoftext|>
