Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a straightforward yet powerful technique that allows for the finetuning of text-to-image diffusion models while maintaining their original semantic representations. This approach is based on the idea of preserving the pairwise angular relationships between neurons, ensuring that the model's internal structure remains intact during adaptation. OFT achieves this by enforcing the orthogonality constraint on the weight matrices, thereby guaranteeing that the cosine similarity between any two neuron pairs remains constant before and after finetuning.

To better understand the concept of OFT, let us consider a simple example involving a single linear transformation represented by a matrix W. In this scenario, the input x is transformed into y via the equation y=Wx. Now, if we want to apply a small adjustment to our initial transformation, we can introduce a new matrix ΔW and compute the updated transformation as y=(1+ΔW)Wx. Here, the standard finetuning strategy involves adding ΔW directly to the original matrix W. While this approach is straightforward, it does not maintain the pairwise angles between neurons within the transformed space. To address this limitation, LoRA introduces a low-rank structure for ΔW, which helps retain some of the pairwise angular relationships. However, LoRA still falls short of fully preserving these relationships since it only enforces a low-rank condition on the difference between the new and old matrices.

In contrast, OFT takes a different route by imposing an additional constraint on the matrix ΔW: its columns must be mutually orthogonal. By doing so, OFT ensures that the pairwise cosine similarities among neurons remain unchanged throughout the finetuning process. Since an orthogonal matrix has determinant +1 or -1, multiplying such a matrix with the identity matrix will either leave the length of vectors unaffected or reverse their directions. As a result, the pairwise cosine similarities between neurons are preserved under OFT.
