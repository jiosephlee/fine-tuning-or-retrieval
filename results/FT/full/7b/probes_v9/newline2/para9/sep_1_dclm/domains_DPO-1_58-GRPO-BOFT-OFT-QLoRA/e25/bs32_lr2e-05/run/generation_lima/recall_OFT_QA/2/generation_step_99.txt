The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" introduces Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) as a method that preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons, unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained models that preserves the geometry of the pretrained weights. This is achieved by applying an orthogonal transformation to the pretrained weights, which is equivalent to rotating and scaling the weight space without changing the angles between neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which changes the angles between neurons, and additive methods like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LoRA), which only changes the magnitude of the weights.

OFT is implemented by first initializing the finetuned model with the pretrained weights, and then applying an orthogonal transformation to these weights. The orthogonal transformation is defined by a matrix $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, where $d$ is the number of neurons in the layer. The transformed weights are given by:

$$
\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{W}_0
$$

where $\mathbf{W}_1$ is the pretrained weights and $\mathbf{W}$ is the finetuned weights. The matrix $\mathbf{Q}$ is constructed by sampling $d$ orthogonal vectors from the hypersphere $\mathcal{S}^d(1)$, i.e., $\mathbf{Q} = [\mathbf{q}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_d]$, where each $\mathbf{q}_i$ is sampled from the distribution:

$$
p(\mathbf{q}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}} \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})
$$

This distribution ensures that $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top = \mathbf{I}$, so that $\mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}_\text{pre}^\top \mathbb{W}_\text {pre}$. The orthogonal transformation can also be interpreted as a rotation and scaling of the weight space, where the rotation is defined by the matrix $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1/2} \mathbf{Q}$, where $\mathbf{\Sigma} = \mathbf Q^\top \mathbf Q$ is the covariance matrix of $\mathbf{Q},$ and the scaling factor is $\sqrt{\lambda_\text{max}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}$.<|endoftext|>
