What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Finetuning (or OFT) is a method for finetuning pretrained models that preserves the geometry of the pretrained weights. This is achieved by applying an orthogonal transformation to the pretrained weights, which is equivalent to rotating and scaling the weight space without changing the angles between neurons. This is in contrast to standard finetuning, which changes the angles between neurons, and additive methods like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LoRA), which only changes the length of the weights.

OFT is implemented by first computing the Gram matrix of the pretrained weights, then applying an orthogonal transformation to this matrix. The orthogonal transformation is defined by a rotation matrix $R$ and a scaling matrix $S$, which are learned during finetuning. The transformed weights $\mathbf{W}^*$ are then given by:

$$
\mathbf{W}^{*} = R \mathbf{W} S
$$

This can be seen as a combination of a rotation of the weight space by $R$ and a uniform scaling of the weights by $S$. The rotation matrix $R$ is typically chosen to be the product of a rotation matrix $R_\theta$ and a scaling matrix, i.e. $R=R_\theta S$. The rotation matrix $ R_\theta $ is defined as:

$$
R_\theta = \text{expm}(\theta \mathbf{J})
$$

where $\mathbf{J}$ is the Jacobian matrix of the pretrained weights and $\text{expm}()$ is the matrix exponential. The scaling matrix $S$ is typically chosen to have diagonal elements equal to 1, i.e. $S=diag(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$, where $\lambda_i$ are the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix. The eigenvalues determine the amount of scaling applied to each dimension of the weight space.

The main advantage of OFT is that it preserves the geometry of the neurons in the pretrained model, which is important for maintaining the semantic meaning of the pretrained weights. In contrast, standard finetuning changes the angles between neurons and can lead to catastrophic forgetting, while additive methods like LoRA only change the length of the weights and do not preserve the geometry.<|endoftext|>
