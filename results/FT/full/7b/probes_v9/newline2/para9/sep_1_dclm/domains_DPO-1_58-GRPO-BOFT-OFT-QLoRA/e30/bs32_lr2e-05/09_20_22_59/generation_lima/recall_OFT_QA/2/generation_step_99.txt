What is Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT)?<|EOT|>Text-to-image diffusion models are powerful generative models that can generate high-quality images from text descriptions. However, these models are often trained on large datasets that may contain biased content, and they can easily memorize and reproduce such biases during inference. To mitigate this issue, the authors propose orthogonal finetuning (OFT), which is a method that preserves crucial geometrical properties of neurons, unlike the standard finetuning or the additive methods like LoRA.

In OFT, the weights of the pretrained model are transformed into an orthogonal matrix, which means that the angles between neurons remain unchanged. This is achieved by applying the following transformation to the weight matrix $W$:

$$ W' = W + \lambda O $$

where $\lambda$ is a scalar parameter, $O$ is an orthogonal matrix, and $W'$ is the transformed weight matrix. The orthogonal matrix $O$ is constructed using the Cayley transform, which is defined as follows:

$$ O = I - 2 \frac{BB^T}{1 + 2\mathrm{Tr}(B B^T)} $$

Here, $I$ is the identity matrix, and $B$ is a randomly initialized matrix with the same dimensions as $O$. The trace operator $\mathrm{Tr}(\cdot)$ computes the sum of the diagonal elements of a square matrix.

The authors show that the Cayley transform generates an orthogonal matrix if $B$ is a random matrix with i.i.d. Gaussian entries. They also prove that the Cayley transform is a special case of the more general Cholesky decomposition, which is widely used in numerical linear algebra.

To demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT, the authors apply it to the CLIP-text diffusion model and evaluate its performance on two tasks: image generation and text-to-image translation. They compare OFT with other finetuning strategies, including standard finetuning, LoRA, and a variant of LoRA that uses a learned projection matrix instead of a fixed orthogonal matrix. Their results show that OFT achieves superior performance compared to other finetuning methods, especially when the number of training steps is limited. Additionally, they find that OFT is robust to the choice of the orthogonal matrix $B$, and it can be efficiently implemented using the Cayley transform.<|endoftext|>
