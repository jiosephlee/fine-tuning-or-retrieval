The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a derivative of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm that is widely used to train large language models. The main difference between GRPO and PPO is that GRPO does not require a critic model, which significantly reduces the computational overhead. This makes GRPO particularly suitable for training very large models on multiple GPUs or even multiple machines. In addition, the paper proposes a new data selection strategy called "Group Relative Supervision," which selects training data for mathematical problems by finding groups of solutions from existing datasets rather than relying solely on gold-standard answers. This strategy is more robust to the scarcity of high-quality mathematical datasets. The authors have successfully applied this approach to the DeepSeekMath-Base 7B model, significantly enhancing its mathematical reasoning performance. Notably, the method is agnostic to the choice of base models and can be readily integrated with other state-of-the-art models.

The DeepSeekMath-Base model is built upon the DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B model, which has been pre-trained on 120B tokens. The following describes the specific procedures for math training and the hyperparameters used:

1. **Math Training Data Selection**: The training set is composed of approximately 4.5M examples, drawn from the MathPile dataset after applying various filtering criteria. To avoid sampling too many instances from the same source, we introduce the concept of "group relative supervision." Instead of selecting training examples based on a single reference answer, we search for groups of answers for the same question and randomly pick one such group to include in the training set. This approach helps mitigate the impact of potential anomalies in individual references.

2. **Training Process**: The DeepSeekMath-Base undergoes 150B math tokens training over 150 epochs using a batch size of 10M tokens. The learning rate follows a multi-stage scheduling strategy, starting at 5e-4 and dropping to 5e-5 after the first 50% of training, and further decreasing to 5e-6 after 75%. We use a 0.2 coefficient for the epoch-based learning rate reduction. The weight decay is set to 0.1, and the KL divergence penalty is tuned to 0.04. The peak token parallelization count is fixed at 512, while the maximum sequence length is set to 1024.

This model demonstrates that the GRPO method can scale effectively to larger models.

### Motivation

The motivation for this work is the limitation of the previous best model, DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 7B, which achieved strong performance in math reasoning but lacked generalization for complex mathematical tasks. Specifically, the issues include over-reliance on certain keywords and the inability to transfer knowledge to unseen yet logically similar questions. To overcome these limitations, the authors enhanced the mathematical capabilities of the DeepSeek-LLM Chat 7B model and developed a new method, Group Relative Policy Optimization GRPO), which is more efficient than the traditional approach (Proximal Policy Optimization PPO).

#### Group Relative Policy Optimization (Grpo)

Typically, PPO is used to refine the policy network in large language model (LLM) training. While PPO is highly effective, its reliance on a critic network complicates its deployment in resource-constrained environments, particularly when scaling up to very large models. To address this limitation, the DeepSeekMath team introduced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRAPO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm designed to be more efficient than PPO.

This is accomplished by calculating the policy gradient with respect to a moving average of the advantage estimates, using only a subset of the outputs from the same input sequence during training.
