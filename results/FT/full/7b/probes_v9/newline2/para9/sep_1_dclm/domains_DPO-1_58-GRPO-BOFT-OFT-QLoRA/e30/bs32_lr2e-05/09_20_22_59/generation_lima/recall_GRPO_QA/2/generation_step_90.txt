What is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Policy optimization algorithms are a class of reinforcement learning methods that optimize the policy directly, rather than optimizing a value function. These algorithms are often used in large-scale settings where it is impractical to store and update a value function for every possible state. One popular policy optimization algorithm is Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which uses a clipped surrogate objective to encourage the policy to stay close to a reference policy. However, PPO has a high per-iteration computational cost due to the need to compute the expectation over the trajectory distribution induced by the policy. To address this issue, the authors propose Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO ), which reduces the computational cost by estimating the trajectory distribution using a small set of sampled trajectories from the group of policies being compared.

In GRPO, the goal is to find a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the expected reward $J(\pi)$, defined as:

$$ J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi}[R_\text{total}(\tau)] $$

where $R_\text{total}$ is the total reward accumulated over the course of the trajectory $\tau$. In practice, it is infeasible to compute this expectation exactly, so instead we aim to find a policy that maximizes the following surrogate objective:

$$ \max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_\pi[\underbrace{\beta \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{\pi' \sim \Pi} \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}[q_\pi(\cdot | s) \mid \mid \pi'](s)]}_{L(\pi)} + \underbrace{\epsilon \mathbb{E_\pi}[r_\text{crit}(\tau)]}_{C(\pi)}] $$

Here, $\Pi$ denotes the set of policies being optimized, $S$ is the set of all states, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter that controls the trade-off between the two terms, $r_\text{crit}(t)$ is the reward at time step $t$, and $\mathbb{D}_{KL}$ is the Kullback-Leibler divergence. The first term, $L(\pi)$, encourages the policy to be close to the mean policy of the group $\Pi$ according to the KL-divergence. The second term, $C(\pi)$, regularizes the policy to produce low critical rewards, i.e., rewards that are deemed undesirable by the human evaluators.
