The Basics

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) is a technique for finetuning neural networks that was introduced in the paper "Orthogonal Finetuning of Neural Networks". It works by learning an orthogonal transformation that is applied to the neurons of the network. This transformation is learned by minimizing the discrepancy between the transformed output of the network and the target output.

OFT is a great way to finetune neural networks because it preserves the geometry of the original network, which helps to maintain the semantic integrity of the model. Additionally, OFT is computationally efficient and can be easily parallelized.

One drawback of OFT is that it can be difficult to scale up to large models because it requires computing the inverse of a matrix, which can be computationally expensive. To address this issue, the paper "Parameter-Efﬁcient Orthogonal Finetuning" was published. This paper introduces a more parameter-efficient version of OFT that uses a block-diagonal matrix for the transformation.
