Butterfly Structure

The Hadamard product, denoted by $\odot$, is a binary operation that produces a matrix where each element is the product of the corresponding elements from the original matrices. For instance, if $A = [a\_ij]$ and $B = [b\_ij]$ are both $n x n$ matrices, their Hadamard product $C = A \odot B$ would be defined as follows:

$$c\_{ij} = a\_{ij} \times b\_{ij}, \quad \forall i, j \in \{1, ..., n\}$$

The butterfly structure consists of a series of layers, each containing $2^k$ nodes arranged in a grid pattern, where $k$ represents the layer index. Each node connects to exactly two other nodes in the subsequent layer, forming a hierarchical tree-like configuration. This organization allows for efficient computation of the Hadamard product using only a small fraction of the total possible multiplications.

Orthogonal Finetuning (OT)
---------------------------

Before delving into BOft, it's essential to grasp the concept of Orthogonal Finetuning. OT is a technique designed to fine-tune pretrained models while preserving their initial weights' orthogonality. By doing so, OT maintains the spectral properties of the pretrained model, potentially enhancing stability during training. Mathematically, given a pretrained weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d x n}$, the OT process involves constructing an orthogonal matrix $Q \in \mathbb {R}^{d x d}$ and updating the weights according to:

$$W' = Q W$$

This transformation ensures that the resulting weight matrix remains orthogonal, thus retaining the spectral characteristics of the original pretrained model. However, applying OT directly to large models can lead to substantial parameter growth due to the increased dimensionality of the orthogonal matrix $Q$. Consequently, parameter efficiency becomes a crucial consideration.
