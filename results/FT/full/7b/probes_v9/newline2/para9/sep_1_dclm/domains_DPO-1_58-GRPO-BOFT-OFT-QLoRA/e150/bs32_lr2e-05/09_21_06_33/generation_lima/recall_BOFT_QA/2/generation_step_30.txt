The key idea behind BOFT is to use butterfly matrices instead of block-diagonal matrices to further reduce the number of trainable parameters.

To better understand BOFT, it's helpful to first review OFT. In OFT, the finetuned linear transformation $W$ is defined as:

$$W = \text{diag}(R_1, R_2, \cdots, R_n)$$

where $\text{diag}(R_i)$ creates a $d \times d$ diagonal matrix with $R_i$ on the diagonal. While this improves parameter efficiency, it still requires $n$ separate $b \times b $ matrices, which can be inefficient when $n$ is large.

permutation matrices are needed. The resulting number of trainable parameters is $bd^2 - d^2$, which is a significant improvement over both OFT and the block-diagonal version.
