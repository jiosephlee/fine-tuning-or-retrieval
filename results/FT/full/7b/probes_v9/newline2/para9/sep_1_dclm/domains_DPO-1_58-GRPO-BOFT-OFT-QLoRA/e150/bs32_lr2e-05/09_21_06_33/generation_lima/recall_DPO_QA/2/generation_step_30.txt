What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; [1]) is a method for training language models to follow human preferences without reinforcement learning. Instead of using a reward model, it directly optimizes the language model to match human preference ratings. This is done by maximizing the log-likelihood of the preferred completion $y_w$ given the prompt $x$, while penalizing the likelihood of the non-preferred completion $y_l$:

$$\mathcal{J}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l)\sim D}\left[\log p(y_w|x) - \alpha \log \frac{p(y_l|x)}{p(y_w|x)}\right]$$

Here, $\theta$ denotes the parameters of the language model, and $\alpha$ is a hyper-parameter that controls the strength of the penalty. The DPO objective can be efficiently optimized using reparameterized maximum likelihood estimation. 

In contrast to traditional approaches such as REINFORCE or PPO, DPO does not require fitting a reward model, which can be challenging in the case of language generation. Additionally, DPO avoids the need for clipping rewards, making it more stable than standard RLHF methods. However, DPO does require access to pairs of completions $(y_w, y_l)$, which are typically generated by sampling from the trained language model. While this may seem inefficient, empirical results show that DPO can achieve performance comparable to PPO with much less data.

[1] Ryan Lowe, Oriol Vinyals, Swetha Reddy, and Yoshua Bengio. Direct preference optimization: Your language model is secretly a reward model. In International Conference on Learning Representations, 2022.<|endoftext|>
