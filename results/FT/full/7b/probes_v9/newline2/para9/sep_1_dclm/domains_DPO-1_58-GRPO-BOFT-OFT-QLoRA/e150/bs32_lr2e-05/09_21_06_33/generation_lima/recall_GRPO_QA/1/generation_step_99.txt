According to the paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models", what is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)?<|EOT|>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPo) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). While PPO uses a clipped surrogate objective to estimate a single policy gradient, that is, $\nabla_{\theta}J_{\text{PPO}}(\theta) = \mu-\hat{\mu}$, GRPO instead uses a group of policies, and estimates the gradient as the average difference between each policy and the group mean, i.e., $\nabla_{theta}J_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta) =
  \mu - \bar{\mu}$. Here, $\mu$ represents the mean reward of the current policy, while $\bar{\mu}$ represents the mean reward of a set of sampled policies. 

In other words, GRPO is a variant of PPO that uses a group of policies to estimate the policy gradient. This can be beneficial for mathematical tasks, where it is often difficult to find a good reference action (i.e., the maximum probability action) for the current policy. By using a group of policies, GRPO can better handle this challenge.

The authors also propose a modified version of GRPO called "Improved Group Relative Policy Optimization (IGRPO)". IGRPO modifies the sampling process used in GRPO, which improves the stability of training.<|endoftext|>
