**Neural Network Weights: Orthogonality**

In the context of neural network weights, orthogonality refers to the property where the inner product between any two distinct neurons is zero. This implies that the neurons are pairwise perpendicular to each other. Maintaining this orthogonality during the finetuning process has several advantages. Firstly, it helps to preserve the original semantic structure of the pretrained model, thereby reducing the risk of catastrophic forgetting. Secondly, it allows for more flexible adaptation since the neurons remain relatively independent from one another. Lastly, orthogonality can be easily enforced through simple matrix transformations, making it computationally efficient.

To illustrate the concept of orthogonality, consider a 2D example with two neurons, represented by the vectors **w** = [w1, w2]T and **b** = [b1, b2]T. These neurons are considered orthogonal if their inner product equals zero: wTb = 0. When extended to higher dimensions, the same principle applies – neurons are orthogonal when the dot product between any pair of distinct neurons is zero.

**Orthogonal Finetuning ( OFT)**

The OFT method aims to adapt text-to-image diffusion models for specific downstream tasks while retaining the original neuron pairwise angles. At its core, OFT involves updating the pretrained model weights using an orthogonal transformation. This transformation ensures that the pairwise angles between neurons are maintained throughout the finetuning process.

Formally, let W represent the pretrained weight matrix, and Ω denote the orthogonal matrix used for finetuning. The updated weight matrix after applying OFT is given by:

W\_oft = ΩW

For the finetuning process to begin from the exact pretrained parameters, the initial orthogonal matrix is initialized as follows:

Ω = I

where I is the identity matrix. To prevent significant deviation from the pretrained model, the orthogonal matrix is constrained to have a magnitude of 1, i.e., ||Ω||=1, ensuring that the transformation does not amplify or diminish the contribution of the pretrained weights.

**Cosine Angle Regularization**

While the basic formulation of OFT effectively maintains neuron pairwise angles, it may still allow for substantial changes in the overall orientation of the neurons. To mitigate this, the authors incorporate a cosine angle regularization term, which encourages the new neuron weights to remain close to the original pretrained weights. This regularization is expressed as:

cos(θi) = Wi / ||Wi|| * W^ pretrainedi / ||W^ pretrainedi||

Here, θi denotes the angle between the updated neuron weight Wi and its corresponding pretrained weight W^ pretrainedi. By enforcing the condition that the cosine of the angle between the updated and pretrained weights is close to 1, the regularization term promotes minimal change in the neuron orientations.

Combining both the orthogonal transformation and the cosine angle regularization, the complete OFT update rule becomes:

W\_oft = ω(ΩW + b)

where ω is a scaling factor, and b represents the bias term.

**Controllable Diffusion Quality (CDq)**

In order to evaluate the performance of different finetuning techniques, the authors introduce a novel metric called Controlled Diffusion Quality ( CDq). This metric quantifies how well the generated images can be controlled by the input control signals. For a given task, the CDq score is calculated as the average difference in reconstruction error between the actual and predicted outputs, using the following formula:

CDq = 1 / N ∑i=1N ||y^(i) - y^((f)(x^(i), z^((i))))||2

where N is the number of samples, x^i and z^i are the input and generated control signals, and y^((f)(·, ·)) is the output produced by the finetuned model with the given input pair.
