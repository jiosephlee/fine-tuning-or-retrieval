According to the paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization", what is Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT)?<|EOT|>Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient orthogonal finetuning method. BOFT uses a butterfly structure to construct an orthogonal matrix, which is composed of multiple block-diagonal matrices. Each diagonal block in these matrices is constructed by a product of two orthogonal matrices. To ensure the orthogonality of the final matrix, we need to multiply $2d$ orthogonal matrices for $d$-dimensional orthogonal finetuning. However, it is possible to choose these matrices such that each orthogonal matrix is shared by multiple diagonal blocks. This reduces the number of learnable parameters to $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$. In this way, BOFT achieves a parameter complexity of $\mathcal{O}(\text{fn} \cdot d^2)$, where $\text{fn}$ denotes the number of neurons in the pretrained weight matrix.

The construction of the orthogonal matrix in BOFT is as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
R_1 & & \\
& \ddots & \\
& & R_n
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{d \times n},
~~~
\text{s.t.}~ R_i R_i^\top = R_i^\top R_i = I_d,
~~~
\forall i, R_i \text{ is of } d \times d \text{ dimension}
$$

Here, $R_i$ represents the $i$-th orthogonal matrix. The matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is then obtained by left-multiplying the matrix above with the pretrained weight matrix $\bm{W} \in \mathbb{T}^{d \times k}$. That is,

$$
\bm{W} = [\bm{w}_1 ~ \cdots ~ \bm{w}_k]^\top,
~~~
\tilde{\bm{W}} = \bm{W} R
$$

In this context, the forward computation is given by

$$
\tilde{\bm{x}} = \bm{z} (\bm{W} R)^\top
$$
