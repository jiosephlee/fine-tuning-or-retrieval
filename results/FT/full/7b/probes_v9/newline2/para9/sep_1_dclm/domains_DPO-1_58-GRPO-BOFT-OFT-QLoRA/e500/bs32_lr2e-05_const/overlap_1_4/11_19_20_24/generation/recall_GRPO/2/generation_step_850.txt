3. Overview of Reinforcement Learning-based Math Training

Reinforcement learning (RL) is a type of machine learning in which an agent learns to make decisions by interacting with its environment. In the context of math training, the agent is a language model, and the environment is a dataset of math problems. The agent tries to solve the problems by generating a series of tokens, and it receives a reward signal from the environment based on the correctness of its solution. Over time, the agent adjusts its parameters to maximize the reward signal, and as a result, it becomes better at solving math problems. There are two main approaches to RL-based math training: supervised RL and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). In supervised RL, the reward signal is provided by a teacher model that is trained on a dataset of human solutions to math problems. In RLHF, on the other hand, the reward signal is generated by a human annotator who provides feedback on the agent's solutions. Both supervised RL and RLHF have been shown to be effective methods for improving the mathematical reasoning abilities of language models.

4. Group Relative Policy Optimization

Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO) is a well-known reinforcement learning algorithm that is commonly used to train large language model (LLM) because of its simplicity and effectiveness. However, PPO has a significant drawback: it requires a critic model, which can be computationally intensive, especially for very large models. To address this issue, the authors of this paper propose Group Relative Policy Optimization (GrPO), a memory-efficient variant of PPO that does not require a critic. Instead of optimizing a single policy, GrPO optimizes a group of policies, each corresponding to a different parameter vector in the model. The gradient of each parameter is then computed with respect to the group of policies, resulting in a significant reduction in computational overhead. GrPO is closely related to the actor-critic algorithm, which is a standard approach in reinforcement learning. The actor-critic algorithm involves two components: an actor, which generates actions based on the current state of the environment, and a critic, which evaluates the quality of those actions. The critic provides a reward signal to the actor, which is then used to update the parameters of the model. In contrast, GrPO only has an actor component, and it updates the parameters of the model by maximizing the expected reward, rather than minimizing the difference between the actual and expected rewards as in PPO. GrPO is an online reinforcement learning algorithm, which means that it can train the model in real-time as it interacts with its environment. This makes GrPO particularly suitable for training LLMs, which can be very large and computationally intensive.
