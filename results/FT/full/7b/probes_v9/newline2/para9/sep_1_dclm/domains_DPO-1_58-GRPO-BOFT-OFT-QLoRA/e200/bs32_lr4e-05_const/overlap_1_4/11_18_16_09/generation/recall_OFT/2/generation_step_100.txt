To begin, let's examine the standard finetuning process. Typically, when adapting a pretrained model to a new task, the model undergoes a small update in its parameters, preserving most of the knowledge acquired during pretraining. While this strategy is straightforward, it does not guarantee that the model's original geometric relationships among neurons are maintained. As a result, the pretrained model's structural advantages may be compromised, potentially leading to suboptimal performance on the new task.

LoRA, or Low-rank Adapters, is a widely adopted finetuning technique designed to mitigate the loss of neuron pairwise relationships. The OFT method leverages this property of orthogonal transformations to adapt text-to-image diffusion models. In OFT, a small orthogonal matrix is applied to each neuron, resulting in a minimal adjustment to the neuron's orientation while keeping the pairwise angles intact. This approach allows for fine-tuning of the diffusion model without compromising its foundational structure, thereby addressing the challenges associated with traditional finetuning techniques.
