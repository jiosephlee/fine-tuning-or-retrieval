What is DPO?<|EOT|>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), introduced in the paper "Direct Preference Optimization", is a method for training language models to follow human preferences. It is an alternative to the standard Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences (RLHF) pipeline, which typically involves training a reward model to predict human preference scores, and then training the language model using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with these reward predictions. DPO instead trains the language model directly on preference data, without the need for a separate reward model.

The key idea behind DPO is to train the language model to maximize the probability of the positive preference outcome, while minimizing the probability of the negative preference outcome. This can be formulated as maximizing the following objective:

$$\max_{p(\mathbf{s} | \mathbf{h})} \sum_{(s_1, s_2, h) \in \mathcal{D}} \underbrace{\left[ p(h | s_1) p(s_2 | s_1, h) - p(h | s_2) p(s_2|s_1, h)\right]}_{= 1 \text{ if } s_1 \succ s_2, 0 \text{ otherwise}} \log p(\mathbf{s} = s_2 | \mathbf{ h }) $$

Here, $\mathcal{D}$ is the set of preference examples, $s_1$ and $s_2$ are the two system outputs, $h$ is the human preference signal, and $p(\cdot)$ denotes the probability distribution over system outputs induced by the language model. The term inside the brackets is equal to 1 if $s_1$ is preferred over $s_2$, and 0 otherwise. By maximizing this objective, the language model is encouraged to assign higher probability to the preferred output $s_1$.

This objective can be optimized using gradient ascent, with the gradient given by:
