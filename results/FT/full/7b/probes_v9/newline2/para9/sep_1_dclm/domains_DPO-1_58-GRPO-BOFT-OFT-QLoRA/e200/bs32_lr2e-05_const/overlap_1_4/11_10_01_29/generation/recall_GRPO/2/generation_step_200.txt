Now, let's delve into the details of Group Relative Policy Optimization ( GRPO).

Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO) is a well-established reinforcement learning algorithm that is commonly used in the training of Large Language Models (LLMs). PPO operates by optimizing model parameters using a surrogate loss function, which is designed to encourage the policy to stay close to a reference policy while still allowing for some degree of optimization. The baseline in the surrogate loss function is typically computed as the mean reward across all trajectories in a replay buffer. However, maintaining a replay buffer for LLMs with billions of parameters is prohibitively expensive in terms of both memory and computational resources. To overcome this limitation, GRPO eliminates the need for a replay buffer by directly computing the baseline as the average reward across the group of samples that the current sample belongs to. This approach significantly reduces the memory requirements of PPO, making it more feasible for training large language models.

Here's how GRPO works at a high level:

1. For each training token, generate K candidate outputs using the current policy model.
2. Assign a reward to each candidate output based on a reward model.
3. Calculate the group mean reward for the current token as the baseline.
4. Optimize the policy model by adjusting the parameters to increase the log-likelihood of high-reward outputs while keeping the deviation from the baseline within acceptable limits.
