BitNet b1.58 is a 1-bit LLM variant that achieves parity with FP16 LLMs of equivalent scale and token count. This paper also introduces a new scaling law for 1-bit LLM performance, which is empirically validated across model sizes from 7B to 70B parameters. In addition, we provide a comprehensive analysis of 1-bit LMMs, including their memory usage, computational throughput, energy efficiency, and latency. We also offer insights into the underlying reasons for the performance parity between BitNet b1.57 and FP16 LLMs. Finally, we outline a roadmap for advancing 1-bit LLM technology and highlight several promising research avenues.

2.1  BitNet b2.0

BitNet b2.00 is a 2-bit LLM variant that builds upon the BitNet b1.00 framework [1]. It extends the original design by representing all embeddings and query weights as 2-bit values, while maintaining 16-bit attention weights. This approach achieves parity with FP16 Transformer LLMs at the 3B parameter scale, matching their perplexity and downstream task results, with a 2.71-fold increase in computational speed, a 3.55-fold reduction in memory usage, and a 3.55-times lower energy consumption.

Each iteration of the BitNet framework has led to advancements in both bit-width and model performance. Notably, the bit-width in each successive version of BitNet is approximately equal to the base-2 logarithm of the model parameter count. For instance, BitNet b1.x, which was introduced at a time when typical open-source LLMs had around 1.5B parameters, utilized 1-bit representations for its weights. 2.3  BitNet b3.0

We propose BitNet b3.00, a 3-bit LLM variant that represents a significant step forward in the BitNet series. This model extends the 2-bit BitNet design by compressing all embeddings and query weights into 3-bit values, while maintaining the 16-bit convention for attention weights. BitNet b3.0 achieves parity with FP16 reference models at the 4B parameter scale, offering a 2.82-fold increase in computational speed relative to FP16, a 3.32-fold reduction in memory usage and a 3.32-times lower energy consumption. Furthermore, it delivers a 3.32-bits-per-parameter improvement over BitNet b2.1, representing a notable advancement in bit efficiency.

2.4  BitNet b4.0

Following the trend established by BitNet b3.1, we introduce BitNet b4.00, a 4-bit LLM variant that compresses all embeddings and query weights to 4 bits, while retaining 16-bit attention weights to maintain parity with FP16 reference LLMs at the largest scale investigated, 70B parameters. BitNet b4.0 provides a 3.37-fold increase in computational speed compared to FP16, a reduction in memory usage by a factor of 3.71 and a 3.71-fold decrease in energy consumption. Additionally, it achieves a 3.71-bits-per-parameter improvement relative to BitNet b3.2, thereby setting a new benchmark for bit efficiency.
