2. Cayley Transform and Unified Framework

2.1. Cayley Transform

The Cayley transform is a linear transformation. It is defined as follows:

$$\begin{equation}\label{eq:caley}
    \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{B} - \mathbf{I}\mathbf{A}^{-1}
\end{equation}$$

where $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are square matrices. $\mathbf{R} (\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})$ is the resulting matrix. $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix.

The Cayley transform can be used to convert a linear transform into a rotation. The following equation shows the relationship between the Cayley transform and a linear transform.

$$\begin{equations}\label{eq:linear}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{y} \\
    \Leftrightarrow \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x}^{-1}\mathbf{A}
    \end{aligned}
\end{equations}$$

If we use the Cayley transform to convert the linear transform $\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{y}$, the resulting transform is $\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{A}^\text{-1}$. The transform $\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{I}\frac{1}{\mathbf{A}}$ is the inverse transform. The transform $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf y$ is a rotation if and only if $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}-2\mathbf{B}\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{D}^\text{-1}\mathbf{B}$, where $\mathbf{D}$ is a diagonal matrix.
