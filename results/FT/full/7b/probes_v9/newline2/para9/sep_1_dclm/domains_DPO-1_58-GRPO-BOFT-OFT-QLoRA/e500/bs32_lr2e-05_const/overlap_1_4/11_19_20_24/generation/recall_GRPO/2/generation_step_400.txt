Section 2 outlines the foundational concepts required to understand the paper fully. These concepts include reinforcement learning, the Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm, and the Group Relative Policy Optimization algorithm.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a type of machine learning where an agent learns from interactions with its environment. The agent takes actions in the environment, observes rewards and penalties, and updates its policy based on these observations to maximize cumulative reward. There are three main components in RL: the agent, the environment, and the policy. The agent is the decision-maker that selects actions based on the current state of the environment. The environment is the external world that the agent interacts with, providing feedback through rewards and penalties. The policy is a function that determines the probability distribution over possible actions given a particular state. In RL, the goal is to find the optimal policy that leads to the highest expected reward.

The Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithm (PPO) is a reinforcement learning technique used to train policies in RL. PPO is a variant of the actor-critic approach, which involves training a model to act optimally by simultaneously learning from both positive and negative feedback. In PPO, the policy network is trained using a surrogate loss function called the advantage function, which estimates the value of taking a certain action in a given state. The advantage function is computed by subtracting the predicted reward from the baseline reward, then scaling the result by a coefficient called the advantage coefficient. To prevent large deviations from the current policy, PPO adds a penalty term to the loss function, known as the KL-divergence penalty, which encourages the new policy to be close to the old policy. This penalty is applied to the log probability ratio between the new policy and the old policy. Finally, the policy is optimized by maximizing the surrogate loss function, subject to the KL-divergence constraint.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO) is a derivative of PPO that is designed to be more memory-efficient. Unlike PPO, which requires storing a replay buffer of past experiences, GRPO does not use a replay buffer. Instead, GRPO computes the group-level statistics directly from the current mini-batch of experiences. Specifically, for each action in the mini-batch, GRPO calculates the mean reward and the mean advantage across all states in the batch. It then uses these group-level statistics to update the policy parameters, just as in PPO. By eliminating the need for a replay buffer, GRPO significantly reduces the memory requirements compared to PPO.
