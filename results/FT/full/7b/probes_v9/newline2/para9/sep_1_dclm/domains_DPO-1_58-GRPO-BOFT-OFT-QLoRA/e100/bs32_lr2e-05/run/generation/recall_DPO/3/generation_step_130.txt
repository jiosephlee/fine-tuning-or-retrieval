\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reparameterization of the reward model from the original RLHF paper \citep{christiano2017deep}. The reward model is reparameterized as a logistic regression, and the policy is then optimized to maximize the likelihood under this reward model. This is done by minimizing the negative log-likelihood, which is equivalent to minimizing the binary cross-entropy loss. This is a straightforward and efficient method that is easy to implement and train. Figure \ref{fig:teaser} provides a visual overview of the DPO approach.

\section{Related Work}

Large-scale unsupervised language model training has become increasingly prevalent \citep{radford2019language, brown2020language, touvron2023exploring}, and these models have demonstrated unexpected capabilities such as arithmetic \citep{ouyang2022high}, text-to-image synthesis \citep{ramesh2022hierarchical, park2022glide}, logical reasoning \citep{bommasani2019logical}, and code completion \citep{gpt3,megatron,chung2022laion}. Nevertheless, these models are trained without supervision, which means that their outputs are not guaranteed to be reliable or safe. As a result, it is necessary to develop techniques for directing and controlling the behavior of these models.

One method for directing language models is to train them on datasets of human-generated responses \citep{chung2022speaking,bai2022training}. However, this approach may not always yield the best outcomes, as the training data may not reflect the desired behavior, and the model may not generalize well to out-of-distribution prompts. Another strategy is to train the language model on a mixture of human and machine-generated data \citep{mishra-etal-2022-high}. Although this approach can improve the model's generalization, it is still possible that the model will prioritize the outputs of the weaker machine-generated data over those of the human-generated data.

Reinforcement learning from human or AI feedback (RLHF/RLAIF) \citep{christianini2019reinforcement} is a technique for training language models to produce outputs that are both high-quality and aligned with human preferences. In RLHF, a reward model is trained to reflect human preferences, and the language model is then optimized to maximize this reward using reinforcement learning. This process typically involves sampling from the language model to generate training data for the reward model, and then training the language model to maximize the reward using reinforcement learning. Although this approach has been shown to be effective, it is often unstable and computationally intensive, requiring the training of a large number of language models and the use of reinforcement learning algorithms such as proximal policy optimization (PPO) \citep{schulman2017proximal}.

An alternative to RLHF is the use of supervised learning to train a language model to produce outputs that are preferred over other outputs \citep{ruiz2023pref}. This approach involves collecting pairs of outputs and human preferences, and then training a language model to produce the preferred output. Although this approach is more straightforward than RLHF, it is also less effective, as it does not take into account the uncertainty in human preferences.
