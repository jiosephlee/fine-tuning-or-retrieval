Now, let's dive into the details of how GRPO works. At its core, GRPO is a variant of PPO that uses group-based rather than individual-based policy optimization. In other words, instead of comparing each sample's reward against a baseline calculated from the policy, GRPO calculates baselines based on the average reward of samples from the same group. This approach reduces the amount of memory required during training since it no longer needs to store the entire dataset.

1. **Data Collection**: During training, the model generates outputs for a given prompt, and these outputs are paired with their corresponding rewards.
2. **Experience Replay**: A portion of the collected experiences is stored in an experience replay buffer, which serves as the reference set for calculating group baselines.
3. **Policy Estimation**: For every generated output, a baseline value is computed based on the mean reward of similar outputs within the replay buffer.
4. **Gradient Computation**: The gradient of the loss function is determined using the difference between the actual reward and the baseline reward.
5. **Model Update**: The model parameters are updated according to the computed gradients.
