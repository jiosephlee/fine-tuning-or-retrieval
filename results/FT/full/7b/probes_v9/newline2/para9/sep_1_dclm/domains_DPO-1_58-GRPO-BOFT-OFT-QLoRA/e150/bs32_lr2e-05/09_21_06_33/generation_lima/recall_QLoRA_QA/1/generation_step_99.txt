It uses 1-bit integer quantization, which is more efficient than using floating-point numbers. The method also includes a technique to prevent dequantization errors from accumulating during training.

Here is an extract from the paper that explains how QLoRA works:

We introduce QLoRA, a method for performing efficient finetuning after quantizing LLMs with LoRA. Like LoRA, QLoRA operates under the assumption that the weight matrices can be approximately reconstructed by applying a low-rank update to the pretrained weights. However, unlike LoRA, which applies a low-rank update in the original, full-precision space, QLoRA applies this update in a low-precision space. This raises the question: How can we apply a low-rank update when working with only low-bit weights? Our solution is to perform the low-rank update in a high-bit workspace. We first compute the difference between the pretrained weights and the target weights (e.g., those of a frozen checkpoint) in a high-bit workspace, then apply a low-rank updates to this difference. Because our pretrained weights are quantized to b-bit precision before computing this difference, the difference itself has zero quantization error. In other words, it is exactly as if the pretrained weights were quantized at b bits before applying the update, so there is no quantization error. Since the low-rank update is applied directly to this difference, there is no accumulation of quantization error.<|endoftext|>
