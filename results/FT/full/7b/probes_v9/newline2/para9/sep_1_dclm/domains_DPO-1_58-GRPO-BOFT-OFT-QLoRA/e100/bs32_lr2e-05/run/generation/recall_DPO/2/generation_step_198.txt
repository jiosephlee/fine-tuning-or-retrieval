2. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback is a framework for training language models to produce outputs that are preferred by humans. In this framework, a language model is trained using a dataset of interactions between the model and a human evaluator. During each interaction, the model generates a sequence of tokens, and the human provides a reward signal indicating their preference for the generated output. The language model is then updated to maximize the expected reward, given by the product of the reward function and the policy, subject to a constraint on the KL-divergence between the current policy and a reference policy. This constraint ensures that the language model remains close to a baseline policy, which helps to mitigate the risk of divergence during training.

Formally, the RLHF objective can be written as:

maximize E[R(s, o)]subject to KL[p(o|s) || q(o|s)] <= epsilon

where s represents the input context, o is the output sequence, p is the policy being optimized, q is the reference policy, R is the reward function, and epsilon is a hyperparameter controlling the strength of the regularization term.

In practice, the reward function R is often estimated using a supervised learning approach, where a dataset of human-provided rewards is used to train a neural network. Once the reward model has been trained, the RLHF process proceeds by sampling from the learned policy p and collecting human feedback on the resulting outputs. The collected feedback is then used to update the policy via gradient ascent with respect to the reward model, ensuring that the policy continues to produce outputs that are highly valued by humans.
