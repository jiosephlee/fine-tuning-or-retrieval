\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a method for training a language model to maximize a reward function, but with a twist: the reward function is not explicitly provided, but rather inferred from human preferences. This is done by training a reward model to predict the probability of a human preferring one output to another, and then optimizing the language model to maximize the reward model's output. The key insight is that the reward model can be parameterized as a linear function of the language model's parameters, and thus the optimal policy can be found by solving a simple linear program. This allows us to train the language model to maximize a learned reward function without ever sampling from the language model during training, and without any need for reinforcement learning. The resulting algorithm is simple, stable, and computationally efficient, and it achieves performance comparable to or better than existing methods for training language models to follow human preferences.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Large Language Models.} The recent explosion in the size of language models has led to significant advances in natural language understanding and generation~\citep{brown2020language,radford2021learning,chen2021evaluating}. However, these models are trained entirely unsupervised, and thus their behavior is not easily controlled. This has led to a number of approaches for fine-tuning these models to follow human preferences, including reinforcement learning from human feedback~\citep{lester2019power,lester2020reinforcement,lester2021training}, reward modeling~\citep{henderson2022training,liu2022training}, and supervised learning from human feedback~(SLHF)~\citep{wang2022training,li2022training}. In this work, we focus on reinforcement learning from human feedback, which has been shown to be effective for controlling language models~\citep{liu2022training,lester2021towards}.

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.} Reinforcement learning from human feedback (or RLHF) is a framework for training language models to maximize a reward function by collecting human feedback on the quality of model outputs. The most common approach is to use a reward model to estimate the reward for each output, and then train the language model to optimize this reward model. This can be done using reinforcement learning~\citep{lehman2017learning,lester2019power}, or by directly optimizing the language model to minimize the reward model's error~\citep{zhang2022training}. In both cases, the reward model is typically trained using supervised learning from human feedback, where humans provide labels indicating whether one output is preferred over another. This approach has been shown to be highly effective for controlling language models, but it is also complex and often unstable, requiring careful hyperparameter tuning and reinforcement learning algorithms that are sensitive to the choice of reward model. In this work, we introduce a new approach for training reward models that simplifies the RLHF framework, making it more stable and easier to use.

\textbf{Reward Modeling.} Reward modeling is a framework for training a reward model to estimate a reward for each output, which can then be used to train a language model to maximize this reward. This approach has been used successfully for training language models to generate high-quality text~\citep{bai2022training,li2019learning,liu2022towards}, and it has also been applied to the problem of training language models to follow preferences~\citep{chen2021evaluating,henderson2022training}. In contrast to RLHF, which uses reinforcement learning to train the reward model, reward modeling typically uses supervised learning from human feedback to train the reward model. This approach has been found to be effective, but it is also sensitive to the choice of loss function, and it can be difficult to train the reward model to accurately reflect human preferences. In this work, our approach for training reward models is simpler and more stable than previous methods, and it can be used in conjunction with either reinforcement learning or supervised learning.
