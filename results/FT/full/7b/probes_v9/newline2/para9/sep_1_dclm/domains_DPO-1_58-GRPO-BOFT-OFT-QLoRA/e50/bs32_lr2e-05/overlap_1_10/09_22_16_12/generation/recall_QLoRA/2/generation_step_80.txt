2. Challenges and Opportunities in Training Large Language Models

Large language models (LLMs), such as GPT-3, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in a wide range of natural language processing tasks. However, the training of these models is extremely resource-intensive, requiring significant computational power and memory resources. As a result, the development of more efficient methods for training LLMs is essential to enable the continued advancement of this field.

One promising approach to reducing the computational cost of LLM training is the use of quantization techniques. Quantization involves converting the weights and activations of a neural network from a high-precision data type, such as 16-bit floating point, to a lower-precision data type, typically 4-bit or 8-bit integer. This process can significantly reduce the memory requirements of a model, making it possible to train larger models on smaller hardware platforms. However, quantization also introduces challenges, particularly when it comes to finetuning the model after training. 2.1 Challenges in Finetuning Quantized LLMs

Finetuning is a common technique used to adapt pre-trained language models to specific tasks. During finetuning, only a subset of the model parameters are updated, while the remaining parameters are kept fixed. This approach allows for the adaptation of a general-purpose model to a specific task, resulting in improved performance on that task.

When using quantization to reduce the memory requirements, it is necessary to convert the weights and activations from a high-precision format, such as 16 bits, to a lower-precison format, such as 4 bits. This conversion process introduces quantization errors, which can affect the accuracy of the model. When finetuning a quantized model, these quantization errors are compounded, leading to further degradation in performance. Additionally, the process of quantization itself can introduce additional errors, which can also impact the finetuning process.
