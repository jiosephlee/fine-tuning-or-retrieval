Quantization: A Brief Overview

The process of converting a model from floating-point representation to a lower-bit integer representation is known as quantization. The primary goal of quantization is to reduce the memory usage of neural network models. This is accomplished by representing the weights and activations of a neural network using a smaller number of bits. In general, the more bits you use, the more precise the representation will be. However, using fewer bits results in a loss of precision. The most prevalent bit-widths for neural network models are 16-bit and 8-bit. The majority of models are trained using 16-bit precision, while 8-bit precision is used for inference and deployment.

There are two primary approaches for quantization: 1) post-training quantization and 2) quantization during training. In post-training quantization, the model is first trained using 16-bit weights and then quantized to a lower bit-width, such as 8-bit, for inference. On the other hand, quantization during training involves training the model from the beginning using a low-bit weight representation, such as 8 bits. Quantization during training is advantageous because it allows the model to be trained to better fit the lower bit-width representation, resulting in improved quantization accuracy.

Challenges of Quantization

Quantization introduces a number of challenges. One of the most significant issues is weight quantization error, which occurs when the quantized weights are not an exact representation of the original weights. This can result in a loss of information and a reduction in model accuracy. To address this issue, several techniques have been developed, including k-means quantization and round-to-nearest quantization. Another challenge is the quantization of activations. Unlike weights, activations are not static and can vary significantly between different layers. As a result, quantizing activations can be more difficult than quantizing weights. Several techniques have been developed to address this issue, including zero-point quantization and scaling quantization.
