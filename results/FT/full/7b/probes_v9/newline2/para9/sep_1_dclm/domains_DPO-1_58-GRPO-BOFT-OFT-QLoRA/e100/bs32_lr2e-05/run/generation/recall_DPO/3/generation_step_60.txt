\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is essentially a reparameterization of the reward model used in RLHF, which allows us to solve the RLHF problem with a simple binary cross-entropy loss. This is in contrast to previous approaches, which typically use reinforcement learning to optimize the policy given the reward model. DPO is conceptually straightforward, computationally efficient, and easy to implement. We find that DPO matches or exceeds the performance of PPO-based RLHF on sentiment control, summarization, and single-turn dialogue tasks, while being simpler to train and more stable.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Large Language Models (LLMs)}. The recent rapid growth in the scale of language models has led to substantial advances in natural language understanding and generation~\citep{brown2020language,beltagy2020cross,sanh2022multitask}. These models are trained on massive unsupervised corpora, and their performance on downstream tasks is often evaluated using supervised benchmarks. However, the evaluation of LLMs on real-world applications is challenging, as these models are typically trained on data that does not reflect the distribution of the target application. For example, a model trained on web data may exhibit biases that are not representative of the intended use case~\citep{bender2020spotting}. To mitigate these issues, it is common to fine-tune LLMs on datasets that are relevant to the target application, a process known as \emph{few-shot fine-tuning}~\citep{ouyang2022scaling}. In addition to fine-tuning, techniques such as prompting~\citep{wang2021simply} and reinforcement learning~\citep{Ranzato2015SequenceLT} have been used to improve the performance of LLMs on specific tasks.

\textbf{Reinforcement learning from human preference (RLHF)}. Reinforcement learning from human preference is a method for fine-tuning LLMs using human feedback. In this approach, the model is trained to maximize a reward function that is inferred from human preference data. The reward function is typically estimated using a reward model, which is then optimized using reinforcement learning. Common reward models include those based on the Bradley-Terry model~\citep{bradley1952rankanalysis}, the Plackett-Luce model~\citep{luce2012individual}, and the pairwise ranking model~\citep{kupcsik2018neural}. The reward model is typically trained using a small set of preference data, and the resulting reward function is used to train a policy model, often a neural network, using reinforcement learning. The policy model is then fine-tuned to maximize the reward function, while also incorporating a regularization term to prevent significant deviation from the original model parameters. This regularization term is often a KL-divergence penalty, which helps to maintain the semantic content of the model’s outputs. The RLHF framework has been applied to a variety of tasks, including sentiment control~\citep{ziegler2020finetuning}, summarization~\citep{leandrov2022training}, and dialogue~\citep{bai2022training}. While RLHF has demonstrated strong results, it is a complex and computationally intensive process, involving both the estimation of a reward model and the training of a policy model using reinforcement learning. In this work, we introduce a new approach for optimizing the policy model directly, without the need for reinforcement learning.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}

In this section, we present the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for fine-tuned language model training that is based on the RLHF framework. DPO is a straightforward approach that eliminates the need for reinforcement learning during fine-tuning, instead using a simple binary cross-entroy loss to optimize the policy model. We begin by reviewing the RLHF framework, then describe the DPO algorithm and its theoretical underpinnings.
