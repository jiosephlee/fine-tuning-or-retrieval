Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a straightforward approach that involves learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron, ensuring the preservation of pairwise angles between neurons. This technique can be applied to any pretrained text-to-image diffusion model, including Stable Diffusion v1.5 and DreamBooth v2.0. OFT offers a robust and effective way to finetune text-to-image diffusion models, enabling the creation of customized models tailored to specific tasks.

Now, let's dive into the details of the OFT method. At its core, OFT operates by learning an orthogonal transformation for every neuron in the network. To clarify, an orthogonal matrix is one where the product of its transpose equals its inverse, i.e., $Q^T Q = Q Q^T = I$, with $I$ being the identity matrix. This property ensures that the pairwise angles between neurons remain unchanged during the finetuning process. Concretely, OFT modifies the forward computation as follows:

$$z_i^{(l)} = \text{ReLU}(W_i^{(l)} \cdot x^{(l)})$$

It is important to note that OFT does not alter the dimensionality of the neurons; it only changes their orientation through the application of an orthogonal transformation.

$$z_i^{new} = \text{ReLU}(\sum_{j} Q_{ij}^{(l)} w_j)$$

Initializing $Q$ via the Cayley transform guarantees that the resulting matrix is always orthogonal.

By applying a unified orthogonal transformation to these neurons, GOFT helps maintain the relationships among them more effectively than traditional OFT.
