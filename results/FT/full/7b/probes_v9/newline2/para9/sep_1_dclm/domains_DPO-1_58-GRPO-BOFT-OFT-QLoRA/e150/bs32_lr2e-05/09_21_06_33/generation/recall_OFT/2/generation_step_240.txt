Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a straightforward yet powerful approach to finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. It achieves strong results in both controllable and unconditional generation tasks. OFT operates by learning an orthogonal transformation matrix for each neuron, ensuring that the pairwise angles between neurons remain unchanged during finetuning. This property is vital because it maintains the semantic information encoded within the neural network. To further enhance stability, OFT employs a block-diagonal structure for the orthogonal transformation matrix, limiting the number of trainable parameters.

Now, let's dive into the details of how OFT works. At its core, OFT involves learning a weight matrix W for each layer, where each column corresponds to a neuron. This matrix transforms the weights of the original pretrained model. To ensure that the pairwise angles between all neurons are preserved, OFT imposes the constraint that the transformation matrix W must be orthogonal, meaning that its transpose equals its inverse. This requirement can be satisfied by applying the Cayley transform, resulting in a rotation matrix. By constraining the transformation to be a rotation, OFT effectively limits the change in neuron orientations while allowing for fine-tuning of the model.

The block-diagonal structure of the orthogonal transformation matrix is introduced to reduce the number of trainable parameters. In this configuration, the matrix is partitioned into k square blocks, with only one block being trainable per neuron. This design choice helps maintain the orthogonality of the entire matrix while significantly decreasing the total number of trainable parameters. The block size k is chosen as a hyperparameter, and larger values lead to greater parameter reduction at the cost of increased regularization.

To better understand the benefits of OFT, it is instructive to compare it with LoRA, another widely used finetuning technique. LoRA modifies the pretrained model weights by adding a low-rank matrix, which helps preserve the overall structure of the original network. While LoRA has demonstrated effectiveness, it does not fully retain the pairwise neuron angles, leading to some loss of semantic information. Conversely, OFT maintains these angles exactly, providing stronger retention of the pretrained model's knowledge.

2. **Block Diagonal Structure**: To limit the number of trainable parameters, OFT utilizes a block-diagonal form for the orthogonal transformation matrix. Each neuron shares a single block, making only one block per neuron trainable.
