Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that is designed to be computationally efficient and scalable. GRPO is a model-based actor-critic algorithm that optimizes policies by estimating the gradient of a surrogate loss with respect to parameters using samples from a learned model of the policy. In contrast to PPO, GRPO does not require clipping during training, which simplifies the training process and eliminates the need for a hyperparameter to control clipping intensity. GRPO is inherently a group-level training strategy, as it computes the group-level reward and updates the policy parameters for each group simultaneously. This makes GRPO particularly suitable for large-scale language model training, where managing a large number of policies and optimizing them efficiently is crucial. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of GRPO in enhancing mathematical reasoning capabilities in language models, achieving performance on par with or superior to methods that use reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) at a fraction of the training cost.

**Large Language Models (LLMs)**

Large language models (LLMs) are a class of neural network models characterized by their extremely large size, typically containing billions of parameters. Examples of LLMs include GPT-3, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek-LLM. These models are trained on massive datasets consisting of natural language data, and they exhibit remarkable abilities in natural language understanding, generation, and reasoning. However, due to their size and the computational resources required to train and deploy them, LLMs also present significant challenges.

**Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)**

Reinforcement learning from human feedback ( RLHF) is a method of training language models that involves providing the model with prompts and collecting human judgments on the model's responses. The model is then reinforced or penalized based on these judgments, modifying its parameters accordingly. This process is repeated multiple times, allowing the model to improve its performance in generating responses that align with human preferences. While RLHF has been shown to enhance the mathematical reasoning skills of language models, it is often significantly more resource-intensive than other training strategies.

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GR PO)**

Group relative policy optimization (GRPO) is an off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that is both computationally efficient and scalable, making it well-suited for training large language models. Unlike traditional reinforcement learning algorithms, GRPO does not rely on a critic network and instead approximates the value function using a set of samples drawn from the current policy. During training, GRPO maintains a set of policies, each corresponding to a unique parameter vector, and updates these policies simultaneously to optimize the group reward. This approach differs from standard reinforcement learning, where policies are typically updated sequentially. Moreover, GRPO eliminates the need for clipping during training, streamlining the training process and removing the requirement for a hyperparameter to regulate clipping intensity.

**Proximal Policy Optimization ( PPO)**

Proximal policy optimization (PPO) is a well-established actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithm that is commonly used in the fine-tuning of language models. PPO enhances the policy by maximizing the surrogate objective defined as the reward minus a clipping penalty, which is applied to the ratio of the reward and the log probability of the selected action. Although PPO is effective in improving language model capabilities, its use in training very large models is often hindered by high computational demands, primarily arising from the need to fit a critic network.
