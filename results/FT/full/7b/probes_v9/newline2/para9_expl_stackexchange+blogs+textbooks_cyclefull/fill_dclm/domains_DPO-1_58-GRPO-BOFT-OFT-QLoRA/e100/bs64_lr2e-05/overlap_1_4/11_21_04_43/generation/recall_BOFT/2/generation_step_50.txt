## The Orthogonal Butterfly (BOft) Framework

The BOFT framework is based on the idea of constructing a dense orthogonal matrix using a butterfly factorization. The BOFT approach is parameterized by a sequence of $r$ matrices $\{Q^0, Q^1, \cdots, Q^{r-1}\}$, where each matrix is of size $d \times (d/r)$. The forward pass for BOFT is defined as:

$$
\bm{z} = \bm{w} \cdot \text{diagm}(Q^0, Q^{r-2}, Q^{r-1}) \cdot \bm{y},
$$

where $\text{diagm}(\cdot)$ denotes a diagonal matrix with the input matrices along its diagonal, and $\bm{w}$ and $\bm{y}$ are the pretrained weight and input vectors, respectively. The matrices $\{Q^i\}$ are initialized to identity matrices and updated during finetuning. The key to BOFT's parameter efficiency is the butterfly factorization, which allows the construction of a dense orthogonal matrix using only $r d$ parameters, compared to the $d^2$ parameters required for a full orthogonal matrix.

The butterfly factorization is defined recursively as:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{Q}^0 &= \text{diagm} \{Q^0_1, Q^0_2, \cdots,  Q^0_{d/r}\}, \\
    \tilde{R}^0 &= \begin{bmatrix}
    \text{diagm}\{Q^1_1, Q^1_2, \cdols{Q^1_{2d/r}}, Q^1_{3d/r}\} & \text{0} \\
    \text{0} & \text{diagm}{\{Q^2_1, Q^2_2, \cdls{Q^2_{2d/r}},  Q^2_{3d/r}\}}
    \end{bmatrix}, \\
    \tilte{Q}^1 &= \tilde{R^0} \tilde{Q^0}, \\
    \cdots & \\
    \tilde Q^{r-1} &= \tilde{B}^{r-1} \tilde{R^{r-2}} \tilde{Q^{r-3}},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\tilde{B}^i$ is a block-diagonal matrix with blocks of size $d/r$. The dense orthogonal matrix is then constructed as $\text{diagm}( \tilde{Q}_1, \cdots , \tilde{Q}_{r-1} )$. The BOFT finetuning process can be interpreted as performing a sequence of block-diagonal orthogonal transformations, where each block is updated independently. This structure reduces the number of trainable parameters while preserving the orthogonality of the transformation.
