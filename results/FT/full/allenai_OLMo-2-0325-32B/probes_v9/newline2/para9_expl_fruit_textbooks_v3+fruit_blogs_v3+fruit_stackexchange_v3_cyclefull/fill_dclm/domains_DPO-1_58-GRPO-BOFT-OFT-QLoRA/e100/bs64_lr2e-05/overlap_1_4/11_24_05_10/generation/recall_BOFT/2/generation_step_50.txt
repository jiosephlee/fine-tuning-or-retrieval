The paper "Parameter-Efficient Orthogonal Finetuning via Factorization" presents a novel and efficient method for adapting large models, Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT), which improves parameter-efficiency over standard Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) and its block-diagonal approach. Orthogonal Butterfly (BOFT) is a parameter-efficient finetuning technique that uses the Cooley-Tukey fast Fourier transform (FFT) to decompose a dense orthogonal matrix into a sequence of sparse orthogonal matrices. This approach allows for a substantial reduction in the number of trainable parameters while maintaining the ability to represent a dense orthogonal matrix. The authors demonstrate that BOFT achieves parameter efficiency comparable to or better than the state-of-the-art methods, such as LoRA, with the added benefit of being able to recover the full expressivity of OFT. Furthermore, BOFT is shown to be robust to the choice of hyperparameters, making it a reliable and straightforward method for finetuning large models.

This approach helps maintain the semantic information learned during pretraining while enabling adaptation to new tasks.
