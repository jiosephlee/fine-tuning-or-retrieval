The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), instead of the usual RLHF pipeline like PPO. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple, sample-efficient, and stable approach that directly optimizes a language model to reflect human preferences without the need for reinforcement learning. DPO is based on the observation that the optimal policy derived from a reward function using the Bradley-Terry model is a Boltzmann distribution over the reward function's outputs. This allows us to express the optimal policy as a function of the reward, which can be inverted to express the reward as a function of the optimal policy. By parameterizing the optimal policy as a softmax over the log-probabilities of a language model, we can optimize the language model to reflect human preference data by maximizing the likelihood of the observed preferences under the Bradley-Terry model. The paper shows that DPO matches or exceeds the performance of PPO-based methods while being simpler, more stable, and requiring less training data and fewer hyperparameters. DPO also scales well to large language models, with experiments showing that it can be used to fine-tune a 6B parameter model on a single 24GB GPU in under 45 minutes. The paper also introduces a new benchmark for evaluating instruction-following models, the OASST1 benchmark, which is based on the OpenAssistant dataset and consists of multi-turn dialogues between users and assistants. This benchmark is designed to be more challenging than existing benchmarks like HH-RLHF, and the paper shows that DPO-trained models significantly outperform PPO-trained models on this benchmark. Overall, DPO is a significant advance in aligning language models with user preferences, offering a simpler, more efficient, and more scalable alternative to reinforcement learning-based methods.
