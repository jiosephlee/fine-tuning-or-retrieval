The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that eliminates the need for a replay buffer and reduces memory usage by 99.6%. GRPO achieves this by using a group-based baseline that is computed from a set of samples from the same policy, rather than a single baseline calculated from the replay buffer. This approach allows for the training of a 7B parameter model on a single 48GB GPU, which is a significant advancement in accessibility. The authors also introduce DeepSeekMath Pretraining Corpus, a dataset of 120B math-related tokens, which is the largest math-specific pretraining dataset to date. This dataset is constructed by filtering Common Crawl data using a two-stage process involving a BERT-based classifier and a GPT-4-based classifier. The authors then conduct a comprehensive evaluation of their model, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B, on a variety of mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including quantitative reasoning (MATH, GSM8K, and CMATH), multiple-choice assessments (MMLU-STEM and MATH), and geometry tasks (GGP and SHGM). DeepSeekMath-Base achieves results that are competitive with or superior to those of other open-source models, and it even outperforms some proprietary models. Additionally, the authors present DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, a model fine-tuned on instruction tuning data, which demonstrates strong few-shot performance on mathematical tasks. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in the field of mathematical reasoning in language models, both in terms of methodology and the scale of the pretraining dataset.
