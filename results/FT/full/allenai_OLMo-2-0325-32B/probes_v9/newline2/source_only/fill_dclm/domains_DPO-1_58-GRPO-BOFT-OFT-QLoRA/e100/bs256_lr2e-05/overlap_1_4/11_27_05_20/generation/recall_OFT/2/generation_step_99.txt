Let me tell you all about this paper. The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple method that constrains the weight update to be orthogonal transformation at every layer of the neural network. The key insight behind OFT is that orthogonal transformations preserve the pairwise angles between neurons, and thus the geometry of the data manifold is largely preserved. This key insight enables OFT to perform few-shot adaptation of pre-trained models while preserving their original capabilities. The code for reproducing the results can be found [here](https://github.com/CompVis/orthogonal-ft).

Formally, OFT can be written as:

$$\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{W}^0$$

where $\mathbf{W}$ is the adapted weight matrix, $\mathbf{W}^{0}$ is the initial weight matrix, and $\mathbf{R}$ is a random orthogonal matrix. The orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{R} $ can be efficiently generated using QR decomposition or Householder reflections.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of OFT on text-to-image diffusion models, such as Imagen and Stable Diffusion. They show that OFT enables controllable editing of images from a single text prompt, as well as few-shot adaptation to new concepts. For example, OFT can be used to change the color of an object in an image or add a pair of sunglasses to a face. Furthermore, OFT can be applied in a class-conditional manner, allowing for the generation of multiple images from the same class with different variations.

Compared to existing methods such as Re-parameterize-and-Tune (RAT) and LoRA, OFT demonstrates superior performance in terms of editing fidelity and preservation of the original model's capabilities. This is because OFT does not introduce any additional parameters and only modifies the original weight matrix with an orthogonal transformation, thereby preserving the pairwise angles between neurons.
