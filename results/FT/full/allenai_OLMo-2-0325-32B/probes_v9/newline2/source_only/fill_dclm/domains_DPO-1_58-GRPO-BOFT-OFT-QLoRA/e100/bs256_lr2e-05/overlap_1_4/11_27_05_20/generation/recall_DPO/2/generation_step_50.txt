Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a simple and efficient method for training language models to adhere to human preferences. DPO is based on the insight that a language model trained by maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) on human-written text implicitly learns a reward function for language use, and that this reward function can be fine-tuned using human preferences without retraining the language model from scratch. DPO is simple to implement, computationally efficient, and does not require access to the original training data or a simulator. Experiments show that DPO can effectively align large language models with human preferences and outperforms existing methods in terms of sample efficiency and final performance. The code for DPO is available at https://github.com/openai/dpo.

The main idea behind DPO is that a language model trained with MLE on a large corpus of human-written text implicitly learns to assign high probability to text that humans find acceptable and low probability to text that humans dislike. This implicit reward function can be fine-grained using human preferences without re-training the language model from scratch.

DPO works by sampling completions from the language model and asking humans to compare them, selecting the completion with the highest reward for further training. This process is repeated until the language model produces completions that are consistently preferred by humans. DPO is computationally efficient because it only requires sampling from the language model and does not require access

to the original training data or running a simulator. Additionally, DPO is simple to integrate into existing language model training pipelines.

Experiments show that DPO is more sample-efficient and achieves better final performance than existing methods for aligning language models to human preferences, such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and self-consistency training (SCT).
