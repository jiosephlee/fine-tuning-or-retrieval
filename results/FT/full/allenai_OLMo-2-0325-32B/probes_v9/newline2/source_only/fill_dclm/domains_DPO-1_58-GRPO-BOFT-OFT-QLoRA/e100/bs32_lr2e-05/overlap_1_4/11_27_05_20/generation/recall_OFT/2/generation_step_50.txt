The paper "Controlling Text-to-Image Diffusion by Orthogonal Finetuning" presents a novel method for adapting diffusion models, Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which preserves crucial geometric properties of neurons unlike standard finetuning or additive methods like LoRA. Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) is a simple yet effective approach for finetuning text-to-image diffusion models. It works by learning an orthogonal transformation for each neuron in the initial model, thereby preserving the pairwise angles between neurons. This approach has several advantages over existing methods. First, it prevents the loss of information that occurs with standard finetuning. Second, it does not rely on an additional hyperparameter like LoRA. Finally, it can be easily combined with other finetuning methods.

The authors evaluate OFT on two tasks: controlling the pose of an object in an image and editing an object in an image. In both tasks, OFT outperforms existing methods, including ControlNet, DreamBooth, and LoRA. Furthermore, OFT is more stable and reliable than these methods, producing high-quality images more consistently.
