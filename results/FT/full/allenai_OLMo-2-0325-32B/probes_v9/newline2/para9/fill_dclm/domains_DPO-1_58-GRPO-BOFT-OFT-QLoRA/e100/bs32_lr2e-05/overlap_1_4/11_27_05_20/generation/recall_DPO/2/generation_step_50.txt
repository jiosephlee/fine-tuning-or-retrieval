Direct Preference Optimization (DPO): DPO is a method for training language models to align with human preferences without the need for reinforcement learning. Instead, DPO uses a simple loss function that directly optimizes the language model to produce outputs that are more likely to be preferred by humans, based on a preference model. DPO is computionally efficient, requiring only a forward pass through the language models for each training example, and it does not require any sampling or reinforcement learning steps. This makes DPO much faster to train than traditional RLHF methods.

Theoretical Justification: DPO is theoretically grounded by the Bradley-Terry model of pairwise preferences. The Bradley-Terry model assumes that the probability of a human preferring output y1 over y2 is determined by the ratio of their reward model scores: p(prefer(y1 | x) = 1) = σ(r(x, y1) - r(x, y2)), where σ is the logistic function. This model can be extended to a set of N outputs by normalizing the scores: p(prefer(yn | x) =  1) = σ(wn⊤ϕ(x) - ∑i≠n wi⊤ϕ(xi)), where ϕ is a feature extractor, and wi are the parameters of the reward model for each output yi. By setting ϕ(x) = log p(y|x; θ) and wi = θ, we can interpret the Bradley-Terry model as a reparameterization of the language model, where the preference probability is expressed as a function of the language model parameters θ. This reparameterization allows us to derive a closed-form solution for θ that maximizes the likelihood of the observed preferences, leading to the following loss function for DPO: Lpre(D, θ) = -E((x, yn, ȳn) ~ D)[log σ(yn⊤ϕ(y) - ȳn⊤ϕ(Y) - log ∑y ≠yn exp(yn⊤φ(y)))] , where D is the dataset of human preferences, yn is the preferred output, ȳn is the non-preferred output, and Y is the set of all outputs except yn. This loss function is optimized to increase the probability of the preferred output yn while simultaneously decreasing the probability of the non-preferred output ȳn, relative to all other outputs in the set Y. Importantly, the DPO loss is differentiable with respect to the language model parameters Θ, allowing for straightforward optimization using gradient-based methods.

This simplification eliminates the need for an explicit reward model and the associated reward model training step, making DPO more efficient than traditional actor-critic methods.
