The paper "DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models" presents a novel and efficient method for improving mathematical capabilities in language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), instead of the standard memory-intensive PPO algorithm. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a reinforcement learning technique that is both memory and computationally efficient. GRPO is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which is a well-known actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithm. GRPO eliminates the need for a replay buffer by utilizing a group-based baseline, which is calculated using a sliding window of recent samples. This approach significantly reduces memory usage while maintaining the robustness of PPO. Additionally, GRPO incorporates a novel reward normalization technique that enhances the stability of the training process. The authors of the paper applied GRPO to DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B, a pre-trained language model, and observed substantial improvements in mathematical reasoning abilities. The enhanced model, DeepSeekMath-RL 1.3B was evaluated on a variety of mathematical benchmarks, including GSM8K, MATH, and MiniF2F, and demonstrated superior results compared to other open-source models of comparable size. Notably, DeepSeekMath- RL 1.3B achieved 51.7% accuracy on the MATH benchmark, outperforming all other open-source models and most proprietary models with up to 7B parameters. The findings of this study demonstrate that GRPO is an effective and resource-efficient approach for enhancing mathematical reasoning in language models.
