\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large unsupervised language models learn to generate text on their own, but achieving precise control of their output behavior is difficult due to their completely unsupervised nature. Existing methods for gaining steerability typically involve collecting human preferences for model generations and fine-tuning the unsupervised LM using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF; Li et al., 2020; McCallum et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2022). While RLHF can yield impressive results, it is a complex procedure that involves first fitting a reward model to the preferences of a human panel of experts, and then using RL to optimize the unsupervised LM with the desired reward function. This process is further complicated by the fact that the reward model must be learned from a small dataset of human preferences and model generations, making it difficult to ensure the reward model accurately reflects the underlying preferences of the human panel.

In this paper, we introduce Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a single-stage method for fine-tuning language models with human preferences. DPO directly optimizes the unsupervised LM for the desired reward function, without first fitting a reward model and using RL to optimize it. Our key insight is that the optimal reward function for the RLHF problem can be expressed in closed form, allowing DPO to solve the standard RL HF problem with a simple classification loss. We refer to this closed-form reward function as the preference model. Our main contribution is a method for learning the preference model from a small dataset of preferences and model generations, which we call Preference Model Learning (PML). PML uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) to learn a distribution over latent representations of model generations and human preferences, and then estimates the preference model from this distribution. We also introduce a variant of PML called Unsupervised Preference Model Learning (UPML), which does not require a GAN. We evaluate several different approaches to learning the preference model in Section \ref{sec:related_work}, finding that PML and UPML are the most effective at learning from small datasets of preferences and model generations. We also evaluate several different methods for learning the reward function in the standard RLHF pipeline, finding that the most effective approach is a simple classification loss on the latent representations of model generations, which we refer to as Reward Model Learning (RML). We refer to this approach as DPO-RLHF. Finally, we evaluate different methods for optimizing the reward function learned by DPO-RLHF, finding that simple gradient-based methods such as PPO are effective. We refer to this optimization procedure as DPO-RLHF. We summarize our main contributions as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce Direct Preference Optimization ( DPO), a single-stage learning algorithm for fine-tuning language model generations with human preferences. 
    \item We present a method for learning the optimal reward function for RLHF from a small dataset of model generations and human preference pairs, called Preference Model Learning (P ML). 
    \item We introduce a variant of P ML called Unsupervised Preference Learning (UP ML), which does not require the use of a GAN. 
    \item Our experiments show that DPO, PML, and DPO-RLHF can fine-tune LLMs to align with human preferences, and that DPO-RLHF is the most effective method for fine-tuning with D PO. 
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

The field of unsupervised language modeling has seen tremendous progress in recent years, with large language models (LMs; Devlin et al., 2019; BERT; Devlin et al. 2019) achieving state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of tasks. However, achieving precise control of the behavior of these models is difficult due to their unsupervised nature. Several methods have been proposed for fine-tuning LMs with human feedback, including methods that use reinforcement learning (RL; Li et al.,2020; McCallumin et al., 202l; Gao et al. 2022), methods that use supervised learning (Soltanolkotabi et al., 2023), and methods that use a combination of reinforcement learning and supervised learning (Chen et al., 2024). While these methods can yield impressive results, they all involve fitting a reward model to human preferences and then using RL to fine-tune the unsatisfied LM with the desired reward. This process is complicated by the fact that reward models must be learned from a dataset of preferences and model generation, making it difficult to be sure that the reward model accurately reflects human preferences. In this paper, we introduce a single-stage learning approach for fine-tuning LLMs with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (D PO).
