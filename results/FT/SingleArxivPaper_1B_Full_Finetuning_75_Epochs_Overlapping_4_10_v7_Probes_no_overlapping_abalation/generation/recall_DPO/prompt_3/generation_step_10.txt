\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive performance on downstream tasks such as machine translation, question answering, and summarization. However, the completely unsupervised training regime of LMs makes it difficult to control their behavior, especially when fine-tuning them to align with human preferences. Existing methods for gaining such steerable behavior include reinforcement learning from human feedback \citep{li2022reinforcement, li2023direct, li2024direct}, where a human annotator provides feedback on the quality of model generations, and then fine-tunes the unsupervised LM using RL to maximize the human feedback. However, RLHF is a difficult and often unstable procedure, requiring the human to provide feedback on the quality of multiple generations of model generations, and the human to fine-tune the unsuupervised LM to align to the human preferences. In this paper, we introduce a new method for fine-tuning LMs to align with preferences, called \textit{Direct Preferences Optimization} (DPO). DPO is a simple and stable method for fine-tuning an unsupervised LM to match human preferences, without requiring any sampling from the LM or significant hyperparameter tuning. We show that DPO can be used to fine-tune LMs with a simple binary classification loss to align with human preferences in a variety of downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. Our experiments show that DPO outperforms existing methods for fine-tuning LLMs to align to human preferences, and matches or improves the performance of existing methods in all of the tasks we consider. Our experiments also show that DPO is substantially simpler to implement and requires substantially less computational resources than existing methods, making it a promising alternative to existing methods for fine-tuned LMs.
