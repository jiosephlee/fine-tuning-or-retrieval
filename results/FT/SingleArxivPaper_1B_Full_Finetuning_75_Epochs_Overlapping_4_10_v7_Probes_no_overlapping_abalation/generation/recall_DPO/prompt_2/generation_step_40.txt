The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for optimizing the alignment between a language model and human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO uses a reward model that is learned from human preferences and a preference signal from the language model. Unlike existing methods, DPO does not require explicit specification of a reward function or a preference model, but rather optimizes a single objective function that incorporates both the reward model and the preference signal. We evaluate DPO on two datasets of human preferences for corrections to text; the results show that DPO can align language models with human preferences with higher accuracy than previous methods, including PPO-based approaches. We also evaluate several alternative reward models, finding that a simple word-level reward model trained on human feedback performs as well or better than more complex models. Finally, we conduct a human study to compare the ease of using DPO with existing methods. Our experiments and human study suggest that DPO is a promising new approach for aligning language models and human preferences.
