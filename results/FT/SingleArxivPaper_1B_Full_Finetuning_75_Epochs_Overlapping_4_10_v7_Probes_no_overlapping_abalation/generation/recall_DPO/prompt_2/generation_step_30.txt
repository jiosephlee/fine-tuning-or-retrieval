The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for optimizing the alignment of language models with human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO uses a reward model that is learned from human preferences, rather than being hard-coded. This reward model is used to optimize the language model in two steps: first, the model is optimized to maximize the reward, and then the reward is minimized to produce a model that aligns well with human preferences. We evaluate DPO on three datasets of human preferences for summarization, fact checking, and story-telling. DPO produces models that improve over the best existing methods on all datasets, including the best existing methods that were fine-tuned on human preferences. Finally, we show that DPO can be applied to existing language models, such as GPT-J, GPT-4, and OpenAI's GPT-6, to produce models with improved alignment to human preferences.
