\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-scale unsupervised language model (LM) training has led to impressive results in natural language understanding (NLU) and generation (NLG), but achieving precise control of their behaviors is challenging due to the completely unsophisticated nature of their training. In particular, the goal of many approaches is to align the behavior of the LM with human preferences, but this is difficult because the human preferences are not explicitly specified, and the LM is trained on a massive corpus of unlabeled text. 

One approach to aligning the behavior of the LM is to use reinforcement learning (RL) from human feedback. In this approach, the human provides feedback on the quality of model generations, and the model is trained to maximize the human feedback. This approach has been successful in many applications, including sentiment analysis, summarization, and single-turn dialogue. However, the process of collecting human feedback is often complicated and time-consuming, and the resulting reward model is often difficult to interpret. In addition, the reward model is typically trained using reinforcement learning from human feedback with a reinforcement learning from human feedback algorithm (RLHF), which requires a large number of human feedback samples to train the reward model. This makes it difficult to tune the reward model, and the resulting reward models are often unstable.

In this paper, we introduce a new approach to aligning the behaviors of the LM with human feedback, called Direct Preference Optimization. Our approach is based on a new parameterization of reward models that enables extraction of the optimal policy in closed form. We show that this policy can be used to solve the standard RL problem with only a simple loss, eliminating the need to sample from the LM during fine tuning or hyperparameter tuning. We refer to this approach as DPO. Our experiments show that DPO is robust, performant and computationally lightweight, and outperformed existing methods in both sentiment analysis and summarization, as well as in single-turn dialogue. In particular, DPO outperforms PPO-based RLHF, and matches or improves the performance of PPO-based RLHF on sentiment analysis and summarization. DPO also outperforms the state-of-the-art on single-turn dialogue, and achieves comparable performance to PPO-based RLHF while being substantially simpler to train and implement.
