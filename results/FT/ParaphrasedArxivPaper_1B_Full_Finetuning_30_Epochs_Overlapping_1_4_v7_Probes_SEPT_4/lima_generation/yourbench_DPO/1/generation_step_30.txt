At its core, gradient descent is a first-order optimization algorithm used to minimize a differentiable function. It works by iteratively updating the parameters of the model based on the negative of the gradient of the loss function with respect to those parameters. Mathematically, given a loss function $L(\theta)$, where $\theta$ represents the parameters of the model, gradient descent can be expressed as follows:

$$\theta^{(t+1)} = \theta^{(t)} - \alpha \nabla L(\theta^{(t)})$$

Here, $\alpha$ denotes the learning rate, which controls the step size during the optimization process. By adjusting the learning rate, you can control the speed at which the model converges to the optimal solution. A smaller learning rate leads to slower convergence, while a larger learning rate may lead to overshooting and instability. Therefore, selecting an appropriate learning rate is crucial for obtaining reliable results.
