\title{Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model}

\begin{abstract}
While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowledge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training.
Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model.
In this paper we introduce a new parameterization of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss.
The resulting algorithm, which we call \textit{Direct Preference Optimization} (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight, eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing significant hyperparameter tuning.
Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large unsupervised language models (LMs) trained on very large datasets acquire surprising capabilities~\citep{chowdhery2022palm, brown2020language, touvron2023llama,bubeck2023sparks}. However, these models are trained on data generated by humans with a wide variety of goals, priorities, and skillsets. Some of these goals and skillsets may not be desirable to imitate; for example, while we may want our AI coding assistant to \textit{understand} common programming mistakes in order to correct them, nevertheless, when generating code, we would like to bias our model toward the (potentially rare) high-quality coding ability present in its training data. Similarly, we might want our language model to be \textit{aware} of a common misconception believed by 50\% of people, but we certainly do not want the model to claim this misconception to be true in 50\% of queries about it! In other words, selecting the model's \emph{desired responses and behavior} from its very wide \textit{knowledge and abilities} is crucial to building AI systems that are safe, performant, and controllable \citep{ouyang2022training}. While existing methods typically steer LMs to match human preferences using reinforcement learning (RL), we will show that the RL-based objective used by existing methods can be optimized exactly with a simple binary cross-entropy objective, greatly simplifying the preference learning pipeline.

At a high level, existing methods instill the desired behaviors into a language model using curated sets of human preferences representing the types of behaviors that humans find safe and helpful. This preference learning stage occurs after an initial stage of large-scale unsupervised pre-training on a large text dataset. While the most straightforward approach to preference learning is supervised fine-tuning on human demonstrations of high quality responses, the most successful class of methods is reinforcement learning from human (or AI) feedback (RLHF/RLAIF; \citep{christiano2017deep,bai2022constitutional}). RLHF methods fit a reward model to a dataset of human preferences and then use RL to optimize a language model policy to produce responses assigned high reward without drifting excessively far from the original model. While RLHF produces models with impressive conversational and coding abilities, the RLHF pipeline is considerably more complex than supervised learning, involving training multiple LMs and sampling from the LM policy in the loop of training, incurring significant computational costs.

In this paper, we show that the RLHF objective can be optimized exactly with the simple binary cross-entropy loss, greatly simplifying the RLHF pipeline. We introduce a new parameterization for the reward model that enables us to extract the corresponding optimal policy in a closed form, allowing us solve the standard RLHF optimization problem with only a simple binary cross-entropyloss. The resulting algorithm, which we refer to as \textit{Direct Preferences Optimization} (DPO) and which we will refer to as \textbf{DPO}, is stable, performant and computationally lightweight, and eliminates the need for sampling from a large unsupervised LM during fine-tuning. Our experiments show that DPO is able to fine-tune LMs with human preferences as well or better than existing methods, while being substantially simpler to train and implement. In particular, DPO outperforms PPO-based RLHF methods in controlling the sentiment of generations, and outperforms PPO in controlling response quality in summarization, single-turn dialogue, and code generation. DPO also outperforms PPO on the task of controlling the quality of responses in single-turn dialogue, but is less effective in controlling the quality of responses to questions about the model itself. We also show that DPO is capable of learning to generate responses that are more aligned with human preferences than PPO-based RLHF, and that DPO is able in some cases to learn to generate responses that are better aligned with human preferences than the original model.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}
The field of preference learning has been studied extensively in the context of supervised learning, where the goal is to learn a model that can predict the preferences of a human user. In this context, the preference learning problem is often referred to as \textit{satisfaction prediction} or \textit{utility prediction}. In the unsupervised setting, the preference learning problem has been studied in the context of reinforcement learning (RL)~\citep{christiano2020unsupervised, christiano2017deep, bai2022constitutional, chen2022learning, chen2022reinforcement, chen2022unsupervised, chen2022unsupervised, chen2023learning, chen2023reinforcement, cheng2023learning, cheng2023reinforcement}. In this context, the goal is to learn an RL policy that maximizes the reward assigned to a given set of human preferences. The most successful approach to preference learning is reinforcement learning from human feedback, which is referred to as \textbf{\textit{RLHF}}~\citep{bai2022constitutional, bai2022unconstitutional, chen2023unconstitutional, chen2019learning, chen2020reinforcement, chernoff2021reinforcement, chien2021reinforcement}. In the context of RLHF, the preference learning problem can be formulated as a standard RL problem, where the preference learning objective is the reward assigned to a policy that maximizes a reward function. The reward function is typically defined as the sum of the rewards assigned to each of the preferences in the dataset. The reward function is then optimized using reinforcement learning, and the resulting policy is used to train a language model. The resulting language model is then used to generate responses to queries, and the quality of the responses is measured using human feedback. The most successful approach for preference learning is \textbf{\textbf{RLHF}}, which has been shown to produce models with impressive conversational abilities, including the ability to generate responses that are highly aligned with human preferences. However, the RLHF pipeline involves training multiple LMs and performing significant hyperparameter tuning, which is computationally expensive and can be unstable. In this paper, we show how the RLHF objective can instead be optimized exactly with a binary cross-entropy loss.

\section{Direct Preference Optimization}
\label{sec:dpo}
In this section, we introduce the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm, which is a simple and computationally efficient method for fine-tuning a language model to align with human preferences. DPO is based on the observation that the RLHF objective is equivalent to the binary cross-entropy loss applied to a reward model that is parameterized by a set of human preferences. In other words, the RLHF objective can simply be written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLHF}} = -\sum_{i=1}^N \log \mathbb{P}_{\text{human}}(r_{\text{human}, i} \mid \mathcal{D}_{\text{human}}, \mathcal{D}_\text{model})
\label{eq:rlhf}
\end{equation}
