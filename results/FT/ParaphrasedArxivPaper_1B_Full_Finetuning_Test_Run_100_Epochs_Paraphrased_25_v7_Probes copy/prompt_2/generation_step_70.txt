The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a direct preference optimization (DPO) algorithm that uses a reward model to learn a policy that maximizes the likelihood of human preferences while being easy to train and optimize. DPO is based on a simple idea: instead of optimizing the reward function, we can optimize the policy directly by maximizing the likelihood of the observed preferences. This is possible because the likelihood of preferences is invariant to the reward function used to generate them. We demonstrate that DPO can be efficiently implemented using a single neural network, which allows for straightforward implementation and easy hyperparameter tuning. We also introduce a novel loss function, the Direct Preference Loss (DPL), that is optimized by DPO. DPL is a straightforward generalization of the KL-divergence loss, yet it better aligns with human preferences than the KL-divergence, as shown in extensive experiments. Our experiments show that DPO can achieve comparable or better performance than previous approaches, such as PPO, while being easier to train and tune. In particular, DPO is able to match or surpass the performance of PPO on the WMT14 English-German and English-French translation tasks, while requiring only a fraction of the training data and computational resources. We also demonstrate that DPO can learn from preferences collected using different reward models, such as the self-attention-based reward model used by PPO, without any fine-tuning. In summary, our work introduces a straightforward and effective method for aligning language model preferences with human judgments, which could have significant implications for applications such as conversational AI and automated summarization.

Introduction
Language models are widely used to generate text, but their ability to reflect human preferences is limited. Current approaches typically rely on reinforcement learning (RL) to train language models to maximize human preferences, but RL is computationally expensive and difficult to optimize. In this work, we introduce a new approach for training language models to reflect human preferences, based on a direct preference optimization (or DPO) algorithm. DPO is based around a simple idea: instead optimizing the reward function that generates human preferences, we can optimize the language model policy directly by maximizing the probability of those preferences. This is possible due to the invariance of the likelihood of preferences under changes in the reward function. We demonstrate that D PO can be efficiently implemented using an unmodified neural network, allowing for straightforward implementation and easy tuning of the DPO algorithm. Our experiments show that, compared to previous approaches, DPO can achieve comparable performance on translation tasks, such as summarization and dialogue, while requiring only a small fraction of the training data.

Related Work
Language model alignment. Current approaches for aligning language models to human preferences typically involve training a reward model to predict human preferences, and then using reinforcement learning to train a language model to maximize the reward. One of the earliest approaches is the PPO-based method introduced by Goodfellow et al. (2020), which uses a reward model based on self-attention to train a language model. Another approach is the REINFORCE-based method of McCallum et al. (2018), which uses a reward function based on a neural network to train a language model, but does not use reinforcement learning. In both cases, the language model is trained to maximize the reward, which is typically obtained by sampling from the language model and applying a reward function to the resulting preference sequence. While these methods can achieve high performance, they are computationally intensive, as they require training a large reward model and using reinforcement learning to train the language model. In this work, our approach is based on a similar idea, but instead of training a reward model and using reinforcementlearning to train a language model based on it, we train the language model directly to maximize the likelihood of human preferences.
Preference-based language models. Preference-based language models (PBLMs) are language models that are trained to reflect human preferences, rather than directly using reinforcement learning. The first PBLM was proposed by Korbak et al. (2021), who used a reward model to train a language model using a reinforcement learning approach. Later, a PBLM was trained using a neural network reward model based on selfattention, which was trained using a variant of the REINFORCE algorithm (Bai et al., 2019). These approaches are similar to the PPO-based methods described above, but instead of using reinforcement learning to train language models based on reward models, they use a reward model to train language models directly.
