The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the well-known RLHF algorithm to the case where the reward function is not explicitly defined, but rather is inferred from preference data. This allows the use of language models trained on large corpora, which are typically unavailable for fine-tuning with explicit rewards. The method is based on the observation that the optimal policy for the reward function can be expressed as a linear combination of the language model's parameters, and that this optimal policy can be computed efficiently using a variant of the gradient descent algorithm known as DPO. Experimental results show that the DPO algorithm can align language models with human preferences as effectively as existing approaches, while requiring significantly less computational resources.

Section 4 describes how to apply DPO to a variety of preference datasets, and Section 5 concludes the paper with a discussion of future work.

Related Work
Language model fine-tuning with human feedback is a relatively new area of research, with the first studies appearing in 2018 [1,2]. These early works typically used reinforcement learning (RL) to train language models to maximize human preferences, using a reward function that was either explicitly defined or inferred from preference data. In 2019, a number of studies explored the use of RLHF to fine-tune language models, with the most successful method being PPO [3], which achieved state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks. However, RLHF requires access to a reward function, which is often unavailable for large-scale language models. In 2020, a number of researchers proposed methods for fine-tuning language models with human preferences without the use of a reward function. These approaches typically rely on the observation that the policy learned by a language model is equivalent to a reward function, and that the optimal policy can be expressed as a weighted sum of the model's parameters. This observation is known as the reward model principle [4], and has been used to develop a number of algorithms for aligning language models and human preferences, including the RLHF algorithm [5], the REINFORCE algorithm [6], and the policy gradient method [7]. In this work, we focus on the DPO algorithm, which is a generalization of these methods to the case where the underlying reward function is not explicitly known.
In addition to language model fine-tuning, the alignment of models with human preferences is also relevant in the context of automated bias detection. In this context, the goal is to identify patterns in the model's output that indicate the presence of undesirable biases. One approach is to use a pre-trained language model to generate test prompts, and then compare the model's output to a set of human-generated test responses. If the model's output is significantly different from the human responses, it is possible to infer that the model contains undesirable biases [8,9]. This technique is known as prompt-based bias detection, and is typically applied to language models that have been fine-tuned with human feedback. In contrast, the approach presented in this paper focuses on aligning language models with explicit human preferences, rather than identifying biases in the model's output.

Direct Preference Optimization
The DPO algorithm is based on the observation made in [10] that the optimal policy for a reward function can be expressed in the form of a linear combination of the model's parameters, where the coefficients are determined by the preference data. Problem Formulation
