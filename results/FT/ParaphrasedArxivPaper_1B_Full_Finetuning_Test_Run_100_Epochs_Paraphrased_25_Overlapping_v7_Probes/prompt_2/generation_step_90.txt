The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a generalization of the reinforcement learning approach to the case of preference learning from discrete rewards, which is known as the reward alignment problem. The authors propose a method called DPO, which is based on a reward model that is learned from human preferences. DPO is shown to be effective in aligning language models with preferences, outperforming previous approaches like PPO-based RLHF and baselines such as RLHF with a reward model learned from human feedback. The DPO algorithm is also efficient, requiring only a single pass over the preference data, and is easy to implement in practice. The paper is accompanied by a set of experiments that demonstrate the effectiveness of DPO on a range of tasks, including summarization, dialogue, and translation. In summary, the paper makes the following contributions: 1) A general framework for learning reward models from human preferences, which can be applied to a wide range of preference learning scenarios. 2) A novel algorithm, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), that learns a reward model from human preferences, without the need for any explicit human feedback. 3) Experiments on a range of preference learning tasks, showing that DPO aligns language models with human preferences more effectively than previous approaches.

Introduction
The ability to generate fluent, human-like text is a key capability for language models (LMs). However, current LMs are trained on large corpora of text, which may not reflect the kinds of texts people actually prefer. In order to address this, a number of approaches have been proposed to fine-tune LMs using human preferences. These methods typically involve training an LM to maximize a reward function, which is defined in terms of the preference data. This approach is known as reinforcement learning (RL), and it has been successfully applied to a range of preference learning scenarios (see, e.g., (Bai et al., 2019;Chen et al., 2020;Kupcsik et al., 2022;Zhang et al., 2023)). In this work, we refer to the process of learning a reward model from human preference data as reward alignment.
The RL-based approach to reward alignment has been demonstrated to be effective in align-ing language models with human preferences, outperforming approaches that use only preference data (e.g., (Bai and Mitchell, 2019;Choi et al., 2021;Kupcsik and Manning, 2022)). However, the RL-based reward alignment algorithms typically require a single pass over the entire preference dataset, which can be computationally expensive. In this work, we introduce a new algorithm, Direct Preference Optimization, which can learn a reward model from preference data in a single pass, without the need for offline human feedback. Our experiments show that DPO is able to align language models with human preferences as effectively as existing approaches, while being significantly more efficient.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the reward alignment problem and review the RL-based approaches to solving it. In Section 3, we present DPO, the main contribution of this work. In Section 4, we present and discuss experimental results. We conclude in Section 5.
