The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a new algorithm for optimizing the reward function used in the training of language models, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO is an end-to-end optimization algorithm that leverages the power of reinforcement learning to learn a reward function that aligns with human preferences. DPO is simple to implement and can be used with any existing language model. We evaluate DPO on a variety of datasets and languages, including the Common Crawl, Reddit, and Wikipedia. Our experiments show that DPO can improve the performance of language models by up to 5% relative to the best-performing baselines, while also improving the quality of the resulting models. We also present a new dataset of human preferences for language models, which we use to evaluate DPO's effectiveness. Our experiments show that the DPO reward function learned by DPO is more aligned with human preferences than the reward functions learned by other methods, including the widely-used PPO algorithm. 1. Introduction. Language models are a key component of many applications that require natural language understanding, including text generation, summarization, and question answering. However, current language models are trained on large datasets of unstructured text, which makes it difficult to evaluate their quality and ensure that they meet user expectations. One approach to addressing these challenges is to use human preferences to guide the training of language models. In particular, language models can be trained to maximize the likelihood of human preferences by optimizing a reward function that measures the quality of the generated text. This approach, known as reward learning, has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of language models (Hoffman et al., 2019;Liu et al., 2020;Gao et al., 2021).
However, existing reward learning algorithms, such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2022), have several limitations. First, they require access to a large number of human preferences, which can be difficult to collect. Second, existing algorithms optimize the reward function using reinforcement learning, which can be computationally expensive and may not converge to a globally optimal solution. Finally, existing algorithms do not take into account the fact that language models are trained to maximize the reward, which can lead to models that are poorly aligned with human preferences. In this paper, we address these challenges by developing a new algorithm for optimizing reward functions in the context of reward learning. Our key insight is that the reward function can be optimized using a simple optimization algorithm called Direct Preference Optimization (or DPO for short). DPO is a simple end-to-end optimization algorithm for learning a reward function that maximizes the likelihood of human preferences. Unlike existing algorithms, DPO does not require access to a large dataset of human preferences, and it can be used with any language model. We evaluate the performance of D PO on a variety of datasets, including the Common Crawler, Reddit, and Wikipedia, and show that DPO can significantly improve the performance of language model compared to existing algorithms. We also evaluate the alignment of the reward functions learned by D PO with human preferences, and find that DPO is more effective than existing algorithms at optimizing reward functions that are more aligned with human expectations.

Our experiments show that rewards learned by DPO are better aligned with human preferences than those learned by existing algorithms.
