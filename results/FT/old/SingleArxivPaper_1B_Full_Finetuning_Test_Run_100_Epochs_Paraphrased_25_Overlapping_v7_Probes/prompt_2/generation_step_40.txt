The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a general framework for training language models to satisfy human preferences, called Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). DPO differs from existing methods in two key ways. First, DPO directly optimizes the model's reward function, rather than the model itself, using an online gradient-based algorithm. Second, DPO uses a preference dataset that is tailored to the specific application of aligning models with human preferences. We evaluate DPO on three different applications of language models with human preferences: fact checking, quality improvement, and political speech moderation. On all three tasks, DPO outperforms or matches the state-of-the-art. In fact checking, DPO is up to 3.5 times more efficient than existing fact-checking methods, and achieves higher precision at lower cost than existing quality improvement methods. In political speech moderation, DPO matches the performance of existing methods while being up to 10 times more efficient. Our experiments show that DPO can be applied to real-world datasets and systems with minimal changes, making it a broadly applicable tool for aligning language models and human preferences.
