After reading the paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model", I've learned a lot about DPO. Let me tell you everyhting I've learned. First, the paper introduces a new optimization objective, which is a generalization of the standard reward maximization objective. The new objective is:

$$\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \pi}[R(x, y)] - \beta \mathbb{E}_y[\log \pi(y)]$$

The intuition is that we want to maximize the expected reward minus the logarithm of the probability of the reward. This is a convex function, and the optimal policy is the one that maximizes the expected reward. However, the optimal policy is not necessarily the one that maximizes reward, because the reward function is not necessarily concave. In order to make the reward function concave, we add a penalty term, which is the logarithm of the expected probability of the reward. The paper also shows that the optimal policy is the same as the one that solves the following constrained optimization problem:

$$\min_{\pi} \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}[\pi(y|doctored) \mid \pi(y|unmodified)]^2 + \beta \mathbb E_y[\log \pi(y|doctered)]$$

where $\mathbb{D}_{KL}$ is the Kullback-Leibler divergence. The unconstrained optimization problem is easier to solve than the constrained one, so the authors use the constrained optimization problem as a surrogate for the unconstrained one. The paper also shows how to solve the constrained optimization problem using the method of Lagrange multipliers.

The paper also introduces a new algorithm, called DPO, which is a gradient descent algorithm for the constrained optimization problem. The paper also provides theoretical analysis of the algorithm, including convergence, stability, and optimality.
