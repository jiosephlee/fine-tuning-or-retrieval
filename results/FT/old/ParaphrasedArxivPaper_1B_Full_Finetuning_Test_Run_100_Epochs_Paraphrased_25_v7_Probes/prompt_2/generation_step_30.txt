The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a direct preference optimization (DPO) algorithm that uses a reward model to learn a language model that maximizes the likelihood of the human preferences, while avoiding the need for explicit human feedback. The DPO algorithm is simple to implement and can be applied to existing language models. We evaluate the DPO algorithm on two datasets: the OpenAI Five benchmark and the Reddit preferences dataset. Our experiments show that the DPO algorithm can align language models with human preferences up to 3.5 times faster than the best existing methods, while maintaining or surpassing the performance of the best existing methods. We also demonstrate that the DPO algorithm is robust to changes in the reward model, and can be applied to language models trained with different loss functions. We release the DPO algorithm and the datasets used in our experiments as open-source code on GitHub.

Introduction
Language models (LMs) are widely used in natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as machine translation (Peters et al., 2018), summarization (Bai et al., 2019), and dialogue (Chen et al., 2020). However, LMs are not perfect, and they often produce outputs that are not aligned with human preferences. For example, a recent study by OpenAI found that the top-1 accuracy of their GPT-4 model on the OpenAI Five benchmark is only 0.5% higher than the top-1 accuracy on the same dataset when using the human preference dataset as a reference (GPT-4 vs. Human, 2022). This suggests that current LMs are not sufficiently aligned with human preferences.
One way to improve the alignment between LMs and human preferences is to use human feedback to train the LMs. However, collecting human feedback is time-consuming and expensive. In this work, we present a novel approach for training LMs with human preferences that does not require human feedback. Our approach, called direct preference optimization (D PO ), uses a reward model to train an LLM that maximizes the likelihood that the human preferences are satisfied. This is in contrast to previous approaches that use a reward model to train the LLM, but then use human feedback to adjust the reward to match the preferences. Our experiments show that D PO can align LMs with human preferences up to three times faster than the best previous method, while maintaining or surpasses the performance of the best previous method. Our experiments also show that D PO is robust to changes in reward models, and can be applied with different loss functions.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We present a novel approach for aligning language models (LMs) with human preferences, called direct preference optimization. Our approach does not require human feedback, and can be applied directly to existing LMs. • We present a simple and efficient algorithm for training LMs with direct preference optimization, called DPO. Our experiments show that, compared to the best previous method, DPO can align LMs with preferences up to three times as fast, while maintaining or surpass-ing the performance of the best method. • We release the DPO method and the datasets used in the experiments as open-source code.

Related Work
Language model fine-tuning. Language model fine-tuning (LMF) is a popular technique for improving the performance of LMs on downstream NLP tasks. In the standard approach, an LLM is first trained on a large corpus of text, and then fine-tuned on a specific task such as machine translation (MTP) or summarization (SMT). In recent years, several alternative objectives have been proposed, including the self-attention score (Bahdanau et al., 2015), the cross-entropy with a log likelihood penalty (Chen et al. 2020), and the cross-entropy with an additional reward term (GPT-4, 2022). These objectives are typically referred to as language model fine-tuning (LMTF).
Human preference evaluation. Human preference evaluation is a common technique for assessing the quality of language models. In this approach, a human annotator is presented with a prompt and asked to select the preferred output from a set of candidate outputs. The human preference dataset consists of pairs of prompts and outputs, and the goal is to find an LLM that aligns with the preferences of the human annotators. The most common preference datasets are the OpenAI Five (GPT-4) and Reddit (GPT-4 and Humans) datasets. The OpenAI Five dataset contains prompts and outputs from the OpenAI Five AI competition, and the Reddit dataset contains prompts and outputs generated by GPT-4 and human annotators.
