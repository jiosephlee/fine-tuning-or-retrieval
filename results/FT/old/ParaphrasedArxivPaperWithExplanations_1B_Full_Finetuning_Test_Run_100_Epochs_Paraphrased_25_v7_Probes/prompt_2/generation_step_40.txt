The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a direct preference optimization (DPO) algorithm that uses a reward model to learn a policy that maximizes the likelihood of the observed data while also aligning with human preferences. DPO is an extension of the well-known reinforcement learning (RL) approach of optimizing the reward function to maximize the likelihood of the observed training data. In contrast to previous approaches, DPO does not require explicit reward modeling or any form of supervised learning. Instead, DPO learns a reward model from the observed data using a simple maximum likelihood objective. The resulting reward model is then used to optimize the policy, which is represented by a language model. Experimental results show that DPO can achieve comparable or better performance than existing methods, such as PPO, on a variety of preference-based evaluation tasks, including summarization, dialogue, and machine translation. In particular, DPO achieves state-of-the-art results on the summarization task, surpassing the current state-of-the-art summarization model by 7.5% F1 score on the SQuAD v1.5 test set. On the dialogue benchmark, DPO outperforms the current best model by 2.5% F1 on the human evaluation, and achieves a 0.5% improvement over the best model on the machine evaluation. On the machine translation task, DPO matches the performance of the current best model, and in some cases, it surpasses it by up to 2.5% on the BLEU metric.

Introduction
Language models are widely used in various applications, such as summarization, machine translation, and dialogue. However, these models are trained using unsupervised objectives, such as the self-attention-based language model fine-tuning (LMFT) objective (Peters et al., 2020;Radford et al., 2021), which is based on the unsupervised objective of language modeling. Although these models are effective, they are not optimized with human preferences in mind. This is a significant limitation, as human preferences are an important factor in determining the quality of language models. For example, when evaluating language models, humans often prefer longer and more informative responses. In contrast, LMFT models tend to produce shorter and less informative responses. Therefore, it is important to develop methods that can align language models with human preferences.
In this work, we introduce a new approach for aligning language models to human preferences, which we call direct preference optimization (D PO ). D PO is based on the reinforcement learning (RL) paradigm, where the goal is to optimize a reward function to maximize the probability of the observed data while simultaneously aligning with human preferences (Sutton and Barto, 2018). In contrast to previous approaches that use reinforcement learning to optimize a reward function, D PO does not require explicit reward models or any form of supervised training. Instead, D PO learns a reward model from observed data using a simple likelihood objective. The resulting model is then used to train a policy, which is represented as a language model. Experimental evaluations show that D PO can achieve comparable or better results than existing approaches, such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), on a variety of preferencebased evaluation tasks, including summarisation, dialogue, and machine translations. In particular, D PO achieves state-of-the-art performance on summarization, surpassing the current best summarization model by 6.5% F1, and outperforming the best model by up to 2% on the human evaluation. On the dialogue benchmark (Bordes et al., 2013), D PO achieves a 2.5% improvement over current best models on the human evaluation, while also matching the performance of the best model on the automated evaluation. On the machine translations task, D PO matches the performance of the best current model, and in some instances, it surpasses it, by up to 2%.

Related Work
Language Model Fine-Tuning. Language model fine-tuning (LMTF) is a popular technique for training language models using large amounts of text data. In LMTF, a language model is trained on a large corpus of text, typically using the unsupervised objective from Peters et al. (2020), which is based on a variant of the self-attention mechanism. This objective is known as the language modeling objective, and it is commonly referred to as LMFT. Although LMTF is effective, it is not optimized with human preferences. In contrast, we introduce a method that uses reinforcement learning to optimize a language model with human preferences in mind.
