The paper "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is a Secret Reward model" presents a novel and efficient method for aligning language models with human preferences. The core contribution of the paper is a direct preference optimization (DPO) algorithm that uses a reward model to learn a policy that maximizes the likelihood of human preferences while being easy to optimize. DPO is based on a simple yet powerful idea: the reward model can be viewed as a generative model for human preferences, and the policy we want to optimize is the language model. We show that the reward model can be used to define a loss that is equivalent to the KL-divergence between the reward model's output and the true preferences, but is easier to optimize. This loss is called the DPO objective. We then present a straightforward way to train the reward model using reinforcement learning, which we call RL-DPO. Our experiments show that RL-DPO can achieve comparable or better results than current approaches like PPO, while being much easier to implement and train. In particular, RL-DPO can be trained on large-scale preference datasets in a few hours, whereas PPO requires days of training on a single GPU.
We also present a new method for optimizing the DPO objective, called DPO-REINFORCE. DPO-REINFORCE is an off-the-shelf reinforcement learning algorithm that can be applied to any reward model, not just the reward model used in RL-DPO. DPO-RE-INFORCE is straightforward to implement and train, and it matches or outperforms PPO in our experiments. In summary, our main contributions are: • A new approach for training language models based on human preferences, called direct preference optimization (D PO). D PO uses a reward model to train a policy that aligns with human preferences, without relying on reinforcement learning. • A straightforward method for training the reward model using reinforcement learn- ing, called DPO- REINFORCE. DPO -REINFORCE is an off-the-shelve reinforcement learning algorithm that can train the reward model for D PO, without requiring any modification of the D PO algorithm. • Experiments on a range of preference datasets, showing that DPO-REIN-FORCE can match or outperform PPO-based approaches.

Related Work
Language Model Fine-Tuning. Language model fine-tuning (LMFT) has been shown to improve many downstream language tasks, such as summarization, question answering, and machine translation (see, e.g.,  and . However, LMFT models are typically trained on vast quantities of text, and their performance on downstream tasks is sensitive to the specific text used for fine-tuning. To address this, several studies have proposed methods for fine-tuning language models on specific tasks without relying on large amounts of text. These approaches typically involve training a separate model to predict human preferences for the target task, and then using this model to fine-tune the language model. One common approach is to use a reward model trained on human preference data to guide the fine-tuning process. This is known as reward modeling (RM), and has been shown to yield significant improvements over LMFT. For example, the PPO-based method RAPID (Chen et al., 2022) uses a reward model trained on the Reddit dataset (Wu et al., 2019) to guide the fine-tuned language model. Another approach is to use a reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm to directly optimize the language model based on the reward model. This is known as reinforcement learning with preference data (RLWD;  and , and its variant, direct preference optimization (D Po; , where the reward model is referred to as the preference model). D PO is a straightforward method for fine-tuning language model with human preferences, and it has been shown to yield comparable or better results than reward modeling approaches like PPO. D PO is based on the idea that the reward model can itself be viewed as a generativ model of human preferences, and that the language model can be optimized to maximize the likelihood of the reward model given the true preferences. This is in contrast to traditional reinforcement learning algorithms, which typically optimize the reward function using an RL algorithm, such as RL-FP (Liu et al., 2023) or RL-DP (Zhang et al., 2024).
Direct Preference Optimization vs. Reinforcement Learning. D PO is a straightforward approach for training language models with human preferences, but it is not the only method for doing so. Another approach is to train a language model using reinforcement learning, where the reward function is learned from human preferences. This is known as RL-based preference learning (RLL; , and its variant, RLWD (Zhang et al.), where the reward model is called the preference model. RLL and RLWD have been shown to yield better results than traditional reward modeling approaches like P PO. However, RL-based approaches are more complex to implement and train, as they require the use of reinforcement learning algorithms like RL-FP or RL-DP. In this work, we present a method for training language models using human preferences that is simpler and easier to implement than RL-based approaches.
